### 1. Executive Summary
This study sequences and assembles four novel equine coronavirus (ECoV) genomes from fecal samples of clinically ill horses in the United States and combines them with 10 publicly available ECoV genomes and representative *Embecovirus* Spike sequences to investigate intra-host variation, genome-wide recombination, and site-specific positive selection (using LoFreq, RDP5, GARD, MEME, and Contrast-FEL). The authors report two intra-host single-nucleotide variants in the nucleocapsid (`N`) gene across the four horses, 12 putative recombination events in ECoV (11 in `ORF1ab` and 1 spanning Spike S2 to the 3' end), 9 ECoV codons with MEME $p \le 0.05$, and a contrast between 42 vs. 2 (or 1) positively selected Spike sites on the single ancestral branches leading to human coronaviruses HCoV-OC43 and HCoV-HKU1.

While the contribution of four full-length North American ECoV genomes and raw metagenomic RNA-seq datasets is valuable for veterinary surveillance, the evolutionary and statistical analyses suffer from major methodological weaknesses: (1) site-level MEME tests across tiny ECoV alignments ($N = 11\text{--}21$) and on single ancestral *Embecovirus* branches are reported at unadjusted nominal $p \le 0.05$ without multiple-testing correction, where expected false positives equal or exceed the number of detected sites; (2) comparing raw site counts on single ancestral branches (42 on HCoV-OC43 vs. 2 on HCoV-HKU1) confounds branch length, taxon sampling density, and unmasked recombination with host-jump adaptation; (3) two single-nucleotide variants (one synonymous, one at 10.16% minor allele frequency) in unreplicated diagnostic fecal RNA-seq are over-interpreted as "intra-host evolution" and "quasispecies development", supported by the factually incorrect claim that Illumina RNA-seq library preparation involves no DNA amplification; and (4) the recombination analysis contradicts itself between whole-genome RDP5 and single-gene GARD.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Unadjusted Multiple Testing Across Small Alignments and Singletons:** In Table 3, MEME is applied to ECoV gene alignments containing only 11 to 21 unique sequences (`HE`: 14, `S`: 16, `E`: 11, `M`: 17, `N`: 21), reporting all sites with nominal $p \le 0.05$ without Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction. Across ~1,360 codons in Spike, ~440 in N, and ~420 in HE (>2,200 codon tests), finding 9 sites with $0.002 \le p \le 0.042$ is entirely consistent with the null expectation under unadjusted $\alpha = 0.05$ (and 6 of the 9 sites in Table 3 are singletons where a single sequence differs from the consensus, e.g., $\text{A}_{13}\text{S}_1$, $\text{V}_{13}\text{L}_1$, $\text{T}_{15}\text{N}_1$, $\text{S}_{20}\text{A}_1$).
* **Single-Branch MEME Inference and Confounded Branch Comparisons:** In the *Embecovirus*-wide Spike analysis (Figure 1B, Table S8), MEME was run with a **single ancestral branch** designated as the foreground branch for each of the 11 clades. Testing a single branch ($B_{\text{fg}} = 1$) at a single codon site provides at most one evolutionary realization along that branch; even with 100 parametric bootstrap replicates, unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ calls across >1,300 codons will be dominated by branch length (total substitutions along that ancestral branch), multinucleotide mutations, and local alignment uncertainty. Attributing the difference between 42 sites on the HCoV-OC43 ancestral branch and 2 sites on the HCoV-HKU1 ancestral branch to multi-host adaptive history without normalizing for ancestral branch length, synonymous substitution saturation, or FDR is statistically invalid.
* **Internal Contradiction Between Whole-Genome (RDP5) and Single-Gene (GARD) Recombination Screening:** On the 14 full-length ECoV genomes, RDP5 identifies Event 12, *"which begins in subunit 2 (S2) of Spike and continues until the end of the genome (Fig. 4)"* (encompassing S2, E, M, and N). Four sentences later, the authors state: *"There was no evidence for genetic recombination within any of the following ECoV viral protein alignments analyzed: HE, S, E, M, and N."* Because GARD lacked power on the short single-gene alignments to detect the breakpoint that RDP5 found in S2, the authors ran MEME on the unpartitioned S and N alignments, ignoring the recombination event they had just reported in Figure 4.
* **Numerical Inconsistency Between Abstract/Results and Conclusion:** The Abstract and Results state that the HCoV-OC43 and HCoV-HKU1 ancestral branches have **42 and 2** positively selected sites, respectively, whereas the final sentence of the Discussion states *"with large numbers of positively selected sites in Spike in HCoV-OC43 ( 42 ) and **only one** in HCoV-HKU1."*

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Four Novel Full-Length US ECoV Genome Assemblies [Well Supported]:** High-coverage assemblies (mean ~9,079× depth) from four clinically affected horses across New York, California, and Florida represent a solid empirical contribution to coronavirus genomic surveillance.
* **Intra-Host Evolution and Quasispecies Development in the N Gene [Overstated / Methodologically Flawed]:** Across four ~30.7 kb genomes, only two intra-host variants met the 10%–90% mAF filter: one non-synonymous variant in Horse 1 (`N` S15F at 10.16% mAF, barely 0.16% above the threshold) and one synonymous variant in Horse 4 (`N` Y185Y at 15.16% mAF). A single time-point fecal sample with one polymorphic site cannot distinguish active intra-host adaptive diversification ("quasispecies development") from mixed infection or reverse-transcription/library amplification artifacts. Moreover, the Discussion's justification that *"since our samples had very high viral loads, and because our method does not require DNA amplification, many of the common sources of error are not applicable"* is factually wrong: viral loads (e.g., RT-qPCR Ct values) were never measured, and the NEBNext Ultra II Directional RNA Library Prep kit explicitly performs reverse transcription followed by PCR amplification of cDNA libraries.
* **Adaptive Evolution in the ECoV Spike Signal Peptide and Ancestral Host-Jump Contrasts [Fragile / Overstated]:** The claim of positive selection at ECoV Spike codon 11 ($\text{T}_{14}\text{G}_2$, unadjusted $p = 0.002$, uncorrected for ~1,360 Spike codons) and the narrative contrasting HCoV-OC43 vs. HCoV-HKU1 ancestral adaptation rest on uncorrected site-level tests and lack functional or structural validation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **High Data & Workflow Transparency:** Raw metagenomic sequencing reads (`PRJNA1005627`), assembled genomes (`OR468097–OR468100`), the Galaxy LoFreq workflow, HyPhy analysis scripts (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`), and all alignments and trees (`data.hyphy.org/web/ECoV/ECoV-data/`) are publicly accessible.
* **Required Fixes:** Apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across all codons tested in Table 3 and Table S8, partition Spike at the RDP5 Event 12 breakpoint before running selection analyses, correct the false statement regarding PCR amplification in NEBNext RNA-seq library preparation, and reconcile the HCoV-HKU1 site count (2 vs. 1) between the Results and Discussion.
