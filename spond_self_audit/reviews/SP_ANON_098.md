## 1. Executive Summary
`SP_ANON_098` introduces `DEPS` (Directional Evolution of Protein Sequences), a maximum-likelihood phylogenetic framework designed to detect directional positive selection toward specific target amino acid residues across protein alignments rooted with an outgroup. Standard codon-based selection tests (`FEL`, `REL`, `PAML`) estimate the ratio of nonsynonymous to synonymous substitution rates ($\omega = d_N / d_S$) under time-reversible Markov models; consequently, they have high power to detect recurrent *diversifying* selection (where a site repeatedly mutates among multiple residues) but frequently miss *directional* selection—such as rapid selective sweeps, consensus replacements, or repeated convergent acquisition of a specific target residue $Y$—because a single sweep adds very few nonsynonymous substitutions to the tree and leaves the post-sweep clade under strong purifying selection ($\omega \ll 1$).

The authors formulate a non-reversible amino acid substitution model in which baseline reversible exchangeabilities $R_{ij}$ (estimated from the alignment via a 190-rate reversible matrix `REV` or empirical matrices like `JTT`/`WAG`/`Flu`) are augmented for a specific target residue $Y$ by a directional bias multiplier $B^{(Y)} \ge 1$ acting on all transitions $X \to Y$ across a fraction $q^{(Y)}$ of sites (modeled via a 2-bin discrete mixture at each target residue). Testing each of the 20 amino acids via a 2-df likelihood ratio test (LRT) against the null $H_0: B^{(Y)} = 1 \text{ or } q^{(Y)} = 0$ (using a conservative $\chi^2_2$ distribution adjusted by $p \le 0.05 / 20 = 0.0025$ Bonferroni correction across the 20 targets) followed by an Empirical Bayes factor ($\text{EBF} \ge 100$) site assignment provides a principled two-stage inference procedure. Extensive parametric simulations validate false-positive control and power across sample sizes and divergence regimes, and application to all 8 genomic segments of avian H5N1 and human H3N2 Influenza A virus (IAV) recovers known antigenic cluster-transition and N-linked glycosylation sites missed by `FEL`. This is a methodologically rigorous, creative, and well-validated contribution to molecular evolution.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit

### Strengths in Mathematical Formulation and Simulation Benchmarking
1. **Non-Reversible Rooted Markov Process with Mixture-of-Targets Formulation:**
   Correctly recognizing that directional selection violates stationarity and time-reversibility (Pulley Principle), the authors root the tree using an explicit outgroup and compute site likelihoods via directional pruning from the root using estimated root equilibrium frequencies $\pi^0$. Separating baseline spatial rate heterogeneity ($\Gamma_4$ over overall site rates $r_s$) from the 2-point directional mixture ($B = 1$ with probability $1 - q$, $B > 1$ with probability $q$) cleanly isolates residue-specific acceleration from general site hypervariability.
2. **Thorough Adversarial Simulation Study of Model Misspecification:**
   In Table 1 and Figure 1, the authors do not merely simulate under the exact parametric null; they explicitly test what happens when sequences evolve under a custom `Flu` baseline matrix (or codon-level diversifying selection) but are analyzed under a misspecified `JTT` baseline model. They demonstrate that severe baseline matrix misspecification inflates the gene-level Type I error rate (up to $29\%$ when $N = 100$), and prove that estimating the 189 free exchangeability parameters directly from the alignment (`REV` baseline) restores the false-positive rate to conservative levels ($\le 1.3\%$ at nominal $\alpha = 5\%$).
3. **Gene-by-Gene Comparative LRT Between Serotypes (H5N1 vs. H3N2):**
   The joint likelihood test comparing shared vs. serotype-specific $(B^{(Y)}, q^{(Y)})$ parameters across 320 segment-by-residue combinations provides a formal statistical framework for comparing directional selective regimes between avian and human hosts.

### Methodological & Statistical Vulnerabilities
1. **Sensitivity of the "Repeated Rare Substitutions" (`RRS`) Category to Single-Event Outliers:**
   - *Technical critique:* In the post hoc heuristic classification of detected sites (Table 2 and Results), the authors note that for some sites classified under `RRS` (such as site 467 in H3N2 NA, where only a *single* $\text{M} \to \text{F}$ substitution occurs in the entire tree, or sites 298 and 753 in H3N2 PB1 with only two $\text{L} \to \text{I}$ substitutions), `DEPS` yields an Empirical Bayes Factor $\ge 100$. Because $\text{EBF}_s^{(Y)} = \frac{\Pr(B > 1 \mid D_s) / \Pr(B = 1 \mid D_s)}{q / (1 - q)}$ divides the posterior odds by the prior odds $q / (1 - q)$, whenever $\hat{q}$ is small and $\hat{B}$ is driven to a very large value (or when the baseline reversible rate $R_{XY}$ in `Flu` is near zero), a single substitution along a short branch can produce an extreme likelihood ratio $L(D_s \mid B = \hat{B}) / L(D_s \mid B = 1) > 100$.
   - *Impact:* The authors candidly acknowledge this vulnerability in the text (*"An even more extreme case is site 467 in H3N2 NA, where a single low-rate substitution from methionine to phenylalanine appears sufficiently significant... such findings may be sensitive to baseline model misspecification and should be treated with caution"*). However, in an Empirical Bayes mixture with point estimates $(\hat{B}, \hat{q})$ plugged in without parameter uncertainty integration, a single unconfirmed sequencing error or rare polymorphism on a short terminal branch can cross $\text{EBF} \ge 100$ if $R_{XY} \approx 0$. Adding a minimum threshold of $\ge 2$ phylogenetically independent transitions toward $Y$ as a filter on site-level calls would prevent singleton substitution artifacts.
2. **Independence Assumption Across the 20 Separate Target Residue Fits:**
   - *Technical critique:* `DEPS` fits 20 separate alternative models (one per target amino acid $Y \in \{1, \dots, 20\}$), each holding the remaining 19 residues at baseline rates, rather than a unified joint model where multiple targets compete simultaneously. At highly polymorphic sites undergoing successive sweeps ($A \to B \to C$, such as H5N1 HA site 156 or 172), running independent tests per target residue treats transitions to $B$ as "background" when testing $C$ and vice versa.
   - *Impact:* While computationally necessary to avoid optimizing 40 mixture parameters simultaneously, this independent-scan architecture means that posterior probabilities across the 20 targets at a single site are not mutually normalized on a common probability space.
3. **Lack of Multiple-Testing Correction Across the 320 Serotype-Comparison Tests:**
   - *Technical critique:* In the "H5N1 and H3N2 Gene-by-Gene Comparison" section (line 38), the authors perform $8 \text{ segments} \times 20 \text{ residues} \times 2 \text{ parameter tests} = 320$ pairwise LRT comparisons at an unadjusted threshold of $P < 0.05$, finding $6 / 320$ ($1.9\%$) significant differences. Under a global null hypothesis across 320 independent tests at $\alpha = 0.05$, the expected number of false positives is $320 \times 0.05 = 16 > 6$.
   - *Impact:* The authors appropriately phrase these 6 hits as *"potentially different ($P < 0.05$)"*, but explicitly noting that $6 / 320$ is below the nominal Type I expectation under the global null would sharpen the statistical interpretation: globally, avian H5N1 and human H3N2 segments exhibit remarkably similar gene-wide distributions of directional selection parameters outside of HA.

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim:** *`DEPS` identifies adaptive selective sweeps and consensus residue replacements that are invisible to—or misclassified as negatively selected by—traditional $d_N / d_S$ methods like `FEL`.* — **[Well Supported]**. Both simulation scenarios (Figure 1, selective sweep power curves where `FEL` has near-zero power while `DEPS` reaches $>80\%$ power) and empirical H3N2/H5N1 HA analyses (Tables 3–4, where known antigenic cluster-defining sweeps from Smith et al. 2004 and Shih et al. 2007 are detected by `DEPS` despite low or negatively selected $d_N / d_S$) convincingly validate this core thesis.
* **Claim:** *Several directionally evolving sites identified by `DEPS` in H5N1 and H3N2 HA correspond to functional N-linked glycosylation motif acquisitions (`N-X-S/T`) and experimentally verified monoclonal antibody escape mutations.* — **[Well Supported]**. Specifically grounded at H5N1 HA site 172 and H3N2 HA sites 151 and 264 (third position of the sequon acquiring S/T), as well as H3N2 HA sites 161, 171, and 174 (antigenic sites A/B and Smith et al. cluster-transition residues).
* **Claim:** *A positive `DEPS` result does not imply that the target residue is an ultimate evolutionary fixation target, and should not be used to extrapolate future viral evolution.* — **[Well Supported]**. The Discussion provides an admirably sober, population-genetically grounded caveat explaining how transient fitness landscapes, frequency-dependent selection, and seasonal reseeding produce fluctuating trajectories (verified on the larger $n = 1,054$ H5N1 and $n = 2,072$ H3N2 NCBI validation sets in Figure 4).

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Software & Method Availability:** The `DEPS` method is implemented in the open-source `HyPhy` package and hosted on the public `Datamonkey` web server (`www.hyphy.org` / `www.datamonkey.org`), with the custom empirical influenza amino acid substitution matrix (`Flu`) and full simulation/analysis scripts distributed alongside `HyPhy`.
* **Data Completeness:** Sequence alignments, inferred trees, and outgroup accessions for all 8 genomic segments of H5N1 and H3N2 are thoroughly documented in the Methods and supplementary archive.
