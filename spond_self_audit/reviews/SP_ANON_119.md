### 1. Executive Summary
`SP_ANON_119` generalizes the Multiclass Synonymous Substitution (`MSS`) codon modeling framework in HyPhy to infer synonymous substitution rate heterogeneity directly from coding sequence alignments without requiring a priori neutral reference partitions. The authors introduce `SynREV` (18 amino-acid-level synonymous rate classes), `SynREVCodon` (67 single-step synonymous codon-pair rate classes), a CHC genetic algorithm model selector (`MSS-CHC`), and a joint multigene maximum-likelihood estimator. Across 11,844 *Drosophila* gene alignments (~4.12 million single-nucleotide synonymous substitutions across 12 species) and 14,371 primate gene alignments, the study demonstrates pervasive codon-pair synonymous rate variation, validates `MSS-CHC` on non-reversible forward Wright–Fisher population genetic simulations, and shows that inferred `SynREVCodon` rates correlate significantly with independent *D. melanogaster* population polymorphism density ($\rho = 0.48, p = 0.002$) and modification-adjusted cognate tRNA abundance ratios ($\rho = -0.41, p = 0.005$). This is a thorough, statistically candid, and biologically well-grounded methodological contribution.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **[Well Supported] Rigorous validation under model misspecification via forward Wright–Fisher simulations:** Recognizing that time-reversible Markov codon models (`MG94`/`MSS`) violate the directional waiting-time asymmetry of mutation-selection-drift equilibrium when one synonymous codon is favored over another, the authors test `MSS-CHC` on forward Wright–Fisher simulations ($N = 500$ diploids, 11 sampled populations, 25 selected vs. 42 neutral codon pairs across $2Ns \in \{0, -0.5, -1, -1.5, -2, -5\}$). `MSS-CHC` achieves strong classification accuracy starting at weak selection ($2Ns = -1$: $\text{MCC} = 0.51, p = 4.02 \times 10^{-5}$, rising to $\text{MCC} = 0.97, p = 2.6 \times 10^{-17}$ at $2Ns = -5$). Crucially, the authors transparently report that under the forward-simulated null ($2Ns = 0$), `MSS-CHC` still finds a $\Delta\text{BIC} = 152.8$ improvement due to phenomenological loading of non-Markovian forward dynamics, whereas parametric null simulations under `MG94` correctly select the null in 22/25 replicates ($\Delta\text{BIC} \le 2$ in the remaining 3).
* **[Well Supported] Widespread codon-pair synonymous rate heterogeneity in *Drosophila*:** In gene-by-gene LRTs across 11,844 *Drosophila* alignments (Table 5), `MG94xREV` is rejected in favor of `SynREVCodon` in 72.2% of genes at Benjamini–Hochberg $\text{FDR } q = 0.10$ (68.3% at nominal $p = 0.05$; 13.7% under strict Holm–Bonferroni FWER), and `SynREV` is rejected in favor of `SynREVCodon` in 59.7% of genes at $q = 0.10$. Within multi-pair amino acids such as Leucine (9 single-step pairs), median relative rates span a nearly 4-fold range from $0.34$ (`CTC:CTG`) to $1.29$ (`CTA:CTT`), confirming that amino-acid-level grouping (`SynREV`) masks substantial codon-pair variance.
* **[Well Supported] Systematic upward bias in standard `MG94` $\omega$ ($d_N/d_S$) when synonymous rates vary:** Across 30,000 parametric simulation replicates (100 alignments $\times$ 3 generating models $\times$ 100 replicates; Table 8), fitting `MG94` when the true generating model is `SynREVCodon` produces a $+66.5\%$ upward relative bias in $\hat{\omega}$ (with severe bias in 61.62% of scenarios), whereas `SynREVCodon` exhibits a smaller downward finite-sample bias ($-10.7\%$, 0% severe bias). Empirically, accounting for codon-pair rate variation reduces gene-wide $\omega$ estimates by a factor of $0.68\times$ in *Drosophila* and $0.82\times$ in 14,371 primate genes.
* **[Well Supported] Hold-out cross-validation of joint multigene `MSS` models:** Jointly fitting `SynREV` and `SynREVCodon` across random subsamples of $K = 4$ to $2,048$ genes (5 replicates each; Table 6) shows that a shared `SynREVCodon` rate matrix consistently improves fit by $\sim 10$ AIC points per alignment for $K \ge 64$, providing a low-variance empirical codon-pair rate model (`Joint codon empirical`) that avoids single-gene zero-inflation.
* **Minor typographical inconsistencies in degrees-of-freedom notation:** Table 2 (line 46) and line 133 correctly state that `SynREVCodon` has $P - 1 = 66$ free synonymous rate parameters (67 codon pairs subject to the mean constraint $\sum_{i=1}^{67} \alpha_i / 67 = 1$). However, line 137 and the footnote to Table 7 (offset line 55) state that the LRT against `MG94` has "67 degrees of freedom" and refer to a "distribution of 68 codon-pair rates"; these should be corrected to 66 degrees of freedom and 67 codon-pair rates. Additionally, in Figure 8's caption (offset line 13), the zero-intercept R formula is printed as `lm(y ~ x + θ)` instead of `lm(y ~ x + 0)`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* The extrinsic biological validations are exceptionally well-designed. First, median interspecies `SynREVCodon` substitution rates across the 67 codon pairs correlate positively with intraspecies synonymous SNP densities across 197 Zambian *D. melanogaster* genomes ($r = 0.52$, Spearman $\rho = 0.48, p = 0.002$), directly confirming the population-genetic prediction that purifying selection simultaneously depresses within-population polymorphism and between-species divergence.
* Second, rather than naively correlating codon rates against raw tRNA gene copy numbers, the authors integrate empirical *D. melanogaster* tRNA-seq transcript counts (`GSE152621`) with biochemical wobble modifications (Inosine modification at wobble base A for 8 anticodons and Queuosine modification at wobble base G for His, Asp, Asn, and Tyr), demonstrating a significant negative correlation between `SynREVCodon` rates and the tRNA pool abundance ratio between exchanged codons (Spearman $\rho = -0.41, p = 0.005$).
* The Discussion maintains exemplary epistemic restraint, explicitly noting that time-variable selection, CpG/context-dependent mutation, and non-Markovian dynamics can load onto `MSS` rate parameters (citing Jones et al. 2018) and cautioning against attributing all inferred rate heterogeneity solely to translational selection.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Complete software and data availability:** The `SynREV`, `SynREVCodon`, joint fitter, and `MSS-GA.bf` genetic algorithm implementations are integrated into open-source HyPhy (v2.5.45–v2.5.62), the forward Wright–Fisher simulator is cited with code reference, and the 12-species *Drosophila* cactus alignments, codon usage tables, and GEO tRNA-seq accessions (`GSE152621`) are fully specified.
