### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript develops a statistical and machine learning framework for inferring and comparing "evolutionary fingerprints"—the bivariate site-to-site probability distribution of synonymous ($\alpha$) and nonsynonymous ($\beta$) substitution rates—across both homologous and nonhomologous protein-coding genes. First, the authors replace standard independent univariate rate distributions with a General Bivariate (GB) discrete distribution $GD_D(\alpha, \beta)$ with $D$ rate categories ($3D - 2$ free parameters), selected via a step-up grid search and $\text{AIC}_c$. Second, they define the Evolutionary Selection Distance (ESD) as the Monge-Kantorovich (Earth Mover's) distance between two bivariate rate distributions in shifted log-rate space ($\ln(\text{rate} + e^{-8})$). Third, to account for estimation variance across alignments of differing sizes and divergences, they approximate the posterior distribution of rate parameters via Sampling Importance Resampling (SIR) and compute posterior ESD distributions. Finally, they apply kernel PCA (KPCA), K-nearest neighbors (KNN), and genetic-algorithm-optimized kernel K-means (KKM) to 500 simulated alignments and ~400 empirical viral alignments from HIV-1 ($n=80$), HCV ($n=84$), and Influenza A virus ($n=221$), plus 10 benchmark alignments.

The methodological synthesis—combining flexible bivariate discrete random-effects codon models, optimal transport (Earth Mover's Distance), importance sampling, and kernel clustering—is mathematically sound and well-motivated. Simulations varying alignment size and divergence confirm 95.8% unsupervised KKM classification accuracy across 5 evolutionary scenarios. A few technical points warrant attention: the effective sample size used in $\text{AIC}_c$ ($s \approx \text{sites} \times \text{sequences}$), sensitivity of the ESD near zero rates to the arbitrary shift constant $\epsilon = e^{-8}$, fixing the RBF kernel bandwidth at $\sigma = 1$, and qualitative rather than permutation-tested evaluation of functional gene clustering in the viral datasets.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Efficiency of General Bivariate (GB) Discrete Rate Distributions [Well Supported]:** By allowing $(\alpha_k, \beta_k)$ pairs to be placed freely in $\mathbb{R}_+^2$ rather than on a Cartesian product grid of independent marginals, the GB model requires $3D - 2$ parameters for $D$ classes instead of $4D - 3$ parameters for $D^2$ grid classes. On 8 of 10 benchmark datasets (Table 2), the GB model achieves superior $\text{AIC}_c$ with fewer rate classes (e.g., $\Delta\text{AIC}_c = 157.4$ on Camelid VHH and $86.0$ on IAV HA), while reducing per-iteration likelihood evaluation cost proportionally to the reduction in rate classes.
* **Monge-Kantorovich / Earth Mover's Distance Metric and Log-Shift Sensitivity [Moderately Supported]:** Formulating ESD as the Earth Mover's Distance solves the problem of comparing discrete distributions with unequal numbers of support points ($D_1 \ne D_2$) via linear programming and satisfies the triangle inequality. However, the coordinate transformation $\tilde{x} = \ln(x + \epsilon)$ with $\epsilon = e^{-8} \approx 3.35 \times 10^{-4}$ means that boundary estimates of $\alpha_k = 0$ or $\beta_k = 0$ are mapped to $-8$. Consequently, the Euclidean ground distance $d_{ij}$ between a strictly conserved class ($\beta = 0$, mapped to $-8$) and a strongly conserved class ($\beta = 0.01$, mapped to $-4.6$) is 3.4 log-units—larger than the distance between neutral ($\beta = 1$, mapped to $0$) and strongly positively selected ($\beta = 10$, mapped to $2.3$) classes. A sensitivity check across $\epsilon \in \{10^{-2}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-8}\}$ would clarify how strongly boundary zero-rate classes dominate ESD values (such as the hepatitis D Ag outlier class noted in Figure 4).
* **$\text{AIC}_c$ Sample Size Definition and Step-Up Stopping Rule [Moderately Supported]:** In the step-up procedure for selecting $D$, the authors define the sample size $s$ in the small-sample $\text{AIC}_c$ penalty $2p(p+1)/(s - p - 1)$ as $s \approx \text{sites} \times \text{sequences}$. Because sequences in a phylogeny share common ancestry and the mixture weights $p_k$ are integrated at the site level ($S$ sites), the effective sample size for estimating the site-level mixing distribution is bounded by the number of codon sites $S$, not $N \times S$. Using $N \times S$ makes the finite-sample correction negligible even on short alignments, though the standard $2p$ AIC penalty still prevents severe overfitting in the simulations (74–92% exact recovery of $D$).
* **Propagation of Uncertainty via SIR [Well Supported]:** Conditioning on nuisance parameters $\Theta_R$ (branch lengths and nucleotide biases) and applying SIR to sample $M$ draws from the posterior of $\Theta_I = \{(\alpha_k, \beta_k, p_k)\}$ effectively stabilizes ESD comparisons across subsampled IAV HA alignments ranging from $N=10$ to $N=349$ sequences (Figure 2).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Simulation Validation Across Varying Alignment Sizes and Divergences [Well Supported]:** By randomizing sequence count, codon length, and branch length scaling across replicates in Simulations 1–3, the authors provide a stringent test that ESD clusters alignments by their underlying selective regime (95.8% unsupervised KKM accuracy; 98% 1-NN accuracy) rather than by alignment size or tree length.
* **Functional Clustering of Viral Genes in HIV-1, HCV, and IAV [Moderately Supported]:** In Figure 5, representing each viral gene by its kernel-space centroid across multiple subtype/cohort alignments shows biologically sensible groupings (e.g., polymerases/replication complexes vs. surface envelope glycoproteins in HIV, HCV, and IAV). However, this functional clustering in Figure 5 is evaluated visually from NJ trees and 2D KPCA projections without a formal hypothesis test (such as an ANOSIM/PERMANOVA $p$-value or silhouette score testing pre-defined functional categories against random gene label permutations).
* **Concordance of Subtype ESD and Genetic Distance in HCV vs. Discordance in HIV-1 RT [Well Supported]:** In Figure 6, the authors apply an exact Mantel test comparing inter-subtype ESD matrices with TN93 genetic distance matrices, finding significant concordance across 6 HCV E1 subtypes ($P = 0.045$, matching unrooted tree topologies) and no correlation across 7 HIV-1 RT subtypes ($P = 0.28$), consistent with known biological differences among HCV genotypes vs. uniform functional constraints on drug-naive HIV-1 RT.
