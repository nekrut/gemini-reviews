## 1. Executive Summary
`SP_ANON_100` investigates a fundamental question in HIV-1 cure and reservoir biology: whether defective proviruses (both non-hypermutated and APOBEC3G/F-hypermutated) enter the long-lived resting $\text{CD4}^+$ T-cell reservoir at the same time relative to antiretroviral therapy (ART) initiation as inducible, replication-competent outgrowth viruses (OGVs), and what host immunologic or viral phenotypic factors govern reservoir seeding dynamics. Building on their earlier longitudinal cohort study of women in the South African CAPRISA 002 subtype C cohort followed from acute/primary infection through years of suppressive ART, the authors combine quantitative viral outgrowth assays (QVOA), template-limiting dilution PacBio SMRT long-read sequencing of $3'$ half-genome proviral DNA ($\sim 5\text{ kb}$, HXB2 4653–9632), longitudinal pre-ART plasma RNA deep sequencing (MiSeq with Primer ID across 5 genomic regions), and in vitro TZM-bl phenotypic entry assays (Maraviroc/AMD3100 coreceptor tropism and soluble CD4 sensitivity).

Across 18 participants (9 previously studied for OGVs plus 9 newly characterized participants selected to span extremes of early vs. late reservoir seeding), the authors establish four important biological findings: (1) overall, replication-competent OGVs, non-hypermutated defective proviral DNA, and APOBEC-hypermutated proviral DNA exhibit concordant timing of reservoir entry ($\sim 60\%$ seeded within the final year before ART initiation across $n = 18$ participants); (2) a lower pre-ART nadir $\text{CD4}^+$ T-cell count is significantly associated with a higher proportion of long-lived viral sequences dating to the final year before ART initiation ($r = -0.60, p = 0.0085$, validated via leave-one-out linear modeling), whereas pre-ART set-point viral load shows no association; (3) the first year of untreated acute/early infection is the *least* active period of stable entry into the long-lived reservoir ($6\%$ observed vs. $25\%$ expected under uniform entry); and (4) reservoir viruses are uniformly T-cell tropic (resistant to low-dose soluble CD4) and preserve the CCR5/CXCR4 coreceptor ratio circulating at ART initiation.

The integration of longitudinal Primer ID plasma RNA standards, single-molecule PacBio proviral sequencing, phylogenetic dating, and wet-lab phenotypic entry assays is of high experimental and biological quality. Two methodological subtleties warrant careful note: (1) APOBEC-hypermutated proviruses required computational masking (`G->A` dinucleotide hypermutated sites converted to `N`), which reduces informative phylogenetic signal and increases dating uncertainty relative to intact OGVs; and (2) collapsing distinct PacBio barcodes with $\le 5$ nucleotide differences across a $5\text{ kb}$ amplicon into a single "clonal" consensus sequence could merge distinct closely related late-stage variants seeded during rapid pre-ART viral expansion.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit

### Strengths in Experimental Design and Quantitative Analysis
1. **Triangulated Phylogenetic Dating Across Multiple Subgenomic Regions and Estimators:**
   To date when each on-ART proviral or OGV sequence last circulated in pre-ART plasma, the authors combine up to 4–5 genomic amplicons (`ENV2`, `ENV3`, `ENV4`, `NEF`, and `GAG1` for OGVs) $\times$ 3 distinct phylogenetic/distance estimators (genetic distance, clade support, and phylogenetic placement onto longitudinal Primer ID plasma RNA trees), taking a weighted median across up to 12–15 estimates per sequence. This multi-locus ensemble substantially buffers against focal recombination or single-gene rate noise.
2. **Rigorous In Vitro Phenotypic Validation of Macrophage vs. T-Cell Tropism and Coreceptor Usage:**
   Rather than relying solely on genotypic V3-loop algorithms (`Geno2pheno`), the authors directly assay 105 replication-competent OGVs on TZM-bl cells in triplicate using soluble CD4 ($0, 2, 20\ \mu\text{g/mL}$) and competitive entry inhibitors (Maraviroc and AMD3100 at $20\text{ nM}$ and $2000\text{ nM}$) alongside cloned subtype C macrophage-tropic (`M-tropic`), `R5 T-tropic`, and `X4` pseudovirus controls.
3. **Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation and Model Selection for Clinical Predictors:**
   Given the modest clinical cohort size ($n = 18$ women), evaluating candidate predictors of late reservoir seeding (nadir CD4, CD4 at ART initiation, pre-ART viral load, duration of untreated infection) via all-subsets linear regression with $\text{AICc}$ and leave-one-out cross-validation ($R^2 = 0.25$ for nadir CD4 alone) avoids multi-collinear overfitting.

### Methodological & Statistical Vulnerabilities
1. **Differential Information Content Between OGVs (5 Regions, Unmasked) and Hypermutated Proviruses (4 Regions, `N`-Masked):**
   - *Technical critique:* In Materials and Methods (lines 27–29), OGV sequences were dated using 5 genomic regions (`GAG1`, `ENV2`, `ENV3`, `ENV4`, `NEF`), whereas proviral DNA sequences were dated using only the 4 regions in the $3'$ half-genome (`ENV2`, `ENV3`, `ENV4`, `NEF`, lacking `GAG1`). Furthermore, for APOBEC-hypermutated proviruses (identified by `Hypermut 2.0`), all hypermutated positions were masked to `N` using a custom script prior to phylogenetic placement. Because `G->A` hypermutation targets `GG` and `GA` dinucleotides that also overlap legitimate reverse-transcriptase evolutionary substitutions in `env` and `nef`, masking all putative hypermutation sites strips informative apomorphies and shortens the effective sequence length.
   - *Impact:* In phylogenetic placement and minimum-distance dating, replacing derived `A` alleles with ambiguous `N` characters pulls masked sequences toward internal ancestral nodes or increases branch placement variance. This masking effect likely contributes to the larger apparent dating dispersion observed among hypermutated proviruses in several participants (`CAP257`, `CAP316`, `CAP336` in Figure 1B) and should be considered when interpreting within-host heterogeneity between hypermutated and intact sequences.
2. **Clonal Collapsing Threshold ($\le 5$ Nucleotide Differences Across $\sim 5\text{ kb}$):**
   - *Technical critique:* In Materials and Methods (line 27), sequences bearing *different* PacBio barcodes that clustered closely on the tree and differed by $\le 5$ nucleotides across the $3'$ half-genome ($\sim 4,980\text{ bp}$, i.e., $\le 0.1\%$ divergence) were deemed "suspected clonal sequences" and collapsed into a single consensus sequence prior to downstream dating analysis. While collapsing identical sequences (0–1 nt difference attributable to PacBio CCS/PCR error) appropriately prevents a single massively expanded T-cell clone from dominating the participant-level median, two genuinely distinct infection events occurring within days/weeks of each other right before ART initiation from the same dominant plasma lineage can easily differ by 2 to 5 nucleotides across $5\text{ kb}$.
   - *Impact:* Collapsing distinct barcodes differing by up to 5 nucleotides may selectively deduplicate closely related variants seeded during the final weeks prior to ART, slightly underestimating the true density of late-seeded variants in participants with low pre-ART diversity.
3. **Two-Phase Cohort Selection Strategy ($n = 9$ Original $+ n = 9$ Targeted Extremes):**
   - *Technical critique:* The first 9 participants (`Group 1`) were sampled from an earlier study where $\ge 71\%$ of OGVs dated to the last year before ART, whereas the second 9 participants (`Group 2`) were specifically chosen from a screening cohort of 14 women based on env-C2C3 amplicon screening to enrich for extreme phenotypes (3 with early seeding, 4 with late seeding, 2 intermediate).
   - *Impact:* While combining `Group 1` and `Group 2` ($n = 18$) expands the dynamic range of the response variable (proportion of sequences seeded within 1 year of ART) and increases statistical power for regression against nadir CD4 (Figure 3A), outcome-dependent sub-sampling (selecting 7 of 9 `Group 2` participants from the tails of the preliminary C2C3 dating distribution) can slightly inflate the Pearson correlation coefficient $r = -0.60$ relative to a simple random sample. The authors transparently report `Group 1`, `Group 2`, and the combined 23-participant screening dataset (`S2 Fig`, $r = -0.45, p = 0.03$), which confirms that the negative association with nadir CD4 holds even without tail enrichment.

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim:** *Overall across the cohort, inducible replication-competent OGVs, non-hypermutated defective proviruses, and APOBEC-hypermutated proviruses enter the long-lived reservoir with similar timing ($\sim 60\%$ seeded in the final year before ART).* — **[Well Supported]**. Demonstrated by the paired linear mixed-effects models across $n = 18$ participants showing no systematic cohort-wide directional offset among the three viral categories ($p \ge 0.25$).
* **Claim:** *Lower pre-ART nadir $\text{CD4}^+$ T-cell count correlates with a higher fraction of reservoir viruses seeded near the time of ART initiation.* — **[Well Supported]**. Supported across both the deeply sequenced 18-participant cohort ($r = -0.60, p = 0.0085$, Figure 3A) and the full 23-participant C2C3 screening cohort ($r = -0.45, p = 0.03$, S2 Fig), with biologically cogent mechanistic discussion of CD4+ T-cell turnover and post-ART homeostatic proliferation.
* **Claim:** *The first year of untreated HIV-1 infection contributes disproportionately few sequences to the long-lived on-ART reservoir ($6\%$ observed vs. $25\%$ expected under uniform seeding).* — **[Well Supported]**. Quantitatively demonstrated across all 18 participants monitored since acute infection (Figure 2).
* **Claim:** *Long-lived reservoir viruses are exclusively T-cell tropic (requiring high CD4 density) and reflect the CCR5/CXCR4 coreceptor ratio circulating immediately prior to ART.* — **[Well Supported]**. Directly grounded in triplicate soluble CD4 and Maraviroc/AMD3100 inhibition assays on 105 OGVs plus deep genotypic tracking in the 4 participants harboring CXCR4-using variants (Figure 4).
