### 1. Executive Summary
This methodological study introduces two phylogenetic models of episodic directional selection—`MEDS` (a site-wise fixed-effects codon model extending `MG94xREV` + `IFEL`) and `EDEPS` (a random-effects empirical Bayes protein model extending `DEPS` with an `HIV-Between` baseline matrix)—designed to identify specific sites and target amino acid residues experiencing accelerated substitution along *a priori* designated foreground branches (e.g., antiretroviral-treated HIV-1 lineages) relative to background (treatment-naive) branches. To isolate the contribution of the directional parameter ($\psi$ modulating transitions to target amino acid $T$ on foreground branches) from episodic diversifying selection ($\beta_F > \alpha_s$) and constant tree-wide directional selection, the authors benchmark `MEDS` and `EDEPS` against `FEEDS` (the nested $\psi = 1$ episodic diversifying codon model) and `DEPS` (tree-wide directional protein model) across three empirical Stanford HIVdb datasets (476 paired pre/post-HAART Reverse Transcriptase sequences, 122 subtype C Protease sequences, and 295 Raltegravir Integrase sequences, validated against IAS-USA/HIVdb drug resistance associated mutations [DRAMs]) as well as extensive 64-taxon simulations (varying foreground branch counts, $\psi \in [2, 1000]$, dual targets, and Dirichlet site-specific amino acid frequency profiles). Across the three HIV-1 datasets, `MEDS` and `EDEPS` each identify 24 true DRAMs with high specificity, outperforming `FEEDS` (14 DRAMs) and avoiding the high false-positive rate of non-episodic `DEPS` (which flags 46 RT and 39 IN sites driven by background immune/host selection).

The model design is well-suited to the biology of drug resistance evolution, where selective sweeps target 1–2 specific resistance residues on treated branches while maintaining purifying selection against other amino acid replacements. The simulation suite thoroughly tests key model misspecifications—most notably proving that site-specific Dirichlet equilibrium amino acid frequencies ($\text{Dir}(\xi)$ down to $\xi = 0.005$) do not inflate false positives in `MEDS` despite its global `CF3x4` baseline frequency parameterization.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Clean nesting of episodic directional (`MEDS`) vs. episodic diversifying (`FEEDS`) models ([Well Supported]):** Because `FEEDS` ($\alpha_s, \beta_B, \beta_F$ free per site; $\psi = 1$) serves as the exact 3-parameter site-wise null model for `MEDS` ($\psi$ free for target residue $T$, 1 df LRT with 20-test Bonferroni correction across amino acids), any gain in detection power or log-likelihood ($\text{LRT}$ favors `MEDS` over `FEEDS` at 24 of 29 resistance sites) is directly attributable to directional targeting rather than unmodeled synonymous rate variation or background diversifying selection.
* **Rigorous stress-testing against site-specific amino acid profile misspecification ([Well Supported]):** A classic vulnerability of codon directional models using alignment-wide nucleotide equilibrium frequencies ($\pi_{CF3\times 4}$) is that strong site-specific purifying constraint favoring a single wild-type amino acid might be mistaken for directional positive selection. By simulating neutral evolution under a Goldman-Yang model with site-specific amino acid frequencies drawn from $\text{Symmetric Dirichlet}(\xi)$ across $\xi \in \{0.005, 0.05, 0.5, 5\}$ (Table 8), the authors show that `MEDS` remains strictly conservative across all regimes (FPR $\le 0.0375$ at nominal $p = 0.05$).
* **Uniform codon exchangeability caveat in `MEDS` vs. `EDEPS` ([Moderately Supported]):** As the authors astutely note in the Results (line 136) and Discussion (line 260), `MEDS` assumes a single baseline nonsynonymous rate $\beta$ across all amino acid pairs (modulated only by GTR nucleotide biases $\theta_{ij}$). Consequently, high-exchangeability radical/conservative transitions in HIV-1 (such as $D \leftrightarrow E$ at Protease sites 60E and 61E) can be absorbed into $\psi > 1$ by `MEDS` but are correctly filtered out by `EDEPS`, which uses the empirical `HIV-Between` amino acid exchangeability matrix.
* **Heuristic Hamming/HKY85 nearest-neighbor background matching in Protease and Integrase ([Moderately Supported]):** While the RT dataset used matched pre- and post-treatment isolates from the same 241 patients, the Protease and Integrase datasets lacked pre-treatment pairs and therefore selected the 2 (Protease) or 25 (Integrase) closest treatment-naive sequences from HIVdb by genetic distance (after masking major resistance sites in Protease, though not explicitly stated for Integrase). When unmatched naive database sequences are attached to post-treatment tips, internal vs. terminal branch lengths can be long, meaning part of the terminal "foreground" branch occurred prior to therapy initiation (which, as the authors note on line 65, dilutes foreground signal and makes the test conservative, or occasionally admits background subtype polymorphisms if naive controls are imperfectly matched).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Superiority of episodic directional over diversifying or constant directional models for drug resistance ([Well Supported]):** Comparing `MEDS`, `EDEPS`, `FEEDS`, and `DEPS` against experimentally validated IAS-USA/HIVdb DRAM ground truth across all three viral enzymes (RT, PR, IN) convincingly demonstrates that drug resistance evolution is both episodic (confined to treated lineages) and directional (converging on specific escape residues such as RT `M184V`, `K103N`, `T215F/Y`; PR `I84V`, `L90M`; IN `Q148H`, `N155H`, `Y143R`).
* **Detection of bidirectional wild-type/resistance toggling and polymorphic accessory selection (`93L`) ([Well Supported]):** The authors provide insightful biological interpretations of two non-obvious model behaviors: (1) detecting directional selection toward *both* the resistance residue and the wild-type residue at RT sites 151, 188, and 215 (reflecting partial resistance evolution or reversion/toggling on branches with suboptimal adherence or regimen switches), and (2) detecting directional selection toward the subtype C consensus residue `93L` in Protease when `93I` naive variants convert to `93L` under PI therapy.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Open code and benchmark alignments:** `MEDS`, `FEEDS`, and `EDEPS` are implemented in the `HyPhy` distribution (v2.0020101225+) and documented with test datasets on the `HyPhy` wiki.
* **Text extraction note:** In the markdown rendering of the manuscript, inline mathematical symbols (such as $\alpha, \beta_F, \beta_B, \psi, \xi$, and site numbers in the first column of Tables 2–4) were stripped by the XML/HTML-to-text converter, leaving blank spaces where math objects were embedded as images/MathML; this is an artifact of the text extraction pipeline rather than the original manuscript.
