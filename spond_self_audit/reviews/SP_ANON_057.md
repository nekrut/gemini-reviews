## 1. Executive Summary
This comparative genomic study searches for signatures of adaptive convergence across four human-infecting betacoronaviruses spanning two deeply divergent subgenera—*Embecovirus* (`HKU1`, `OC43`) and *Sarbecovirus* (`SARS-CoV-1`, `SARS-CoV-2`). Using a concatenated `Orf1ab + S` codon alignment ($N=1,314$ initial and $N=1,455$ expanded genomes), the authors reconstruct maximum-likelihood ancestral amino acid states (`TreeTime`) and screen 6,681 variable positions with a custom tree-traversal algorithm to identify 30 shared nonsynonymous sites exhibiting putative "homoplasy" or "stepwise evolution." Crossing these 30 sites with `HyPhy` selection scans (`MEME`, `FEL`, `Contrast-FEL`) and 3D protein structures (`PyMOL`, `PISAebi`), they highlight four candidate adaptive convergence sites (`18121` [nsp14], `21623` [Spike NTD], `21635` [Spike NTD], and `23948` [Spike S2]). While the overarching question—whether endemic human embecoviruses preview the long-term evolutionary trajectory of SARS-CoV-2—is biologically interesting, the cross-subgenus inference in Spike suffers from severe positional homology breakdown and synonymous saturation between *Embecovirus* and *Sarbecovirus*, unadjusted nominal $p$-values ($p \approx 0.022\text{--}0.048$), internal coordinate and lineage-naming errors in the text, and contradicting selection directions within SARS-CoV-2.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Positional Homology Breakdown in the Hypervariable Spike S1 Subunit Between *Embecovirus* and *Sarbecovirus* [Methodologically Flawed]:** `HKU1`/`OC43` (*Embecovirus*) and `SARS-CoV-1`/`SARS-CoV-2` (*Sarbecovirus*) diverged deeply, utilize unrelated host receptors via different domains (`S1A`/NTD binding sialoglycans in embecoviruses vs. `S1B`/RBD binding ACE2 in sarbecoviruses), and exhibit extreme sequence divergence and indels across `S1` (as the authors note on line 31, `0%` of sites in `S1B` are conserved across the four species). Two of the manuscript's four headline sites—`21623` (`Spike residue 21`) and `21635` (`Spike residue 25`)—reside in the hypervariable extreme N-terminal loop of `S1A` where primary sequence homology between `OC43`/`HKU1` and `SARS-CoV-2` is non-existent and gap placement in profile-to-profile alignment is arbitrary. Even at the more conserved `S2` site `23948` (`Spike residue 796`), the authors admit (line 86 and Table 1) that adding sequences in the expanded alignment flipped the inferred amino acid states across entire clades due to *"alignment uncertainty across genome regions of highly divergent virus taxa."* Treating alignment-dependent coincidences of amino acid letters in non-homologous N-terminal loops across subgenera as "convergent homoplasy" is a classic cross-family alignment artifact.
* **Fitting Codon $d_N/d_S$ Models Across Deeply Divergent, Synonymously Saturated Inter-Subgenus Branches [Fragile]:** The authors run `MEME`, `FEL`, and `Contrast-FEL` on the global `Orf1ab + S` alignment spanning both subgenera (partitioned into 14 `GARD` fragments) and report positive selection for `21623` (`MEME p = 0.047`) and `21635` (`MEME p = 0.048`) along the deep ancestral branches leading to the `HKU1`, `OC43`, and `SARS-CoV-2` clades (Table 1, line 92). Between *Embecovirus* and *Sarbecovirus* (and between `HKU1` and `OC43`), synonymous substitutions ($d_S$) are heavily saturated, violating the assumptions of codon likelihood models on deep inter-clade branches. Furthermore, all selection $p$-values reported in Table 1 (`0.022, 0.035, 0.047, 0.048, 0.044, 0.031`) are unadjusted nominal $p$-values barely below $0.05$ across `8,962` aligned codons (`6,681` variable sites), none of which would survive false discovery rate correction.
* **Inadequate Null Simulation Model (`AliSim` LG Without Indels or Selection Heterogeneity) [Moderately Supported]:** To argue that the 30 homoplasic/stepwise sites exceed neutral drift expectations, the authors simulate three amino acid alignments in `AliSim` under an empirical `LG` amino acid matrix on the fixed global tree (chunk 2, line 13). However, a uniform `LG` model does not account for site-specific amino acid repertoire restrictions (where only 2–3 residues are biophysically tolerated at a site across divergent clades, guaranteeing frequent homoplasy under pure purifying selection) or alignment indel errors.
* **Internal Coordinate Discrepancies and Factual Reversal of Delta vs. Omicron Lineages [Fragile]:**
  1. **Coordinate mismatches:** In the Abstract (line 6) and Table 1 (line 46), site `18121` is listed as `nsp14/residue 28`, whereas in the Introduction (line 18) it is listed as `[nsp14/27]`. Similarly, in the Abstract (line 6), Table 1 (lines 54–55), and Figure 5 (line 104), sites `21623` and `21635` are identified as Spike residues `21` and `25` (`Arg 21` and `Pro 25`), whereas in the Results text (line 102) they are described as *"mapping to residue 29 within the S1A domain"* and *"mapping to residue 33 in S1A"*. And on line 100, the figure caption states *"The side chain of Ser 25 and Thr 25 are indicated"* instead of `Ser 28` and `Thr 25`.
  2. **Delta vs. Omicron reversal at Spike 796 (`23948`):** On line 88, the Results text states: *"following emergence and global spread of the B.1.1.529 virus lineage (Omicron variant of concern [VOC] and descending sublineages), amino acid state 'Y' replaced amino acid state 'D,' displaying a predominant trend associated with the dominance of the B.1.617.2 lineage (Delta VOC and descending sublineages)"*—erroneously associating the `Y` replacement with Delta (`B.1.617.2`) dominance, before stating the opposite in the Discussion (line 115: `D` in Delta replaced by `Y` in Omicron).

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Discordance Between Claimed Adaptive Convergence and Observed Selection Within SARS-CoV-2 [Overstated]:** Among the four headline sites promoted as predictive markers of SARS-CoV-2 adaptation to human endemicity (`18121, 21623, 21635, 23948`), Table 1 shows that `18121` (`nsp14/28`) is completely invariant (`S`) in SARS-CoV-2 with no within-species selection signal, while `21623` (`S/21`) and `21635` (`S/25`) are both under **pervasive negative (purifying) selection (`NSS`)** in SARS-CoV-2 (`R` and `P` are the ancestral Wuhan-Hu-1 consensus states!). Only `23948` (`Spike D796Y`, an Omicron-defining mutation near the `S2'` cleavage loop) actually underwent positive selection (`PSS`) and lineage fixation in SARS-CoV-2. Claiming that `S/21` and `S/25` demonstrate predictive convergent evolution in SARS-CoV-2 because Wuhan-Hu-1 already carried `R21` and `P25` at baseline overstates the evidence.
