# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_079
**Composite Score:** 6.5 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.1/3.5 | Claims: 1.6/2.5 | Biology: 1.3/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.5/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Complete Risk-Group Confounding (San Diego MSM vs. Tijuana IDU/FSW) & Severe Sample Size Imbalance ($n=646$ vs. $n=27$)
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

## 1. Executive Summary
This study applies phylogeographic and population-genetic methods to assess the degree of viral mixing between the HIV-1 subtype B epidemics in San Diego, California ($n = 646$ subtype B *pol* sequences from 395 primary and 267 chronic infection patients) and Mexico ($n = 127$ subtype B *pol* sequences, comprising $n = 100$ publicly available GenBank sequences from 2003–2005 and $n = 27$ newly generated sequences from injection drug user [IDU] and female sex worker [FSW] cohorts in Tijuana from 2004–2006). Analyzing a 453-nt *pol* alignment in HyPhy using $F_{\text{ST}}$ (under both nucleotide TN93 and codon-based synonymous MG94xREV distances), the Slatkin–Maddison cladistic migration test on a PhyML tree, and synonymous genetic distance clustering ($< 0.5\%$), the authors report that while San Diego and Mexico as a whole are not significantly differentiated by synonymous $F_{\text{ST}}$ ($F_{\text{ST}} = 0.01, p = 0.62$), the San Diego ($n = 646$) and Tijuana ($n = 27$) sequences show statistically significant differentiation ($F_{\text{ST}} = 0.08, p = 0.02$; 14 Slatkin–Maddison migrations, $p < 0.01$; only 2 of 72 clusters containing both Mexican and San Diego sequences, $p < 0.01$). They conclude in the Abstract that *"the HIV-1 epidemics in San Diego, California and Tijuana, Mexico are distinct."*

The authors' decision to use maximum-likelihood synonymous codon distances (`MG94xREV`) to eliminate spurious population structure driven by convergent drug-resistance and CTL selection is a clear methodological strength—and in fact exposes why nucleotide TN93 $F_{\text{ST}}$ gave a false-positive signal ($F_{\text{ST}} = 0.07, p < 0.01$) when 42 Mexican GenBank sequences with unknown ART exposure were included. However, the study's headline epidemiological conclusion regarding cross-border separation between San Diego and Tijuana is undermined by a fundamental sampling design flaw: **complete confounding between geography and transmission risk group, coupled with an extreme 24-to-1 sample size imbalance ($n = 646$ vs. $n = 27$).**

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit

### 2.1 Complete Confounding of Geographic Location with Transmission Risk Group
- As the authors acknowledge late in the Discussion (line 95), *"the bulk of the sequences from San Diego in this study came from MSM, whereas the bulk of the sequences from Tijuana came from cohorts of FSW and IDUs."* Specifically, all $n = 27$ Tijuana sequences were collected exclusively from FSW and IDU cohorts, with **zero MSM sampled in Tijuana**.
- It is well established in HIV-1 molecular epidemiology that even within a single city, MSM sexual networks and IDU/heterosexual networks often segregate into distinct phylogenetic sub-epidemics with elevated $F_{\text{ST}}$ and low cluster overlap.
- Because geographic origin (San Diego vs. Tijuana) is nearly 100% collinear with risk group (MSM vs. FSW/IDU) in the study design, attributing the observed $F_{\text{ST}} = 0.08$ ($p = 0.02$) and lack of clustering to the international border ("the HIV-1 epidemics in San Diego, California and Tijuana, Mexico are distinct") rather than to within-region risk-group segregation is statistically unidentifiable.

### 2.2 Severe Sample Size Imbalance ($n = 646$ vs. $n = 27$) and Interpretation of Slatkin–Maddison Counts
- For the focal San Diego vs. Tijuana comparison, the sample sizes are $n = 646$ and $n = 27$ (a $24:1$ ratio).
- In the Slatkin–Maddison parsimony test on a tree with 646 tips of state A and 27 tips of state B, the maximum possible number of migration steps is bounded above by $\min(n_A, n_B) = 27$ (which occurs when every single Tijuana tip is an isolated singleton nested inside San Diego clades). Observing **14 inferred migrations out of a theoretical maximum of 27** indicates that more than half of the Tijuana lineages trace independently into the broader tree, with theremaining 13 clustering in small local FSW/IDU pairs or triples!
- Similarly, in the Mexico ($n = 127$) vs. San Diego ($n = 646$) comparison, observing **84 inferred migrations out of a theoretical maximum of 127** shows extensive historical lineage interdigitation. While a random tip-permutation test rejects complete panmixia ($p < 0.01$, because even 2–3 local pairs among the 27 Tijuana IDU/FSW samples lowers the parsimony count below the panmictic expectation of ~26), interpreting $14 / 27$ or $84 / 127$ migrations as evidence that the two cities' epidemics are "distinct" and "not mixing" conflates rejection of the extreme null hypothesis of 100% panmixia with absence of substantial cross-border gene flow.

### 2.3 Inclusion of 42 Non-Naive Mexican GenBank Sequences and Short Alignment Length (453 nt)
- Only 453 nucleotides of *pol* were aligned across the combined cohort, limiting phylogenetic resolution for deep branches in PhyML (which Slatkin–Maddison treats as fixed without integrating over topological uncertainty) and restricting the number of synonymous sites (~110 synonymous sites per sequence, where a $<0.5\%$ synonymous distance cutoff corresponds to $\le 0.5$ synonymous substitutions!).
- Furthermore, 42 of the 100 Mexican GenBank sequences lacked confirmation of ART-naive status, whereas all San Diego and Tijuana cohort sequences were ART-naive (with 12.4% NRTI and 12.3% NNRTI TDR in San Diego vs. 0% in Tijuana). This difference in drug-resistance mutation prevalence directly explains why nucleotide TN93 $F_{\text{ST}}$ was significant ($0.07, p < 0.01$) while synonymous $F_{\text{ST}}$ was completely null ($0.01, p = 0.62$) for the San Diego vs. Mexico comparison.

### 2.4 Minor Numerical Inconsistency in Sample Size Reporting
- **Line 76 vs. Line 85:** Line 76 states that 16 of the 789 sequences (all from San Diego) were non-subtype B and excluded, leaving $773$ subtype B sequences ($646$ San Diego + $127$ Mexico, as correctly stated on line 85). However, two sentences later on line 76, the text states: *"When only the sequences from San Diego (662) and Tijuana (27) were analyzed..."*—inadvertently citing the pre-exclusion San Diego total ($395 + 267 = 662$) instead of the subtype B total ($646$).

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality

### Claim Audit Summary
- **Claim 1:** *"Analysis of this very large dataset of HIV-1 sequences suggested that the HIV-1 epidemics in San Diego, California and Tijuana, Mexico are distinct... the social networks of the majority of HIV-positive individuals in San Diego are not mixing with the social networks of HIV-positive individuals in Tijuana."* — **[Overstated]**: Because only $n = 27$ individuals from Tijuana were sequenced—all from FSW and IDU cohorts, whereas the $n = 646$ San Diego cohort was predominantly MSM—the study cannot distinguish cross-border geographic separation from MSM vs. FSW/IDU risk-network separation.
- **Claim 2:** *"When using codon-based synonymous distances, the Mexico vs. San Diego comparison was not significant ($F_{ST} = 0.01, p = 0.62$), whereas nucleotide TN93 distance was ($F_{ST} = 0.07, p < 0.01$)."* — **[Well Supported]**: A valuable methodological demonstration that nucleotide-based $F_{\text{ST}}$ in HIV-1 *pol* can be confounded by nonsynonymous convergent selection (such as ART resistance mutations).
- **Claim 3:** *"A conservative cluster analysis (synonymous genetic distance < 0.5%) identified 72 clusters with >= 2 sequences, with only two containing both Mexican and San Diego sequences."* — **[Moderately Supported]**: Computation is valid on the observed sample, but given the $<0.5\%$ synonymous cutoff on a 453-nt fragment (~0.5 synonymous substitutions) and the absence of Tijuana MSM sequences, low cross-border cluster overlap is expected by design.

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Exemplary Data Availability:** The newly generated Tijuana sequences are deposited in GenBank (`GU304608–GU304632`), and the full NEXUS alignment and PhyML tree are publicly hosted (`http://www.hyphy.org/pubs/Mehta2009/`), with all phylogeographic tests (`F_ST`, Slatkin–Maddison, MG94xREV clustering) implemented in the open-source HyPhy package.
