# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_005
**Composite Score:** 5.8 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.6/3.5 | Claims: 1.4/2.5 | Biology: 1.4/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.4/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Model Misspecification (Independent Single-Frame Codon Models on Overlapping Reading Frames)
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Basic/Mechanistic Discovery

### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript combines an evolutionary rate analysis of the mammalian *INK4a/ARF* dual-coding tumor suppressor locus across seven species (plus 8,467 human–mouse one-to-one orthologs) with a genome-wide bioinformatic screen of human and mouse splicing graphs (`AltSplice`/`ASD`), identifying 90 putative conserved dual-coding exons (`cDCEs`). While the genome-wide comparative splicing-graph filter and randomization benchmark for frameshift tolerance provide a useful catalog of candidate dual-coding exons, the molecular evolutionary analysis of overlapping reading frames is compromised by a fundamental methodological flaw: fitting standard single-frame `MG94xHKY85` codon models independently to out-of-phase overlapping reading frames (`INK4a/2` and `ARF/2`) where synonymous sites in one frame are nonsynonymous in the other.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Invalid Application of Independent Single-Frame Codon Models to Overlapping Reading Frames:** In exon 2 of *CDKN2A*, the `INK4a` and `ARF` reading frames overlap in shifted reading frames for 317 bp (`INK4a/2` and `ARF/2`). In an out-of-phase dual-coding region, third-position (synonymous) sites in one reading frame correspond to first- or second-position (predominantly nonsynonymous) sites in the alternate reading frame. Despite acknowledging in the Discussion that "current codon models cannot take into account simultaneous evolution in two reading frames" (line 103), the authors fit standard single-frame `MG94xHKY85` models separately to `INK4a/2` and `ARF/2` in `HyPhy` (`mol.evol`, Fig. 2 and Table 1), reporting $\omega = 7.54$ ($d_S = 0.08, d_N = 0.41$) in `ARF/2` and $d_S = 1.15, d_N = 0.56$ ($\omega = 0.52$) in `INK4a/2`. Because purifying selection on amino acid replacements in `INK4a/2` suppresses third-position substitutions in `ARF/2` ($d_S = 0.08$), while synonymous third-position substitutions in `INK4a/2` ($d_S = 1.15$) manifest directly as nonsynonymous replacements in `ARF/2` ($d_N = 0.41$), the inflated $\omega = 7.54$ in `ARF/2` is a mathematical artifact of cross-frame constraint rather than Darwinian diversifying selection. Context-dependent overlapping reading frame Markov models (e.g., Pedersen & Jensen 2001; Hein & Støvlbæk 1995) are required to disentangle frame-specific selection.
* **Frame-Numbering Inconsistency in Text:** On line 56, the manuscript states that when `INK4a` is designated as frame `+1`, `ARF` is translated in frame `+2`, and on the next sentence states that the alternate reading frame in opossum `INK4b` is in the "`+3` frame (similar to `ARF`)". Because `+2` and `+3` are distinct reading frames, this inconsistency obscures whether the opossum `INK4b` open reading frame is homologous in phase to placental `ARF`.
* **Genome-Wide `cDCE` Discovery and Randomization Controls:** The comparative splicing-graph screen across human and mouse (`AltSplice` build 2, requiring $>80\%$ BlastN identity, $\ge 60\text{ bp}$ exon length, out-of-phase internal translations across exon flanks, and zero stop codons in both species) reduces 1,622 human and 695 mouse candidates to 90 conserved dual-coding exons. The sequence randomization simulation (preserving codon usage and nucleotide identity between human and mouse orthologs to estimate false-positive stop-free alternate frames) is a well-designed null control for sequence conservation, though it does not prove active translation or functional protein stability of the minor frame *in vivo*.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *The overlapping coding region of `INK4a/ARF` evolves rapidly and asymmetrically due to elevated synonymous rate in `INK4a/2` ($d_S = 1.15$) and strong positive selection in `ARF/2` ($\omega = 7.54$).* **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** — The $\omega = 7.54$ estimate in `ARF/2` and asymmetric $d_S/d_N$ behavior are direct consequences of applying single-frame codon models to coupled out-of-phase reading frames where $d_S$ in one frame is coupled to $d_N$ in the other.
* **Claim 2:** *Approximately 90 alternatively spliced exons are conserved as dual-coding regions between human and mouse, exceeding expectations from neutral sequence conservation alone.* **`[Moderately Supported]`** — The comparative transcriptomic filter and stop-codon depletion simulations support evolutionary conservation of the alternate open reading frame in a subset of shorter exons ($\le 150\text{ nt}$), though ribosome profiling or mass-spectrometry validation of dual translation is absent.
* **Claim 3:** *An ancestral `ARF`-like alternate reading frame existed upstream of `INK4b` in marsupials (opossum) prior to the `INK4a/b` tandem duplication.* **`[Fragile]`** — Relies solely on an unexpressed genomic open reading frame in opossum without transcriptomic, promoter, or functional evidence, and is complicated by the `+2` vs. `+3` phase discrepancy in the text.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Ensembl release versions (`v37`, Feb 2006), `AltSplice` release (`v2`, April 2005), parameter thresholds, and species lists are clearly stated.
* **Deficiencies:** Supplementary Data (`supp_data.xls`, `supp_table.pdf`) are referenced at a personal/institutional web URL (`http://www.bx.psu.edu/~anton/share/cDCE/`) rather than a permanent archival repository, and the custom `Python`/`HyPhy` scripts are not archived.
