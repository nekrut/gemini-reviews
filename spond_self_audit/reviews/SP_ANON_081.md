### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript tests whether viral suppressors of RNAi (VSRs) across 12 plant single-stranded RNA viruses (spanning 10 viral genera, averaging 57 non-identical isolates per virus) undergo pervasive diversifying selection expected under a classic one-to-one molecular arms race with eukaryotic antiviral RNAi machinery. Using PAML (`codeml` M0, M1a/M2a, M8a/M8) and HyPhy (SLAC, FEL, REL, PARRIS, evolutionary fingerprinting, and MEME) after GARD recombination partitioning, the authors show that while VSRs exhibit a slightly elevated mean $d_N/d_S$ compared to non-VSR genes (ASReml meta-analysis effect size $= 0.04$, $p < 0.001$), they are no more likely than other viral genes to show pervasive positive selection ($p = 0.54$ for M8a/M8; $p = 0.57$ for PARRIS) or distinct bivariate evolutionary fingerprints ($p = 0.31$). Instead, VSRs rank highest in the proportion of codons experiencing episodic diversifying selection detected by MEME in 7 of 12 viruses (Fisher's combined $p = 0.0008$; Mann–Whitney $U$-test $p = 0.024$).

The study is well-framed and notable for reporting a negative result against its initial pervasive arms-race hypothesis while offering a quantitative timescale-asymmetry argument comparing eukaryotic and viral substitution rates. The main methodological weaknesses are: (1) treating thresholded site counts ($p \le 0.05$ from MEME) as exact proportions in downstream rank and non-parametric tests without accounting for gene-length variance or false discovery rate control; (2) non-independence of the three Potyvirus species (*Turnip mosaic virus*, *Plum pox virus*, and *Potato virus Y*) and dual VSRs (P1 and HcPro) in Fisher's combined probability test; and (3) unadjusted exploratory correlations with database host-range metrics ($S_s, F_s, S_t, F_t$).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Two-Stage Meta-Analysis and Thresholded Proportion Tests (Pillars 3 & 4):** For mean $d_N/d_S$, the authors appropriately weight log-transformed M0 point estimates by inverse delta-method variances in ASReml with viral family as a random effect. However, for the core positive finding (episodic selection via MEME), the authors compute the fraction of codons per gene with nominal $p \le 0.05$ and feed these raw proportions into a Mann–Whitney $U$-test ($p = 0.024$) and Fisher's combined ranking test ($p = 0.0008$). Because non-recombinant segment lengths vary by more than two orders of magnitude (9 to 1,711 codons; mean 336), the binomial sampling variance of the empirical proportion $\hat{p}_i = k_i / L_i$ is highly heteroskedastic across genes, and nominal $p \le 0.05$ guarantees a baseline expected false-positive proportion of up to $5\%$ even under strict neutrality.
* **Non-Independence in Fisher's Method for Combining Rankings (Pillar 3):** Fisher's combined probability test assumes independent tests across the 12 viruses. However, three of the 12 viruses belong to the genus *Potyvirus* (TurMV, PPV, PVY), and each potyvirus encodes two VSRs (P1 and HcPro; Table 1, Table 2 shows 15 VSR entries across 12 viruses). The text does not specify how ties or multiple VSRs within a single viral genome were handled when computing the discrete uniform rank probabilities combined via $-2\sum \ln p_i \sim \chi^2_{2k}$.
* **Host Range Correlation Multiple Testing (Pillar 4):** In the Discussion (line 127), the authors test four host-range metrics ($S_s, F_s, S_t, F_t$) against multiple evolutionary rate summaries using linear models and Spearman rank correlations on $n = 11$ viruses without multiple-testing correction, reporting nominal $p$-values while noting that linear model $p = 0.001$ corresponds to only $r_s = 0.37$, indicating heavy leverage from outliers in a small sample ($n = 11$).
* **Tree Inference Asymmetry Between Packages (Pillar 5):** PAML analyses used Bayesian maximum clade credibility (MCC) trees inferred in MrBayes (with visual burn-in inspection and split-frequency variance $< 0.05$ over as few as $10,000$ MCMC iterations, which is very short for 50–100 taxa), whereas HyPhy analyses used neighbor-joining trees generated within the Datamonkey GARD pipeline.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Pervasive Positive Selection Null Result [Well Supported]:** Across PAML M8a/M8, PARRIS, SLAC, FEL, REL, and ESD fingerprint permutation tests ($p = 0.31$), VSRs show no enrichment for pervasive diversifying selection over coat proteins or polymerases. The evolutionary timescale calculation in the Discussion (comparing host mutation rate $\sim 10^{-8}\text{ site}^{-1}\text{ yr}^{-1}$ vs. viral rate $\sim 10^{-3}\text{ site}^{-1}\text{ yr}^{-1}$) provides a sound population-genetic explanation for why one-to-one host-virus coevolution cannot sustain genome-wide $d_N/d_S > 1$ in RNA viruses.
* **Elevated Episodic Diversifying Selection in VSRs [Moderately Supported]:** The MEME enrichment in VSRs is biologically plausible given intermittent host shifts and variable plant genotypes, and holds across both within-virus ranking and across-gene comparisons. However, because overlapping reading frames occur in several of these viral VSRs (e.g., *Cucumovirus* 2b overlapping 2a, *Polerovirus* P0, *Tymo/Potexvirus* overlaps), synonymous site constraint ($d_S$ suppression in overlapping frames) can inflate apparent site-specific $\omega = d_N/d_S$ unless synonymous rate variation is carefully decoupled from overlapping-frame purifying selection. While MEME models site-to-site synonymous rate variation ($\alpha$), extreme localized $d_S \to 0$ in overlapping ORFs can still trigger spurious episodic $\beta^+ > \alpha$ calls.
* **Host-Range Association with VSR Evolution [Fragile]:** Properly caveated by the authors as exploratory and confounded by uneven literature ascertainment of susceptible plant species.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data & Code Availability:** Alignments and supplementary tables (Tables S1–S5) are provided in the electronic supplementary material, and standard public software packages (`codeml` in PAML v4, HyPhy via Datamonkey, MrBayes, ASReml) were used. However, exact GenBank accession lists, GARD breakpoint coordinates, and custom scripts for the ESD permutation test and rank-combination calculations are not deposited in an external code repository.
* **Internal Consistency:** In Table 2, the VSR row sums to $5 + 10 = 15$ genes (and non-VSR total sums to $17 + 49 = 66$), whereas Table 3 VSR row sums to $9 + 6 = 15$ genes, but non-VSR total sums to $40 + 24 = 64$ genes (with "other" dropping from 43 in Table 2 to 41 in Table 3) without explanation of which two non-VSR gene segments were omitted in Table 3.
