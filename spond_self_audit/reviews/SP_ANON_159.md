# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_159
**Composite Score:** 5.6 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.7/3.5 | Claims: 1.2/2.5 | Biology: 1.5/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.2/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Partner-Tracing Ascertainment Confounding, Static-Graph Tautology in Targeted ART Simulation & Proportion Inversion Errors
**Verdict:** Reject (or Major Revision)
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
`SP_ANON_159` analyzes HIV-1 `pol` sequences from 648 participants in the San Diego Primary Infection Cohort (SDPIC, 1996–2011; 478 acute/early index cases plus 170 recruited sexual and social contacts) using a pairwise Tamura-Nei 93 (`TN93 < 1.5%`) genetic distance network. The authors define a baseline degree-percentile metric—the Transmission Network Score (`TNS`)—evaluate its association with acquiring a "new outbound edge" within 1 year among 157 clustered individuals enrolled after 2004, and simulate TNS-targeted vs. random antiretroviral therapy (ART) on the reconstructed network. While molecular cluster surveillance is an important public health topic, the study suffers from severe epidemiological ascertainment circularity (active partner contact tracing confounds baseline degree and "subsequent outbound connections"), a tautological static-graph simulation of targeted ART, and glaring numerical/statistical errors between the text and tables.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Active Partner Contact-Tracing Ascertainment Bias in `TNS` and "New Outbound Edges" (`Methodologically Flawed`):** The cohort was assembled by enrolling acute/early index cases and then *actively recruiting and testing 170 of their named sexual and social contacts* (`Lines 12, 85`). Furthermore, for chronically infected contacts, the estimated date of infection (EDI) was arbitrarily set to 180 days prior to enrollment (`Line 18`), while edge directionality (`outbound` vs. `inbound`) was assigned whenever one partner's imputed EDI was $\ge 30$ days after the initial partner's sampling date (`Line 25`). This creates two major biases:
  1. Whether an enrolled individual has a "new outbound connection within 1 year" depends heavily on whether their sexual partners were subsequently recruited into the study via contact tracing $>30\text{--}210$ days later, and how chronic vs. acute infection staging shifted the imputed EDI relative to the 30-day cutoff.
  2. In evaluating `TNS` among the $n=157$ post-2004 subset (`Line 71`), the authors restricted the analysis exclusively to the **339 clustered participants** (`d >= 1`), omitting all 309 unclustered individuals (`d = 0`, 47.7% of the cohort). Even within clustered nodes, a participant's baseline degree $d$ at enrollment is determined by how many genetically linked partners had already been sampled in the clinic.
- **Tautological Static-Graph Simulation of Targeted ART (`Lines 40–42, 96–105, Figure 2`):** To evaluate TNS-guided ART (`Table 3`, `Figure 2`), the authors take the *retrospective, static network* (`339 clustered nodes`), remove nodes with `TNS >= 0.90` (which by definition are the highest-degree hubs in that static network), and assume that if $K$ of a neighbor's $N$ undirected connections are removed, that neighbor avoids infection with probability $K/N$. Removing the highest-degree nodes from a static graph and observing that more incident edges are hit than under random node removal (`3.3 vs 0.96 infections prevented`) is a mathematical tautology of degree centrality—it completely ignores time and edge directionality! Most edges attached to a high-`TNS` node already existed *at or before* the node's baseline enrollment and therefore could not possibly be prevented by starting ART after diagnosis.
- **Severe Numerical & Conditional-Probability Errors (`Lines 64, 71, 88, 103`):**
  1. **Inverted conditional probabilities and mismatched numbers (`Line 71` vs `Table 2`):** Line 71 states that *"18.5% (n = 29) of clustered participants had a new outbound connection within one year"*, and two sentences later states: *"participants enrolled during acute and early infection were not significantly more likely to develop a new outbound edge within the first year of follow up than persons with established (i.e., chronic) infection (**75.5% vs. 67.2%**, p = 0.52)."* Looking at `Table 2` (`Line 88`), `72.1%` (not `75.5%`, and note `72.1% + 27.6% = 99.7%` in that column!) is the proportion of **Transmitters (`n=29`) who were Acute/Early**, and `64.1%` (not `67.2%`) is the proportion of **Non-Transmitters (`n=128`) who were Acute/Early**! The authors reported the column percentages $P(\text{Acute} \mid \text{Transmission})$ (`72.1% vs 64.1%`, misquoted in text as `75.5% vs 67.2%`) as if they were the 1-year incidence of transmission $P(\text{Transmission} \mid \text{Acute})$ (which is actually $\sim 20.4\%$ vs $14.8\%$, since overall incidence is $29/157 = 18.5\%$)!
  2. **Malformed 95% confidence intervals (`Line 71`):** Line 71 reports *"339 individuals (52.3%, 95% CI: 333-392) and 90 clusters (95% CI: 68–90)"*, mixing percentages with raw integer counts and reporting bootstrap CIs where the point estimate (`90`) sits at the extreme upper bound of the interval (`68–90`).
  3. **Inequality sign reversal (`Line 103`):** Line 103 states that treating nodes by viral load or CD4 *"did not provide a measurable improvement over a randomly selected subset of nodes (probability of improving on a random treatment **>0.5** in all cases)"*—a probability $>0.5$ means it *does* improve on random treatment (should be `<0.5` or not significantly greater than `0.5`).
  4. **Table 1 typo (`Line 64`):** Viral load stratum is printed as `1501–,000 copies/mL` instead of `1501–10,000 copies/mL`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Baseline `TNS` correlates with baseline viral load ($R^2 = 0.017, p = 0.016$) (`[Fragile]`).** An $R^2$ of $0.017$ (1.7% of variance explained) is biologically negligible and likely reflects stage-of-infection confounding (acute index cases have higher viral loads than chronically infected traced contacts).
- **Claim 2: High `TNS` prospectively predicts future onward transmission within 1 year (`[Overstated]`).** Because partner tracing, unclustered node exclusion, and retrospective EDI imputation heavily confound edge directionality and discovery within 1 year of an index case's enrollment, the observational association cannot be interpreted as an unbiased prospective risk score.
- **Claim 3: Targeting ART to high-`TNS` patients is three-fold more effective at preventing transmissions than random ART (`[Methodologically Flawed]`).** This claim is derived from removing high-degree hubs in a retrospective static graph without respecting temporal order (counting pre-existing baseline links as "prevented infections").

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Because of patient privacy constraints in a localized clinical cohort, neither the 648 HIV-1 `pol` sequences nor the anonymized network adjacency list/enrollment dates are publicly deposited.
- **Methodological Transparency:** While the `TN93` threshold ($1.5\%$) and bootstrap procedures are described, the exact R/HyPhy scripts for the `TNS` static network deletion simulation are not publicly archived.
