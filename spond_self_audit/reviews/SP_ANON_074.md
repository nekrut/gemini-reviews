## 1. Executive Summary
This study integrates atomistic explicit-solvent molecular dynamics (MD) simulations with large-scale molecular evolutionary analysis ($N = 18,097$ Influenza A N1 neuraminidase sequences from GISAID) to investigate the structural and adaptive role of residue 347—the only variable position among the eight calcium-binding and substrate-proximal residues (118, 151, 152, 224, 276, 292, 347, 371) surrounding the N1 catalytic site. Using 195-ns AMBER ff14SB / GLYCAM06 MD trajectories and MMPBSA binding free energy calculations on the 1918 H1N1 neuraminidase (`3B7E`, carrying N347) and an *in silico* N347Y mutant docked with avian-type $\alpha\text{2-3}$ (`3S`) and human-type $\alpha\text{2-6}$ (`6S`) sialyl-pentasaccharides, the authors propose that Y347 preferentially stabilizes avian `3S` substrates via direct side-chain hydrogen bonding, whereas N347 coordinates a bridging water network and induces pyranose ring distortion of `6S`. Complementing the structural modeling, codon-based selection analyses (HyPhy FEL, group-partitioned FEL, and MEME on 17,856 sequences across 10 host/subtype groups) demonstrate that site 347 undergoes episodic diversifying selection specifically in avian-to-mammalian transition lineages (`H5av-1` and Eurasian avian-like swine `H1sw-2`), while remaining under strong purifying selection for Y347 in wild birds (`H1av`, `H5av-2`) and for N347 in pandemic/seasonal human H1N1 (`H1N1pdm`, `H1hu`).

The synthesis of biophysics and global phylogenetics is compelling, and the phylogenetic selection analyses are conducted at scale with appropriate clade partitioning. However, several methodological and reporting issues require attention:
1. **Single-Trajectory MD Without Independent Velocity Replicates:** Each of the four protein–ligand complexes (`3S-Y347`, `3S-N347`, `6S-Y347`, `6S-N347`) appears to have been simulated in only a single 195-ns trajectory, with MMPBSA standard errors ($\pm 0.3\text{–}0.4\text{ kcal/mol}$) calculated across 325 autocorrelated snapshots from a single 65-ns window (`130–195 ns`) rather than across independent replicate trajectories.
2. **Over-Interpretation of Classical MM Ring Dihedrals as Oxocarbonium Transition-State Precursors:** Classical fixed-charge molecular mechanics force fields (`GLYCAM06`) cannot model electronic polarization or covalent glycosidic bond cleavage; inferring catalytic activation toward an oxocarbonium ion from a shift in the C1–C2–C3–C4 dihedral angle from $72^\circ$ to $93^\circ$ (still far from the $\pm 180^\circ$ boat conformation) is speculative.
3. **Descriptive Cross-Segment HA–NA Association:** While intra-NA epistasis was formally tested via Bayesian Graphical Models (BGM, yielding no significant intra-NA links to site 347), the claim of coordinated evolution between NA site 347 and hemagglutinin (HA) receptor-binding site (RBS) mutations in H5N1 is based solely on qualitative inspection of clade tables (Table 4) without a formal phylogenetic test of correlated evolution.
4. **Numerical Discrepancies Between Text and Table 3:** Minor arithmetic/reporting inconsistencies appear in the summary of amino acid frequencies in Table 3 and the corresponding prose.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit

### 2.1 Single-Trajectory MD Sampling and Autocorrelated MMPBSA Error Bars
- For each of the four Pentasaccharide–NA systems, the authors run a 195-ns production trajectory and extract 325 frames at 200-ps intervals over the final 65 ns (`130–195 ns`) to compute MMPBSA binding enthalpies ($\Delta G_{\text{bind}}^{\text{PB}}$, Table 1).
- In biomolecular simulations of flexible pentasaccharides in a calcium-stabilized active site, a single 195-ns trajectory frequently samples only one local rotameric or water-bridging basin. Computing the standard error of the mean ($\text{SEM} = \pm 0.3\text{ to } 0.4\text{ kcal/mol}$) by dividing the standard deviation across $N = 325$ sequentially correlated snapshots by $\sqrt{325}$ underestimates true sampling uncertainty because consecutive frames separated by 200 ps are not statistically independent, and solute configurational entropy ($-T\Delta S$) is omitted from the MMPBSA estimate. Running 3–5 independent velocity-seeded replicates per complex and reporting inter-replicate means and SEMs is standard best practice.

### 2.2 Classical Force-Field Limitations in Modeling Catalytic Ring Distortion
- In Section *Substrate Distortion by Residue 347* and Figure 3, the authors measure the Neu5Ac C1–C2–C3–C4 dihedral angle, observing a bimodal distribution in `6S-N347` with peaks at $72^\circ$ and $93^\circ$, and interpret the $93^\circ$ state as ring distortion toward a boat conformation that facilitates formation of the planar oxocarbonium ion intermediate ($\text{C2}=\text{O}$, dihedral $\pm 180^\circ$).
- Because `GLYCAM06` uses harmonic/cosine dihedral potentials parameterized for ground-state chair conformations and lacks quantum-mechanical (QM/MM) treatment of anomeric bond elongation, small torsional fluctuations ($72^\circ \to 93^\circ$) reflect ground-state steric contacts with R371/Y406 rather than authentic transition-state stabilization.

### 2.3 Lack of Formal Co-Evolution Testing for Cross-Segment HA–NA Epistasis
- For intra-gene interactions within NA, the authors appropriately apply Spidermonkey/BGM to the 10 phylogenetic partitions and transparently report that no statistically significant co-evolving site pairs involving residue 347 were detected.
- In contrast, to evaluate inter-segment co-evolution between NA residue 347 and HA receptor-binding site residues in clade `H5av-1`, the authors rely on manual inspection of 11 branches/clusters where NA 347 mutated (Table 4), noting that "in roughly half of clusters... amino acid replacements occurred at HA near the RBS." Because H5N1 HA undergoes frequent positive selection across the globular head regardless of NA state, observing HA substitutions in 5–6 of 11 clusters without a background rate comparison or a formal phylogenetic independence test (e.g., Pagel's discrete trait correlation or BGM on concatenated HA–NA alignments) does not establish functional co-evolution above chance.

### 2.4 Numerical Discrepancies Between Text and Table 3
- **Avian H6N1 347F Percentage (Line 9 vs. Table 3 Line 54):** Line 9 of the Results states that in avian H6N1, *"the overwhelming majority of sequences contain either 347Y (88.4%) or 347F (11.6%)."* From the raw counts in Table 3 ($410\text{ Y} + 54\text{ F} = 464\text{ total}$), $410 / 464 = 88.36\%$ and $54 / 464 = 11.64\%$ (so $88.4\%$ and $11.6\%$ sum to $100.0\%$). However, Table 3 prints `88.4` for Y and `12.6` for F (summing to `101.0%`), an obvious typographical error (`12.6` instead of `11.6`).
- **H1N1pdm 347Y Count vs. Total Non-N Percentage (Line 11 vs. Table 3 Line 54):** Line 11 states: *"In fact, only one H1N1pdm sequence (0.09%) ... contained the avian-type 347Y."* In Table 3 (`H1N1pdm, all`), out of $32,723 + 29 = 32,752$ sequences, 1 sequence has Y ($1 / 32,752 = 0.003\%$), while 29 sequences in total have any non-N residue ($12\text{ D} + 1\text{ G} + 2\text{ H} + 11\text{ K} + 2\text{ S} + 1\text{ Y} = 29 / 32,752 = 0.089\% \approx 0.09\%$). Thus, the text conflates the count of 347Y ($n = 1$, $0.003\%$) with the combined percentage of all non-N variants ($0.09\%$).

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality

### Claim Audit Summary
- **Claim 1:** *"Our evolutionary analyses of large sequence datasets confirm differential maintenance of mostly N347 in human and swine viruses, and mostly Y347 in avian viruses, and document extensive adaptive diversifying evolution at site 347 across specific avian H5N1 and swine H1N1 clades."* — **[Well Supported]**: Strongly backed by FEL ($dN/dS = 0.000, p < 0.001$ in `H1av`, `H5av-2`, `H1N1pdm`, and `H1hu` vs. $dN/dS = 2.752, p = 0.017$ in `H5av-1`) and MEME ($p = 0.011$ in `H5av-1` with 22 branches; $p = 0.015$ in `H1sw-2` with 6 branches).
- **Claim 2:** *"The presence of a bulky aromatic sidechain at position 347 helps better coordinate oligosaccharides overhead of the catalytic site when the sialic acid has an alpha2-3 linkage (3S), whereas the polar carboxamide group of asparagine (N) prefers alpha2-6 oligosaccharides (6S) via bridging water coordination."* — **[Moderately Supported]**: Structurally plausible and consistent with the observed MD hydrogen-bond networks and radial distribution functions ($g(r)$), but tempered by the reliance on single 195-ns trajectories per complex and neglect of entropic contributions in MMPBSA.
- **Claim 3:** *"N347 shifts the Neu5Ac ring of 6S toward a boat conformation (dihedral 93 deg) that lowers the activation barrier for oxocarbonium ion formation."* — **[Fragile]**: Inferred from a minor $21^\circ$ torsional shift in a classical non-polarizable force field that cannot represent bond-breaking transition states.
- **Claim 4:** *"Roughly half of clusters in H5av-1 with NA 347 substitutions also had amino acid replacements in HA near the RBS, suggesting epistatic interaction."* — **[Fragile]**: Observational co-occurrence in 5–6 clades without a statistical baseline for background HA substitution rates.

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** The phylogenetic pipeline uses standard, open-source tools (MAFFT, FastTree2, HyPhy FEL, MEME, BGM) on 18,097 GISAID sequences, and the structural modeling uses standard AMBER 14 (`ff14SB`, `GLYCAM06`, `TIP3P`) and AutoDock 4.2 protocols starting from PDB `3B7E`.
- **Gaps:** Input parameter files, initial docked pentasaccharide coordinates (`3S` and `6S`), MD trajectory archives, and the GISAID EPI_SET / acknowledgment table or aligned phylogenetic trees for the 10 partitions are not linked in a public repository.
