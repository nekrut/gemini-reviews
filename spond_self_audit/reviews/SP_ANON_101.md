# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_101
**Composite Score:** 5.4 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.5/3.5 | Claims: 1.4/2.5 | Biology: 1.4/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.1/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Small-Sample Separation / Post-Hoc Variable Selection Inflation
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Hybrid Computational + Wet-Lab Functional Validation
**Technology Maturity:** Mid-Stage High-Throughput
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
This retrospective observational study examines the association between HIV-1 dual infection (DI) and HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder (HAND) in $N = 38$ antiretroviral therapy (ART)-treated adults (>50 years of age) selected from the CHARTER cohort. The authors performed 454 pyrosequencing of four PCR-amplified HIV-1 proviral DNA coding regions (*gag* p24, *pol* RT, *pol* PR, and *env* C2-V3) from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) across two longitudinal visits separated by >4 years. Using sliding-window divergence thresholds and maximum-likelihood phylogenies in HyPhy/Datamonkey, the authors identified DI in 9 of 38 participants (23.7%) and global neurocognitive impairment in 21 of 38 participants (55.3%). While unadjusted Fisher's exact testing showed no statistically significant relationship between DI and neurocognitive impairment, backward stepwise multivariable logistic regression yielded an adjusted odds ratio of $\text{OR} = 18.30$ ($95\% \text{ CI}: 1.94\text{--}414.16, p = 0.028$).

While the application of longitudinal proviral deep sequencing to detect archived dual infection in virally suppressed or partially suppressed ART recipients is biologically well-motivated, the primary statistical conclusion rests on a fragile, overfitted multivariable logistic regression model applied to a small sample ($N = 38$, with only 9 DI cases). The 200-fold confidence interval ($1.94\text{--}414.16$) and the leap from a non-significant unadjusted association to an extreme adjusted odds ratio of $18.30$ following backward stepwise selection at $p < 0.20$ are classic symptoms of small-sample sparse-data bias (quasi-complete separation) and post-selection inference inflation. Furthermore, the manuscript omits the raw $2 \times 2$ cross-tabulation of DI by HAND status and does not provide sequence accession numbers. Re-analysis using Firth's penalized likelihood regression, transparent reporting of the unadjusted contingency table, and public sequence deposition are necessary to make the findings interpretable.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Extreme sparse-data bias and stepwise overfitting in logistic regression ([Methodologically Flawed]):** The study's headline claim ($\text{OR} = 18.30, 95\% \text{ CI}: 1.94\text{--}414.16, p = 0.028$) is derived from a multivariable logistic regression model with three predictors (DI status, estimated duration of infection, and current CD4 T-cell count) fit to $N = 38$ total participants (21 impaired, 17 unimpaired; only 9 with DI). With only 17 events in the smaller outcome class, fitting 3 covariates after screening $>10$ candidate demographic, clinical, and behavioral variables via univariate likelihood ratio tests ($p < 0.20$) and backward elimination violates standard events-per-variable requirements and inflates Type I error. Worse, the text states that *"DI was not significantly associated with impairment"* in unadjusted analysis, yet adjusting for duration of infection and CD4 count—neither of which differs significantly between mono-infection and DI in Table 1 ($p = 0.22$ and $p = 0.32$)—inflates the OR to $18.30$ with an upper confidence bound of $414.16$. This indicates near-separation in the $2 \times 2$ table or collinearity/confounding amplification in a tiny sample.
* **Omission of the primary $2 \times 2$ contingency table and unadjusted OR ([Fragile]):** Remarkably, neither Table 1 nor Table 2 reports the actual number of DI participants ($N = 9$) who were neurocognitively impaired versus unimpaired, nor the unadjusted OR and Fisher's exact $p$-value. Because $21/38$ (55.3%) of the overall cohort is impaired, the integer count of impaired DI subjects can only be 6, 7, or 8 out of 9:
  * If $7/9$ DI vs. $14/29$ mono-infected are impaired: unadjusted $\text{OR} = 3.75$, Fisher's exact $p = 0.148$.
  * If $8/9$ DI vs. $13/29$ mono-infected are impaired: unadjusted $\text{OR} = 9.85$, Fisher's exact $p = 0.027$.
  Since the text explicitly notes that unadjusted DI was *not* significantly associated with impairment, the count is almost certainly $7/9$ (or $6/9$). Jumping from an unadjusted OR of $\le 3.75$ ($p \ge 0.15$) to an adjusted OR of $18.30$ ($p = 0.028$) because DI participants happened to have slightly higher median current CD4 counts ($513$ vs. $464\text{ cells}/\mu\text{L}$) and longer infection duration ($15.5$ vs. $10.3\text{ years}$) demonstrates that the result is entirely dependent on parametric model conditioning across 9 observations.
* **Inappropriate parametric tests on skewed clinical variables ([Moderately Supported]):** The Statistical Methods state that continuous variables were compared using independent-samples $t$-tests, whereas Tables 1 and 2 report medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) for skewed variables (CD4 counts, viral loads censored at $<1.7\log_{10}$, duration of infection). Applying $t$-tests to left-censored log viral loads and skewed CD4 counts without non-parametric verification (e.g., Mann-Whitney $U$ test) is mismatched to the data distribution.
* **Longitudinal mismatch between exposure confirmation and outcome definition ([Fragile]):** Neurocognitive impairment was defined strictly at the *first* study timepoint (where 21 participants were impaired, only 11 of whom remained impaired at the second timepoint $>4$ years later). However, DI was defined using sequences across *both* longitudinal timepoints (including superinfection events that could have occurred between timepoint 1 and timepoint 2, or co-infections present at baseline). The manuscript does not distinguish whether both viral lineages were already present at timepoint 1 when HAND was diagnosed, creating potential temporal ambiguity (lookahead bias if superinfection occurred after baseline neurocognitive testing).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Identification of HIV-1 dual infection via PBMC proviral deep sequencing ([Well Supported]):** The experimental workflow—454 deep sequencing across four coding regions, sliding-window divergence screening in HyPhy, phylogenetic verification against subtype B background sequences, laboratory contamination checks, and independent re-sequencing confirmation—is rigorous and well-suited for detecting archived proviral lineages in ART-experienced individuals.
* **Biological confounding by neurocognitive comorbidities and IVDU ([Overstated]):** In Table 1, contributing neurocognitive comorbidities are nearly three times as frequent in the DI group ($5/9 = 56\%$) as in the monoinfection group ($6/29 = 21\%, p = 0.088$), and intravenous drug use (IVDU) occurs exclusively in the DI group ($2/9 = 22\%$ vs. $0/29 = 0\%, p = 0.051$, both of whom were also impaired in Table 2). Because backward elimination dropped contributing comorbidities ($p = 0.28$ univariately with impairment), the multivariable model does not control for the substantial excess of contributing neurocognitive comorbidities or IVDU in the DI group.
* **Claim of adequate statistical power ([Overstated]):** In the Discussion (line 211), the authors assert that *"While a larger sample size would have increased power, our study had adequate power to test our primary hypothesis."* Post-hoc assertions of adequate power based on achieving $p = 0.028$ with an OR point estimate of $18.30$ represent the "winner's curse" (Type M / magnitude error): a study of $N = 38$ only achieves statistical significance when the sample estimate is massively inflated relative to any biologically plausible true effect size.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Missing sequence data deposition:** No GenBank or SRA accession numbers are provided for the 454 pyrosequencing reads or consensus/haplotypic contigs generated from the 38 participants.
* **Incomplete statistical reporting:** As noted above, Table 2 omits the row for Dual Infection vs. Monoinfection in the unadjusted section, depriving readers of the raw counts and unadjusted $p$-value for the primary hypothesis of the paper.
* **Typos and formatting errors:** Double bracket typo `[[ 13 ]]` on line 82; misspelling `nerocognitively` on line 156; missing degree-of-freedom and test-statistic values alongside $p$-values in Tables 1 and 2.
