### 1. Executive Summary
This study extends the `HyPhy RELAX` codon model to jointly analyze multi-partition/multi-segment viral alignments, testing whether natural zoonotic spillover stem branches (`H1N1pdm09`, `EBOV`, `MARV`, `MPXV`, `SARS-CoV`, `SARS-CoV-2`) exhibit genome-wide relaxation or intensification of natural selection compared to 1977 re-emergent `H1N1` and laboratory-passaged/attenuated viral controls (`WSN33`, lake-ice `H1N1`, measles/mumps vaccines, passaged `MHV`, cold-adapted `H2N2`, ferret-passaged `H5N1`). The authors find significant relaxation on the 1977 `H1N1` stem ($K = 0.71, p = 0.043$) and several heavily passaged controls, but no significant selection shift on natural pre-zoonotic stems (such as `SARS-CoV-2`). While the joint multi-partition `RELAX` formulation is sound and the biological distinction between prolonged *in vitro* attenuation and natural reservoir maintenance is well reasoned, the statistical power on short single stem branches is severely limited, and therefore a non-significant `RELAX` test cannot rule out brief laboratory passage or short-term intermediate-host adaptation.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Low Statistical Power on Single Short Stem Branches (Type II Error Risk):** The core test of natural vs. unnatural emergence evaluates a *single* internal phylogenetic branch (e.g., the `1977 H1N1` stem, the `H1N1pdm09` segment stems, or the `SARS-CoV-2` stem). Across the entire concatenated `1977 H1N1` genome, the target stem branch contains only 48 inferred nucleotide substitutions (31 nonsynonymous, 17 synonymous; line 106), yielding a borderline nominal $p = 0.043$ ($K = 0.71$) that would not survive multiple-testing correction across the 20+ branch sets evaluated in Tables 1–3. Conversely, as the authors acknowledge in the Discussion (line 161), if a virus underwent brief laboratory passage or short-term circulation in an intermediate host accumulating only a handful of mutations, a single-branch `RELAX` test has near-zero statistical power to reject the null ($K = 1$).
* **Boundary Parameter Degeneracy ("Unbalanced Model" $K \to 0$ when $\omega_3 \approx 1$):** In `RELAX`, selection intensity on test branches is parameterized as $\omega_{\text{test}} = \omega_{\text{ref}}^K$. When the reference branch partition estimates the third rate class near neutrality ($\omega_3 \approx 1.00$, as occurred in `EBOV`, `MPXV`, `Measles`, `Mumps`, and `MHV`; Tables 1 and 2) while test branches experience strong positive selection at a small fraction of sites ($\omega_{\text{test},3} \gg 1$), the exponent $K$ cannot expand $1.00^K$ above $1$ unless $K$ is driven toward $0$ or extreme boundary values, producing numerical instability ("unbalanced model" where $K = 0.00$ with $p < 0.001$ alongside an alternative local optimum with $K > 1$). Although the authors candidly diagnose this mathematical degeneracy and re-run constrained two-rate or clade-restricted models, it demonstrates that the single-parameter power-law transformation $\omega^K$ is poorly specified when $\omega_{\text{ref}}$ sits on the neutral boundary $\omega = 1$.
* **Inhomogeneous Reference Partitioning Across Viruses:** For `EBOV` and `MPXV`, because intra-outbreak human branches were scarce or short, all internal branches separating distinct historical outbreaks were assigned to the "Reservoir" reference partition, whereas for `SARS-CoV-2` and `SARS-CoV`, human outbreak internal branches were contrasted directly against bat sarbecovirus branches. Conflating deep inter-outbreak divergence with active reservoir transmission alters the baseline $\omega$ distribution.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *Heavily passaged or live-attenuated vaccine viruses (`Measles`, `Mumps`, `Cold-adapted H2N2`, `Ferret-passaged H5N1`) and the 1977 `H1N1` re-emergence stem exhibit measurable shifts in genome-wide selection intensity compared to natural circulation.* **`[Moderately Supported]`** — Strongly supported for multi-branch vaccine/passage datasets (`H2N2` $p < 0.001$, `H5N1` $p = 0.011$), but fragile for the single `1977 H1N1` stem branch ($p = 0.043$ unadjusted across 48 substitutions) and complicated by $\omega_3 = 1$ boundary degeneracy in `Measles`/`Mumps`/`MHV`.
* **Claim 2:** *Zoonotic spillover stems (`H1N1pdm09`, `SARS-CoV-2`, `EBOV`, `MARV`, `MPXV`) show no detectable relaxation of selection prior to human emergence, consistent with natural reservoir evolution.* **`[Moderately Supported]`** — The null `RELAX` results ($p > 0.05$) are consistent with uninterrupted purifying selection in natural reservoirs (`SARS-CoV-2` stem $\omega = 0.05$), though they strictly show an *absence of evidence* for prolonged relaxation ($\ge \text{dozens of substitutions}$) rather than *evidence of absence* of brief passage or a short-term intermediate host.
* **Claim 3:** *Including early civet and human `SARS-CoV-1` branches on the spillover stem produces a detectable relaxation signal ($K = 0.56, p = 0.006$) driven by adaptive host-jumping mutations.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Demonstrates how early host-adaptation bursts (pushing $\omega$ toward 1) can mimic relaxation of purifying selection under the `RELAX` parameterization.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** All sequence alignments, partition files, Newick trees with branch annotations, and `HyPhy RELAX` outputs are publicly archived on Zenodo/GitHub (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`), with complete parameter estimates ($\omega_1, \omega_2, \omega_3, p_1, p_2, p_3, K$, LRT, and $p$-values) reported across Tables 1–3.
* **Deficiencies:** None in data availability; adding simulation power curves quantifying the minimum number of branch substitutions required for `RELAX` to detect $K = 0.7$ at $\alpha = 0.05$ would significantly strengthen the methodological transparency.
