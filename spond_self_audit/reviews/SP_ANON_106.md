### 1. Executive Summary
This methodological and empirical study evaluates the impact of site-to-site synonymous substitution rate variation (SRV) on gene-wide tests of episodic diversifying selection, introducing `BUSTED[S]`—an extension of the `BUSTED` branch-site random-effects model that incorporates a 3-bin general discrete distribution (GDD) for site-specific synonymous substitution rates ($\alpha_s$, constrained to unit mean $E[\alpha_s] = 1$) alongside the 3-bin nonsynonymous-to-synonymous rate ratio distribution ($\omega_{bs}$). Through parametric simulations across two empirical tree topologies (16 and 31 taxa, 100 to 5,000 codons) and 3,278 avian gene trees with empirically derived $\alpha_s$ distributions, the authors demonstrate that ignoring SRV causes catastrophic Type I error inflation in standard `BUSTED` (and even worse in `M1a-M2a` site models), with false positive rates exceeding $50\%$ once the coefficient of variation of synonymous rates $\text{CV}(\alpha) > 0.5$ and approaching $100\%$ for $\text{CV}(\alpha) > 1.0$. Applying both methods to 13,416 vertebrate gene alignments from the Selectome database reveals that `BUSTED[S]` is preferred by $\text{AIC}_c$ in $91.4\%$ of genes (median $\Delta\text{AIC}_c = 112$, median $\text{CV}(\alpha) = 0.65$) and that $54.4\%$ of positive selection calls made by standard `BUSTED` ($1,485 / 2,735$) disappear when SRV is modeled.

This is an exemplary methodological contribution. The experimental design pairs large-scale empirical screening (13,416 Selectome alignments plus 11 classic benchmark datasets) with targeted null and power simulations that directly bridge the empirical $\text{CV}(\alpha)$ distribution to the theoretical regime where unmodeled SRV induces "phenomenological load" on $\omega_3$. The claims are quantitatively precise, the mathematical formulation is sound, and all alignments, simulated datasets, and open-source software (`HyPhy` v2.5.1+ and `Datamonkey`) are publicly available.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Rigorous diagnosis of phenomenological load from unmodeled SRV ([Well Supported]):** Under standard `BUSTED` ($\alpha_s \equiv 1$), a hypermutable codon with high synonymous and nonsynonymous substitution counts ($\alpha_s \gg 1$, true $\omega \le 1$, such as Rhodopsin codon 213 with $\hat{\alpha} = 6.25$ in Fig. 3) can only be accommodated by assigning it to the unconstrained $\omega_3 > 1$ category. By adding 4 free parameters for a 3-bin unit-mean GDD on $\alpha_s$, `BUSTED[S]` restores nominal $5\%$ Type I error across the entire spectrum of $\text{CV}(\alpha)$ (Fig. 4B, Fig. 5A) while retaining equivalent statistical power when $\text{CV}(\alpha) < 0.5$ (Fig. 4D, F).
* **Empirical realism of the 3,278 avian null simulations ([Well Supported]):** Rather than relying solely on synthetic GDD parameterizations, the authors fit `BUSTED[S]` to 3,278 empirical avian alignments from Shultz and Sackton (2019), extract the empirical tree topologies and inferred 3-bin $\alpha_s$ distributions, and simulate 5,000-codon neutral alignments ($\omega_3 = 1$). Showing that `BUSTED` false positive rates still climb to $100\%$ for $\text{CV}(\alpha) > 0.5$ regardless of the skewness or higher moments of the empirical $\alpha_s$ distribution (Fig. 5B) rules out parameterization artifacts.
* **Generality verified against site models (`M1a-M2a`) ([Well Supported]):** Testing the classic Wong et al. (2004) `M1a-M2a` site model on the same simulations (Fig. S8) demonstrates that the pathology is not specific to branch-site random-effects models; in fact, `M1a-M2a` suffers even steeper FPR inflation at lower $\text{CV}(\alpha)$.
* **Unmodeled branch-level SRV and multi-nucleotide substitutions ([Moderately Supported]):** As noted in the Methods (line 121), `BUSTED[S]` models site-to-site variation in $\alpha_s$ (constant across branches) but does not model branch-to-branch variation in synonymous rates or instantaneous multi-nucleotide (double/triple) codon substitutions, which can also inflate $\omega_3$ in branch-site tests. While outside the scope of a 4-parameter site-level SRV extension, briefly noting how branch-site SRV or multi-hit substitutions interact with $\alpha_s$ would provide useful context.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Empirical demonstration on 13,416 Selectome alignments and 11 classic benchmarks ([Well Supported]):** The empirical divergence curve between `BUSTED` and `BUSTED[S]` detection rates as a function of inferred $\text{CV}(\text{SRV})$ in Selectome (Fig. 2)—where Cohen's $\kappa \approx 0.9$ for $\text{CV}(\text{SRV}) < 0.1$, stays $\ge 0.8$ up to $\text{CV}(\text{SRV}) \approx 0.4$, and then collapses as `BUSTED` rejection rates surge 2–3$\times$ higher above $\text{CV}(\text{SRV}) > 0.5$—mirrors the exact threshold predicted by the neutral simulations in Figs. 4A and 5A.
* **Proportional framing of false positive estimates ([Well Supported]):** The authors carefully frame the $54.4\%$ ($1,485/2,735$) discordant Selectome calls as *"candidates for being false positives"*, explicitly weighing the alternative hypothesis (power loss in `BUSTED[S]` at extreme $\text{CV}(\text{SRV}) > 1.5$) and demonstrating that the vast majority of Selectome alignments fall in the $0.5 < \text{CV}(\text{SRV}) < 1.5$ regime where `BUSTED[S]` suffers minimal power loss.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Complete open-science compliance:** All 13,416 Selectome NEXUS alignments, the 11 reference alignments, the simulated alignments, and `README.md` instructions are hosted at `data.hyphy.org/web/busteds/`, and the method is distributed in `HyPhy` v2.5.1+ and on the `Datamonkey` web server.
