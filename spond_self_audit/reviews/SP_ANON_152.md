### 1. Executive Summary
This study analyzes over one million global SARS-CoV-2 genome sequences from GISAID (sampled through June 1, 2021) using a scalable haplotype-compression and phylogenetic selection pipeline (`bealign`, binomial error filtering, `tn93-cluster`, `RapidNJ`/`raxml-ng`, and internal-branch-restricted `FEL`, `MEME`, `SLAC`, and `PRIME` in HyPhy) to characterize the selective pressures accompanying the late-2020 emergence of the three 501Y lineages: Alpha (`B.1.1.7`/V1), Beta (`B.1.351`/V2), and Gamma (`P.1`/V3). Using a temporal split in which internal-branch positive selection and inter-lineage convergence were identified in sequences collected prior to March–April 2021 and subsequently evaluated for $\ge 2$-fold frequency increases between March 15 and June 1, 2021, the authors identify 29 convergent adaptive sites and combine them with 4 convergent deletions and 3 high-frequency RBD substitutions (`L18F`, `K417N/T`, `N501Y`) to define a 35-site "501Y meta-signature."

The bioinformatic engineering for pandemic-scale selection tracking—particularly restricting codon selection tests to internal branches to filter out within-host deleterious mutations and validating pre-March selection calls against post-March frequency trajectories—is a strong methodological contribution, backed by exemplary open-data notebooks. However, two primary analytical caveats temper the broader evolutionary claims: (1) the apparent sudden "global shift" in both positively and negatively selected site densities in November 2020 (Figure 2A–B) is substantially confounded by the non-linear power curve of site-level likelihood ratio tests as 90-day tree lengths expand; and (2) the 35-site "meta-signature" is constructed via a post-hoc union of criteria and extrapolated to predict that SARS-CoV-2 evolution will culminate in variants accumulating progressively larger subsets of this single fitness peak.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Non-Linear FEL Power Scaling vs. the "November 2020 Global Selective Shift" (Figure 2A–B):** The authors normalize the number of sites with FEL $p \le 0.01$ in each 90-day sliding window by kilobases and total internal tree length (`subs/site`), arguing that this correction rules out increased sampling/diversification as the cause of the November 2020 jump in positively and negatively selected site densities. However, site-level FEL power does not scale linearly with tree length $T$: when $T$ is small (as in March–October 2020), nearly all codon sites have $0\text{--}2$ substitutions and FEL has near-zero power ($P(\text{reject})/T \approx 0$); once 90-day tree length crosses a critical mutational depth threshold (driven by global autumn/winter epidemic surges and the co-circulation of divergent clades), hundreds of sites cross the 3–5 substitution threshold needed for asymptotic $\chi^2_1$ significance at $p \le 0.01$, causing the ratio $(\text{significant sites}) / T$ to jump sharply. This statistical power threshold effect is directly evidenced by **Figure 2B**, where the density of *negatively* selected sites surges simultaneously in November 2020—not because purifying selection on structural and replication proteins suddenly intensified worldwide in November 2020, but because longer 90-day trees finally provided sufficient synonymous substitutions per site for FEL to reject $dN = dS$ in favor of $dN < dS$.
* **Unadjusted Site-Level $p < 0.05$ Screening and Basal-Branch Inclusion:** In the lineage-specific analyses (151 sites across V1, V2, and V3 at MEME/FEL $p < 0.05$), significance is evaluated at unadjusted nominal $p < 0.05$ without FDR correction, and as the authors acknowledge in the text, including the long lineage-defining basal branches biases the test toward calling signature mutations positively selected. While the permutation test for pairwise overlap of non-signature selected/convergent sites across V1/V2/V3 ($p \le 0.002$) properly conditions on variable sites, site-level FDR $q$-values should be reported in Table S1.
* **Phylogenetic Non-Independence in $2 \times 2$ Contingency Table Frequency Tests:** Testing whether a mutation's frequency changed between pre-March 15 and post-March 15 GISAID bins using a standard $2 \times 2$ chi-square test on raw sequence counts treats epidemiologically linked sequences within an expanding sub-lineage or regional sequencing surge as independent observations, which severely inflates chi-square test statistics. Requiring a $>2$-fold relative risk increase mitigates trivial effect sizes, but $p$-values from raw GISAID sequence counts do not reflect independent evolutionary replicates.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Parallel Convergent Evolution at Key Functional Loci Across 501Y Lineages [Well Supported]:** The independent recurrence and expansion of mutations in the Spike RBD (`417`, `484`, `501`), NTD antigenic supersite (`18`, `26`, `138`, `215`), furin cleavage proximal region (`655`, `681`, `701`, `716`), and `nsp6` (`11288–11296` deletion) across V1, V2, and V3 are strongly supported by both internal-branch $dN/dS$ signals and subsequent frequency doubling.
* **Extrapolation that Ongoing Evolution Will Culminate in Variants Summiting the 35-Site "501Y Meta-Signature" Peak [Overstated]:** Because the 35-site meta-signature was assembled post hoc by combining pre-March selected sites that doubled in frequency, 9 additional signature sites discovered only in the June 2021 frequency screen, 4 deletions, and 3 pre-fixed RBD sites, counting matches to this list favors 501Y-like lineages (`B.1.620`, `B.1.526`, `B.1.621`) while scoring rapidly expanding non-501Y lineages like `B.1.617.2` (Delta, with only 3 matches) as low-matching. Treating an additive tally of 35 sites as a single predictive evolutionary summit understates inter-lineage clonal interference and historical contingency.
* **Speculative Proposal for Live-Virus In Vitro Recombination Passaging [Fragile]:** In the concluding paragraphs of the Discussion, the authors propose co-infecting laboratory cultures with live or synthesized V1/V2/V3 viruses in the presence of convalescent/vaccine sera to generate recombinant escape chimeras. Given the Dual Use Research of Concern (DURC) biosafety implications and the authors' own caveat that cell-culture passaging selects for culture-specific adaptations (e.g., furin-cleavage deletions in Vero cells), this speculative section adds little to the computational paper and could be omitted or framed around safe pseudovirus/deep mutational scanning assays.
