### 1. Executive Summary
This study introduces the Multiclass Synonymous Substitution (`MSS`) codon model—an extension of the Muse–Gaut 94 (`MG94`) reversible Markov codon framework implemented in `HyPhy`—which partitions sense codons into $K$ disjoint classes ($C_1, \dots, C_K$), designating $C_1$ as a strictly neutral synonymous reference class ($\alpha_1 \equiv 1$) while estimating relative synonymous substitution rates $\alpha_k$ within (and $\alpha_{kl}$ between) selected synonymous classes, thereby defining the selection parameter as $d_N / d_{Sn}$ (nonsynonymous rate divided by the strictly neutral synonymous rate). To identify a putatively neutral codon subset empirically across 13 *Enterobacterales* bacterial genomes (1,613 orthologous genes), the authors perform factor analysis on gene-level codon frequencies, validate that Factor 1 (`F1`) loadings strongly track *E. coli* RNA-seq gene expression ($r = -0.79, p < 10^{-5}$), and select the two fourfold-degenerate amino acids whose codons consistently exhibit the lowest absolute `F1` loadings across species (Alanine and Valine, 8 codons) as the neutral reference class $C_1$. Applying this 1-parameter `MSS` extension ($\alpha_s = d_{Ss} / d_{Sn}$) to the 1,613 alignments shows that the selected synonymous rate is on average $80.0\%$ of the neutral rate (mean $d_S / d_{Sn} = 0.800$, Theil–Sen slope $= 0.785$), rejecting $d_{Ss} = d_{Sn}$ in $57\%$ of genes ($52.4\%$ at $\text{FDR } q = 0.05$) and improving $\text{AIC}_c$ in $75.8\%$ of genes. Coupling these estimates with a population-genetic waiting-time model for twofold and fourfold sites demonstrates that very weak translational selection (mean $Ns = 0.8$) suffices to explain the $20\%$ reduction in $d_S$ and implies that standard `MG94` analyses underestimate purifying selection by $\sim 25\%$ and overestimate McDonald–Kreitman adaptive substitution proportions ($\alpha_{\text{MK}}$ drops from $0.56$ to $0.26$ in *E. coli*).

This is a creative, mathematically rigorous, and biologically insightful study that directly tackles a foundational problem in molecular evolution: how to calibrate $d_N/d_S$ when codon usage bias depresses $d_S$ genome-wide. The authors validate `MSS` through parametric MG94 null simulations, negative control codon partitions (Proline + Threonine), RNA-seq expression correlations, `BUSTED-MSS` episodic selection comparisons, and analytical fixation-probability theory. Only minor algebraic/figure-reference typos and a conceptual caveat regarding residual selection within the Ala/Val reference set warrant attention.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Well-calibrated `MSS` Markov generator and parametric null validation ([Well Supported]):** Partitioning the codon space by amino acid (Ala + Val vs. the remaining 16 redundant amino acids) cleanly eliminates inter-class one-step synonymous transitions ($\alpha_{kl}$ terms vanish because synonymous mutations cannot change the encoded amino acid), reducing the `MSS` extension to a single identifiable parameter $\alpha_s = d_{Ss}/d_{Sn}$ nested with 1 degree of freedom inside `MG94` ($\alpha_s = 1$). Parametric simulations across all 1,613 gene trees under `MG94` (Fig. 1A) confirm unbiasedness (Theil–Sen slope $= 1.005$, mean $\hat{\alpha}_s = 1.02 \pm 0.15$), nominal Type I error ($4.8\%$ at $\alpha = 0.05$), and the expected $\sim 1.9\times$ increase in RMSE ($0.013$ vs. $0.0069$) from restricting the neutral denominator to 8 codons.
* **Negative control partition (Threonine + Proline) ([Well Supported]):** Re-running `MSS` with two fourfold-degenerate amino acids that have *high* absolute `F1` loadings (Thr and Pro) as the reference class $C_1$ predictably flips $\hat{\alpha}_s > 1$ and degrades $\text{AIC}_c$ in $78.3\%$ of genes relative to the Ala+Val model, confirming that the `F1`-guided partition captures genuine rate heterogeneity between low-bias and high-bias amino acid families.
* **Analytical population-genetic model of alternating synonymous substitutions ([Well Supported]):** Modeling the steady-state waiting time between alternating favored ($+s$) and disfavored ($-s$) synonymous substitutions under Kimura's haploid fixation probability $p(s) = \frac{2s}{1 - e^{-2Ns}}$ yields exact closed-form expressions for $d_{Ss}/d_{Sn}$ at twofold ($\frac{4Ns e^{2Ns}}{e^{4Ns}-1}$) and fourfold ($\frac{e^{4Ns} + 8Ns e^{2Ns} - 1}{3(e^{4Ns}-1)}$) sites, showing that a $20\%$ rate reduction ($d_{Ss}/d_{Sn} = 0.80$) corresponds to $Ns \approx 0.8$.
* **Minor algebraic and figure-label typos in the text ([Moderately Supported]):**
  * On line 77, the sentence *"under a simulation in which all synonymous codons are neutral ($\alpha_s = 1$), the estimated $d_N / d_{Sn}$ rate should be the same as the estimated $d_N / d_{Sn}$ rate"* has a subscript typo in the second term (should be *"the same as the estimated $d_N / d_S$ rate"*), and the next sentence refers to *"the mean of estimated $\alpha_s$ (i.e., $d_N / d_{Sn}$) values was 1.02"* whereas $\alpha_s$ is $d_{Ss} / d_{Sn}$, not $d_N / d_{Sn}$.
  * On line 95, the linear identity $d_S / d_{Sn} = \theta + (1 - \theta)(d_{Ss} / d_{Sn})$ is rearranged into the regression form `d_S / d_{Sn} = (\theta - d_{Ss}/d_{Sn}) / (\theta - 1)`, which is algebraically incorrect (since setting $d_{Ss}/d_{Sn} = 1$ in that fraction gives $\frac{\theta - 1}{\theta - 1} = 1$, but the slope with respect to $d_{Ss}/d_{Sn}$ would be $\frac{-1}{\theta - 1} = \frac{1}{1 - \theta} > 1$ instead of $1 - \theta < 1$; the correct linear relation is simply $d_S / d_{Sn} = \theta + (1 - \theta)(d_{Ss} / d_{Sn})$). Nevertheless, the numerical estimate $\hat{\theta} = 0.1896$ ($1 - \hat{\theta} = 81.0\%$ selected synonymous substitutions, matching the $75/87 = 86.2\%$ share of synonymous pairs outside Ala/Val) is completely consistent with the correct linear equation.
  * On lines 108–110, the text refers to `Fig. 3 A` for the theoretical $Ns$ curves and `Fig. 3 B` for the histogram of estimated $Ns$ values, whereas the caption of Figure 3 (line 97) labels the regression of $\theta$ as `( A )`, the theoretical $Ns$ curves as `( B )`, and the $Ns$ histogram as `( C )`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Conservative nature of the Ala/Val neutral proxy ([Well Supported]):** The authors transparently note (Discussion, line 117) that because `F1` factor loadings measure *gene-by-gene covariation* in codon usage (driven by expression differences across genes), any uniform genome-wide codon preference within Alanine or Valine—or site-specific mRNA secondary structure constraint—would still slightly depress $d_{Sn}$ below the true mutation rate, making their $20\%$ reduction ($d_S / d_{Sn} = 0.80$) a conservative lower bound on the impact of synonymous selection.
* **Downstream impact on McDonald–Kreitman $\alpha$ and `BUSTED` ([Well Supported]):** Demonstrating that replacing $d_S$ with $d_{Sn}$ cuts the classic *E. coli* McDonald–Kreitman adaptive substitution estimate $\alpha$ in half (from $0.56$ to $0.26$, assuming neutral or less-selected $p_S$) and alters $10.2\%$ ($164/1,613$) of `BUSTED` episodic positive selection calls highlights the practical importance of neutral baseline calibration.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Software and data availability:** The `MSS` and `BUSTED-MSS` models are implemented in open-source `HyPhy` v2.5, and GenBank links and codon factor loadings for the 14 *Enterobacterales* genomes are provided in the SI Appendix.
* **Editorial fixes:** Correct the subscript typos on line 77, the algebraic regression formula on line 95, and the `Fig. 3A/B` vs. `Fig. 3B/C` panel references on lines 108–110.
