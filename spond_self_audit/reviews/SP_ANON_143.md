# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_143
**Composite Score:** 6.7 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.1/3.5 | Claims: 1.5/2.5 | Biology: 1.7/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.4/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Denominator & Arithmetic Error in Incidence Stratification
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Hybrid Computational + Wet-Lab Functional Validation
**Technology Maturity:** Mid-Stage High-Throughput
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

---

### 1. Executive Summary
This study uses 454 GS FLX Titanium ultradeep pyrosequencing (UDS) across three HIV-1 coding regions (`gag` p24, `pol` RT, and `env` C2-V3) to estimate the prevalence of intrasubtype B coinfection and the incidence of superinfection among 118 recently infected, antiretroviral therapy (ART)-naive men who have sex with men (MSM) in the San Diego Primary Infection Cohort. Applying a multi-step confirmation protocol requiring excessive maximum divergence (MDI > 5% in `env` or > 2.5% in `gag`/`pol`), bootstrap support > 90% for distinct phylogenetic clusters, BLAST screening against local reads, and independent RNA re-extraction and re-sequencing from a second plasma aliquot, the authors identify 7 baseline coinfections (5.9%) and 10 superinfections (overall cumulative dual infection prevalence 14.4%, overall superinfection incidence 4.96 per 100 person-years).

While the laboratory and bioinformatic confirmation pipeline for dual infection is rigorous and well-controlled against carryover contamination, the epidemiological incidence calculations contain severe internal mathematical contradictions and arithmetic errors. Specifically, the reported first-year superinfection incidence rate of 29.20 per 100 person-years lies outside its own reported 95% confidence interval (1.13–6.50) by an order of magnitude and is mathematically incompatible with the cohort's person-time structure, invalidating the central secondary claim that superinfection rates decline sharply between Year 1 and Year 2. Additional discrepancies in total follow-up time (201.6 vs. 215 person-years) and a 10-fold arithmetic discrepancy in the Early Test primary HIV incidence denominator require comprehensive recalculation before publication.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Incompatible Point Estimate, Confidence Interval, and Person-Time Denominator for Year-1 Superinfection:** In the Results, the authors report 6 superinfection cases in the first year after the estimated date of infection (EDI) and 4 in the second year, stating a *"first-year incidence rate of 29.20 per 100 person-years, 95% CI, 1.13–6.50"* and a *"second-year incidence rate of 9.25 per 100 person-years, 95% CI, 3.47–24.64."* First, a point estimate of 29.20 cannot lie outside its own 95% confidence interval (1.13–6.50). Second, 6 events yielding an incidence of 29.20 per 100 person-years implies a denominator of only $6 / 0.2920 = 20.55$ person-years in Year 1. However, the cohort includes 101 monoinfected and 10 superinfected participants ($N = 111$ at risk), all of whom met the inclusion criterion of deferring ART for at least 6 months after EDI, and enough of whom remained under follow-up in Year 2 to contribute $4 / 0.0925 = 43.24$ person-years during Year 2 alone. Every participant followed into Year 2 necessarily contributed a full 1.0 person-year during Year 1, meaning total Year-1 person-time at risk across the 111 participants must be at least $70\text{--}95$ person-years (yielding a true Year-1 incidence of $\sim 6.3\text{--}8.5$ per 100 person-years, lower than or comparable to Year 2). It appears the authors either computed the Year-1 denominator solely from participants whose follow-up terminated during Year 1 (omitting Year-1 person-time contributed by subjects censored in Year 2+) or suffered a decimal-shift error when transcribing rates and confidence bounds.
* **Contradictory Total Follow-Up Time Between Results and Discussion:** In the Results, overall superinfection incidence is calculated from 10 cases over **201.6 person-years** ($10 / 201.6 \times 100 = 4.96$ per 100 person-years). In the Discussion, the text states that *"10 superinfections were observed over **215 person-years**, giving an incidence of HIV-1 superinfection of 4.96 per 100 person-years"* ($10 / 215 \times 100 = 4.65$, not 4.96).
* **Ten-Fold Arithmetic Error in Primary HIV-1 Incidence:** In the Results, the authors state that among repeat Early Test clients, *"92 seroconverted over 21 065 person-years, resulting in an HIV incidence of 4.37 per 100 person-years (95% CI, 3.56–5.36)."* Dividing 92 by 21,065 person-years yields **0.437** per 100 person-years, not 4.37. Either the true person-time denominator was 2,106.5 person-years or the primary infection incidence rate is 10-fold lower than reported.
* **Midpoint Imputation of Superinfection Timing:** Estimating the timing of superinfection as the midpoint between the last monoinfected sample and the first dual-infected sample ignores interval censoring when sampling intervals widen (from every 4–12 weeks early to every 24 weeks after week 48), which can artificially compress events near interval boundaries and should be handled via interval-censored survival estimation.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Cumulative Dual Infection Prevalence (14.4%) and Overall Superinfection Rate (4.96/100 PY) [Well Supported]:** The UDS screening protocol, physical separation on 454 picoliter plates, temporal separation of same-patient runs, BLAST contamination checks, and mandatory confirmation in an independently extracted second plasma aliquot provide strong experimental protection against PCR recombination and cross-contamination artifacts.
* **Claim that Superinfection Incidence Declines Over Time Due to Developing Immune Protection [Methodologically Flawed / Overstated]:** Both the Abstract (*"however, superinfection rates declined over time... development of stronger immune responses to the initial infection may protect from superinfection"*) and the Discussion rely directly on the contrast between 29.20 per 100 person-years in Year 1 and 9.25 per 100 person-years in Year 2. Because the Year-1 rate of 29.20 per 100 person-years is an arithmetic/denominator artifact (and 6 events in Year 1 vs. 4 events in Year 2 over properly partitioned person-time shows no statistically significant decline), the immunological protection narrative is unsupported by the data.
* **Claim that Superinfection Incidence is Comparable to Primary HIV-1 Incidence [Fragile]:** If the Early Test denominator is indeed 2,106.5 person-years (4.37 per 100 PY), the rates are numerically similar; if the denominator is 21,065 person-years (0.437 per 100 PY), superinfection incidence in this cohort is an order of magnitude higher than primary incidence in the screening program.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data & Software Availability:** Processed UDS alignments are deposited in the LANL HIV-DB Next Generation Sequence Archive, and the bioinformatics pipeline is publicly available in HyPhy and Datamonkey.
* **Table and Unit Inconsistencies:** In Table 1, the last row labels `cDNA input` with units `median log 10 copies/mL (IQR)`, whereas the Methods and Results text specify that cDNA input is calculated per `10 μL` reaction volume (`log 10 copies/10 μL`). All person-time denominators (Year 1, Year 2, overall 201.6 vs. 215 PY, and Early Test 21,065 vs. 2,106.5 PY) and their exact Poisson/Byar confidence intervals must be audited and corrected across the text, Abstract, and Table 1.
