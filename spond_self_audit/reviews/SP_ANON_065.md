### 1. Executive Summary
This paper presents TopHap, a heuristic filtering and phylogenetic reconstruction pipeline designed to infer backbone trees of major haplotypes from massive low-diversity viral genomic datasets (applied to 68,057 first-year SARS-CoV-2 genomes [68KG] and 1,106,862 genomes through September 2021 [1MG]). TopHap partitions data into monthly and country-level spatiotemporal slices, retains alignment positions with minor allele frequency $> \text{maf}$ (e.g., 5% or 1%), collapses sequences to these $k$ positions, retains unique haplotypes exceeding frequency $> \text{hf}$ (5% or 1%) in any slice, pools them with outgroup sequences reduced to the same $k$ sites, and builds a Maximum Parsimony (MP) tree with bootstrap support derived by resampling genomes (rather than sites) within each spatiotemporal slice. The authors show that TopHap trees are concordant with Nextstrain, PANGO, and Mutation Order Approach (MOA) backbones and evaluate five early SARS-CoV-2 rooting scenarios after manually re-inserting a rare early variant ($x = \text{C29095U}$, global frequency 0.4%).

As a fast data-reduction heuristic for visualizing common pandemic clades on a laptop (<3 hours for 1.1 million genomes), TopHap is practical and effective. However, several methodological and inferential claims are overstated or insufficiently justified: (1) the genome-resampling bootstrap measures only sample-frequency stability above the $\text{hf}$ cutoff rather than phylogenetic character support for internal branches; (2) reducing divergent bat/pangolin outgroups (RaTG13, RmYN02, pangolin-CoV) solely to the 83 SARS-CoV-2 polymorphic sites and running unweighted MP ignores outgroup branch lengths and transitional saturation; (3) the core early-rooting analysis (Figure 7) required manually overriding TopHap's own frequency filter to inject site 29,095 (0.4% frequency); and (4) the mid-September to early-October 2019 tMRCA calculation is a back-of-the-envelope point division of 4 mutations by a published clock rate without modeling Poisson substitution variance or coalescent tree priors.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Misinterpretation of Genome-Resampling Bootstrap Support [Methodologically Flawed]:** Standard Felsenstein site-bootstrapping resamples alignment columns ($k$) to measure whether character evidence supports a clade against homoplasy. TopHap instead resamples $n$ genomes with replacement within each spatiotemporal slice, re-applies the $\text{maf}$ and $\text{hf}$ cutoffs, and prunes haplotypes that do not survive in 100% of replicates. When $n$ is large (tens of thousands of genomes) and a haplotype's sample frequency is well above 5% (say 15%), multinomial resampling of genomes will virtually always yield $>5\%$ frequency, producing the identical set of haplotypes and the identical MP tree on the fixed $k$ sites in 100% of replicates—even if a branch is supported by only a single homoplasious mutation. Thus, TopHap's ">95% or 100% bootstrap support" reflects multinomial frequency stability above the cutoff, not topological support across sites.
* **Outgroup Rooting on Ascertained Within-Lineage Polymorphisms Under Maximum Parsimony [Fragile]:** To root the 68KG tree, TopHap restricts the bat (RaTG13, RmYN02) and pangolin outgroup genomes to the $k = 83$ positions that are polymorphic at $>5\%$ within human SARS-CoV-2 and runs unweighted MP. Because bat/pangolin outgroups are $\sim 4\%–10\%$ divergent genome-wide (thousands of substitutions away) and SARS-CoV-2 early polymorphism is dominated by $\text{C}\to\text{U}$ hypermutation, outgroup states at those 83 sites are highly susceptible to homoplasy along the long outgroup branch, which unweighted MP cannot correct for.
* **Ad-Hoc Manual Pooling and Threshold Sensitivity [Moderately Supported]:** Spatiotemporal slicing requires arbitrary manual pooling of countries and months with $<500$ genomes, and changing the threshold from 5% to 1% on 1MG increases haplotypes from 150 to 594 and drops bootstrap values due to rare recombinant/homoplasious haplotypes. Crucially, the focal root variant $x$ (C29095U, frequency 0.4%) was excluded by TopHap at both 5% and 1% thresholds and had to be manually added based on external knowledge (Bloom 2021), demonstrating that automated high-frequency filtering strips out rare early basal lineages unless they are already known *a priori*.
* **Unpropagated Poisson Variance in Molecular Clock Dating [Overstated]:** On line 82, the authors infer that because Wuhan-1 is 4 mutations away from their MP MRCA (`proCoV2`), dividing 4 mutations by a clock rate of $6.64 \times 10^{-4}$ to $9.27 \times 10^{-4}$ subs/site/year dates the progenitor to "7.7–10.8 weeks before December 24, 2019 (mid-September to early-October 2019)." Dividing a discrete count of $k = 4$ mutations by a rate without accounting for the massive Poisson variance of a 4-event counting process ($\text{SD} = \sqrt{4} = 2$ mutations, giving a 95% CI spanning $\sim 1$ to $9$ mutations) or genealogical coalescent variance produces a spuriously narrow interval and overstates precision relative to full Bayesian phylodynamic models (Pekar et al. 2021).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Recovery of Major Pandemic Clade Backbone and VOC Placements [Well Supported]:** For major lineages (19A, 19B, 20A–20C, and WHO Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, Lambda), TopHap's 5%-filtered haplotype tree accurately recovers the macro-evolutionary branching order of Nextstrain and PANGO in a fraction of the runtime.
* **Distinguishing Alternative Early SARS-CoV-2 Rooting Scenarios [Fragile]:** In Figure 7, scenarios A, B, and C all require exactly 9 parsimony steps (with one homoplasy/reversion in either $\alpha_1$ or $x$) and differ in log-likelihood by $<1.7$ units, while scenario D (PANGO A/B sister rooting) requires 10 steps (1 additional step). A difference of 1 parsimony step or $\Delta \ln L < 1.7$ on 8 ascertained sites is statistically indistinguishable from noise and cannot definitively refute scenario D.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Code and Data Availability:** TopHap source code is available on GitHub (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`), and GISAID data sources and filtering criteria are documented.
* **Simulation Benchmarking Gap:** While Figure 1 presents a 3-site cartoon schematic to motivate haplotype filtering, the manuscript includes no quantitative simulation benchmark (e.g., coalescent/epidemiological trees simulated with realistic sequencing error rates and $\text{C}\to\text{U}$ homoplasy) comparing TopHap's topological accuracy against UShER, IQ-TREE2, or MAPLE.
