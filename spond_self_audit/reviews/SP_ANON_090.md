### 1. Executive Summary
This concise mathematical and empirical note proves a general theorem regarding equiprobable discrete approximations of non-negative rate distributions (such as Yang's standard $K$-category discrete gamma model of site-to-site rate heterogeneity): for any non-negative discrete random variable $X$ taking $K$ equiprobable values $0 \le X_1 \le \dots \le X_K$ with mean $\mu$, the variance is strictly bounded above by $\text{Var}(X) \le (K - 1)\mu^2$ (and higher central moments satisfy $\mathbb{E}[(X - \mu)^n] \le (K - 1)^{n-1}\mu^n$), which caps the empirical coefficient of variation at $\text{CV}(X) \le \sqrt{K - 1}$. Evaluating this bound across 721 metazoan mitochondrial gene alignments (13 protein-coding genes across 56 orders) and 1,000 nuclear gene alignments from Selectome in HyPhy across $K \in \{3, 4, 5, 7, 10\}$, the authors demonstrate that standard equiprobable discrete-gamma estimates of nonsynonymous and synonymous CVs frequently hit the hard $\sqrt{K - 1}$ ceiling ($13.7\%$ of mtDNA alignments and $4.1\%$ of nuclear alignments at $K = 3$) and exhibit severe range compression even when $K = 10$ compared to an unconstrained General Discrete Distribution (GDD) where both rates and category weights are estimated.

This is a sharp, mathematically exact, and practically important methodological contribution. The proof is elementary and airtight, the empirical demonstration across 1,721 alignments is clear, and the practical recommendations (distinguishing between likelihood-surface approximation where $K = 4\text{--}5$ discrete gamma suffices vs. moment/CV estimation where GDD or large $K$ is required, while noting the $2K - 2$ parameter variance cost of GDD) are balanced and well-reasoned.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Mathematical Proof of the Moment and CV Bounds (Pillar 3):** Given $\Pr(X = X_i) = 1/K$, $X_i \ge 0$, and $\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^K X_i = \mu$, we have $\sum_{i=1}^K X_i^2 \le \left(\sum_{i=1}^K X_i\right)^2 = K^2 \mu^2$ with equality if and only if $K - 1$ categories equal $0$ and one category equals $K\mu$. Dividing by $K$ and subtracting $\mu^2$ immediately yields $\text{Var}(X) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^K X_i^2 - \mu^2 \le K\mu^2 - \mu^2 = (K - 1)\mu^2$, so $\text{CV}(X) = \sqrt{\text{Var}(X)}/\mu \le \sqrt{K - 1}$. The derivation is exact and holds regardless of whether quantile medians or category means are used to discretize the underlying continuous density, provided the $K$ categories are assigned equal prior weight $1/K$ and rescaled to mean $\mu$.
* **Variance-Bias Trade-off in GDD at Large $K$ (Pillars 3 & 4):** While GDD removes the $\sqrt{K - 1}$ ceiling by allowing a tiny fraction of sites $p_K \ll 1/K$ to take a large rate $X_K$, estimating $2(K - 1)$ free parameters on finite alignments (especially short mitochondrial genes of $100\text{--}300$ codons) can produce boundary estimates where a single hypervariable codon receives weight $p_K \approx 1/L$ and a huge rate $X_K$, inflating the sample variance $\sum p_i (X_i - \mu)^2$ upward due to finite-sample estimation noise. The authors acknowledge this trade-off qualitatively in line 33 (*"one could rather quickly find a number of parameters in their model that are doing little more than adding unnecessary variability to estimates"*), though a brief parametric simulation with known true $\text{CV} > \sqrt{K - 1}$ comparing the RMSE of discrete-gamma plug-in ($1/\sqrt{\hat{\alpha}}$), discrete-gamma empirical moment, and GDD empirical moment estimators would have quantified how much of the wider GDD spread in Figure 1 is reduced negative bias vs. positive finite-sample variance.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Empirical Prevalence of Ceiling Truncation in Codon Rate Variation [Well Supported]:** Because nonsynonymous substitution rates in protein-coding genes are typically L-shaped (the vast majority of sites under strong purifying selection $\omega \approx 0$ and a small fraction of sites evolving neutrally or adaptively $\omega \ge 1$), the true nonsynonymous $\text{CV}$ routinely exceeds $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$ ($K = 3$) or $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$ ($K = 4$). The empirical finding that $13.7\%$ of mtDNA genes hit the exact $\sqrt{2}$ wall at $K = 3$ directly validates the theoretical bound in real biological applications.
* **Practical Guidance on When Discrete Gamma Suffices vs. Fails [Well Supported]:** The conclusion (line 35) avoids overstating the impact on tree topology inference—where rate variation acts as a nuisance parameter—and focuses squarely on studies that interpret the estimated rate distribution moments or empirical Bayes site rates.
