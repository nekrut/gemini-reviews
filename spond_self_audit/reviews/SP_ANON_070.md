# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_070
**Composite Score:** 6.4 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.0/3.5 | Claims: 1.7/2.5 | Biology: 1.4/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.3/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Overfitted Multivariate Logistic Regression (Events-Per-Variable Violation) & Uncorrected Multiple Testing
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
This cross-sectional observational study examines behavioral and substance-use correlates of transmitted HIV-1 drug resistance (TDR) among 117 recently HIV-infected men who have sex with men (MSM) enrolled in the Southern California Acute Infection and Early Disease Research Program (SC-AIEDRP) between 2002 and 2006 (selected from 225 MSM who completed computer-assisted self-interview [CASI] questionnaires). Baseline serum samples were assayed for phenotypic resistance (PR, $n=117$, Monogram PhenoSense $\text{FC} \ge 10$) and genotypic resistance (GR, $n=112$, Stanford score $\ge 60$), identifying 16 PR cases (13.7%) and 14 GR cases (12.5%; $\kappa = 0.87$). Fitting separate multivariate logistic regression models for each substance used during sex with the 3 most recent partners—adjusting for age, race/ethnicity, total partner count in the past 12 months, known HIV-positive partner, and prior ART exposure—the authors report statistically significant adjusted odds ratios (aORs) between PR and any substance use ($\text{aOR} = 4.21$), polysubstance use ($\text{aOR} = 5.64$), methamphetamine ($\text{aOR} = 4.00$), MDMA/Ecstasy ($\text{aOR} = 7.16$), and GHB ($\text{aOR} = 6.98$).

While the study addresses an important public health question using standardized resistance assays and CASI questionnaires, the statistical modeling suffers from severe small-sample overfitting (fitting 6+ predictors to only 14 GR and 16 PR outcome events, violating the Peduzzi events-per-variable rule and inducing sparse-data bias that inflates adjusted ORs above unadjusted ORs), lack of correction for multiple comparisons across 9 correlated substance exposures, and unmodeled transmission cluster non-independence.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Events-Per-Variable (EPV) Violation and Sparse-Data Overfitting in Multivariate Logistic Regression [Methodologically Flawed]:** The dataset contains only 14 GR events (out of $N=112$) and 16 PR events (out of $N=117$). Yet each multivariate logistic regression model adjusts simultaneously for 6 covariates (and more parameters if race/ethnicity is multi-categorical): age, race/ethnicity, total sex partners in the past 12 months, HIV-positive partner, prior ART exposure ($n=3$), plus the focal substance variable—yielding an EPV of $\le 2.3$ (far below the minimum standard of 10 events per parameter). For rare exposures like Ecstasy (only 3 GR cases and 6 non-GR cases) and GHB (5 GR cases), maximum likelihood logistic regression suffers from classic finite-sample/sparse-data upward bias. This is directly visible in Table 1: every single unadjusted OR increases substantially upon adding the 5 adjustment covariates (e.g., Any substance use PR non-aOR = $2.34$ [0.76–7.21, non-significant] jumps to aOR = $4.21$ [1.13–15.68]; Polysubstance PR non-aOR = $3.76$ jumps to aOR = $5.64$; Ecstasy PR non-aOR = $5.28$ jumps to aOR = $7.16$ with a 95% CI of $1.40$ to $36.59$; GHB PR non-aOR = $4.46$ jumps to aOR = $6.98$ with a 95% CI of $1.82$ to $26.80$). Exact logistic regression or Firth's penalized likelihood with at most 1–2 covariates is required for 14–16 events.
* **Inclusion of Participants with "Previous Exposure to Antiretroviral Medications ($n=3$)" in a Transmitted Resistance Study [Fragile]:** The Abstract and Introduction frame the outcome as "transmitted HIV drug resistance" in "treatment-naive recently HIV-infected MSM." However, the Table 1 footnote and line 107 reveal that the multivariate models controlled for "previous exposure to antiretroviral medications ($n=3$)" (presumably PEP or PrEP/monotherapy). Including ART-exposed individuals in an analysis of *transmitted* drug resistance—and introducing a $n=3$ binary indicator into an already overparameterized logistic model with 14–16 events—confounds acquired vs. transmitted resistance and destabilizes the regression estimates.
* **Collinearity of Polysubstance Use and Unadjusted Multiple Testing [Moderately Supported]:** Because 35% of the cohort (69% of all substance users) engaged in polysubstance use, individual indicators for methamphetamine, MDMA/Ecstasy, and GHB are heavily confounded with one another; fitting separate regressions for each substance without mutually adjusting or correcting $p$-values across the 9 tested substance variables overstates the independent effect of MDMA and GHB.
* **Selection from 225 to 117 Participants and Transmission Cluster Non-Independence [Moderately Supported]:** Only 117 of 225 MSM who completed CASI questionnaires were analyzed (those with both resistance data and 3-partner module responses); no comparison of baseline characteristics between the 117 included and 108 excluded participants is provided. Furthermore, because protease and RT sequences were already generated for all 112 GR subjects, the authors did not check whether methamphetamine/GHB users with TDR belonged to a single linked phylogenetic transmission cluster (founder effect), which would violate the independence assumption of logistic regression.
* **Text vs. Table 1 Percentage Discrepancies [Minor]:** In the Results text (line 72), the authors write: "GR (54% vs. 30%; P = 0.09), and PR (63% vs. 31%; P = 0.01)" for polysubstance use, whereas Table 1 reports GR polysubstance use as $8/14 = 57.1\%$ vs. $30/98 = 30.6\%$, and PR as $10/16 = 62.5\%$ vs. $31/101 = 30.7\%$. Also in Table 1, Cocaine GR is listed as $1$ ($7.7\%$), whereas $1/14 = 7.1\%$ (likely a missing value reducing denominator to 13, not footnoted).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Concordance of Genotypic and Phenotypic Resistance Assays [Well Supported]:** Pairing population sequencing (Stanford score $\ge 60$) with recombinant phenotypic susceptibility testing (PhenoSense $\text{FC} \ge 10$) and demonstrating high concordance ($\kappa = 0.87$) provides a reliable biological endpoint.
* **Network Mixing vs. Physiological Susceptibility Interpretation [Well Supported]:** The authors appropriately test and rule out baseline CD4 count ($p=0.16$) and viral load ($p=0.54$) differences between substance users and non-users, and reasonably interpret the association between club-drug use (methamphetamine, GHB, MDMA) and TDR as reflecting assortative sexual mixing within higher-risk, anonymous partner networks rather than direct physiological enhancement of resistant-virus transmission.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Blinding Note:** Author names and affiliations remained unredacted in the markdown file header (lines 7–27); the manuscript was evaluated strictly on its internal statistical and epidemiological design.
* **Data and Code Availability:** Neither the de-identified individual-level behavioral table nor the baseline HIV-1 *pol* sequences ($n=112$) are deposited in a public repository or supplementary file.
