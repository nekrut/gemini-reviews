# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_156
**Composite Score:** 8.4 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.8/3.5 | Claims: 2.1/2.5 | Biology: 1.9/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.6/2.0)
**Bias Category:** None (Methodologically Sound)
**Verdict:** Accept (Minor Revision)
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

### 1. Executive Summary
`SP_ANON_156` constructs a comprehensive amino-acid variability, coevolution, and structural reference profile of the HIV-1 Group M capsid (CA, 231 residues) across 21,012 one-per-person sequences compiled from 180 publications, motivated by the clinical introduction of the long-acting capsid inhibitor lenacapavir. The authors develop an Expectation-Maximization (EM) sequence quality filter based on APOBEC3G/F hypermutation signatures and rare ("unusual") amino acid variants, quantify residue-level Shannon entropy across major subtypes (`A1, B, C, D, F1, G, CRF01_AE, CRF02_AG`), assess correlations with subtype divergence and published HLA/CTL escape associations, infer 58 coevolving residue pairs via Bayesian Graphical Models (BGM) in HyPhy, and map conservation onto hexameric/pentameric CA crystal and cryo-EM structures (`3H4E`, `3J34`, `5I4T`, `5L93`) as well as known lenacapavir resistance loci. This is a thorough, clinically useful reference resource, with minor methodological caveats regarding literature-count proxies for CTL selection and a numerical typo in the lenacapavir binding-site residue list.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Literature-Count Proxy for CTL Selection Pressure:** To test whether host CTL pressure drives capsid variability, the authors assign each CA position a score from `0 to 8` corresponding to the number of published studies (among 8 cohort studies) reporting an HLA association at that site, then correlate this study count with Shannon entropy ($\rho = 0.43, p = 7.4 \times 10^{-12}$) and fit a rank-based regression (`Rfit`) on the 70 positions with $\ge 1\%$ mutation prevalence (`Lines 77–81, 122`). Counting the number of publications reporting an HLA association conflates biological selection intensity with study cohort overlap and HLA allele frequency bias (e.g., canonical subtype B/C epitopes restricted by well-studied alleles like `HLA-B*57` or `B*27` are repeatedly rediscovered across cohorts). Moreover, restricting the rank regression to the 70 most variable positions conditions on the dependent variable (entropy).
- **Potential Circularity in "Unusual Mutation" Quality Filtering:** The EM quality filter (`Lines 36–46`) removes sequences containing $\ge 8$ "unusual mutations," defined as variants with global prevalence $<0.1\%$ in the pooled dataset. Because subtype C (42.8%) and subtype B (30.5%) comprise nearly three-quarters of the dataset, subtype-specific polymorphisms in poorly sampled subtypes (`F1, G, H, J, K`) risk being classified as $<0.1\%$ global "unusual mutations," potentially over-filtering legitimate divergent non-B/C sequences.
- **Internal Numerical Discrepancy in Lenacapavir Binding-Site Positions (`Line 105`):** Line 105 states that lenacapavir contacts 20 CA positions (`37, 38, 41, 50, 53, 54, 57, 59, 63, 69, 73, 106, 130, 135, 169, 172, 173, 179, 182, and 183`), and immediately follows with: *"Fourteen of these 20 positions had a conservation level ≥99.5% and contained only conservative mutations, while positions **31**, 41, 50, 54, 179, and 183 contained one or more non-conservative mutations."* Position `31` is not among the 20 listed lenacapavir-contacting residues (it is almost certainly a typographical error for position `37`).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Over two-thirds (70.1%) of HIV-1 Group M capsid positions are $\ge 99\%$ conserved, with intrapentameric and intrahexameric interface residues exhibiting strong structural constraint (`[Well Supported]`).** Mapping Shannon entropy onto four experimental CA quaternary structures demonstrates clear enrichment of conserved residues at multimerization interfaces, the prime-side $\beta$-hairpin, and the major homology region (MHR), contrasted with higher entropy on solvent-exposed helices and loops.
- **Claim 2: Lenacapavir resistance-associated positions (`L56, N57, M66, Q67, K70, N74, A105, T107`) are conserved ($\ge 99.5\%$) across ART-naive Group M subtypes (`[Well Supported]`).** The empirical prevalence table across 21,012 sequences confirms that canonical lenacapavir resistance mutations are virtually absent ($<0.1\%$) in capsid-inhibitor-naive populations across all major subtypes.
- **Claim 3: Subtype-specific conservation (`chi-square`) and CTL escape (`HLA study count`) independently explain CA residue variability (`[Moderately Supported]`).** Both factors clearly correlate with site entropy, though as noted above, publication counts are an indirect proxy for CTL selection strength and subtype allelic differences partly reflect founder fixations of ancestral CTL escape variants.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Web Resource Availability:** The curated mutation prevalence dataset and interactive query tools (`Calib`, `Caprot`, sequence quality control) have been integrated into the public Stanford HIV Drug Resistance Database (`HIVDB`), and extensive supplementary tables (S1–S4) document mutation prevalences, BGM coevolution pairs, and structural mappings.
- **Code Availability:** Standard statistical packages (`Rfit`, HyPhy `BGM`) are cited, though custom scripts used for the GenBank BLAST harvesting and EM mixture model filtering are not deposited in a standalone code repository.
