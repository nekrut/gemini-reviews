### 1. Executive Summary
This public health surveillance report describes the nationwide expansion (from 27 jurisdictions in 2016 to 59 state, tribal, local, and territorial health departments by 2018) and operational outcomes (2018–2023) of molecular HIV cluster detection and response (CDR) in the United States using quarterly national CDC runs of HIV-TRACE and monthly local runs of Secure HIV-TRACE. Applying a $0.5\%$ pairwise Tamura–Nei genetic distance threshold on a $1,497\text{ nt}$ HIV-1 *pol* alignment among persons diagnosed within the preceding 3 years—with national priority criteria of $\ge 5$ diagnoses in the prior 12 months ($\ge 3$ in low-burden jurisdictions with $<200$ annual diagnoses)—CDC identified 404 national priority clusters during 2018–2023 (median size 7 at detection, growing to median 13 by December 2023), of which $325$ ($80\%$) spanned multiple jurisdictions. Concurrently, during 2020–2023, health departments submitted cluster report forms for 403 newly detected clusters, $298$ ($74\%$) of which were first identified via molecular analysis and $248/298$ ($83\%$) first detected locally via monthly Secure HIV-TRACE analysis.

As a descriptive public health implementation and surveillance evaluation, the manuscript is clearly structured, transparent about operational trade-offs (timeliness of monthly local analyses vs. cross-jurisdictional visibility of quarterly national deduplicated datasets), and grounded in well-defined cluster prioritization rules. The primary quantitative limitations are the $52\%$ national sequence completeness (meaning nearly half of newly diagnosed HIV infections lack a reported sequence $>100\text{ bp}$), the inclusion of sequences as short as $101\text{ bp}$ in sequence completeness statistics vs. the $1,497\text{ nt}$ *pol* target, and a minor numerical discrepancy between the Abstract and Results.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Impact of $52\%$ Sequence Completeness on Cluster Detection Sensitivity (Pillar 2):** Sequence completeness across the 59 jurisdictions rose from $41\%$ (2016–2018) to $52\%$ (2021–2023), with a median 34-day (IQR 20–50) lag from sample collection to health department receipt in 2023 (line 74). Because molecular cluster detection requires at least $\ge 5$ (or $\ge 3$) linked sequences within a 12-month window at $d \le 0.005$, missing $48\%$ of new diagnoses quadratically reduces the probability of observing edges and causes substantial false-negative omission of active transmission clusters in jurisdictions with low clinical resistance-testing uptake or delayed electronic laboratory reporting. While the manuscript reports the aggregate $52\%$ completeness, it does not report the inter-jurisdictional range or IQR of sequence completeness across the 59 health departments, which likely drives part of the regional variation in cluster detection (Figure 3).
* **Mismatch Between Sequence Completeness Definition ($>100\text{ bp}$) and Network Minimum Overlap (Pillar 3):** In the Methods (line 69), sequence completeness is defined as the percentage of diagnosed persons with a reported sequence $>100\text{ bp}$, whereas HIV-TRACE aligns a $1,497\text{ nt}$ *pol* segment (line 34) and requires a minimum overlap (typically $500\text{ nt}$ in standard HIV-TRACE) to compute reliable $0.5\%$ pairwise distances. Counting sequences between $101\text{ bp}$ and $499\text{ bp}$ as "complete" inflates the effective sequence coverage usable for cluster inference.
* **Retention of Multiple Sequences per Person in Quarterly National Runs (Pillar 3):** Line 34 notes that national quarterly cluster detection uses *"all available HIV sequences of suitable quality, which can include multiple sequences per person."* While cluster size is tallied by unique persons (after eHARS deduplication), retaining longitudinal sequences per person in the distance graph can bridge two individuals whose baseline sequences exceed $0.5\%$ distance if an intermediate or convergent longitudinal sample falls within $0.5\%$ of both.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Complementarity of Monthly Local and Quarterly National Molecular Surveillance [Well Supported]:** The data clearly demonstrate both limbs of the operational rationale: $83\%$ ($248/298$) of health-department-reported molecular clusters were first detected locally via monthly Secure HIV-TRACE runs before quarterly national reconciliation, while $80\%$ ($325/404$) of national priority clusters eventually involved $>1$ jurisdiction ($11\%$ having $\ge 2$ jurisdictions with "substantial involvement" of $\ge 3$ recent cases or no single jurisdiction with $\ge 3$ cases), which isolated local sandboxes cannot link.
* **Ethical, Privacy, and Governance Framework [Well Supported]:** The discussion (line 120) candidly addresses community concerns regarding HIV criminalization, explaining the technical separation of PII (kept behind local eHARS firewalls and stripped prior to Secure HIV-TRACE upload) and federal Assurance of Confidentiality restrictions.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data & Code Availability:** Because NHSS surveillance records are protected under a federal Assurance of Confidentiality and individual-level sequence release is prohibited without consent, raw patient sequences are appropriately restricted. The underlying open-source engine (`HIV-TRACE`) is publicly available.
* **Numerical Discrepancy Between Abstract and Main Text:**
  * Abstract line 6 states: *"During 2020–2023, HDs reported 298 molecular clusters to CDC; **249** were first detected by HDs."*
  * Results line 99 states: *"Of those, 298 (74%) were first detected through national or state/local molecular analysis; **248** (83%) of 298 were first detected through state/local analysis... and 224 (90%) of **248** met national priority criteria."*
