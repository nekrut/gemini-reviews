# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_062
**Composite Score:** 7.6 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.6/3.5 | Claims: 2.0/2.5 | Biology: 1.5/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.5/2.0)
**Bias Category:** High-Dimensional Multiple Testing / Post-Hoc Hypothesis Generation
**Verdict:** Minor Revision
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mid-Stage High-Throughput
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

### 1. Executive Summary
This study conducts an exploratory full-proteome genomic sieve analysis of breakthrough HIV-1 CRF01_AE infections ($n = 109$ subjects; 43 vaccine, 66 placebo; median 10 sequences/subject) from the phase III RV144 HIV-1 vaccine trial ($N = 16,395$ MITT cohort, overall VE = 31%). Extending a prior V1/V2-targeted sieve analysis, the authors apply a broad battery of site-scanning methods (DVE, GWJ, MBS, EGWJ, MMB), 9-mer scanning (KmerScan), continuous distance-based hazard modeling (SmoothMarks), physicochemical property tests (PCP and codon-level PRIME), covarying site networks, and HLA-restricted T-cell epitope escape predictors (EscapeCount, PercentEpitopeMismatch, EpitopeDistance) across both vaccine-immunogen regions (Env-gp120, Gag, Pro) and non-vaccine proteins. The scan flags 56 amino acid "signature sites" (19 in vaccine immunogens, 37 outside) and 119 9-mers at unadjusted $p \le 0.05$.

The manuscript is notable for its candor in acknowledging its own negative and null-calibrated findings: the authors explicitly conclude in the Abstract and Discussion that because only 1 of 19 vaccine-region sites (Pol 51) passes the pre-specified $q < 0.20$ FDR threshold, because twice as many nominal signatures appear in non-vaccine proteins (37 vs. 19), and because half of the nominal signatures are "vMismatch" effects (greater divergence from the vaccine insert in placebo than in vaccine recipients), few of the detected whole-genome differences represent true vaccine-induced immune pressure. Nevertheless, the study suffers from tension between this rigorous null conclusion and lengthy post-hoc biological storytelling around unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ hits (such as single-site VE elimination claims and enrichment tests on 9-to-12 nominal sites).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Multiplicity Burden Across Sites, Reference Strains, and Methods [Moderately Supported]:** Across 453 variable vaccine-region sites (evaluated against up to 3 reference immunogens: 92TH023, CM244, MN/LAI) and 683 non-vaccine sites (evaluated against CON-AE), defining a "signature site" as achieving unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ in *at least one* of three correlated primary methods (DVE, GWJ, MBS) against *any* reference sequence guarantees puluhan of false positives under the global null. Observing 19 nominal hits in 453 vaccine sites (4.2%) and 37 nominal hits in 683 non-vaccine sites (5.4%) is entirely consistent with the uniform null distribution of $p$-values under zero proteome-wide sieve effect outside V2.
* **Post-Hoc Selection Circularity in Composite Decapeptide SmoothMarks Curve [Fragile]:** In the Results (S3 and S4 Figs), the authors select the 10 Env-gp120 signature sites that already had $p \le 0.05$ for vaccine-vs-placebo divergence, combine them into a composite decapeptide genetic distance, and re-run SmoothMarks on the same 109 subjects to show that VE peaks at 59% and declines to 0%. Constructing a composite distance exclusively from sites pre-selected for treatment-group differences in the same dataset introduces classic feature-selection circularity ("double dipping").
* **Enrichment Testing on Nominal Hits and Table/Text Discrepancy [Moderately Supported]:** Table 6 tests whether the 12 nominal Env-gp120 signature sites are enriched in 6 antibody-related site sets via Fisher's exact test. While this provides useful structural context, two of the 12 sites (Env 169 and 181) were already known from the prior V1/V2 study that motivated the trial's follow-up assays, and the Hotspots set was itself derived from peptide microarrays on RV144 vaccinees. Furthermore, there is a numerical discrepancy between the main text ("Fisher's exact test $p = 0.0035$" for `nAb-sites`, and "$p = 0.0049$" for `Focus` in the text vs. $p = 0.0021$ in the Abstract) and Table 6 (which lists `nAb-sites` as $p = 0.0001$, `Hotspots` as $p = 0.0049$, and `Focus` as $p = 0.0021$).
* **Conditioning on Infection vs. MITT Time-to-Event Sieve Conditions [Well Supported]:** The Methods clearly distinguish between MITT time-to-event methods (DVE, SmoothMarks) that estimate prospective acquisition hazard ratios and case-only methods (GWJ, MBS, PCP, PRIME) that condition on infection and cannot separate acquisition barriers from post-acquisition within-host selective pressure over the 6-monthly diagnostic testing intervals.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Lack of Proteome-Wide Sieve Signal Outside Known V2 Epitopes [Well Supported]:** The authors' central negative conclusion—that signature sites are not preferentially restricted to vaccine immunogens, fail $q < 0.20$ FDR correction (except Pol 51 and out-of-vaccine Tat/Vpu 9-mers), and show equal proportions of vMatch (10/19) and vMismatch (10/19) effects—is well grounded and appropriately prevents over-claiming whole-genome vaccine sieve effects.
* **Single-Mutation Vaccine Efficacy Elimination Claims [Overstated]:** In the Results and Discussion, the authors state that "VE can be eliminated or greatly strengthened with the mutation of a single residue" based on DVE point estimates (Table 3–4). Because these 56 sites were selected from >1,100 sites precisely for having extreme DVE/GWJ/MBS statistics ($p \le 0.05$), their point estimates of genotype-specific VE suffer from severe Winner's Curse (selection bias). Equally extreme single-site VE swings (e.g., VE = 68% vs. -13% at Nef 125; VE = 62% vs. -24% at Vif 31, Table 4) occur in non-vaccine proteins that could not possibly be targeted by the vaccine.
* **Covariation Hub Analysis [Moderately Supported]:** Finding that the 12 Env signature sites covary with more sites (mean 32.1 vs. 0.9, $p < 0.0001$) is biologically plausible given surface loop flexibility, but potentially confounded by site entropy: site-scanning tests have higher statistical power at polymorphic/high-entropy sites, and high-entropy sites also have higher power to reach $p < 0.05$ in pairwise covariation tests unless site entropy is explicitly controlled as a covariate.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data and Code Availability:** Sanger sequences for the 109 subjects are deposited in GenBank (`JX446645–JX448316`), and complete site-level/k-mer output tables are provided in S1–S4 Datasets. Custom statistical methods (GWJ, MBS, SmoothMarks, PRIME) are linked to published R packages or HyPhy modules.
* **Internal Numerical Consistency:** Aside from the text-vs-Table 6 Fisher's exact test $p$-value swap noted above (`nAb-sites` $p=0.0035$ in text vs. $0.0001$ in Table 6; `Focus` $p=0.0049$ in text vs. $0.0021$ in Table 6 and Abstract) and Table 2 vs. Table 4 listing Vif 22 in Table 4 (`DVE p = 0.046`) but omitting Vif 22 from Table 2, the tables and survival estimates are thoroughly documented.
