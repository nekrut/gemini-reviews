### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the origin, duplication dynamics, and selective regimes of 5,525 ciliate-restricted ("lineage-specific") gene families (LSGFs) assembled from 46 transcriptomes and 12 annotated somatic genomes across eight ciliate classes, grouped into four genome-architecture categories: Extensive Fragmenters (EF), Non-Extensive Fragmenters (NEF), Heterotrichea (He), and Karyorelictea (Ka). Using OrthoFinder2, PhyloToL, Count (Dollo parsimony), GeneRax (undated duplication-loss reconciliation), and partitioned codon models in HyPhy (group-level RELAX and BUSTED[S]), the authors conclude that: (1) $69.7\%$ of LSGFs are restricted to a single ciliate class; (2) EF taxa with scrambled germline genomes harbor roughly twice as many, smaller LSGFs as non-scrambled relatives; and (3) among 1,414 multi-group LSGFs, EF branches experience stronger selection intensity ($K < 1$ in non-EF groups relative to EF in 209/224 well-fitting RELAX alignments) and higher rates of episodic diversifying selection (BUSTED[S]: $24.5\%$ in EF vs. $12.0\text{--}15.5\%$ in NEF/He/Ka for matched branch-count bins).

While the biological question linking ciliate macronuclear fragmentation and germline unscrambling to gene family evolution is compelling and the HyPhy partitioned branch-site analyses (RELAX and BUSTED[S] stratified by branch count) are thoughtfully applied, the gene-family birth, size, and duplication inferences are heavily confounded by an extreme modality imbalance across clades (12 whole genomes vs. 46 single-stage vegetative transcriptomes) and several internal contradictions and template placeholders in the manuscript text.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Confounding of Gene Family Births (Dollo Parsimony) and Duplications (GeneRax) by Data Modality (Pillars 2 & 3):** Twelve taxa have complete annotated somatic genomes (8 of 11 in Oligohymenophorea, 3 of 14 in Spirotrichea, 1 of 13 in Heterotrichea, and 0 in Karyorelictea, Armophorea, Litostomatea, Colpodea, and Nassophorea), while the remaining 46 taxa are represented only by vegetative RNA-seq (often single-cell transcriptomes). Under Dollo parsimony in Count, an LSGF is placed at the lowest common ancestor of the species in which it is *detected*. Because stage-specific or lowly expressed paralogs are systematically missed in single-stage transcriptomes, an ancient ciliate gene family expressed during conjugation or encystment will be detected in whole-genome species (*Oxytricha*, *Stylonychia*, *Tetrahymena*, *Paramecium*) plus whichever sporadic transcriptome happened to express it, artificially pushing Dollo "births" and GeneRax "duplication pulses" toward genome-sequenced tips and clades (as visible in Figure 3).
* **Contradictory Direction of Effect Between Conserved Gene Families and LSGFs (Pillars 1 & 3):** In the Abstract and Introduction (lines 6, 18, 62, 87), the authors emphasize their prior findings that extensive fragmentation and germline scrambling produce *larger* gene families with *more relaxed* selection. In the present study, they find the exact opposite for LSGFs: scrambled EF taxa have *smaller* LSGFs (mean $1.799$ vs. $2.064\text{--}2.516$ genes/LSGF, line 62; overall ANOVA across the 4 groups is non-significant at $p = 0.427$, line 46) and *intensified* (stronger) rather than relaxed selection relative to NEF, He, and Ka in RELAX (Figure 4, line 70; though line 97 in the Discussion contradicts line 70 by stating *"We also found that the EF clade possessed the greatest proportion of young gene families experiencing relaxed selection"*).
* **Uncontrolled Phylogenetic Non-Independence in Scrambled vs. Non-Scrambled Comparisons (Pillar 4):** In lines 62–64, the comparison of LSGF counts between scrambled Spirotrichea ($\sim 1,039$ LSGFs) and non-scrambled *Euplotes* ($634$), Armophorea ($425$), and Litostomatea ($559$) is reported as raw group means without a phylogenetic generalized least squares (PGLS) or phylogenetic ANOVA test, despite all scrambled Spirotrichea belonging to a single nested subclade (Hypotrichia/Stichotrichia; Figure 3).
* **Filtering of Short ORFs ($<200$ aa) in De Novo / Young Gene Discovery (Pillar 2):** Retaining only complete ORFs $\ge 200$ amino acids ($\ge 600\text{ bp}$, line 27) and within $10\text{--}90\text{th}$ percentile GC3 of 200 conserved core genes (line 31) is a useful contamination screen against bacterial food sources, but systematically excludes genuinely young/de novo eukaryotic genes, which are overwhelmingly short ($<150$ aa) and compositionally atypical.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Elevated Episodic Diversifying Selection in EF Lineages Controlling for Branch Count [Moderately Supported]:** Because BUSTED[S] power scales with the number of foreground branches, binning LSGFs by foreground branch count (increments of 5; Table 1 shows mean $14.4\text{--}14.6$ branches for EF, NF, HE) is a sound control showing higher EDS detection in EF ($24.5\%$) than NF ($15.5\%$) or HE ($12.9\%$).
* **Germline Scrambling Drives Higher Numbers of Smaller LSGFs [Fragile]:** Heavily confounded by the fact that all scrambled spirotrichs form a single clade containing two of the three spirotrich reference genomes (*Oxytricha trifallax* and *Stylonychia lemnae*) and that overall LSGF size differences across the four architecture classes are statistically non-significant ($p = 0.427$).
* **RELAX Selection Intensity Direction [Contradictory Between Results and Discussion]:** Results line 70 and Figure 4 state that NEF, He, and Ka have $K < 1$ (relaxed selection) relative to the EF reference (meaning selection is *stronger/intensified* in EF), whereas Discussion line 97 states that the EF clade possessed the greatest proportion of young gene families experiencing *relaxed* selection.
