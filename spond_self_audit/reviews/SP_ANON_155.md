# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_155
**Composite Score:** 6.8 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.1/3.5 | Claims: 1.6/2.5 | Biology: 1.7/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.4/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Asymmetric Subsampling Bias & Topological Over-Interpretation Without Formal Phylogeographic Reconstruction
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
`SP_ANON_155` investigates the evolutionary history and global dispersal of HIV-1 subtype F (specifically F1) using 531 publicly available `pol` sequences (>900 nt, sampled 1987–2007) retrieved from the Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) HIV Sequence Database, with a particular focus on the relationship between the Romanian epidemic (including the late-1980s nosocomial pediatric outbreak) and sequences from Angola and South America. After screening out inter-subtype recombinants with SCUEAL, the authors infer a maximum-likelihood phylogeny of 606 sequences in PhyML (GTR+$\Gamma_4$) and perform relaxed-clock Bayesian coalescent dating in BEAST v1.5.3 on a downsampled alignment of 156 sequences (plus a Romania-only subset). While the study appropriately filters inter-subtype recombinants and transparently reports a dramatic sensitivity of molecular clock estimates to outgroup inclusion, its core "phylogeographic" claims rely entirely on visual inspection of tree topology under an asymmetrically downsampled taxon set without any formal discrete-trait phylogeographic ancestral reconstruction.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Absence of Formal Phylogeographic Inference (`Methodologically Flawed`):** Despite being framed around "phylogeography" and claiming to reconstruct migration pathways between Angola, Romania, Brazil, and Cuba, the study does not apply any formal phylogeographic model (such as discrete-trait continuous-time Markov chain diffusion or structured coalescent inference in BEAST, which was already available in BEAST v1.5.3). Instead, conclusions about the direction and multiplicity of viral introductions are drawn informally from visual inspection of clade nesting and paraphyly.
- **Asymmetric Subsampling Bias in BEAST Analyses:** To construct the 156-sequence BEAST dataset, the authors randomly subsampled at most 5 sequences per country per calendar year, *except* that they retained **all** sequences from the Romanian pediatric nosocomial outbreak (`Lines 54–56`). Meanwhile, Angolan sequences were available only from sparse sampling years (`2001` and `2006`, $N=31$ total). Retaining 100% of one country's outbreak cohort while heavily thinning other countries and confronting severe temporal gaps in Angola guarantees that the densely sampled Romanian clade will dominate coalescent skyline dynamics and increases the probability that sparsely sampled lineages appear nested within or paraphyletic to the oversampled clade.
- **Severe Molecular Clock Sensitivity to Outgroup Inclusion:** The authors candidly report (`Line 115`) that including four non-subtype-F Group M outgroup references halves the estimated mean substitution rate of the subtype F `pol` alignment from $3.15 \times 10^{-3}$ ($2.41\text{--}3.91 \times 10^{-3}$) to $1.76 \times 10^{-3}$ ($1.40\text{--}2.17 \times 10^{-3}$) substitutions/site/year, shifting the inferred Romanian clade tMRCA by 23 years—from `1978 (95% HPD: 1972–1983)` without outgroups to `1955 (95% HPD: 1942–1966)` with outgroups (and the Angolan clade tMRCA from `1983` to `1975`). This sensitivity demonstrates substantial rate heterogeneity between deep inter-subtype branches and recent intra-subtype tip branches that a single uncorrelated lognormal relaxed clock does not adequately absorb.
- **Editorial/Textual Lapses:** Several grammatical errors remain in the text (e.g., Abstract `Line 6`: *"suggests that there were multiple introductions of subtype F occurred into..."*; Discussion `Line 129`: *"for all for all Romanian F1 sequences"*).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: The Romanian HIV-1 subtype F1 epidemic originated from a single founder introduction (`[Moderately Supported]`).** All 236 pure subtype F1 Romanian sequences (spanning both adult heterosexually infected patients and the 1989 pediatric cohort) form a single monophyletic lineage in the 606-sequence ML tree (save for interspersed Angolan and 2 Cuban sequences), consistent with a major point-source/founder expansion.
- **Claim 2: There were "multiple introductions of subtype F occurred into Angola and Romania" (`[Overstated]`).** In the ML and BEAST trees (`Figure 1`), a monophyletic Angolan cluster (`bootstrap 86, posterior 0.99`) is nested *inside* the Romanian clade. Topologically, a single Angolan subclade nested within a Romanian clade is equally (or more parsimoniously) explained by a single exportation from Romania to Angola (or undersampled ancestral diversity in Angola where only one lineage survived/was sampled in 2001–2006) rather than multiple independent introductions into Romania and Angola. Without formal ancestral location reconstruction and sensitivity testing for sampling density, claiming multiple bilateral introductions overstates the topological evidence.
- **Claim 3: The Romanian epidemic tMRCA dates to ~1978 (`1972–1983`) (`[Fragile]`).** While the 1978 estimate (derived without outgroups) aligns better with historical epidemiological records than the 1955 outgroup-rooted estimate, the 23-year shift between the two specifications underscores that the molecular clock calibration is fragile on this 900-bp `pol` fragment.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Sequences were retrieved from the public LANL database, and country/year counts are summarized in Supplementary Table 1; however, the curated sequence alignment, SCUEAL recombinant filtering list, and BEAST XML configuration files are not deposited in a public repository (`Line 50`: *"Alignment was performed manually, and is available from the authors upon request"*).
- **Software Versions:** Software tools (`SCUEAL`, `PhyML`, `BEAST v1.5.3`, `Tracer v1.5`) and MCMC chain lengths ($2.5 \times 10^7$ to $1.0 \times 10^8$ steps, ESS > 100) are clearly documented.
