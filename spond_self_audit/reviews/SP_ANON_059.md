## 1. Executive Summary
This comparative study investigates the selective forces acting on four human small heat shock protein genes associated with inherited neuromuscular disorders and cataracts (`HspB1`, `HspB3`, `HspB5`, and `HspB8`) across 130 to 152 jawed vertebrate (`Gnathostomata`) orthologs spanning ~450 million years of evolution. Using `FastTree 2.1` and `HyPhy`'s `FEL` and `FitMG94`/`FitMG94-Compare` methods, the authors estimate codon-level $\omega = \beta/\alpha$ profiles and compare aggregate $\bar{\omega}$ ratios across full-length coding sequences, domain-like sequence partitions (`NTR`, `CeR`, `αCD`, `CTE`), and exons (`Exons 1–3`), representing significant pairwise likelihood-ratio test (`LRT`) differences via Hasse diagrams. They confirm that all four genes evolve predominantly under purifying selection ($\text{HspB8} > \text{HspB1}, \text{HspB5} > \text{HspB3}$), that the core $\alpha$-crystallin domain (`αCD` / `Exon 2`) experiences stronger purifying constraint than the disordered flanking regions (`NTR`, `CeR`, `CTE`), and that human disease-associated missense mutations localize exclusively to codons under historical purifying selection. While the `FitMG94-Compare` framework is appropriately applied to full-length genes and *a priori* exon boundaries, the domain partition boundaries were delineated post-hoc by visually inspecting the `FEL` $\omega$ plots of the same alignments, and the Discussion over-extrapolates differences in vertebrate $\bar{\omega}$ across just four genes to explain clinical mutation incidence and Mendelian dominance in human neuropathies.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Circular Post-Hoc Delineation of Sequence Partitions From `FEL` $\omega$ Profiles ("Double Dipping") [Methodologically Flawed]:** In the Methods (chunk 1, lines 45 and 56), the authors state that to compare selective pressures across protein regions (`NTR`, `CeR`, `αCD`, `CTE`), *"These partitions were primarily inferred from the profiles of the $\omega$-values as returned by the FEL-method (cf. Figure 1), thereby separating regions with relatively high, medium, or low average $\omega$-values."* They then apply 1-d.f. likelihood ratio tests (`FitMG94-Compare`, Figure 3b and Table S4) to those exact same alignments to test the null hypothesis that the adjacent partitions have equal aggregate $\bar{\omega}$. Visually choosing segment breakpoints to separate regions of low vs. high site-level $\omega$ and then running frequentist hypothesis tests on the same data to prove that those segments have significantly different aggregate $\bar{\omega}$ is circular post-selection inference, which guarantees inflated LRT statistics and artificially small $p$-values. Domain boundaries should have been defined strictly *a priori* from structural coordinates (e.g., $\beta$-sandwich boundaries of the $\alpha$CD in crystal/NMR structures) or PFAM profiles, just as the exon partitions (Figure 4) were defined *a priori* from Ensembl genomic coordinates.
* **Asymmetric Exclusion of Invariant vs. Variable `Met`/`Trp` Codons in `FitMG94` [Fragile]:** On line 43 (chunk 1), the authors note that internal Methionine (`ATG`) and Tryptophan (`TGG`) codons—which lack synonymous degeneracy—have undefined site-level ratios ($\beta/\alpha = 0/0$) when invariant, and state: *"These codons (marked in Table S3 and by columns of gray x in Fig. 1) which were included in the FEL analysis were excluded from the subsequent selection analyses using the FitMG94 method (see below), while other internal, variable methionine, and tryptophan residues were included in both analyses."* Because `FitMG94` estimates an alignment-wide or partition-wide parameter $\bar{\omega}$ jointly across all codons in a partition (rather than averaging site-by-site $\beta/\alpha$ ratios), selectively stripping invariant internal `Met`/`Trp` codons ($\beta = 0$) while retaining variable `Met`/`Trp` codons ($\beta > 0$) systematically biases the partition-level $\bar{\omega}$ upward.
* **Unaddressed Synonymous Saturation Across 450 Myr of `Gnathostomata` Divergence [Moderately Supported]:** Aligning coding sequences from cartilaginous fish (`Chondrichthyes`), ray-finned fish (`Neopterygii`), amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals spans ~450 Myr of divergence, over which synonymous substitutions at third codon positions experience substantial saturation ($d_S > 1$ on deep inter-class branches) and divergent GC-content/codon usage biases. Neither synonymous saturation diagnostics nor site-to-site synonymous rate variation (`SRV`) controls are evaluated.

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Speculative "Goldilocks Plasticity" Explanation for Human Neuropathy Mutation Counts Across $N=4$ Genes [Overstated]:** In the Discussion (chunk 2, lines 22–26), the authors attempt to explain why clinical neuropathy cohorts find ~7-fold more patient mutations in `HspB1` than in `HspB8` (and virtually none in `HspB3`) by arguing that `HspB8` has the lowest $\bar{\omega}_{\text{FL}}$ (so mutations are embryonic lethal/purged), `HspB3` has the highest $\bar{\omega}_{\text{FL}}$ (so mutations are benign polymorphisms), and `HspB1` sits in an intermediate "plasticity" zone that permits late-onset dominant disease alleles to accumulate. However:
  1. Late-onset dominant peripheral neuropathies (`CMT2`/`dHMN`) manifest in adulthood and impose minimal reproductive fitness selection in modern human populations, so embryonic/purifying purging cannot explain why `HspB8` has fewer late-onset dominant gain-of-function mutations than `HspB1`.
  2. `HspB1` and `HspB5` have statistically indistinguishable $\bar{\omega}_{\text{FL}}$ values (Figure 2b), yet `HspB1` causes peripheral neuropathies while `HspB5` causes myopathies and cataracts—reflecting tissue-specific expression levels (motor neurons vs. skeletal/cardiac muscle and lens) and toxic oligomeric gain-of-function assembly rather than gene-wide vertebrate $d_N/d_S$.
  3. Similarly, attributing the presence of only 1 known disease mutation in `Exon 2` of `HspB5` (vs. several in `Exon 2` of `HspB1` and `HspB8`) to a modest difference in vertebrate $\bar{\omega}$ ignores CpG mutational hotspot density and clinical sequencing ascertainment.

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Species lists and taxonomic rankings (`Table S2`, 130–152 species per gene), site-level `FEL` estimates (`Table S3`), partition-level `FitMG94` estimates with 95% profile-likelihood confidence intervals (`Table S4`), and clinical mutation catalogs (`Tables S1` and `S5`) are thoroughly documented in the Supplementary Information.
* **Deficiencies:** Multiple sequence alignment software and parameters used to generate the final codon alignments fed into `FastTree 2.1` and `HyPhy` (beyond pairwise `EMBOSS-Needle` screening and `Clustal Omega` orthology trees) and exact `HyPhy` version numbers are not specified in the Methods.
