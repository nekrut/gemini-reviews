## 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript combines phylogenetic comparative modeling of bulk HIV-1 *nef* sequences ($N=686$ antiretroviral-naive patients from the HOMER cohort) with clonal sequencing ($n=85$ clones) and Roche/454 GS-FLX massively parallel pyrosequencing (MPP) in 8 selected patient plasma samples to investigate whether residue–residue epistatic interactions inferred at the host-population level also govern within-host viral evolution. Using Bayesian graphical models (BGMs) applied to maximum-likelihood ancestral nonsynonymous substitution maps and bulk-sequence chromatogram mixtures, the authors identify 40 high-probability inter-residue edges ($\text{posterior} > 0.95$) and select two pairs overlapping bulk mixture correlations (`Y120/Q125` and `N157/S169`) for deep sequencing with multi-stage experimental replication. While the study is a thoughtfully designed early application of next-generation sequencing to intra-host viral evolution—notably quantifying how RNA extraction and RT-PCR contribute ~20-fold greater allele-frequency dispersion than second-round PCR and pyrosequencing alone—its central biological conclusion of repeated "convergent within-host evolution" across 2 to 9 independent lineages per host rests on single-tree phylogenetic reconstructions of short (247 nt) amplicons without accounting for *in vivo* HIV-1 recombination or *in vitro* RT-PCR chimerism, and exhibits minor numerical discrepancies between Table 1 and the main text.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Unmodeled Intra-Host Recombination and *In Vitro* PCR Chimerism in Short-Read Trees [Fragile]:** To infer within-host coevolution, the authors collapse redundant 247-nt forward 454 reads into unique haplotypes, build a single neighbor-joining + ML tree per patient, reconstruct joint ML ancestral states, and count independent clades showing correlated substitutions at `Y120/Q125` or `N157/S169` (reporting 2 to 9 independent instances per patient, Table 1). However, HIV-1 undergoes rapid *in vivo* recombination, and nested RT-PCR followed by three rounds of second-round PCR amplification on high-diversity templates generates substantial *in vitro* template-switching chimeras. When two major haplotypes (e.g., `YQ` and `FH`, or `NS` and `SN`) co-circulate at 20–50% frequency within a host alongside polymorphic flanking sites across a 247-nt window, any recombination or PCR chimera event transfers the target two-site motif onto a different flanking background. Forcing recombinant short reads onto a single bifurcating tree systematically manufactures apparent homoplasy—i.e., multiple "independent lineages" acquiring the same correlated substitutions. Although the Discussion acknowledges recombination as a theoretical caveat for the among-host bulk alignment (arguing that recombination breaks down linkage equilibrium), it overlooks that *within-host* recombination and PCR chimerism between two co-existing high-frequency variants in a short amplicon directly inflate the count of apparent convergent evolutionary events on a bifurcating phylogeny.
* **Ascertainment Bias in Selecting the Eight Deep-Sequencing Patients [Moderately Supported]:** The 8 plasma samples subjected to clonal and 454 sequencing (`P3, P5, P6` for `Y120/Q125`; `P1, P2, P4, P7, P8` for `N157/S169`) were explicitly chosen because their bulk Sanger sequences already contained simultaneous nonsynonymous chromatogram mixtures at both sites of the candidate pair (which required minor allele frequencies of ~20–50% at both positions). Because both alleles were guaranteed to be segregating at high frequency in these 8 samples by design, observing transitions between the consensus and double-mutant genotypes within each patient's read tree is an expected consequence of the sample selection filter rather than an independent validation across unselected hosts.
* **Numerical Discrepancies Between Table 1 and Main Text [Fragile]:**
  1. On line 149, the text states that *"the number of independent lineages carrying correlated substitutions ranged among patients from 2 to 9 with a mean of 4.6 (table 1)"*. However, summing the "Correlated Substitutions" column in Table 1 (`2 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 9 + 6 + 7 + 4`) yields `35` across the 8 patients, for a mean of `35 / 8 = 4.375` (`4.4`), not `4.6`.
  2. On line 155, the text states that *"site 120 acquired a substitution before site 125 in 4 out of 13 cases, and site 157 before site 169 in 14 of 20 cases (table 1; in nearly all cases, the inferred order of substitutions was unambiguous en route to a terminal sequence)"*. In Table 1, for sites `120,125` (`P3, P5, P6`), the total correlated substitutions are `2 + 9 + 6 = 17`, while the ordered parenthetical counts `(first, second)` are `(0,1) + (3,6) + (1,2) = (4,9)` (`4 of 13` ordered cases, leaving `4/17 = 23.5%` where both substitutions occurred on the same branch and order could *not* be inferred). For sites `157,169` (`P1, P2, P4, P7, P8`), the total correlated substitutions in Table 1 are `2 + 2 + 3 + 7 + 4 = 18`, and the ordered parenthetical counts sum to `(2,0) + (1,1) + (2,1) + (5,1) + (2,2) = (12,5)`—i.e., `12 of 17` ordered cases (`18` total), contradicting the main text's `14 of 20 cases`.
* **Rigorous Quantification of Multi-Stage Pyrosequencing Variance [Well Supported]:** The experimental replication design—performing triplicate second-round PCRs for all 8 patients and full replicate RNA extractions + triplicate RT-PCRs for 3 patients (`P2, P4, P8`)—provides a valuable empirical benchmark. Using the variance-to-mean ratio across shared sequence variants, the authors demonstrate that full protocol replication starting from RNA extraction increases dispersion by 20-fold ($7.6\times 10^{-3}$ vs. $3.7\times 10^{-4}$ for second-round PCR onward, raising the expected standard deviation of a 1% variant from 0.19% to 0.87%), convincingly showing that clonal plasmid control mixtures severely underestimate true clinical deep-sequencing variance.

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Control for Nucleotide-Level Confounding in *nef* [Well Supported]:** Because the C-terminal region of *nef* overlaps the 3' LTR (including the negative response element and polypurine tract) and forms conserved RNA secondary structures, amino acid covariation could be confounded by nucleotide-level selection. The authors appropriately run a parallel BGM analysis on synonymous substitutions and cross-check against experimental HIV-1 RNA secondary structure maps (Watts et al. 2009), showing zero overlap between the 40 nonsynonymous BGM edges and the 10 synonymous BGM edges (four of which localize to the 3' LTR core negative response element).
* **Structural and Immunological Plausibility of BGM Edges [Well Supported]:** The among-host BGM network recovers established functional motifs in Nef, including the PAK-2 activation triad (`L85, H89, F191`), the ubiquitinated lysine cluster (`K82, K92, K94, K105`), and known HLA class I CTL escape associations (`Y120F/Q125H`).

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Bulk sequence GenBank accessions are referenced via Brumme et al. (2007), clonal vs. MPP alignments and reproducibility derivations are provided in Supplementary Texts S1–S3, and custom HyPhy batch scripts and Python post-processing scripts are hosted at `http://www.hyphy.org/pubs/Nef`.
* **Deficiencies:** Raw 454 pyrosequencing read archives (e.g., NCBI SRA accession numbers) for the 24 barcoded amplicon libraries and the 85 new clonal *nef* sequences are not deposited or listed with accession numbers in the manuscript, and the alignment pre-processing pipeline relies partly on manual curation in Seaview/Se-Al and custom scripts described as *"available upon request"* (line 88).
