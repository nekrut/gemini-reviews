### 1. Executive Summary
This resource and comparative genomics paper presents the 28-way vertebrate whole-genome alignment track (`multiz28way` on human assembly `hg18` / NCBI Build 36) in the UCSC Genome Browser and Galaxy platform, integrating 2 finished genomes (human, mouse), 16 high-coverage draft assemblies ($5.1\times\text{--}8.5\times$), and 10 low-coverage mammalian shotgun assemblies ($\sim 2\times$) via `BLASTZ`, `axtChain`, `axtNet`, `MULTIZ`, and `phastCons` / `phyloFit` fourfold-degenerate (`4D`) phylogenetic conservation models. To illustrate the utility and pitfalls of the 28-way alignment, the authors present three analytical applications: (1) protein-coding indel vignettes (testing rate uniformity across 209 coding indels along the human placental lineage, examining 11 human-specific coding indels such as *SULF1* and *GFM2*, and testing whether *PAH* frame-preserving deletions in PKU occur at conserved columns); (2) start versus stop codon alignability across 11,729 reviewed RefSeq genes, showing that lower start-codon alignability in draft genomes is largely driven by sequencing coverage dropout in GC-rich CpG islands and alignment failure on short ($<100\text{ bp}$) initial coding exons; and (3) decay of genomic alignability as a function of `4D` neutral substitution distance across coding exons, ultraconserved elements (UCEs), predicted regulatory modules (PRPs), ENCODE ChIP-chip putative transcriptional regulatory regions (pTRRs), and non-repetitive non-coding background DNA.

As a community infrastructure and exploratory analysis paper, the manuscript is exceptionally transparent, well-engineered, and self-critical. Rather than over-claiming biological discoveries from preliminary vignettes, the authors explicitly diagnose technical artifacts in whole-genome alignments (such as `2x` draft CpG island dropout, short-exon chaining limits, human polymorphism in `hg18` for *GFM2*, and `phastCons` gap-as-missing-data behavior) and provide open-source tools (`bx-python` LZO-indexed MAF access, Galaxy tools, FTP downloads, and C source code).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Scalable whole-genome alignment, syntenic/reciprocal-best net filtering, and LZO-indexed MAF access ([Well Supported]):** Applying syntenic net filtering to high-coverage mammalian assemblies and reciprocal-best filtering to $2\times$ shotgun assemblies minimizes paralog/pseudogene misalignment, while chunked LZO compression with interval indexing in `bx-python` / Galaxy enables fast semi-random access across massive whole-genome MAF files.
* **Diagnosis of technical vs. biological drivers of start/stop codon drift ([Well Supported]):** The observation that start codons align less frequently than stop codons in all 26 draft genomes—but *not* in the finished mouse genome ($97.7\%$ starts vs. $96.8\%$ stops)—prompted the authors to stratify RefSeq genes by CpG island overlap, initial coding exon length ($\ge 100\text{ bp}$ vs. $<100\text{ bp}$), and alternative promoters (Table 3). Demonstrating that within placental mammals the start/stop discrepancy vanishes in non-CpG-island genes ($p = 0.16$), while at deeper vertebrate distances short initial exons ($<100\text{ bp}$) drive unalignability ($p < 10^{-3}$), is a textbook example of separating sequencing/alignment artifacts from evolutionary signal.
* **Phylogenetic decay modeling of functional feature alignability ([Well Supported]):** Measuring alignability carefully by excluding target-species assembly gaps ("potentially missing" vs. contiguous "not aligned" in `maf_interval_alignability.py`) and plotting feature retention against `phyloFit` REV `4D` substitution distances cleanly distinguishes exponential background turnover from the prolonged conservation plateaus of coding exons and UCEs and the intermediate exponential decay of regulatory elements (PRPs and ENCODE pTRRs).
* **Ascertainment bias in parsimony-assigned coding indels along the human lineage ([Moderately Supported]):** In Application 1 (lines 130–135), the authors identify 209 coding indels where human differs from both elephant and opossum and where the indel can be *"unambiguously assigned to a currently sampled branch of the human lineage"*, finding a higher apparent rate in early placental evolution ($\sim 4/\text{Myr}$) than on recent primate branches ($<2/\text{Myr}, p < 10^{-3}$). Because the tree contains many more species flanking deep placental nodes than recent hominid nodes—and because parsimony/agreement filtering across 16 incomplete draft genomes (10 of which are $2\times$ coverage) interacts with branch depth and missing-data patterns—some of the apparent rate difference between deep and terminal branches may reflect differential ascertainment or branch-length calibration (taken from Murphy et al. 2007) rather than a true slowdown in coding indel fixation. The authors themselves note in the Conclusion (line 211) that their indel modeling is simplistic compared to probabilistic indel models (e.g., Diallo et al. 2007).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Exemplary restraint on human-specific indels (*SULF1*, *GFM2*) and *PAH* deletions ([Well Supported]):** The authors explicitly caution against leaping from bioinformatic indel lists to adaptive storytelling: they show that the 6-bp *GFM2* "human-specific" insertion in `hg18` is actually an unfixed human polymorphism located in an unconstrained surface loop, and they candidly report a null result ($p \approx 0.69$) for their small-sample test of *PAH* frame-preserving deletions.
* **Conservation turnover of cis-regulatory elements vs. exons and UCEs ([Well Supported]):** Comparing sequence-alignment-derived PRPs against alignment-independent biochemical ENCODE ChIP-chip pTRRs and CTCF sites (Fig. 7) avoids circularity and demonstrates that $70\%$ of biochemically active human pTRRs are conserved across eutherian mammals but turn over outside eutherians.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Gold-standard open resource availability:** All 28-way alignments (`hgdownload.cse.ucsc.edu/goldenPath/hg18/multiz28way`), Galaxy interactive tools (`g2.bx.psu.edu`), `bx-python` indexing/alignability scripts (`bx-python.trac.bx.psu.edu`), and custom C programs (`bx.psu.edu/miller_lab/publications/`) are publicly released.
* **Note on text extraction:** Tables 1, 2, and 3 were rendered as headers/captions without their body rows in the plain-text conversion of the manuscript, though their key numerical values are quoted in the main text.
