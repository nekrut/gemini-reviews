**Composite Score:** 5.2 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.5/3.5 | Claims: 1.2/2.5 | Biology: 1.1/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.4/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Spatiotemporal Ascertainment Bias in Coalescent Skygrid & Unscreened Recombination
**Verdict:** Major Revision

**Technology Maturity:** Commodity/Hype-Cycle Template
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
This study re-analyzes 519 publicly available human norovirus `GII.2` full-length `VP1` capsid sequences deposited in GenBank (spanning 1976–2017) using maximum-likelihood phylogenetics (`PhyML`), Bayesian coalescent demography (`BEAST` Skygrid and `skygrowth`), and standard `Datamonkey`/`HyPhy` codon selection tools (`MEME`, `FEL`, `SLAC`, `FUBAR`). The authors report five phylogenetic clusters, a sharp decline in effective population size ($N_e$) from 2012 to 2015 followed by rapid growth in 2016, and seven positively selected codons (only one by `FEL`/`SLAC`/`FUBAR`, six by `MEME`). The study follows a formulaic secondary GenBank re-analysis template and suffers from severe spatiotemporal sampling bias that directly confounds the coalescent Skygrid demographic inferences, alongside a complete omission of intragenic recombination screening prior to phylogenetic and selection modeling.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Extreme Spatiotemporal Ascertainment Bias Confounding Coalescent `Skygrid` Demography:** Coalescent demographic estimators (`Skygrid` and `skygrowth`) assume that sequence sampling is either proportional to or independent of subpopulation structure; unmodeled shifts in geographic sampling density or outbreak-targeted sequencing produce spurious oscillations in inferred effective population size ($N_e\tau$). As shown in the authors' own Results (lines 58–62), out of 519 `GII.2` sequences spanning 41 years, only 6 sequences were collected before 2000, 61 during 2000–2009, and 452 (87.1%) during 2010–2017—and crucially, 302 of the 519 sequences (58.2%) derive from a single burst of outbreak sequencing in China during 2016–2017 (`Cluster V`). Feeding an unsubsampled dataset where $>58\%$ of all tips come from a single country in the final two years into a panmictic `Skygrid` model inevitably creates an apparent coalescent bottleneck in 2012–2015 followed by explosive growth in 2016, reflecting intense local outbreak sampling rather than global `GII.2` effective population size dynamics.
* **Omission of Intragenic Recombination Screening:** Noroviruses frequently undergo recombination not only at the `ORF1/ORF2` junction (which gave rise to the `GII.P16-GII.2` recombinant driving the 2016–2017 outbreak) but also within the `VP1` capsid gene (`S` and `P1`/`P2` domains). The authors perform no recombination screening (`GARD`, `RDP`, or `3SEQ`) on the 519-sequence `VP1` alignment before inferring a single `PhyML`/`BEAST` tree and running `MEME`/`FEL`/`SLAC`/`FUBAR`. Unaccounted recombination distorts molecular clock calibration, inflates terminal branch lengths, and generates false positives in episodic selection (`MEME`).
* **Internal Inconsistency Between Abstract and Main Text:** The Abstract states that the dataset comprises 519 sequences from "7 countries" (line 6), whereas the Results section and Table 1 list 9 geographic origins (China, Japan, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Netherlands, Australia, USA, UK, and Malaysia; lines 60–62).
* **Over-Interpretation of Single-Branch Episodic Selection (`MEME`):** Across 542 codons, alignment-wide pervasive methods (`SLAC`, `FEL`, `FUBAR`) agree on only a single positively selected site (codon 345), whereas `MEME` flags 6 additional sites (`61, 71, 162, 245, 386, 401`) at nominal $p < 0.05$ without multiple-testing correction or separation of internal vs. terminal branches (where sequencing errors or transient polymorphisms reside).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *Global `GII.2` norovirus effective population size remained stable until 2012, dropped sharply until 2015, and expanded rapidly in 2016.* **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** — Directly confounded by extreme spatiotemporal ascertainment bias (302/519 sequences sampled in China in 2016–2017 vs. sparse global sampling prior to 2015) without geographic subsampling or structured coalescent modeling (`MASCOT`/`MultiTypeTree`).
* **Claim 2:** *Natural selection on the `VP1` capsid protein (`7` positively selected sites) played an important role in driving the 2016–2017 `GII.2` epidemic.* **`[Overstated]`** — The 2016–2017 `GII.2` surge was driven by acquisition of a novel `GII.P16` polymerase (`ORF1`) while the `VP1` capsid sequence remained antigenically conserved (as the authors themselves admit on lines 113–115, noting that the selected `VP1` residues did not occur on the 2016–2017 epidemic stem).
* **Claim 3:** *The genome-wide evolutionary rate of `GII.2` `VP1` is $3.35 \times 10^{-3}$ substitutions/site/year with a `tMRCA` around 1966.* **`[Moderately Supported]`** — Broadly consistent with calicivirus capsid rates, though precision is affected by the 302-sequence 2016–2017 Chinese cluster and lack of root-to-tip clock validation (`TempEst`).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** GenBank accession numbers and `BEAST`/`Datamonkey` model choices (`GTR+I+G4`, relaxed lognormal clock, Skygrid) are listed in the Methods and Supplementary Table S1.
* **Deficiencies:** No `BEAST` XML files, trimmed alignments, or `skygrowth` R scripts are deposited in a public repository, and effective sample sizes (`ESS`) or root-to-tip regression $R^2$ diagnostics confirming temporal signal are not numerically reported.
