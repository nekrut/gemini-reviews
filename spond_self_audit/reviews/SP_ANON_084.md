### 1. Executive Summary
This study develops and benchmarks a nested hierarchy of mechanistic Muse–Gaut-style codon substitution models (1H, 2H, 3HSI, 3H, and 3H+) that permit instantaneous two-nucleotide ($\delta$) and three-nucleotide ($\psi_s$ for synonymous codon-island jumps, $\psi$ for general triple hits) substitutions alongside a 3-bin general discrete distribution (GDD) for site-to-site nonsynonymous rate variation ($\omega_k$). Applying this hierarchy to 13 benchmark alignments and 42,498 empirical coding sequence alignments across four major comparative genomic collections (Selectome bony vertebrates, avian orthologs, mammalian orthologs, and metazoan mtDNA), the authors find that $61.0\%$ of alignments reject the standard single-hit (1H) model in favor of 2H ($p < 0.01$, Bonferroni-corrected), and $23.1\%$ prefer 3H+ over 2H. Using site-level evidence ratios ($\text{ER} > 5$), joint ancestral state reconstructions, parametric power/FPR simulations, and INDELible+MAFFT alignment-error simulations, they show that statistical support is concentrated in a small fraction of sites per gene (median $0.67\%$ for 2H, $0.52\%$ for 3H), with synonymous serine island jumping ($\text{AGY} \leftrightarrow \text{TCN}$) along short branches contributing heavily to the 3H signal.

This is a thorough, large-scale methodological and empirical investigation backed by open-source software (`hyphy fmm` and Datamonkey). The primary conceptual limitation—which the authors explicitly acknowledge in the Discussion as "phenomenological load"—is that in baseline models lacking site-to-site synonymous rate variation (SRV) and branch-site $\omega$ heterogeneity ( heterotachy / episodic selection), rapid successive single-nucleotide substitutions at hypermutable CpG dinucleotides or under episodic selection on a single branch can be absorbed into global instantaneous multi-hit parameters ($\delta, \psi_s, \psi$).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Model Hierarchy and Boundary Asymptotics (Pillars 3 & 4):** The five models (1H, 2H, 3HSI, 3H, 3H+) are cleanly parameterized as extensions of the MG94+CF3x4+GDD(3) framework, adding global rate multipliers $\delta, \psi_s, \psi$. Because the null hypotheses ($\delta = 0$, $\psi_s = 0$, $\psi = 0$) lie on the boundary of the parameter space $[0, \infty)$, the true asymptotic null distributions of the likelihood ratio test statistics are mixtures of $\chi^2$ distributions (e.g., $\frac{1}{2}\chi^2_0 + \frac{1}{2}\chi^2_1$). Using standard $\chi^2_1$ and $\chi^2_2$ critical values with a 5-way Bonferroni threshold ($p < 0.01$) is therefore conservative, as confirmed by the two-sequence and four-gene parametric null simulations (Table 5, Figure S3).
* **Confounding by Unmodeled Synonymous Rate Variation and Branch-Site Heterogeneity (Pillar 3):** The baseline 1H model assumes a constant synonymous substitution rate across all sites ($\alpha_i \equiv 1$) and constant $\omega_k$ across all branches at a given site. In empirical vertebrate and viral alignments, CpG hypermutability and localized mutation-rate spikes create extreme site-to-site synonymous rate variation, while episodic adaptation or relaxed constraint creates branch-specific bursts of substitution. Because the parametric null simulations (lines 79–82) were generated under spatially homogeneous 1H/3H+ models *without* SRV or branch-site heterotachy, they verify statistical calibration under the exact model specification but do not quantify how much of the $61\%$ empirical 2H rejection rate is driven by unmodeled SRV or branch-site $\omega$ bursts rather than Simultaneous Multi-Nucleotide Mutations (MNMs) or serine fitness-valley crossings.
* **Alignment Error Control via INDELible and Selectome Masking (Pillar 5):** The authors directly test Whelan and Goldman's hypothesis that 3H signals are alignment artifacts by simulating sequences with indels in INDELible (rates $0.01\text{--}0.06$), re-aligning with codon-aware MAFFT, and comparing masked vs. unmasked Selectome alignments (76% vs. 76% for 2H:1H; 74% vs. 77% for 3H:1H). This provides a strong control demonstrating that alignment error alone does not explain the bulk of the empirical signal.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Widespread Statistical Preference for Multi-Hit Codon Models in Comparative Data [Well Supported]:** Across 42,498 alignments, the statistical preference for models permitting instantaneous 2H and 3H transitions is rigorously documented and shown to be driven by a sparse subset of sites ($\sim 0.5\text{--}1.7\%$).
* **Prominence of Synonymous Serine Island Jumps ($\text{AGY} \leftrightarrow \text{TCN}$) along Short Branches [Well Supported]:** The decomposition of 3H into 3HSI ($\psi_s$) and 3H+ ($\psi$) cleanly demonstrates that $37.9\%$ of datasets supporting 3H+ over 2H do not require non-synonymous triple hits once synonymous codon-island jumps are accommodated, and that 3H+ support is specifically enriched on short branches (median $0.09$ vs. $0.26$ subs/site) where sequential single-step non-synonymous intermediates (Thr or Cys) are improbable under standard 1H rates.
* **Upward Bias in $\omega$ and Branch Lengths Under Standard 1H Models [Well Supported]:** The systematic reduction in gene-wide $\omega$ ($\omega_{2H} \approx 0.965\,\omega_{1H}$, and $<0.75\,\omega_{1H}$ in $5.7\%$ of significant datasets) and branch lengths ($0.93\times$) when 2H/3H transitions are accommodated is consistent across empirical and simulated datasets.
* **Biological Mechanism: Simultaneous Replication Error vs. Rapid Sequential Fixation [Well Supported Framing]:** The authors appropriately distinguish statistical fit from mechanistic proof, explicitly noting (lines 197–201) that interspecific comparative alignments lack the temporal resolution to separate simultaneous polymerase-$\zeta$ multi-nucleotide mutations from rapid sequential single-nucleotide fixations driven by strong selection against deleterious intermediates.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data & Code Availability:** Exemplary reproducibility. All 42,498 empirical alignments and simulation scripts are publicly accessible via Dryad, GitHub (`https://github.com/veg/hyphy-analyses/`), and the HyPhy data archive (`data.hyphy.org`), and the method is distributed in standard HyPhy (`hyphy fmm`) with an interactive visualization web app.
