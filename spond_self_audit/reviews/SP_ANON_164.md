### 1. Executive Summary
`SP_ANON_164` evaluates whether non-reversible nucleotide substitution models—specifically a 6-rate strand-symmetric model (`NREV6`) and an unconstrained 12-rate non-reversible model (`NREV12`)—fit empirical viral genome alignments better than the standard general time-reversible (`GTR`) model across 141 viral datasets (30 dsDNA, 31 dsRNA, 33 ssDNA, and 47 ssRNA), and uses 5,500 simulated alignments to test whether ignoring non-reversibility degrades phylogenetic tree inference accuracy. While the core biological observation that strand-specific mutational biases (`NREV12`) are widespread across both single-stranded and double-stranded viruses is sound and interesting, the manuscript contains serious internal contradictions and numerical/table errors: (1) the Abstract claims that increasing non-reversibility makes *tree topologies more accurate* while making *branch lengths less accurate*, whereas the Results and Methods never separate topology from branch lengths (reporting only combined weighted Robinson-Foulds `wRF` distances, which worsen everywhere); (2) the simulation parameterization of "Degree of Non-Reversibility" (`DNR = 0 to 20` in `Table 5`) uses unnormalized additive rates that are incompatible with the normalized empirical `DNR` scale (`Tables 1–4`); and (3) `Tables 1–4` contain duplicate rows, missing datasets (`34` rows instead of `47` in `Table 4`), contradictory text citations of maximum `DNR` (`10.890` in `Table 1` ignored in text), and impossible `AIC`/`LRT` combinations.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Direct Contradiction Between Abstract and Main Text on Topological vs. Branch-Length Accuracy (`Methodologically Flawed`):**
  - The **Abstract** (`Line 6`) explicitly states: *"As simulated degrees of non-reversibility (DNRs) increased, **the tree topology inferences using both NREV12 and GTR became more accurate, whereas inferred tree branch lengths became less accurate.**"*
  - However, in **Results and discussion** (`Line 219`, `Line 262`) and **Materials and methods** (`Lines 234, 258`), the authors evaluate tree accuracy *exclusively* via the **weighted Robinson-Foulds (`wRF`)** distance (which conflates topological bipartition mismatches and branch-length errors into a single scalar) and report only that `wRF` monotonically *increased* (i.e., trees became *less* accurate) as `DNR` increased! Unweighted topological RF distance is never reported anywhere in the Results, Methods, or Figures. Thus, a headline finding of the Abstract has no supporting analysis in the paper and contradicts the Results narrative.
- **Apples-to-Oranges Scaling Between Simulated `DNR` (`Table 5`) and Empirical `DNR` (`Tables 1–4`):**
  - In `Line 228`, `DNR` is defined as the average across the 6 nucleotide pairs of $|q_{ij} - q_{ji}|$ under `NREV12`. Because substitution rates $q_{ij}$ in `HyPhy` (`NucleotideNonREV`) are normalized relative to a reference rate or unit expected substitutions per site, empirical `DNR` values in `Tables 1–4` almost all fall between `0.02` and `0.66`.
  - In the simulations (`Table 5`, `Lines 241–255`), however, the authors fix six directional rates (`A->C = 0.166`, `A->G = 1`, `T->A = 0.14`, `C->G = 0.131`, `G->T = 0.118`, `T->C = 1.101`) and simply add $+2, +4, \dots, +20$ to the six opposite rates (`C->A`, `G->A`, `A->T`, `G->C`, `T->G`, `C->T`) without re-normalizing the rate matrix before computing `DNR`! Adding $+8$ to $+20$ to unnormalized rates creates extreme 10- to 150-fold directional rate asymmetries (`C->A / A->C = 20.166 / 0.166 = 121.5x`) and inflates the total substitution rate of the matrix by ~10-fold, yet `Line 219` directly compares the unnormalized simulation threshold (`DNR > 8`) to the normalized empirical estimates (`max DNR = 1.56`) to argue that empirical viral datasets are "more than fourfold lower" than the point where `NREV12` outperforms `GTR`!
- **Severe Table, Mathematical, and Numerical Errors Across `Tables 1–4`:**
  1. **Missing 13 ssRNA datasets in `Table 4` & duplicate rows (`Lines 174–208`):** The Abstract and text (`Lines 6, 33, 128`) state that **47** ssRNA virus datasets were analyzed (`40/47` favoring `NREV12`), and `Line 213` specifically cites the `SARS-CoV-2` ssRNA dataset (`DNR = 1.536`) as having the highest ssRNA `DNR`. Yet `Table 4` contains only **34 rows** (13 ssRNA datasets, including `SARS-CoV-2`, are completely missing!). Furthermore, in `Table 4` (`Lines 184–185`), the row for `AMS` (`24337.2 | 24360.3 | 24343.9 | >0.05 | <0.001 | 0.073`) is an exact copy-paste duplicate of `CMV RNA3` immediately above it!
  2. **Unexplained `DNR = 10.890` outlier in `Table 1` contradicted by text (`Lines 71, 213, 219`):** `Table 1` (`Line 71`) reports `DNR = 10.890` for `HMAV_B` (Human mastadenovirus B). Yet `Line 213` states *"The dsDNA virus dataset with the highest DNR was Human mastadenovirus D (DNR = 0.646)"*, and `Line 219` states *"the highest estimated DNR in any of the empirical datasets that we analysed was 1.56"*—directly contradicting `HMAV_B = 10.890` in `Table 1`!
  3. **Impossible AIC vs. LRT $p$-value combinations (`Table 3, Line 147`):** For `MSV` (`Table 3, Line 147`), `AIC GTR = 252,645.3` (bolded as best), while `AIC NREV-6 = 254,347.5` and `AIC NREV-12 = 254,347.5` (`NREV-12` is **1,702.2 AIC units worse** than `GTR` and identical to `NREV-6`!). Yet the columns `p-Value GTR vs NREV-12` and `p-Value NREV-6 vs NREV-12` both state `<0.001`! A more general model (`NREV12`) cannot simultaneously have an identical AIC to `NREV6`, a log-likelihood ~850 units worse than `GTR`, and an LRT $p < 0.001$ in favor of `NREV12`.
  4. **Mathematical errors in Introduction (`Lines 14–16`):** Line 14 writes the detailed balance condition as $q_{ji}\pi_i = q_{ji}\pi_j$ (both subscripts are $ji$ instead of $q_{ij}\pi_i = q_{ji}\pi_j$), and Line 16 falsely claims that the GTR instantaneous rate matrix $Q_{ij}$ *"is symmetrical, e.g., the relative rate at which A changes to G is the same as the relative rate at which G changes to A"* (in GTR, the exchangeabilities $r_{ij} = r_{ji}$ are symmetrical, not the transition rates $Q_{ij} = r_{ij}\pi_j$ unless $\pi_A = \pi_G$).
  5. **Garbled table entries (`Table 2, Lines 98, 113, 120`):** `BTV_B` has `48893.`, `HRVA_H` has `20413.8 7 | 20420.5.6`, and `BPAV` has `p = 0.00` instead of `<0.001`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Strand-specific nucleotide substitution biases (`NREV12` outperforming `NREV6` and `GTR`) are pervasive not only in ssRNA and ssDNA viruses, but also in dsDNA (`20/30`) and dsRNA (`21/31`) viruses (`[Moderately Supported]`).** The widespread statistical preference for `NREV12` across all four Baltimore classes is biologically plausible (reflecting asymmetric transcription/replication single-stranded exposure of the coding/+ strand in dsRNA and rolling-circle/leading-lagging asymmetries in dsDNA), though the exact dataset counts and table entries require careful re-verification given the table corruption noted above.
- **Claim 2: As simulated `DNR` increases, tree topology inference becomes more accurate whereas branch lengths become less accurate (`[Methodologically Flawed]`).** Stated in the Abstract with zero supporting evidence in the manuscript text or figures.
- **Claim 3: `NREV12` only yields statistically significant improvements in tree accuracy over `GTR` at `DNR > 8`, which exceeds all empirical viral datasets (`[Methodologically Flawed]`).** This comparison rests on an unnormalized additive rate parameterization in `Table 5` that is not commensurate with the normalized `DNR` metric reported for empirical alignments.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Code & Model Availability:** The `NucleotideNonREV` `HyPhy` analysis module is publicly available on GitHub and Datamonkey (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`), and dataset metadata are referenced in Supplementary File 1.
