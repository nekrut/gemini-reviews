### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript presents AUTO-TUNE, an automated heuristic procedure for choosing the pairwise TN93 genetic distance threshold $d$ in HIV-TRACE molecular transmission network analyses using sequence data alone. For each candidate distance threshold $d_{L_i}$, AUTO-TUNE computes a priority score on $[0, 2]$ by summing (1) a normalized Z-score of the sliding-window jump ratio in $R_{12}$ (the size ratio of the largest to the second-largest cluster, intended to detect giant-component formation) and (2) a Gompertz-transformed normalized cluster count $C_i$. The authors apply AUTO-TUNE across 21 published HIV-1 *pol* datasets, 4 non-HIV datasets, subtype- and gene-stratified LANL sequences, a Chinese CRF07_BC dataset evaluated via Degree-Weighted Homophily (DWH), a three-dataset benchmark against `clustuneR`, and subsampled replicates of Rhee et al. (2019).

While automating distance threshold selection to avoid giant-component percolation is a reasonable pragmatic goal, the manuscript suffers from severe internal numerical contradictions, duplicated table rows, impossible network node counts, and unit-scaling errors across Tables 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, and the main text. Methodologically, the scoring heuristic relies on arbitrary magic constants without any ground-truth simulation validation, fails to reach its own decision threshold ($\ge 1.9$) in 17 of 21 empirical HIV-1 datasets, and evaluates DWH improvements without controlling for severe network sparsification at lower distance thresholds.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Pervasive Numerical Contradictions and Copy-Paste Errors Across Tables and Text:**
  1. **Duplicate Rows in Table 2 (`Chato et al. (2020) TN` vs. `Alberta`):** In Table 2 (lines 148 and 166), every single network statistic for `Chato et al. (2020) TN` ($N=2,750$ sequences) and `Chato et al. (2020) Alberta` ($N=808$ sequences) is 100% identical (`Nodes: 394 | 445`, `Clusters: 108 | 109`, `R12: 1.0 | 1.7`, `Scale parameter: 2.7 | 2.9`), indicating a copy-paste duplication error.
  2. **Direct Contradiction Between Text and Table 9 (`ClustuneR Comparison`):** In the text (offset line 87) and Table 1 (lines 122, 123, 127), the authors state: *"AUTO-TUNE found the thresholds with the highest scores to be 0.01872 for Middle Tennessee, 0.01538 for Seattle, and 0.01201 for Northern Alberta Table 9."* Yet Table 9 immediately below reports completely different AUTO-TUNE thresholds and maximum scores: Seattle = `0.01354` (Max score `1.53325` vs. `1.16` in Table 2), Tennessee = `0.01431` (Max score `1.25807` vs. `2.00` in Table 2), and Canada = `0.01099` (Max score `1.01678` vs. `1.03` in Table 2).
  3. **Impossible Node Counts and Conflicting Numbers for `Li et al. (2022)`:** Table 1 (line 131) reports that `Li et al. (2022)` has $N = 295$ sequences and an AUTO-TUNE threshold of `0.01483` (`1.48%`). However, Table 2 (line 147) reports that the network contains **1,364 nodes (Published) and 1,224 nodes (AUTO-TUNE)** with **277 clusters**—more than four times the total number of sequences in the dataset! Meanwhile, the main text (line 184) states that for `Li et al. (2022)`, *"AUTO-TUNE suggests D = 0.01483 subs/site with robust (1.76) confidence... While the total number of the clusters only increases by 1, the number of nodes connected in the network grows from 95 to 119,"* and Table 3 (line 202) lists the Size $\ge 2$ threshold as `1.15%` with score `2.0`. Four incompatible sets of numbers are reported for the same dataset.
  4. **Conflicting Full-Dataset Thresholds and Sample Sizes for `Rhee et al. (2019)`:** Section 2 (line 107) states `Rhee et al. (2019)` comprises **6,034 samples**; Table 1 (line 126) lists $N = 4,553$ with an AUTO-TUNE threshold of **0.01139 subs/site** and score **1.95** (Table 2) or **1.94** (Table 3); and text line 182 discusses reducing the threshold from 0.015 to **0.01139 subs/site**. Yet the subsampling section (offset line 105) states for the exact same dataset: *"The original full dataset confidently determined **0.01699 (AUTO-TUNE score 1.9998)**."*
  5. **Additional Cross-Table Discrepancies (`Little et al. 2014`, `Yan et al. 2021`, `Fabeni et al. 2020`):** Text line 180 states that for `Little et al. (2014)`, AUTO-TUNE (`0.02495`) *"reduced $R_{12}$ from 2.5 to 1.5,"* whereas Table 2 (line 156) reports that $R_{12}$ **increased** from `2.5` to `6.1`, and Table 3 (line 211) lists the Size $\ge 2$ threshold as `2.35%` (score `1.58` vs. `1.31`). Similarly, between Table 1 and Table 3 (Size $\ge 2$), the threshold for `Yan et al. (2021)` changes from `0.00839` (`0.84%`) to `1.22%`, and `Fabeni et al. (2020)` changes from `0.0037` (`0.37%`) to `1.77%`.
  6. **Unit-Scaling Errors in Tables 4 and 6:** In Table 4 (header: *"Distance threshold, subs/site"*), values in subs/site (`0.010`, `0.0325`, `0.0251`, `0.005`) are mixed indiscriminately with values in percent (`2.3`, `3.3`, `1.933`, `1.572`), along with corrupted strings (`0.359 1)`, `0.523 1)`). In Table 6 (offset lines 48–52, header: *"AUTO-TUNE D, subs / site"*), `pr + rt` thresholds are reported in subs/site (`0.02081`, `0.03266`, `0.01635`) while `Integrase`, `gag`, and `gp41` thresholds are reported as `2.0005`, `3.137`, `5.095`, `4.754`, `5.325`—off by a factor of 100.
* **Ad-Hoc Heuristic Specification Without Ground-Truth Simulation Validation:** The priority score combines a sliding window of width $W = \min(\max(N/100, 3), 30)$ for $R_{12}$ jump ratios with a hardcoded Gompertz gating function $1 - e^{-e^{-25x+3}}$ on normalized cluster counts, using a cutoff of $1.9$. No epidemic simulation benchmarks (e.g., birth-death-sampling or FAVITES contact-network simulations with known transmission trees) are conducted to test whether this score recovers true epidemiological clusters or how sensitive it is to the arbitrary Gompertz constants $(-25, +3)$ and window size $W$.
* **Confounding of DWH by Network Sparsification (Tables 7–8):** In the CRF07_BC analysis, lowering the threshold from `0.015` to `0.0076` and `0.0019` reduces the number of clustered nodes from 5,923 to 3,537 (-40%) and 1,654 (-72%). Because closer genetic thresholds preferentially retain immediate transmission pairs while pruning longer chains, homophily naturally increases monotonically as the graph is sparsified; this does not prove that `0.0076` or `0.0019` (which had weak AUTO-TUNE scores of 1.137 and 1.030) are epidemiologically optimal thresholds.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Low Decisiveness Across Empirical Datasets:** Only 4 of 21 empirical HIV-1 datasets in Table 1 (`Rhee et al.`, `Temereanca et al.`, `Li et al.`, `Bbosa et al.`) achieve the $\ge 1.9$ automatic selection threshold, and 15 of 21 have priority scores below 1.5. Thus, on the majority of real-world surveillance datasets, the heuristic does not identify a clear phase transition.
* **Overstated Comparative Claims:** The Discussion claims that *"AUTO-TUNE generated thresholds for all three examined datasets (Middle Tennessee, Seattle, and Northern Alberta) that outperformed clustuneR using DWH on 3-year collection date windows"* and *"often outperformed those manually selected in prior studies."* Given that `clustuneR` optimizes prospective cluster growth prediction (GAIC) rather than assortativity, that Canadian DWH values in Table 9 are negative for both methods (`-0.0448` vs. `-0.0536`), and that Table 9 contradicts the thresholds reported in the text and Table 1, these superiority claims are unsupported.

---

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Editorial & Technical Quality:** In addition to the severe cross-table numerical inconsistencies detailed above, Section 2 contains two large blocks of text (lines 33–51 and lines 71–91) where the entire numbered step-by-step lists are duplicated verbatim. While GitHub repositories (`github.com/veg/auto-tune`, `github.com/stevenweaver/autotune-app`) are cited, a complete audit and re-execution of all tables is required to reconcile the conflicting thresholds, scores, node counts, and units before the manuscript's empirical results can be trusted.
