# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_054
**Composite Score:** 7.5 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.3/3.5 | Claims: 2.0/2.5 | Biology: 1.7/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.5/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Sample-Size Reporting Contradictions, Severe Statistical Underpowering, & Non-Adherence Confounding
**Verdict:** Minor Revision
**Study Modality:** Hybrid Computational + Wet-Lab Functional Validation
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

## 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates whether mutations in HIV-1 *gag* or the *gp41* cytoplasmic domain (CD) explain virological failure (VF) in patients receiving ritonavir-boosted protease inhibitor (PI/r) regimens who fail therapy without developing major protease (PR) drug resistance mutations. The authors perform pre- and post-therapy Sanger sequencing of *gag* and *gp41* in patients experiencing VF on PI/r regimens ($N=41$) versus a control group failing NNRTI regimens ($N=20$) drawn from Kaiser Permanente Northern California (KPNC) and the ACTG A5202 clinical trial, applying a battery of phylogenetic and bioinformatic tests (`RELAX`, `FEL`, `MEDS`, a LANL prevalence-based "selection index," CTL epitope mapping, and an LSVM octamer cleavage predictor). They find that evolutionary rates ($d_N/d_S$), gene-wide selection intensity, cleavage site mutations, and directional selection patterns do not significantly differ between PI/r and NNRTI failures, concluding that *gag* and *gp41* mutations were not a common cause of PI/r VF in these cohorts. While the inclusion of an NNRTI control group and the candid reporting of a largely null clinical finding are commendable, the manuscript contains internal numerical contradictions in sample size (`40` vs. `41` vs. `34` rows in Table 2), is severely underpowered for codon-level selection inference (`n=24` *gag* and `n=28` *gp41* pairs in the PI/r group), does not adjust site-level $p$-values for multiple testing across hundreds of codons, and is heavily confounded by treatment non-adherence and Sanger mixture resolution.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Internal Sample-Size Contradictions Across Abstract, Text, and Table 2 [Fragile]:**
  1. The Abstract (line 6) states that *gag* and/or *gp41* were sequenced in *"61 individuals with VF on a PI/r (n = 40) or NNRTI (n = 20) containing regimen"*—where $40 + 20 = 60 \ne 61$.
  2. The Results text (line 58) states that sequences were obtained from *"41 subjects who experienced VF on a PI/r regimen"* and *"20 subjects who experienced VF on an NNRTI regimen"* ($41 + 20 = 61$).
  3. However, Table 2 (lines 64–97, titled *"Clinical feature of 41 subjects undergoing Virological Failure on a PI/r Regimen"*) contains only **34 patient rows** (`PID 4712` through `PID 259514`), omitting 7 of the 41 PI/r subjects without explanation. This omission also creates downstream discrepancies between the text and Table 2 (e.g., line 108 states *"17 individuals with a pre-therapy NRTI mutation"*, whereas only 16 rows in Table 2 list a pre-therapy NRTI mutation).
* **Severe Underpowering and Uncorrected Multiple Testing in Site-Level Selection Scans (`FEL` and `MEDS`) [Fragile]:** Successful paired pre-/post-therapy sequences were obtained for only `n = 24` *gag* and `n = 28` *gp41* patients in the PI/r group (`n = 16` and `n = 17` in the NNRTI group). Running site-by-site `FEL` and `MEDS` (episodic directional selection on the single terminal post-therapy branch per patient) across ~500 codons in *gag* and ~345 codons in *gp41* at an unadjusted nominal threshold of $p \le 0.05$ (lines 125–129) has near-zero statistical power to detect any mutation that does not recur at the exact same codon in multiple patients, while simultaneously guaranteeing ~25–40 false-positive codons by chance alone under the null. Consequently, the scattered sites flagged by `FEL` and `MEDS` (`gag` codons `340, 441, 453, 469, 479, 500` and `gp41` codons `49, 250, 314` in the PI group) are statistically indistinguishable from multiple-testing noise.
* **Ad-Hoc "Selection Index" Treating Substitutions as Independent Bernoulli Trials [Moderately Supported]:** To assess whether post-therapy substitutions move away from subtype B consensus (suggesting drug or immune selection) or toward consensus (reversion), the authors define a "selection index" $\sum \log_{10}(p_{\text{pre}} / p_{\text{post}})$ using amino acid frequencies from ~1,800–2,300 subtype B sequences in LANL, and compare PI vs. NNRTI distributions via a Wilcoxon test (`gag`: median `0.62` vs. `0.26`, $p = 0.25$; `gp41`: median `0.60` vs. `0.85`, $p = 0.6$). However, **36% of all observed *gag* "mutations" in the PI group** (`44%` in NNRTI) and **35% of *gp41* "mutations" in the PI group** (`56%` in NNRTI) were actually resolutions of pre-therapy ambiguous Sanger mixtures (e.g., `R -> A` or `R -> G`) following a population bottleneck (lines 116–118), which represent sorting of standing intra-host polymorphism rather than *de novo* therapy-driven evolution.

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Candid Recognition of Non-Adherence Confounding [Well Supported]:** Only 3 of the 41 PI/r failures (`7.3%`) developed major protease DRMs and only 1 of 17 with baseline NRTI resistance developed a new NRTI mutation at VF (compared to 11/20 NNRTI failures acquiring NNRTI DRMs). As the authors transparently acknowledge in the Discussion (line 193), because PI/r regimens have a high genetic barrier to resistance, clinical VF without PR mutations in routine practice is overwhelmingly driven by suboptimal medication adherence—meaning that most of the 41 PI/r viruses were likely rebounding under weak or absent drug pressure, diluting any true *gag*/*gp41* PI-resistance signal.
* **Proportional Negative Conclusion [Well Supported]:** Despite applying multiple exploratory bioinformatic lenses (octamer cleavage scores, CTL epitopes, `RELAX`, `FEL`, `MEDS`), the authors resist cherry-picking the unadjusted $p < 0.05$ site hits into false mechanistic claims and appropriately conclude that neither *gag* cleavage/non-cleavage mutations nor *gp41*-CD changes were a frequent driver of PI/r VF in these cohorts.

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Wet-lab nested RT-PCR amplification protocols, sequencing primers, GenBank accession number ranges (`KY579388–KY579803`), and clinical metadata columns (Table 2) are provided, and all phylogenetic methods (`MAFFT`, `RAxML`, `HyPhy RELAX`, `FEL`, `MEDS`, `PAML`) are cited.
* **Deficiencies:** Table 2 is missing 7 of the 41 PI/r subjects, the Abstract sample sizes do not sum to 61 (`40 + 20 = 60`), and exact software versions, parameter settings for the LSVM octamer cleavage predictor, and custom scripts used to compute the LANL "selection index" and mixture-resolution adjustments are not archived in a public repository.
