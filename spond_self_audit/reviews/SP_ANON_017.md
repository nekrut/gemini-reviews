# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_017
**Composite Score:** 5.4 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.5/3.5 | Claims: 1.2/2.5 | Biology: 1.3/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.4/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Ecological Fallacy (Unlinked Population HLA Frequencies vs. Consensus Viral Fixations) & Founder Confounding
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
This study downloads 873 unique HIV-1 `CRF01_AE` protease (`pro`, 99 codons) sequences from the Los Alamos National Laboratory (`LANL`) database (primarily from Thailand, Vietnam, China, and Cambodia) and compares their consensus sequence and polymorphism frequencies against the subtype B consensus sequence, Stanford Drug Resistance Database protease resistance-associated mutations (`PRAMs`), and Thai/Chinese population-level `HLA` class I allele frequencies retrieved from `dbMHC`. While the phylogenetic characterization of `CRF01_AE` protease (`SLAC` $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}S = 0.242$, `DEPS` directional evolution, and `BGM` co-evolving residue pairs) is competently executed, the study's central immunological claim—attributing the six fixed consensus amino acid differences between `CRF01_AE` and subtype B (`I13V, E35D, M36I, R41K, H69K, L89M`) to host `HLA` selection in Asian populations—rests on an unlinked ecological comparison confounded by the single central African founder bottleneck of `CRF01_AE` and furthermore contradicts its own directional counts (where $80\%$ of `HLA` associations actually favored the subtype B amino acid as the escape variant).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Internal Contradiction in the Direction of `HLA` CTL Escape Associations ($80\%$ Favoring Subtype B vs. $20\%$ Favoring `CRF01_AE`):** In the Abstract (line 54) and Discussion (line 120), the authors claim that "Of the predominant HLA haplotypes in the Asian regions of CRF01_AE origin, 80% were positively associated with the observed polymorphisms" and conclude that Asian host `HLA` haplotypes selected for the `CRF01_AE` consensus polymorphisms (`R41K`, `H69K`). However, the Results section (line 111) states the exact opposite for 24 of the 30 associations: *"At these five residues, 30 HLA-polymorphism associations were found... Of these associations, 24 (80%) demonstrated that the subtype B consensus amino acid would be favored during CTL escape, and 6 (20%) demonstrated that the CRF01_AE consensus amino acid would be favored."* If $80\%$ of the Thai/Chinese `HLA` associations predict that CTL escape selects for the **subtype B** consensus residue rather than the **CRF01_AE** consensus residue, then `80%` of the associations *contradict* CTL-driven fixation of the `CRF01_AE` consensus in Asia! Conflating "subtype B escape variant" (`24/30`) with "positive association explaining `CRF01_AE` polymorphisms" in the Abstract is a major interpretive reversal.
* **Ecological Fallacy and Founder Bottleneck Confounding:** The six "polymorphisms" analyzed against `dbMHC` (`I13V, E35D, M36I, R41K, H69K, L89M`) are simply the six fixed consensus differences between `CRF01_AE` and subtype B. Historically, Asian `CRF01_AE` descended from a single founder introduction from Central Africa into Thailand in the late 1980s, and residues such as `M36I`, `R41K`, `H69K`, and `L89M` are ancestral across virtually all non-B HIV-1 group M subtypes (`A, C, D, F, G, CRF01_AE`) in Africa prior to any circulation in Thai or Chinese populations. Because no individual-level patient `HLA` typing is linked to any of the 873 viral sequences, overlaying `dbMHC` Thai/Chinese allele frequencies onto 6 ancestral inter-subtype consensus differences cannot distinguish Asian CTL selection from the pre-existing African founder genotype.
* **Uncontrolled Cross-Country Confounding in Temporal `PRAM` and Polymorphism Trends:** When comparing `1990–2000` ($n=168$) vs. `2001–2007` ($n=470$) sequences via unadjusted $\chi^2$ tests (Fig. 1), the geographic composition of `LANL` submissions shifted substantially over time across countries with vastly different protease inhibitor rollout dates and treatment-failure sequencing practices (e.g., Japan had $41.3\%$ `PRAMs` vs. $1.7\%$ in Cambodia; line 102). Testing pooled temporal shifts without stratifying by country or treatment status (naive vs. PI-experienced) confounds calendar time with submission country and clinical sampling indication.
* **Unполное Blinding Artifact in Anonymized File:** Note that author names and correspondence metadata remained at lines 7–36 and 143–150 of `SP_ANON_017.md`; per strict double-blinded review rules, these lines were ignored and the manuscript was evaluated strictly on its internal scientific and statistical content.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *Accessory protease polymorphisms at codons `10` (`L10V`), `20` (`K20R/M/I`), and `62` (`I62V`) in `CRF01_AE` are significantly associated with the presence of major protease inhibitor resistance mutations (`PRAMs`).* **`[Well Supported]`** — Supported by substantial odds ratios ($\text{OR} = 3.4, 5.2, \text{ and } 8.5$) and consistent with known compensatory pathways under nelfinavir/PI exposure.
* **Claim 2:** *Purifying selection dominates `CRF01_AE` protease ($\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}S = 0.242$), with positive selection (`SLAC`), directional evolution (`DEPS`), and coevolution (`BGM`) acting at specific drug-resistance and compensatory codons (`12, 13, 63, 70, 74, 82, 93`).* **`[Well Supported]`** — Derived from appropriate phylogeny-aware codon models (`SLAC`, `DEPS`, `BGM`) on 873 deduplicated, recombination-screened sequences.
* **Claim 3:** *Predominant `HLA` haplotypes in Asian populations selected for the `CRF01_AE` consensus polymorphisms (such as `R41K` and `H69K`), with $80\%$ of `HLA` haplotypes positively associated with the observed polymorphisms.* **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** — Directly contradicted by line 111 showing that $24/30$ ($80\%$) of `HLA` associations actually favor the *subtype B* residue as the escape variant, and confounded by unlinked ecological data and the non-B ancestral founder state.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** The curated 873-sequence alignment was made available at an institutional URL (`http://www.hyphy.org/pubs/AE/AE.fas`), and database access dates (`April–October 2008`) are documented.
* **Deficiencies:** The exact `IEDB` prediction algorithm (`ANN`, `SMM`, `ARB`) and IC50 thresholds used to define the 30 `HLA`-epitope associations and the `19–40 fold` affinity decreases are not specified in the Methods, nor is the list of the 30 `HLA` allele–epitope pairs tabulated.
