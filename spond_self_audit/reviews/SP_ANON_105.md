### 1. Executive Summary
This comparative genomics study investigates the distribution, sequence correlates, and selective constraints of natural recombination breakpoints across whole-genome alignments from six coronavirus subgenera (*Pedacovirus* in *Alphacoronavirus*; *Embecovirus*, *Merbecovirus*, *Nobecovirus*, and *Sarbecovirus* in *Betacoronavirus*; and *Igacovirus* in *Gammacoronavirus*, comprising 16 to 412 dereplicated genomes at $<99\%$ identity per subgenus). Using the `RDP5` suite (combining 7 primary/secondary detection algorithms and HMM breakpoint refinement) alongside polymorphism-preserving permutation tests, `SuPER`/Levenshtein-based transcriptional regulatory sequence (`TRS-B`) prediction, and 3D structure-based `SCHEMA` chimeric folding disruption tests, the authors demonstrate that detectable recombination breakpoints are non-randomly distributed across all six subgenera. Specifically, breakpoint hotspots consistently cluster immediately upstream of the Spike (*S*) gene and colocalize with conserved `TRS-B` motifs (even after controlling for gene-edge proximity and excluding the *S* gene `TRS-B`), while breakpoint densities are significantly depleted within `ORF1a` (especially *nsp3* and *nsp4*), inside coding regions relative to intergenic regions, in the interior 90% of genes relative to the terminal 5% edges, and at sites predicted by `SCHEMA` to disrupt Spike protein 3D folding contacts (in *Igacovirus* and *Sarbecovirus*).

The study is methodologically rigorous and unusually self-critical regarding potential statistical confounders in recombination mapping. In particular, the authors explicitly account for polymorphism-density detection bias in their breakpoint permutation null model, recognize and quantify the elevated false discovery rate ($17.27\%$) of spatial colocalization between `TRS-B` sites and breakpoints via circular genome-shift null simulations, and perform targeted sensitivity controls (dropping the Spike `TRS-B` and conditioning on intergenic/5'-gene-edge regions via an $N-1$ $\chi^2$ test) to rule out trivial confounding.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Polymorphism-conditioned breakpoint permutation null model ([Well Supported]):** Because recombination detection power and breakpoint localization precision scale positively with local nucleotide polymorphism density, naive uniform randomization across alignment coordinates creates artifactual hotspots in hypervariable regions and coldspots in conserved regions. The `RDP5` permutation scheme used here (lines 35–40) preserves the exact number of polymorphic sites separating $5'$ and $3'$ breakpoints within each recombinant triplet as well as the non-overlapping spacing of multiple events within individual sequences, providing a well-calibrated null against detection bias.
* **Explicit calibration of `TRS-B` colocalization false discovery rate and gene-edge confounding ([Well Supported]):** The authors recognize that both `TRS-B` motifs and selection-filtered recombination breakpoints naturally cluster at intergenic boundaries, which inflates naive spatial association tests. By running 200 circularly shifted `TRS-B` coordinate sets per alignment, they empirically measure an average false discovery rate of $17.27\%$ (range $5\%\text{--}29\%$ at nominal $p < 0.05$) and therefore require replication across multiple independent subgenera plus a conditional test restricted to the $300\text{ nt}$ upstream of gene starts ($p = 0.0392$) and removal of the dominant Spike `TRS-B` hotspot.
* **`SCHEMA` 3D structural disruption permutation test ([Well Supported]):** To test purifying selection against misfolded chimeras, the authors apply `SCHEMA` ($4.5\text{ \AA}$ heavy-atom contact disruption $E$-score) to pdb structures of Spike across the four subgenera with $\ge 10$ intragenic Spike breakpoints, finding statistically significant avoidance of contact disruption in *Igacovirus* and *Sarbecovirus* ($p < 0.05$) and marginal avoidance in *Pedacovirus* ($p = 0.079$).
* **Table 3 numerical duplication error ([Moderately Supported]):** In Table 3 (lines 7–9 of the second text segment), the row for `Embecovirus | ORF1a` reports `43` breakpoints inside the region, `130` outside, and `P = 0.001`, and the immediately following row for `Merbecovirus | ORF1a` reports the exact same numbers (`43 | 130 | 0.001`). However, in the Methods (line 47) and Results (line 62), *Merbecovirus* had only 65 unique recombination events (and in Table 1, $1 + 66 = 67$ total breakpoints; in Table 2, $5 + 127 = 132$ breakpoints), whereas *Embecovirus* had 255 events. Thus, the `43 | 130` counts for *Merbecovirus* in Table 3 are a copy-paste duplication from the *Embecovirus* row above it.
* **Sequence-based `TRS-B` inference without subgenomic RNA-seq validation ([Moderately Supported]):** Because experimental sgRNA-seq data were unavailable across all six subgenera, `TRS-B` sites were inferred bioinformatically via Levenshtein distance $\le 1$ from the reference `TRS-L` motif and $\ge 75\%$ alignment conservation. While pragmatic, short motif matching can miss non-canonical `TRS-B` sites or include non-functional internal matches.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Mechanistic template-switching (`TRS-B`, low GC) vs. post-recombination purifying selection ([Well Supported]):** The manuscript carefully separates mechanistic predisposition to copy-choice RdRp template switching (colocalization with `TRS-B` body sequences and lower local GC content within 10–20 nt) from post-recombination purifying selection (enrichment of observable breakpoints in intergenic regions and terminal 5% gene edges, avoidance of `ORF1a` *nsp3*/*nsp4* ER-vesicle complex partners, and minimization of `SCHEMA` $E$-scores in Spike).
* **Modular transfer of Spike S1/S2 domains ([Well Supported]):** The recombination region count matrices (Fig. 2) and hotspot/coldspot profiles (Fig. 1) provide clear comparative evidence that Spike—and specifically the S2 fusion subunit or the complete S gene—is transferred as an intact module flanked by a conserved upstream `TRS-B` hotspot and internal NTD/S2 coldspots.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Code and data availability:** The custom Python scripts for `TRS-B` identification and circular shift FDR permutation are publicly available on GitHub (`phillipswanepoel/trsb-finder`), and full alignments and `RDP5` project files are referenced in the Supplementary Data.
