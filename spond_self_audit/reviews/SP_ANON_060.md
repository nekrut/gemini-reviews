# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_060
**Composite Score:** 7.8 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.3/3.5 | Claims: 1.9/2.5 | Biology: 1.6/2.0 | Reproducibility: 2.0/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Unpartitioned Recombination & Deep-Branch Saturation Confounding 5-Taxon Spike Positive Selection Test
**Verdict:** Minor Revision
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

## 1. Executive Summary
This early pandemic open-science study (initial analysis February 2020; proof update July 2020) audits the data-sharing and methodological transparency of the first four published SARS-CoV-2 genome papers and demonstrates that complete viral genomic workflows—spanning short-read quality control and host-read depletion, *de novo* genome assembly (`SPAdes`/`Unicycler`), root-to-tip MRCA dating, intra-host minor variant calling ($\ge 5\%$ allele frequency), recombination screening (`GARD`), and branch-site positive selection analysis—can be executed reproducibly on public computational infrastructure using open-source `BioConda` tools deployed via `Galaxy` and `Jupyter`. The manuscript makes a compelling, well-documented case for mandatory raw sequencing read deposition and reproducible workflow sharing (highlighting, for instance, that three of the first four SARS-CoV-2 papers withheld raw reads and one misidentified the variant caller `FreeBayes` as a genome assembler). However, several of the illustrative biological analyses performed on the February 2020 data—most notably a 5-taxon branch-level positive selection test claiming diversifying selection at ~7% of Spike sites along the ancestral branch leading to SARS-CoV-2, the pooling of reads across patients for assembly, and the structural over-interpretation of a single sub-consensus variant (`K921Q`)—suffer from methodological shortcuts.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Confounding by Recombination and Taxon Omission in the Ancestral Spike Positive Selection Test [Methodologically Flawed]:** On line 42, the authors run a branch-level test for positive selection on a full-length codon alignment of the `S` gene comprising only five viral lineages (`SARS-CoV-2`, `SARS-Tor2`, `Bat ZC45`, `Bat ZXC21`, and `Bat Rp3`) and report *"statistically significant evidence of positive diversifying selection (approximately 7% of S gene sites) along the branch leading to SARS-CoV-2 (Fig 4)."* This inference is an artifact of two compounding errors:
  1. Even though the preceding paragraph (line 40, Fig. 3) notes potential recombination breakpoints in `S` where segments of `SARS-CoV-2` alternate in similarity between `Bat ZC45`/`ZXC21` and `SARS-Tor2`, the authors run the branch-level selection test on the *unpartitioned* full-length `S` gene alignment using a single phylogeny.
  2. In the Receptor Binding Domain (`RBD`), `Bat ZC45` and `ZXC21` are highly divergent from `SARS-CoV-2` (due to ancestral recombination), and closer bat sarbecoviruses (`RaTG13`) were not included in the 5-taxon alignment. Forcing the recombinant `RBD` onto the genome-wide tree topology where `SARS-CoV-2` is sister to `ZC45`/`ZXC21` concentrates massive nonsynonymous and saturated synonymous divergence onto the terminal branch leading to `SARS-CoV-2`, manufacturing a false-positive signal of episodic diversifying selection (`7%` of sites) that disappears when recombinant segments are properly partitioned and closer bat relatives are included.
* **Pooling Sequencing Reads Across Distinct Patient Specimens Prior to *De Novo* Assembly [Fragile]:** In Stage 1–2 (line 28), the authors state that they preprocessed the six available raw sequencing read datasets (`S1 Table`) by removing adapters and human transcripts *"and combined the resulting data sets... These were used as inputs for SPAdes assembler and Unicycler"*. Pooling raw metatranscriptomic reads across multiple independent patients to generate a single composite assembly graph is poor bioinformatic practice, as it masks sample-specific assembly failures, coverage dropouts, and inter-patient sequence differences.
* **Exemplary Forensic Audit of Literature Irreproducibility [Well Supported]:** The manuscript's audit of the first four primary SARS-CoV-2 genome papers (Table 1) is concrete and verifiable, documenting the complete absence of software parameter specifications across all four studies, the withholding of raw SRA reads in three of the four, and the impossible claim in Holshue et al. (`[4]`) that short reads were assembled with `FreeBayes`. Similarly, their `GARD` reanalysis (line 40) appropriately refutes Wu et al.'s (`[3]`) statistically unsupported claim of recombination between `Bat ZC45`/`ZXC21` and `SARS-Tor2`/`SZ3` directly generating `SARS-CoV-2`.

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Over-Interpretation of a Single Unvalidated Intra-Host Variant (`Spike K921Q` at 38% AF in `SRR10903401`) [Overstated]:** From mapping Illumina reads across the six early SRA runs (line 34), the authors highlight an `A -> C` substitution at 38% minor allele frequency in a single sample (`SRR10903401`) causing a `Lys921Gln` (`K921Q`) change in Spike `HR1`. They argue that `K921Q` disrupts a salt bridge with `Gln1188` in `HR2` (noting that `Gln` cannot form a classical ionic salt bridge with `Lys`, as `Gln` is uncharged polar, though it forms a hydrogen bond) and call it *"This potentially adaptive change"* that *"may have structural and functional implications for the spike protein structure and, consequently, SARS-CoV-2 virulence."* Labelling a singleton sub-consensus variant observed in one early metatranscriptomic run as "potentially adaptive" without ruling out RT-PCR amplification bias or sequencing artifacts overstates a single unconfirmed observation.

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** This manuscript sets a gold standard for computational reproducibility: every tool is version-pinned via `BioConda`, every step is executable through public `Galaxy` workflows and histories mirrored across four international instances (`usegalaxy.org`, `usegalaxy.eu`, `usegalaxy.be`, `usegalaxy.org.au`), and all notebooks and data tables are archived on GitHub (`https://github.com/galaxyproject/SARS-CoV-2`, `https://covid19.galaxyproject.org`) and Zenodo (`https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3685264`).
* **Deficiencies:** None regarding reproducibility or data availability.
