### 1. Executive Summary
This study analyzes 98 full-length Vesicular Stomatitis Indiana Virus (VSIV) genomes from GenBank—including 87 US outbreak isolates from 2019–2020 (73 from horses in Colorado) and 11 historical/endemic reference genomes from Mexico, Central America, and South America—to characterize the evolutionary dynamics of the 2019–2020 US epidemic lineage. Using MEGA X (GTR+G phylogeny, ancestral reconstruction, pairwise $p$-distances), Meta-CATS, HyPhy ($F_{ST}$, FEL, MEME with 100 parametric bootstrap replicates, RELAX, and GARD), R `pegas` (AMOVA), and JMP (hierarchical clustering and linear regression of geographic vs. genetic distance in Colorado), the authors divide the 87 US isolates into four phylogenetic groups (I–IV), identify 21 clade-differentiating SNPs (16 synonymous, 5 nonsynonymous), report 42 positively selected sites and 565 negatively selected sites across the five viral genes (N, P, M, G, L), and claim adaptive population-level and geographic specialization driven by both synonymous mutations and singleton/doubleton terminal nonsynonymous substitutions.

While the topological finding that the 2019–2020 US VSIV outbreak forms a monophyletic lineage sister to 2017 Chiapas, Mexico cattle isolates (sharing a diagnostic 14-nt G–L intergenic insertion and L-R1784Q substitution) is solid, the manuscript's core evolutionary claims suffer from fundamental statistical and population-genetic flaws: (1) conflating singleton unpurged mutations on terminal tips and founder-effect clade markers with adaptive positive selection; (2) circular conditioning in Meta-CATS and AMOVA; (3) running tree-wide FEL/MEME across deeply divergent South/Central/North American clades (1983–2020) and misattributing the resulting selection calls to evolution *during* the 2019–2020 US outbreak (where mean sequence identity is $99.88\%$); and (4) performing an ordinary linear regression and one-way ANOVA on non-independent pairwise distance matrices without a Mantel test.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Confounding of Singleton Terminal Substitutions with Positive Selection in Ultra-Low-Diversity Outbreak Data (Pillars 3, 4 & 6):** Within the 87 US outbreak genomes, average pairwise nucleotide identity is $99.88\%$ (line 52; roughly 13 nucleotide differences across an $11.2\text{ kb}$ genome). Yet the authors report 42 positively selected codons via FEL/MEME at nominal $p \le 0.05$ without multiple-testing correction across $\sim 3,600$ codons (where $3600 \times 0.05 = 180$ false positives are expected under the null). Examining the authors' own text (lines 80–90) reveals that almost all of these "positively selected sites" are **singletons occurring in a single isolate on a terminal leaf branch** where $d_S = 0$ and $1\text{ or }2$ nonsynonymous changes occurred (e.g., P-194 and P-212 in single isolates; M-33 disrupting the M2 start codon in a single isolate; G-403, G-234, G-248, G-115 in single isolates; L-1590, L-1591, L-1887, L-2006, L-2045 in single isolates). Furthermore, the tree used for FEL/MEME (line 68) included the ancient 1983–2001 Central and South American bovines alongside the 87 US isolates, mixing inter-clade divergence over 40 years with intra-outbreak tips. In RNA viruses, singleton nonsynonymous mutations on terminal branches represent transient, unpurged replication errors (deleterious or neutral polymorphism), not adaptive positive selection.
* **Circularity in Meta-CATS SNP Discovery and AMOVA / $F_{ST}$ Validation (Pillars 2 & 3):** The authors first define Groups I–IV from the maximum-likelihood phylogeny of the 87 US sequences, and then run $F_{ST}$ ($p < 0.008$), AMOVA ($p = 0.015$), and Meta-CATS (Bonferroni threshold printed with a reversed inequality sign as *"p-value > 0.0000047"*, line 35) on those exact same phylogenetically defined groups. Testing for genetic differentiation between groups that were defined by cutting a phylogenetic tree is guaranteed to reject the null hypothesis of panmixia ($F_{ST} = 0$) by construction; likewise, any mutation falling on the internal branch defining Group I, II, III, or IV is automatically flagged as "highly significant" by a $\chi^2$ contingency test across the tip groups because all descendants inherit the clade-defining founder mutation identically by descent.
* **Pairwise Distance Matrix Regression and ANOVA Without Accounting for Dyadic Non-Independence (Pillar 4):** In lines 47 and 98–100, the authors compute pairwise genetic distances and pairwise geographic distances among the 73 Colorado isolates ($\binom{73}{2} = 2,628$ pairs), fit a standard ordinary least squares linear regression ($R^2$, $p < 0.05$) instead of a Mantel permutation test, and then apply hierarchical clustering followed by one-way ANOVA and Tukey's HSD directly to the distance matrix to define "three significant distinct geographical zones." Pairwise distances violate independence because each isolate appears in $N - 1$ pairs, rendering standard regression and ANOVA $p$-values invalid.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Monophyletic Origin of the 2019–2020 US Outbreak from Chiapas, Mexico [Well Supported]:** The phylogenetic clustering with 2017 Chiapas cattle isolates (IN0817CPB and IN1017CPB) and the shared 14-nt G–L intergenic insertion provide clear epidemiological evidence that the 2019–2020 outbreak was an independent introduction from southern Mexico rather than a re-emergence of the 1997–1998 US lineage.
* **Adaptive Role of 16 Clade-Defining Synonymous Mutations in Vector RNAi Evasion [Overstated]:** Because 16 of the 21 clade-defining mutations across the four US outbreak sub-lineages are synonymous, the authors argue (lines 121–125, chunk 2 lines 1–4) that fixation at internal nodes proves natural selection rather than drift and hypothesize that synonymous changes were positively selected to evade insect RNAi. During rapid spatial expansion of an arbovirus outbreak, neutral synonymous mutations hitchhike or fix via serial founder bottlenecks at internal clade bifurcations; observing synonymous synapomorphies on internal branches of an outbreak tree is the standard expectation under neutrality.
* **Geographic Adaptation to County-Level Ecological Zones in Colorado via Singleton/Doubleton Mutations [Methodologically Flawed]:** Claiming that two isolates from the same town (Carbondale in Garfield County) sharing a mutation at L-1345 while one isolate in neighboring Basalt (Pitkin County) lacks it *"suggests that ecological conditions in counties Garfield and Pitkin may represent two different challenges in terms of adaptation"* (line 104) confuses local transmission linkage (two horses infected on neighboring premises by the same local sub-chain) with county-specific ecological adaptation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data & Code Availability:** All 98 sequences are publicly available in GenBank, and standard GUI/web tools (MEGA X, Datamonkey, R `ape`/`pegas`, JMP) were used. However, no alignment file, tree file, or analysis script is deposited in a public repository.
* **Textual & Editorial Errors:**
  * Line 35 writes *"a conservative p-value > 0.0000047 was considered significant"* (inequality sign reversed; should be $< 4.5 \times 10^{-6}$, as $0.05 / 11185 = 4.47 \times 10^{-6}$).
  * Line 84 repeatedly misspells "domain" as *"domine"* (*"transmembrane domine (TM)"*, *"DIII/pH domine"*), repeated in chunk 2 line 9 (*"domine II"*).
  * Automated de-identification replaced the word "viruses" with `[JOURNAL REDACTED]` in multiple places (e.g., lines 6, 20, 35, 37, 56, 60, 88, 115), which was recognized as a scrubbing artifact and not penalized.
