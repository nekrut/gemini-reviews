## 1. Executive Summary
This study contrasts the selective regime acting on human SARS-CoV-2 during the first 11 months of the COVID-19 pandemic ($N=133,741$ GISAID genomes through October 2020) against the deeper evolutionary history of bat and pangolin *Sarbecoviruses* ($N=69$ genomes partitioned into nonrecombinant segments). Using a suite of codon-based likelihood methods (SLAC, FEL, MEME, aBSREL, and an extended BUSTED[S] incorporating a 3-rate hidden Markov model for spatially autocorrelated synonymous rate variation and multi-nucleotide substitutions), alongside a new base-composition-corrected Synonymous Dinucleotide Usage metric ($\text{SDU}_c$), Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) phylogenetic trait shift modeling (`PhylogeneticEM`), and BEAST local-clock dating, the authors show that early human SARS-CoV-2 evolution was dominated by weak purifying selection ($d_N/d_S \approx 0.4\text{--}0.9$) and sparse episodic diversification (~2% of sites in Spike and RdRp), whereas strong episodic positive selection and a pronounced adaptive shift toward CpG depletion occurred on the deep ancestral branch leading to the SARS-CoV-2-containing ("nCoV") bat clade. Furthermore, analysis of the recombinant bat virus `RmYN02` demonstrates that its non-nCoV Spike segment retains high CpG content while its nCoV backbone shares low CpG content with SARS-CoV-2, proving that high- and low-CpG lineages co-infect the same bat reservoir hosts. The methodology is careful, self-critical, and well-controlled for recombination, terminal-branch sequencing errors, synonymous rate variation, and multi-nucleotide mutations.

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Internal-Branch Filtering and Recombination Partitioning [Well Supported]:** The authors identify early on that applying positive selection scans to pandemic-scale SARS-CoV-2 trees including terminal branches generates pervasive false positives driven by sequencing errors, singleton deleterious mutations, and laboratory chimeras. Restricting FEL and MEME scans to internal branches (`http://covid19.datamonkey.org`) and partitioning the 69 *Sarbecovirus* genomes into recombination-free segments prior to selection and clock inference effectively removes the two largest sources of phylogenetic false positives in coronavirus comparative genomics.
* **Accounting for Autocorrelated Synonymous Rate Variation and Multi-Nucleotide Substitutions [Well Supported]:** Recognizing that conserved RNA structures, overlapping reading frames, and context-dependent mutation biases create extreme site-to-site synonymous rate variation (exceeding 20-fold across rate classes in BUSTED[S]-HMM), the authors model synonymous rate autocorrelation via a 3-state HMM and further test sensitivity to simultaneous multi-nucleotide substitutions (`HyPhy v2.5.24`). They transparently report that incorporating multi-nucleotide substitutions improves information-theoretic fit in 11 of 20 segments and eliminates the apparent positive selection signal in `ORF1ab/regE` (leaving `ORF N` robust).
* **Forced Ultrametricity and Small Clade Sample Size in the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck CpG Shift Test [Moderately Supported]:** To infer adaptive shifts in $\ln(\text{SDU}_c)$ along the `NRR1` and `NRR2` phylogenies using `PhylogeneticEM`, the authors use a scalar Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) process, noting on line 3 (chunk 2) that *"The trees were forced to be ultrametric, as required for the scalar OU model, by extending all the external edges of the trees to match the most recently sampled tip."* While tip sampling dates span only a few decades relative to a root depth of centuries, artificially stretching terminal branches distorts the terminal phylogenetic variance–covariance matrix. Moreover, because the `nCoV` clade contains only a handful of partially non-independent viral genomes (median pairwise $K_s = 0.54$), the lasso-selected OU shift on the ancestral branch leading to the `nCoV` clade reflects a single clade-wide contrast in $\text{SDU}_c$ evaluated on a fixed maximum clade credibility (MCC) tree without integrating over topological or branch-length uncertainty.
* **Three-Point One-Tailed $t$-Test in Figure 4B and Minor Segment-Count Inconsistency [Fragile]:**
  1. In Figure 4B (line 55), to test whether the absolute difference in $\text{SDU}_c$ between SARS-CoV-2 (`Wuhan-Hu-1`) and `RmYN02` is larger in the recombinant `non-nCoV` Spike region than in the `nCoV` Spike region, the authors treat the 3 codon frame positions (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge`) within each region as $n=3$ independent observations and apply an unpaired one-tailed Welch $t$-test ($t_{2.07} = 3.03, p = 0.0450$). A 3-vs-3 comparison across frame positions of the same sequence segment has minimal degrees of freedom, violates independence across overlapping codon frames, and only clears $p < 0.05$ because a one-tailed test was used; a permutation or bootstrap test across codons would be much more appropriate.
  2. There is a minor numbering inconsistency in the text regarding the number of nonrecombinant segments: line 33 states *"For each of the 19 nonrecombinant ORF regions identified"*, whereas Figure 2A/B (line 35), line 43, and Supplementary Table S3 refer to *"$N = 20$"* nonrecombinant fragments (`9/20` in aBSREL, `11 out of 20` in BUSTED[S]+MNS).

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Proportional Interpretation of the Generalist Ancestor Hypothesis [Well Supported]:** The manuscript carefully avoids overclaiming that intermediate hosts could *not* have been involved in spillover, explicitly stating that an undiscovered facilitating intermediate species or adaptive drift on the unsampled terminal lineage cannot be ruled out. At the same time, the combination of deep branch-site selection (`aBSREL`/`BUSTED[S]`), ancestral CpG depletion ($\text{SDU}_c$) consistent with mammalian ZAP/APOBEC3 antiviral pressure, and the `RmYN02` Spike recombination mosaic provides compelling comparative evidence that the progenitor of SARS-CoV-2 evolved its generalist receptor-binding and low-CpG phenotype within bat reservoirs decades prior to 2019.
* **Candid Assessment of Molecular Clock Identifiability [Well Supported]:** In the BEAST dating analysis (`NRR1` and `NRR2`), the authors explicitly test both a single-branch local clock on the internal branch leading to the `nCoV` clade and a clade-wide local clock across the entire `nCoV` clade, transparently reporting that the data cannot statistically distinguish between transient and clade-wide rate acceleration and showing how clock specification alters deeper node age estimates while keeping the `RmYN02`/SARS-CoV-2 split (~1976) consistent with independent estimates.

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Exact software versions (`HyPhy v2.5.14` and `v2.5.24`, `MAFFT v7`, `RAxML`, `RDP5`, `BEAST v1.10`, `BEAGLE v3`, `PhylogeneticEM`, `seqinr`) and mathematical equations for $\text{SDU}_c$ (Eq. 1) are fully documented; GISAID and GenBank accession tables (`S4 Table`, `S5 Table`), BEAST XML specifications (`S1 Data`), and live interactive results (`http://covid19.datamonkey.org`) are provided.
* **Deficiencies:** None of material significance (raw sequence alignments are omitted from `S1 Data` solely to comply with GISAID redistribution terms, with full accession lists provided).
