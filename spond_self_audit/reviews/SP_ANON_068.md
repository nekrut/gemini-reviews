### 1. Executive Summary
This study develops an empirical two-stage Bayesian-plus-maximum-likelihood relaxed-clock codon approach to estimate absolute rates of synonymous ($dS$) and nonsynonymous ($dN$) substitution over time in serially sampled within-host HIV genealogies. First, BEAST v1.3 is used to sample 200 time-scaled genealogies under an uncorrelated exponential relaxed clock and Bayesian skyline coalescent prior using HKY85+$\Gamma$. Second, for each sampled tree, HyPhy fits a branch-specific ("local") MG94×HKY85 codon model with total branch lengths constrained to the rescaled nucleotide branch lengths to decompose each branch's rate into absolute $dS$ and $dN$ components across "backbone", "internal", and "external" branch sets. Applying this pipeline to longitudinal *env* C2V3 sequences from the classic Shankarappa cohort ($n = 9$ HIV-1 subtype B patients), 2 full-*env* nAb-escape patients, 2 HIV-1 group O patients, and HIV-2 cohorts, the authors report three main findings: (1) terminal branches have elevated substitution rates reflecting transient deleterious mutation load; (2) pre-progression backbone $\log(dS)$ rate correlates negatively with time to AIDS progression ($r = -0.79, p = 0.011$) and CD4 decline slope ($r = -0.72, p = 0.028$), whereas $dN$ does not; and (3) backbone $dN$ drops significantly after clinical progression ($p = 0.001$), whereas $dS$ remains constant ($p = 0.333$), supporting the "immune relaxation" hypothesis over "cellular exhaustion."

The separation of backbone/internal branches from external branches and the decomposition of absolute $dS$ vs. $dN$ trajectories over posterior tree distributions are methodologically clean and directly test competing biological hypotheses (immune activation driving viral generation time via $dS$, vs. humoral immune pressure and late-stage immune collapse driving $dN$). The primary statistical caveats are the small sample size ($n = 9$ patients) for the across-patient correlation tests, sensitivity to right-censored progression time in Patient 11, and potential downward bias in $dS$ along high-$dN$ branches due to nucleotide-to-codon branch length rescaling constraints.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Phylogenetic Separation of Backbone/Internal vs. External Branches [Well Supported]:** By partitioning within-host trees into backbone, internal, and external branches across 200 posterior trees, the authors empirically demonstrate that external branches consistently exhibit inflated substitution rates across all 9 patients (confirmed not to arise under neutral coalescent simulations in Table S1), preventing transient deleterious mutations from biasing long-term divergence rates.
* **Small Sample Size ($n = 9$) and Right-Censored Progression Time in Correlation Tests [Fragile]:** The central across-patient correlations between $\log(\text{backbone } dS)$ and progression time ($r = -0.79, p = 0.011$), CD4 slope ($r = -0.72, p = 0.028$), and $\log\text{VL}$ slope ($r = 0.65, p = 0.059$) rest on only $n = 9$ patients (Figure 3). Furthermore, the caption to Figure 3 notes that for Patient 11 (a slow non-progressor whose CD4 count never dropped below 200 cells/$\mu$l), "progression time was set at the last sampling time (144 months)." Treating a right-censored survival time as an exact event time at 144 months (the extreme right-hand point in the 9-point Pearson regression) leverages the regression slope; reporting a rank-based Spearman correlation and a Cox/accelerated failure time test accounting for right-censoring on Patient 11 is needed to verify robustness.
* **Paired Pre- vs. Post-Progression Rate Comparison [Well Supported]:** Testing the "immune relaxation" vs. "cellular exhaustion" hypotheses via paired within-patient comparisons before vs. after progression time (Table 1; paired $t$-test $p = 0.001$ and Wilcoxon signed-rank $p = 0.008$ for backbone $dN$ decline, vs. $p = 0.333$ and $p = 0.461$ for backbone $dS$) uses each patient as their own control and is statistically sound. (Note: since Patient 11 had progression time set to the last sample at 144 months, only the remaining 8 patients can contribute post-progression branches).
* **Two-Stage Nucleotide-to-Codon Branch Length Constraint [Moderately Supported]:** Because BEAST samples time-trees under HKY85+$\Gamma$ where branch rates are drawn from a single uncorrelated exponential distribution, and HyPhy then decomposes fixed nucleotide branch lengths into $\alpha_i t_i$ and $\beta_i t_i$ under a local MG94 model, total substitution length on each branch is held fixed to the HKY85 estimate. In hypervariable C2V3 *env* regions with strong diversifying selection ($\omega \gg 1$), nucleotide models can underestimate total codon substitutions or conflate nonsynonymous bursts with overall branch rate variation, though the authors verify in Table S3 that site-to-site $dS$ rate distributions are nearly identical across the 9 patients.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Distinguishing Immune Relaxation from Cellular Exhaustion [Well Supported]:** If late-stage stabilization of viral divergence were caused by target-cell exhaustion slowing viral replication cycles, both $dS$ and $dN$ would decline after AIDS onset. Observing that $dS$ continues linearly while $dN$ drops sharply after CD4 drops below 200 cells/$\mu$l provides direct evolutionary support for humoral immune relaxation.
* **Immune Activation as the Driver of $dS$ and Viral Generation Time [Moderately Supported]:** While the authors did not directly measure T-cell activation markers (e.g., CD38+ HLA-DR+) in the 9 Shankarappa patients, linking higher synonymous substitution rates to shorter viral generation times driven by persistent immune activation—and corroborating this with lower $dS$ rates in less pathogenic HIV-2 infections (Table 2)—is biologically coherent and carefully framed alongside alternative hypotheses (CTL fitness trade-offs and latent reservoir reactivation dynamics) in the Discussion.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Code and Data Availability:** All analyzed longitudinal datasets (Shankarappa, Shriner, Frost, HIV-1 group O, and HIV-2) are publicly available in GenBank, and the custom HyPhy batch scripts for local codon decomposition across posterior BEAST trees are provided at `http://evolve.zoo.ox.ac.uk` and `http://www.hyphy.org`.
