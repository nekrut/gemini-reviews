### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents a thorough evolutionary and phylogeographic investigation of 78 whole-genome *Sarbecovirus* sequences (focusing on the 20-virus `nCoV` lineage containing SARS-CoV-2 and its closest bat and pangolin relatives). Combining multi-method recombination breakpoint detection (`RDP5` with 7 component algorithms and `GARD`), permutation tests for recombination hot- and coldspots (`BDT` and `RRT`) calibrated via forward population-genetic simulations (`SANTA-SIM`), partition-specific maximum-likelihood phylogenetics across 22 non-recombinant regions (`IQ-TREE`), local-clock Bayesian molecular dating (`BEAST` on the 3,238-bp non-recombinant region `RBP 5`), and *Rhinolophus* host species distribution mapping, the authors demonstrate pervasive reticulate evolution across China and Southeast Asia, confirm recombination hotspots flanking Spike and N alongside coldspots in Spike S1 (`NTD`/`RBD`), and date the divergence between SARS-CoV-2 and its closest Yunnan cluster (`RmYN02`, `RpYN06`, `PrC31`) to $\sim 1982$ ($95\%\text{ HPD}: 1970\text{–}1994$). The methodology is rigorous, self-critical, and well calibrated via explicit null simulations.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Simulation-Calibrated Recombination Hotspot Testing (`RDP5` + `BDT`/`RRT` + `SANTA-SIM`):** A standout feature of this manuscript is that the authors do not merely report nominal permutation $p$-values from the Breakpoint Distribution Test (`BDT`) and Recombinant Region Test (`RRT`). Instead, they simulate four 100-replicate forward-time datasets in `SANTA-SIM` matched to the empirical diversity and breakpoint density of the sarbecovirus alignment (with $0\times, 4\times, 8\times,$ and $16\times$ hotspot intensities) and run unsupervised `RDP5` + `BDT`/`RRT` on each. They candidly report that false-positive clustering rates are $1.5\text{–}2\times$ higher than nominal $\alpha = 0.01$, meaning $2\text{–}3$ apparent hotspot peaks across a 30-kb genome are expected by chance under uniform recombination, and cross-validate the Spike and N hotspots using an independent `GARD` breakpoint permutation test (lines 27, 75–87).
* **Partitioned Non-Recombinant Phylogenetics (`GARD` 22 RBPs):** Rather than forcing a mosaic whole-genome alignment onto a single tree or slicing the alignment at all 236 `RDP5` breakpoints (which would leave fragments too short for phylogenetic resolution), the authors use `GARD` ($\text{AIC}_c$ step-up model selection retaining breakpoints supported in $>2/3$ of models) on the 22 focal viruses to define 22 informative Recombination Breakpoint Partitioned (`RBP`) regions, reconstructing separate `GTR+I+G4` ML trees with 10,000 ultrafast bootstraps for each.
* **Prior-Dependent Molecular Clock Calibration on `RBP 5`:** Because heterochronous tip dates among bat sarbecoviruses span only $\sim 15$ years and lack strong internal root-to-tip clock signal across deep nodes, the `BEAST` dating analysis on `RBP 5` (`3,238 bp`) relies on an informative normal prior on the substitution rate ($\text{mean } 5 \times 10^{-4},\ \text{SD } 2 \times 10^{-4}\text{ subst/site/yr}$) and a separate local clock on the deepest `nCoV` branch following MacLean et al. (2021) (Tail line 5). While completely reasonable and clearly documented, the absolute calendar node ages (e.g., `1982` for the Yunnan cluster split, `1907` and `1883` for the Cambodian and Thai lineages) scale directly with this external rate prior.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *Bat and pangolin sarbecoviruses in the `nCoV` clade have undergone extensive historical recombination across a geographic range spanning $>2,500\text{ km}$ from Southeast Asia (Thailand, Cambodia) to Southwest and East China (Yunnan, Guangxi, Guangdong, Zhejiang).* **`[Well Supported]`** — Unambiguously demonstrated by topological shifts of `PrC31`, `RmYN02`, `RpYN06`, `RShSTT182/200`, and `RacCS203` across the 22 `GARD` `RBP` trees.
* **Claim 2:** *Recombination breakpoints are distributed non-uniformly across the sarbecovirus genome, with enriched hotspots upstream of and within `Spike` and `N` and coldspots within the `Spike` `NTD`/`RBD` and `ORF8`.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Supported by `RDP5` `BDT`/`RRT`, `GARD` permutation tests, and appropriately caveated with `SANTA-SIM` false-positive expectations.
* **Claim 3:** *Overlapping geographic ranges and shared roosting of horseshoe bats—particularly `Rhinolophus pusillus` and `Rhinolophus affinis`—provide the ecological connectivity linking `nCoV` recombinants across southern China and Southeast Asia, though the direct proximal ancestor of SARS-CoV-2 ($\text{tMRCA} \approx 1982$ with the Yunnan cluster) remains unsampled.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Grounded in `IUCN` host range overlays, `RBP 5` divergence dating, and subsequent independent confirmation from Lao bat sampling (`Temmam et al. 2022`).
