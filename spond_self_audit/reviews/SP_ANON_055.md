## 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript introduces `PRIME` (PRoperty Informed Models of Evolution), a family of maximum-likelihood codon substitution models implemented in `HyPhy` that parameterize amino acid exchangeability $\beta_{xy} = \exp(\psi - \sum_{i=1}^D \lambda_i |x_i - y_i|)$ as a function of $D$ normalized physicochemical properties across three scales: gene-wide (`G-PRIME`), episodic branch-site random effects (`E-PRIME`), and site-specific fixed effects (`S-PRIME`). The authors evaluate `PRIME` across 24 benchmark datasets, a genome-wide screen of 18,944 mammalian genes, 1,447,182 structurally mapped human PDB residues (evaluated on multimeric biological assemblies), `ESM-2` deep learning residue embeddings, H3N2 Hemagglutinin deep mutational scanning (DMS) data, ~5,000 parametric power/null simulations, and `Pyvolve` mutation–selection (`MutSel`) model-misspecification simulations. Beyond demonstrating substantial improvements in statistical fit (`G-PRIME` outperforms `MG94` in 98.6% and `CoRa` in 98.5% of mammalian genes, and exhibits strong synergy with rate variation in `E-PRIME`), the manuscript stands out for its rigorous two-stage selective False Discovery Rate control (Benjamini–Bogomolov + Holm–Bonferroni) and an exceptionally candid simulation audit that defines the informational redundancy ratio ($R = N_{\text{subs}} / N_{\text{aa}}$, $\text{AUC} = 0.91$) and exposes the exact failure modes of site-level biophysical inference (the invariance trap, two-state toggling complete separation, saturation, and property cross-talk).

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Rigorous Hierarchical Selective Inference and Simulation Stress-Testing [Well Supported]:** Testing $M+1$ hypotheses (`1` omnibus $M$-d.f. test plus $M$ individual 1-d.f. property LRTs) across $N$ codons risks severe post-selection inference bias if individual properties are tested only at omnibus-significant sites without adjustment. The authors properly implement the two-stage Benjamini–Bogomolov selective FDR procedure (`BH` at $q \le 0.10$ for the omnibus test across $N$ sites, followed by Holm–Bonferroni at level $\alpha \cdot R / N$ within the $R$ selected sites). Furthermore, their simulation design—splicing 80% neutral `MG94` and 20% alternative `G-PRIME` sites across 24 empirical tree topologies, complemented by 7 `Pyvolve` Gaussian `MutSel` regimes including Frequency-Biased Neutral (`FBN`) compositional controls and heterotachy—is exemplary.
* **Complete Separation, Boundary Clamping ($\lambda = \pm 15$), and Asymptotic Breakdown in Two-State Toggling Regimes [Well Supported / Transparently Audited]:** Because `S-PRIME` fits $D+2$ parameters per site using an exponential linear distance multiplier $\exp(\psi - \sum_i \lambda_i |x_i - y_i|)$, any site where observed substitutions are restricted to $N_{\text{aa}} = 2$ amino acids (or strictly avoid changes along a given property axis) creates an under-determined linear system with complete separation in the log-likelihood. Consequently, the maximum-likelihood estimator diverges to the artificial numerical box boundary ($\lambda = \pm 15.0$, visible in the empirical results at H3N2 site 226 $\lambda_{\text{pI}} = 15.0$, HIV-1 RT sites 184 and 100 $\lambda = 15.0$, and the mammalian buried core medians $\lambda_{\text{Vol}} = \lambda_{\text{pI}} = \lambda_{\text{Hydro}} = 13.89$). As the authors transparently demonstrate in Figure 10a, when $N_{\text{aa}} = 2$ and substitution count is high (toggling), boundary divergence violates the interior-point regularity conditions of Wilks' theorem, driving the raw omnibus FPR above 65% at $R \ge 20$ (though `BH` correction at $q \le 0.10$ suppresses dataset-wide neutral FDR because high-$R$ neutral toggling sites are rare, and `HyPhy v2.5.99` now emits an automated warning when $R < 2.0$).
* **Spurious "Diversifying Selection" ($\lambda < 0$) Induced by Property Cross-Talk and Exponential Distance Scaling [Moderately Supported]:** Under Equation 2, $\lambda_i < 0$ models substitution rates as growing exponentially with physicochemical distance $|x_i - y_i|$—implying selection favors maximal chemical difference rather than stabilizing around a shifted optimum. More critically, in the `Pyvolve` `MutSel` Scenario S2 simulations (chunk 3, line 5), the authors reveal that pure stabilizing (purifying) selection on Hydrophobicity alone causes `S-PRIME` to infer not only Hydrophobicity conservation ($\lambda_{\text{Hydro}} = 15.0$) but also statistically significant *Volume diversification* ($\lambda_{\text{Vol}} = -4.4$), because restricting substitutions to hydrophobic residues (`Val, Ile, Phe`) that happen to differ substantially in volume produces greater per-substitution volume variance than expected under an unconstrained neutral baseline. This finding implies that empirical negative $\lambda$ estimates (such as the widespread $\lambda_{\text{Helix}} < 0$ across 44% of the mammalian proteome or $\lambda_{\text{Hydro}} = -2.49, \lambda_{\text{Vol}} = -1.81$ at H3N2 site 226 alongside $\lambda_{\text{pI}} = 15.0, \lambda_{\text{Helix}} = 11.8$) can reflect collateral variance channeled by conservation of another property or relaxed constraint rather than active diversifying selection for property change. While the Discussion (chunk 3, line 34) candidly notes this limitation, claims of "active selection for change" in $\alpha$-helix propensity across the mammalian proteome should be interpreted with this cross-talk mechanism in mind.
* **Omission of Site-to-Site Synonymous Rate Variation (SRV) in `G-PRIME` and `E-PRIME` [Moderately Supported]:** While `S-PRIME` estimates a site-specific synonymous rate multiplier $\alpha(s)$, both `G-PRIME` and `E-PRIME` assume a single global synonymous rate across the alignment. In large, divergent alignments, unmodeled SRV can inflate $\Delta\text{AICc}$ and interact with the mixture components of `E-PRIME`.

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Multi-Scale Structural Validation on Multimeric Biological Assemblies [Well Supported]:** Computing Relative Solvent Accessibility (`RSA`) and 10 Å coordination numbers ($CN_{10}$) across `1,447,182` human PDB residues using multimeric biological assemblies (rather than isolated monomeric chains that misclassify quaternary interface residues as solvent-exposed) provides strong physical validation of the inferred $\lambda$ parameters, confirming a consistent core-to-surface constraint decay across 3,025 mammalian genes.
* **Honest Benchmarking Against Deep Mutational Scanning (DMS) and `ESM-2` [Well Supported]:** In the H3N2 Hemagglutinin DMS comparison (`A/Perth/16/2009`), the authors report not only Pearson $r = 0.605$ (vs. `0.533` for `ESM-2`), but also the much lower Spearman rank correlation $\rho = 0.157$ (vs. `0.305` for `ESM-2`) and higher Jensen–Shannon divergence (`0.382` vs. `0.327`), explaining that phylogenetic imputation on a single alignment assigns $>90\%$ probability mass to the single historical wild-type residue at each site and therefore cannot rank minor sub-optimal amino acids as accurately as a protein language model trained across millions of sequences.

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** The models are production-implemented in open-source `HyPhy` (`v2.5.94+` / `v2.5.99`), all mathematical parameterizations, statistical tests, and multiple-testing workflows are explicitly tabulated (Tables 1–3), and comprehensive supplementary tables (`Tables S1–S12`) document the 24 benchmarks, mammalian proteome fits, simulation scenarios, and `MutSel` evaluations.
* **Deficiencies:** None of material significance.
