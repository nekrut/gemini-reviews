## 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the phylodynamics and molecular adaptation of human Norovirus (NoV) genogroup II genotype 4 (GII.4) from 1987 to 2008 using two surveillance datasets: $N = 1,383$ short partial polymerase sequences (Region A, 247 nt) from European (FBVE) and global (Noronet) outbreak surveillance, and $N = 194$ full-length VP1 capsid sequences (1,623 nt), alongside a matched subset of $N = 172$ partial polymerase sequences (241 nt). Applying Bayesian Skyline Plot (BSP) coalescent models in BEAST, the authors show that the dense 1,383-taxon polymerase dataset captures seasonal epidemic peaks corresponding to the 2002, 2004, and 2006a/b variant waves, whereas the sparser 194-taxon capsid and 172-taxon matched polymerase datasets lack the temporal sampling density required to resolve rapid post-bottleneck expansions. Extending Drummond and Suchard's posterior predictive genealogical neutrality test (Fu and Li's $D_F$) to piecewise-constant BSP demographic histories, the authors reject neutral evolution for both datasets ($p = 0.019$ capsid, $p = 0.018$ polymerase). They then apply a suite of maximum-likelihood codon and protein evolution models (GBDD/REL, FEL, internal-branch iFEL, DEPS directional evolution, and Spidermonkey/BGM co-evolution) to the capsid alignment, identifying surface-exposed antigenic/ligand-binding sites (e.g., 294, 297, 352, 368, 372, 394, 395, 407) as well as internal N-terminal codons 6 and 9, which they link via RNA secondary structure modeling (`RNAfold`) to subgenomic RNA stem-loop conservation near the ORF2 start codon.

The manuscript is methodologically ambitious and makes two standout contributions: (1) generalizing posterior predictive genealogical neutrality testing (`D_F`) to nonparametric Bayesian Skyline Plot priors, and (2) offering a compelling mechanistic hypothesis (5' ORF2 RNA secondary structure constraint leaving unpaired nucleotides for ribosome binding) for why non-surface N-terminal capsid codons 6 and 9 repeatedly appear as false-positive "antigenic" targets in naive $dN/dS$ scans. However, several statistical and interpretive caveats require revision:
1. **Phylogenetic Noise and Clock Rate Inflation in the 1,383-Taxon, 247-nt Polymerase BEAST Analysis:** Estimating an uncorrelated lognormal relaxed-clock phylogeny of 1,383 tips plus a 40-group Bayesian Skyline Plot from a **247-nt alignment** (~0.18 nucleotides per taxon) operates in a severely information-starved regime where tree topologies are poorly resolved and branch-length uncertainty inflates the inferred substitution rate more than two-fold ($8.98 \times 10^{-3}$ subs/site/yr in the 1,383-taxon set vs. $4.32 \times 10^{-3}$ in the 172-taxon matched polymerase set).
2. **Overstated Epidemic Forecasting for the 2008 Variant:** Predicting in the Abstract and Discussion that the 2008 variant is "more likely to cause a future epidemic than the 2007 variant" based solely on two amino acid differences at codons 393–395 is an unvalidated extrapolation.
3. **Minor Reporting & Data Availability Gaps:** A typographical mismatch in site numbering between the text (`209`) and Table 3 (`509`), and providing sequence alignments only "on request."

## 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit

### 2.1 Identifiability and Rate Inflation in the 247-nt, 1,383-Taxon Relaxed-Clock Analysis
- In Table 1, the median substitution rate for the 172-taxon matched polymerase dataset (241 nt) is $4.32 \times 10^{-3}$ subs/site/yr (95% HPD: $3.50\text{–}5.20 \times 10^{-3}$), which agrees closely with the 194-taxon full capsid dataset ($5.33 \times 10^{-3}$, 95% HPD: $4.62\text{–}6.02 \times 10^{-3}$). However, when all $N = 1,383$ partial polymerase sequences (247 nt) are analyzed together, the estimated substitution rate doubles to $8.98 \times 10^{-3}$ subs/site/yr (95% HPD: $7.73\text{–}10.4 \times 10^{-3}$), with non-overlapping 95% HPD intervals.
- While the authors attribute this 2-fold discrepancy to the sensitivity of the proportion of invariant sites ($I$) to taxon count, fitting an uncorrelated lognormal relaxed clock and a 40-step piecewise-constant BSP to 1,383 sequences of length 247 nt introduces massive topological uncertainty and dense terminal branching. On a 247-nt fragment, single nucleotide polymorphisms and transient deleterious mutations on short terminal branches (confirmed by the strongly negative Fu & Li $D_F$ statistic) are accommodated by the relaxed clock as elevated branch rates, inflating both the global clock rate and short-term $N_e\tau$ fluctuations.

### 2.2 Sound Extension of Genealogical Neutrality Testing (`D_F`) and Internal-Branch Selection (`iFEL`)
- The authors' extension of the Drummond & Suchard posterior predictive neutrality test (`D_F`) from simple parametric coalescent priors (constant/exponential) to the 40-group piecewise-constant BSP model is methodologically rigorous and directly addresses the concern that unmodeled population bottlenecks could mimic non-neutral terminal branch excesses.
- Likewise, recognizing from the negative $D_F$ statistic that terminal branches carry an excess of slightly deleterious within-host mutations, the authors appropriately deploy internal-branch Fixed Effects Likelihood (`iFEL`) alongside dataset-matched neutral simulations (confirming nominal FPR of 0.04 at $p = 0.05$ and 0.08 at $p = 0.10$) to isolate population-level adaptive substitutions along internal trunk/clade branches.

### 2.3 Incomplete Recombination Screening Sample ($n = 20$)
- To rule out recombination before running phylogenetic analyses, the authors run GARD, VisRD, and RDP3 on only $n = 20$ concatenated ORF1–ORF2 sequences (2 per variant), identifying the known ORF1/ORF2 junction breakpoint in the `2003Asia` variant. While they also run the `Phi` test on the separate polymerase ($N = 1,383$) and capsid ($N = 194$) alignments, the `Phi` test has limited power on a 247-nt alignment, and intra-capsid recombination (e.g., between shell and P2 domains) is not screened via GARD across the full 194-sequence capsid alignment.

### 2.4 Typographical Discrepancy Between Text and Table 3
- **Line 79 vs. Table 3 (Lines 93 and 105):** Line 79 of the Results states: *"Sites 231 and 209, and 238 and 504, which co-evolved as two coupled sets (Figures S3A and S3B), were not involved in recent variant transitions."* However, in Table 3, row `231` lists `x (509)` and row `509` lists `x (231)` (with no site `209` anywhere in the table). Thus, `209` in the prose is a typographical error for `509`.

## 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality

### Claim Audit Summary
- **Claim 1:** *"Using a Bayesian skyline plot (BSP) model as demographic function... significantly more negative D_F values than expected under neutrality were observed for the capsid data set (P = 0.019) and the complete polymerase data set (P = 0.018)."* — **[Well Supported]**: Directly supported by posterior predictive coalescent simulations under the inferred BSP trajectories.
- **Claim 2:** *"Eight positively selected codons were identified at p < 0.05 with the iFEL approach (6, 9, 47, 352, 372, 395, 407, 534)... Codon 394 is part of an intricate network of co-evolving positions, also containing codons 297, 368 and 372."* — **[Well Supported]**: Convergently supported by iFEL, DEPS, and BGM, and structurally localized to the surface-exposed P2 carbohydrate-binding loops on PDB `2OBT`.
- **Claim 3:** *"Secondary structure predictions of these RNA regions provide an explanation for [selection at codons 6 and 9]... Nucleotides A, C and G at position 26 all result in almost identical structures, in which 7 nucleotides from the start of ORF2... are left free. When theoretically a U/T is inserted here the predicted structure changes, resulting in a diminished length of the free nucleotide strand."* — **[Moderately Supported]**: An insightful and biologically plausible biophysical explanation for why second-position nonsynonymous changes (`AAC/ACC/AGC`) occur at codon 9 without synonymous third-position changes or `U` at position 26; the authors appropriately note that site-directed mutagenesis is required for definitive validation.
- **Claim 4:** *"We highlight codons that may prove useful in identifying emerging novel variants, and, using these, indicate that the novel 2008 variant is more likely to cause a future epidemic than the 2007 variant."* — **[Overstated]**: Forecasting future global epidemic dominance from just two amino acid substitutions at codons 393–395 (`D/NTA` vs. `STT`), when the 2008 variant is otherwise closer to 2006b across the full capsid, overreaches the predictive power of descriptive site mapping.

## 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Model parameterizations (BEAST v1.4.7 `GTR+I+Gamma4`, 40-group BSP, HyPhy GBDD with $D = 6$ rate classes selected via $\text{AIC}_c$, iFEL neutral simulation FPR calibration, DEPS, BGM) are thoroughly described, and strain metadata are listed in Table S1.
- **Gaps:** Sequence alignments and BEAST XML/tree files are not deposited in a public repository (the Methods state only that *"Sequence alignments can be obtained from the authors on request"*).
