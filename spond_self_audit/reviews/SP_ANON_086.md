### 1. Executive Summary
This study introduces a genetic algorithm (GA) framework to infer structured multi-rate codon substitution models that partition the 75 single-nucleotide-accessible amino acid exchangeabilities into $K$ discrete rate classes estimated directly from a sequence alignment. Recognizing that standard BIC over-fits when combinatorial assignment of the 75 pairs to $K$ classes is optimized alongside continuous rate parameters, the authors calibrate a modified criterion ($c\text{BIC}$, which doubles the parameter penalty $2p \log N$) via two-sequence and multi-taxon (16- and 32-taxon) parametric simulations, achieving $\le 1\%$ overfitting and high cluster recovery (Rand statistic $C_R > 0.81$). Applying the method to 7 training alignments and 4 independent validation alignments (HIV-1 *pol* subtypes B and C, Influenza A HA H5N1 and H3N2), they demonstrate that GA-selected structured models ($\text{GA}_S$, inferring $K = 3\text{--}7$ classes) and model-averaged numerical matrices ($\text{GA}_N$) consistently outperform single-rate ($S$), empirical (ECM, ECM$+\omega$), and 5-property mechanistic (LCAP) codon models, while matching or outperforming the 75-parameter general reversible codon model (REV) on out-of-sample validation data.

This is a well-executed methods paper that carefully addresses the combinatorial overfitting hazard inherent in partitioning substitution matrices on single-gene alignments by validating against out-of-sample alignments from the same viral genes. The main methodological caveats are the heuristic nature of the $2p \log N$ penalty and the computational shortcut of holding site-to-site rate heterogeneity constant during the GA structural search.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Combinatorial Degrees of Freedom and the Heuristic $c\text{BIC}$ Penalty (Pillars 3 & 4):** A structured model with $K$ rate classes has $K$ continuous rate parameters plus a discrete partition vector of length 75 mapping each amino acid pair to $\{1, \dots, K\}$ (Stirling number of the second kind $\left\{ \begin{matrix} 75 \\ K \end{matrix} \right\}$ configurations). Standard BIC counts only the continuous parameters $p$, ignoring the combinatorial entropy $\ln \left\{ \begin{matrix} 75 \\ K \end{matrix} \right\} \approx 75 \ln K$ of searching the discrete partition space. Rather than penalizing the exact combinatorial search complexity $\ln \left\{ \begin{matrix} 75 \\ K \end{matrix} \right\}$, the authors adopt an empirical penalty $2p \log(\text{characters})$ tuned on two-taxon simulations (Figure 1, Table 2). While $c\text{BIC}$ works well across the tested regime ($K \le 7$, $N \ge 500$ codons), because the true discrete complexity scales with the number of partitioned pairs ($M = 75$) whereas $2p \log(\text{characters})$ scales with $K \log(N_{\text{taxa}} \times L)$, the penalty is slightly conservative for very large alignments and only an approximate surrogate for the exact minimum description length (MDL) or prior over partitions.
* **Out-of-Sample Generalization Validation (Pillar 5):** The authors explicitly recognize that comparing an in-sample GA-selected model against fixed models (SR, ECM, LCAP) on the training alignment is asymmetric. They resolve this by freezing the rate-class structure inferred on HIV-1 Group M *pol* and seasonal IAV HA and evaluating it on four independent test alignments (HIV-1 subtype B and C *pol*; H5N1 and H3N2 HA; Table 4), where $\text{GA}_S+\text{F61}$ beats ECM, ECM$+\omega$, and LCAP on all four test sets (and beats REV on 3 of 4).
* **Decoupling of GA Search from Site-to-Site Rate Variation (Pillar 3):** For computational tractability (36–48 hours on 32 MPI nodes), the GA search assumes constant rates across sites. The authors test whether residue-pair rate classes are confounded with site-to-site rate heterogeneity on the vertebrate rhodopsin alignment (Table 5) and with multi-nucleotide substitutions (Table 6), showing roughly orthogonal log-likelihood improvements ($+428.4$ without SRV vs. $+280.4$ with bivariate SRV). However, on alignments with strong site-specific purifying selection vs. hypervariable loops, inferring exchangeability classes without site-to-site rate variation still partially conflates residue exchangeability with site conservation frequency.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Inadequacy of Single-Rate and Generalist Empirical Codon Models for Gene-Specific Evolution [Well Supported]:** Table 4 demonstrates that the generalist Pandit-trained ECM model fits viral alignments (HIV-1 *pol*, Influenza HA) significantly worse than even the 1-parameter single-rate model, whereas gene-specific $\text{GA}_S$ models capture consistent taxic and structural constraints.
* **Sensitivity of Ancestral Reconstruction and Site-Specific Selection (FEL) to Residue Exchangeability [Well Supported]:** On vertebrate rhodopsin, switching from SR to a 4-class $\text{GA}_S$ model alters inferred ancestral non-synonymous counts at 26 of 330 sites and changes FEL site-level diversifying selection calls from 1 site (codon 54) to class-specific detections (0, 2, 1, and 5 sites across the four rate classes). The authors properly caution (Discussion, line 52) that class-specific FEL testing may also inflate false positives and requires dedicated simulation calibration.
* **Biochemical Outliers in Data-Driven Rate Clusters [Well Supported]:** Correlating model-averaged $\text{GA}_N$ rates against physicochemical distances (polarity, volume, pI, hydropathy) confirms overall negative correlation while identifying specific high-rate radical exchanges in HIV-1 *pol* (e.g., $\text{C} \leftrightarrow \text{W}$ and $\text{R} \leftrightarrow \text{W}$ in the $\omega = 0.998$ cluster) driven by immune or drug selection.
