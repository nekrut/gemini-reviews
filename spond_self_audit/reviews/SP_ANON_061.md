### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript introduces a Mixed Effects Model of Evolution (MEME) designed to detect episodic and pervasive positive selection at individual codon sites along a phylogeny. The model treats the selective regime at a site as a two-component discrete mixture of non-synonymous rates ($\beta^- \le \alpha$ and unrestricted $\beta^+$) shared as fixed effects across the alignment's tree, while treating the branch-specific assignment to either selective category as an independent random effect marginalized directly within Felsenstein's pruning algorithm via mixture transition matrices. The authors validate Type I error calibration on neutral simulations across TreeBase and empirical topologies, compare statistical power against the fixed effects likelihood (FEL) model across combinations of background purifying selection ($\omega^-$), episodic positive selection ($\omega^+$), and branch proportions ($q^+$), and re-analyze 16 empirical protein-coding alignments (including vertebrate rhodopsin, JEV *env*, and camelid VHH).

The mathematical formulation and inferential validation are rigorous, directly resolving a well-known pathology of lineage-homogeneous site models where strong purifying selection on background branches masks transient bursts of adaptive evolution. Moreover, the authors explicitly test and expose the severe statistical limitations of empirical Bayes branch-site localization ("selection inference uncertainty principle"), demonstrating that neutral background branches produce a 41% false discovery rate when attempting to pinpoint individual adaptive branches at a single site. Minor limitations include the assumption of branch-to-branch independence of selective regimes, reliance on alignment-wide branch length and nucleotide substitution approximations from a simpler codon model without propagating first-stage parameter uncertainty, and primary reporting of site counts in Table 2 at unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ rather than FDR-controlled $q$-values.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Tractable Marginalization over $2^B$ Branch Configurations [Well Supported]:** By assuming branch category assignments are independent across branches, the authors show that summing over all $2^B$ branch-level rate assignments reduces algebraically to replacing each branch's transition matrix $P_b(t_b)$ with the convex combination $q^- P_b(\alpha, \beta^- | t_b) + (1 - q^-) P_b(\alpha, \beta^+ | t_b)$, keeping likelihood evaluation $O(B)$ per site rather than exponential in $B$.
* **Boundary Hypothesis Testing and Null Calibration [Well Supported]:** Under the null constraint $\beta^+ \le \alpha$, when $\beta^+ = \alpha$ or $q^+ = 0$, parameters lie on the boundary of the parameter space with non-identifiable nuisance parameters under the null. The authors derive an empirical asymptotic mixture distribution ($0.33 \chi^2_0 + 0.30 \chi^2_1 + 0.37 \chi^2_2$) under the worst-case strict neutral null ($\omega = 1$ everywhere) and verify across $N=8$ to $N>100$ taxa and 70 TreeBase trees that site-wise Type I error remains bounded near or below the nominal $\alpha = 0.05$ level.
* **Empirical Bayes Branch Identification Critique [Well Supported]:** Rather than overselling branch-level localization, the authors simulate 5 selected branches on the vertebrate rhodopsin tree and demonstrate that although empirical Bayes factors ($EBF \ge 20$) detect at least one selected branch in 60% of cases under a conserved background ($\omega^- = 0$), they yield false positives on at least one background branch in 11% of conserved-background cases and 41% of neutral-background cases. This self-critical evaluation properly bounds the method's resolution.
* **Three-Stage Plug-In Approximation and Branch Independence [Moderately Supported]:** Stage 1 fixes relative branch lengths and GTR nucleotide rates from an alignment-wide single-$\omega$ codon model before Stage 2 fits the 4 site-specific parameters ($\alpha, \beta^-, \beta^+, q^-$). Uncertainty in the tree topology and relative branch lengths is not propagated to site-level LRTs. In addition, the assumption that $\omega_b$ is independent across adjacent branches ignores heterotachy/covarion autocorrelation, and multinucleotide mutations (simultaneous double/triple substitutions within a codon) are not modeled, which can inflate $\beta^+$ on short branches if present in empirical alignments.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Superior Power for Episodic Selection Masked by Purifying Selection [Well Supported]:** Table 1 demonstrates that when $q^+ = 0.10$ or $0.25$ and background $\omega^- \in \{0, 0.2, 0.4\}$, FEL has near-zero power ($0.00 - 0.15$) while MEME achieves substantially higher power across JEV *env*, vertebrate rhodopsin, and camelid VHH topologies.
* **Resolution of the Rhodopsin $\lambda_{\max}$ and Sequence-Subsampling Paradox [Moderately Supported]:** The re-analysis of vertebrate rhodopsin explains why adding taxa previously caused constant-$\omega$ site models to lose power: purifying selection across the broader clade diluted clade-specific adaptive bursts. MEME recovers 3 of 12 experimentally characterized $\lambda_{\max}$-shifting sites (sites 96, 183, 195; vs. 0 by FEL) and appropriately notes that one-off directional sweeps (3 additional sites) cannot be captured by diversifying $\omega > 1$ models.
* **Claim that Episodic Selection is Widespread ("Nearly 4 Times as Many Sites as FEL") [Moderately Supported / Minor Overreach in Main Text]:** In Table 2 and the Discussion, the headline comparison (237 sites detected by MEME vs. 63 by FEL across 16 alignments) relies on unadjusted site-wise $p \le 0.05$. While the authors compute Benjamini-Hochberg $q$-values in Supplementary Tables S4–S19 and explicitly discuss multiple testing in the Discussion, summarizing total discovered sites across 5,075 tested codons in Table 2 at nominal $p \le 0.05$ inflates the apparent number of episodic sites with expected false positives under the null.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Equations and Numerical Consistency:** Equations in the markdown conversion have stripped inline mathematical symbols (leaving blank spaces where variables like $\omega$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, and numbers in the text were rendered as images/math macros in the original source), though the mathematical structure and Table 1–2 numerical entries are intact and internally consistent (e.g., Table 2 sums: $130 + 51 + 56 = 237$ MEME+ sites; $51 + 12 = 63$ FEL+ sites).
* **Code and Data Availability:** The method is implemented in the open-source HyPhy package and hosted on the Datamonkey web server, with all 16 empirical alignments and simulation scripts referenced in Supplementary Material.
