### 1. Executive Summary
`SP_ANON_158` develops and validates two HIV-1-specific empirical amino-acid substitution rate matrices—`HIV-Bm` (between-host, trained on 97 alignments across all 9 HIV-1 genes totaling 1,093 sequences) and `HIV-Wm` (within-host, trained on 50 longitudinal/single-host alignments totaling 927 sequences)—using joint maximum-likelihood optimization in HyPhy with a 5-bin discretized $\beta\text{--}\Gamma$ site-to-site rate heterogeneity distribution. The authors evaluate out-of-sample performance across 47 within-host and 11 between-host test alignments (ensuring reciprocal monophyly between training and test sets) against 19 general, viral (`rtREV`), and mechanistic codon-derived models using $\text{AIC}_c$ and Shimodaira-Hasegawa (SH) tests, and derive corresponding log-odds pairwise alignment scoring matrices (`HIV-Bs` and `HIV-Ws`). The methodology is rigorous and well-validated, with minor caveats regarding $\text{AIC}_c$ sample-size parameterization and a basic biochemical typo in the Introduction.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Joint ML Rate Matrix Estimation & Out-of-Sample Validation (`Well Executed`):** Unlike early counting methods (`Dayhoff`/`BLOSUM`), the authors jointly optimize all 189 free exchangeability parameters (with zero-rate pseudocount regularization of $10^{-5}$ to prevent boundary lock-in during variable-metric optimization) and equilibrium frequencies across dozens of genes while accommodating flexible lineage-specific rate variation via a 5-bin $\beta\text{--}\Gamma$ distribution. Enforceable reciprocal monophyly between training and validation alignments prevents phylogenetic data leakage.
- **Definition of Sample Size $s$ in $\text{AIC}_c$ (`Line 54`):** In the small-sample corrected Akaike Information Criterion, $\text{AIC}_c = 2(-L + ps/(s - p - 1))$, the authors set sample size $s$ equal to the number of amino-acid alignment columns (`79 to 357` sites in Table 2). While common in phylogenetics, when evaluating the unconstrained alignment-specific `REV` model ($p = 189$ rate parameters plus branch lengths), $s - p - 1$ approaches zero or becomes negative for shorter genes (`vpr, tat, vpu, rev`), creating an extreme or undefined penalty against `REV`. The authors also run non-parametric Shimodaira-Hasegawa column-permutation tests (`Table 4`), which confirm `HIV-Bm`/`HIV-Wm` superiority over existing empirical models independently of $\text{AIC}_c$ penalties.
- **Subtype B Training Restriction:** Both `HIV-Bm` and `HIV-Wm` were trained exclusively on Subtype B alignments (`Line 38`). Although the authors demonstrate strong generalization to Subtype C (`ENV-C`, `POL-C`) and Group M (`GAG-M`, `POL-M`, `ENV-M`) between-host alignments (`Table 4`), subtype-specific codon usage and epistatic constraints in non-B subtypes are not directly represented in the training data.
- **Factual Typo in Nucleotide Chemistry (`Line 12`):** Introduction Line 12 states: *"whether the bases are purines (adenine and cytosine) or pyrimidines (guanine and thymine)"*—adenine (A) and guanine (G) are purines, whereas cytosine (C) and thymine (T) are pyrimidines. Additionally, several inline mathematical equations in the Methods (`Lines 42, 44, 54, 62, 64`) were stripped during text extraction.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: HIV-1 amino-acid substitution patterns differ fundamentally from general cellular (`WAG`, `JTT`) and retroviral (`rtREV`) empirical matrices, with `HIV-Bm` and `HIV-Wm` improving log-likelihood on all 58 validation alignments (`[Well Supported]`).** Across all 47 within-host and 11 between-host test alignments, `HIV-Bm`/`HIV-Wm` consistently outperform `JTT`, `WAG`, `Dayhoff`, and `rtREV` by large $\Delta\text{AIC}_c$ margins (up to $>1,000$ $\text{AIC}_c$ points on group M alignments) and statistically significant SH tests ($p < 0.05$ on 10/11 between-host alignments).
- **Claim 2: The HIV-1 rate matrix is strongly structured by the genetic code, favoring single-nucleotide nonsynonymous transitions (such as $I \leftrightarrow V$, $K \leftrightarrow R$, $D \leftrightarrow N$, $E \leftrightarrow K$) and strongly penalizing multi-nucleotide amino-acid exchanges (`[Well Supported]`).** The bubble plots (`Figure 2`) and PAM-distance divergence comparisons (`Table 5`) clearly show that at short evolutionary distances within HIV-1, single-step transition-accessible amino-acid exchanges occur at rates up to an order of magnitude higher than in `WAG` or `rtREV`.
- **Claim 3: `HIV-Wm` and `HIV-Bm` perform comparably across within-host and between-host datasets (`[Well Supported]`).** Cross-application on the validation panels shows that both HIV-specific matrices capture essentially the same core biochemical and mutational hierarchy, with `HIV-Bm` slightly favored overall due to its larger training sample size.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Model & Code Availability:** The `HIV-Bm` and `HIV-Wm` rate matrices and scoring matrices (`HIV-Bs`, `HIV-Ws`) are directly integrated into the open-source `HyPhy` distribution and provided in the manuscript/supplementary tables (`Tables 2–6`).
- **Data Completeness:** Training and validation alignment summary statistics are fully tabulated (`Tables 1–2`), with LANL/GenBank source alignments available upon request.
