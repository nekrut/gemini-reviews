### 1. Executive Summary
This methods manuscript introduces `MoleRate`, a maximum-likelihood phylogenetic relative evolutionary rate test implemented in HyPhy (v2.5.71+) for detecting gene-wide acceleration or deceleration of nucleotide or protein substitution rates along phenotype-labeled foreground branches relative to a genome-wide reference tree $T_\theta$. Unlike two-step post-hoc regression approaches (such as `RERconverge`) that treat pre-estimated branch lengths as exact point observations, `MoleRate` embeds proportional branch-length scaling directly into the phylogenetic likelihood function, comparing a 1-parameter global scaling null (`Proportional`) against a 2-parameter foreground/background scaling alternative (`Proportional Partitioned`), an `Unconstrained Test` model ($L+1$ parameters), and a leave-one-out single-branch `Proportional + 1` model for outlier detection. The authors evaluate `MoleRate` on 30,000 simulated protein alignments across an avian phylogeny and apply it to 19,149 UCSC mammalian gene alignments across three convergent phenotypes (subterranean, marine, and hairless) plus a 6-species random control, corroborating rate shifts with codon-level `RELAX` analyses and benchmarking precision–recall against `RERconverge`.

The statistical formulation of `MoleRate` is clean, computationally fast (~12 seconds per alignment), and addresses two genuine weaknesses of existing relative-rate pipelines: (1) failure to account for estimation variance on short or information-poor branches, and (2) high vulnerability to single-lineage assembly/alignment artifacts masquerading as multi-lineage convergence (demonstrated convincingly here, where $>50\%$ of raw $\text{FDR} \le 0.01$ hits—such as *SEPT7* in the star-nosed mole—are driven by single-branch outliers). The manuscript is well-calibrated and transparent about the limitations of protein rate shifts (which cannot distinguish positive selection from relaxed purifying selection without follow-up codon tests like `RELAX`). Several mathematical notation and table reporting errors in the text should be cleaned up prior to publication.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Direct phylogenetic likelihood formulation of relative rate tests ([Well Supported]):** Modeling gene-specific branch lengths $g_i = R_L t_i$ on foreground branches and $g_i = R_\theta t_i$ on background branches within the substitution likelihood function naturally weights branches by their sequence information content and Poisson substitution variance. The simulation study (Fig. 3, Figs. S1–S3) confirms proper Type I error control ($\le 0.05$ at $\alpha = 0.05$) under the null ($R_L = R_\theta = 1$), robustness to substitution matrix misspecification (`JC69` vs. `WAG`), and predictable power gains with alignment length and effect size.
* **Leave-one-out branch outlier filtering (`Proportional + 1`) ([Well Supported]):** The two-part branch classification procedure—testing `Proportional` vs. `Proportional + 1` (1 df) to identify single-branch shifts, and `Proportional + 1` vs. `Unconstrained Test` ($L-1$ df) to verify residual signal across the remaining $L-1$ foreground branches—provides a practical safeguard against assembly and annotation errors in comparative genomics datasets. Table 4 demonstrates that across all tested phenotypes, between 23% and 52% of genes passing $\text{FDR} \le 0.01$ are driven by a single outlier branch.
* **Precision–recall benchmark against `RERconverge` on small background trees ([Moderately Supported]):** Figure 4 shows that when the background species tree is subsampled to 20 species, `MoleRate` (especially with outlier filtering) achieves higher precision fold-change at comparable recall on curated ocular gene sets than `RERconverge`. Two minor caveats apply: first, the figure caption for Figure 4 (line 297) erroneously states *"using all background species"*, contradicting the main text on line 295 and 299 which specifies *"a tree with 20 background species"* (whereas the full species set is in Figure S6). Second, because true positive convergent gene sets are unknown in empirical data, curated MGI/HPA ocular gene lists serve as proxy silver standards primarily reflecting relaxed purifying selection in subterranean mammals.
* **Degrees of freedom and mathematical typos in tables and text ([Moderately Supported]):**
  * In Table 2 (line 96), the degrees of freedom for `Proportional` (1 parameter: $R$) vs. `Unconstrained*` ($N$ parameters: $N$ branch lengths) is listed as `N`, whereas it should be `N - 1` (consistent with `Proportional Partitioned` [2 parameters] vs. `Unconstrained*` [$N$ parameters] having `N - 2` df on line 98). Similarly, `Proportional` vs. `Unconstrained Test` ($L + 1$ parameters: $R_\theta$ plus $L$ foreground lengths) has $L$ df, which is correct.
  * In Table 3 (line 153), the log-likelihood difference between `Proportional` ($\ln L = -10473.6$) and `Proportional Partitioned` ($\ln L = -10471.9$) is $\Delta \ln L = 1.7$ ($2\Delta \ln L = 3.4$, which corresponds to $p = 0.065$ on $\chi^2_1$), yet the table lists the Holm-Bonferroni corrected p-value as `~0`. Similarly, `Unconstrained Test` has $\ln L = -10471.1$ ($\Delta \ln L = 0.8$ vs. `Proportional Partitioned`), yet its p-value is also listed as `~0`. Either the log-likelihood values in Table 3 were transcribed from a different model/gene or there is a typographical error in the integer digits of $\ln L$.
  * In the caption of Table 3 (line 148), the text reads *"MoleRate applied to the KRT10 gene with the three subterranean ."*—a copy-paste error since the section and text (line 144–146) analyze *KRT10* in **hairless** mammals (where 6 branches drive the signal).
  * On line 247, intensified selection in subterranean decelerated genes is written as `(RELAX K<1)`, whereas intensified selection in `RELAX` corresponds to $K > 1$ (as correctly stated on lines 245, 254, 256, and 263).
  * On line 263 (under the *Hairless phenotype* section), the text states *"On the other hand, for marine decelerated genes, 20/37 genes..."* where `"marine"` is a typo for `"hairless"` (since Table 4 shows hairless has 37 decelerated genes).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Distinguishing rate acceleration from positive selection via `RELAX` ([Well Supported]):** The authors explicitly emphasize that accelerated protein evolution ($R_L / R_\theta \ge 2$) commonly reflects relaxation of purifying selection upon trait loss rather than adaptive positive selection, and they substantiate this by running `RELAX` on the candidate sets—showing that $80/113$ subterranean accelerated genes (enriched for eye lens and visual perception) and $82/120$ marine accelerated genes (enriched for olfactory receptors) experience statistically significant relaxation of constraint ($K < 1$).
* **Negative control via random species labeling ([Well Supported]):** Labeling 6 random mammalian species yielded individual false-positive/lineage-specific gene hits (as expected when testing ~19,000 genes across real lineages with varying life histories) but zero significantly enriched functional or pathway categories in STRING-DB, validating the specificity of the phenotype-level functional enrichments.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Exemplary software, simulation data, and visualization availability:** `MoleRate` is integrated into open-source HyPhy (v2.5.71+), the batch scripts and documentation are hosted on GitHub (`veg/hyphy-analyses` and `veg/molerate`), and interactive ObservableHQ notebooks are provided for single-gene and genome-wide exploration.
* **Editorial cleanup needed:** Fixing the Table 2 df (`N-1`), checking the Table 3 log-likelihoods (`-10473.6` vs. `-10471.9`) and caption (`subterranean` $\to$ `hairless`), fixing the Figure 4 caption (`all background species` $\to$ `20 background species`), and correcting the `RELAX K<1` / `marine` typos on lines 247 and 263 will make the manuscript technically airtight.
