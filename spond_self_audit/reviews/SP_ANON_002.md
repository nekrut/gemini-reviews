### 1. Executive Summary
This study introduces a statistical and mathematical framework for contrasting within-host polymorphism against between-host divergence in rapidly evolving RNA viruses by comparing site-specific nonsynonymous and synonymous IUPAC base-calling mixtures ($m_N - m_S$) in bulk population Sanger sequences to phylogenetic substitution rates ($d_N - d_S$ via `SLAC`). Applying this approach to HIV-1 protease ($n=3,458$), reverse transcriptase ($n=1,997$), HCV E1 ($n=2,691$ raw chromatograms), and HCV E2 HVR1 ($n=346$), and coupling it with stochastic Moran and deterministic ODE models of within-host CTL escape, reversion, and transmission bottlenecks, the authors demonstrate that within-host and between-host selective signatures frequently diverge. The theoretical integration is rigorous and insightful, tempered primarily by heterogeneous base-calling mixture ascertainment in public sequence repositories.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Mathematical and Stochastic Modeling Rigor:** The two-scale population genetic model (stochastic birth–death Moran process with population size $N=1,000$, escape rate $e$, reversion rate $r$, and transmission bottleneck followed by deterministic ODE approximation) is mathematically sound and transparently parameterized. It cleanly demonstrates how sites with rapid within-host escape/reversion relative to transmission intervals generate detectable polymorphism mixtures without proportional net phylogenetic branch substitutions, and vice versa.
* **Heterogeneous Base-Calling Mixture Ascertainment (HIVDB vs. Phred HCV Chromatograms):** For HCV E1, the authors re-called raw ABI chromatograms uniformly using `Phred` at controlled secondary peak ratios (`0.1` to `0.5`). In contrast, the HIV-1 PR/RT and HCV E2 HVR1 datasets rely on pre-existing IUPAC ambiguity codes deposited in the Stanford HIV Drug Resistance Database and GenBank across diverse clinical laboratories, sequencing chemistries, and manual chromatogram editing practices. Because clinical HIV genotyping laboratories often manually curate or preferentially call mixtures at known drug-resistance codons while ignoring background secondary peaks elsewhere, site-to-site variation in $m_N$ and $m_S$ in HIVDB is partially confounded by operator reporting bias.
* **Consensus Resolution of Ambiguous Bases Prior to `SLAC` Inference:** Before running `SLAC` to estimate phylogenetic $d_N$ and $d_S$, all ambiguous nucleotides were resolved to the most frequent base at that alignment column (page/line 74). Resolving within-host mixtures to the population consensus systematically strips polymorphisms located on terminal branches, artificially deflating terminal-branch $d_N$ and $d_S$ at polymorphic sites and exaggerating the contrast between $m_N > m_S$ and $d_N < d_S$. Running `SLAC` with native `HyPhy` ambiguity handling (or comparing internal-branch-only $d_N - d_S$) would avoid this induced negative bias.
* **Poisson Binomial / Exact Binomial Testing of $m_N$ vs. $m_S$:** The site-level binomial test comparing observed nonsynonymous mixtures to the expected sequence-weighted opportunity $P(\text{nonsynonymous})$ is well formulated, and the authors appropriately report both nominal $p < 0.01$ and false discovery rates ($\text{FDR} = 0.20$).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *Bulk Sanger chromatograms and database sequences contain quantifiable site-specific signals of within-host purifying and diversifying selection ($m_N - m_S$) that generally parallel overall selective constraints.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Validated across four viral datasets and confirmed via sensitivity sweeps across `Phred` secondary-peak thresholds (`0.1`–`0.5`) on 2,691 raw HCV E1 traces.
* **Claim 2:** *Discrepancies between within-host mixture selection ($m_N - m_S$) and between-host phylogenetic selection ($d_N - d_S$) arise naturally from the interplay of CTL escape/reversion rates, HLA polymorphism frequency, and transmission bottlenecks.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Analytical and simulation results directly substantiate why rare HLA-restricted epitopes or deleterious escape mutations reverting upon transmission yield discordant within- vs. between-host signals.
* **Claim 3:** *Specific discordant codons in HIV-1 PR/RT represent biological trade-offs between within-host adaptation and between-host transmission fitness.* **`[Moderately Supported]`** — Biologically plausible and supported by known resistance/epitope annotations, though partially vulnerable to uneven clinical mixture reporting in Stanford HIVDB and consensus-resolution prior to `SLAC`.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Mathematical equations, parameter tables, and statistical test formulations are presented in full detail within the text and appendices, and the public databases (`Stanford HIVDB`, `LANL HCV`) are clearly specified.
* **Deficiencies:** No direct URL or repository archive is provided for the custom `HyPhy` batch scripts, the Moran/ODE simulation code, or the processed sequence alignments and `Phred`-called HCV E1 mixture matrices.
