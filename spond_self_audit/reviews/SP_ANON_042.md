### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript investigates the clinical, virological, and immunological correlates of broadly neutralizing antibody (bnAb) development and maps the targeted Env epitopes in the IAVI Protocol C longitudinal primary HIV-1 infection cohort across five African countries ($N=439$ ART-naive participants evaluated across 2,220 longitudinal plasma samples from month 24 to 90 post-infection, including $N=228$ followed for $\ge 48$ months). Using a validated 6-pseudovirus cross-clade panel (calibrated against 37- and 105-virus panels), the authors find that 15% of participants followed $\ge 48$ months develop broad neutralization (score $\ge 1$, corresponding to $\ge 50\%$ breadth), typically plateauing between years 2 and 4. Set-point and longitudinal viral load, low CD4 T-cell counts after month 6, total and Env-specific IgG binding titers (without increased bulk avidity), subtype C, and HLA-A*03(-) status correlate with breadth in bivariate analyses, with viral load remaining the dominant independent predictor in multivariable modeling. Epitope mapping of the top 42 broad neutralizers reveals that glycan-dependent specificities—predominantly the N332-glycan V3 supersite (40%)—account for 60% of broad responses, whereas broad CD4bs and gp41-MPER neutralizers are rare despite frequent non-neutralizing or narrow binding antibodies to those regions.

This is a comprehensive, well-executed longitudinal cohort and experimental mapping study. Its primary methodological weaknesses are restricted to specific statistical modeling choices: (1) adding an arbitrary small constant ($+0.0001$) to zero neutralization scores in a log-link Gamma GLM, (2) conditioning the Kaplan-Meier time-to-breadth curves (Fig. 2B) exclusively on the subset of participants who eventually achieved neutralization score $\ge 0.5$ ($N=157$), and (3) unmodeled survivor/ART-initiation selection bias when restricting correlates analysis to participants who remained ART-naive for $\ge 48$ months.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Log-Link Gamma GLM with $+0.0001$ Zero-Value Shift (line 87):** Neutralization scores on the 6-virus panel include exact zeros (8% of the cohort neutralized 0/6 viruses). Because the Gamma distribution is strictly supported on $(0, \infty)$ and uses a $\log(\mu)$ link, the authors added $0.0001$ to all scores to avoid $\log(0)$. On a log scale where non-zero scores lie between $0.1$ and $2.33$ ($\ln(y) \in [-2.3, 0.85]$), shifting zeros to $10^{-4}$ places them at $\ln(10^{-4}) = -9.21$, creating high-leverage boundary points that depend arbitrarily on the chosen epsilon ($10^{-4}$ vs. $10^{-2}$). A two-part hurdle model (logistic regression for score $>0$ plus Gamma GLM for positive scores), a Tweedie compound Poisson-gamma GLM, or ordinal regression would handle structural zeros without arbitrary log-scale distortion.
* **Post-Hoc Conditioning on Eventual Responders in Kaplan-Meier Analysis (Fig. 2B, line 35):** While the Methods (line 89) mention running Kaplan-Meier analyses both on all $N=439$ participants and on responders only, Fig. 2B presents the Kaplan-Meier curves restricted solely to the $N=157$ participants who eventually achieved a neutralization score $\ge 0.5$. Conditioning a time-to-event survival analysis on future occurrence of the event excludes right-censored non-responders, conflating the speed of response among responders with overall cumulative incidence and inducing collider/selection bias across baseline clinical strata.
* **Survivor / ART-Free Selection Bias in the M48+ Subset:** Restricting the correlates analysis to the $N=228$ M48+ subset appropriately eliminates confounding by follow-up duration and number of visits tested (which correlated with peak score in the full $N=439$ cohort; S5A Fig). However, eligibility required remaining ART-naive for at least 48 months post-infection. Participants with rapid CD4 decline or severe clinical progression who initiated ART before month 48 were excluded from the M48+ subset, truncating the upper tail of disease progression and potentially attenuating associations with baseline CD4 count and protective/susceptible HLA alleles.
* **Visit-Code Binning and Peak-Score Selection:** Taking the maximum neutralization score across a variable number of visits (1–12 samples per donor) introduces an upward order-statistic bias in donors with more frequent sampling; although insignificant at $p < 0.05$ within the M48+ subset, linear mixed-effects modeling of longitudinal score trajectories (including visit month as a spline or random slope) would utilize all 2,220 measurements without reducing each donor to a single peak summary.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Multi-Assay Epitope Mapping Rigor:** The experimental dissection of bnAb specificities across the top 42 neutralizers is thorough and well-controlled. Rather than relying on a single indicator virus, the authors combine multi-strain monomeric rgp120 bead depletions (verified by ELISA and paired with saturating b6 competition to isolate CD4bs specificities), RSC3/KO-RSC3 and TriMut core competition, HIV-2/HIV-1 MPER chimeras with MPER peptide competition, kifunensine glycosidase inhibition, and panel-wide site-directed mutagenesis (N160K, V2 apex 166/169/171, N332 supersite, and gp120/gp41 interface).
* **Claim Proportionality:** The authors' biological interpretations are measured and proportional to the statistical evidence. They explicitly note that the bivariate associations of HIV-1 subtype C and HLA-A*03(-) with neutralization breadth become barely significant in multivariable GLM once set-point viral load is controlled for (Fig. 2A, line 64), and they appropriately interpret low CD4 T-cell counts after month 6 as a downstream consequence of high viral replication rather than an independent causal driver. They also note the caveat that bulk gp120 NaSCN elution avidity measures total binding antibodies and may mask affinity differences within the smaller neutralizing antibody fraction (line 43).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Experimental Transparency:** Wet-lab protocols, recombinant protein expression/purification steps, competition concentrations, pseudovirus panels, and the mathematical formula for the 6-virus neutralization score ($\text{Score} = \text{Average}(\log_3(\text{dilution}/100) + 1)$) are fully specified in the Materials and Methods.
* **Data & Code Availability:** While heatmaps and summary tables are referenced in S1 Text, the participant-level longitudinal dataset (2,220 neutralization scores, clinical covariates, and ELISA titers) and the R Bioconductor statistical scripts used for the GLM and survival analyses are not deposited in a public repository.
