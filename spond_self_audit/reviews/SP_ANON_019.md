### 1. Executive Summary
This methods manuscript introduces `RelTime with Dated Tips` (`RTDT`), an algebraic tip-dating algorithm implemented in `MEGA X` that extends the relative rate framework (`RRF`) of `RelTime` to heterochronous pathogen phylogenies without requiring Bayesian clock or coalescent priors. Through an extensive node-by-node simulation benchmark across constant (`CBR`), independent (`IBR`), and autocorrelated (`ABR`) branch-rate models on HIV-1 subtype F and Influenza A topologies—plus previously published `To et al.` (`LSD`) and `Sagulenko et al.` (`TreeTime`) simulated datasets and six empirical viral datasets—the authors compare `RTDT` point-estimate accuracy (`ME`, `RMSE`), 95% confidence interval (`CI`) coverage probabilities, and runtime against `BEAST`, `MCMCTree`, `LSD`, `TreeTime`, and `treedater`. The benchmarking is broad and informative, demonstrating strong `RTDT` performance under `ABR` rate variation and rapid execution, though several algorithmic constraints and simulation filtering choices warrant scrutiny.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Dependence on Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Root-to-Tip Regression for Temporal Calibration:** As derived in Figure 1B–F (lines 78–86), `RTDT` first fits a simple ordinary least squares (OLS) linear regression of root-to-tip path length ($\lambda_i$) against tip sampling time ($t_i$) to project tips forward to an "internal-root" ($\lambda = 0$), then algebraically rescales sister branch lengths using geometric-mean relative rates $(r_1 \times r_2)^{1/2} = 1$, and finally converts projected internal node lengths back into calendar dates using the initial OLS regression line. Because tips sharing deep internal branches are phylogenetically non-independent, OLS root-to-tip regression weights heavily sampled clades disproportionately and breaks down when the global root-to-tip correlation ($r^2$) is low or when rate shifts coincide with unbalanced sampling epochs—explaining why `RTDT` deteriorates sharply when only 3 sampling time points exist ($r^2 < 0.3$, Fig. 6D) or on the empirical `Rabies` and `HIV-2` datasets (line 142).
* **Requirement for an Known Outgroup-Rooted Topology:** Unlike `BEAST`, `TreeTime`, `LSD`, and `treedater` (which can co-estimate or optimize the root position using temporal information), `RTDT` strictly requires an outgroup-rooted phylogeny (line 74). In many rapidly evolving outbreak or intra-host viral datasets, choosing a distant outgroup introduces long-branch attraction and substitution saturation on the basal ingroup stem, which directly perturbs the root-to-tip path lengths $\lambda_i$ used in Step 1 of `RTDT`.
* **Exclusion of Simulated Datasets Containing Identical Sequences:** In the Materials and Methods (Tail lines 10, 14), the authors state that they discarded any simulated dataset containing at least two identical sequences between different taxa (removing `1` `IBR` and `7` `ABR` HIV-1 datasets, `12` `ABR` Influenza datasets, and `77/400` `To et al.` datasets) because "identical sequences contain no information for sequence divergence." In real pathogen surveillance (and under `ABR` simulations where low-rate lineages produce zero-length terminal branches), identical sequences sampled at different times are ubiquitous and informative for evolutionary rate upper bounds; selectively discarding `19%` of `To et al.` replicates and `24%` of Influenza `ABR` replicates removes zero-branch-length boundary cases that challenge geometric-mean rate rescaling $(r_1 \times r_2)^{1/2} = 1$.
* **Analytical Confidence Interval Coverage vs. Width Trade-Off:** `RTDT` achieves notably higher 95% CI coverage probabilities ($82\%\text{–}97\%$) than `LSD` and `TreeTime` ($23\%\text{–}77\%$; Figs. 4 and 5C). However, coverage probability alone does not report interval width; in Table 1 (`Rabies` nodes 1–4), `RTDT`'s 95% CIs span `1936–1971`, `1936–1972`, `1936–1973`, and `1936–1973` while the point estimates are `1967, 1971, 1982, 1973`—Note that for `Rabies Node 3`, the point estimate `1982` lies **outside** its own reported 95% CI `(1936–1973)` (Main text line 49)! This numerical anomaly in Table 1 (`1982 (1936–1973)`) indicates either a typographical error or a boundary truncation bug in the analytical CI formula when root-to-tip $r^2$ is poor.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *`RTDT` accurately estimates node divergence times and provides well-calibrated 95% CIs on heterochronous phylogenies with substantial temporal signal ($r^2 > 0.3$), outperforming `BEAST` (lognormal IBR) and fast non-Bayesian methods (`LSD`, `TreeTime`, `treedater`) when branch rates are autocorrelated (`ABR`).* **`[Well Supported]`** — Thoroughly demonstrated across node-by-node `ME`, `RMSE`, and coverage metrics on simulated HIV-1 and Influenza A `ABR` datasets.
* **Claim 2:** *When temporal signal is weak ($r^2 < 0.3$) or distinct sampling dates are sparse (e.g., 3 time points, or empirical `Rabies` and `HIV-2` datasets), all tip-dating methods suffer reduced accuracy and Bayesian methods (`BEAST` / `MCMCTree` with `CorrTest`-selected clock priors) outperform `RTDT`.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Candidly demonstrated and incorporated into the user decision guidelines in the Discussion.
* **Claim 3:** *`RTDT` is orders of magnitude faster than `BEAST` and `MCMCTree`, completing 289-taxon timetree and CI inference within minutes on a single CPU core.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Directly benchmarked in Figure 8.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Simulated alignments and true/inferred trees are deposited on GitHub (`https://github.com/cathyqqtao/RTDT`), and `RTDT` is freely available in cross-platform `MEGA X` (`v10.1+`).
* **Deficiencies:** Table 1 row `Rabies Node 3` lists `1982 (1936–1973)`, where the point estimate `1982` falls outside the reported 95% CI `(1936–1973)`; additionally, behavior on zero-length branches (identical sequences) should be benchmarked rather than filtered out.
