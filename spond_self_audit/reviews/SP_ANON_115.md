### 1. Executive Summary
This critique and re-analysis refutes the hypothesis of Xia (2020, *Mol. Biol. Evol.*) that feral dogs served as an intermediate host for the ancestor of SARS-CoV-2 based on low genomic CpG dinucleotide deficiency ($I_{\text{CpG}}$). The authors identify three fatal methodological and biological flaws in Xia's inference: (1) **omission of closely related sarbecoviruses and host controls**: by adding seven pangolin betacoronaviruses, the bat sarbecovirus `RmYN02`, four additional canine alphacoronaviruses, and two pangolin pestiviruses to Xia's $I_{\text{CpG}}$ vs. $\text{GC}\%$ dataset (Fig. 1, Table S1), the authors show that multiple bat and pangolin sarbecoviruses exhibit $I_{\text{CpG}}$ and $\text{GC}\%$ values indistinguishable from SARS-CoV-2 and `RaTG13`, directly falsifying Xia's claim that no natural-host betacoronaviruses share this compositional profile; (2) **confounding of convergent compositional statistics with host identity and adaptive selection**: dinucleotide composition ($I_{\text{CpG}}$) across distantly related viral genera (*Alphacoronavirus* vs. *Betacoronavirus*) is shaped by mutational biases, drift, and shared mammalian innate immune factors (ZAP and APOBEC3G, which the authors verify via comparative genomics in Tables S2–S3 are conserved in bats and pangolins just as in dogs and humans) and is a known poor predictor of host species; and (3) **phylogenetic and experimental implausibility**: attributing low $I_{\text{CpG}}$ in the common ancestor of SARS-CoV-2, `RaTG13`, `RmYN02`, and pangolin sarbecoviruses to passage through dog intestines requires an unparsimonious back-and-forth cross-species transmission chain (bats $\to$ dogs $\to$ bats/pangolins $\to$ humans, Fig. 2) and contradicts experimental inoculation and ACE2 structural modeling studies showing low canine susceptibility to SARS-CoV-2.

This is a rigorous, lucid, and proportionate forensic rebuttal. Every counter-argument is grounded in direct empirical re-analysis (expanding the $I_{\text{CpG}}$ vs. $\text{GC}\%$ dataset with omitted pangolin/bat sarbecoviruses and orthology checks for mammalian *ZAP*/*APOBEC3G*), standard molecular evolutionary null models (mutation-drift vs. unsubstantiated adaptive storytelling), and phylogenetic parsimony.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Direct empirical refutation of ascertainment bias in Xia (2020) ([Well Supported]):** Xia's core argument rested on an apparent gap in the $(I_{\text{CpG}}, \text{GC}\%)$ scatter plot between SARS-CoV-2/`RaTG13` and other betacoronaviruses. By computing the exact same indices ($I_{\text{CpG}}$ and genomic $\text{GC}\%$, Fig. 1 and Table S1) for the closely related bat virus `RmYN02` and seven pangolin betacoronaviruses that were omitted from Xia's figure, the authors show that the entire bat/pangolin sarbecovirus clade clusters together at low $I_{\text{CpG}}$ and low $\text{GC}\%$, eliminating the statistical outlier status upon which Xia's canine alphacoronavirus analogy was built.
* **Expose of phylogenetic non-parsimony in compositional host attribution ([Well Supported]):** Because low $I_{\text{CpG}}$ is a shared ancestral character (plesiomorphy/synapomorphy) of the entire SARS-CoV-2 + `RaTG13` + `RmYN02` + `Pangolin-2019` clade (which diverged 20–70 years ago), invoking canine gastrointestinal selection to explain that CpG depletion logically forces the ancestral lineage to have evolved in dogs *before* radiating back into horseshoe bats (`RaTG13`, `RmYN02`) and pangolins (Fig. 2)—an unparsimonious multi-jump scenario when bats and pangolins themselves encode functional *ZAP* and *APOBEC3G* (Tables S2–S3).
* **Critique of one-dimensional dinucleotide summary metrics ([Well Supported]):** The authors correctly point out that gross genomic ratios like $I_{\text{CpG}} = P_{\text{CpG}} / (P_{\text{C}} P_{\text{G}})$ conflate lineage-specific mutational biases ($C \to U$ deamination, replication fidelity), GC content, reading-frame constraints, and selection across unrelated viral families (illustrated by pangolin pestiviruses having even lower $I_{\text{CpG}}$ than SARS-CoV-2 in the same host species).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Comparative genomics of *ZAP* (`ZC3HAV1`) and *APOBEC3G* across mammalian hosts ([Well Supported]):** To test whether canine tissues possess any unique mechanistic machinery for CpG targeting, the authors compare mammalian orthologs of *ZAP* and *APOBEC3G* (Tables S2–S3), confirming that bats and pangolins possess conserved *ZAP* and *APOBEC3G* genes (with bat and pangolin *APOBEC3G* actually showing higher sequence similarity to human *APOBEC3G* than dog does).
* **Integration of experimental challenge and ACE2 receptor-binding evidence ([Well Supported]):** The manuscript triangulates its evolutionary critique with experimental dog challenge data (Shi et al. 2020, showing poor replication and zero detectable viral RNA in dog organs) and comparative ACE2 binding affinity models (Damas et al. 2020), while maintaining appropriate epistemic humility that wildlife sampling remains incomplete and the proximal progenitor over the last 20–70 years is still unsampled.
