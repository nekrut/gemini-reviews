# Blinded Review: SP_ANON_088
**Composite Score:** 7.1 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.3/3.5 | Claims: 1.9/2.5 | Biology: 1.5/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.4/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Pre-Selection Conditioning & Asymmetric Branch Power in Selection Scans
**Verdict:** Minor Revision
**Study Modality:** Hybrid Computational + Wet-Lab Functional Validation
**Technology Maturity:** Mid-Stage High-Throughput
**Epistemic Stage:** Basic/Mechanistic Discovery

### 1. Executive Summary
This study generates a chromosome-level PacBio HiFi/Hi-C reference genome for the lesser salmon catfish (*Neoarius graeffei*, $2.34\text{ Gbp}$, N50 $84\text{ Mbp}$, $97.8\%$ BUSCO) alongside 66 short-read Illumina assemblies, 18 exon-capture datasets, and 36 legacy-marker taxa (totaling 119 of $\sim 157$ sea catfish species in Ariidae) to investigate genomic and morphometric evolution across three macroevolutionary habitat transitions: marine-to-freshwater, stenohaline-to-euryhaline, and benthic-to-pelagic planktivory. Integrating SIMMAP ancestral state reconstructions across 10 time-calibrated phylogenies with HyPhy selection tests (BUSTED-E, aBSREL, BUSTED-PH, RELAX, and MEME) on 2,310 coding alignments (249 candidate and 2,061 non-candidate single-copy orthologs), the authors filter to 119 alignments passing BUSTED-E ($q < 0.05$) and compare positively selected gene (PSG) counts between ancestral vs. derived habitats and between the Australia–New Guinea (AU-NG) adaptive radiation vs. non-radiating freshwater clades. They report more PSGs in younger freshwater lineages (61 PSGs, $22\text{--}39\%$ of tree time) than in ancestral marine lineages (43 PSGs, $61\text{--}78\%$ of tree time), highlight episodic selection on prolactin (*PRL*) along the AU-NG freshwater stem and *FGF4* in the high-raker planktivore *Brustiarius nox*, and report no significant habitat differences in transposable element content after phylogenetic ANOVA FDR correction.

The genomic data generation and multi-tree topological sensitivity framework (requiring PSG support in $\ge 9$ of 10 trees) are substantial strengths, as is the application of BUSTED-E to absorb alignment/assembly error in short-read RagTag assemblies. However, several statistical conditioning and power asymmetries weaken the quantitative comparisons of PSG counts across habitats and candidate gene sets.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Two-Stage Pre-Selection Conditioning on BUSTED-E (Pillars 3 & 4):** In the Methods (line 34) and Table 1, the authors first screen all 2,310 genes with BUSTED-E (an alignment-wide test for episodic diversifying selection anywhere on the tree while absorbing error into an anomalous sink rate class), retaining only the 119 genes significant under BUSTED-E, and *only then* run aBSREL, BUSTED-PH, and RELAX on those 119 genes across the three habitat partitions. Conditioning branch-specific (aBSREL), phenotype-associated (BUSTED-PH), and selection-intensity (RELAX) tests strictly on the 119 genes that already passed an unpartitioned tree-wide BUSTED-E screen introduces selection bias: genes experiencing strong clade-specific diversifying selection on a small foreground subset (e.g., the 5% of tree time spent in pelagic planktivory) or undergoing pure relaxation/intensification of purifying selection ($K \ne 1$ with $\omega \le 1$) will fail the global BUSTED-E screen and be excluded before BUSTED-PH or RELAX is ever run.
* **Misapplication of BUSTED-PH Decision Logic to Count "Ancestral" vs. "Derived" PSGs (Pillars 3 & 5):** BUSTED-PH tests whether episodic selection is *specifically associated* with a binary trait by fitting an unconstrained model and comparing it to constrained nulls where (i) foreground $\omega_3 \le 1$, (ii) background $\omega_3 \le 1$, and (iii) foreground and background share the same $\omega$ distribution. In Table 1, out of the 119 pre-selected BUSTED-E genes, the authors report 45 PSGs for "BUSTED-PH ancestral habitat" and 67 PSGs for "BUSTED-PH derived habitat" (summing to 112 of 119, with extensive overlap), and similarly 64 for AU-NG vs. 59 for remaining clades (summing to 123 out of 119). Rather than requiring the BUSTED-PH comparison between foreground and background distributions to be significant (which establishes phenotype association), the authors appear to count any gene among the 119 where the foreground-only or background-only BUSTED sub-test had $p < 0.05$, and then compare raw counts (61 freshwater vs. 43 marine) without a formal statistical test accounting for branch-length/topology structure.
* **Terminology Inversion in "Convergent" vs. "Parallel" Amino Acid Substitutions (Pillar 3):** In lines 59, 65, and Figure 4c caption, the authors invert the standard molecular evolution definitions of parallel and convergent substitutions—and contradict themselves within three sentences: line 59 first defines *"parallelism (identical amino acid changes) or convergence (different changes)"*, then three sentences later states *"Six PSSs displayed convergence, indicating that independent lineages arrived at the same amino acid substitutions... Additionally, we observed 12 PSSs exhibiting parallel changes, suggesting independent but similar amino acid substitutions from the same ancestral state"*, and then in Figure 4c caption reverts to *"parallel (same amino acid mutation), and convergent (different amino acid mutation) PSSs identified with MEME."* Note also that MEME detects sites with $\beta^+ > \alpha$ on a non-zero proportion of branches; different amino acid replacements at the same codon across branches represent *divergent/diversifying* selection at a shared site, not molecular convergence.
* **RagTag Reference-Guided Assembly Bias for Structural/Repeat Analyses (Pillar 2):** The 66 short-read genomes were assembled with SPAdes (contig N50s are not reported; only post-RagTag scaffold N50s of $8.87\text{--}67.48\text{ Mbp}$ are reported, with BUSCO completeness down to $73.2\%$) and scaffolded onto the single *Neoarius graeffei* reference using RagTag. While the authors appropriately report the null result for RepeatMasker TE comparisons (FDR $p > 0.05$), short-read SPAdes assemblies with $73\text{--}82\%$ BUSCO completeness systematically collapse young repetitive elements.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Macroevolutionary Phylogeny and Recurrent Habitat Transitions in Ariidae [Well Supported]:** The 119-species time-calibrated phylogenomic framework (1,039 exons + 3,551 BUSCO genes) and 10-tree SIMMAP reconstructions robustly resolve the 48.3 Ma crown age, the 13.0 Ma AU-NG adaptive radiation, and independent marine-to-freshwater, euryhaline, and planktivorous transitions.
* **No Elevated Genome-Wide Selection Rate or Repeat Expansion in the AU-NG Radiation [Well Supported]:** The authors transparently report that their initial hypothesis—that the AU-NG adaptive radiation would show higher genome-wide PSG counts and TE expansions than non-radiating freshwater clades—was refuted by the data (Table 1, Figure 4d).
* **Candidate Gene Stories (*PRL* in AU-NG Osmoregulation; *FGF4* Position 218 in *Brustiarius nox* Gill Rakers) [Fragile / Hypothesis-Generating]:** The *FGF4* claim rests on a single amino acid substitution at position 218 in a single species (*B. nox*), and the *PRL* signal rests on 3 MEME sites on the AU-NG stem. Both are appropriately framed by the authors as candidate hypotheses rather than causal proofs, though the narrative emphasis in the Abstract and Discussion is strong given the descriptive comparative nature of the evidence.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data & Code Availability:** Raw reads and the *N. graeffei* reference assembly are deposited under NCBI BioProject PRJNA839496, and comprehensive supplementary datasets (Supplementary Data S1–S17) and Source Data files are provided.
* **Minor Numerical Typo:** Line 31 reports the *N. graeffei* scaffold N50 as *"83,992 Mb"* (which would be $84\text{ Gbp}$, $36\times$ the entire $2.34\text{ Gbp}$ genome) instead of $83.992\text{ Mb}$ ($83,992\text{ kb}$).
