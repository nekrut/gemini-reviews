### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates epistatic interactions among potential N-linked glycosylation sites (PNGSs, defined by the $\text{NX}_1\text{(S/T)X}_2$ sequon) across 711 full-length HIV-1/SIVcpz envelope (*env*, gp120 + gp41) sequences from the LANL database, containing 224 variable PNGS columns. First, fitting a nested hierarchy of binary covarion (hidden rate-switching) models in HyPhy along a neighbor-joining tree (inferred after stripping PNGS codons), the authors show strong heterotachy in PNGS gain/loss rates ($\chi^2_2 = 639.8, p \ll 0.001$). Second, they introduce a 4-state continuous-time reversible paired-character phylogenetic model (the "disequon" model, parameterized by site gain/loss asymmetries $b_1, b_2$, relative rate $d$, and epistatic interaction multiplier $\varepsilon$), testing 6,455 PNGS pairs where all four binary states $\{00, 01, 10, 11\}$ are observed. Under strict Bonferroni correction ($\alpha / 6455$), 22 pairs show significant interaction ($\varepsilon \ne 1$): 9 overlapping sequon pairs (8 of 9 mutually exclusive with $\varepsilon < 1$, plus 1 single-sequence `NNTSNNTSY` insertion outlier) and 13 non-overlapping pairs (8 positive with $\varepsilon > 1$, 5 negative with $\varepsilon < 1$). Third, they apply a K2 greedy Bayesian network search (with 100 bootstrap subsamples of 200 sequences and MCMC subtype-stratified networks) to 17 intermediate-frequency ($20\%\text{--}80\%$) PNGSs, recovering 10 consensus arcs. Mapping interacting pairs onto the CD4-bound gp120 crystal structure reveals a positive regression between $\varepsilon$ and intramolecular distance ($F = 15.8, p = 0.001$): mutually exclusive ($\varepsilon < 1$) interactions occur between spatially proximal or overlapping glycans ($<12\text{--}20\text{ \AA}$), whereas cooperative ($\varepsilon > 1$) interactions occur between distant glycans.

This is a creative, multi-method evolutionary study that pairs a phylogenetic 4-state Markov model with graphical models and structural mapping to uncover biophysical packing constraints in the HIV-1 glycan shield. The main methodological limitations are: (1) the Bayesian network K2 search treats aligned sequences as independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations, ignoring phylogenetic structure except via a post-hoc within-subtype check; (2) several interacting PNGSs lie in truncated variable loops (especially V4: N406, N411, N413) lacking crystal coordinates, where 3D distances were approximated by a linear $4.0\text{ \AA} \times \Delta\text{residue}$ formula; and (3) Tables 1 and 2 are missing their body rows in the manuscript text.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Phylogenetic Non-Independence in the Bayesian Network K2 Search (Pillars 3 & 5):** Unlike the disequon model—which evaluates paired binary transitions $\{00, 01, 10, 11\}$ along the branches of the *env* phylogeny—the Bayesian network analysis (lines 46–55, 103–111) treats the 711 sequences (or random subsamples of 200 sequences) as independent realizations from a static directed acyclic graph. In a multi-subtype alignment spanning HIV-1 groups M, N, O, and SIVcpz, clade-specific founder differences in PNGS presence automatically generate strong marginal contingency-table odds ratios across tips even in the absence of functional epistasis. The authors partially mitigate this by running subtype-specific MCMC networks within subtypes A, B, C, D, and CRF01 (line 54), showing that 5 of the 10 consensus arcs replicate across multiple subtypes (including all arcs shared with the phylogenetic disequon model), while the other 5 arcs are potentially confounded by subtype structure or intra-subtype phylogeny.
* **Linear Distance Approximation ($4.0\text{ \AA}$ per Residue) for Unmodeled Variable Loops (Pillar 3):** In the structural regression of $\varepsilon$ against intramolecular distance ($F = 15.8, p = 0.001$; lines 35, 42), the CD4-bound gp120 crystal structure has truncated V1/V2 and V4 loops. Because 8 of the 13 significant non-overlapping disequon pairs involve N411 in the V4 loop (line 44), many of the plotted distances in Figure 1 rely on the heuristic approximation (line 42): *"When an asparagine residue corresponding to a PNGS was not present in the structural model, the distance between pairs was approximated by 4.0 Å times the number of residues separating the PNGSs, to a maximum of five residues."* In flexible surface loops, $C_\alpha\text{--}C_\alpha$ distances across $1\text{--}5$ residues vary widely due to $\beta$-turns, and pairs separated by $>5$ residues with an unmapped partner had to be dropped from the 3D regression entirely, reducing the effective degrees of freedom of the $F = 15.8$ regression (whose exact sample size $n$ of mapped non-overlapping pairs is not stated in line 35, though $df = 1$ is only the numerator degree of freedom).
* **Exclusion of Pairs Missing Any of the Four Binary States $\{00, 01, 10, 11\}$ (Pillar 2):** In line 101, the authors filter the $\binom{224}{2} = 24,976$ (or $49,952$ ordered) pairs down to $6,455$ pairs where all four states $\{00, 01, 10, 11\}$ occur at least once. While this prevents boundary divergence of $\hat{\varepsilon} \to 0$ or $\infty$, it systematically excludes the strongest possible mutually exclusive interactions—namely pairs of polymorphic PNGSs that *never* co-occur in the same sequence ($n_{11} = 0$, such as strictly incompatible overlapping sequons like `N134–N136` and `N332–N334` which were caught by the Bayesian network in line 52 but filtered out of the disequon scan).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Steric Exclusion at Short Range vs. Cooperative Shielding at Long Range [Well Supported]:** The biophysical interpretation—that glycans within $\sim 15\text{--}20\text{ \AA}$ (matching the $15\text{--}40\text{ \AA}$ physical diameter of high-mannose/complex carbohydrate trees) experience steric clash or functional redundancy ($\varepsilon < 1$), whereas distant glycans cooperate to maintain global surface coverage ($\varepsilon > 1$)—is physically sound and corroborated by prior SIV/HIV mutagenesis studies (Wei et al., Ohgimoto et al.) and the SHIV-86P macaque passage comparison in the Discussion (line 68).
* **Alternative Glycan Shifting Around the 2G12 Epitope (N295 vs. N337/N442) [Well Supported]:** The identification of negative interactions between N295 and adjacent V3-base sequons (N337, N442) provides a concrete structural mechanism for how HIV-1 shifts glycan positions to escape glycan-dependent antibodies while preserving local epitope masking.
