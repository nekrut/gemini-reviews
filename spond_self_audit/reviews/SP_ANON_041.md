### 1. Executive Summary
This study evaluates homologous and heterologous live-virus neutralization between first-wave (non-VOC D614G) SARS-CoV-2 and the 501Y.V2 (B.1.351) variant of concern using convalescent plasma from hospitalized patients in Durban, South Africa across the first ($n=14$, including 13 non-VOC and 1 S:E484K-only infection) and second ($n=6$) epidemic waves. Using a live-virus focus-forming microneutralization assay with automated image segmentation, the authors demonstrate that first-wave plasma suffers an 8.4-fold homologous-to-heterologous reduction (and a 15.1-fold drop relative to second-wave plasma) against 501Y.V2, whereas second-wave 501Y.V2-elicited plasma retains substantial cross-neutralization against first-wave virus (4.1-fold relative reduction; $\text{PRNT}_{50} = 149.7$ vs. $344.0$ for first-wave plasma).

The primary biological conclusion—that antigenic escape of 501Y.V2 from first-wave antibodies is pronounced while immunity elicited by 501Y.V2 cross-neutralizes earlier variants more effectively—is supported by well-controlled BSL-3 live-virus experiments, including positive-control monoclonal antibody (A02051) titration and incubation-time controls for focus size. However, the quantitative comparison of absolute titers between waves ($\text{PRNT}_{50} = 619.7$ vs. $344.0$) and the suggestion of higher intrinsic immunogenicity of 501Y.V2 are confounded by a major clinical severity imbalance between cohorts (83% vs. 23% requiring supplemental oxygen) and the post-hoc exclusion of two second-wave donors with undetectable neutralization. In addition, confidence intervals for group $\text{PRNT}_{50}$ estimates are derived from curve-fitting residuals of group means rather than between-participant variance.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Post-Hoc Exclusion of Second-Wave Non-Responders:** In the Methods (line 186), the authors report that *"Two plasma donors initially measured from the second infection wave in South Africa did not have detectable neutralization of either 501Y.V2 or the first-wave variant and were not included in the study."* Removing 2 of 8 (25%) second-wave donors due to undetectable neutralization upward-biases the second-wave group mean $\text{PRNT}_{50}$ ($619.7$ homologous; $149.7$ heterologous) and artificially narrows the apparent gap between second-wave heterologous and first-wave homologous neutralization (reported as 2.3-fold). Both excluded donors should be retained in an intention-to-analyze sensitivity comparison (assigned the limit-of-detection value) alongside the responder-only analysis.
* **Uncontrolled Confounding by Clinical Disease Severity:** Inspection of Extended Data Table 1 reveals that 5 of 6 (83.3%) included second-wave plasma donors required supplemental oxygen, compared with only 3 of 13 (23.1%) non-VOC first-wave donors (which also included 2 asymptomatic cases). Because neutralizing antibody titers in convalescent COVID-19 patients correlate strongly with clinical severity and oxygen requirement, the nearly two-fold higher homologous $\text{PRNT}_{50}$ observed in the second wave ($619.7$ vs. $344.0$) is heavily confounded by disease severity rather than reflecting higher intrinsic viral immunogenicity.
* **Incomplete Viral Sequencing in the Second-Wave Cohort:** While all 14 first-wave donors had sequenced infecting viruses, only 3 of the 6 second-wave donors had viral sequences permitting variant calling, and only 2 had good coverage of the spike gene (line 107). Although epidemiological dominance of 501Y.V2 in January 2021 (97%) makes 501Y.V2 infection highly probable, half of the second-wave group lacks sequence confirmation.
* **Group-Mean Curve Fitting and Underestimated Uncertainty (Fig. 3c):** To compute combined $\text{PRNT}_{50}$ values and 95% confidence intervals in Fig. 3c, the authors fitted a 1-parameter sigmoidal curve ($\text{Tx} = 1 / [1 + D/\text{ID}_{50}]$, fixing the Hill slope to 1) directly to the participant means across the 6 dilution points. Consequently, the reported 95% CIs (e.g., $517.8\text{--}771.5$ for $n=6$ second-wave donors) capture only the nonlinear least-squares regression residual of the 6 averaged points rather than the substantial inter-individual biological variability shown in Fig. 2e (where individual fold-changes span $3.2\text{--}41.9$). Group comparisons should be based on geometric mean titers (GMT) and 95% CIs computed across individual participant $\text{PRNT}_{50}$ values, paired with non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank (within-donor) and Mann-Whitney U (between-wave) tests.
* **Pseudo-Replication in Normality Assessment (Extended Data Fig. 5):** Normality was assessed via `normplot` by pooling $n=42$ (first wave) and $n=21$ (second wave) experimental runs per dilution, which treats 3–4 technical replicate plates from the same donor as independent observations. Furthermore, percentage neutralization was truncated at 0% whenever $\text{Tx} > 1$ (line 194).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Live-Virus Isolation & Assay Controls:** The experimental virology is carefully executed. First-passage isolation of 501Y.V2 on engineered ACE2-overexpressing H1299-E3 cells prevented rapid cell-culture selection of furin cleavage site mutations during initial outgrowth, and the authors sequenced their expanded viral stocks, identifying and excluding a Vero-adapted isolate (`501Y.V2.HVdF002`) with furin cleavage site mutations from the main figures.
* **Focus-Size and Saturation Controls:** Because 501Y.V2 produced larger foci at 28 h, the authors repeated experiments at 18 h to match focus sizes with the first-wave virus and included an internal positive-control monoclonal antibody (A02051) with comparable $\text{PRNT}_{50}$ against both variants ($6.5$ vs. $3.5\text{ ng ml}^{-1}$) on every plate to rule out assay saturation.
* **Claim Proportionality:** The directional asymmetry in cross-neutralization (4.1-fold drop for second-wave plasma vs. 8.4-fold drop for first-wave plasma relative to homologous virus) is well supported because within-donor homologous-to-heterologous ratios self-control for donor baseline titer. However, the claim in the Discussion (line 137) regarding the *"potentially higher immunogenicity of the 501Y.V2 variant indicated by the high $\text{PRNT}_{50}$ of 501Y.V2-elicited plasma"* is overstated given the 83% vs. 23% supplemental oxygen imbalance and the exclusion of two non-neutralizing second-wave donors. The single donor infected with an S:E484K-only virus is appropriately caveated as $n=1$.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data Availability:** Viral genome assemblies are deposited in GISAID, and full patient-level clinical metadata (age band, sex, HIV status and viral load, supplemental oxygen use, symptom onset to sampling interval) are transparently reported in Extended Data Table 1. Raw plate images for all participants are shown in Extended Data Figs. 2–4.
* **Code & Numerical Completeness:** The automated focus-counting image segmentation pipeline and curve-fitting routines in MATLAB v.2019b are described procedurally in the Methods, but the custom MATLAB scripts and the participant-level numerical table of individual $\text{PRNT}_{50}$ estimates across replicates are not deposited in a public repository.
