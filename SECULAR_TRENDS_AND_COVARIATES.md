# Experiment 5: Secular Quality Trends (1991–2025) & Structural Covariate Decomposition ($N = 70$ Longitudinal + $N = 231$ Pooled Wild Manuscripts)
## 1. Executive Summary & Core Findings
To test Stephanie's hypotheses regarding **(1) the secular decline of scientific paper quality over time (`1991–2025`)** and **(2) the structural covariates driving quality differences across biomedical research**, we conducted a two-part blinded audit:
1. **35-Year Longitudinal Cohort ($N = 70$ Full-Text Manuscripts, `1991–2025`):** Stratified across **seven 5-year time windows** (`1991–1995`, `1996–2000`, `2001–2005`, `2006–2010`, `2011–2015`, `2016–2020`, `2021–2025`; $n = 10$ per window) and **four continuous core biomedical tracks** (*Molecular Evolution & Phylogenetics*, *Viral Evolution & Virology*, *Population & Statistical Genetics*, *Gene Expression & Functional Genomics*). All 70 manuscripts were **temporally and metadata-blinded** (`TIME_ANON_01`–`TIME_ANON_70`), with all 4-digit calendar years (`1980–2026`) replaced by `[YEAR]` and all titles, authors, journals, and identifiers removed prior to independent review by `anon-reviewer` subagents.
2. **Pooled Structural Covariate Analysis ($N = 231$ Wild Manuscripts):** Combining our `101` Core Wild papers (`Cohort C`), `60` Out-of-Domain Wild papers (`OOD`), and `70` Longitudinal Wild papers (`SEC_01`–`SEC_70`) to evaluate multivariable effects of **Annual Field Publication Volume**, **Author Count (`# of authors`)**, **Megajournal vs. Legacy Venue Structure**, **Physical/Functional Outcome vs. Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis**, **Technology Maturity**, and **Epistemic Stage (`Basic Discovery` vs. `Translational` vs. `Clinical Observational`)**.
### Headline Quantitative Results
- **Significant Secular Decline (`1991–2025`, $N = 70$):**
  - **By Decade:** Mean Composite Score drops from **`7.14 ± 2.33 / 10.0`** (`Median = 8.20`, `< 5.0` failure rate = **`25.0%`**) in the **1990s (`1991–1999`)** to **`4.81 ± 1.96 / 10.0`** (`Median = 4.15`, `< 5.0` failure rate = **`65.0%`**, `< 6.0` = **`75.0%`**) in the **2020s (`2020–2025`)** — a net drop of **`-2.33` points**.
  - **Not an Artifact of Modern Reproducibility Norms:** When evaluated strictly on the **8.0-Point Scientific Core (`Rigor /3.5 + Claims /2.5 + Biology /2.0`, excluding `Reproducibility /2.0`)**, the secular decline is even stronger: Pearson **$r = -0.327$ ($p = 0.0058$)**, Spearman **$ho = -0.332$ ($p = 0.0050$)**, declining from **`5.74 / 8.0`** (`7.18 / 10` scaled) in the 1990s to **`3.69 / 8.0`** (`4.61 / 10` scaled) in the 2020s (**`-0.74` scaled points per decade**).
- **Publication Volume Explosion (`# of papers`) vs. Team Size (`# of authors`) vs. "The Good Old Days":**
  - Annual biomedical publication volume in our core domains expanded **11.2-fold** (`57,850` papers/year in 1991 $\rightarrow$ `648,325` papers/year in 2025), while Open-Access volume expanded **295-fold** (`3,124` in 1991 $\rightarrow$ `921,565` in 2025). Composite quality strongly anti-correlates with $\log_{10}(\text{Annual Field Volume})$ ($r = -0.318, p = 0.0072$; **`-2.20` points per 10-fold volume expansion**).
  - **Author count is NOT the culprit:** Although mean author count more than doubled from `3.1` (`1991–1995`) to `7.4` (`2021–2025`), within the pooled $N = 231$ corpus **larger author teams score significantly HIGHER** ($\beta = +0.374 \pm 0.122$ points per doubling of authors, $t = +3.08, p = 0.0023$). Small 1–3 author teams churning out dry-lab database re-analyses drag down modern quality, whereas multi-lab experimental collaborations raise it.
  - **Megajournal Dilution vs. Within-Journal Drift:** Open-Access Megajournals (`PLOS ONE`, `Scientific Reports`, `Frontiers`, `IJMS`/`MDPI`, `Medicine`, etc.; $n = 80$) average **`4.65 ± 1.63`** (`< 6.0` = **`83.8%`**) vs. **`5.90 ± 2.03`** (`< 6.0` = `51.0%`) for Legacy/Society/Selective journals ($n = 151$, $\Delta = -1.25$ pts, $p < 10^{-5}$). However, even **restricting strictly to non-megajournals**, quality fell from **`7.14`** (`1990s`) to **`5.01`** (`2020s`), and in multivariable OLS regression (`Table 6`), **~31% of the secular decline is explained by compositional shift** (megajournals, template pipelines, secondary database mining) while **~69% ($\beta = -0.464$ pts/decade, $p = 0.0030$) is true within-category erosion**.
- **Physical/Functional Outcome vs. Pure Dry-Lab Cohort/Database Re-Analysis ($N = 231$):**
  - **Wet-Lab Primary Experimental Studies ($n = 61$):** **`6.10 ± 2.02`** (`Median = 6.00`, `< 5.0` = `32.8%`, `< 6.0` = `49.2%`).
  - **Hybrid Computational + Wet-Lab Functional Validation ($n = 51$):** **`5.51 ± 2.00`** (`Median = 5.40`, `< 5.0` = `45.1%`, `< 6.0` = `56.9%`).
  - **Dry-Lab Theory / Methods / Simulation ($n = 12$):** **`5.40 ± 2.68`** (`Median = 4.95`, `< 5.0` = `50.0%`; in the longitudinal cohort, classical theory/methods papers like Huelsenbeck `PMC1449705` and Randall et al. `PMC5013904` score `8.4–9.2`).
  - **Pure Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort / Database Re-Analysis (No Physical Outcome, $n = 107$):** **`5.10 ± 1.80`** (`Median = 4.80`, **`< 5.0` = `52.3%`, `< 6.0` = `72.9%`**) — a full **`-1.00` point deficit** relative to primary wet-lab experimental work ($p = 0.0012$).
- **Technology Maturity & Epistemic Stage ($N = 231$):**
  - **Mature / Classical Technologies ($n = 97$):** **`5.90 ± 2.13`** (`< 5.0` = `38.1%`) vs. **Mid-Stage High-Throughput ($n = 76$):** **`5.61 ± 1.88`** (`< 5.0` = `40.8%`) vs. **Commodity / Hype-Cycle Template Pipelines ($n = 58$):** **`4.57 ± 1.56`** (`< 5.0` = **`63.8%`**, `< 6.0` = **`82.8%`**; and **`3.75 ± 0.95`**, `< 6.0` = **`100.0%`** in the blinded longitudinal cohort).
  - **Basic / Mechanistic Discovery ($n = 116$):** **`5.87 ± 2.03`** (`< 6.0` = `56.0%`) vs. **Clinical / Epidemiological Observational ($n = 53$):** **`5.30 ± 1.84`** (`< 6.0` = `60.4%`) vs. **Translational / Preclinical / Biomarker ($n = 62$):** **`4.86 ± 1.86`** (`< 6.0` = **`75.8%`**).

> **Note on the Two-Wave Decline (`2001–2005` and `2016–2025`):** Table 1 reveals that quality did not decline as a single smooth line; instead, it experienced **two distinct technological shocks**:
> 1. **First Shock (`2001–2005`, Mean = `5.06`, `< 6.0` = `80%`):** Coincides with the first wave of commercial high-throughput kits, uncalibrated early `codeml`/microarray pipelines, and small-$N$ candidate-gene SNP case-control studies (e.g., `SEC_24` `PMC2361986` [$N=3$ subgroup genotypes out of HWE], `SEC_29` `PMC1490005` [automated EST/rRNA contamination], `SEC_22` `PMC126259` [$d_S=0 \implies \omega=99$ division-by-zero artifacts]), before community standards (MIQE, GWAS $p < 5\times 10^{-8}$, Benj.-Hochberg FDR, BEAST/HyPhy model checks) temporarily restored rigor during **`2006–2015` (`6.32–6.53`)**.
> 2. **Second Shock (`2016–2025`, Mean = `4.99` $\rightarrow$ `4.63`, `< 6.0` = `70%–80%`):** Coincides with the massive expansion of APC-driven megajournals and turn-key bioinformatic/biomarker template pipelines (GEO/TCGA DEG + PPI + LASSO wrappers, unvalidated in silico docking, and automated gene-family `codeml`/`DIVERGE` scripts).
