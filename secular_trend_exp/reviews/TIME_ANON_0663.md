### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This immunology and protein biochemistry study identifies the physiological cell-surface ligand for murine `CD2`. While human `CD2` binds `LFA-3` (`CD58`), the murine counterpart had remained elusive. The authors engineer a soluble receptor globulin chimera fusing the extracellular domain of mouse `CD2` to the human IgG1 Fc region (`mCD2Rg`) and immunize hamsters to raise a monoclonal antibody (`HM48-1`) selected for blocking `mCD2Rg` binding to murine T cell lines at the ligand site. Biochemical characterization reveals that the `HM48-1` antigen is a 45-kDa glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored glycoprotein whose expression is restricted primarily to hematopoietic cells. N-terminal protein microsequencing (18 amino acid residues) of affinity-purified `HM48-1` antigen demonstrates near-identity to mouse `CD48` (`BCM1` / `Blast-1` / `sgp-60` / rat `OX-45`). This identity is confirmed by reciprocal cross-reactivity and competition using soluble recombinant `CD48` (`sCD48`) and a rat anti-`sCD48` mAb, both of which block `mCD2Rg` binding. Functionally, `HM48-1` inhibits phytohemagglutinin (PHA)-induced T cell proliferation and, upon crosslinking, co-stimulates anti-CD3-driven splenic T cell activation.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
**Strengths:**
- **Unambiguous Biochemical & Immunological Triangulation:** Rather than relying solely on antibody cross-blocking (which can be confounded by steric hindrance of adjacent surface molecules), the authors establish ligand identity via five orthogonal lines of evidence: (1) functional receptor-globulin (`mCD2Rg`) binding blockade by `HM48-1`, (2) PI-PLC/GPI-anchor and 45-kDa molecular mass characterization, (3) direct Edman N-terminal microsequencing (18 aa) of the affinity-purified native protein matching mouse `CD48`, (4) direct binding of `HM48-1` to recombinant soluble `CD48` (`sCD48`) and cross-reactivity with rat anti-`sCD48` mAb, and (5) direct competitive inhibition of `mCD2Rg` cell-surface binding by purified `sCD48` protein.
- **Functional Reciprocity in Primary T Cells:** The authors demonstrate that targeting the ligand (`CD48`) with `HM48-1` recapitulates the functional effects of targeting the receptor (`CD2`) in splenic T cells—inhibiting mitogen (PHA) responses when soluble and augmenting sub-optimal anti-CD3 TCR activation when crosslinked.

**Weaknesses & Methodological Audit:**
- **Archival Text Truncation in Markdown:** Because the markdown file contains only the abstract and bibliography (referencing a 1.1 MB PDF for the main text), exact binding affinity constants ($K_d$), flow cytometry mean fluorescence intensities, and proliferation assay replicate variances (`[3H]thymidine` cpm $\pm$ SD) cannot be audited directly from the text file.
- **Single-Residue Microsequencing Discrepancy:** The abstract notes that the 18-residue N-terminal sequence is "almost identical with mouse CD48"; while recombinant `sCD48` binding resolves any doubt about gene identity, the exact position and cause of the microsequencing mismatch (e.g., allelic polymorphism between mouse strains vs. Edman degradation cycle ambiguity at a Cys/glycosylated residue) are not detailed in the abstract.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** `CD48` is a 45-kDa GPI-anchored hematopoietic glycoprotein recognized by mAb `HM48-1` that serves as the cell-surface ligand for murine `CD2`. `[Well Supported]` by N-terminal sequencing, `sCD48` competition, and reciprocal mAb blocking.
- **Claim 2:** Unlike human `LFA-3` (`CD58`), which is broadly expressed on both hematopoietic and non-hematopoietic/endothelial tissues, the murine `CD2` ligand (`CD48`) is predominantly restricted to hematopoietic cells. `[Well Supported]` and aligns with comparative genomic mapping cited in the references (`Wong et al.`, `Kingsmore et al.`) showing differential evolution of the `CD2`/`CD48`/`CD58` immunoglobulin superfamily cluster on mouse chromosomes 1 and 3.
- **Claim 3:** `CD48` engagement regulates murine T cell activation in a manner analogous to `CD2` engagement. `[Well Supported]` by PHA inhibition and crosslinked anti-CD3 co-stimulation assays.
