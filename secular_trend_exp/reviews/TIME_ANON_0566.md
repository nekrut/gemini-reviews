### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This purely computational study evaluates the predicted percentage of intrinsic disorder (`PID`, calculated via the `PONDR VLXT` neural network predictor) of the Nipah virus (`NiV`) nucleocapsid (`N`, 532 aa) and matrix (`M`) proteins across 9 UniProt sequences grouped into three historical categories (`Malaysia-Singapore 1998–99`, `Bangladesh-India 2001–05`, and `Bangladesh-India 2007–10/12`) and correlates these group `PID` values (`41.73%`, `42.67%`, and `43.32%`) against World Health Organization (WHO) human outbreak case-fatality rates (`CFR`: `38%`, `72%`, and `85%`). Based on a linear regression across these grouped data ($r^2 = 0.92\text{–}0.93, p < 0.01$) and visual inspection of `PONDR VLXT` profiles, `ANCHOR` plots, a crystal structure of the truncated `N–P` complex (`PDB 4CO6`), and a 4-virus bar chart comparing `HeV`, `NiV`, `MeV`, and `HIV-1` (Figure 7), the authors claim that small increases in N-protein intrinsic disorder drive Nipah virus human case-fatality rates via "Trojan horse" rapid replication, dictate fecal-oral vs. respiratory transmission modes, predict vaccine feasibility, and solve the "age-old biomedical mystery" of viral attenuation during serial passage (such as Sabin poliovirus). Forensic audit reveals fatal methodological flaws: overt cherry-picking (`2 of 11` NiV sequences excluded as "anomalous"), circular misassignment of non-human *Pteropus* bat isolates from Thailand and Malaysia (which caused zero human cases) into the `Bangladesh-India CFR = 85%` group *because* of their `PID` values, undisclosed "noise adjustment" of WHO CFR statistics, internal numerical contradictions across the text and tables, ecological and phylogenetic pseudoreplication, and extreme over-interpretation.

### 2. STRENGTHS
- **Structural Context of the Paramyxovirus $N_{\text{TAIL}}\text{–}P_{\text{XD}}$ Interaction:** The Introduction and Results accurately review the structural organization of the henipavirus nucleoprotein (`N_NTD`, `N_CTD`, and the intrinsically disordered C-terminal `N_TAIL` spanning residues `384–532` that binds the phosphoprotein `P_XD` domain) and note that most amino acid substitutions between the Malaysian (`NiV-M`) and Bangladeshi (`NiV-B`) lineages occur within the C-terminal tail.

### 3. WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT (Methodological & Statistical Audit)
- **Overt Exclusion of "Anomalous" Sequences and Circular Misassignment of Thai and Malaysian Bat Isolates into the Bangladeshi Human CFR Group (Table 1):**
  - Look at the footnotes to **Table 1** (Line 48):
    1. **Exclusion of non-conforming sequences (Footnote `b`):** *"Samples from two anamalous [sic] strains are not included: `H6V868`, `H6V854`. In general, a few anamalous strains should be expected in cases where a large number of outbreaks/strains is involved. The data used involve 9 isolates and 6 strains."* Excluding 2 out of 11 available UniProt sequences (`18%` of the sample) simply because their `PONDR VLXT` scores did not fit the hypothesized correlation is textbook data dredging / cherry-picking.
    2. **Circular assignment of a Thai bat isolate (`A0A141HUS6`) and a Malaysian bat isolate (`E4Z9F9`) to the `Bangladesh-India 85% human CFR` group (Footnotes `c` and `d`):** Of the 5 UniProt accessions placed in the **`Bangladesh-India [YEAR]-10` (`CFR = 85%`)** row of Table 1 (`D2DEB7, A0A141HUS6, H6V855, H6V857, E4Z9F9`), two are **not** from Bangladesh or India and were **not** isolated from human outbreaks: `A0A141HUS6` was isolated from bats in **Thailand** (a country with **zero** recorded human Nipah cases or fatalities), and `E4Z9F9` was isolated from bats in **Malaysia** (where no human Nipah cases have occurred since 1999)!
    3. Why did the authors place a Thai bat sequence and a Malaysian bat sequence into the `Bangladesh-India 85% human fatality` group? Footnote `c` explicitly states: *"because the PID and genetic makeup is closer to that of the [YEAR]-10 strains and because [YEAR] is close to [YEAR], we suspect that this strain falls into the [YEAR]-10 strains."* And in Figure 6 (phylogenetic tree, Line 81 & 92), the authors themselves admit that `A0A141HUS6` (Thailand) and `E4Z9F9` (Malaysia) phylogenetically cluster with the **Southeast Asian / Malaysian clade**, NOT the Bangladesh clade! Assigning bat surveillance sequences with `0%` human CFR to the `85%` Bangladeshi human CFR category *based on their high PID value*, and then regressing `PID` against `CFR` to claim $r^2 = 0.93$, is completely circular and invalid.
- **Undisclosed "Noise Adjustment" of WHO Epidemiological Data and Internal Numerical Contradictions:**
  - In Line 40 and the Figure 2 legend, the authors state: *"It should be noted that the plot is adjusted for the 'noise'. The calculations and data shown have been adjusted for 'noise' especially involving samples with the number of cases in the outbreaks that are too small to be reliable."* No inclusion criteria, sample-size cutoff, or list of excluded/modified WHO outbreaks is provided anywhere in the paper.
  - Furthermore, the reported CFRs, $r^2$ values, and $F$-statistics contradict one another across the manuscript:
    - **CFRs for the 3 groups:** Abstract & Line 40 state `~40% (39.5 ± 1%)`, `~71% (71 ± 3.5%)`, and `~93% (92.8 ± 6%)`; the Figure 2 legend states `~40%`, `~75%`, and `~85%`; and Table 1 states `38%` (`106/276`), `72%` (`123/170`), and `85%` (`88/103`).
    - **CFR vs. Year statistics:** Line 40 reports One-Way ANOVA `F = 9.93, p < 0.01` and regression `r^2 = 0.83, F = 42.43, p < 0.01`, whereas the Figure 2 legend three lines below reports One-Way ANOVA `F = 49, p < 0.01` and regression `r^2 = 0.81, F = 44.1, p < 0.01`.
    - **PID vs. CFR statistics:** Line 48 reports *"via regression (`r^2 = 0.92, F = 39.4, p < 0.01`) and ANOVA (`r^2 = 0.92, F = 39.4, p < 0.01`)"* (copy-pasting the exact same regression $r^2$ and $F$ as the ANOVA result), whereas the Abstract and Table 1 report `r^2 = 0.93`.
- **Ecological Fallacy, Uncontrolled Confounding, and Trivial `PID` Difference (`41.73%` vs. `43.32%`):**
  - Across the 532-amino-acid NiV N protein, a `PID` shift from `41.73%` (`222.0` residues) to `42.67%` (`227.0` residues) and `43.32%` (`230.5` residues) represents a difference of **5 to 8 predicted disordered residues** (`1.6%` of the protein).
  - Crucially, the Malaysian (`1998–99`) and Bangladeshi/Indian (`2001–12`) outbreaks differed profoundly in: (i) **healthcare infrastructure and intensive care access** (modern ICU mechanical ventilation in Malaysia and Singapore vs. rural district hospitals in Bangladesh), (ii) **route and dose of infection** (occupational contact with pig respiratory/urine secretions vs. drinking raw date palm sap heavily contaminated with bat secretions), and (iii) **hundreds of fixed nucleotide and amino acid substitutions across all six viral genes** (`N, P/V/W/C, M, F, G, L`). Attributing the `38%` vs. `72–85%` human CFR difference to a 1.6% change in `PONDR VLXT` score on the N protein across 3 ecological data points without controlling for phylogenetic non-independence or non-viral epidemiological confounders is a severe ecological fallacy.
- **Misrepresentation of Crystal Structure `PDB 4CO6` in Figure 5:**
  - As the authors themselves admit in Line 73, the crystal structure shown in Figure 5 (`PDB 4CO6`, truncated residues `28–383`) **lacks the entire C-terminal disordered tail (`residues 384–532`)** where almost all of the sequence and `PONDR VLXT` differences between strains actually reside (Figure 3E). Highlighting residue `~315` in pink on the structured `N_CTD` domain and claiming that *"Fig. 5 shows the pink area virtually touching the protein, P"* (when in `4CO6` the `P_XD` peptide is artificially co-crystallized against the truncated core and the true primary binding partner is the deleted `N_TAIL`) does not biochemically validate the `PONDR VLXT` claims.

### 4. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *NiV N protein contains a highly disordered C-terminal domain (`N_TAIL`, residues 384–532) that harbors most amino acid differences between the Malaysian and Bangladeshi lineages.* — `[Well Supported]` by multiple sequence alignment (Figure 3E) and prior structural literature.
- **Claim 2:** *Predicted intrinsic disorder (`PID`) of the NiV N protein strongly and causally correlates with human case-fatality rate ($r^2 = 0.93, p < 0.01$).* — `[Methodologically Flawed]`; invalidated by the exclusion of two "anomalous" strains (`H6V868`, `H6V854`), the circular assignment of Thai and Malaysian bat isolates (`0` human cases) to the `85%` Bangladeshi human CFR group based on their `PID`, undisclosed "noise" filtering of WHO data, and ecological/phylogenetic pseudoreplication.
- **Claim 3:** *N and M shell disorder dictates fecal-oral vs. respiratory transmission modes across paramyxoviruses and HIV-1, predicts vaccine feasibility, and explains viral attenuation by serial passage (Sabin poliovirus).* — `[Overstated]` / `[Methodologically Flawed]`; completely unsupported by statistical testing or experimental evidence.
