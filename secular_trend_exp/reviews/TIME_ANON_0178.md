### 1. Executive Summary
This phylogenomic study assembles a concatenated supermatrix of 2,557 orthologous protein partitions (1,062,841 amino acid characters) across 16 land plant species (5 angiosperms, 7 gymnosperms, and 4 seed-free outgroups) from whole-genome and expressed sequence tag (EST) databases (`OrthologID` + `ASAP`). Using equal-weights maximum parsimony (`PAUP*` 4b10), the authors recover a single most parsimonious tree (length 932,201; >99% bootstrap/jackknife support) and calculate Partitioned Bremer Support (PBS) and Partitioned Hidden Bremer Support (PHBS) for every gene partition at every node. They then test whether partitions providing positive PBS or PHBS at specific nodes are enriched for Gene Ontology (GO) categories (`Sungear` $z$-score $> 7$ and `Biomaps` hypergeometric $P < 0.05$), highlighting post-transcriptional small-RNA regulation (*AGO1* and *RDR6*) in angiosperms/rosids. However, the study suffers from severe methodological confounding: (1) it compares nodes composed of complete sequenced genomes (*Arabidopsis*, *Oryza*, *Populus*, *Vitis*) against nodes composed of sparse EST libraries without controlling for gene occupancy/missing data; (2) it uses the whole *Arabidopsis* genome rather than the 2,557-gene matrix (or node-present genes) as the null background in `Sungear`; and (3) it conflates parsimony character support (PBS/PHBS) with positive/adaptive natural selection without performing any $d_N/d_S$ or selection tests.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Confounding of Partitioned Bremer Support by Whole-Genome vs. EST Missing Data:** Four of the five angiosperms (*Arabidopsis thaliana*, *Oryza sativa*, *Populus trichocarpa*, *Vitis vinifera*) have complete sequenced genomes (Table 1 footnote `a`), whereas *Amborella*, all gymnosperms, and the seed-free outgroups are represented primarily by incomplete EST libraries. In a concatenated parsimony supermatrix, a gene partition can only contribute positive PBS or PHBS to a node if sequences are present (non-missing) for the taxa defining that split. Consequently:
  1. Nodes uniting the fully sequenced angiosperm genomes—specifically Node 6 (*Arabidopsis*, *Populus*, *Vitis*) and Node 7 (*Populus*, *Vitis*)—have near-100% gene presence and full-length protein coverage, whereas gymnosperm nodes are restricted to high-abundance transcripts captured in EST libraries. This directly explains why *"Node 6 (Arabidopsis, Populus, Vitis) and node 7 (Populus, Vitis) have the largest outlier vessels for both PBS and PHBS"* (Results, line 118)—an artifact of genome completeness rather than accelerated functional evolution in rosids.
  2. Low-expression regulatory genes (such as small-RNA pathway factors *AGO1* and *RDR6*, transcription factors, and circadian clock genes) are systematically present in full genome annotations of *Arabidopsis*, *Oryza*, *Populus*, and *Vitis* but frequently absent or fragmentary in gymnosperm EST libraries, artificially inflating their apparent enrichment at angiosperm/rosid nodes.
- **Improper Null Distribution in `Sungear` GO Enrichment:** In the `Sungear` analysis (Methods, lines 75–77), the authors test GO overrepresentation of positive-PBS/PHBS genes at each node against the **entire *Arabidopsis* genome** as the null background rather than against the set of annotated genes actually present at that node in the 2,557-gene matrix. Because EST-derived matrices are heavily skewed toward abundant metabolic and photosynthetic transcripts, testing subsets against the whole *Arabidopsis* genome conflates matrix-construction bias with clade-specific evolutionary signal. Furthermore, no multiple-testing correction is applied to the `Biomaps` hypergeometric tests ($P < 0.05$ across dozens of GO categories and 4 angiosperm nodes).
- **Conflation of Parsimony Synapomorphies (PBS) with Natural Selection:** The Abstract states: *"We provide a general method for detecting genes or groups of genes that may be under selection in directions that are in agreement with the phylogenetic pattern."* However, Partitioned Bremer Support merely counts the excess of parsimony-informative amino acid substitutions congruent with a given topology over the shortest alternative topology; it does not normalize for protein length, synonymous mutation rate ($d_S$), or branch length. Longer proteins (such as `AGO1` at ~1,050 aa and `RDR6` at ~1,196 aa) and genes under relaxed purifying selection naturally accumulate more parsimony steps along a branch under neutral drift. No codon-based likelihood models ($d_N/d_S$) are actually run in the paper.
- **Internal Species Count & Table 1 Discrepancy:** In the Methods (line 26), the authors state that the 16 species comprise *"11 seed plants: five angiosperms (Amborella, rice, Arabidopsis, poplar, and grape) and six gymnosperms (Cryptomeria, pine, two cycads, gingko, Gnetum, and Welwitschia) and four seed-free plants: Filicalian fern (Adiantum), a thalloid liverwort (Marchantia), a moss (Physcomitrella), and a Lycophyte (Selaginella)."* First, the listed gymnosperms actually total **7 species** (*Cryptomeria*, *Pinus*, *Cycas*, *Zamia*, *Ginkgo*, *Gnetum*, *Welwitschia*), making **12 seed plants** ($5 + 7 = 12$, plus 4 seed-free plants $= 16$ species). Second, Table 1 ("Species List and Corresponding Genomic Databases") contains only **15 rows**, completely omitting the moss *Physcomitrella patens*!

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Concatenated maximum parsimony analysis of 2,557 nuclear protein partitions recovers monophyletic gymnosperms (with gnetophytes sister to other gymnosperms) and *Amborella* sister to other angiosperms with >99% bootstrap/jackknife support. `[Moderately Supported]` — High parsimony bootstrap in a 1-million-character concatenation is expected, though equal-weights parsimony on deep land-plant amino acid alignments is susceptible to long-branch attraction (notably the placement of rapid-evolving *Gnetum*/*Welwitschia* at the base of gymnosperms and *Vitis* sister to *Populus* rather than sister to *Arabidopsis*+*Populus*).
- **Claim 2:** Partitioned Bremer Support (PBS/PHBS) combined with GO enrichment identifies genes under positive/adaptive selection driving seed plant and angiosperm morphological divergence (such as *AGO1* and *RDR6*). `[Overstated]` — Positive PBS reflects total congruent amino acid changes (heavily confounded by protein length and full-genome vs. EST missing data), not positive selection ($d_N/d_S > 1$).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Supplementary Data Availability:** The authors report that the full 1,062,841-aa NEXUS alignment (Supplementary Table 1), partition support metrics (Supplementary Table 2), and GO mappings (Supplementary Tables 3–6) are deposited as online Supplementary Material, and database sources (`OrthologID`, `ASAP`, `VirtualPlant`, `TAIR` v8) are cited. Table 1 in the main text needs correction to include *Physcomitrella patens* and fix the seed-plant/gymnosperm count in the text.
