### 1. Executive Summary
This study analyzes a multi-year experimental evolution dataset in which HIV-1 strain NL4-3 was serially passaged for 240–300 passages (~400–600 generations) across 16 evolutionary lines in two human T-cell environments (MT-2 and MT-4, including 4 lines that switched host cell types at passage 50), with near-full-length Illumina deep sequencing performed every 10th passage. By combining longitudinal allele-frequency tracking, explicit neutral evolution simulations, randomization tests for environment-specific mutation sharing, co-occurrence/exclusivity tests ("always" and "never" combinations) for epistasis, and direct viral growth-rate assays, the authors show extensive parallel adaptation across environments alongside striking environment-dependent sweep dynamics (rapid selective sweeps with hitchhiking in MT-2 versus gradual, background-dependent accumulation in MT-4). The experimental and statistical design is rigorous, transparent about technical caveats (including an early cross-contamination event between replicates A and B), and supported by open data and code on Zenodo.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Explicit Neutral Baseline Simulations & Randomization Controls:** To establish non-neutral evolution, the authors benchmark the observed 823 unique majority mutations (378 shared across lines, 415 fixed) against 32 matched forward-time neutral simulations across two mutation rates ($10^{-5}$ and $10^{-4}$), demonstrating that neither rate can reproduce the empirical combination of high sharing and high fixation. Similarly, environment-specific sharing and epistatic co-occurrence ("always" and "never" combinations) are evaluated against 1,000-permutation null distributions that preserve line-specific mutation counts and emergence-time constraints.
* **Transparent Handling of Non-Independence and Contamination:** The authors explicitly document in the main text (lines 128, 153, 199, 208) and S1 Text that an early cross-contamination event occurred between replicates A and B grown in 12-well plates, and that derived switch lines share 50 passages of ancestral history. Crucially, they restrict their statistical tests of independent sharing and "always" combinations to strictly independent lines (excluding shared A/B mutations and pre-switch mutations), preventing shared ancestry from inflating parallelism or epistasis estimates.
* **Limitations of Population-Level Short-Read Sequencing for Epistasis Inference:** Because sequencing was performed via pooled RT-PCR amplicons with $2 \times 250\text{ bp}$ reads, physical haplotype linkage across the 9.7 kb genome cannot be directly observed. The authors infer "never-combinations" from the pigeonhole inequality $f_{ct} < 1 - f_{ft}\ \forall t$ and "always-combinations" from background frequency ordering ($f_{\text{bg}} > f_{\text{focus}}$ at $0.05$ and $0.50$). While clever, in a population experiencing clonal interference (where competing beneficial clones rise and fall), two beneficial mutations on competing clones will satisfy $f_{ct} < 1 - f_{ft}$ without any negative biochemical epistasis between them. Similarly, differing sweep speeds between MT-2 and MT-4 can alter the power to detect "always-combinations" independently of the underlying epistatic roughness of the fitness landscape.
* **Transfer Volume Confounding Between Environments:** As noted in the Discussion (line 124) and Methods (lines 150, 159), the passaging inoculum volume transferred into MT-4 was 3-fold larger than into MT-2 ($3\ \mu\text{L}$ vs. $1\ \mu\text{L}$ in A/B lines; $4.5\ \mu\text{L}$ vs. $1.5\ \mu\text{L}$ in derived lines), although equal in lines 1 and 2 ($2\ \mu\text{L}$). A larger bottleneck size $N_b$ in MT-4 increases mutational supply and clonal interference while slowing single-clone selective sweeps, which partially contributes to the contrast between MT-2 and MT-4 dynamics.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Mechanistic Integration of Sweep Dynamics, Hitchhiking, and Fitness:** The manuscript connects the saturation of minority variants ($\ge 5\%$), the rapid erratic accumulation of majority variants ($\ge 50\%$), and the excess of line-unique majority mutations in MT-2 into a coherent population-genetic model: strong selective sweeps purge standing minority diversity while carrying linked neutral or mildly deleterious hitchhikers to high frequency. This interpretation is corroborated by direct p24 ELISA growth-rate assays (S3 Text) showing that MT-2-evolved lines perform less well than MT-4-evolved lines that accumulated fewer hitchhikers.
* **Unbiased Identification of Consensus Reversions and Non-Coding Selection:** By defining reversion sites a priori against the global HIV-1 group M consensus (831 sites where ancestral NL4-3 differs from consensus), the authors cleanly demonstrate that reversions reach significantly higher maximum frequencies (14% vs. 6–7%, permutation $p < 0.01$). They also test and rule out differential APOBEC3G hypermutation (G-to-A rate differences) as a confounder between MT-2 and MT-4.
* **Proportional Claims:** While the metaphor comparing MT-2 vs. MT-4 cell-line switches to host-to-host transmission in the absence of immune pressure is an idealization, the authors explicitly caveat the absence of adaptive immunity, compartmentalization, and high-MOI recombination.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Exemplary Open Data and Code Availability:** Raw underlying data and analysis scripts for all main figures are publicly archived with a persistent Zenodo DOI (`https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3787242`), and the reference sequence accession (`AF324493.2`) is specified.
* **Detailed Experimental & Bioinformatic Protocols:** Cell culture conditions, MOI measurements, transfer volumes, Illumina MiSeq RT-PCR amplicon sequencing, BWA-MEM mapping quality thresholds ($\text{MQ} > 35$, coverage $\ge 1,000\times$), forward simulation parameters, and permutation algorithms are thoroughly documented.
