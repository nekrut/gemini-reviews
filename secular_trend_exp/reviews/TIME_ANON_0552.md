### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This computational study screens 33,400 complete human viral genomes (870 unique species), alongside 1,397 non-human primate, 9,160 avian, and 570 fish viral genomes and 252,766 individual viral coding sequences, for evolutionary footprints of APOBEC3 (A3) cytidine deaminase restriction. Adapting the codon-position-aware $k$-mer approach of Warren et al., the authors quantify the $\log_2(\text{observed}/\text{expected})$ representation ratio of the canonical $5'\text{-TC}$ target motif across the three codon positions on both the coding strand ($\text{NNTCNN}$, $\text{TCN}$, $\text{NTC}$) and template strand ($\text{GAN}$, $\text{NGA}$, $\text{NNGANN}$) relative to 1,000 random nucleotide shuffles of each concatenated coding genome. Because $\text{C}\to\text{T}$ deamination at the third codon position ($\text{NTC}$ on the coding strand or $\text{NNGANN}$ on the template strand) is selectively synonymous and therefore more permissive to evolutionary fixation, selective depletion of $\text{NTC}$ and/or $\text{NNGANN}$ (>2 SD below the viral species median) is used to identify A3-footprinted viruses. The authors report genome-wide footprints in $\sim 22\%$ of human viral species—most intensely across *Papillomaviridae*, *Polyomaviridae*, autonomous *Parvoviridae* (notably erythroparvovirus B19), and endemic human coronaviruses (*229E*, *NL63*, *OC43*, *HKU1*, but not zoonotic *SARS-CoV-1*, *MERS-CoV*, or *SARS-CoV-2*)—as well as spatially localized footprints at the lagging/displaced strands of lytic replication origins in *Adenoviridae* (`E1A`, `E4`) and EBV (`BHLF1`, `BHRF1`, `LF3`, `RPMS1`, `A73`).

### 2. STRENGTHS
- **Mechanistically Informative Cross-Host and Replication-Strand Controls:** Comparing human and non-human primate viruses against avian and fish viruses (Figure 3B) provides an evolutionary negative control consistent with the mammal-specific expansion of the *APOBEC3* locus. Furthermore, mapping gene-level $\text{NTC}$ vs. $\text{NNGANN}$ depletion onto the replication fork polarity of linear adenoviruses (strand-displacement replication at terminal `E1A` and `E4` genes; Figure 8) and EBV `OriLyt` lagging strands (Figure 9) yields a compelling, strand-specific mechanistic model of A3 targeting during initial single-stranded DNA exposure.
- **Reading-Frame Overlap Validation in Erythroparvovirus B19 (Figure 5B):** Demonstrates that the rare surviving $\text{NTC}$ codons inside the deeply depleted *NS1* gene of parvovirus B19 fall precisely within the overlapping reading frame of the *7.5k* protein where the same `TC` dinucleotides occupy the non-synonymous $\text{TCN}$ position, providing strong internal validation of purifying selection against non-synonymous A3 editing.
- **Full Open Data Availability:** Complete genome-level and gene-level $k$-mer ratio tables (`S1–S3 Tables`) for all 33,400 human and 11,424 non-human viral genomes are publicly archived on Dryad (`10.5061/dryad.n8pk0p2sd`).

### 3. WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT (Methodological & Statistical Audit)
- **Mononucleotide Shuffling Null Model Confounds $3\text{rd}$-Position Codon Bias with Dinucleotide $5'\text{-TC}$ Specificity:** In the Materials and Methods ("Calculation of the K-mer representation ratio"), the expected count $n_{\text{exp}}$ is computed by shuffling the coding sequence 1,000 times *"retaining only the nucleotide composition"* across all three codon positions combined, rather than shuffling synonymous codons within amino acid families or conditioning on position-specific mononucleotide frequencies. In viruses with strong genome-wide $3\text{rd}$-codon-position $\text{U/T}$ enrichment and $\text{C}$ depletion—most notably endemic human coronaviruses such as *HCoV-HKU1* and *HCoV-NL63*, which exhibit extreme synonymous $\text{NNU} \gg \text{NNC}$ codon bias across *all* preceding nucleotides (`AUC/UUC/GUC/CUC`, `ACC/UCC/GCC/CCC`, `AAC/UAC/GAC/CAC`)—pooling constrained $1\text{st}/2\text{nd}$ codon positions with $\text{C}$-depleted $3\text{rd}$ codon positions during unconstrained nucleotide shuffling causes *every* $\text{NNC}$ codon (including $\text{NTC}$) to appear depleted relative to $2\text{nd}$-position $\text{TCN}$ (which encodes Serine and is maintained by amino acid constraint). While Figure 3C checks global distributions of $\text{NAC}$, $\text{NGC}$, and $\text{NCC}$ across the entire 33,400-genome database, Figure 6 does not verify that $\text{NTC}$ is depleted significantly more than $\text{ACC/CCC/GCC}$ or $\text{AAC/CAC/GAC}$ specifically within the endemic coronaviruses.
- **Pseudoreplication in Unpaired Student's *t*-Tests Across Redundant GenBank Isolates:** In Figures 2, 4B, 5A, 6, and 11, unpaired two-tailed Student's *t*-tests (`*** p < 0.001`) are computed by treating every sequenced viral isolate in GenBank (e.g., thousands of near-identical HIV-1, HBV, SARS-CoV-2, or BKPyV genomes) as an independent observation. Because viral isolates within a species share $>95\text{–}99.9\%$ sequence identity by common descent, treating $N$ database isolates as $N$ independent degrees of freedom severely inflates test statistics.
- **Speculative Hypothesis on SARS-CoV-2 *ORF10* as a Vif-Like CUL2 Antagonist of APOBEC3 (Discussion, Line 153):** The Discussion speculates that SARS-CoV-2 `ORF10` acts as a Vif-like APOBEC3 antagonist in bats and humans to explain the absence of an A3 footprint in sarbecoviruses; however, recent zoonotic spillover alone fully accounts for why SARS-CoV-2 reflects its reservoir evolutionary history, and `ORF10` is not conserved as a functional protein across bat sarbecoviruses.

### 4. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **[Well Supported] *Papillomaviridae*, *Polyomaviridae*, and autonomous *Parvoviridae* (especially B19 erythroparvovirus) exhibit deep, genome-wide, dual-strand depletion of synonymous $5'\text{-TC}$ ($\text{NTC}$ and $\text{NNGANN}$) motifs:** Robustly supported by position-specific and overlapping-frame analyses.
- **[Well Supported] *Adenoviridae* and EBV exhibit localized, strand-asymmetric $\text{NTC}$ depletion at displaced/lagging strands flanking lytic replication origins (`E1A`/`E4` and `OriLyt`):** Supported by clear spatial and strand-polarity concordance (Figures 8–9).
- **[Moderately Supported] Endemic human coronaviruses (*HKU1*, *NL63*, *OC43*, *229E*) carry a positive-strand A3 footprint absent from zoonotic coronaviruses:** Consistent with prolonged human circulation and experimental *NL63* restriction data, though partially confounded by general $3\text{rd}$-position $\text{C}\to\text{U}$ mutational bias under the mononucleotide shuffling null model.

### 5. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
Data availability and methodological transparency are strong (`1.8 / 2.0`), with all processed $k$-mer ratios and GenBank accession IDs across all 44,824 analyzed viral genomes deposited in Dryad.
