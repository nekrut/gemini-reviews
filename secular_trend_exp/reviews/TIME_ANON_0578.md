### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This dry-lab bioinformatics study investigates the phylogenetic relationships, functional divergence (Type I and Type II via DIVERGE 2.0), codon-level selective pressures ($K_a/K_s$ via Selecton), structural variation (ProDy principal component analysis of 71 PDB structures / 227 conformations), and coarse-grained normal mode dynamics (Anisotropic Network Model, ANM, on crystal structures and Modeller 9v8 homology models) of 10 $\text{I}\kappa\text{B}$ family members across vertebrates and invertebrates. While integrating sequence evolution with structural dynamics is a reasonable conceptual goal, the execution contains severe methodological, statistical, and reporting flaws: applying a nucleotide distance model (Jukes–Cantor) to amino acid alignments, claiming bootstrap support for Bayesian MCMC trees, reporting negative $\theta_I$ divergence coefficients, extracting 33 "Type II functional divergence sites" after the omnibus test for Type II divergence failed ($p > 0.05$), misinterpreting null model `M8a` in $K_a/K_s$ tests, and contradicting sequence counts across sections.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
* **Invalid Substitution Model and Support Metrics in Phylogenetic Reconstruction:** In Methods ("Sequence alignments and phylogenetic analysis", line 35), the authors align *protein* sequences (`BLOSUM62` matrix in MAFFT) and then state: *"The molecular distances between the aligned sequences were calculated using the Jukes-Cantor genetic distance model."* The Jukes–Cantor model is a 4-state nucleotide substitution model (or an unrealistic equal-rate 20-state Poisson model), inappropriate for deep metazoan protein divergences spanning sponge to human where empirical amino acid rate matrices (e.g., WAG, LG, JTT) with gamma rate heterogeneity are required. Immediately thereafter, for the MrBayes v3.1.2 MCMC analysis, the authors state: *"Branch points were tested for significance by bootstrapping with 1000 replicates."* Bayesian MCMC phylogenetic inference estimates posterior clade probabilities from sampled trees, not bootstrap proportions.
* **Statistical Fallacy in Type II Functional Divergence Analysis (DIVERGE 2.0):** In Results ("Sites contributing to type I and II functional divergence...", line 163), the authors explicitly report that *"In contrast to the significant estimates of $\theta_I$, no evidence for type II functional divergence was observed (data not shown) between any of the pairwise groups with extremely small $\theta_{II}$ values... (p>0.05)."* Despite failing to reject the null hypothesis $\theta_{II} = 0$ in every pairwise subfamily comparison, the authors proceed to identify and tabulate 33 "Type II functional divergence sites" (Table 3, Fig. 4C) using an arbitrary post-hoc site ratio cutoff ($>17$). When the global parameter $\theta_{II}$ is indistinguishable from zero, posterior site ratios reflect background noise rather than statistically supported Type II divergence.
* **Out-of-Bounds Negative Parameter Estimate in Table 2 ($\theta_I$):** In Methods (line 45), the authors state that the coefficient of Type I functional divergence satisfies $\theta \in [0, 1]$, where $\theta = 0$ indicates no functional divergence. Yet in Table 2, the pairwise comparison between `Beta` and `NF-kB1` reports $\theta_I = -0.3538 \pm 0.0880$ (and `ND` in the lower triangle for the LRT statistic), without explanation. Furthermore, the `NF-kB2` vs. `Bcl3` cell in Table 2 contains a corrupted value (`1.[YEAR]`, likely a 4-digit decimal scrubbed by an automated regex).
* **Misinterpretation of Selecton $K_a/K_s$ Models M8 and M8a:** In Results ("Selective pressure at amino acid sites...", chunk 2 line 43), the authors state: *"The M8 evolutionary model showed that the overall Ka/Ks ratio was <1 for all I$\kappa$B subfamily members, except I$\kappa$B$\beta$, I$\kappa$BNS, Relish and NF-$\kappa$B1... Moreover, the M8a null model showed a statistical significance for all I$\kappa$B subfamily members."* In codon-based likelihood ratio testing, `M8a` ($\beta + \omega_s = 1$) is the null hypothesis against which the alternative positive-selection model `M8` ($\beta + \omega_s \ge 1$) is tested via $2\Delta \ell$; a null model cannot "show statistical significance" on its own, and no log-likelihoods, $2\Delta \ell$ statistics, $p$-values, or Empirical Bayes posterior probabilities for the claimed positively selected sites are reported.
* **Internal Sequence Count Contradictions:**
  1. Table 1 lists subfamily sequence counts summing to 386 (`67 + 36 + 37 + 39 + 41 + 32 + 19 + 36 + 42 + 37 = 386`).
  2. Results line 121 states the final phylogenetic dataset contained `340 sequences`, but the itemized list (`64 IκBα, 35 IκBβ, 36 IκBε, 25 Bcl3, 32 IκBNS, 38 IκBζ, 14 Cactus, 25 Relish, 36 NF-κB1 and 34 NF-κB2`) sums to `339`.
  3. Results line 123 and Figure 2 state that the all-$\text{I}\kappa\text{B}$ tree included `328 sequences`, while Figure 3A (`297 vertebrate`) + Figure 3B (`43 invertebrate`) sums to `340`.
* **Twilight-Zone Homology Modeling with Unaligned Insertions:** The homology model of *Drosophila* Relish was built on template `1N11` (human ankyrin-R) sharing only 20% sequence identity, while retaining 14–31 residue unaligned loop insertions in `IκBNS`, `IκBζ`, `Cactus`, and `Relish` (`Figure S2`). Running coarse-grained elastic network normal mode analysis (`ANM`) on unrefined, template-less 20–31 residue loop insertions inevitably produces large artificial C$\alpha$ fluctuations in those poorly packed loops (Figures 6–9), creating a circular artifact when the authors conclude that "the highly flexible regions generally coincided with the finger loop regions."

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
* **Broad Subfamily Clustering of Vertebrate and Invertebrate $\text{I}\kappa\text{B}$ Homologs: `[Moderately Supported]`**  
  Grouping the 10 analyzed $\text{I}\kappa\text{B}$ members into RHD-containing precursors (`Relish`, `NF-κB1`, `NF-κB2`), canonical cytoplasmic inhibitors (`Cactus`, `IκBα`, `IκBβ`, `IκBε`), and nuclear regulators (`Bcl3`, `IκBζ`, `IκBNS`) is consistent with established domain architecture and prior phylogenies (e.g., Huguet et al., Gilmore et al.).
* **Identification of 33 Type II Functional Divergence Sites Driving Partner Specificity: `[Methodologically Flawed]`**  
  Because the global test for Type II divergence was non-significant ($p > 0.05$) across all subfamilies, the 33 sites reported in Table 3 lack statistical validity.
* **Positive Selection Claims in Non-ARD Regions of `IκBβ`, `IκBNS`, `Relish`, and `NF-κB1`: `[Methodologically Flawed]`**  
  Reporting site-specific positive selection without valid `M8` vs. `M8a` likelihood ratio test statistics, posterior probability thresholds, or alignment curation of hypervariable non-ARD disorder regions (which are prone to alignment-induced false positives in $K_a/K_s$ pipelines) is unsupported.
