### 1. Executive Summary
This study performs genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and haplotype regression analysis (HTR) for Northern Corn Leaf Blight (NCLB, caused by *Setosphaeria turcica*) resistance across three tropical/sub-tropical CIMMYT maize association mapping panels: CAAM ($N = 376$ phenotyped out of 419), DTMA ($N = 224$ out of 285), and IMAS ($N = 324$ out of 380). Field trials with artificial inoculation were conducted in India across three locations for CAAM (Mandya, Arabhavi, Kashmir; alpha-lattice design, 2 reps), one location for DTMA (Mandya; alpha-lattice, 2 reps), and two seasons at Mandya for IMAS (augmented block design). Using imputed 955K GBS markers filtered to ~294K–310K SNPs (CR $\ge 0.7$, MAF $\ge 0.05$) and a single-locus mixed linear model (MLM: G + 10 PCs + IBS Kinship) on days-to-anthesis (DA)-adjusted BLUPs, the authors report 22 significant SNPs across the three panels and 17 haplotype blocks shared between at least two panels at $\text{FDR} \le 0.05$. The multi-panel field evaluation and explicit correction for flowering time (anthesis date) and population structure/kinship are clear methodological strengths, though the haplotype selection procedure introduces circularity ("double dipping") and the genome-wide significance threshold uses an overly permissive $\alpha = 1$ Bonferroni criterion.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Circularity in Two-Step Haplotype Regression (Double Dipping):** In the "GWAS and haplotype regression" section (lines 121, 233), the authors select the top 0.1% most significant SNPs from the single-SNP GWAS in each panel (842 SNPs forming 112 haplotype blocks) and then perform stepwise haplotype regression on the exact same phenotypic BLUPs in the same panels, claiming significance at $\text{FDR} \le 0.05$. Because the SNPs forming these haplotypes were pre-selected for extreme marginal association with the phenotype on the same dataset, subsequent regression p-values and FDR estimates are severely biased downward, and the reported $R^2$ values (up to 13.90%) are inflated.
- **Permissive Genome-Wide Significance Threshold:** The authors state that they used the effective number of independent markers ($r^2 < 0.1$) with a "Bonferroni corrected P value threshold at $\alpha = 1$" (line 231), which yields permissive cutoffs around $P \sim 1.6 \times 10^{-5}$ to $8.6 \times 10^{-6}$ (Table 4). Under a standard family-wise error rate of $\alpha = 0.05$ across ~65,000–140,000 independent LD blocks ($P < 3.5 \times 10^{-7}$ to $7.5 \times 10^{-7}$), only the chromosome 7 lead SNP in CAAM (`S7_165196774`, $P = 5.27 \times 10^{-7}$) and the chromosome 8 cluster at 157.98 Mb in IMAS (`S8_157987611`, $P = 4.98 \times 10^{-8}$) survive strict genome-wide significance.
- **Phenotypic Modelling & Anthesis Date Adjustment:** The authors appropriately identify a strong negative genetic correlation between NCLB severity and days to anthesis ($r_g = -0.33$ to $-0.49$, $P \le 0.001$) and compute DA-adjusted BLUPs prior to GWAS, preventing spurious maturity-driven associations. However, in Table 2, the significance asterisks for genotypic variance in IMAS single seasons (`0.120` and `0.385`) are omitted despite the text claiming significant genotypic variance ($P \le 0.001$).
- **Inconsistent Haplotype Allele Directionality Across Panels:** In Table 5, several "common haplotypes" actually have opposite or distinct "favourable alleles" across panels (e.g., `Hap_1.2` favourable allele is `CTATG` in DTMA vs. `GCGAC` in IMAS; `Hap_4.2` is `GAGC` in CAAM vs. `ATAT` in IMAS; `Hap_8.2` is `AGCAA` in DTMA vs. `TAAGG` in IMAS; `Hap_10.3` is `ATA` in CAAM vs. `CCT` in DTMA). While this can occur if the causal variant is on different haplotypic backgrounds or if *S. turcica* race composition differs across trials, the manuscript treats them as directly shared resistance haplotypes without discussing phase reversal.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim: Major association at Chr 8 bin 8.06 (~157.98 Mb) in IMAS and Chr 7 bin 7.04 (~165.19 Mb) in CAAM co-localizes with known *Ht2/Htn1* and *Ht3* resistance regions** — `[Well Supported]`. The IMAS signal on Chr 8 (`S8_157987611`, $P = 4.98 \times 10^{-8}$) is robust and aligns with well-characterized NCLB resistance loci (*Ht2* / *Htn1*).
- **Claim: 17 common haplotypes are validated across panels** — `[Overstated]`. Please note that the abstract claims "two common haplotypes across three maize panels" (line 7) whereas the Results explicitly state "No haplotype was identified to have a significant effect on the trait in all three AM panels" (line 121)—an internal contradiction between the Abstract and Results. Furthermore, pre-selecting the top 0.1% SNPs before haplotype regression invalidates the nominal FDR claims, and 6 of the 17 haplotypes have discordant favourable alleles between panels.
- **Claim: Functional candidate genes (`GRMZM2G116426`, `GRMZM2G319130`, etc.) underlie NCLB resistance** — `[Moderately Supported]`. These are plausible positional annotations based on B73 RefGen_v2 physical coordinates, though no functional validation or expression data are presented.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** The manuscript notes that the 955K GBS dataset is available through Panzea (`www.panzea.org`), and SNP coordinates on B73 AGPV2 are explicitly reported in Tables 4 and 5. However, the exact sample IDs of the 376 CAAM, 224 DTMA, and 324 IMAS subsets and their phenotypic BLUPs are not deposited in an accessible repository.
- **Methodological Detail:** Field inoculation protocols, statistical mixed models for alpha-lattice and augmented designs, and filtering parameters are clearly specified.
