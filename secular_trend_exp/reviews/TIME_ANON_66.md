### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates why primary resting mouse $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells exhibit delayed c-Jun $\text{NH}_2$-terminal kinase (JNK) enzymatic activation (peaking at 24–48 h rather than 15–120 min) following T cell receptor (TCR/CD3) and CD28 costimulation, in stark contrast to the rapid activation previously characterized in immortalized Jurkat T cells. Combining Northern blotting, in vitro GST–c-Jun pull-down kinase assays, Western blotting, intracellular phospho-JNK and total-JNK flow cytometry, CTLL IL-2 bioassays, and AP-1 luciferase reporter transgenic mice, the authors demonstrate that: (1) unlike brain, lung, liver, Jurkat cells, or immature $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD8}^+$ and $\text{CD4}^+$ single-positive thymocytes, peripheral resting splenic and lymph node $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells (and B cells) express negligible *Jnk1* mRNA/protein and very low *Jnk2* mRNA/protein; (2) TCR ligation alone (anti-CD3 mAb, or in vivo superantigen SEB challenge), even when CD28 costimulation is blocked by soluble CTLA4-Ig, is sufficient to transcriptionally induce *Jnk1* and *Jnk2* mRNA and protein expression over 24–48 h; (3) however, enzymatic phosphorylation/activation of the newly synthesized JNK protein strictly requires both TCR (anti-CD3) and CD28 costimulatory signals; and (4) initial IL-2 secretion by primary $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells precedes the induction of JNK and AP-1 transcriptional activity, explaining why *Jnk1*- or *Jnk2*-deficient naive $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells produce normal early IL-2 levels.

This is a biologically insightful and mechanistically cohesive study that resolves a major discrepancy between immortalized T-cell line signaling models (Jurkat) and primary naive lymphocytes. By using multiple orthogonal assays (Northern blot, immunoblot, solid-phase kinase assay, intracellular FACS, and transgenic reporter mice) and clean costimulation blockade controls (CTLA4-Ig), the core conclusions are strongly supported. The primary limitations are the absence of quantitative densitometry/replicate statistics across experiments, missing figures in the text-only view, and several secondary claims cited as "(data not shown)" or "unpublished data".

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Orthogonal Multi-Assay Validation:** Every step of the signaling cascade is interrogated by at least two independent techniques: mRNA induction by Northern blot across multiple stimuli (PMA/ionomycin, anti-CD3 $\pm$ anti-CD28, CTLA4-Ig, in vivo SEB); total protein expression by both SDS-PAGE immunoblot (resolving p46 and p55 isoforms) and intracellular FACS; enzymatic activation by both solid-phase GST–c-Jun $[\gamma\text{-}^{32}\text{P}]\text{ATP}$ PhosphorImager kinase assays and dual-phospho-Thr/Tyr intracellular FACS; and downstream functional readout via AP-1 luciferase transgenic T cells and CTLL proliferation assays.
* **Control for Endogenous APC Costimulation:** When testing whether TCR signaling alone suffices to induce *Jnk1/2* expression (lines 77–79), the authors verify both in purified $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells (confirming absence of IL-2 production without anti-CD28 to rule out contaminating APC costimulation) and in unfractionated splenocytes treated with soluble CTLA4-Ig to block B7.1/B7.2 engagement. This two-pronged control cleanly separates the signaling requirements for *Jnk* gene induction (Signal 1 alone) from JNK phosphorylation (Signal 1 + Signal 2).
* **Absence of Replicate Counts ($n$) and Statistical Variability Metrics:** While the qualitative and kinetic shifts (e.g., absence of *Jnk1* in resting peripheral T cells vs. strong induction at 24–48 h) are all-or-none effects, the manuscript does not state the number of independent mice or biological replicates ($n$) performed for each Northern blot, Western blot, FACS profile, or luciferase/IL-2 time course, nor are standard deviations or statistical tests reported.
* **Purity of Negatively Selected $\text{CD4}^+$ T Cells:** In Materials and Methods (line 28), $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells are isolated from spleen and lymph nodes by negative selection using anti-NK1.1, anti-CD8, and anti-MHC class II mAbs. The percentage purity of the resulting $\text{CD4}^+$ population (and the proportion of naive $\text{CD44}^{\text{low}}\text{CD62L}^{\text{high}}$ vs. memory $\text{CD44}^{\text{high}}$ cells, which could account for the faint basal *Jnk2* band) is not reported, although the intracellular FACS gating on $\text{CD4}^+$ cells (lines 43, 85) directly confirms the single-cell phenotype within gated $\text{CD4}^+$ cells.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Two-Tier Regulation of JNK in Primary T Cells (TCR-Driven Expression vs. CD28-Dependent Phosphorylation) [Well Supported]:** The central claim—that resting peripheral T cells maintain a transcriptionally silent/low *Jnk1/2* state that requires TCR-triggered gene expression prior to CD28-dependent kinase activation—is convincingly demonstrated both in vitro and in vivo (SEB injection).
* **Resolution of the Jurkat vs. Primary T Cell Kinetic Paradox [Well Supported]:** Showing that unstimulated Jurkat T cells constitutively express high levels of *Jnk1/2* mRNA and protein (matching pre-activated blasts or thymocytes rather than resting peripheral T cells) provides an immediate mechanistic explanation for why Jurkat cells activate JNK within minutes of CD3+CD28 crosslinking whereas primary $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells require 24–48 h.
* **Developmental Downregulation Between Single-Positive Thymocytes and Peripheral T Cells [Moderately Supported]:** Figure 4 shows that both $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD8}^+$ DP and $\text{CD4}^+$ SP thymocytes constitutively express *Jnk1/2* mRNA and protein, whereas peripheral $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells do not. This is well supported, though the finer localization of this downregulation to $\text{HSA}^{\text{high}}$ vs. $\text{HSA}^{\text{low}}$ SP thymocyte subsets is cited as "unpublished data" (line 85), and the teleological hypothesis that silencing *Jnk* in resting peripheral T cells prevents activation-induced apoptosis upon initial antigen encounter remains speculative.
* **Induction of Upstream Kinases (*Mkk4* and *Mkk7*) [Fragile]:** The claim that *Mkk4* and *Mkk7* mRNAs are also upregulated in activated T cells (line 73) is cited entirely as "(data not shown)".

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Reagent Specification:** Antibody clones/vendors (PharMingen anti-NK1.1, anti-CD8, anti-JNK mAb; New England Biolabs anti-phospho-JNK polyclonal), mouse reporter lines, and kinase assay protocols are well referenced.
* **Missing Elements:** Exact concentrations/clone numbers of the stimulating anti-CD3 and anti-CD28 mAbs, immobilization method (plate-bound vs. soluble/crosslinked), concentration of SEB injected in vivo, concentration of CTLA4-Ig, and the nucleotide coordinates of the *Jnk1* and *Jnk2* cDNA Northern blot probes are not detailed in the text, and four results are cited as "(data not shown)" or "unpublished data" (lines 63, 73, 77, 85).
