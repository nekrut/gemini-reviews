### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the frequency, phenotypic diversity, and underlying molecular genetic mechanisms of human leukocyte antigen (HLA) class I loss in 30 freshly isolated primary cervical carcinoma suspensions. By combining four-color multiparameter DNA flow cytometry (separating keratin-positive epithelial tumor cells from CD45/vimentin-positive stromal/leukocyte internal controls and gating on DNA ploidy) with fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), chromosome 6p microsatellite (MS) loss-of-heterozygosity (LOH) mapping, allele-specific PCR, and Sanger sequencing of HLA-A/B exons 2–4 and $\beta_2$-microglobulin ($\beta_2$m) exons 1–2, the authors demonstrate that 90% (27/30) of primary cervical tumors harbor HLA class I alterations. Most notably, 50% (15/30) exhibit loss of a full HLA haplotype associated with LOH at 6p21.3, often compounded by somatic point mutations, frameshifts, or deletions in a third allele or $\beta_2$m. The experimental design is exceptionally thorough in overcoming two classical pitfalls of tumor immunogenetics: incomplete antibody coverage on frozen tissue sections and normal leukocyte DNA contamination during molecular genotyping.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Internal Control Design & Flow Cytometric Gating:** The four-color staining strategy (anti-HLA-APC, anti-keratin-RPE, anti-CD45/vimentin-FITC, and propidium iodide for DNA ploidy) provides rigorous internal patient-matched normal controls (`LCA+/Vim+/Ker-` diploid cells) alongside autologous peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBLs). The authors carefully account for mouse IgG isotype cross-reactivity by substituting FITC-labeled anti-vimentin (IgG2b) when murine IgG1 anti-HLA antibodies are deployed. Spectral cross-talk of propidium iodide (FL2 into FL1) is explicitly recognized and gated around (Figure 1A).
- **LOH and Allelic Imbalance Thresholds:** Microsatellite allelic ratios were computed by normalizing the peak height ratio ($p_1/p_2$) of normal cells to that of flow-sorted tumor cells (taking the inverse for values $<1$). Retention of heterozygosity (ROH) is defined as $\le 1.3$, allelic imbalance (a.i.) as $\ge 1.8$, and complete LOH or ratios $\ge 4.0$ as LOH. While these cutoffs are reasonable for FACS-purified populations, values falling in the intermediate gray zone ($1.3 < \text{ratio} < 1.8$) are shown without formal statistical confidence intervals on peak height measurement variance.
- **Reclassification Consistency:** In the Results section, the authors initially note 26/30 (87%) tumors with phenotypic alterations and 4/30 (13%) with constitutive expression. However, Tumor 13—which was homozygous at HLA-A/B—showed extensive 6p LOH by MS markers and was retrospectively moved into the haplotype loss category, bringing total altered cases to 27/30 (90%) and haplotype loss to 15/30 (50%). Similarly, Tumors 46 and 47 showed phenotypic loss of only a single HLA-B allele, but MS LOH and allele-specific PCR showed genomic loss of HLA-A11 despite residual phenotypic staining ("rest expression"), prompting reclassification into haplotype loss. While biologically transparent, reclassifying phenotypically normal or single-allele tumors based on genotypic LOH slightly conflates phenotypic surface loss with genomic LOH in the summary percentages (Figure 6).
- **Sample Size & Descriptive Statistics:** With $n = 30$ consecutive clinical specimens (21 squamous, 8 adenocarcinoma, 1 adenosquamous), the study is well-powered to estimate the overall prevalence of HLA class I defects in operable cervical cancer, though subgroup comparisons across HPV genotypes (18 HPV16/18, 7 other HPV types, 5 HPV-negative) lack statistical power for formal hypothesis testing.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Altered HLA class I expression occurs in ~90% of primary cervical cancers and is predominantly (~67–70%) driven by irreversible genetic alterations at 6p21.3 or $\beta_2$m.* `[Well Supported]` — Directly substantiated by flow-sorted MS analysis across 6–8 markers spanning 6p and direct sequencing of truncating stop codons (Tumors 6, 15), indels (Tumors 14, 30), and $\beta_2$m frameshifts (Tumor 49) in Table 2.
- **Claim 2:** *Flow cytometry on fresh tumor suspensions is superior to immunohistochemistry (IHC) for resolving intratumor heterogeneity and complete allelic repertoires.* `[Well Supported]` — Human allo-mAbs cannot be used on frozen tissue sections due to endogenous human Ig background, and IHC frequently misclassified bimodal/heterogeneous expression as uniformly negative or positive.
- **Claim 3:** *HLA class I loss can occur early (prior to aneuploidization in diploid keratin-positive tumor fractions) as well as during or after aneuploidization.* `[Moderately Supported]` — The ploidy-stratified sorting in Tumors 6, 10, 25, 36, and 56 demonstrates HLA loss and LOH in diploid `Ker+` fractions alongside aneuploid fractions, though pseudodiploid evolution cannot be entirely excluded without karyotyping.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Complete primer sequences, annealing profiles, and genomic positions for HLA-A, HLA-B (exons 2–4), and $\beta_2$m (exons 1–2) PCR and sequencing are provided in Table 1.
- Exact nucleotide and codon positions of all identified somatic mutations are documented in Table 2, and full tumor-by-tumor phenotypic/genotypic matrices are provided in Figure 4.
- Minor omission: GenBank accession numbers for the novel somatic mutant alleles are not listed, and several figures (e.g., allele-specific PCR gels for Tumors 4, 30, 46, 47) are cited as "data not shown."
