# Blinded Review: TIME_ANON_51
**Composite Score:** 4.0 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.1/3.5 | Claims: 1.0/2.5 | Biology: 1.0/2.0 | Reproducibility: 0.9/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Severely Underpowered Subgroup/Interaction Fishing & Internal Text-Result Contradiction
**Verdict:** Reject
**Study Modality:** Wet-Lab Primary Experimental
**Technology Maturity:** Mid-Stage High-Throughput
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript reports a cross-sectional candidate-gene resting-state fMRI study investigating the interaction between two oxytocin receptor (*OXTR*) single-nucleotide polymorphisms (rs53576 and rs2228485) and bipolar disorder (BD; $n = 39$ patients vs. $n = 32$ healthy controls [HC]) on striatocortical functional connectivity (FC), alongside subgroup correlations with plasma oxytocin (OXT) levels and Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ) scores. While integrating clinical phenotyping, genotyping, and neuroimaging addresses a clinically relevant question, the study is severely underpowered for $2 \times 2$ gene-by-diagnosis interaction modeling ($n = 14\text{--}25$ per cell), performs unadjusted multiple testing across multiple seeds, SNPs, and within-cell covariate regressions, relies on unvalidated plasma OXT immunoassays, and reverses the direction of its own empirical findings between the Results section and the Abstract/Discussion.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Extreme Small-Sample Subgroup Partitioning ($n = 14\text{--}25$ per cell):** Dividing $N = 71$ total participants into four genotype-by-diagnosis subgroups yields cell sizes of $n = 14$ (BD AA homozygotes), $n = 25$ (BD G-allele carriers), $n = 17$ (HC AA homozygotes), and $n = 15$ (HC G-allele carriers) (Table 1). Testing whole-brain voxelwise $2 \times 2$ interactions and subsequently running separate whole-brain regressions against plasma OXT and CTQ within individual cells of $n = 14$ to $n = 25$ has negligible statistical power and is highly susceptible to outlier-driven false positives and inflated effect sizes (Type I and Type M errors).
* **Uncontrolled Multiplicity Across Analytic Dimensions:** Although individual SPM12 maps use voxel-level $p < 0.001$ (uncorrected) with cluster-level FWE $p < 0.05$, no correction is applied across the combinatorial testing space: 2 SNPs (rs53576 and rs2228485) $\times$ 4 striatal seeds (left and right ventral and dorsal striatum) $\times$ multiple factorial pairwise contrasts $\times$ 4 subgroup whole-brain regressions $\times$ 2 covariates (plasma OXT and CTQ) $\times$ 2 one-tailed directions.
* **Direct Contradiction Between Results and Abstract/Discussion:** In the Abstract (line 11) and Discussion (lines 180 and 188), the authors claim that striatocortical FC alterations were "reversed among BD patients, with hypo-FC in AA homozygotes and hyper-FC in G-allele carriers." However, the Results section (lines 132–134) explicitly reports the exact opposite: (1) "There was no significant difference between G allele carrier BD patients and AA homozygous BD patients" (line 132), and (2) "In rs2228485 G allele carriers, BD patients demonstrated decreased ventral striatocortical FC with the primary auditory cortex and auditory association area compared to HC... Conversely, among rs2228485 AA homozygotes, BD patients exhibited increased ventral striatocortical FC with the visual association area and insula compared to HC" (line 134). Thus, BD AA homozygotes showed *hyper*-FC relative to HC AA homozygotes and BD G-allele carriers showed *hypo*-FC relative to HC G-allele carriers, with no significant difference between genotypes within BD.
* **Terminological and Statistical Misstatement in Regression Analysis:** In Methods (line 67), the authors state that "two one-tailed two-sample t-tests were conducted to assess the correlations between FC and either plasma OXT levels or CTQ scores for each genotype." A correlation with a continuous regressor within a single group is a one-sample slope $t$-test in a general linear model, not a two-sample $t$-test. Furthermore, Table 1 reports a single overall chi-square $p$-value ($0.145$) under the "Interaction" column for sample size rather than the Group $\times$ Genotype contingency test, and lists two separate row $p$-values ($0.073$ and $0.864$) for gender without specifying the comparison.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Unreliable Peripheral Plasma OXT Assay:** In Table 1, mean plasma OXT concentrations are reported as $229.08 \pm 215.58\text{ pg/mL}$ in BD AA homozygotes and $114.48 \pm 72.01\text{ pg/mL}$ in HC AA homozygotes. In unextracted plasma immunoassays, physiological OXT (<10 pg/mL) is overwhelmed by cross-reacting non-OXT plasma matrix proteins, producing values in the 100–300 pg/mL range with massive variance (SD $\approx$ mean). While the authors acknowledge in the Limitations (line 203) that OXT immunoreactivity cannot infer true OXT concentrations, they still rely on relative correlations with these unvalidated measurements to claim "oxytocinergic modulation of the attention networks."
* **Uncontrolled Medication and Clinical State Confounding:** All 39 BD patients were receiving heterogeneous psychotropic regimens (valproic acid, lithium, antipsychotics, antidepressants; line 82) while all 32 HCs were unmedicated, and 33.3% of BD patients were non-euthymic. Thus, the "disease" factor is completely confounded with psychotropic medication exposure.
* **Overstated Mechanistic and Clinical Claims:** Despite a cross-sectional sample of $N = 71$ and no significant genotype main effects or interactions on any clinical scale (CTQ, HDRS, YMRS, or loneliness; Table 1, all genotype and interaction $p > 0.08$), the Discussion speculates on genetic profiling for intranasal oxytocin clinical trials and early intervention programs for healthy G-allele carriers (line 194).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Omission of Essential Experimental and MRI Parameters:** The Methods section (line 45) defers all details of plasma OXT assay protocols, MRI scanner field strength, pulse sequences (TR, TE, voxel resolution, scan duration), head-motion scrubbing/regression, global signal regression, and spatial smoothing to previously published papers.
* **No Public Data or Statistical Map Availability:** Neither the de-identified subject-level demographic/clinical table, the raw or preprocessed neuroimaging data, nor the unthresholded whole-brain statistical $t$/$z$-maps (e.g., via NeuroVault) are deposited in a public repository.
