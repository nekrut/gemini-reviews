### 1. Executive Summary
This candidate-gene association study screens exons 2–5 of the porcine *METTL23* gene via Sanger sequencing in $N = 228$ multiparous Kele sows and tests three identified SNPs—`g.4804958 G>T` (intron 2), `g.4805082 C>T` (exon 2, synonymous Pro), and `g.4806821 A>G` (exon 3, synonymous Leu)—and their reconstructed haplotypes/diplotypes against six correlated reproductive traits (piglets born alive, litter birth weight, average birth weight, number of weaned piglets, weaning litter weight, and weaning weight). The authors also run RNAfold to predict minimum free energy (MFE) changes in mRNA secondary structure for the two synonymous SNPs and conclude that *METTL23* (specifically genotype `GG` at `g.4804958` and diplotype `H4H4` [$n = 6$]) can be used for marker-assisted selection (MAS). Forensic audit reveals severe biological confusion regarding what protein *METTL23* actually encodes, mathematical and arithmetic errors across Tables 2 and 4, failure to account for repeated records across parities or familial relatedness in a single breeding farm, lack of multiple-testing correction, and over-interpretation of a tiny subgroup ($n = 6$ sows for `H4H4`).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Internal Mathematical & Tabular Inconsistencies:**
  - **Table 2 (PIC & Chi-Square Errors):** In the Abstract (line 7) and Results (line 79), the authors state that the Polymorphism Information Content (PIC) for `g.4804958 G>T` is `0.264`, whereas Table 2 (line 85) reports `0.364`. Given allele frequencies $p = 0.603$ and $q = 0.397$, heterozygosity is $H = 2pq = 0.4788$ and $\text{PIC} = H - 2p^2q^2 = 0.3642$, meaning the Abstract, Results, and Discussion text (`0.264`) are all wrong. Furthermore, for a biallelic HWE $\chi^2$ test with 1 degree of freedom, $\chi^2 = 7.774$ yields $p = 0.0053$ (not `0.02` as reported in Table 2), and $\chi^2 = 6.537$ yields $p = 0.0106$ (not `0.03`).
  - **Table 4 (Diplotype Sum & Frequency Errors):** The sample size is $N = 228$ sows. However, summing the diplotype counts in Table 4 gives $46 + 38 + 26 + 25 + 19 + 20 + 16 + 8 + 24 + 6 = 228$, yet for `H2H2` ($n = 19$), the reported frequency is $19 / 228 = 0.0833$, whereas Table 4 reports `0.104` (which would correspond to $n = 23.7$). Even more glaringly, summing the `GG` homozygotes at `g.4804958` across Table 4 diplotypes (`H2H2` [19] + `H2H3` [20] + `H2H4` [16] + `H3H3` [8] + `H3H4` [24] + `H4H4` [6]) equals **93**, matching Table 2 (`GG = 93`), but summing the `AA` homozygotes at `g.4806821` across Table 4 (`H1H1` [46] + `H1H2` [38] + `H2H2` [19]) equals **103**, while summing `GG` homozygotes at `g.4806821` (`H3H3` [8] + `H3H4` [24] + `H4H4` [6]) equals **38**, and `AG` heterozygotes (`H1H3` [26] + `H1H4` [25] + `H2H3` [20] + `H2H4` [16]) equals **87**. Yet in the text (line 103), the authors claim *"Diplotype H1H2 had the highest frequency (0.202)"* when Table 4 actually shows `H1H1 (46)` has frequency `0.202` and `H1H2 (38)` has frequency `0.167`!
  - **Table 5 & Table 6 Inconsistency for Identical Genotypes:** Notice that diplotype `H1H1` ($n = 46$, `TT-CC-AA`) is the *only* diplotype carrying the `TT` genotype at `g.4804958` ($n = 46$ in Table 2 and Table 5). Thus, the 46 `TT` individuals in Table 5 and the 46 `H1H1` individuals in Table 6 are the **exact same 46 sows**. Yet their reported trait means and SDs differ between Table 5 and Table 6: e.g., Piglets Born Alive is `7.568 ± 2.534` in Table 5 vs. `7.643 ± 2.792` in Table 6; Litter Birth Weight is `6.403 ± 2.736` in Table 5 vs. `6.271 ± 2.050` in Table 6; Number of Weaned Piglets is `5.405 ± 2.327` in Table 5 vs. `5.750 ± 2.102` in Table 6; Weaning Weight is `5.808 ± 1.301` in Table 5 vs. `5.638 ± 0.683` in Table 6. The exact same discrepancy occurs between `TT` at `g.4805082` ($n = 8$,Table 5: Weaning Litter Weight `32.363 ± 4.800`) and `H3H3` ($n = 8$, the only `TT` diplotype, Table 6: Weaning Litter Weight `31.362 ± 4.800`). This is a critical internal consistency failure.
- **Uncontrolled Relatedness, Repeated Measures, and Multiple Testing:** All 228 sows were sampled from a single farm with "two to four births" recorded per sow. The linear model $y_{ijk} = \mu + G_i + P_j + G_i \times P_j + e_{ijk}$ includes neither a random permanent environmental effect for repeated parities of the same sow nor a pedigree/genomic kinship matrix ($A$ or $G$) to control for family structure within a closed local breed farm. Testing 3 SNPs and 10 diplotypes across 6 traits (45 pairwise diplotype comparisons per trait) at nominal $p < 0.05$ without multiple-testing correction guarantees false positives.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Fundamental Biological Confusion on *METTL23* Identity:** The authors completely conflate two unrelated enzymes in the Abstract, Introduction, and Discussion:
  - In the Abstract (line 7) and Discussion (line 170), they state that *"METTL23 is a kind of RNA methyltransferase that catalyzes the methylation transfer to the N6-adenosine of RNA (m6A)"* and bizarrely claim in line 170 that *"Alarcón et al. [41] found that the METTL 23 gene (also known as ZCCHC4) plays an important role as an m6A-modifying enzyme... interacting with HNRNPA2B1."* In reality, Alarcón et al. (2015) studied **METTL3** (not *METTL23*, and neither is *ZCCHC4*, which is a 28S rRNA m6A methyltransferase!).
  - Meanwhile, in the Introduction (line 16), the authors correctly note that *METTL23* is a protein arginine methyltransferase that catalyzes histone H3R17 dimethylation. Conflating *METTL23* with *METTL3* and *ZCCHC4* demonstrates a severe lack of basic biological grounding.
- **Claim: Diplotype `H4H4` ($n = 6$) is the optimal marker for MAS in Kele pigs** — `[Methodologically Flawed]`. Recommending breeding selection based on 6 sows in an unadjusted single-farm candidate gene screen where none of the 3 SNPs change an amino acid is scientifically indefensible.
- **Claim: Synonymous SNPs stabilize *METTL23* mRNA secondary structure to increase translation and improve litter size** — `[Overstated]`. Running RNAfold on full-length mRNA and citing a $\sim 8$–$16\text{ kJ/mol}$ ($\sim 0.5\%$) difference in predicted MFE ($-2657$ vs. $-2665\text{ kJ/mol}$) without any expression or functional assay is pure boilerplate speculation.
