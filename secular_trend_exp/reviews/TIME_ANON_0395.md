### 1. Executive Summary
This study follows a formulaic multi-database bioinformatics plus *in vitro* cell-line validation template in breast cancer. Three GEO microarray datasets (`GSE42568`, `GSE29431`, `GSE21422`) are intersected via `limma` ($|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$, adjusted $p < 0.05$) to obtain 231 overlapping DEGs, which are passed to STRING and Cytoscape CytoHubba (Degree centrality) to select four "hub" genes: *PPARG*, *LEP*, *CD36*, and *PLIN1*. The authors then query web portals (GSCA, HPA, OncoDB, cBioPortal, KM Plotter, TargetScan, TISIDB) and perform RT-qPCR and ELISA across 6 breast cancer and 5 non-cancerous cell lines, plus transient CMV-plasmid overexpression of all four genes in MCF-7 and T47D cells assessed by CCK-8, colony formation, and scratch wound healing assays. While the manuscript presents a large volume of figures (Figs. 1–11), it exhibits severe internal contradictions, biologically implausible mutation frequencies in cBioPortal (claiming 45% of breast cancers carry *PPARG* and *CD36* missense mutations based on $n=11$ samples), confounding of bulk tissue adipocyte depletion with epithelial tumor-suppressor silencing, and a mechanistic model (Fig. 11) that directly contradicts the paper's own functional experiments.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Direct Self-Contradiction Between Functional Assays (Figs. 7–10) and Mechanistic Model (Fig. 11):**
  * Throughout Results (Figs. 7–10), the Abstract, and Conclusion, the authors report that *LEP* (leptin) and *CD36* are downregulated tumor-suppressor hub genes whose overexpression in MCF-7 and T47D cells *inhibits* proliferation, colony formation, and migration ($p < 0.001$).
  * Yet in the final paragraph of Results (line 221) and Figure 11 ("Mechanistic model of hub gene functions in breast cancer progression"), the authors state the exact opposite for *LEP* and *CD36*: *"LEP (leptin), secreted into the extracellular space, binds to its receptor LEPR, activating downstream JAK/STAT3 and PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathways (Fig. 11). This cascade promotes cell proliferation, survival, migration, and EMT... CD36, a fatty acid transporter located at the plasma membrane, facilitates fatty acid (FA) uptake and accumulation into lipid droplets, which can protect cancer cells from lipotoxicity and apoptosis. This mechanism contributes to enhanced fatty acid metabolism, EMT, and therapy resistance (Fig. 11)."* Presenting *LEP* and *CD36* as oncogenic drivers of proliferation/EMT in Fig. 11 after claiming that overexpressing them suppresses proliferation and migration in Figs. 7–10 is a fundamental scientific contradiction.
* **Grossly Misapplied cBioPortal Mutational Analysis ($n=11$ Mutated Subset Treated as Cohort Denominator):** In line 183 ("Genomic alteration analysis of hub genes", Fig. 4A–B), the authors state: *"mutations were observed in all 11 breast cancer samples analyzed, with CD36 and PPARG each altered in 45% of samples, and PLIN1 in 18%, while no mutation was found in LEP genes."* In TCGA-BRCA (>1,000 patients), somatic mutations in *PPARG*, *CD36*, and *PLIN1* occur in <1% of tumors. The authors restricted the cBioPortal OncoPrint to *only the 11 altered samples* out of the entire cohort and reported $5/11 = 45\%$ and $2/11 = 18\%$ as the mutation frequency in breast cancer!
* **Adipose Tissue Composition Confounding in Bulk Breast Microarrays and TCGA:** *PPARG*, *LEP* (leptin), *CD36*, and *PLIN1* (perilipin-1) are the four canonical markers of mature white adipocytes. Normal breast surgical specimens consist predominantly of mammary adipose tissue, whereas invasive breast carcinoma biopsies consist of dense epithelial tumor nests and desmoplastic stroma that displace adipocytes. Consequently, *PPARG*, *LEP*, *CD36*, and *PLIN1* always appear massively "downregulated" in bulk tumor vs. normal breast tissue comparisons (and higher in less cellular Luminal A than hypercellular Basal/HER2 tumors, Fig. 6B) purely due to tissue cellularity/adipocyte fraction rather than epithelial tumor-suppressor loss.
* **Erroneous Normal-Like Subtype Description and Cell Line Panel Issues:** In line 199, the authors state: *"Moreover, Normal-like tissues exhibited lower expression levels of PPARG, LEP, CD36, and PLIN1 across all the molecular subtypes of breast cancer (Fig. 6B)"*—contradicting Fig. 1 and Fig. 2A where normal breast tissue has the *highest* expression. Additionally, the "non-cancerous breast epithelial cell line" panel (line 51) includes `HBL-100`, a cell line long established to harbor integrated SV40 large T antigen and to be tumorigenic/contaminated, and it is biologically implausible that cultured mammary epithelial cell lines in serum-free MEGM express high baseline levels of adipocyte-restricted secreted hormone Leptin (*LEP*) and Perilipin-1 (*PLIN1*) quantifiable by RIPA cell-lysate ELISA (line 61).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Downregulation of *PPARG*, *LEP*, *CD36*, and *PLIN1* in bulk breast tumor vs. normal tissue (`GSE42568`, `GSE29431`, `GSE21422`, TCGA):** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Driven overwhelmingly by the replacement of normal mammary adipose tissue with epithelial tumor cells in bulk biopsies, without single-cell or laser-capture epithelial adjustment.
* **45% somatic missense mutation rate in *PPARG* and *CD36* in breast cancer (Fig. 4):** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Artifact of dividing the number of mutated samples ($5$) by the number of samples carrying a mutation across the 4 genes ($n=11$) in cBioPortal rather than the total sequenced cohort ($N \approx 1,084$).
* **Unified tumor-suppressive mechanism of *LEP*, *CD36*, *PLIN1*, and *PPARG*:** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Directly contradicted by the authors' own pathway model in Figure 11 and line 221, which depicts *LEP* and *CD36* as pro-proliferative, pro-EMT, and pro-survival factors.
