### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript sequences and characterizes ten complete genomes of *Phlebovirus mukawaense* (MKWV, formerly Gomselga virus) recovered from *Ixodes persulcatus* ticks across three Russian regions (Karelia, Chelyabinsk, and Tuva). Using overlapping PCR enrichment, Illumina sequencing, maximum-likelihood phylogenetics, patristic-distance clustering across the L, M, and S segments, and cell-culture inoculation assays in mammalian Vero and Huh-7.5 cells, the authors demonstrate that previously described "Gomselga virus" isolates share 97–98% RdRp amino acid identity with reference strain MKW73, placing them within *P. mukawaense* under ICTV demarcation criteria (<95% RdRp aa identity for species separation) as a distinct genotype ("Gomselga genotype"). They also report topological incongruence across segments indicative of intra- and inter-genotype reassortment and show that five tested Gomselga-genotype tick suspensions fail to replicate in Vero or Huh-7.5 cells over 21 days.

While the complete genome assemblies (deposited under GenBank accessions PQ204551–PQ204580) and taxonomic unification of Gomselga virus into *P. mukawaense* are solid empirical contributions, the computational and experimental analyses have notable methodological weaknesses. Specifically, the segment reassortment analysis explicitly omits bootstrap filtering before computing patristic-distance clusters, conflating poorly resolved shallow nodes with genuine reassortment events. Furthermore, the wet-lab mammalian replication assay lacks an infectious positive control (or tick-cell viability control) to verify that the archived tick homogenates contained replication-competent virions rather than degraded particles with intact RNA. Finally, the manuscript shows internal numerical discrepancies in alignment lengths and overstates the public health and arboviral threat of the Gomselga genotype despite negative mammalian cell culture findings.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Uncalibrated Patristic-Distance Reassortment Workflow:** In the *Segment reassortment* section, the authors extract patristic distances from maximum-likelihood consensus trees for the S, M, and L segments, apply `TreeCluster` (`max_clade`) to optimize $k$ on the L segment via the Calinski–Harabasz index ($k=7$), and impose $k=7$ on the M and S segments. Crucially, the authors state: *"Because no bootstrap filtering was applied, all resolved branches contributed to the distance matrix and fine-scale splits were preserved."* In closely related regional viral isolates (sharing 88–95% nucleotide identity and short alignment fragments such as 914 bp for L), shallow intra-clade splits frequently have low bootstrap support (<70%). Treating every zero- or low-support bifurcation in a consensus tree as a hard topological assignment and projecting cross-segment cluster switches onto a chord diagram (Fig. 7, Table 2) conflates phylogenetic estimation noise with biological segment reassortment. Formal reassortment inference requires either bootstrap-supported topological conflict tests (e.g., Shimodaira–Hasegawa or Approximately Unbiased tests) or coalescent/recombination-aware reassortment models (e.g., GiRaF, TreeKnit, or CoalRe).
* **Unquantified "Correlation" Between Genetic and Geographic Distance:** In the Abstract and Results, the authors claim that *"Genetic distance correlated with geographic origin for the L and M segments"* and *"the correlation between sequence identity and geographic location for the S segment is much lower."* However, no formal statistical test (such as a Mantel test or linear regression of pairwise genetic distance against great-circle geographic distance with a correlation coefficient $r$ and $p$-value) is ever computed; the claim rests entirely on qualitative inspection of similarity ranges relative to a single Japanese reference strain (MKW73).
* **Internal Inconsistencies in Alignment Lengths and Isolate Identifiers:**
  * In *Materials and methods* (and Fig. 6 caption), trimmed alignment lengths are reported as **914 bp** (L), **2,879 bp** (M), and **1,438 bp** (S). However, the captions for Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 state that the ML trees were constructed from a **2,681 bp** fragment of the M segment and a **1,236 bp** fragment of the S segment, respectively.
  * In Table 2, isolate `T20618-21` (L2, Singletons M) is mislabeled as `T20618-621` under Singletons S, and `T27077` is labeled `T27077 L001` under M3.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Taxonomic Classification of Gomselga Virus as a Genotype of *P. mukawaense*: [Well Supported].** Complete L, M, and S segment sequencing shows 97–98% RdRp amino acid identity with MKW73, well above the 95% ICTV species demarcation threshold. Unifying Gomselga virus and Mukawa virus into a single species with two major geographic/phylogenetic lineages is well grounded.
* **Lack of Replication of the Gomselga Genotype in Mammalian Cells: [Fragile].** The authors inoculate Vero cells (5 tick suspensions) and Huh-7.5 cells (1 suspension, `T27359`) and monitor supernatant viral RNA by RT-qPCR for up to 21 days, observing no replication. However, these tick suspensions were archived homogenates from earlier surveys. The experiment includes no positive control (such as a known infectious tick-borne virus or isolation on tick cell lines like ISE6) to demonstrate that viable, infectious viral particles survived homogenization, freeze-thaw storage, and filtration. Without proving that the inoculum contained infectious virions capable of replicating in permissive arthropod cells, failure to replicate in Vero/Huh-7.5 cells cannot be definitively attributed to mammalian host-range restriction rather than sample non-viability.
* **Widespread Intra-Genotype Segment Reassortment: [Overstated].** While the placement of isolate `Irkutsk-4` (which clusters with Gomselga in L1 and M3 but with Mukawa in S1) provides plausible evidence of inter-genotype S-segment reassortment, the broader claim that *"the majority of samples"* have undergone reassortment based on unbootstrapped $k=7$ patristic clustering (Fig. 7) is overstated and sensitive to phylogenetic resolution limits on short fragments (914 bp L segment).
* **Characterization of MKWV/Gomselga as a Probable Arboviral Pathogen with Public Health Relevance: [Overstated].** In the Abstract and Conclusion, the authors assert that *"MKWV is a probable arboviral pathogen with an extensive geographic range and potential public health relevance."* Their own data show that the Russian Gomselga genotype has never been detected in vertebrates, fails to replicate in mammalian Vero or Huh-7.5 cells, and has no demonstrated vector competence or vertical transmission. Extrapolating human public health risk from a single uncharacterized GenBank entry (`PP999500`, described in the Introduction as "human liver" and in the Discussion as "human liver spheroids") and from the Japanese MKW73 strain overreaches the empirical evidence.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Complete genome sequences for all 10 isolates (30 segments) are deposited in GenBank (`PQ204551–PQ204580`), with exact GPS coordinates, sample codes, and prior L-fragment accessions documented in Table 1. Software versions for read trimming, assembly, variant calling, alignment, and phylogenetics are clearly specified.
* **Deficiencies:**
  1. The primer sequences for the multiplexed `primerscheme` tiled amplicon panel (~500 bp products across S, M, and L segments) are neither listed in the text nor provided in a supplementary table, preventing wet-lab reproduction of the genome enrichment protocol.
  2. Raw sequencing reads (Illumina MiSeq 300 bp PE enriched and shotgun metagenomic fastq files) are not deposited in NCBI SRA.
  3. The custom Python/BioPython/Holoviews scripts used for the patristic-distance clustering and chord diagram generation are not deposited in a public repository.
