### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript is an archival text record of a primary experimental virology study demonstrating that the neurotropic Mouse Hepatitis Virus (MHV) strain JHM (both JHM-DL and JHM-DS isolates), unlike the hepatotropic strain A59, productively infects the human hepatocellular carcinoma cell line HuH-7 to high viral titers with extensive syncytium formation. Note that the archival text file contains only the complete Abstract and the 63-citation Reference list (the main text body is not present in the digitized file). Using recombinant chimeric viruses exchanging the spike (S) glycoprotein between strains A59 and JHM, together with antibody blockade against canonical murine biliary glycoprotein (CEACAM1 / Bgp) and anti-JHM spike monoclonal antibodies, the authors demonstrate that cross-species infection and cell-cell fusion in human HuH-7 cells are governed by the JHM spike glycoprotein interacting with a non-canonical surface receptor distinct from murine biliary glycoprotein.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Gain-of-Function Chimeric Spike Genetics and Antibody Blockade Controls:** The experimental design summarized in the abstract employs definitive reverse-genetic and immunological controls: (1) contrasting two JHM isolates (`JHM-DL` and `JHM-DS`) against strain `A59`, (2) demonstrating that a chimeric recombinant virus carrying the JHM spike in an A59 background (`A59/JHM` S-chimera) acquires productive HuH-7 tropism, (3) showing that polyclonal antibody against murine biliary glycoprotein fails to block JHM infection of HuH-7 cells (ruling out cross-reactive canonical receptor usage), and (4) showing that anti-JHM S monoclonal antibodies block both entry and syncytia formation.
- **Archival Text Truncation:** Because the main text body, plaque assay growth curves, and immunofluorescence/Northern blot figures are omitted from the digitized file, exact viral titers (PFU/mL), MOI, replicate counts, and statistical comparisons cannot be audited directly.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **JHM Spike Glycoprotein Mediates Cross-Species Entry and Syncytia Formation in Human HuH-7 Cells:** `[Well Supported]` The combination of chimeric A59/JHM spike recombinants and neutralizing anti-JHM spike monoclonal antibodies directly establishes that the S protein is both necessary and sufficient for the extended host range of MHV-JHM in HuH-7 cells.
- **Usage of an Alternative Non-Biliary Glycoprotein Receptor on HuH-7 Cells:** `[Moderately Supported]` The failure of anti-murine biliary glycoprotein antibodies to inhibit HuH-7 infection combined with the complete refractoriness of HuH-7 cells to MHV-A59 (which uses canonical CEACAM1) supports usage of an alternative entry factor, though the molecular identity of the human surface receptor is not isolated.
