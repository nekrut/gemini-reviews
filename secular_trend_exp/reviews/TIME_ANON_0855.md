### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This comparative evolutionary genomics and structural modeling study reconstructs the deep evolutionary history of the septin gene family across 22 opisthokont genomes (encompassing animals, fungi, and early-diverging unicellular holozoan and holomycotan protists) and maps sequence conservation onto monomer–monomer binding interfaces. Using PSI-BLAST, HMMER, and CDD domain filtering, the authors curate 120 opisthokont septins, trim alignments objectively with Aliscore/Alicut (344 sites), and infer gene phylogenies using three orthogonal approaches: maximum likelihood (RAxML LG+$\Gamma$+I with autoMRE bootstrapping), Bayesian inference (MrBayes, 200M generations, ASDSF $< 0.01$, ESS $> 200$), and species-tree-aware Bayesian gene-tree inference (jPRIME, 50M generations parameterized on an OrthoFinder/r8s ultrametric species tree). Reconciling the gene and species trees with NOTUNG and Mesquite (MK1/MK2 likelihood models) demonstrates that up to four septin paralogs (including Groups 1A and 4, and both coiled-coil-containing and coiled-coil-lacking septins) originated prior to the divergence of animals and fungi ~1.3 billion years ago. Furthermore, homology modeling of 34 Group 1 and Group 2 septins across 17 taxa against five human crystal dimer interfaces (using Modeller, `reduce`, and `probe`), paired with Jensen-Shannon conservation scoring, identifies 49 interface residues (29 in the G-interface and 20 in the NC-interface) that explain the functional conservation of the septin S1–S4 and SUE motifs. The study is methodologically rigorous, transparent about deep phylogenetic uncertainty, and well-grounded in structural biology.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
* **Multi-Method Phylogenetic Concordance and Uncertainty Quantification (Major Strength):** Deep single-gene protein phylogenies (~344 aligned sites across >1 billion years of evolution) are notoriously susceptible to long-branch attraction and low support at basal nodes. Rather than relying on a single tree or overclaiming unresolved nodes, the authors compare RAxML, MrBayes, and species-tree-guided jPRIME side-by-side (Fig. 2C), rearrange poorly supported nodes ($PP < 0.90$) during NOTUNG duplication/loss reconciliation, test MK1 vs. MK2 character transition models via likelihood ratio (Wilks) tests, perform Approximately Unbiased (AU) topology tests in CONSEL, and explicitly display proportional likelihood pie charts under both monophyletic and paraphyletic Group 2 scenarios (Fig. 2A).
* **Integration of Homology Dimer Modeling with All-Atom Contact Probing:** Instead of relying on visual inspection of monomer models, the authors generate 25 independent Modeller dimer structures per taxon across five distinct human crystal templates (PDB `2QA5`, `3TW4`, `2QAG`), optimize side-chain hydrogen-bond networks with `reduce` (`-build -FLIP`), and objectively extract van der Waals and H-bond contacts across chains using `probe` ($0.5\text{ \AA}$ probe radius, requiring conservation across $\ge 10/17$ taxa).
* **Potential Circularity Between jPRIME Species-Tree Prior and Downstream NOTUNG Reconciliation:** Because jPRIME already uses the ultrametric species tree as a generative prior on gene duplication and loss rates during MCMC tree inference, subsequently passing the jPRIME Maximum Clade Credibility tree into NOTUNG to count duplications and losses (and into Mesquite to reconstruct ancestral presence/absence) partially double-counts the species-tree prior, naturally favoring earlier ancestral duplications and lower conflict than the unconstrained RAxML/MrBayes trees (which placed 114/120 and 99/120 sequences into the 7 groups vs. 120/120 for jPRIME). The authors mitigate this by presenting all three methods in Fig. 2C, though this prior sensitivity should be kept in mind.
* **Arbitrary Single-Paralog Selection in Interface Modeling:** For taxa harboring multiple paralogs within Group 1 or Group 2, a single representative protein was chosen arbitrarily for dimer homology modeling (line 55). If paralogs within the same group have undergone subfunctionalization or asymmetric divergence at their G- or NC-interfaces, sampling only one paralog could slightly under-estimate intra-group interface variability.
* **Minor Textual Inconsistency Between Main Text and Figure S5 Legend on PDB IDs:** In the Methods (line 57), `2QA5` is described as the Group 1–1 G-interface dimer and `2QAG` as the human septin hexamer, whereas in the legend to Figure S5 (line 138), these two PDB descriptions are swapped (`2QA5` is called the Sept 2/6/7 hexamer and `2QAG` the Sept2 dimer; in PDB, `2QA5` is Sept2 and `2QAG` is the Sept2/6/7 hexamer).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
* **Strengths:** Thoughtful taxon sampling of early-diverging holozoans (*Monosiga*, *Salpingoeca*, *Capsaspora*, *Sphaeroforma*) and holomycotans (*Fonticula*, *Rozella*, *Gonapodya*, *Catenaria*), rigorous MCMC convergence diagnostics (RWTY and Tracer ESS $> 200$), and compelling structural synthesis linking the previously mysterious S1–S4 and SUE sequence motifs to physical G- and NC-interface contacts.
* **Claim 1 (Septin gene duplication began in the opisthokont ancestor prior to the animal–fungal divergence, with Groups 1A and 4 strongly supported as ancestral):** `[Well Supported]` — Supported across RAxML, MrBayes, jPRIME, NOTUNG, and Mesquite proportional likelihoods ($>95\%$).
* **Claim 2 (Whether Groups 1B, 2, and 5 were present in the opisthokont ancestor vs. gained/lost later or evolved convergently remains equivocal):** `[Well Supported]` — Exemplary epistemic restraint; the authors explicitly highlight where low bootstrap/posterior support and patchy taxon distributions preclude definitive ancestral reconstruction.
* **Claim 3 (Conserved septin motifs S1–S4, PB $\alpha 0$, and SUE map directly to the 29 G-interface and 20 NC-interface contact residues, while Group 5 septins lack interface conservation):** `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by multi-template homology dimer contact analysis and Jensen-Shannon divergence profiles, aligning with experimental evidence that Group 5 septins do not incorporate into core heterooligomeric rods.
