### 1. Executive Summary
This study performs a two-part investigation of common intronic polymorphisms in the serine/threonine kinase 39 (*STK39*) gene (rs6749447, rs3754777, rs35929607) and a 9p21.3 SNP (rs4977950) previously implicated in blood pressure (BP) regulation by an Old Order Amish GWAS. First, the authors genotype 1,372 individuals across 247 British Caucasian families ascertained via hypertensive probands and test for association with clinic, daytime ambulatory, and night-time ambulatory systolic and diastolic BP using family-based variance-components association analysis in MERLIN. Second, taking advantage of lower linkage disequilibrium (LD) and higher heterozygosity for a 3'-UTR transcribed marker (rs1061471, non-polymorphic in CEU) in a cohort of 309 South African blood donors, they quantify in vivo allelic expression imbalance (AEI) in peripheral blood leukocyte cDNA across 33 informative heterozygotes using four-replicate Sequenom MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry normalized to 19 genomic DNA controls. They find that while all three *STK39* SNPs are significantly associated with cis-acting allelic expression differences in vivo—led by rs6749447 ($P = 9.9 \times 10^{-4}$, 13% overexpression of the G allele, which accounts for the signals at rs3754777 and rs35929607 upon conditional adjustment)—none of the SNPs are associated with clinic or ambulatory BP in the British family cohort (all $P > 0.05$, with 95% confidence intervals ruling out effects $>2.9\text{ mmHg}$).

This is a methodologically rigorous, well-powered negative replication study paired with an elegant trans-ethnic in vivo AEI experiment that cleanly separates cis-regulatory eQTL activity from clinical phenotype association.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Phenotypic Precision & Family-Based Association Modeling:** The use of 24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring ($\ge 20$ daytime readings, individualized sleep/wake logs, simultaneous auscultatory calibration within 5 mmHg) in 1,138 clinic / 1,134 daytime / 903 night-time phenotyped participants substantially reduces white-coat and measurement variance. Notably, Table 1 demonstrates that covariate-adjusted narrow-sense heritability ($h^2$) increases from 11.2–12.2% for clinic BP to 24.3% (night systolic) and 34.6% (night diastolic), confirming enhanced genetic signal-to-noise. Family structure and relatedness are properly modeled in MERLIN v1.1.2, and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and Mendelian inheritance are verified in PEDSTATS (>99% genotyping completeness, all HW $P \ge 0.32$ in Table 2).
- **Confidence Interval & Upper-Bound Effect Size Reporting (Table 3):** Rather than relying solely on $P > 0.05$ to claim no association, the authors report point estimates and 95% confidence intervals in mmHg for every SNP-by-phenotype combination alongside the upper bound of variance explained (0.4–1.7%), directly demonstrating statistical power to exclude the ~3 mmHg/allele effect originally reported in the Amish discovery cohort.
- **Allelic Expression Imbalance (AEI) Design & Conditional Dissection:**
  - Because rs1061471 is monomorphic in CEU Caucasians, screening 309 South African donors to identify 35 heterozygotes (33 passing QC) is a clever trans-ethnic design that both enables AEI measurement and leverages shorter LD blocks ($D' = 0.54$ between rs6749447 and rs1061471) to separate the effects of the three intronic SNPs.
  - Technical precision is thoroughly documented: 4 cDNA replicates per sample normalized to 19 gDNA controls (4 replicates each), with identical mean standard error of log-ratios in gDNA (0.0146) and cDNA (0.0149).
  - Using the likelihood-based phase-modeling approach of Teare et al. (Table 4) alongside non-parametric Mann-Whitney U comparisons of homozygotes vs. heterozygotes (Figure 1, $P = 0.005$), the authors show that adjusting for rs6749447 ($P = 0.001$) abolishes the marginal AEI associations of rs35929607 ($P = 0.02 \rightarrow 0.39$) and rs3754777 ($P = 0.006 \rightarrow 0.71$).
- **Minor Methodological Critique — Admixed Cohort Substructure in AEI:** The 309 South African donors comprise mixed self-reported ancestry (200 Cape Mixed Ancestry, 67 black African, 30 Indian, 10 white, 2 other). While within-individual AEI ratios are inherently immune to trans-acting confounders and population stratification, the Teare et al. model estimates phase/LD parameter $D'$ between the untyped/typed cis-regulatory SNP and the transcribed reporter SNP across the 33 heterozygotes. If haplotype phase between rs6749447 and rs1061471 differs across the ancestral subgroups within the 33 heterozygotes, pooling them could slightly attenuate $D'$ estimates, though this would be conservative and would not create a false-positive elevation in heterozygote variance (Figure 1 column 3).
- **Medication Adjustment Method:** Antihypertensive drug effects (diuretics and $\beta$-blockers in 456 treated participants) were estimated from the cohort data by linear regression rather than using fixed external constants or Tobit censored normal regression; while previously validated in this cohort, regressing on treatment indicator in an ascertained sample can conflate treatment indication severity with drug effect.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** "No significant association was seen between the SNPs tested and systolic or diastolic BP in clinic or ambulatory measurements (all p > 0.05)... our data exclude an effect as large as the 3 mmHg increase in systolic BP per copy of the risk allele reported by Wang and colleagues." `[Well Supported]` — Backed by complete 95% CIs across 6 clinic and ambulatory phenotypes in 902–1,138 individuals.
- **Claim 2:** "The tested SNPs were all associated with allelic expression differences in peripheral blood cells (p < 0.05), with the most significant association for the intronic SNP rs6749447 (P = 9.9 × 10^-4). In individuals who were heterozygous for this SNP, on average the G allele showed 13% overexpression compared to the T allele." `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by replicate MALDI-TOF AEI measurements, gDNA equimolar controls, and conditional haplotype regression.
- **Claim 3:** "Correlation between *STK39* SNPs and expression of this gene does not necessarily mean that these SNPs will influence BP... A limitation of the present study is that expression was only tested in white blood cells, rather than in a tissue of potentially greater relevance to BP regulation." `[Well Supported]` — The authors maintain exemplary epistemic restraint, explicitly noting tissue-specificity limitations (peripheral blood vs. renal distal convoluted tubule/thick ascending limb where SPAK regulates NCC/NKCC2) and Winner's Curse / founder-effect LD differences.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Assay Transparency:** dbSNP rsIDs, chromosome locations, allele frequencies across cohorts, HWE $P$-values, effect sizes with 95% CIs, LD metrics ($D'$ and $r^2$ in Figure 2), RNA extraction/DNase/cDNA synthesis protocols, and QC exclusion criteria (2/35 samples excluded for high replicate SE) are all transparently reported, with primer sequences and minor allele frequencies of the 5 rare transcribed SNPs provided in Additional File 1.
