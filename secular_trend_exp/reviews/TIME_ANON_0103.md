### 1. Executive Summary
This primary experimental study investigates whether mammalian Toll-like receptors (TLR1, TLR2, TLR4) act as transmembrane signal transducers for bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and its bioactive moiety lipid A in human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293 cells. Using transient and stable transfections of Flag-tagged TLRs and CD14 alongside an ELAM-1 (E-selectin) promoter luciferase reporter and electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSA), the authors report that overexpression of TLR2—but not TLR1, TLR4, or CD14 alone—confers dose-dependent LPS- and lipid A-induced NF-κB activation. They further show that C-terminal truncations of the TLR2 cytoplasmic domain (including a 15-amino-acid deletion removing the conserved Toll/IL-1R homology block) abrogate signaling, that serum or purified recombinant soluble CD14 (sCD14) and coexpressed membrane CD14 shift LPS sensitivity by ~3 orders of magnitude, and that dominant-negative mutants of MyD88, TRAF6, NIK, IKK-α, and IKK-β (but not TRAF2) block TLR2-mediated LPS signaling.

While the molecular dissection of the intracellular Toll/IL-1R signaling cascade (MyD88 $\rightarrow$ TRAF6 $\rightarrow$ NIK $\rightarrow$ IKK) and the cytoplasmic truncation series are cleanly executed, the central conclusion that TLR2 is the primary signaling receptor for LPS suffers from two major technical/assay confounds: (1) TLR4 overexpression in 293 cells caused high constitutive baseline NF-κB activation (in the absence of its essential cofactor MD-2), masking ligand inducibility and leading to a false-negative dismissal of TLR4; and (2) the study relies on commercial phenol-extracted LPS preparations (up to 10 µg/ml) in an overexpression system without rigorous biochemical re-purification (e.g., phenol-water re-extraction to remove trace bacterial lipopeptides/lipoproteins, as ion-exchange or gel filtration does not dissociate lipopeptides bound to LPS micelles).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Replication & Statistical Reporting Deficiencies:** Figure legends and Methods state that "All bar diagrams are shown as mean ± SEM for one representative experiment in which each transfection was performed in duplicate. Each experiment was repeated at least twice in all cases." Calculating an SEM from $n = 2$ technical duplicates within a single representative transfection is statistically uninformative, and no formal hypothesis tests ($t$-tests, ANOVA, or dose-response regression models) or pooled biological replicate analyses are reported anywhere in the manuscript.
- **TLR4 Constitutive Overexpression Artifact (Figure 1A):** In Figure 1A, transient transfection of 1 µg of TLR4 plasmid induces constitutive reporter activity (~30-fold over vector), and addition of 10 µg/ml LPS yields no further increase. Because TLR4 is already saturated or misfolded/aggregated at high plasmid dose without cofactors, concluding that TLR4 does not transduce LPS signals is a classic assay ceiling/cofactor artifact. No plasmid dose titration of TLR4 (e.g., 1–50 ng, as done for TLR2 at 50 ng in Figure 5A) was performed to reduce constitutive background.
- **Commercial LPS Purity & Concentration Regime:** Most experiments (Figures 1A, 1C, 3, 6) stimulate cells with 1–10 µg/ml of commercial phenol-extracted *E. coli* O111:B4 LPS. Although the Methods state that "Further purification of LPS by ion exchange chromatography or gel filtration (Sigma Chemical Co.) had no effect on the biological activity," neither method removes hydrophobic bacterial lipoproteins co-purifying with LPS aggregates, which potently activate TLR2 at picogram-to-nanogram concentrations when total LPS is applied at microgram doses. Furthermore, the synthetic lipid A control was not tested; instead, biologically extracted lipid A from *E. coli* K12 D31m4 was used (Figure 2), which is subject to the same trace contaminant profile.
- **Missing Expression Controls for Truncation and Dominant-Negative Mutants:** While Figure 1B commendably verifies comparable protein expression of wild-type Flag-TLR1, TLR2, TLR4, and CD14 by immunoprecipitation and immunoblotting, Figure 3 (TLR2 cytoplasmic deletion mutants $\Delta 609\text{--}784$, $\Delta 681\text{--}784$, $\Delta 747\text{--}784$, $\Delta 770\text{--}784$) and Figure 6 (dominant-negative IKK-α, IKK-β, NIK, TRAF2, TRAF6, MyD88) do not include immunoblots or FACS surface-staining controls to verify that the non-signaling TLR2 truncation mutants are stably expressed at the plasma membrane.
- **Unpublished Data Citation:** In the Discussion, the authors rest a key mechanistic distinction on "binding studies indicate that TLR2 does not bind LPS with high affinity (our unpublished observation)" without presenting the binding data, Kd estimates, or assay methodology.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** "Overexpression of TLR2, but not TLR1, TLR4, or CD14 conferred LPS inducibility of NF-κB activation in mammalian 293 cells... These findings suggest that TLR2 may be a signaling component of a cellular receptor for LPS." `[Fragile]` — While the gain-of-function response in 293-TLR2 cells to commercial LPS and D31m4 lipid A is reproducible within the assay, the exclusion of TLR4 is confounded by high constitutive baseline signaling at 1 µg plasmid input, and the activation of TLR2 is vulnerable to trace endotoxin-associated lipoprotein contaminants in commercial bacterial extracts. Furthermore, no loss-of-function experiments (e.g., blocking antibodies or genetic deficiency in primary macrophages or monocytic lines) are presented to show that endogenous TLR2 is required for LPS signaling.
- **Claim 2:** "Mutational analysis demonstrated that this LPS response requires the intracellular domain of TLR2... deletion of the COOH-terminal 15 amino acids of the cytoplasmic domain of TLR2 was sufficient to abrogate LPS-induced NF-κB reporter gene activation." `[Well Supported]` — The C-terminal truncation series (Figure 3) cleanly maps the requirement of the conserved Toll/IL-1R homology box (residues 770–784), though surface expression controls for the truncated constructs would further strengthen this result.
- **Claim 3:** "Coexpression of CD14 synergistically enhanced LPS signal transmission through TLR2. In addition, purified recombinant sCD14 could substitute for serum to support LPS-induced TLR2 activation." `[Well Supported]` — Figures 4 and 5A–B clearly demonstrate the 3-log serum shift and dose-dependent reconstitution of low-dose (1–100 ng/ml) responsiveness by membrane CD14 or affinity-purified recombinant sCD14.
- **Claim 4:** "LPS stimulation of TLR2 initiated an interleukin 1 receptor–like NF-κB signaling cascade." `[Well Supported]` — The epistatic dissection in Figure 6 using dominant-negative MyD88, TRAF6, TRAF2, NIK, and IKK-α/β with parallel IL-1β and TNF-α controls in 293–IL-1RI cells provides strong internal pathway specificity (TRAF6- and MyD88-dependent, TRAF2-independent).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Reagent & Primer Reporting:** Cell lines, commercial antibodies (M2 anti-Flag, MEM18 anti-CD14), custom CD14 immunogen peptide sequence (`VEVEIHAGGLNLEPFLKRVDADADPRQYADTVK`), TLR2 RT-PCR primer sequences (`5'-GCCAAAGTCTTGATTGATTGG` and `5'-TTGAAGTTCTCCAGCTCCTG`), and exact amino acid boundaries of the TLR2 truncation mutants (1–608, 1–680, 1–746, 1–769) are clearly documented.
- **Missing Construct & Quantitative Details:** Exact boundaries/point mutations for the dominant-negative signaling constructs (IKK-α, IKK-β, NIK, TRAF2, TRAF6, MyD88) and the PCR cloning primers for full-length TLR1/2/4 are not listed in the Methods (only referenced to prior literature). Raw luciferase luminescence units and β-galactosidase normalization values are not provided in tabular form.
