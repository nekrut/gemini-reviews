### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This evolutionary genetics study dissects the relative contributions of pleiotropy versus tight genetic linkage within the classic $16\text{-kb}$ freshwater adaptive haplotype at the *Ectodysplasin* (`Eda`) locus on chromosome IV of threespine stickleback (*Gasterosteus aculeatus*). Combining two complementary experimental designs—(1) controlled laboratory F1/F2 crosses of rare wild-caught Puget Sound marine sticklebacks heterozygous for full-length (`full-L`), truncated (`short-L`), or `NAKA SNP`-only freshwater haplotypes ($n = 497\text{--}578$ phenotyped offspring across 11 `Stn382` crosses plus 4 `NAKA SNP` crosses), and (2) high-resolution fine-scale association mapping in a naturally polymorphic, interbreeding freshwater population from Lake Washington ($n = 885$ genotyped fish, of which $198\ [22\%]$ carried historical intra-haplotype recombination events across 17 SNP/indel markers)—the authors test 91 morphological, meristic, branchial, and lateral-line phenotypes. They demonstrate that: (1) the previously proposed causal 3'-enhancer mutation (`NAKA SNP`) is neither necessary nor sufficient in the homozygous `LL` state to cause lateral plate reduction or neuromast changes (`Fig. 5`, and exhibits the lowest LOD score across the haplotype in Lake Washington, `Fig. 6`); (2) a $1.4\text{-kb}$ interval within the first intron of `Eda` (marked by indels `LP3621` and `Cnv770`, containing $\sim 19$ polymorphisms) exerts massive pleiotropic effects on three traits—left lateral plate count ($\text{LOD} = 344,\ \text{PVE} = 93.6\%$ in crosses; $\text{LOD} = 218,\ \text{PVE} = 76.4\%$ in Lake Washington), dorsal-ventral (`D-V`) posterior lateral line neuromast patterning ($\text{PVE} = 93.3\%$ and $67.9\%$), and posterior trunk (`Mp`) neuromasts per segment ($\text{PVE} = 51.0\%$ and $26.5\%$); (3) additional linked mutations near the 3' end of the $16\text{-kb}$ haplotype (`SNPs12/13`) act as small-effect modifiers of plate count and neuromast patterning (`Figs. 3C, 7`) and affect gill raker length in Puget Sound crosses ($\text{PVE} = 3.7\%$) and head/jaw morphometric landmarks (`Y10`, `X16`, `Y18`, $\text{PVE} = 2.5\text{--}3.3\%$) in Lake Washington; and (4) most other chromosome IV QTL traits (e.g., pelvic/dorsal spines, pharyngeal teeth) do not map to the $16\text{-kb}$ `Eda` haplotype despite $\ge 90\%$ statistical power to detect $\text{PVE} \ge 3.4\%$.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
- **Exceptional Statistical Rigor, Covariate Control, Permutation Stratification, and Power Calculation:**
  - The linear modeling framework (`LOD` and `PVE` derived from residual sums of squares of nested covariate models) explicitly accounts for sex, standard length, family, collection month, holding tank, days in captivity, neuromast staining method (`DASPEI` vs. newborn alkaline phosphatase `NBT/BCIP` protocol), and subjective staining quality, including biologically appropriate interaction terms (`sex * month`, `size * month`, `size * family`).
  - Significance thresholds are established via 5,000 stratified permutations (permuting within families of matched haplotype length in Puget Sound and within collection months in Lake Washington) followed by strict Bonferroni correction across all tested traits ($\alpha = 0.0029$ for 17 traits in Puget Sound; $\alpha = 0.0005$ for 91 traits in Lake Washington).
  - Formal post-hoc power calculations (`qtlDesign::detectable()` at $90\%$ power) are reported for every non-significant trait (`Table S2`), transparently distinguishing well-powered true negatives (e.g., spine and girdle lengths, detectable $\text{PVE} \approx 3.3\text{--}3.4\%$) from lower-powered branchial skeleton sub-samples ($n = 98$ and $n = 76$, detectable $\text{PVE} \approx 18\%$).
- **Conservative Handling of Missing Genotypes:** When fine-mapping Lake Washington recombinants, markers `SNP7` ($386/884$ genotyped) and `SNP8` ($706/884$ genotyped) had substantial missingness. Rather than relying solely on flanking-marker haplotype imputation, the authors repeated the entire association analysis on a strictly trimmed dataset omitting imputed genotypes and `SNP7`/`SNP8`, verifying that all primary conclusions remained unchanged.
- **Minor Limitation in Testing Epistasis vs. Dominance at `SNPs12/13`:** In `Figure 7`, the modifier effect of `SNPs12/13` on plate count ($t_{12} = 4.4,\ P = 0.00085$) and `D-V` neuromast patterning ($t_{12} = 2.7,\ P = 0.03$) in Lake Washington is tested within `Cnv770` heterozygotes (`CL`) across a relatively small recombinant sub-sample ($\text{df} = 12$, whereas in Puget Sound `Fig. 3C` the modifier effect was observed within `Stn382` `LL` homozygotes, $t_{151} = -6.54,\ P = 4.4\times 10^{-10}$). This shift in the background genotype class where the `SNPs12/13` modifier is most visible (`LL` in marine crosses vs. `CL` in Lake Washington) is noted but warrants cautious interpretation regarding dominance/epistasis architecture.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
- **Definitive Refutation of the `NAKA SNP` Sufficiency Hypothesis `[Well Supported]`:** Prior high-profile work proposed that a single 3' regulatory SNP (`NAKA SNP`) caused freshwater armor plate reduction. By isolating wild-caught Puget Sound marine carriers heterozygous *only* at the `NAKA SNP`, generating homozygous `LL` F1 offspring (`Fig. 5`), and cross-checking against wild recombinants in Lake Washington (`Fig. 6`) and outgroup percomorph genomes (`Fig. S5`), the authors decisively prove that the `NAKA SNP` alone has zero effect on plate number or lateral line traits.
- **Fine-Mapping Major Pleiotropic Effects to a $1.4\text{-kb}$ Intron 1 Region (`LP3621–Cnv770`) `[Well Supported]`:** The virtually identical LOD profiles across the 17 markers for lateral plate count, `D-V` neuromast patterning, and `Mp` neuromast density (`Fig. 6`), narrowing the causal interval in low-plated recombinants to $1,401\text{ bp}$ in `Eda` intron 1 (`gasAcu1` chrIV: $12,802,847\text{--}12,808,303$), provides a textbook demonstration of fine-scale resolution in a natural recombinant population.
- **Resolution of the "Pleiotropy vs. Linkage" Debate in Adaptive Hotspots `[Well Supported]`:** The authors show that the `Eda` adaptive hotspot is not an "either/or" dichotomy: it combines a major pleiotropic regulatory module in intron 1 controlling three developmentally coupled ectodermal traits with linked 3' modifier alleles (`SNPs12/13`) that fine-tune plate count and cranial/gill-raker morphology, explaining why selection maintains the full $16\text{-kb}$ haplotype despite active local recombination ($22\%$ recombinants in Lake Washington and abundant `short-L` haplotypes in marine standing variation).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** All phenotypic datasets, genotype matrices (both imputed and trimmed), and R analysis scripts are publicly archived on Dryad (`https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.05qfttf0j`). Furthermore, the authors provide a remarkably detailed step-by-step wet-lab protocol for their newly adapted bulk alkaline phosphatase (`NBT/BCIP`) + Alizarin red double-staining method (`Methods`, line 75), reference sequence accessions (`AC144489.2`, `AY897589.1`, `AANH00000000.1`), and full primer/assay tables (`Table S1`).
- **Weaknesses:** None. This manuscript is a model of open, reproducible evolutionary genetics.
