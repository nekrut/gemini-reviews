### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the regulatory role of the AAA+ ATPase chaperone/unfoldase subunit `ClpX` in the heat-shock activation of the `CtsR` (class three stress gene repressor) regulon in prototrophic *Bacillus subtilis* `BSB1`. Using newly constructed chromosomal in-frame deletion mutants (`ΔclpX`, `ΔclpC`, `ΔclpP`, `ΔctsR`, `ΔclpE`) and an anhydrotetracycline (`aTc`)-inducible conditional `clpX` strain (`BCSH01` `P_TRE-clpX` and `BCSH04` `P_TRE-clpX ΔctsR`), the authors monitor `clpE` mRNA levels (chosen as a pure $\sigma^{\text{A}}$-dependent readout with five `CtsR` operator sites) by quantitative near-infrared (NIR) Northern blotting ($n = 3$ biological replicates) and quantify `ClpE`, `ClpC`, `ClpP`, `CtsR`, and `ClpX` protein dynamics at $37^\circ\text{C}$ and $10\text{ / }30\text{ min}$ after a $50^\circ\text{C}$ heat shock via data-independent acquisition mass spectrometry (`DIA-MS`, $n = 3\text{–}4$ biological replicates). They demonstrate that deleting or depleting `ClpX` reduces heat-induced `clpE` transcript accumulation ~8-fold and `ClpE` protein accumulation ~9.6-fold (an effect not seen in `ΔclpC` or `ΔclpP`), and that pre-inducing `ClpX` to wild-type levels (`20 ng/ml aTc` 30 min prior to heat shock) fully restores `clpE` mRNA and protein induction (within 1.2-fold of wild-type). They also resolve an apparent discrepancy with a 20-year-old study (`BEK90`) by resequencing the historical `clpX` mutant and showing it retained the N-terminal zinc-finger domain in an auxotrophic (`trpC2 lys-3`) background. While the wet-lab experiments and proteomics are carefully executed, the central mechanistic claim—that `ClpX` acts directly as a chaperone on `CtsR` to catalyze its heat-induced conformational dissociation from operator DNA (`Figure 6`)—lacks direct biochemical proof of `ClpX–CtsR` interaction or an epistatic `ΔctsR ΔclpX` (uninduced `BCSH04` `-aTc`) control to exclude `Spx`-mediated transcriptional effects.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Missing Uninduced `-aTc` Control for the Double Mutant `BCSH04` (`con_clpX ΔctsR`):**
  - If the 8-fold reduction in `clpE` transcription in `ΔclpX` at $50^\circ\text{C}$ is caused strictly by a failure of `CtsR` to dissociate from its heptanucleotide DNA operators (as claimed throughout the paper and illustrated in `Figure 6`), then deleting `ctsR` (`ΔctsR`) should make `clpE` transcription completely insensitive to the presence or absence of `ClpX` (i.e., epistatic).
  - The authors constructed the exact strain needed to test this—`BCSH04` (`ΔctsR` + conditional `P_TRE-clpX`, `Table 1`)—and included `BCSH04 + aTc` (`ClpX` induced) in `Figures 4B` and `5`. However, they **omitted `BCSH04 - aTc` (`ClpX` uninduced in the `ΔctsR` background)** from both the Northern blot (`Figure 4B`) and the `DIA-MS` proteome (`Figure 5`)!
  - Why is `BCSH04 - aTc` critical? As the authors themselves note in the Introduction (line 23), `clpE` transcription is strongly positively activated by the global thiol/heat-stress transcriptional regulator `Spx` (`Rochat et al.`), and `Spx` turnover and activity are directly governed by `ClpXP`! Furthermore, `ClpX` depletion causes global proteostasis defects at $50^\circ\text{C}$. Without showing that `BCSH04 - aTc` (`ΔctsR`, no `ClpX`) achieves the same high `clpE` mRNA level as `BCSH04 + aTc` (`ΔctsR`, with `ClpX`), the authors cannot rule out that `ClpX` is required for `Spx`-mediated transcriptional activation or $\sigma^{\text{A}}$ RNA polymerase initiation at `clpE` rather than `CtsR` operator dissociation.
- **Absence of Direct Biochemical or *In Vivo* DNA-Binding Evidence for `CtsR` Retention:**
  - No ChIP-qPCR showing prolonged `CtsR` occupancy at the `clpE` promoter at $50^\circ\text{C}$ in `ΔclpX`, and no *in vitro* electrophoretic mobility shift assay (`EMSA`) or fluorescence anisotropy testing whether purified `ClpX` (+ATP) accelerates thermal release of `CtsR` from operator DNA, was performed.
- **Forensic Resequencing of Historical `BEK90` Strain and Conditional `aTc` Titration:**
  - Two notable methodological strengths are (1) resequencing the historical `BEK90` `clpX` insertional mutant (`Supplementary Figures 2–3`) to prove it retained the native promoter, start codon, and N-terminal `Cys4` zinc-finger dimerization/substrate-binding domain, and (2) carefully titrating anhydrotetracycline (`20 ng/ml aTc` added 30 min prior to `OD_500 = 0.4`, `Supplementary Figure 1`) so that induced `ClpX` protein levels matched endogenous wild-type levels without overexpression artifacts.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **`[Well Supported]` Full transcriptional and proteomic induction of `clpE` upon $50^\circ\text{C}$ heat shock requires both heat and `ClpX`, but not `ClpC` or `ClpP`:** Demonstrated across independent in-frame deletion strains (`Figures 2–3`) and reversibly recapitulated in the conditional `P_TRE-clpX` strain (`Figures 4B–5`).
- **`[Well Supported]` Constitutive derepression in `ΔctsR` leads to massive `ClpC` and `ClpP` accumulation that rapidly degrades `ClpE` protein despite high `clpE` mRNA:** Comparing `Figure 2`/`4B` (`clpE` mRNA at 160–200% in `ΔctsR`) with `Figure 3A`/`5A` (`ClpE` protein near baseline while `ClpC` and `ClpP` are strongly elevated in `Figure 3B`/`5B`) cleanly demonstrates post-translational proteolysis of `ClpE` by `ClpCP`.
- **`[Fragile]` `ClpX` acts directly as a chaperone on DNA-bound `CtsR` to assist the heat-induced conformational switch in its glycine-rich loop and trigger operator dissociation (`Figure 6`):** While this is a plausible hypothesis, it remains unproven without (i) testing `BCSH04` (`con_clpX ΔctsR`) in the absence of `aTc` to prove `CtsR` epistasis and (ii) biochemical `CtsR–DNA` dissociation or `CtsR` ChIP assays.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Reproducibility is outstanding. Raw and processed `DIA-MS` proteomics datasets for both the Orbitrap Exploris 480 (in-frame mutants) and Bruker timsTOF HT (conditional mutants) experiments are deposited in ProteomeXchange/MassIVE under accessions `MSV000099009` and `MSV000099010`. All strain genotypes (`Table 1`), primer sequences (`Supplementary Table 1`), LC-MS/MS and `Spectronaut v16/v20` parameters (`Supplementary Tables 2–5`), PCA outlier exclusions (`Supplementary Table 6`), and `ROPECA` peptide-level statistical outputs (`Supplementary Table 7`) are provided.
