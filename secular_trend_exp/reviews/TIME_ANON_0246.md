### 1. Executive Summary
This candidate-locus association study investigates genetic risk factors for comorbid Type 1 Diabetes and Celiac Disease ("double autoimmunity") by genotyping 543 T1D+CeD cases on the Illumina ImmunoChip and comparing them against two single-disease control cohorts: 2,472 T1D-only controls ($n=483$ T1D+CeD cases retained after QC/matching) and 2,223 CeD-only controls ($n=432$ T1D+CeD cases retained after QC/matching). The authors test 66 established genome-wide significant non-HLA SNPs (37 T1D, 29 CeD) and 5 SNPs tagging HLA-DQ2.5, DQ2.2, and DQ8 using PLINK 5-dimensional IBD cluster matching and Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) tests. They report that *CTLA4* (`rs3087243`) and *IL2RA* (`rs61839660`) are associated with double autoimmunity vs. CeD-only, *LPP* (`rs1464510`) and *RGS1* (`rs2816316`) are associated with double autoimmunity vs. T1D-only, and HLA `DQ2.5/DQ8` heterozygosity confers the highest risk relative to CeD-only. While the comorbid cohort is valuable and hard to assemble, the manuscript contains severe internal mathematical contradictions in Table 4 (where odds ratios of $5.086$, $0.255$, and $0.467$ with tight 95% confidence intervals far from $1.0$ are paired with non-significant p-values of $0.163$, $0.422$, and $0.500$, and where the Abstract's headline `DQ2.5/DQ8` $\text{OR} = 5.22, P = 2.25 \times 10^{-29}$ contradicts Table 4's $\text{OR} = 1.47, P = 3.31 \times 10^{-10}$), an allele-label flip for *CTLA4* in Table 2, and severe country-of-origin confounding between US comorbid cases and Dutch/UK CeD-only controls.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Severe Mathematical Contradictions in Table 4 and Abstract (`HLA-DQ` Analysis):**
  1. **Headline Abstract vs. Table 4 Mismatch:** The Abstract and Results state that `DQ2.5/DQ8` heterozygosity provides the highest risk for double autoimmunity compared with CeD-only with **$\text{OR} = 5.22, P = 2.25 \times 10^{-29}$**. However, in Table 4 (`T1D+CeD vs CeD`), the `DQ2.5/DQ8` row (frequency $0.350$ in T1D+CeD vs. $0.067$ in CeD) reports **$\text{OR} = 1.47\ (95\%\ \text{CI}: 1.41, 1.53), P = 3.31 \times 10^{-10}$**! Note that the ratio of frequencies is $0.350 / 0.067 = 5.224$ (a relative risk / proportion ratio rather than an odds ratio, as the crude odds ratio is $(0.350/0.650)/(0.067/0.933) = 7.50$), and the Table 4 values ($\text{OR} = 1.47, P = 3.31 \times 10^{-10}$) are completely different from the Abstract and text.
  2. **Impossible CI / P-Value Pairs in Table 4:** Under `T1D+CeD vs CeD` in Table 4 (Haplotype section):
     - `DQ8` (frequency $0.273$ vs. $0.056$) is reported as $\text{OR} = 5.086$ with 95% CI $(3.883, 6.662)$, yet **$P = 0.163$**! A 95% CI of $(3.883, 6.662)$ corresponds to $\ln(\text{OR}) = 1.6265, \text{SE} = 0.1378, Z = 11.80$, which yields $P \approx 4 \times 10^{-32}$, not $0.163$.
     - `DQ2.2` (frequency $0.044$ vs. $0.107$) is reported as $\text{OR} = 0.255$ with 95% CI $(0.173, 0.374)$, yet **$P = 0.422$** (a CI excluding $1.0$ by $Z = -7.0$, $P \approx 3 \times 10^{-12}$).
     - `Other` (frequency $0.176$ vs. $0.214$) is reported as $\text{OR} = 0.467$ with 95% CI $(0.366, 0.595)$, yet **$P = 0.500$** ($Z = -6.1, P \approx 10^{-9}$).
     This indicates a major column shift or software parsing error when compiling the Table 4 p-values and odds ratios.
- **Risk Allele Mislabelling in Table 2 vs. Table 3 (`rs3087243` *CTLA4*):** In Table 2 (`T1D+CeD vs T1D`), `rs3087243` (*CTLA4*) lists Minor/Major allele as `A/G` and "Risk allele" as `A`, yet reports "Risk allele frequency" = $0.58$ and $\text{OR} = 1.41$ ($P = 1.29 \times 10^{-5}$). In Table 3 (`T1D+CeD vs CeD`), the same SNP lists "Risk allele" as `G` with "Risk allele frequency" = $0.58$. Because `A` is the minor allele, $0.58$ is the frequency of the major allele `G`, meaning the risk allele column in Table 2 is inverted for `rs3087243` (and similarly `rs689` *INS* lists `A/T` with risk allele `T` at frequency $0.79$).
- **Geographic / Country-of-Origin Confounding (Table 1):** Table 1 shows that $460/543$ ($84.7\%$) of the T1D+CeD cases were recruited in the United States (plus 49 Australians and 34 Italians; $0$ from the UK or Netherlands), whereas $100\%$ of the CeD-only controls ($1,134$ Dutch + $1,089$ UK = $2,223$) are from the Netherlands and the UK ($0$ from the US). Comparing US cases against exclusively Dutch and British controls introduces European sub-continental population stratification (e.g., greater Southern/Eastern European admixture in US white cohorts vs. Dutch/UK cohorts). While the authors used 5-dimensional IBD cluster matching in CMH tests, they do not report the genomic inflation factor ($\lambda_{\text{GC}}$) across the ImmunoChip background SNPs for the US-vs-Dutch/UK comparison.
- **Multiple Testing Correction:** The authors set an arbitrary nominal threshold of $P < 0.005$ as "suggestive" rather than applying a formal Bonferroni ($0.05 / 66 = 7.6 \times 10^{-4}$) or Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across the 66 non-HLA SNPs tested in two comparisons (132 tests). Only *CTLA4* (`rs3087243`, $P = 1.29 \times 10^{-5}$) in T1D+CeD vs. T1D and *IL2RA* (`rs61839660`, $P = 1.07 \times 10^{-4}$) in T1D+CeD vs. CeD pass strict Bonferroni correction, whereas *RGS1* ($P = 1.09 \times 10^{-3}$), *LPP* ($P = 3.32 \times 10^{-3}$), and *PTPN2* ($P = 3.64 \times 10^{-3}$) are only nominally suggestive.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Enrichment of `HLA-DQ2.5/DQ8` Heterozygosity in T1D+CeD Double Autoimmunity vs. CeD-Only:** `[Well Supported]` Biologically and statistically, the massive enrichment of `DQ2.5/DQ8` compound heterozygotes in comorbid patients ($35.0\%$ vs. $6.7\%$ in CeD-only and $9.2\%$ in T1D-only) is robust and reflects the synergistic requirement for both the CeD-predisposing DQ2.5 heterodimer and the T1D-predisposing DQ8 heterodimer, though Table 4's corrupted OR/p-value entries must be corrected.
- **Role of Non-HLA Loci (*LPP*, *RGS1*, *CTLA4*, *IL2RA*) as Clinical Stratification Biomarkers:** `[Moderately Supported]` The direction of effects is biologically consistent with adding CeD risk alleles (*LPP*, *RGS1*) onto a T1D background and T1D risk alleles (*IL2RA*, *INS*, *PTPN22*) onto a CeD background, but effect sizes are modest ($\text{OR} \approx 1.24\text{--}1.43$) and several do not survive strict multiple-testing correction.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Full summary results for all 66 non-HLA SNPs across both comparisons are provided in Supplementary Tables S1 and S2, and QC software (`KING`, `PLINK`, `EIGENSTRAT`) and thresholds (call rate $>98\%$, MAF $>1\%$, HWE $P \ge 10^{-5}$) are clearly specified.
- **Limitations:** Individual-level or summary ImmunoChip genotype data for the 543 double-autoimmunity cases are not deposited in dbGaP or EGA, and the exact 5 tagging SNPs used to impute the HLA-DQ haplotypes are referenced to external literature (`[9]`) rather than listed with rsIDs in the Methods.
