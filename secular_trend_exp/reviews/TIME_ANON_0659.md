### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This genomics and developmental transcriptomics study reports a 173-Mb draft nuclear genome assembly and stage-specific RNA-seq resource for the polyphagous phytoseiid predatory mite *Neoseiulus cucumeris* (Acari: Parasitiformes: Mesostigmata), a major commercial biological control agent against pest mites and thrips. Using ~40,000 pooled <24-h eggs to minimize heterozygosity, the authors generated 51.75 Gb of Illumina HiSeq 2500 reads (287x depth across 3 paired-end [180–500 bp] and 9 mate-pair [2–15 kb] libraries) and assembled 3,715 contigs ($\text{N50} = 222.9\text{ kb}$) into 1,173 scaffolds ($\text{N50} = 1.57\text{ Mb}$, 95.22% complete arthropod BUSCOs), annotating 20.6 Mb (11.9%) of transposable elements and 18,735 protein-coding genes (`GLEAN` + `Trinity`/`PASA` + `GeMoMa`), along with developmental transcriptomes across 5 stages (`12h eggs`, `36h eggs`, `larvae`, `nymphs`, `adults`). The authors also perform comparative gene-family (`OrthoMCL`, `CAFE`) and phylogenomic (`PhyML`, `RAxML`, `MCMCTREE`) analyses across 9 chelicerate species and catalog candidate genes in vitellogenesis, oviposition, pseudoarrhenotokous epigenetic sex determination (`tra`/`dsx` duplications), Hox cluster organization, chemoreception (`GRs`, `IRs`), detoxification (`CYPs`, `GSTs`, `CCEs`, `ABCs`), and innate immunity (`Toll`, `Imd` loss, `RNAi`). While the draft genome assembly itself has high contiguity ($\text{N50} = 1.57\text{ Mb}$) and reveals biologically interesting features (such as an intact Hox cluster compared to atomized Hox genes in *Metaseiulus occidentalis*, loss of `Scr`, and `tra`/`dsx` duplications), the manuscript suffers from **four serious methodological flaws**: (1) claiming to resolve Acari monophyly (`Acariformes + Parasitiformes`) using a 9-taxon tree with only 2 non-mite arachnids under a site-homogeneous `JTT` model that is famously susceptible to Long-Branch Attraction (`LBA`), compounded by a paralog-contaminated `1,262`-gene dataset; (2) calling thousands of differentially expressed genes across developmental stages (`Cufflinks` $P < 0.05$) from **unreplicated** single-pool RNA-seq libraries ($n=1$ per stage); (3) reporting bacteriophage/bacterial genes (`45 phage terminase-small subunit genes`, `11 phage integrase genes`, `22 bacterial extracellular solute-binding protein genes`) and unmasked retrotransposon `reverse transcriptases` (`24 genes`) as the top "significantly expanded *N. cucumeris* gene families" in `CAFE`; and (4) omitting all public database accession numbers (`NCBI BioProject`, `SRA`, `WGS`) for the genome and transcriptome data.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS, WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT

#### Key Strengths
- **High-contiguity Illumina PE + Mate-Pair genome assembly from pooled haploid/diploid eggs:** Sequencing 3 PE and 9 long-insert MP libraries (up to 15 kb) at 287x coverage with `ALLPATHS-LG`, `SSPACE`, and `GapCloser` produced a compact, well-scaffolded 173-Mb assembly (`1,173` scaffolds, $\text{N50} = 1.57\text{ Mb}$, `95.22%` complete arthropod BUSCOs, `99.32%` alignment of transcripts $>1000\text{ nt}$) with extensive microsynteny (`142` scaffolds spanning 137.85 Mb) to the congeneric-family phytoseiid *M. occidentalis* (`Fig. 1c`).
- **Detailed comparative curation of developmental, reproductive, and detoxification repertoires:** The targeted phylogenetic and domain curations of intact Hox cluster organization (`Fig. 3`, showing parasitiform-specific loss of *Sex combs reduced* [`Scr`]), three-copy duplications of *transformer* (`tra`) and *doublesex* (`dsx`), loss of `Dnmt1`/`Dnmt3` and canonical `Imd`/`Relish` immunity components, and intermediate detoxification repertoire size (`69 CYPs`, `15 GSTs`, `53 CCEs`—larger than the specialist predator *M. occidentalis* [`63, 12, 42`] but smaller than the polyphagous herbivore *Tetranychus urticae* [`86, 31, 68`], `Fig. 4`) provide valuable biological insights for acarine biology.

#### Methodological Weaknesses & Audit Findings
1. **Unmasked Bacterial/Bacteriophage Contigs and Retrotransposons Misidentified as Top "Expanded *N. cucumeris* Gene Families" (`Lines 69, 94`):**
   - In `Results` line 94, the authors report their `CAFE v3.0` gene family expansion analysis ($P < 0.01$): *"Based on the function annotation against the Pfam database, the most significantly expanded gene families are related to DUF1705 (45 genes), phage terminase-small subunit (45 genes), ABC transporter transmembrane region (28 genes), reverse transcriptase (RNA-dependent DNA polymerase) (24 genes), and bacterial extracellular solute-binding proteins-family 3 (22 genes)."* Similarly, line 69 reports *"11 genes belonging to 2 expanded phage integrase families"* and *"at least 83 reverse transcriptase genes"*.
   - **Phage terminase small subunit (`45 genes`), phage integrase (`11 genes`), and bacterial extracellular solute-binding proteins (`22 genes`) are prokaryotic/bacteriophage genes**, not eukaryotic mite gene families! Why did they remain in the assembly? Look at `Methods` line 270: when running `BlobTools`, *"Taxonomy id was assigned to each sequence through BLASTn against NCBI's nt database... Scaffolds labelled with 'no-hit' by BlobTools were not considered as the putative contaminants."* Because divergent mite endosymbiont/prophage sequences (e.g., *Wolbachia*, *Cardinium*, *Rickettsia*, or gut bacteria from the prey mite *Aleuroglyphus ovatus*) frequently fail to pass the high-stringency megablast `BLASTn` threshold against `nt` (whereas `DIAMOND`/`BLASTx` against `nr` is required in standard `BlobTools` workflows), prokaryotic and prophage genes were annotated as "lineage-specific" or "expanded" *N. cucumeris* genes! Likewise, the 83 `reverse transcriptase` and 20 `transposase` genes are unmasked transposable element ORFs that were left inside the host protein-coding gene catalog prior to running `OrthoMCL` and `CAFE`.
2. **Phylogenomic Claim of Acari Monophyly (`Fig. 1a`) Driven by Long-Branch Attraction (`LBA`), Sparse Non-Acari Sampling, and Paralog Inclusion:**
   - The Abstract (line 9) and Discussion (lines 206–212) highlight support for a monophyletic `Acari` (`Acariformes + Parasitiformes`), claiming this refutes recent genomic studies supporting Acari diphyly.
   - However, the authors' taxon sampling (`Fig. 1a`) includes 6 fast-evolving mites/ticks (`4` Parasitiformes + `2` Acariformes) but **only two non-mite arachnids** (one spider [*Stegodyphus mimosarum*] and one scorpion [*Mesobuthus martensii*], both belonging to `Arachnopulmonata`, while omitting Pseudoscorpiones, Opiliones, Solifugae, Ricinulei, and Palpigradi) rooted on the distant horseshoe crab *Limulus polyphemus*. Worse, the trees were inferred using a **site-homogeneous `JTT + G4` model** in `PhyML`/`RAxML` (and even Neighbor-Joining in `MEGA7`, line 77!). In chelicerate phylogenomics, it is well established that both Acariformes and Parasitiformes exhibit severely accelerated substitution rates; when non-pulmonate arachnid orders are omitted and site-homogeneous matrices (`JTT`) are used instead of site-heterogeneous mixture models (`CAT-GTR`), the two long-branched mite lineages artificially attract each other via Long-Branch Attraction (`LBA`).
   - Furthermore, to expand from `130` single-copy orthologs to `1,262` genes (`line 319`), the authors included multi-copy gene families by picking *"one gene for each species... according to the sequence similarity to the gene in N. cucumeris"*. Selecting genes from multi-copy families by pairwise similarity to a single focal species violates orthology (mixing out-paralogs with orthologs) and systematically biases sequence similarity toward *N. cucumeris*.
   - Note also a taxonomic swap in the `Fig. 1a` legend (`lines 77, 80`): the text writes *"Stegodyphus mimosarum (Scorpiones Mesobuthus martensii (Araneae)"* — *Stegodyphus mimosarum* is the African social velvet spider (`Araneae`), whereas *Mesobuthus martensii* is the Chinese golden scorpion (`Scorpiones`).
3. **Unreplicated Developmental RNA-Seq ($n=1$ Pool per Stage) Used to Call Thousands of Statistically Significant DEGs (`Lines 118, 295–297`):**
   - For the 5 developmental stages (`12h eggs`, `36h eggs`, `larvae`, `nymphs`, `adults`), `Methods` line 295 describes collecting a single pool of $>3,000$ individuals per stage without biological replication, and line 297 states: *"Genes with an absolute fold-change cutoff of 2 and P-value < 0.05 were regarded as differentially expressed genes (DEG)"* (reporting `2,808`, `3,443`, and `3,353` DEGs between adjacent stages in line 118, plus temperature and diet transcriptomes mentioned in line 60 that are never described in the Methods at all!). Without biological replicates, `Cufflinks` $p$-values measure only Poisson/technical read-sampling variance within a single library, not biological variance, and no multiple-testing (`FDR`/`q-value`) adjustment was applied.
4. **Inconsistent Gene Counts and Missing Public Data Accessions:**
   - The total number of annotated protein-coding genes is stated as `18,735` (`17,514` homology/ab initio + `1,221` transcriptome-based, lines 9, 60, 114), yet line 123 states `"among all the 18,380 genes"`, and line 99 states that `11,871` genes represent `~67.83%` of *N. cucumeris* genes ($11,871 / 0.6783 = 17,501$, omitting the 1,221 transcriptome-annotated genes).
   - Line 297 reports RNA-seq quantification as `FPKM` via `TopHat2`/`Cufflinks`, whereas line 123 reports the exact same expression values as `RPKM`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** The *N. cucumeris* nuclear genome is ~173 Mb with ~11.9% repetitive elements, conserved microsynteny with *M. occidentalis*, an intact Hox cluster (lacking `bcd`, `zen`, and `Scr`), and duplicated `tra` (3 copies) and `dsx` (3 copies) sex-determination genes. `[Well Supported]`
- **Claim 2:** *N. cucumeris* harbors an expanded detoxification repertoire (`69 CYPs`, `15 GSTs`, `53 CCEs`) compared to the specialist predator *M. occidentalis* (`63 CYPs`, `12 GSTs`, `42 CCEs`), consistent with its broader polyphagous diet. `[Well Supported]`
- **Claim 3:** Phylogenomic analysis resolves the evolutionary origin of mites in favor of a monophyletic `Acari`. `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Confounded by severe Long-Branch Attraction under a site-homogeneous `JTT` model with only 2 non-mite arachnid species and paralog-contaminated multi-copy gene selection.
- **Claim 4:** *N. cucumeris* has significantly expanded gene families encoding `phage terminase-small subunit` (45 genes), `bacterial extracellular solute-binding proteins` (22 genes), and `reverse transcriptase` (24 genes). `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Artifacts of unscreened bacterial/prophage sequences (`BlobTools` `nt`-only `'no-hit'` bypass) and unmasked transposable element ORFs retained in the gene catalog.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Sequencing library insert sizes (`180 bp–15 kb`), assembly metrics (`Table 1`), and bioinformatic tool versions (`ALLPATHS-LG 52488`, `SSPACE`, `GapCloser`, `BUSCO v2.0.1`, `RepeatMasker v4.0.5`, `GeMoMa v1.3.1`, `TopHat2`, `Cufflinks v2.2.1`, `OrthoMCL`, `PhyML 4.0`, `PAML v4 MCMCTREE`, `CAFE v3.0`) are documented.
- **Deficiencies:** **No NCBI BioProject, SRA, or GenBank WGS accession numbers** are provided anywhere in the manuscript for the *N. cucumeris* genome assembly or the RNA-seq datasets, and the temperature/diet RNA-seq experiments mentioned in line 60 have no experimental description in the Methods.
