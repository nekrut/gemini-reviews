### 1. Executive Summary
This commissioned mechanistic review synthesizes structural, biochemical, genomic, and clinical gene-therapy advances in retroviral integration site selection, focusing on the comparative biology of lentiviruses (HIV-1) and gammaretroviruses (Moloney murine leukemia virus, MoMLV). The authors delineate two hierarchical layers of specificity: (1) local target DNA sequence preferences at the staggered strand-transfer cut site, governed by DNA bendability around nucleosomes and central flexible `YR`/`RYXRY` dinucleotide steps accommodated within the integrase (IN) intasome (informed by Prototype Foamy Virus [PFV] intasome crystal structures); and (2) genome-wide chromatin targeting, governed by bimodal host chromatin tethers—LEDGF/p75 (and secondarily HRP2) for HIV-1 IN, which binds the IN catalytic core domain (CCD) dimer interface via its C-terminal IBD and engages $\text{H3K36me3}$ + nucleosomal DNA via its N-terminal PWWP domain to direct integration into active gene bodies; versus BET family proteins (BRD2, BRD3, BRD4) for MoMLV IN, which bind the gammaretrovirus-specific disordered 28-residue IN C-terminal tail (`Wx`$\Phi$`xxpxxPLb`$\Phi$`b`$\Phi$`xR`) via their extra-terminal (ET) domain and engage acetylated H3/H4 tails ($\text{H3K9ac}$, $\text{H3K27ac}$, $\text{H4K5/8/12/16ac}$) + nucleosomal DNA via dual bromodomains and basic motifs A/B to direct integration into strong enhancers and active transcription start sites (TSSs). The authors also integrate the upstream role of HIV-1 capsid (CA)–nucleoporin (`NUP358`/`RanBP2`, `NUP153`, `TNPO3`) nuclear entry pathways and evaluate translational strategies to prevent insertional oncogenesis (such as *LMO2* activation in SCID-X1 trials) via self-inactivating (SIN) vectors, BET bromodomain inhibitors (`JQ-1`), chimeric tethers, or MoMLV IN C-terminal tail truncation mutants.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Quantitative and Structural Rigor in a Review Format:** Unlike superficial narrative reviews, this manuscript grounds its synthesis in primary biophysical affinities (e.g., contrasting weak $\sim 2\text{--}3\text{ }\mu\text{M}$ binding of isolated BRD4 BD-I to tetra-acetylated H4 peptides or naked DNA against tight $K_d \approx 60\text{ nM}$ cooperative binding to native mononucleosomes), structural alignments with explicit GenBank accessions and chemical conservation groupings (Fig. 2B, D), crystal/NMR structural coordinates (Fig. 2C, E), and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve area heatmaps with matched-random-control $P$-values across ~3.9 million mapped integration sites (Fig. 3).
- **Balanced Treatment of Unresolved Controversies and Residual Targeting:** The authors carefully highlight experimental discrepancies in the primary literature—such as whether the MoMLV IN CCD ($\alpha$-helix 6) contributes to BET binding beyond the C-terminal tail (residues 386–408), why LEDGF/p75 stimulates HIV-1 IN catalysis in vitro yet `Ledgf/p75` knockout cells retain wild-type in vitro PIC activity, and why residual non-random targeting persists in `Ledgf/Hrp2` double-knockout cells (for HIV-1) and in `JQ-1`-treated or IN C-terminal truncation infections (for MoMLV).
- **Minor Inconsistency in BRD4 Isoform Nomenclature:** In the Figure 1B legend (line 41), the C-terminal motif (CTM) is correctly stated as *"unique to BRD4 isoform A"* (the long isoform, residues 1–1362), whereas in the main text (line 56), the authors inadvertently write *"BRD4 isoform C contains a C-terminal motif (CTM)"* (despite Figure 1B showing that isoform C is the short 1–722 isoform lacking the CTM).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *"Cooperative binding to both cognate histone marks and nucleosomal DNA is a shared bimodal mechanism employed by LEDGF/p75 (H3K36me3 + DNA) and BET proteins (acetylated H3/H4 + DNA) to tether HIV-1 and MoMLV intasomes to active gene bodies vs. strong enhancers/TSSs."* — `[Well Supported]`. Rigorously supported by convergent biochemical, NMR/crystallographic, ChIP-seq, knockout/siRNA, pharmacological (`JQ-1`/`I-BET`), and domain-swap retargeting (`LEDGF(1-324)/BRD4(ET/SEED)` and `CBX1`/`HP1`-IBD) studies.
- **Claim 2:** *"Generating clinical MoMLV vectors that lack the C-terminal tail of MoMLV IN and hence do not engage BET proteins may prove less genotoxic than previous MoMLV-based clinical vectors."* — `[Well Supported]`. Appropriately framed as a promising, practical translational strategy (reducing integration within 1 kb of TSSs from $\sim 12\%$ to $\sim 2.5\%$ without requiring ectopic host-cell chimeric protein expression or systemic BET inhibition), while candidly noting that residual TSS enrichment remains higher than in lentiviral vectors.
