### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This study evaluates brown rice grain iron (`GFe`) and zinc (`GZn`) concentrations (via ED-XRF) across 170 rice accessions from the 3K Rice Genomes Project plus 4 checks grown in an augmented randomized complete block design (4 blocks) across four Indian environments differing in DTPA-extractable soil Fe and pH: New Delhi (`DEL`, 16 ppm Fe, pH 8.0), Aduthurai (`ADT`, 24.6 ppm Fe, pH 6.3), Barapani normal (`BPN-N`, 24.6 ppm Fe, pH 5.63), and Barapani Fe-toxic stress (`BPN-S`, 72.0 ppm Fe, pH 5.2). Using 140,487 filtered SNPs and the `BLINK` model in GAPIT, the authors report 5 marker-trait associations (MTAs) for `GFe` and 1 MTA for `GZn`, and test linked SSR markers (`RM1986` and `RM179`) in 192 $\text{F}_{2:3}$ families from a `Shahsarang` $\times$ `IR64` cross. While the multi-environment field phenotyping and follow-up in an $\text{F}_{2:3}$ biparental cross are valuable in principle, the manuscript contains pervasive statistical and mathematical contradictions, invalid variance decompositions (where factorial regression interaction components sum to $>110\%$ of total $G \times S$ variance), severely inflated/swapped phenotypic variance explained (PVE) values (`32.2%`–`42.3%` in $N=170$, including a GWAS hit explicar 32.2%–41.7% of `GFe` variance at `BPN-N` where the ANOVA in Table 1 showed **no significant genotypic variation** for `GFe`), and direct contradictions between the Abstract, Results, and Tables regarding which QTLs were validated.

### STRENGTHS
- **Multi-location field evaluation spanning soil pH and Fe gradients:** Evaluating 170 3K-panel lines across alkaline (`DEL`), neutral-acidic (`ADT`), and strongly acidic lowland (`BPN-N` and `BPN-S`) soils captures realistic genotype-by-environment interactions relevant to rice biofortification.
- **Inclusion of an independent $\text{F}_{2:3}$ biparental validation population:** Testing candidate chromosome 12 regions in 192 $\text{F}_{2:3}$ families (`Shahsarang` $\times$ `IR64`) goes beyond purely correlative GWAS.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Discovery of a major `GFe` QTL (`PVE = 32.2%–41.7%`, $p = 6.24 \times 10^{-10}$) in an environment (`BPN-N`) where genotypic variance for `GFe` is statistically non-significant:** In **Table 1** and Results (line 88), the ANOVA at `BPN-N` shows that `Treatment` ($5.4$), `Check` ($6.6$), and `Test` genotypes ($5.3$) are **all statistically non-significant** for `GFe` ("Variation due to checks and genotype at BPN-N was not significant for GFe"). Yet in **Table 4**, the authors report a highly significant GWAS hit for `GFe` at `BPN-N` (`qGFe12.2 BPN-N`, $p = 6.24 \times 10^{-10}$) claiming to explain `32.23%` (Table 4) or `41.7%` (text, line 184) of the total phenotypic variance.
- **Swapped and internally contradictory PVE, additive effect, and QTL names across Abstract, Text, Table 4, and Table 5:**
  - In **Abstract** (line 11), the authors state: *"Two marker-trait associations (MTAs), `qGFe12.1 ADT` and `qGZn12.1 BPN-N`, were validated in an $\text{F}_{2:3}$ population"*, whereas **Results** (line 212), **Table 5**, and **Discussion** (line 5 of chunk 2) state that `qGFe12.2 BPN-N` (`12:21260572`, not `qGFe12.1 ADT` at `12:18503677`) and `qGZn12.1 BPN-N` were validated. Meanwhile, the Abstract (line 11) also claims *"Superior haplotypes for two candidate genes were identified, with the analysis revealing their frequencies and allelic effects in different subgroups,"* yet no haplotype frequency or allelic effect analysis across subgroups appears anywhere in the Results, Tables, or Figures.
  - In **Results** (line 184), `qFe12.2 BPN-N` (`GFe`) is stated to explain `41.7%` variation and `qGZn12.1 BPN-N` (`GZn`) `32.2%` variation, whereas **Table 4** (lines 197–198) reports the exact opposite (`32.23%` for `qGFe12.2 BPN-N` and `41.71%` for `qGZn12.1 BPN-N`).
  - In **Results** (line 212, QTL validation), the text states that `RM179` was associated with `GZn` at `BPN-S`, whereas **Table 5** (line 220) lists the location as `BPN-N` (and Discussion calls the marker `RM170` instead of `RM179`). Furthermore, line 212 states: *"The genotypes which carry the homozygous allele of IR64 (RM1986) showed an average GFe of 12.5 mg/kg at BPN-S and 10.6 mg/kg at BPN-N, whereas genotypes which carry homozygous Shahsarang alleles showed 17.6 mg/kg at BPN-S and 12.6 mg/kg at BPN-N. The PVE of the 12:21260572 SNP was found to be 55.3% and 16.4% with additive effects of 0.9 and 2.5 mg/kg at BPN-S and BPN-N, respectively."* Half the difference between homozygotes is $(17.6 - 12.5)/2 = 2.55\text{ mg/kg}$ at `BPN-S` and $(12.6 - 10.6)/2 = 1.0\text{ mg/kg}$ at `BPN-N`—meaning the authors swapped the `BPN-N` and `BPN-S` additive effects and $R^2$ values between the text and Table 5!
- **Mathematical errors in Factorial Regression Analysis (Table 2), Table 1 summary statistics, and Bonferroni threshold:**
  - In **Table 2**, the %GSI shares for the three soil covariates (`G x pH`, `G x SFe`, `G x EC`) sum to $47.9\% + 36.1\% + 32.4\% = 116.4\%$ for `GFe` (sum of variances $5,274.6 + 3,975.4 + 3,570.2 = 12,820.2 > 11,015.2$) and $58.2\% + 21.8\% + 30.9\% = 110.9\%$ for `GZn` ($23,175.3 > 20,895.9$), because the authors fitted non-orthogonal collinear soil covariates across only 4 sites (where 3 environmental covariates saturate all $J - 1 = 3$ environmental degrees of freedom!) separately rather than sequentially/orthogonally.
  - In **Table 1**, under `BPN-S` for `GZn`, `Minimum = 34.9`, `Maximum = 41.9` (total range $= 7.0\text{ ppm}$), `Mean = 37.2`, and `CV% = 2.2%`, yet `SD` is reported as `6.0` (which is impossible when the entire min–max range across 174 lines is only $7.0$ and $\text{CV} = 2.2\%$ implies $\text{SD} \approx 0.82$). Furthermore, because un-replicated test genotypes were grown in an augmented design within each site, estimating within-site genotypic variance ($\text{GCV}\%$) and broad-sense heritability ($h^2_{\text{BS}} = 76.7\%\text{--}98.2\%$) treats residual error from the 4 checks ($df = 9$) as the error variance for 170 un-replicated plots.
  - In **Methods** (line 53), the formula for Grain Iron Assimilation Efficiency (`GIAE` / `IAE`) is missing from the text; and in line 70, the Bonferroni threshold is stated as "calculated from the reciprocal of the total number of markers used for analysis $[p < 4.78\times 10^{-7}; -\log_{10}(p) > 6.32]$", whereas $1 / 140,487 = 7.12 \times 10^{-6}$ and $0.05 / 140,487 = 3.56 \times 10^{-7}$ ($4.78 \times 10^{-7}$ corresponds to $0.067 / 140,487$ or $1 / 2,092,050$).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1 (Excess soil Fe at `BPN-S` increases absolute brown rice grain Fe concentration [mean 22.3 mg/kg] but lowers grain Fe assimilation efficiency relative to soil Fe [IAE = 0.31 vs 1.20 at `DEL`]): `[Well Supported]`** — Directly supported by the multi-site soil and grain XRF measurements, reflecting nonlinear saturation/homeostatic restriction of root-to-grain Fe translocation under acidic Fe toxicity.
- **Claim 2 (Individual GWAS SNPs explain 32%–42% of phenotypic variance for `GFe` and `GZn` in the diversity panel and 55.3% in $\text{F}_{2:3}$): `[Overstated]`** — Single-marker $R^2$ values of 32%–42% in an unreplicated augmented trial of $N=170$ structured rice lines (especially at `BPN-N` where genotypic ANOVA was non-significant) reflect Beavis effect / winner's curse and unadjusted subpopulation structure in simple single-marker regression (`lm(Y ~ SNP)` without PCs/kinship used to compute PVE after BLINK).
- **Claim 3 (Superior haplotypes of candidate genes were identified across subgroups): `[Methodologically Flawed]`** — Claimed in the Abstract, but completely absent from the body of the manuscript.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
While the 3K Rice Genomes Project SNP dataset is publicly available via Rice SNP-Seek, the manuscript omits the formula for `GIAE`/`IAE` (line 53), omits the promised haplotype analysis, and contains numerous swaps and arithmetic contradictions across Tables 1, 2, 4, and 5.
