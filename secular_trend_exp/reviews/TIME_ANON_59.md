**Bias Category:** None (Methodologically Sound)

**Study Modality:** Wet-Lab Primary Experimental
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Basic/Mechanistic Discovery

### 1. Executive Summary
This study provides a definitive biochemical and immunological demonstration that a naturally occurring oncogenic hotspot mutation in human p53 (R273H), located at the P1' position immediately flanking the C-terminus of the HLA-A*0201-restricted cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitope p53.264–272 (`LLGRNSFEV`), abolishes antigen presentation and enables tumor immune escape by blocking 20S proteasomal cleavage between residues 272 and 273. The authors establish this mechanism across five complementary experimental levels: (1) selective resistance of R273H-expressing osteosarcoma transfectants and canarypox-infected targets to lysis by p53.264–272-specific CTLs while retaining sensitivity to p53.149–157-specific CTLs; (2) in vivo tumor escape of R273H-expressing tumors in rVV-ES264-immunized (but not rVV-ES149-immunized) HLA-A*0201/$K^b$ transgenic mice; (3) kinetic in vitro 20S proteasome digestions of synthetic 27-mer WT and R273H polypeptides; (4) online RP-HPLC electrospray tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) quantification showing a 14- to 20-fold abrogation of C-terminal cleavage at V272 in the R273H substrate and direct MS/MS identification of the low-abundance 9-mer epitope in active HPLC fractions; and (5) acid elution and two-stage RP-HPLC resolution of natural MHC class I-bound peptides from $1.2 \times 10^9$ tumor cells proving that the 9-mer `264–272` (rather than the more abundant 13-mer `260–272` precursor) is the natural surface-presented ligand.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Symmetric Internal Controls Across All Cellular and Biochemical Assays:** Every experiment incorporates matched positive and negative controls that isolate the exact effect of the R273H flanking mutation:
  * In cellular cytotoxicity assays (Fig. 1), Saos-2 cells expressing p53 R273H are compared against p53-null Saos-2 and Saos-2 expressing distant p53 hotspot mutations (V143A and R175H), while parallel lysis by CTL A2 149 (recognizing p53.149–157) proves intact MHC class I expression, antigen processing machinery, and p53 protein expression in the R273H targets.
  * In 20S proteasome digestions (Figs. 3–5), substrate turnover (>95% degradation of both WT and mutant 27-mers, Fig. 5 panel 4) and generation of an internal competing cleavage fragment (`266–275`, which does not use the 272–273 cleavage site, Fig. 5 panel 1) confirm equal enzymatic activity across reactions.
  * In natural peptide elution (Fig. 7), two-step RP-HPLC with a shallow gradient and 0.5-min fractionation cleanly separates the 9-mer (`264–272`, fraction 12) from the N-terminally extended 13-mer (`260–272`, fraction 24) by a 6-minute retention time difference, ruling out co-elution artifacts.
* **Minor Statistical Reporting Caveats:** In the in vivo tumor protection experiment (Fig. 2, line 46), group sizes are $n = 3$ mice per group and growth curves are plotted as means without error bars or formal longitudinal/survival statistical tests, although the binary divergence between complete protection (rVV-ES149) and 100% tumor outgrowth matching negative controls (rVV-ES264, rVV-VPE16, untreated) is unambiguous. Similarly, cytotoxicity curves show representative assays without error bars across biological replicates, though the text reports consistency across three independent 27-mer digests (2.3–2.7 log shift, line 100).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Rigorous Resolution of Precursor vs. Minimal Epitope Presentation:** When initial bulk MS screening of the WT 27-mer digest detected the N-terminally extended 13-mer (`260–272`) and 17-mer (`256–272`) products cleaved at V272—but not the minimal 9-mer (`264–272`) due to interference from the dominant `266–275` internal cleavage product—the authors did not rely on assumption. Instead, they concentrated the antigenic HPLC fractions (24–25) to confirm direct proteasomal generation of the 9-mer via argon collision-activated MS/MS fragmentation ($m/z = 517.2$, matching synthetic `b6`, `b7`, `b8` fragment ions, Fig. 6C–D) and then extracted natural MHC-bound peptides from $1.2 \times 10^9$ cells to prove that only the mature 9-mer is presented on surface HLA-A*0201 (Fig. 7).
* **Proportional and Mechanistically Grounded Conclusions:** Every claim in the Abstract and text is directly supported by experimental data, establishing a foundational principle in tumor immunology: oncogenic driver mutations can simultaneously confer a selective advantage by disrupting flanking proteasomal cleavage sites required for CTL surveillance.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Detailed Biochemical and Analytical Protocols:** Purification of 20S proteasomes from human T1 cells, buffer compositions, RP-HPLC column specifications and elution gradients, MS/MS $m/z$ scan parameters, acid peptide extraction protocols, E/T ratios, and synthetic peptide sequences are specified with precision sufficient for full independent replication.
* **Minor Editorial Typo:** In the legend to Figure 1 (line 87), `peptide epitopes p63.264–272 (A2 264)` contains a typographical error (`p63` instead of `p53`).
