### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This immunology study demonstrates that human monocyte-derived immature dendritic cells (DCs) respond to influenza virus (strain PR8) infection and synthetic double-stranded RNA (poly I:C) via a dual program of: (1) autocrine type I interferon (IFN-$\alpha/\beta$) production that rapidly induces the antiviral protein MxA and protects DCs from viral cytopathic effects; and (2) cellular maturation paired with a paradoxical *increase* (5–10-fold) in total and MHC class I protein synthesis, prolonged MHC class I half-life, and production of bioactive IL-12 p75 and type I IFN that polarize naive $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD45RA}^+$ T cells into IFN-$\gamma$-producing Th1 cells. By contrasting DCs with HeLa cells (where poly I:C and PR8 shut down protein synthesis after IFN-$\alpha$ priming), the authors establish how DCs sustain viral antigen presentation on MHC class I while resisting viral lysis.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Descriptive Reporting Without Formal Hypothesis Testing:** In keeping with classical cellular immunology conventions of its era, flow cytometry histograms, pulse-chase PhosphorImager quantifications, and T-cell proliferation curves are presented as representative experiments (out of 3–4 independent experiments) with ranges reported in the text (e.g., "5–10-fold in four experiments", "70–90%, in different experiments"), rather than aggregated mean $\pm$ SD across blood donors with formal paired $t$-tests or ANOVA.
- **Missing Table I and Figure Panels in Text Stream:** While Table I and Figures 1–7 are described in detail in the Results text, the tabular numerical values for Table I (surface marker MFI across stimuli) and figures are not embedded in the markdown text stream.
- **Causal Attribution of Cytoprotection Specifically to MxA:** While neutralizing antisera against type I IFN (*Iivari* and *Kaaleppi*) convincingly demonstrate that autocrine type I IFN drives MxA induction, type I IFN induces hundreds of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs, such as PKR, OAS, IFITMs). Thus, the protective resistance of LPS- or PR8-stimulated DCs to viral cytopathicity is correlated with MxA expression rather than genetically proven to be mediated solely by MxA (e.g., via MxA depletion).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality / STRENGTHS
- **STRENGTHS:**
  - Elegantly designed mechanistic experiments dissecting why DCs—unlike epithelial cells (HeLa) or peptide-pulsed DCs—can sustain MHC class I antigen presentation to HLA-A2-restricted influenza matrix $\text{M}_{58\text{--}66}$-specific T-cell clones over 24 hours.
  - Rigorous biochemical demonstration via $^{35}\text{S}$-Met/Cys pulse-chase immunoprecipitation (W6/32 mAb) that PR8 and poly I:C boost HLA class I synthesis rate 5–10-fold and extend class I complex stability 2–3-fold in DCs, whereas HeLa cells undergo translational arrest.
  - Clean functional dissection using two independent sheep neutralizing antisera against human type I IFN and neutralizing anti-IL-12 antibodies in cord-blood / negatively-selected $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD45RA}^+$ naive T-cell polarization assays.
- **Claim Evaluation:**
  - *Claim that dsRNA and influenza virus induce DC maturation and boost MHC class I synthesis and stability:* `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated by surface phenotyping, $^{35}\text{S}$ pulse-chase, and $\text{M}_{58\text{--}66}$ CTL clone stimulation kinetics.
  - *Claim that autocrine type I IFN induces MxA and correlates with resistance to viral cytopathicity:* `[Well Supported]` — Supported by IFN bioassays, western blots, intracellular HA/MxA dual staining, Annexin V/PI viability assays, and antibody neutralization.
  - *Claim that poly I:C-matured DCs drive IL-12/IFN-dependent Th1 polarization of naive T cells:* `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated by intracellular IFN-$\gamma$ vs. IL-4 staining and reversal to Th2 upon IL-12/type I IFN neutralization.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Specific reagent sources, antibody clones (W6/32, L243, 2331, HB15a, 3-4A4, OKT10, mAb 1-10), cytokine concentrations, and assay sensitivities (Daudi bioassay $0.2\text{ U/ml}$) are thoroughly documented, enabling high experimental reproducibility.
