### 1. Executive Summary
This study combines auxin-inducible acute protein degradation (NANOG-AID and UTP15-AID knock-in mouse embryonic stem cell lines), 5-ethynyluridine (EU) nascent RNA sequencing, and RNA interactome capture using click chemistry (RICK) coupled to quantitative mass spectrometry to map direct transcriptional targets of NANOG and the dynamics of nascent RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) upon acute NANOG depletion. The authors identify 1,812 genes with altered nascent transcription within 2 hours of NANOG depletion (1,788 downregulated, 24 upregulated including *Gata6*) and 195 candidate RBPs with altered nascent RNA binding (106 decreased, 89 increased). Focusing on UTP15—a small subunit processome (SSUP) component whose nascent RNA binding drops upon NANOG degradation—they generate UTP15-AID and POLR1A-dTAG lines, perform UTP15 CLIP-seq, spike-in normalized ChIP-seq (UTP15 and Pol II NTD/Ser5P/Ser2P), and rescue assays with a nucleolar localization signal deletion mutant ($\Delta\text{NoLS}$, aa 318–337). They demonstrate that UTP15 binds TSS-proximal nascent transcripts and promoter chromatin in a transcription-dependent manner to promote Pol II recruitment and pluripotency gene transcription independently of Pol I rRNA biogenesis. The experimental design is multi-layered and rigorous, with only minor caveats regarding the interpretation of 1,6-hexanediol and in vitro crowding droplet assays for phase separation.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Spike-In Normalization Across Multi-Omic Assays:** A major technical strength of the study is the systematic use of exogenous spike-in controls across sequencing modalities where global transcriptional output shifts: EU-labeled spike-in RNA for nascent RNA-seq (analyzed via `DESeq2` `estimateSizeFactors` with `controlGenes`), ERCC spike-in mixes for total RNA-seq at 24 h / 48 h post-UTP15 depletion, and 20% *Drosophila* S2 chromatin / H2Av spike-in for UTP15 and Pol II ChIP-seq (`bamCoverage --scaleFactor`). In addition, CLIP-seq and ChIP-seq peak calling enforced Irreproducible Discovery Rate ($\text{IDR} < 0.05$) filtering across biological replicates.
- **Expression-Matched Permutation Control:** To test whether the downregulation of the 685 UTP15 target genes upon NANOG depletion could be an artifact of baseline expression level, the authors stratified the transcriptome into 20 expression bins at 0 h and ran 5,000 expression-matched random permutations (excluding UTP15 targets), confirming specific co-regulation.
- **Separation-of-Function Controls for Ribosome Biogenesis:** Because UTP15 is a canonical 18S rRNA processome factor, pleiotropic translational or nucleolar stress could easily confound pluripotency phenotypes. The authors address this directly with two orthogonal experiments: (1) acute 4-hour IAA depletion followed by nascent RNA-seq/qPCR, and (2) rescue with the UTP15 $\Delta\text{NoLS}$ mutant (Fig. 4j), which fails to restore 5'ETS pre-rRNA transcription but significantly rescues nascent transcription of pluripotency genes (*Esrrb*, *Klf4*, *Pou5f1*), complemented by acute POLR1A-dTAG degradation and PRO-seq reanalysis (Fig. 4k, l) showing that ablating Pol I transcription does not acutely reduce transcription of the 685 UTP15 target genes.
- **Proteomics Imputation and Multiple Testing Caveats:** In the RICK MS analysis (`DEP` R package), missing LFQ values were imputed prior to linear modeling, and differential nascent-RNA binding across IAA timepoints (0 h, 2 h, 6 h) was filtered using unadjusted moderated $t$-test $p < 0.05$ (`fold change > 1.5`) rather than FDR adjustment, which may inflate the count of 195 dynamic RBPs.
- **Phase Separation Assay Limitations:** In Figure 6, in vitro co-condensation of GFP-UTP15-IDR (10 $\mu$M) and mCherry-POLR2A-CTD (10 $\mu$M) is assayed in the presence of 16% dextran (a high molecular crowding concentration), and in vivo condensate sensitivity is tested using 3% 1,6-hexanediol for 10 minutes (Fig. 6e, f). High-concentration 1,6-hexanediol is well documented to induce hyperosmotic chromatin compaction and kinase/phosphatase perturbations in live cells, and the authors' own FRAP curves (Supplementary Fig. 6h) show slow and incomplete recovery. While SIM$^2$ super-resolution quantification of endogenous Pol II clusters upon acute UTP15-AID degradation (Fig. 6i, j; $n = 20$ nuclei per group) is much more physiological, the mechanistic attribution to liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) specifically—versus multivalent protein-RNA-Pol II recruitment—remains correlative.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Acute NANOG degradation combined with 15-min EU nascent RNA-seq and RICK identifies 1,812 immediate transcriptional target genes (including rapid *Gata6* derepression within 2 h) and 106 RBPs with decreased nascent RNA association. `[Well Supported]`
- **Claim 2:** UTP15 is essential for mESC self-renewal and pre-implantation blastocyst development (E3.5 zygote siRNA knockdown) and activates transcription of 685 target genes (including key pluripotency factors) independently of Pol I rRNA biogenesis. `[Well Supported]`
- **Claim 3:** Nascent transcripts at NANOG-regulated promoters recruit UTP15 to TSS chromatin (sensitive to ActD/TPL transcriptional inhibition and NANOG depletion) to promote Pol II initiation (Ser5P) and elongation (Ser2P). `[Well Supported]`
- **Claim 4:** UTP15 promotes pluripotency gene transcription by driving Pol II liquid-liquid phase separation via its C-terminal IDR. `[Moderately Supported]` — UTP15 depletion clearly reduces nuclear Pol II SIM$^2$ puncta and chromatin occupancy, and purified UTP15-IDR enhances CTD droplet formation in vitro, but whether LLPS per se (independent of structured SSUP/coactivator interactions) is the causal mechanism in vivo is not proven by IDR phase-separation-deficient point mutants.
