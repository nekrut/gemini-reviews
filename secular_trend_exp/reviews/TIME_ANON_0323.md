### 1. Executive Summary
This study conducts a candidate-gene family-based association test (FBAT) of 12 tagging SNPs in *CACNA1A* (chromosome 19p13, encoding the P/Q-type voltage-gated calcium channel $\alpha_{1\text{A}}$ subunit) in Chinese Han autism spectrum disorder (ASD) parent-offspring trios. The authors first genotyped 12 tagSNPs on the Sequenom MassARRAY platform in a discovery subset of 239 trios, selected 3 nominally significant SNPs (`rs7249246`, `rs12609735`, `rs2419244`), and expanded the sample by adding 314 trios for a combined analysis of 553 trios (1,659 individuals). In the combined cohort of 553 trios, `rs12609735` showed a nominal association under the additive model ($Z = 1.998, p = 0.046$) and `rs7249246` showed a nominal association under the recessive/dominant model ($Z = -2.668, p = 0.008$), while `rs2419244` was completely null ($p = 0.380$) and two-marker haplotype analysis (`rs7249246`–`rs12609735`) was also null ($\text{global } p = 0.210$, permutation $p = 0.187$). Crucially, none of the associations survive the authors' own Bonferroni threshold ($p < 0.0042 = 0.05/12$), and the statistical strength of `rs12609735` actually weakened upon adding the 314 new trios. Spinning a negative candidate-gene result that fails multiple testing correction as positive evidence that *"CACNA1A might play a role in the etiology of autism"* exemplifies classic candidate-gene over-interpretation.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Failure of Replication in the Independent 314 Trios (Implicit):** Rather than analyzing the 314 additional trios as an independent replication cohort, the authors pooled them with the 239 discovery trios ($N = 553$). We can forensically deduce the signal in the 314 added trios by comparing the $Z$-scores between the 239-trio discovery set and the 553-trio combined set:
  - For `rs12609735` (additive model), the discovery cohort ($n=239$) had $Z = 2.261, p = 0.024$. After more than doubling the sample size to $n=553$, the $Z$-score *decreased* to $Z = 1.998, p = 0.046$ (Table 1), and the recessive model became completely non-significant ($Z = 1.169, p = 0.242$ in Table 2 vs. $Z = 2.008, p = 0.045$ in discovery). This proves that in the 314 new trios, transmission of `rs12609735` was close to the null expectation ($Z \approx 0.6$).
  - For `rs2419244`, the discovery cohort signal ($Z = -2.039, p = 0.041$) vanished in the 553-trio combined cohort ($Z = -0.879, p = 0.380$ additive; $Z = -1.591, p = 0.112$ recessive), meaning transmission in the 314 new trios went in the *opposite* direction.
  - Only `rs7249246` under the recessive/dominant model changed from $Z = -2.276$ ($p = 0.023$) in 239 trios to $Z = -2.668$ ($p = 0.008$) in 553 trios, which still fails the Bonferroni threshold of $p < 0.0042$ (especially when accounting for testing both additive and dominant/recessive models across 12 SNPs, which entails $12 \times 2 = 24$ effective tests, or $\alpha = 0.0021$).
- **Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium (HWE) Deviation in Cases at `rs7249246`:** The authors note that `rs7249246` deviates from HWE ($p < 0.05$) in affected children in the 553 trios while parents are in HWE, and argue in the Discussion (line 122) that *"This could represent a potentially interesting disease association"*. Simultaneously, `rs7249246` is completely null under the additive TDT model ($Z = -0.837, p = 0.402$, where 372 G alleles were transmitted vs. 381.5 expected, and 404 T alleles vs. 394.5 expected, Table 1), and only shows $p = 0.008$ under the recessive/dominant contrast (deficit of GG homozygotes, $S = 74$ vs. $E(S) = 93.75$). In Sequenom MALDI-TOF genotyping, a deficit of one homozygote class in probands without an overall allelic transmission distortion ($p = 0.402$ additive) can easily arise from heterozygote calling bias or subtle genotyping cluster skew.
- **Unrealistic Power Calculation Assumptions:** The authors claim 87% power in 239 trios and $>90\%$ power in 553 trios using Quanto v1.2.4, without stating the assumed genetic effect size (odds ratio / relative risk). To achieve 87% power in 239 trios at $\alpha = 0.05$, one must assume an unrealistically large common-variant relative risk ($\text{RR} \ge 1.5\text{--}1.6$), whereas realistic common-variant effects in ASD have $\text{OR} < 1.15$, for which 553 trios have $<15\%$ power.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *rs7249246 and rs12609735 in CACNA1A are associated with autism in the Chinese Han population and suggest that CACNA1A plays a role in ASD etiology.* `[Overstated]` — Both SNPs fail Bonferroni correction ($p = 0.008$ and $p = 0.046$ vs. threshold $0.0042$), the haplotype test is null ($p = 0.210$), `rs7249246` is null under the primary additive model ($p = 0.402$), and `rs12609735` weakened when expanding from 239 to 553 trios. The proper scientific conclusion is a null finding in this cohort.
- **Claim 2:** *In silico annotations (HaploReg, is-rSNP) and Human Brain Transcriptome expression profiles support a regulatory role for these noncoding intronic SNPs in ASD.* `[Fragile]` — Generic public database lookups showing that *CACNA1A* is expressed in the cerebellum and that intronic SNPs overlap predicted transcription factor motifs do not rescue a statistically non-significant genetic association.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Tables 1–4 provide complete FBAT summary statistics (allele frequencies, informative family counts `Fam`, observed score `S`, expected score `E(S)`, variance `Var(S)`, $Z$, and $p$), which are internally consistent (e.g., for `rs12609735` additive: $Z = (306 - 284.5)/\sqrt{115.75} = 21.5 / 10.7587 = 1.998$).
- Supporting tables (`S1`–`S9`) are referenced for individual genotype counts and discovery cohort statistics. However, Sequenom PCR/extension primer sequences and the exact relative risk parameter used in the Quanto power calculation are omitted.
