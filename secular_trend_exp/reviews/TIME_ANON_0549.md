### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This study characterizes the expression and subunit composition of the extracellular matrix (ECM) glycoproteins tenascin-C (260 and 210 kDa splice isoforms), laminin (B1/B2 chains lacking the A and s chains), and nidogen (150–160 kDa) in native mouse bone marrow and Dexter-type long-term bone marrow cultures, and investigates their regulation by glucocorticoid hormones ($10^{-6}\text{ M}$ hydrocortisone and $10^{-7}\text{–}10^{-5}\text{ M}$ dexamethasone). Using immunofluorescence on newborn and adult mouse femurs, immunoblotting, Northern blot densitometry normalized to *G3PDH* and $\beta$-actin, and $[^{35}\text{S}]$-methionine/cysteine metabolic labeling with immunoprecipitation in stromal preadipocytes (`MC3T3-G2/PA6`) and fibroblasts (`3T3`), the authors show that glucocorticoids downregulate both tenascin-C and laminin B1 mRNAs by $70\text{–}90\%$ during the 3-week initiation of bone marrow stromal cultures. Once the stromal layer is established, laminin B1 mRNA becomes refractory to glucocorticoid withdrawal or addition, whereas tenascin-C mRNA and protein synthesis remain strongly and selectively suppressed by glucocorticoids (reaching $\sim 90\%$ mRNA reduction within 12 h in 3T3 cells without altering $\beta_1$-integrin or fibronectin synthesis).

### 2. STRENGTHS
- **Thorough Immunochemical and Northern Blot Controls:** Figure 4 includes explicit secondary-antibody-only and non-immune serum controls for both native bone marrow and cultured stroma, identifying and accounting for a $\sim 130\text{ kDa}$ non-specific secondary-antibody band in cultured marrow extracts (lane 4). Similarly, EHS tumor extract is included on the same blot (lane 6) as a positive control to prove that the anti-laminin antiserum detects the 400 kDa laminin A chain when present, validating its absence in bone marrow (lanes 7–8).
- **Specificity & Vehicle Controls in Hormone Time-Course Assays:** In the 3T3 time-course (Figure 7) and `MC3T3-G2/PA6` metabolic labeling (Figure 8), the authors include an ethanol vehicle control (Figure 7, lane 2) and probe simultaneously for $\beta_1$-integrin, $\beta$-actin, *G3PDH*, and immunoprecipitated fibronectin (Figure 8, lanes 4–6), demonstrating that dexamethasone selectively represses tenascin-C biosynthesis rather than causing global transcriptional or translational toxicity.
- **Stage-Specific Dissection of Marrow Stroma Initiation vs. Maintenance:** Distinguishing the 3-week stromal establishment phase (Figure 6B, where both tenascin-C and laminin B1 are suppressed) from established stroma (Figure 6C, where only tenascin-C remains glucocorticoid-repressible) separates developmental stromal composition shifts from direct transcriptional repression.

### 3. WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT (Methodological & Statistical Audit)
- **Absence of Replicate Counts, Error Bars, and Statistical Tests in Quantitative Densitometry (Figure 6):** Figure 6A–C presents laser densitometry bar charts of tenascin-C and laminin B1 mRNA normalized to *G3PDH* (expressed as percent of untreated control) without reporting the number of independent biological replicates ($N$), standard deviations/standard errors, or statistical significance tests.
- **Mouse Strain Heterogeneity Across Experimental Modalities:** In the Materials and Methods ("Animals and Cells"), C57BL mice were used for long-term bone marrow cultures, whereas DBA/1, NMRI, or $(\text{C57BL} \times \text{NMRI})$ mice were used for immunofluorescence and immunoblotting without explanation, although qualitative protein isoform patterns were consistent across native marrow and cultured stroma.
- **Typographical Error in Figure 8 Legend Concentration:** The Figure 8 legend states that `MC3T3-G2/PA6` cells were cultured in "$10^{-2}\text{ M}$ (lanes 2 and 5) and $10^{-7}\text{ M}$ dexamethasone," whereas the Methods section ("Immunoprecipitation", page 1038) correctly specifies "$10^{-5}\text{ M}$ and $10^{-7}\text{ M}$ dexamethasone" ($10^{-2}\text{ M}$ dexamethasone is insoluble in aqueous media).
- **Indirect Inference of *Cis*-Regulatory Mechanism:** The conclusion in the Abstract and Discussion that glucocorticoids act "through *cis* regulatory elements of the tenascin gene" is inferred solely from the 4–12 h time course of steady-state mRNA reduction in 3T3 cells, without nuclear run-on assays, actinomycin D mRNA half-life measurements, or cycloheximide protein-synthesis inhibition to rule out secondary glucocorticoid-induced repressors or mRNA destabilization.

### 4. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **[Well Supported] Native mouse bone marrow and Dexter-type stromal cultures express 260/210 kDa tenascin-C, nidogen, and laminin B1/B2 chains lacking the laminin A and s chains:** Demonstrated by double-immunofluorescence and reducing SDS-PAGE immunoblotting with positive EHS controls.
- **[Well Supported] Glucocorticoids strongly downregulate steady-state tenascin-C mRNA and protein synthesis in primary bone marrow stroma, `MC3T3-G2/PA6` preadipocytes, and `3T3` fibroblasts:** Replicated across three cellular systems and verified at both the mRNA (Northern blot) and nascent polypeptide ($[^{35}\text{S}]$ immunoprecipitation) levels.
- **[Moderately Supported] Glucocorticoid repression of tenascin-C is mediated directly via *cis*-regulatory elements of the tenascin promoter and modulates hematopoietic progenitor adhesion:** Plausible given the 4–12 h kinetics and known anti-adhesive properties of tenascin-C, but promoter binding and functional hematopoietic adhesion rescue were not directly tested.

### 5. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
Antibody clones (`MTn 12`, `MTn 15`, `mAb 201`, `mAb D5`), cDNA probes (`p-5'6`, `M20/I`), cell lines, and culture/hybridization protocols are well documented. Adding biological replicate counts ($N$) and error bars to Figure 6 and correcting the $10^{-2}\text{ M} \to 10^{-5}\text{ M}$ typo in the Figure 8 legend would complete the experimental record.
