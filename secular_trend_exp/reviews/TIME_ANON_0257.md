### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript investigates the genetic architecture of drought tolerance (DT) in a panel of 564 Chinese cultivated soybean accessions (CCSP: 319 landraces and 245 released cultivars) using a restricted two-stage multi-locus multi-allele genome-wide association study (RTM-GWAS) based on 24,694 SNP linkage disequilibrium blocks (SNPLDBs) derived from 108,610 RAD-seq SNPs. Phenotyping was conducted in a greenhouse split-plot pot experiment with 5 replicates under stress (30 mL/day) and non-stress (100 mL/day) regimes, measuring membership index values of relative plant weight (MPW) and relative plant height (MPH) at 20 days post-treatment. The authors report 75 QTL (261 alleles) for MPW explaining 54.7% of phenotypic variance and 64 QTL (207 alleles) for MPH explaining 47.1% of variance, construct "QTL-allele matrices" to compare landraces vs. released cultivars, simulate 158,766 potential crosses to predict transgressive recombinants, and screen 354 positional candidate genes via qRT-PCR in two contrasting lines (177 showing >5-fold expression change under 1 h 10% PEG-6000). While the sample size ($N = 564$) and multi-replicate phenotyping provide a solid empirical base, the analytical pipeline suffers from severe statistical overfitting in the two-stage stepwise regression, circular in-sample variance explanation claims, unadjusted multiple testing across 135 chi-square tests and candidate gene F-tests, and an un-replicated/poorly controlled qRT-PCR screen.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Two-Stage Stepwise Regression Overfitting (RTM-GWAS):** In Stage 1, markers are pre-selected at an unadjusted $p < 0.05$ threshold from 24,694 SNPLDBs, passing 7,795 and 7,382 multi-allelic markers (with 2 to 12 haplotypes per block) to Stage 2 stepwise regression on $N = 564$ accessions. Fitting a stepwise regression model with 75 multi-allelic loci (261 parameters) or 64 loci (207 parameters) on 564 observations without out-of-sample cross-validation or an independent test cohort guarantees severe in-sample overfitting and selection bias (Winner's Curse). Consequently, the reported $R^2$ values (54.7% and 47.1%) and individual allele effect estimates are substantially inflated.
- **Heterozygote Masking and Low-Depth RAD-seq Imputation:** Sequencing was performed at $\sim 3.86\times$ depth covering 4.57% of the genome, with missing/heterozygous rates up to 30%, and heterozygous calls were converted to missing values prior to FastPHASE imputation. At $3.86\times$ depth, allelic dropout is substantial, and collapsing multi-SNP blocks (up to 200 kb) into up to 12 haplotypic alleles per SNPLDB risks creating synthetic recombinant haplotypes driven by imputation error.
- **Unadjusted Subpopulation Differentiation & Candidate Gene Tests:** The comparison of allele frequency distributions between landraces (LRS) and released cultivars (RCS) applies chi-square tests across 135 QTL at nominal $p = 0.05$ without multiple-testing correction, nor does it account for background genome-wide drift/differentiation ($F_{ST}$) between LRS and RCS. Similarly, the candidate-gene allele ANOVA ($F$-test at $p = 0.05$ across 45 SNP-bearing genes) is unadjusted for multiple comparisons and re-tests the same phenotypic data used to discover the QTL.
- **Cross Simulation Circularity:** Predicting 10,000 progeny for each of 158,766 crosses using in-sample overfitted additive allele effects (assuming no epistasis, linkage drag, or estimation error) predictably yields extreme transgressive 95th percentiles (MPW > 2.39 vs. max parent 1.41), which are statistical artifacts of summing dozens of upward-biased effect estimates.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim: 75 and 64 QTL form a "relatively thorough" genome-wide QTL-allele matrix explaining 54.7% and 47.1% of DT variance** — `[Overstated]`. Many "small-contribution major QTL" ($R^2 = 0.1\%$ to $1.0\%$, $-\log_{10}P$ down to 2.2) in a 261-parameter stepwise model on $N = 564$ lines are likely false positives.
- **Claim: 177 candidate genes are "validated" by qRT-PCR** — `[Fragile]`. The qRT-PCR assay tested only two accessions (one tolerant, one sensitive) at 0 h and 1 h after acute 10% PEG-6000 osmotic shock in hydroponics (quite distinct from the 20-day soil pot water deficit used for GWAS), using an arbitrary $>5$-fold cutoff in any single tissue/genotype comparison without reporting biological replicate variance or statistical significance (p-values/FDR) in the main text.Showing transcriptional response to 1 h PEG shock in a single genotype does not validate a gene as the causal variant underlying a GWAS locus.
- **Claim: Evolution from LRS to RCS was non-directional for DT based on 58 excluded and 16 emerged alleles** — `[Moderately Supported]`. While the phenotypic means of LRS and RCS are indeed similar (MPW 0.434 vs. 0.401), claims of "emerged new alleles" in RCS ignore sampling variability and imputation artifacts in rare haplotypes (MAF $\ge 1\%$).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Missing Tables & Data Availability:** Although Tables 1–8 are referenced in the text, their actual tabular contents are omitted from the manuscript body, and no public repository accession numbers (e.g., NCBI SRA/PRJNA for the 170.85 Gb of RAD-seq data, or genotype/phenotype matrices) are provided.
- **Primer Sequences:** Primer sequences for the 354 / 177 qRT-PCR assays are not provided or referenced with sufficient detail for independent replication.
