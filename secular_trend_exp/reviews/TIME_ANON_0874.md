### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This computational phylogenomics and algorithmic methods study introduces `PhyChro`, a bottom-up phylogenetic tree reconstruction method based on large-scale chromosomal rearrangements (synteny block adjacencies and breakpoints). Rather than requiring universal synteny blocks shared across all $N$ taxa—which rapidly degrade across divergent eukaryotic clades—`PhyChro` operates on all $\binom{N}{2}$ pairwise synteny block comparisons (generated by `SynChro` or `i-ADHoRe 3.0`). For each breakpoint $[(BA)_G, (BC)_H]$ between genomes $G$ and $H$, `PhyChro` tests all other genomes $K \neq G,H$ for gene-level adjacency conservation to define a **partial split** $S(BA) \parallel S(BC)$ of disjoint taxon subsets. Using the collection of nontrivial partial splits, `PhyChro` computes a multi-genome **Partial Split Distance (`PSD`)**—consisting of an incompatibility count $f_{\text{inc}}(G,H)$ (the number of nontrivial partial splits separating $G$ and $H$) and a compatibility count $f_{\text{comp}}(G,H)$—and iteratively agglomerates sister taxa minimizing $f_{\text{inc}}(G,H)$ among the $n/2$ pairs with the smallest ratio $R(G,H) = \frac{f_{\text{inc}}(G,H)+1}{f_{\text{comp}}(G,H)+1}$, recomputing partial splits and distances from scratch at each coalescent step.

Applied to 13 vertebrate genomes (130,485 breakpoints; 17,848 partial splits) and 21 Saccharomycotina yeast genomes (179,649 breakpoints; 20,924 partial splits), as well as 200 simulated 13-taxon vertebrate and 21-taxon yeast datasets undergoing Poisson-distributed inversions, translocations, duplications, deletions, fusions/fissions, and whole-genome duplications (WGD), `PhyChro` reconstructs the reference vertebrate and yeast phylogenies—including the notoriously difficult placement of *Candida glabrata* as sister to *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* rather than *Naumovozyma castellii*—and achieves 97–98% split accuracy on simulated trees.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
- **Elegant Mathematical Formulation of Partial Split Distance (`PSD`):** By restricting topology inference to nontrivial partial splits (where both $\lvert S(BA)\rvert \ge 2$ and $\lvert S(BC)\rvert \ge 2$) and ignoring autapomorphic rearrangements on terminal branches (trivial splits), `PhyChro` effectively suppresses long-branch/short-branch attraction caused by lineage-specific chromosomal shattering (such as in *C. glabrata*).
- **Forensic Audit of Overstated Claims & Methodological Weaknesses:**
  1. **Placement of Rodentia Rests on a Single-Split Tie-Break with Zero Branch Support (`length = 0, cS = 0.03`):** In the Abstract and Results (`line 44`), the authors emphasize that `PhyChro` correctly places Rodentia as sister to Primates (Euarchontoglires) rather than Laurasiatheria, outperforming `ProtPars`, `Neighbor`, `FastME`, and `MLGO`. However, in the text (`lines 44 and 72`), the authors disclose that **zero partial splits positively support the `(Primates, Rodentia)` branch** (`branch length = 0`, confidence score `cS = 0.03`), and that at default $\Delta = 3$, the three competing topologies `((Primates, Rodentia), Laurasiatheria)`, `(Primates, (Rodentia, Laurasiatheria))`, and `((Primates, Laurasiatheria), Rodentia)` are contradicted by **36, 37, and 42** partial splits, respectively! Thus, `PhyChro` recovered the canonical placement of Rodentia by a margin of **1 contradictory split (`36 vs. 37`)** out of 17,848 partial splits. Confirming this fragility, when `PhyChro` is run on `i-ADHoRe 3.0` blocks (`line 70`) or on `SynChro` blocks with $\Delta = 2$ (`line 244`), it misplaces Rodentia. Similarly, the placement of *Pichia pastoris* in the yeast tree has a confidence score of `cS = 0` (`#Support + #Contradict = 0`, line 51).
  2. **Handicapped Sequence-Based Baseline (`80` Orthologs in Yeasts) and Internal Ortholog Count Contradiction (`389` vs. `357`):**
     - First, there is an internal numerical contradiction: Results (`line 44`) state that `PhyML` was run on **389** families of vertebrate orthologs, whereas Materials and Methods (`line 279`) states that **357** families of syntenic homologs sharing $>90\%$ similarity were identified and used.
     - Second, for the 21 yeast genomes, the authors restricted the sequence-based comparison methods (`PhyML`, `ProtPars`, `Neighbor`, `FastME`) to only **80** ortholog families filtered at an arbitrary $>80\%$ sequence similarity threshold (`line 279`). Restricting genome-wide yeast phylogenomics to the 80 most ultra-conserved (>80% identity) proteins strips away phylogenetically informative sites and handicaps the sequence-based baselines.
  3. **Questionable Simulation Benchmark Setup for `MLGO` (`Figure 3`):** In Figure 3 (`line 90`), `MLGO` recovers only 1% (yeast) and 3% (vertebrate) of simulated trees, performing barely better than random shuffling. However, in the simulation (`line 83`), *"genes are represented by numerical identifiers, not by actual nucleotide or amino acid sequences"*, whereas for real genomes `MLGO` input was prepared by `OrthoMCL` sequence clustering (`line 272`), and `PhyChro` was fed pre-computed synteny blocks while `MLGO` was fed raw gene-order lists (`line 274`). The text does not explain how `SynChro` (which relies on BLASTP reciprocal best hits, `line 255`) or `MLGO` (`OrthoMCL`) was executed on sequence-free numerical gene identifiers in the simulation.
  4. **Runtime Claim Contradiction (`<15 min` vs. `19 min` + `1,765 min` Preprocessing):** The Abstract (`line 7`) states that `PhyChro` reconstructs the vertebrate and yeast phylogenies *"in <15 min"*, but Materials and Methods (`line 260`) reports that the yeast reconstruction took **19 min** (and vertebrate 13 min), excluding the **1,185 min (19.8 h)** and **1,765 min (29.4 h)** required by `SynChro` to compute the pairwise synteny blocks.
  5. **Formatting Omission (`Table 1`):** `Table 1` (`line 199–201: "Table 1. PhyChro Algorithm."`) is rendered as an empty table cell (`| |`), though the 7 algorithm steps are written out in the text immediately below it (`lines 203–237`).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
- `[Well Supported]` **Chromosomal rearrangement breakpoints encoded as partial splits contain rich phylogenetic signal capable of resolving deep eukaryotic relationships and overcoming *C. glabrata* long-branch attraction:** Demonstrated on 21 yeast and 13 vertebrate genomes and 200 forward genome-rearrangement simulations.
- `[Well Supported]` **Small synteny blocks ($2\text{--}20$ genes) and microrearrangements ($\Delta = 3\text{--}4$) carry critical phylogenetic signal across divergent genomes:** Demonstrated by the systematic comparison of `SynChro` vs. `i-ADHoRe 3.0` block-size distributions (`Fig S5–S7`) and $\Delta \in \{2,3,4,5,6\}$ sensitivity sweeps (`Fig S8`).
- `[Overstated]` **`PhyChro` reliably resolves the branching of Rodentia and outperforms sequence-based phylogenetics:** The Rodentia split in `PhyChro` has `branch length = 0`, `cS = 0.03`, and rests on `36 vs. 37` contradictory splits (failing under `i-ADHoRe` or $\Delta = 2$), while the sequence-based yeast comparison was artificially restricted to 80 ultra-conserved proteins.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Open-Source Software & Parameters:** `PhyChro` and `SynChro` are publicly available under a BSD license (`http://www.lcqb.upmc.fr/phychro2/` and `SynChro.html`), and all algorithmic steps, complexity bounds ($O(B \times N)$), simulation parameters, and `i-ADHoRe 3.0` / `OrthoMCL` / `PhyML` / `FastME` settings are documented in detail.
