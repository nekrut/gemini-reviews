### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates morphological divergence, phylogenetic relationships, and candidate-gene adaptation across five Alaskan subspecies of song sparrow (*Melospiza melodia*: *maxima*, *sanaka*, *insignis*, *caurina*, and *rufina*). The authors analyze eight morphometric traits across $n = 227$ adult male museum specimens (demonstrating that western sedentary island subspecies are ~1.6-fold heavier in body mass and ~1.2-fold larger in linear dimensions than eastern migratory subspecies), reconstruct a maximum-likelihood phylogeny from 4,793 ultraconserved element (UCE) loci across 5 ingroup subspecies ($n = 1$ individual per subspecies) and 3 outgroups (recovering *M. m. maxima* as sister to the remaining four Alaskan subspecies with 100% ultrafast bootstrap support), and perform McDonald-Kreitman (MK) tests on 37 literature-curated candidate genes comparing *M. m. maxima* ($n = 2$ diploid genomes) against *M. georgiana* ($n = 1$). While the morphometric analysis and UCE phylogenomics are well executed and openly documented, the MK selection analysis is severely underpowered ($n = 2$ ingroup individuals, low sequencing depth averaging $6.7\times$ down to $2.1\times$), contains an incomplete table omitting an entire functional category discussed in the text, and overinterprets completely non-significant neutrality indices ($p = 0.27 - 1.0$) as evidence of positive selection.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Invalid Inference of Positive Selection from Non-Significant MK Tests ($N = 2$):** The central evolutionary claim in the Abstract and Discussion—that "seven candidate genes associated with bill size, circadian rhythm regulation, plumage color, and salt tolerance exhibited signs of putative positive selection"—is statistically unsupported by the authors' own Table 1:
  1. Across all 37 candidate genes tested, only two genes had unadjusted Fisher's exact test $p < 0.05$: `CD36` ($p = 0.026$) and `LAP3` ($p = 0.029$). Neither survives Benjamini-Hochberg FDR or Bonferroni correction across 31–37 informative tests ($\alpha_{\text{Bonf}} = 0.05 / 31 = 0.0016$). More importantly, both `CD36` ($D_s = 3, D_n = 0, P_s = 3.5, P_n = 14.5$) and `LAP3` ($D_s = 8, D_n = 0, P_s = 4, P_n = 5$) have $D_n = 0$ and an excess of nonsynonymous *polymorphism* ($NI = \infty / \text{Undefined}$), which is the opposite direction of positive selection (reflective of purifying selection or segregating slightly deleterious variants).
  2. The seven genes claimed to be under "putative positive selection" (`DLK1`, `CLOCK`, `PER3`, `BCO2`, `TYR`, `MMP17`, `WNK2`) were selected solely because their point estimate of the Neutrality Index satisfied $0 < NI < 1$, despite having Fisher's exact test $p$-values of `1.0` (`DLK1`: $D_s=3, D_n=1, P_s=9, P_n=2, NI=0.667$), `0.524` (`CLOCK`: $D_s=2, D_n=3, P_s=3, P_n=1, NI=0.222$), `1.0` (`PER3`: $D_s=7, D_n=2, P_s=15, P_n=4, NI=0.933$), `0.632` (`BCO2`: $D_s=2, D_n=3, P_s=14, P_n=10, NI=0.476$), and `1.0` (`TYR`: $D_s=3, D_n=2, P_s=6, P_n=3, NI=0.750$). Interpreting small-count ratios with $p = 0.52 - 1.0$ as biological evidence of positive selection violates basic statistical inference.
  3. Furthermore, four additional genes in Table 1 (`PER2`, `BCO1`, `STARD3`, `AGRP`) have $NI = 0$ (because $P_n = 0$ and $D_n > 0$), yet the authors inconsistently exclude them from the seven "NI < 1" genes while including `PER3` ($NI = 0.933, p = 1$).
- **Internal Table 1 Omission ("Salt Tolerance" Genes Missing):** In the Methods (line 48) and Results (line 81), the authors state that 37 candidate genes were tested, and in the Discussion (line 156) they explicitly state: *"Finally, we found potential positive selection in two salt-tolerance candidate genes: MMP17 and WNK2 (Table 1)."* However, inspection of Table 1 reveals only **34 genes** listed across six categories (Bill size: 5; Body size: 6; Dispersal: 3; Migration: 8; Plumage color – carotenoid: 6; Plumage color – melanin: 6). The entire "Salt tolerance" category (3 genes including `MMP17` and `WNK2`) was accidentally truncated/omitted from Table 1, and accounting for `MMP17` and `WNK2` reveals that only 5 genes in the printed Table 1 (`DLK1`, `CLOCK`, `PER3`, `BCO2`, `TYR`) have $0 < NI < 1$ (plus 2 missing salt-tolerance genes = 7).
- **Low Sequencing Coverage and Genotype Calling:** Whole-genome sequencing coverage averaged $6.7\times$ across individuals and was as low as $2.1\times$ in the lowest sample. Calling heterozygous polymorphic sites ($P_n, P_s$) with GATK at $2.1\times - 6.7\times$ coverage with a Q30 filter results in substantial allelic dropout, severely biasing polymorphism counts in a sample of $N = 2$ individuals.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Western sedentary Aleutian/Kodiak song sparrow subspecies (*maxima*, *sanaka*, *insignis*) are significantly larger (~1.6$\times$ body mass, ~1.2$\times$ linear dimensions) than eastern migratory subspecies (*caurina*, *rufina*), separating cleanly along PC1 (55% variance explained). `[Well Supported]`
- **Claim 2:** Concatenated ML phylogenomics across 4,793 UCE loci places *M. m. maxima* as sister to the clade containing the other four Alaskan subspecies (*sanaka*, *insignis*, *caurina*, *rufina*). `[Moderately Supported]` — Strongly supported by the nuclear UCE concatenation (100% bootstrap), though with $n = 1$ individual per subspecies and no coalescent species-tree or introgression ($D$-statistic) analysis, incomplete lineage sorting or gene flow between *maxima* and *sanaka* could affect single-individual concatenation.
- **Claim 3:** Seven candidate genes (`DLK1`, `CLOCK`, `PER3`, `BCO2`, `TYR`, `MMP17`, `WNK2`) show evidence of positive selection in *M. m. maxima*. `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Every single one of these genes has a completely non-significant McDonald-Kreitman Fisher's exact test ($p = 0.524$ to $1.0$), and the two nominally significant genes (`CD36`, `LAP3`) reflect $D_n = 0$ (purifying selection / polymorphism excess) and fail multiple-testing correction.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Open Science & Repositories:** Excellent data and code availability: raw WGS reads are archived in NCBI SRA under BioProject `PRJNA1114297`, reference genome annotations are specified (`GCF_035770615.1`), and both the morphometric R scripts/raw measurements (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`) and custom MK-test scripts (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`) are publicly available on GitHub.
- **Table Completeness:** Table 1 must be corrected to restore the missing 3 salt-tolerance genes (`MMP17`, `WNK2`, and the third salt-tolerance gene).
