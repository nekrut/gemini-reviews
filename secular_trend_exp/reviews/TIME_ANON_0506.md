### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This manuscript is a comprehensive veterinary virology textbook chapter / pedagogical review covering host innate and adaptive antiviral immunity, viral immune evasion mechanisms, and animal vaccination principles (including inactivated, live-attenuated, subunit, DNA, and viral-vectored vaccines, maternal antibody interference, and DIVA strategies). Because it is an educational synthesis rather than a primary empirical or modeling study, it contains no original datasets, statistical tests, or novel mathematical models, and must be evaluated on the accuracy, internal consistency, and biological fidelity of its immunological synthesis.

### 2. STRENGTHS
- **Broad Comparative Synthesis Across Veterinary Species:** Integrates mechanistic immunology (PRRs, TLR/RLR signaling, Type I/II/III IFNs, ISGs, NK receptor balance, MHC Class I/II antigen processing, T/B lymphocyte activation) with practical veterinary vaccinology across livestock, companion animals, poultry, and fish.
- **Clear Exposition of Applied Veterinary Concepts:** Accurately explains maternal antibody interference windows, mucosal vs. systemic Ig isotype dynamics, DIVA (Differentiating Infected from Vaccinated Animals) marker vaccines for eradication programs, and vaccine delivery modalities (in ovo poultry vaccination, immersion fish vaccination, oral wildlife baiting).

### 3. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Absence of Primary Quantitative Analysis or Systematic Literature Methodology:** As a textbook chapter ("This chapter argues..."), there are no primary experiments, meta-analytic inclusion criteria, or quantitative uncertainty estimates; figures are reproduced from secondary textbooks and reviews (e.g., *Robbins & Cotran*, *Viruses and Human Disease*, *Nat. Rev. Drug Discov.*).
- **Technical Typographical and Mechanistic Imprecisions:**
  - In the caption to Figure 4.4 ("The antiviral state"), PKR is erroneously defined as *"double-stranded DNA (dsRNA)-dependent protein kinase (PKR)"* ("double-stranded DNA" instead of double-stranded RNA).
  - In the section on Type I IFN action, the text states that *"The importance of type I IFN to innate resistance is graphically confirmed by the fact that IFN receptor (IFNAR)-deficient mice are highly susceptible to lethal infections with viruses, but not with intracellular pathogens such as the bacteria Listeria monocytogenes"*, without clarifying that `Ifnar1-/-` mice are actually *more resistant* to *L. monocytogenes* because Type I IFN sensitizes lymphocytes to Listeria-induced apoptosis.
  - Under *Gene Silencing (Interfering RNA)*, mammalian miRNA/siRNA pathways are presented as a canonical antiviral innate defense alongside IFNs without clarifying the ongoing debate and limited physiological role of antiviral RNAi in differentiated somatic mammalian cells compared to plants and invertebrates.

### 4. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Innate and adaptive immune responses are mechanistically linked via dendritic cells, PRR signaling, and cytokines:** `[Well Supported]` — Accurately reflects established immunological mechanisms (TLR3/7/8/9, RIG-I/MDA5-MAVS, JAK/STAT, MHC I/II presentation).
- **DIVA vaccines and adjuvant systems enable disease control and eradication in livestock:** `[Well Supported]` — Well-grounded in veterinary practice (e.g., pseudorabies, IBR, avian influenza, foot-and-mouth disease).
- **Antiviral RNAi acts as a standard mammalian innate antiviral defense mechanism:** `[Overstated]` — Conflates endogenous miRNA gene regulation and invertebrate/plant antiviral siRNA with mammalian somatic antiviral immunity.

### 5. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Secondary Pedagogical Resource:** Contains no primary data or code; third-party figure sources are properly attributed with original page numbers and copyright permissions, though inline primary literature citations are omitted in the body text as is standard for textbook chapters.
