### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This landmark molecular immunology study reports the isolation, complete nucleotide and deduced amino acid sequence, binding kinetics, biochemical cross-linking, expression profile, and functional signal-transduction reconstitution of the human interleukin-5 receptor $\alpha$-chain (`hIL-5R`). Using a murine IL-5R (`mIL-5R`) cDNA probe under low-stringency hybridization to screen oligo(dT)- and random-primed cDNA libraries constructed from enriched human peripheral blood eosinophils, the authors isolated multiple overlapping clones (`ph5R.1`, `λh5R.12`, `λh5R.27`) encoding a 420-amino-acid membrane-bound precursor (20-aa signal peptide; 322-aa extracellular domain containing two fibronectin-type-III cytokine receptor modules with four conserved cysteines and a `WSXWS` motif; 20-aa transmembrane domain; and 58-aa cytoplasmic domain lacking kinase domains; mature $M_r\ 45,556$), as well as an alternatively spliced clone (`λh5R.25`) lacking the transmembrane domain and encoding a putative soluble receptor isoform (335 aa; GenBank `X61176`–`X61178`, `X62156`). Through transient expression in COS7 cells (`pCAGGS-h5R.12` and `pCAGGS-h5R.27`), Scatchard radioligand binding with both $^{125}\text{I-hIL-5}$ and $^{35}\text{S-mIL-5}$, $\text{BS}^3$ affinity cross-linking, Northern blotting across normal human eosinophils and hematopoietic cell lines, and stable transfection into murine IL-3-dependent `FDC-P1` cells, the authors demonstrate that: (1) hIL-5R shares 70% amino acid identity with mIL-5R; (2) unlike mIL-5R$\alpha$ (which binds IL-5 with low affinity alone, $K_d \approx 10\text{–}30\text{ nM}$), the human IL-5R $\alpha$-chain alone expressed on COS7 cells binds IL-5 with high/intermediate affinity ($K_d = 250\text{–}590\text{ pM}$, only ~2-fold higher than on native human eosinophils); (3) $\text{BS}^3$ cross-linking identifies a $55\text{–}60\text{ kDa}$ glycoprotein (`95–100 kDa` adduct minus `45 kDa` dimeric IL-5); (4) `5.3-kb` and `1.4-kb` hIL-5R transcripts are selectively expressed in human eosinophils, `HL-60`, and `TF-1` cells; and (5) expressing hIL-5R$\alpha$ in murine `FDC-P1` cells (which express endogenous murine $\beta_c$/`AIC2B`) reconstitutes high-affinity binding ($K_d = 530\text{ pM}$) and dose-dependent proliferative signaling (`1–10 pM` half-maximal response) to human IL-5.

### 2. STRENGTHS
- **Multi-Modal Molecular and Functional Proof:** The study progresses seamlessly from primary human eosinophil cDNA cloning and sequence analysis (Figures 1–2) to ligand specificity competition against six cytokines (`hIL-5, mIL-5, hIL-3, hGM-CSF, hIL-4, hIL-6`; Figure 3), quantitative Scatchard equilibrium binding with two independent radioligands (`125I-hIL-5` and `35S-mIL-5`; Figure 4), chemical cross-linking on COS7 transfectants vs. native human eosinophils (Figure 5), Northern blot expression profiling (Figure 6), and functional proliferation reconstitution in `FDC-P1` cells (Figure 7).
- **Discovery of Both Membrane-Bound and Soluble (`λh5R.25`) Human IL-5R$\alpha$ Isoforms:** Sequencing four independent cDNA clones revealed two $5'$ untranslated exon variants (`λh5R.12` vs. `λh5R.27`) and a soluble receptor transcript (`λh5R.25`) that diverges cleanly at an `AG/GT` splice donor site (`nt 1039`) immediately upstream of the transmembrane domain, matching the `1.4-kb` abundant transcript seen on Northern blots of human eosinophils.
- **Rigorous Cross-Species Mechanistic Insight:** By comparing `hIL-5R` against `mIL-5R` using the exact same $^{35}\text{S-mIL-5}$ preparation on COS7 cells (`Kd = 500–590 pM` for hIL-5R vs. low-affinity `nM` binding for mIL-5R) and testing `hIL-5R` in murine `FDC-P1` cells (`AIC2B+`), the authors establish two major biological principles: (i) the human $\alpha$-chain possesses a much higher intrinsic affinity for IL-5 than the mouse $\alpha$-chain, and (ii) human IL-5R$\alpha$ can functionally couple with the murine common $\beta$-chain (`AIC2B`) to transduce mitogenic signals even though human IL-5 cannot activate murine IL-5R$\alpha$.

### 3. WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT (Methodological & Statistical Audit)
- **Radioligand Specific Activity Constraint at Sub-100 pM Concentrations for $^{125}\text{I-hIL-5}$:**
  - As the authors transparently note in Results (Line 73), Bolton-Hunter radioiodination of yeast-derived recombinant hIL-5 yielded moderate specific radioactivity ($6.9 \times 10^5\text{ cpm/pmol}$), preventing accurate binding measurements below `~100 pM` where bound cpm approached background. The authors appropriately overcame this technical limitation by performing parallel Scatchard analyses with metabolically labeled $^{35}\text{S-mIL-5}$ ($1.5 \times 10^6\text{ cpm/pmol}$), which confirmed a single high-affinity binding class ($K_d = 500\text{–}590\text{ pM}$) across a broader concentration range (`Figure 4B, D`).
- **Co-Transfection with the Cloned Human Common $\beta$-Chain (`KH97`) Deferred to Future Work:**
  - While the authors show that hIL-5R$\alpha$ alone on COS7 cells has $K_d = 250\text{–}590\text{ pM}$ (compared to $K_d = 170\text{–}330\text{ pM}$ on purified human eosinophils and $10\text{–}170\text{ pM}$ on `HL-60`/`TF-1` cells) and functionally couples with the *murine* $\beta$-chain in `FDC-P1` cells, direct co-expression of `hIL-5R` with the cloned *human* IL-3R/GM-CSFR common $\beta$-chain (`KH97`) in COS7 cells was not yet completed in this manuscript (noted as in progress in Discussion, Line 113).
- **Soluble Receptor (`λh5R.25`) Protein Secretion Not Directly Assayed:**
  - Although the sequence and Northern blot (`1.4-kb` band) strongly support a soluble `hIL-5R` isoform analogous to soluble `mIL-5R`, conditioned supernatants from `λh5R.25`-transfected COS7 cells were not biochemically assayed for secreted IL-5 binding activity in this paper.

### 4. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *The cloned cDNAs (`λh5R.12`, `λh5R.27`) encode the functional human IL-5 receptor $\alpha$-chain (`420 aa` precursor, $55\text{–}60\text{ kDa}$ mature glycoprotein), a member of the cytokine receptor superfamily sharing 70% identity with murine IL-5R$\alpha$.* — `[Well Supported]` by complete sequencing, COS7 expression, $\text{BS}^3$ cross-linking, and Northern blot tissue specificity.
- **Claim 2:** *Unlike murine IL-5R$\alpha$, the human IL-5R $\alpha$-chain alone binds IL-5 with sub-nanomolar affinity ($K_d = 250\text{–}590\text{ pM}$) in COS7 cells.* — `[Well Supported]` by concordant Scatchard analyses with both $^{125}\text{I-hIL-5}$ and $^{35}\text{S-mIL-5}$ across two independent cDNA clones (Figure 4).
- **Claim 3:** *Human IL-5R$\alpha$ expressed in murine IL-3-dependent FDC-P1 cells reconstitutes responsiveness to picomolar concentrations of human IL-5 via interaction with the endogenous murine $\beta$-chain.* — `[Well Supported]` by MTT proliferation assays (`Figure 7`) and cold competition.

### 5. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Sequence Accession Numbers:** All four novel human cDNA sequences are deposited in EMBL/GenBank/DDBJ under accession numbers `X61176`, `X61177`, `X61178`, and `X62156`, and full nucleotide/amino acid alignments are printed in Figures 1 and 2.
- **Experimental Protocols:** Library construction, hybridization stringencies, expression vectors (`pCAGGS`), radioligand specific activities, $\text{BS}^3$ cross-linking conditions, and `FDC-P1` electroporation/MTT proliferation protocols are documented with exemplary precision.
