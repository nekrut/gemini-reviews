# Blinded Review: TIME_ANON_68
**Composite Score:** 4.1 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.0/3.5 | Claims: 1.1/2.5 | Biology: 1.0/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.0/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Post-Hoc Spatial Window Selection (Texas Sharpshooter Fallacy) & Underpowered Cross-Sectional Sample ($N=15$)
**Verdict:** Reject
**Study Modality:** Wet-Lab Primary Experimental
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
This study sequences the PCR-amplified precore and core gene ($944\ \text{bp}$, nt 1604–2547) of hepatitis B virus (HBV, subtype *adr*) directly from the sera of $N = 15$ Korean patients with biopsy-proven chronic hepatitis B (14 males, 1 female; mean age 30.0 years), stratified into Group I ($n = 8$ with chronic persistent hepatitis, CPH) and Group II ($n = 7$ with chronic active hepatitis, CAH). Across the 15 patients, 181 nucleotide substitutions (158 synonymous/silent and 23 missense) were identified relative to a prototype Japanese *adr* consensus sequence. Although overall nucleotide substitution rates ($2.230 \times 10^{-2}$ vs. $2.082 \times 10^{-2}$) and codon missense rates ($0.683 \times 10^{-2}$ vs. $1.015 \times 10^{-2}$) did not differ significantly between CPH and CAH ($p > 0.05$), the authors visually define two "mutational hot spots"—codons 21–34 (MHS1) and codons 85–100 (MHS2)—and note that $4/7$ ($57.1\%$) CAH patients vs. $0/8$ ($0\%$) CPH patients carried missense mutations in MHS1, whereas $5/8$ CPH and $4/7$ CAH patients carried missense mutations in MHS2. They conclude that missense mutations in MHS1 (which overlaps the HLA-A2 CTL epitope at residues 18–27), but not MHS2, are related to the severity of chronic hepatitis B in Korean patients.

While the authors appropriately acknowledge in the final sentence that a longitudinal study is warranted, the core analytical claim rests on post-hoc window definition ("Texas sharpshooter" circularity) in an severely underpowered cross-sectional cohort ($n = 8$ vs. $n = 7$), compounded by unconfounded clinical variables (HBeAg status, serum ALT, HBV DNA viral load, disease duration, and host HLA-A2 genotype—none of which are reported or controlled for) and several technical/numerical inconsistencies in the text.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Post-Hoc Boundary Definition of "Mutational Hot Spots" (Circular Conditioning):** The HBV core gene encodes 183 amino acids. With only 23 total missense mutations observed across all 15 patients ($\sim 1.5$ missense mutations per patient across 183 codons), scanning the 183-codon alignment by eye after seeing the data to draw tight boundaries around codons 21–34 (14 codons, MHS1) and codons 85–100 (16 codons, MHS2) and then comparing CPH ($0/8$) vs. CAH ($4/7$) within those post-hoc windows severely inflates the Type I error rate. Even before correcting for multiple window scanning across the 183-codon gene, a two-sided Fisher's exact test on $4/7$ vs. $0/8$ yields $p = 0.0256$—which does not survive any scan-statistic or multiple-testing correction across candidate regions of the core gene.
* **Arithmetic and Textual Errors:**
  1. **Percentage Error in Results (line 55):** "six of the 7 CAH (87.1%) patients had at least one missense mutation" — $6 / 7 = 85.7\%$, not $87.1\%$.
  2. **dNTP Concentration Error in Methods (line 36):** The PCR mixture is stated to contain "400 mM of each dNTP" ($1.6\ \text{M}$ total dNTPs, which is physically impossible and would chelate all $1.5\ \text{mM}\ \text{MgCl}_2$); this is a 1,000-fold typographical error for $400\ \mu\text{M}$.
* **Missing Clinical and Immunological Confounders (No HLA Typing or HBeAg Stratification):** Although the Methods (line 26) state that HBsAg and HBeAg/anti-HBe were measured by radioimmunoassay, the manuscript text never reports how many CPH vs. CAH patients were HBeAg-positive vs. anti-HBe-positive (Table 1 and Figures 1–2 are absent from the text). Because core gene missense mutations accumulate strongly during and after HBeAg seroconversion (immune clearance phase), any imbalance in HBeAg status or duration of infection between the 8 CPH and 7 CAH patients directly confounds the mutation counts. Moreover, the Discussion attributes MHS1 mutations (codons 21–34) to HLA-A2 restricted CTL escape (targeting core residues 18–27) based on the general Korean population frequency of HLA-A2 ($53.9\%$), yet none of the 15 patients were HLA-typed!
* **Direct Population Sequencing Sensitivity Limit:** Direct cycle sequencing of uncloned PCR products loaded in `GGGGAAAATTTTCCCC` track format detects only majority/consensus variants ($>20\text{--}25\%$ of the viral quasispecies) and uses non-proofreading *Taq* polymerase, though the use of direct PCR product sequencing (rather than single-clone sequencing) avoids sporadic *Taq* incorporation errors.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim of Ethnic-Specific Mutational Clustering and Association of MHS1 with CAH Severity [Fragile / Overstated]:** Inferring ethnic-specific mutation clustering and disease-severity associations from 4 patients out of 7 in a cross-sectional snapshot of 15 individuals is statistically fragile. In chronic HBV infection, core mutations are more plausibly the *consequence* of prolonged immune-active hepatitis (repeated flares of CTL-mediated hepatocyte lysis in CAH selecting for escape variants over time) rather than the *cause* of severe liver damage—a reverse-causality problem that cross-sectional comparison of CPH (immune-tolerant or inactive) vs. CAH (immune-active) cannot disentangle.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Primer and Protocol Details:** The PCR and bidirectional cycle-sequencing primer sequences and nucleotide coordinates (P1–P4, M1–M5) are completely specified (lines 36, 42).
* **Missing Data and Accessions:** Table 1, Figure 1, and Figure 2 are omitted from the manuscript text, and the 15 HBV precore/core nucleotide sequences were not deposited in GenBank/EMBL/DDBJ, nor are the exact nucleotide/amino acid substitutions listed in the text.
