### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Complete Absence of Primary Data, Methods, and Sequence Tables:** The manuscript file lacks all experimental details, including FACS cell-surface markers used to define tonsillar GC B cells (e.g., CD38, CD77, PNA, sIgD), sorting purities, cell numbers input into RNA extraction, family-specific $V_H4$ leader/framework primer sequences, and the breakdown of how the 50 sequenced cDNA clones were distributed across the six B-cell subsets (tonsillar GC, tonsillar $\text{IgM}^+\text{IgD}^+$, PB $\text{CD5}^+\text{IgM}^+$, PB $\text{CD5}^-\text{IgM}^+$, PB $\text{CD5}^+\text{IgG}^+$, PB $\text{CD5}^-\text{IgG}^+$). Dividing 50 total clones across 4–6 B-cell compartments yields an average of only $\sim 8\text{–}12$ clones per subset.
- **Unverifiable Age-Trend Claim from an $N=1$ Subject:** In the Abstract (line 7), the authors state that *"somatically mutated memory B cells which have switched isotype accumulate in the PB already at young age. Their frequency seems to increase with age."* Drawing a conclusion about age-dependent accumulation from $N=1$ four-year-old child (without an adult or infant control cohort sequenced in parallel under identical PCR/cloning conditions) is statistically unsupported within the study design.
- **Potential Confounding by PCR Error, Clonal Amplification Bias, and Unmapped Germline Polymorphism:** When amplifying bulk cDNA from small B-cell subsets, *Taq* polymerase errors ($\sim 10^{-4}\text{–}10^{-3}\text{/bp/duplication}$) can mimic low-level somatic mutation, and dominant B-cell clones or plasmablasts with higher mRNA transcript abundance can skew the apparent germline gene usage (`35/50` clones in `VH4.21`, `V71-4`, and `3D279D`) unless unique VDJ rearrangements (CDR3 clonotypes) and donor-specific germline $V_H4$ alleles are explicitly distinguished. None of these controls can be audited in the missing text.
