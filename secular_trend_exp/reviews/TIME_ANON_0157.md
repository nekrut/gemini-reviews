### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This clinicopathological and molecular oncology study screens 46 primary vaginal carcinomas (collected 1973–1994 at a single referral center; 41 squamous cell carcinomas, 3 adenocarcinomas, 2 other; mean age 66 years) for *TP53* alterations using formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) immunohistochemistry (polyclonal CM1 antiserum and monoclonal PAb 1801) and constant denaturant gel electrophoresis (CDGE) / denaturing gradient gel electrophoresis (DGGE) across the four conserved regions of exons 5–8 ($n = 27$ tumors with sufficient DNA). Nuclear TP53 accumulation was detected in 22/46 (48%) tumors with CM1 and 15/46 (33%) with PAb 1801. CDGE/DGGE identified *TP53* mutations in 6/27 (22%) cases (4 in exon 5, 2 in exon 8), with 70% concordance (19/27) between IHC and mutation status. Overall, 23/46 (50%) vaginal carcinomas harbored a *TP53* alteration, with no statistically significant association with FIGO stage ($P = 0.41$) or 5-year cancer-related survival (47% vs. 42%, log-rank $P = 0.95$).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
#### STRENGTHS
- **Rare Tumor Cohort & Dual-Platform Screening:** Primary vaginal carcinoma represents only 1–2% of gynecological malignancies; assembling 46 primary cases with long-term survival follow-up (median 43 months, range 1–214 months) and comparing two distinct antibodies (polyclonal CM1 vs. monoclonal PAb 1801) alongside physical melting-gel mutation screening (CDGE confirmed by perpendicular DGGE) is well-structured.
- **Internal Table & Concordance Consistency:** Table I and Table II numbers cross-check accurately: 22/46 (47.8%) CM1-positive (patients 1–5, 7–23) plus 1 CM1-negative/mutation-positive case (patient 6, exon 5) yields exactly 23/46 (50.0%) cases with *TP53* alterations. Among the 27 CDGE-screened cases (patients 1–13 and 24–37), 5 are IHC+/Mut+ (patients 1–5), 14 are IHC-/Mut- (patients 24–37), 1 is IHC-/Mut+ (patient 6), and 7 are IHC+/Mut- (patients 7–13), giving $(5 + 14)/27 = 19/27 = 70.4\%$ concordance.
- **Proportional Clinical Conclusions:** The authors avoid over-interpreting the non-significant trend across FIGO stages (38%, 47%, 56%, 80%; $\chi^2\ P = 0.41$) or survival ($P = 0.95$), explicitly noting the limited statistical power due to sample size.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Failure of Direct Sequencing in 5 of 6 CDGE/DGGE-Positive FFPE Tumors:** Direct solid-phase dideoxy sequencing succeeded in only 1 of the 6 mutation-positive tumors (Case 2, fresh-frozen tissue, codon 280 $\text{AGA} \rightarrow \text{ACA}$, Arg $\rightarrow$ Thr), failing in the remaining 5 FFPE-derived mutants due to DNA degradation/template scarcity. Consequently, the exact nucleotide/amino acid substitutions (missense vs. nonsense/frameshift or silent polymorphism) in Cases 1 and 3–6 (especially IHC-negative Case 6) remain unverified.
- **Incomplete Genomic Coverage of *TP53*:** PCR-CDGE screened only four short fragments covering codons 128–153 (exon 5A), 155–185 (exon 5B), 237–253 (exon 7C), and 265–301 (exon 8D), entirely omitting exon 6 (a major conserved hotspot region, e.g., codons 190–225), exons 2–4, exons 9–11, and splice junctions.
- **Missing Primary Data on HPV Co-Infection:** In the Discussion, the authors state that "In 11 of 14 cases with TP53 alteration, HPV 16 was also detected (unpublished findings)" to argue against an inverse HPV–*TP53* relationship, yet no HPV detection methods, controls, or wild-type comparison rates are presented in the paper.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Nuclear TP53 protein accumulation occurs in 48% (22/46) of primary vaginal carcinomas by CM1 IHC (33% by PAb 1801), and exons 5/7/8 CDGE/DGGE mutations occur in 22% (6/27). `[Well Supported]` by Table II and Figs. 1–2.
- **Claim 2:** *TP53* alterations are not significantly associated with FIGO stage ($P = 0.41$) or 5-year cancer-related survival ($P = 0.95$) in this cohort. `[Well Supported]` by $\chi^2$ and Kaplan-Meier/log-rank analysis.
- **Claim 3:** There is no inverse relationship between HPV DNA and *TP53* alterations in vaginal carcinomas. `[Fragile]`—relies on unpublished data (11/14 cases) without reporting HPV prevalence in the *TP53*-wild-type group or assay methodology.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- CDGE/DGGE denaturant concentrations (45.5%/55% for 5A, 55% for 5B, 49.5% for 7C and 8D), IHC dilutions (CM1 1:300, PAb 1801 1:100), and patient-level results (Table II) are clearly reported, whereas primer sequences are referenced to Børresen et al. and 5 of 6 mutations lack sequence determination.
