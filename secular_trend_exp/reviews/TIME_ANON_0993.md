### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This virology and membrane biophysics study investigates the mechanism by which the nonenveloped minor-group human rhinovirus serotype 2 (HRV2) penetrates late endosomal membranes during uncoating, contrasting two competing models: bulk endosomal lysis/disruption (exemplified by adenovirus) versus size-selective transmembrane pore formation. The authors establish a cell-free in vitro endosome-release assay in which isolated endosomes loaded in vivo with co-internalized biotinylated dextrans of two distinct molecular masses (10 kD and 70 kD) are challenged with viruses or viral amphipathic peptides at acidic vs. neutral pH. Validating the assay with an influenza hemagglutinin HA2-derived fusogenic peptide (GALA-INF3), they demonstrate that while human adenovirus induces low-pH-dependent release of up to 46% of internalized biotin-dextran *without* size selectivity (releasing both 10-kD and 70-kD dextrans equally, consistent with membrane lysis), HRV2 induces release of 27% of the 10-kD biotin-dextran but only trace amounts of the 70-kD dextran. A synthetic amphipathic N-terminal peptide of HRV2 capsid protein VP1 recapitulates this membrane-permeabilizing activity in vitro. Together, these findings provide direct quantitative evidence that HRV2 uncoating proceeds via a size-selective endosomal membrane pore mediated by the externalized N-terminus of VP1 rather than wholesale endosome lysis. Although the full text is locked in an unextracted PDF (1.5 MB), the quantitative results and controls in the abstract are well-conceived and convincing.

### STRENGTHS
- **Elegant Size-Selective Endosomal Dextran Release Assay:** Co-internalizing 10-kD (Stokes radius ~2.3 nm) and 70-kD (Stokes radius ~6.0 nm) biotin-dextrans into isolated functional endosomes provides a direct physical sieve to distinguish finite pore formation from catastrophic membrane rupture.
- **Built-in Positive Controls for Both Mechanisms:** Comparing HRV2 side-by-side against adenovirus (a classic endosome-disrupting nonenveloped virus that releases up to 46% of both 10-kD and 70-kD dextrans non-selectively) and GALA-INF3 (a pore-forming/fusogenic peptide that shows size-dependent release) anchors the interpretation quantitatively.
- **Mechanistic Dissection of the Capsid Effector Domain (VP1 N-Terminus):** Demonstrating that a synthetic peptide corresponding to the amphipathic N-terminal region of VP1 (which externalizes during the low-pH conversion of native 150S HRV2 virions to 135S subviral A-particles) mimics the activity in isolated endosomes links viral structural transitions to membrane pore formation.

### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Archival Text Completeness Caveat:** The main text of the article is in an unextracted PDF ("The Full Text of this article is available as a PDF (1.5 MB)"), leaving only the Abstract and 59 References in the markdown file. Thus, the exact amino acid length and concentration of the VP1 N-terminal peptide, whether the isolated VP1 peptide retains the same strict 10-kD vs. 70-kD size selectivity as intact HRV2 virions, pH titration curves, and replicate error bars cannot be verified directly from the text stream.
- **WIN-Compound / Neutral-pH / Bafilomycin Specificity Controls:** To confirm that the 27% release of 10-kD dextran by HRV2 is strictly triggered by the low-pH conformational transition of the capsid in late endosomes (rather than mechanical fragility of isolated endosomes during centrifugation/incubation), essential controls include testing HRV2 pre-stabilized with capsid-binding WIN compounds (Gruenberger et al., cited in references), heat-inactivated/empty capsids, or neutral-pH buffers where V-ATPase is inhibited by bafilomycin A1.
- **Endosome Subpopulation Heterogeneity:** Only 27% of 10-kD dextran (and up to 46% for adenovirus) is released; this upper bound likely reflects the fraction of dextran-containing endosomes that also co-internalized sufficient virus particles and acidified to the threshold pH ($\text{pH} \le 5.6$ for HRV2), which should be accounted for when interpreting fractional release percentages.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Adenovirus induces low-pH-dependent release of up to 46% of co-internalized biotin-dextran from isolated endosomes without significant size selectivity between 10-kD and 70-kD dextrans. `[Well Supported]` — Directly matches the known penton-base/pVI-mediated endosomal lysis mechanism of adenovirus.
- **Claim 2:** HRV2 mediates low-pH-dependent release of 27% of 10-kD dextran but only traces of 70-kD dextran, supporting a size-selective pore-forming mechanism for HRV2 uncoating rather than bulk membrane disruption. `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by the differential molecular-weight sieving data.
- **Claim 3:** An amphipathic N-terminal peptide of HRV2 capsid protein VP1 mediates endosomal release in the in vitro system, substantiating a role for VP1 in HRV2 uncoating. `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by the peptide assay, though free synthetic amphipathic peptides at high micromolar concentrations can also induce carpet-like membrane destabilization unless shown to preserve the same size selectivity at physiological stoichiometry.
