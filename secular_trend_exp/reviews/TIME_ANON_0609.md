### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This translational study investigates the role of the CD95 (APO-1/Fas) receptor and CD95 ligand (CD95L) system in human hepatocyte apoptosis and clinical liver disease. First, using primary human hepatocytes isolated from surgical resections, the authors demonstrate that low concentrations (10–100 ng/ml) of agonistic anti-APO-1 monoclonal antibody trigger rapid apoptosis in >95% of freshly plated hepatocytes within 4–7.5 h (verified by phase-contrast blebbing, trypan blue exclusion curves, DAPI nuclear fragmentation, and internucleosomal DNA laddering on agarose gels), whereas control anti-influenza mAb has no effect. Second, in a cohort of 35 explanted human livers—normal liver ($n = 5$), alcoholic cirrhosis ($n = 13$), chronic HBV cirrhosis ($n = 9$), and acute/subacute liver failure ($n = 8$)—immunohistochemistry reveals low constitutive CD95 receptor expression in normal (mean grade 0.8) and alcoholic cirrhotic (1.15) livers but marked upregulation in HBV cirrhosis (2.45) and acute liver failure (2.5). Third, radioactive ($^{35}\text{S}$) RNA in situ hybridization demonstrates that CD95L mRNA localizes to infiltrating periportal lymphocytes in HBV cirrhosis, whereas in alcoholic cirrhosis CD95L mRNA is expressed directly inside hepatocytes within cirrhotic nodules, suggesting paracrine/autocrine or "fratricidal" hepatocyte death.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS, WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
**Strengths:**
* **Well-Controlled Primary Human Hepatocyte Apoptosis Assays:** Uses primary human hepatocytes (rather than transformed hepatoma lines, which often lose sensitivity or alter death pathways), includes an isotype/irrelevant mAb negative control at matched concentrations, quantifies time-course viability across triplicate wells (mean ± SD, Fig. 2), and confirms true apoptotic death via both nuclear morphology (DAPI, Fig. 3) and biochemical internucleosomal DNA cleavage (Fig. 4).
* **Discovery of Culture-Duration Dedifferentiation Effect:** Demonstrates that hepatocyte sensitivity to 20 ng/ml anti-APO-1 drops from >95% at 12 h post-plating (Fig. 2a) to ~60% at 48 h post-plating (Fig. 2b), an important technical control for in vitro hepatocyte studies.
* **Cold-Competitor Specificity Control in RNA In Situ Hybridization:** Validates the $^{35}\text{S}$-labeled antisense CD95L riboprobe (aa 100–246) by showing signal ablation upon co-hybridization with a 20-fold molar excess of unlabeled competitor antisense RNA.

**Weaknesses & Methodological Audit:**
* **Small Subsample Size for CD95L In Situ Hybridization ($n = 3$ per group):** Although the immunohistochemistry cohort comprises 35 livers (Table 2), the CD95L mRNA in situ hybridization was only performed on 3 normal livers, 3 HBV cirrhosis cases, 3 alcoholic cirrhosis cases, and 3 acute liver failure cases (of which 2 had tissue destruction too advanced for morphological analysis, leaving only $n = 1$ evaluable acute LF case, Case 4).
* **Subjective Ordinal Immunohistological Grading Without Blinding or Non-Parametric Testing (Table 2):** CD95 staining was graded on a 0–3 ordinal scale (`0 = none, 1 = weak, 2 = intermediate, 3 = high`) and summarized as parametric means (`0.8, 1.15, 2.45, 2.5` with `SE < 0.4`) without stating whether slides were scored by blinded observers or performing formal non-parametric hypothesis tests (e.g., Kruskal-Wallis / Mann-Whitney U test).
* **Lack of Sense-Strand Riboprobe Control and Protein-Level CD95L Verification:** While cold antisense competition reduces hybridization signal, a $^{35}\text{S}$-labeled sense-strand RNA probe is the standard control for non-specific background binding to necrotic/cholestatic liver parenchyma; furthermore, demonstrating functional CD95L protein or soluble CD95L release by ethanol-exposed hepatocytes would strengthen the "fratricide" hypothesis beyond mRNA detection.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Primary human hepatocytes are acutely sensitive to CD95/APO-1 receptor cross-linking in vitro: [Well Supported].** Conclusively demonstrated by dose-response, time-course (12 h vs. 48 h plating), DAPI nuclear morphology, and DNA laddering.
* **CD95 receptor is upregulated on hepatocytes in HBV cirrhosis and acute liver failure, accompanied by CD95L mRNA in infiltrating lymphocytes: [Well Supported].** Consistent across 8/9 HBV cirrhosis and 8/8 acute LF cases by IHC and corroborated by periportal lymphocytic ISH signal.
* **In alcoholic cirrhosis, hepatocyte death occurs via autocrine/paracrine or fratricidal hepatocyte-expressed CD95L: [Moderately Supported].** The observation of silver grains over hepatocytes in 3/3 alcoholic cirrhosis nodules (Fig. 6b–c) is striking and carefully framed by the authors as a hypothesis (*"might occur by fratricide"*), though direct functional demonstration of hepatocyte-to-hepatocyte CD95L killing is not performed.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Protocol Detail:** Hepatocyte perfusion/isolation buffers, hormone supplements, mAb concentrations, cryosection fixation, avidin-biotin blocking (crucial for endogenous liver biotin), and $^{35}\text{S}$ ISH exposure protocols are thoroughly described.
* **Patient Cohort Documentation:** Clinical diagnostic criteria for alcoholic cirrhosis, chronic HBV, and acute/subacute/hyperacute liver failure (including individual case etiologies in Table 1) are clearly specified.
