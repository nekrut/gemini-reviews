### 1. Executive Summary
This study conducts a genome-wide association study (GWAS) for milk protein percentage in a two-breed Romanian dairy cattle cohort ($N = 313$ post-QC cows: $n = 271$ dual-purpose Romanian Simmental and $n = 42$ Romanian Brown) maintained at a single research station (`RDSCB Arad`). Samples were genotyped on the Axiom Bovine Genotyping v3 Array (`>63,000` markers) and phenotyped by Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (`CombiFoss FT+`) for 305-day lactation protein percentage (`mean = 3.43% ± 0.25%`, range `2.45%–4.34%`). Following quality control (`MAF >= 0.01`, SNP call rate `>=95%`, sample call rate `>=90%`, `HWE p >= 1e-6`) and LD pruning (`--indep-pairwise 50 5 0.5`), 33,531 autosomal SNPs were tested using ordinary least squares linear regression (`PLINK v1.9 --linear`) adjusting for breed and the first 5 principal components (`PC1–PC5`). No SNP reached the standard genome-wide Bonferroni threshold (`p < 1.49e-6`, i.e., $0.05/33,531$); two SNPs exceeded the suggestive $1/N_{\text{SNP}}$ threshold (`p < 2.98e-5`): `AX-115105679` on `BTA3` (`p = 6.32e-6`, $\beta = -0.340 \pm 0.074$, intronic in `C7orf61`) and `AX-124380115` on `BTA3` (`p = 2.57e-5`, $\beta = -0.335 \pm 0.078$, upstream of `SLC1A7`), with 9 additional markers reaching $p < 10^{-4}$ (`Table 1`).

Methodologically, the study has three significant statistical design weaknesses: (1) an underpowered sample size ($N = 313$ across two unequally sized breeds) for a highly polygenic trait; (2) use of standard OLS linear regression with 5 PCs (`PLINK --linear`) rather than a linear mixed model incorporating a genomic relationship matrix (GRM, e.g., `GCTA-MLMA` or `GEMMA`), leaving substantial residual genomic inflation ($\lambda = 1.124$) due to unmodeled half-sib sire families within a single institutional herd; and (3) applying aggressive LD pruning ($r^2 > 0.5$) *to the GWAS marker set itself* rather than solely for PCA computation, thereby discarding ~47% of the genotyped array markers (potentially including `CSN1S1`/`CSN2`/`CSN3` casein cluster or `DGAT1` tags). However, the manuscript stands out for its exceptional scientific honesty: the authors explicitly report $\lambda = 1.124$, explain why `C7orf61` (a testis-specific spermatogenesis gene) and `SLC1A7` are likely statistical false positives driven by small sample size and residual cryptic relatedness, and deposit complete summary statistics on Zenodo (`10.5281/zenodo.17458734`).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Unmodeled Cryptic Relatedness and Genomic Inflation ($\lambda = 1.124$):**
  - In dairy cattle populations—especially when all 313 cows are sampled from a single research station (`RDSCB Arad`)—widespread paternal half-sibships and pedigree loops from artificial insemination bulls create strong familial relatedness.
  - Adjusting only for a binary breed indicator and `PC1–PC5` in an ordinary linear model (`PLINK --linear`) captures coarse breed divergence (`PC1 = 23.02%`) but fails to control fine-scale family structure, resulting in an inflated genomic inflation factor of $\lambda = 1.124$ (`Figure 5`).
  - Standard quantitative genetics practice requires fitting a linear mixed model (LMM) with a leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) genomic relationship matrix (`GCTA --mlma-loco` or `GEMMA`) to properly calibrate Type I error in livestock cohorts.
  - Indeed, if one applies genomic control correction ($\chi^2_{\text{GC}} = \chi^2 / 1.124$) to the top SNP `AX-115105679` ($t \approx -4.595$, $\chi^2 \approx 20.39$), the GC-adjusted statistic drops to $\chi^2_{\text{GC}} \approx 18.14$ ($p_{\text{GC}} \approx 2.05 \times 10^{-5}$), weakening its suggestive signal by more than threefold.
- **Misapplication of LD Pruning to the Discovery Marker Set:**
  - The authors pruned the post-QC dataset with `--indep-pairwise 50 5 0.5`, retaining 33,531 SNPs, and ran the GWAS only on those 33,531 pruned SNPs. While LD pruning is necessary to obtain independent markers for PCA (`--pca`), pruning the GWAS scan itself at $r^2 = 0.5$ removes almost half the array and degrades tagging power at major milk protein QTLs (such as the `BTA6` casein cluster and `BTA14` `DGAT1` region), as the authors candidly acknowledge in Section 4.4 (`line 151`).
- **Significance Thresholds and Allele Coding Anomaly in `Table 1`:**
  - In `Methods` (`line 91`) and `Results` (`line 125`), the authors label $p = 1/33,531 = 2.98 \times 10^{-5}$ (`-log10(p) = 4.53`) as the *"genome-wide significance threshold"* and $p = 1 \times 10^{-4}$ (`-log10(p) = 4.00`) as the *"suggestive significance threshold."* Under Lander & Kruglyak / Bonferroni standards, $1/M$ ($2.98 \times 10^{-5}$, where 1 false positive is expected per genome scan under the null) is the **suggestive** threshold, while $0.05/M = 1.49 \times 10^{-6}$ is the Bonferroni **genome-wide** threshold (which no SNP in this study reaches).
  - In `Table 1`, row 7 (`AX-185118865` on `BTA7`, pos `32,202,913`, $p = 5.36 \times 10^{-5}$) lists `EA/OA` as `C/C` (identical Effect Allele and Other Allele), which the text (`line 134`) notes as an unresolved *"ambiguous allele assignment"* from the array manifest/PLINK export.
- **Covariate Omission (Parity, Calving Season, Age):**
  - Only `Breed` and `PC1–PC5` were included as covariates (`Equation 1`); parity (lactation number), calving season/year, and days in milk adjustments beyond the 305-day standardization were not included in the fixed-effects model.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *"Notably, our top association lies within C7orf61, a gene with no established biological role in lactation and expression restricted to testis tissue... We therefore consider it more likely that the C7orf61 signal represents either a false positive arising from our limited sample size and residual population stratification (λ = 1.124) or LD with an uncharacterized regulatory element, rather than a direct functional effect of C7orf61 on milk protein synthesis."* `[Well Supported]`
  - The authors' self-critical evaluation of their top hits (`C7orf61` and `SLC1A7`), including their explicit discussion of Winner's Curse inflation of effect sizes ($\beta \approx -0.34\%$, or `~1.36` phenotypic SDs) and absence of canonical `BTA6`/`BTA14` peaks, is exemplary and entirely proportional to the data.
- **Claim 2:** *"Ten suggestive associations were identified across BTA2, BTA3, BTA7, BTA8, BTA10, BTA12, BTA14, and BTA24, implicating genes involved in growth factor signaling (FGF12, EPHA5), calcium signaling (SLIT2, CACNB2,RYR2), cell adhesion (PCDH9), and stress response (DNAJC3)."* `[Fragile]`
  - At $p < 10^{-4}$ with $\lambda = 1.124$ across 33,531 tests, roughly $33,531 \times 10^{-4} \approx 3.4$ hits are expected under a calibrated null and ~8–10 hits under $\lambda = 1.124$ inflation alone. Thus, the 11 total loci at $p < 10^{-4}$ (`Table 1`) do not exceed the global null expectation once $\lambda = 1.124$ is accounted for.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Complete GWAS summary statistics for all 33,531 tested SNPs are publicly deposited on Zenodo (`https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17458734`).
- **Pipeline Transparency:** All `PLINK v1.9` QC flags, PCA settings, R packages (`qqman`, `ggplot2`), genome build (`ARS-UCD1.2` / `Ensembl Release 110`), and AI code assistance disclosures are clearly documented. Re-running the full unpruned marker panel (~60k SNPs) using a GRM-based linear mixed model (`GCTA-MLMA` or `GEMMA`) would substantially strengthen the paper and resolve the $\lambda = 1.124$ inflation.
