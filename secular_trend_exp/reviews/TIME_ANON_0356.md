### 1. Executive Summary
This brief methodological and population genetics paper extends the $\beta^{(1)}$ summary statistic for detecting long-term balancing selection (which measures the local build-up of polymorphic sites at allele frequencies closely matching a core SNP, $\hat{\theta}_\beta - \hat{\theta}_W$) in two ways:
1. It introduces $\beta^{(2)} = \hat{\theta}_\beta - \hat{\theta}_D$, replacing Watterson's polymorphism estimator $\hat{\theta}_W$ with a divergence-based mutation rate estimator $\hat{\theta}_D = \frac{D}{T/(2N_e) + 1/n}$ derived from the count of fixed substitutions $D$ relative to an outgroup species, thereby capturing both the excess of intermediate-frequency linked polymorphisms and the deficit of fixed substitutions characteristic of ancient balancing selection (analogous to combining $\beta^{(1)}$ with an HKA-like signal).
2. It derives analytical closed-form variances for $\beta^{(1)}$, $\beta^{(1)*}$ (folded), and $\beta^{(2)}$ under neutrality (building on Achaz's SFS covariance framework), enabling variance-standardized statistics $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(1)}$, $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(1)*}$, and $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(2)}$ that are robust to local window size, sample size, and mutation rate heterogeneity.

The authors benchmark these statistics using forward-in-time SLiM 2 simulations against NCD2, $\text{NCD}_{\text{mid}}$, $T_1$, $T_2$, Tajima's $D$, and HKA, apply $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(2)}$ to 1000 Genomes Project populations (YRI, CEU, CHB), validate enrichment at human–chimpanzee trans-species haplotypes ($p = 1.08 \times 10^{-14}$), GTEx eQTLs, and GWAS Catalog hits, and highlight two high-scoring missense variants (`rs17856650`, `rs17856651`) in *ACSBG2*. The software (`BetaScan2`) is released open-source on GitHub.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Mathematical Formulation of $\beta^{(2)}$ and Variance Standardization:**
  * Equation (1) defines $\hat{\theta}_D = \frac{D}{T/(2N_e) + 1/n}$, which properly accounts for the expected neutral coalescence time in the ancestral population plus the sample correction $1/n$ (such that $E[D] = \theta [T/(2N_e) + 1/n]$ under neutrality, making $E[\beta^{(2)}] = E[\hat{\theta}_\beta - \hat{\theta}_D] = 0$).
  * In Equation (3), the standardized statistic is written as $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(2)} = \frac{\hat{\theta}_\beta - \hat{\theta}_D}{\sqrt{\text{Var}[\hat{\theta}_\beta] + \text{Var}[\hat{\theta}_D]}}$ (note that the square root symbol was lost in the plain-text XML rendering of Equation 3, though correctly derived in the supplement). This variance decomposition assumes $\text{Cov}(\hat{\theta}_\beta, \hat{\theta}_D) = 0$. While conditional on a fixed genealogy $T_{\text{MRCA}}$, higher polymorphism (longer internal branches) is negatively correlated with fixed substitutions before speciation, across an unlinked or recombining window with speciation time $T \gg 2N_e$, treating $\text{Cov}(\hat{\theta}_\beta, \hat{\theta}_D) \approx 0$ under the standard neutral model is a reasonable analytical approximation, which the authors verify against coalescent/forward simulations (Supplementary Figs 1–2).
  * Note a typographical error in the final sentence of Materials and Methods (line 62): *"We recommend $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(1)*}$ in cases without outgroup data to polarize ancestral states, $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(1)*}$ when ancestral states are known but no informative outgroup is available to call substitutions..."* — the second instance should read $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(1)}$ (unfolded), not $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(1)*}$ (folded). Also, the Abstract is printed twice at the top of the manuscript (lines 5–9).
* **Simulation Benchmarking & Empirical Validation:**
  * Power is evaluated via partial ROC AUC over $\text{FPR} \in [0, 0.05]$ across 1,000 bootstrapped SLiM 2 replicate sets, testing equilibrium frequencies ($0.25, 0.50, 0.75$), 2-fold mutation rate heterogeneity ($\mu = 2.5 \times 10^{-8}$ vs. $1.25 \times 10^{-8}$), recombination rates, and $N_e$. $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(2)}$ matches the composite likelihood ratio test $T_2$ without requiring computationally expensive demographic/grid simulations, and substantially outperforms NCD2, HKA, and Tajima's $D$.
  * The authors transparently report a key limitation on real data: when rare variants are included, $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(2)}$ shows lower mean percentile enrichment on human–chimpanzee trans-species haplotypes than $T_2$ (0.64 vs. 0.72) because $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(2)}$ relies on the theoretical neutral SFS variance rather than the empirical genome-wide demographic SFS used by $T_2$. Filtering out low-MAF core SNPs largely resolves this sensitivity.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *"$\beta^{(2)}$ has higher power than either $\beta^{(1)}$ statistic, with the greatest gain in power at equilibrium frequencies 25% and 75%... When there is mutation rate variation across simulations, we find that standardization improves power."* — **[Well Supported]**. Demonstrated by the partial ROC AUC benchmarks in Figure 1A–B; at asymmetric equilibrium frequencies (0.25/0.75), polymorphism frequency clustering alone has lower signal than at 0.50, so incorporating the deficit of outgroup substitutions ($\hat{\theta}_D$) provides substantial orthogonal power.
* **Claim 2:** *"SNPs in trans-species haplotypes... had a significantly higher $\beta_{\text{std}}^{(2)}$ value than SNPs which are not (Mann-Whitney U P-value = $1.08 \times 10^{-14}$)."* — **[Well Supported]**. Provides strong empirical validation on known ancient balanced loci (while Note: because linked SNPs within the same trans-species haplotype window are non-independent due to LD, a locus-level permutation or block-bootstrap $p$-value would be more conservative than a SNP-level Mann–Whitney U test).
* **Claim 3:** *"Two missense SNPs in the gene ACSBG2, rs17856650 and rs17856651, have standardized $\beta$ scores in the top 99.9 percentile in the CEU population..."* — **[Moderately Supported]**. *ACSBG2* is a compelling statistical candidate (top 0.1% in CEU, top 0.5% in CHB, top 1% in YRI, and concordant with $T_2$), though functional validation of balancing selection on spermatogenesis/fatty acid metabolism remains a hypothesis for future experimental work.
