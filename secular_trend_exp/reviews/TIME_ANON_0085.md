### 1. Executive Summary
This is a comprehensive, meticulously executed molecular cloning, genetic mapping, and cell biological characterization of mouse `Ank3` (encoding epithelial ankyrin / ankyrin-G), a third member of the mammalian ankyrin gene family distinct from erythroid `Ank1` and brain `Ank2`. By screening two mouse kidney cDNA libraries (`lambda gt11` and `lambda ZAPII`) and performing RT-PCR across overlapping clones, the authors assemble the complete 7,084-bp coding and untranslated sequence of full-length kidney `Ank3` (`Ank3-7kb`, encoding a 1,961-amino-acid, 214-kDa polypeptide containing 24 N-terminal ankyrin repeats, a spectrin-binding domain, and a C-terminal regulatory domain; GenBank `L40631`) as well as a major truncated 5,074-bp isoform class (`Ank3-5kb`, GenBank `L40632`) that utilizes an alternative 5' untranslated exon to initiate translation directly at the spectrin-binding domain, lacking the entire 24-repeat membrane-binding domain. Using two independent interspecific mouse backcross panels ($n=77$ `(BALB/cJ x CAST/Ei)F1 x BALB/cJ` and $n=94$ `(C57BL/6J x M. spretus)F1 x M. spretus`; MGD `MGD-CREX-228`), they map `Ank3` to the mid-region of mouse Chromosome 10 near `Bcr` and `Ros1`, syntenic with human `10q21`. Finally, using domain-specific Northern blots (`probes 1–4` and `PCR1`), affinity-purified regulatory-domain antibodies (`antiAnk3-R1`, `antiAnk3-R2`) with 200-fold molar excess fusion-protein competition controls, a splice-insert B peptide antibody (`antiAnk3-B`), and tissue immunocytochemistry, they map the tissue-specific isoform architecture (`215/200, 170, 120, 105 kDa`) across epithelia (basolateral renal tubules, airway, pancreas, gut, skin), myocytes (cardiac intercalated discs, skeletal muscle triads), neuronal axon initial segments, megakaryocytes, and testicular Leydig cells.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Genetic Mapping Rigor (Fig. 2):** Chromosomal localization of `Ank3` on mouse Chromosome 10 is independently replicated across two community interspecific backcross panels ($n=77$ CAST/Ei backcross mice and $n=94$ *M. spretus* `BSS` backcross mice), with exact recombination fractions and standard errors (`e.g., D10Mit3 to Ank3: 1/77 = 1.3 ± 1.3 cM`; `Ank3 to D10Mit4: 2/77 = 2.6 ± 1.8 cM` in the CAST cross; cosegregation with `D10Mit20` and `D10Bir9` in the BSS cross) and public deposit of the raw typing data (`MGD-CREX-228`).
- **Domain-Specific Northern Blot Dissection of Alternative Splicing (Fig. 6):** To verify that the repeat-less cDNA clones (`pA3-2`, `pA3-13`, `pA3-15`) are genuine physiological transcripts rather than reverse-transcription truncation artifacts, the authors hybridize four domain-specific probes to mouse kidney mRNA: `probe 2` (repeat domain) labels only the `7.5` and `6.9 kb` mRNAs; `probe 3` (unique 5' UTR of the repeat-less clones) strongly labels the `6.3` and `5.7 kb` mRNAs; and `probe 4` (588-bp acidic `insert C` in the regulatory domain) selectively labels the `7.5, 6.9, 6.3, and 5.1 kb` species while sparing the `5.7 and 4.6 kb` transcripts. Furthermore, parallel Northern blots of kidney mRNA probed for `Ank1` and `Ank2` (even after 3-day overexposure, Fig. 6e) prove that `Ank3` is overwhelmingly the predominant ankyrin in kidney.
- **Immunoblot & Immunocytochemical Specificity Controls (Figs. 7–10):** Because earlier studies of non-erythroid ankyrins suffered from cross-reactivity of antibodies raised against conserved repeat/spectrin-binding domains of `Ank1` or `Ank2`, the authors raise `antiAnk3-R1` and `antiAnk3-R2` against a non-homologous C-terminal segment of the `Ank3` regulatory domain (`residues 1776–1943`, sharing $<19\%$ identity with `Ank1`/`Ank2`). Western blots are probed in the presence of a 200-fold molar excess of free GST (to eliminate anti-GST reactivity) and controlled against a 200-fold molar excess of purified `GST/Ank3` fusion protein, which completely abolishes the `215, 200, 170, 120, and 105 kDa` bands while leaving a single 70-kDa band marked explicitly as non-specific (`NS`, Fig. 7a). Cross-reactivity with `Ank1` and `Ank2` is further excluded by showing zero reactivity against erythrocyte ghosts (`Ank1`) and neuronal somata/Purkinje cell dendrites (`Ank2`).
- **Transparent Acknowledgment of Unresolved Isoform Details:** The authors are commended for explicitly noting where direct biochemical proof is still pending—specifically, that attempts to raise an antibody against the 6 unique N-terminal amino acids (`MALPHS`) of the predicted `Ank3-5kb` translation product or to N-terminally sequence the `120/105 kDa` renal membrane proteins were unsuccessful, and that the exact mRNA precursor of the `170 kDa` `insert B`-containing protein isoform remains to be defined.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *`Ank3` encodes a novel mammalian ankyrin on mouse Chromosome 10 that is the major ankyrin expressed in kidney and polarized epithelia.* `[Well Supported]` — Conclusively established by full-length cDNA cloning, two-panel interspecific backcross mapping, comparative Northern blots (`Ank1` vs. `Ank2` vs. `Ank3`), and basolateral epithelial immunolocalization correlating with known `Na/K-ATPase` distribution along the nephron.
- **Claim 2:** *`Ank3` undergoes extensive alternative splicing and alternative promoter/5'-UTR usage, generating full-length (`~215/200 kDa`) isoforms as well as truncated (`~120/105 kDa`) isoforms lacking the N-terminal 24-ankyrin-repeat domain.* `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by independent cDNA clones (`pA3-2, -13, -15`), unique 5'-UTR Northern hybridization (`probe 3`), and matching `120/105 kDa` polypeptide doublets in kidney, liver, and testis membranes.
- **Claim 3:** *In adult brain, `Ank3` (`215 kDa`) is selectively localized to neuronal axon initial segments and Purkinje cell axons, complementary to somatodendritic `Ank2`.* `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated by immunocytochemistry (Fig. 10a–b) and brain membrane immunoblotting (Fig. 7a).
