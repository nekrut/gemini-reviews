# Blinded Review: TIME_ANON_52
**Composite Score:** 3.9 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.0/3.5 | Claims: 1.1/2.5 | Biology: 1.0/2.0 | Reproducibility: 0.8/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Severe Ascertainment Bias & Mathematical/Statistical Reporting Errors
**Verdict:** Reject
**Study Modality:** Wet-Lab Primary Experimental
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
This single-center retrospective observational study examines the distribution of *MTHFR* C677T genotypes across TOAST ischemic stroke subtypes in 100 Hungarian patients aged $\le 50$ years, concluding that small vessel disease (SVD) is the most common subtype (52%) and is significantly associated with *MTHFR* 677TT homozygosity and hypertension. However, the study suffers from severe clinical ascertainment bias (retrospective inclusion of only patients ordered for *MTHFR* testing at physician discretion, with no stroke-free control group), omits measurements of plasma homocysteine or folate/B12 levels, and contains major mathematical and statistical errors—including reporting logistic regression log-odds coefficients ($\beta$) as Odds Ratios (resulting in point estimates that fall outside their own 95% confidence intervals) and inconsistent genotype counts across the Abstract, Methods, and Tables.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Mathematically Impossible Odds Ratios and Confidence Intervals (Confusing $\beta$ with $\text{OR}$):** In the Results (line 86), multivariate logistic regression for SVD-related stroke is reported as `hypertension (p = 0.002, OR 1.378, 95% CI 1.624–9.683)` and `MTHFR TT homozygosity (p = 0.014, OR 1.619, 95% CI 1.390–18.338)`. For hypertension, the reported point estimate (`1.378`) lies completely outside its own 95% confidence interval (`1.624–9.683`). Taking the natural logarithm of the confidence bounds reveals the exact error: $\frac{\ln(1.624) + \ln(9.683)}{2} = \frac{0.4849 + 2.2704}{2} = 1.3776 \approx 1.378$, meaning the true $\text{OR}$ is $\exp(1.378) = 3.967$ and `1.378` is the unexponentiated log-odds regression coefficient ($B$). The exact same error occurs for *MTHFR* TT homozygosity (also reported in the Abstract, line 11): $\frac{\ln(1.390) + \ln(18.338)}{2} = \frac{0.3293 + 2.9090}{2} = 1.6191 \approx 1.619$, meaning `1.619` is the log-odds coefficient $B$ and the true $\text{OR}$ is $\exp(1.619) = 5.048$.
* **Mutually Contradictory Sample Sizes Across Sections:** The total study size is stated as $N = 100$, but the genotype counts are reported differently in three places:
  1. Abstract (line 9): 21 TT + 45 CT + 35 CC = **101 patients** (with "65 patients had MTHFR C677T variants", yet $21 + 45 = 66$).
  2. Methods (line 30): 21 TT + 41 CT + 35 CC = **97 patients** (with "MTHFR C677T polymorphism was found in 65 out of 100 patients", yet $21 + 41 = 62$).
  3. Table 1 & Table 2 (lines 52, 67): 21 TT + 44 CT + 35 CC = **100 patients** ($21 + 44 = 65$).
* **Corrupted Table Entry and Unspecified Regression Coding:** In Table 2 (line 70), the SVD count for heterozygous patients is printed as `20 (45.4549.33%)`, a garbled concatenation of `45.45%` ($20/44$) and `49.33%`. In addition, the backward likelihood ratio logistic regression does not specify the reference category for the three-level *MTHFR* genotype predictor or how zero-count cells in Table 2 (0 TT homozygotes in "Other determined" and "Cryptogenic" stroke) affect subgroup comparisons.
* **Severe Selection and Referral Bias:** As noted in Methods (line 30), "there was no institutional protocol for the indication of MTHFR genotyping. It was left to the discretion of the treating physician." Because *MTHFR* testing in young stroke patients is typically ordered when conventional large-artery or cardioembolic causes are absent or when lacunar/cryptogenic stroke is suspected, restricting the cohort to patients who underwent discretionary genotyping induces strong selection bias.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Absence of Intermediate Biochemical Phenotypes (Homocysteine, Folate, Vitamin B12):** The biological rationale linking *MTHFR* C677T to small vessel disease operates entirely through elevated plasma homocysteine (hHcy). However, neither homocysteine, folate, nor vitamin B12 concentrations were measured in these patients.
* **Confounding by Hyperlipidemia and Diagnostic Modality:** In Table 1, wild-type (CC) patients had nearly double the prevalence of hyperlipidemia (68.57%) compared to CT (34.09%) and TT (38.09%) patients ($p = 0.007$), suggesting differential referral for genetic testing among patients lacking traditional lipid risk factors. Moreover, 15% of patients had only CT scans without MRI, and lacunar stroke was diagnosed even when no infarct was visible on imaging based on clinical syndrome and exclusion of other causes (line 30), risking misclassification between SVD and cryptogenic stroke.
* **Unsupported Clinical Recommendations:** Extrapolating from a retrospective, unblinded 100-patient case series without homocysteine measurements to recommend routine *MTHFR* C677T genetic screening and vitamin supplementation for secondary stroke prevention (line 111) overreaches the empirical evidence.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Insufficient Genotyping and Statistical Protocol Details:** The Laboratory Analysis section (line 37) consists of two sentences mentioning "real-time PCR with LightCycler probe system (Roche)" without primer/probe sequences, catalog numbers, or cycling conditions.
* **No Individual-Level Data or Full Regression Output:** Full regression tables (coefficients, standard errors, Wald statistics, and reference levels for all candidate covariates) and anonymized patient-level data are not provided.
