### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This methodological and population-genetic benchmark study investigates why standard SNP microarray GWAS and reference-panel imputation fail to capture the malaria-protective $-\alpha^{3.7\text{I}}$ $\alpha$-thalassaemia 3.7-kb deletion in East Africa, using $N=3,036$ children (1,432 severe *Plasmodium falciparum* malaria cases and 1,604 community controls) from Kilifi, Kenya typed both by direct gap-PCR for $-\alpha^{3.7}$ and on the Illumina HumanOmni2.5-4 array. Combining statistical phasing (`SHAPEIT2`, 178 flanking SNPs across 400 kb on chr16p), LD and Extended Haplotype Homozygosity (`rehh`) analyses, targeted Sanger sequencing of paralogous *HBA2*/*HBA1* differences in 25 $-\alpha/-\alpha$ homozygotes and 9 $\alpha\alpha/\alpha\alpha$ wild-type controls, 1,000-run bootstrap cross-validation of `IMPUTE2` using internal population reference panels ($n=100$ individuals / 200 chromosomes), and four microarray probe-intensity inference methods across 6 internal deletion probes (density trough cutoffs, hierarchical clustering, multinomial regression `MRM`, and `CART` decision trees), the authors demonstrate that: (1) while direct PCR typing confirms a strong protective association with severe malaria ($\text{aOR} = 0.78, p = 1.1 \times 10^{-5}$; homozygote $\text{aOR} = 0.59, p = 2.43 \times 10^{-5}$), zero of the 178 flanking SNPs reach significance ($p < 0.0003$) because maximum pairwise LD with $-\alpha^{3.7\text{I}}$ is $r^2 = 0.16$ ($5'$) and $r^2 = 0.081$ ($3'$); (2) Sanger sequencing confirms that all 25 deletion homozygotes carry the $-\alpha^{3.7\text{I}}$ subtype, but polymorphism at paralogous site 7 (`rs2362746` T/G) indicates recurrent independent origin across at least two distinct crossover breakpoints; and (3) neither internal-panel `IMPUTE2` nor trained probe-intensity models (`MRM`/`CART`) reliably recover the malaria association, with several intensity-based classifiers producing highly significant **inverted** (false-risk, $\text{OR} = 1.43\text{--}2.61, p < 10^{-8}$) associations. This is a thorough, self-critical, and highly reproducible forensic study.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
#### Strengths
* **Gold-Standard Ground Truth Across $N=3,036$ Individuals ($6,072$ Chromosomes):** Having direct gap-PCR genotypes for 1,139 $\alpha\alpha/\alpha\alpha$, 1,474 $-\alpha/\alpha\alpha$, and 423 $-\alpha/-\alpha$ children alongside Illumina 2.5M array calls and raw $X+Y$ channel intensities allows exact quantification of sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, and downstream epidemiological distortion.
* **Molecular Resolution of Recurrent Breakpoint Origins via Sanger Sequencing:** Sequencing 14 paralogous *HBA2* vs. *HBA1* diagnostic positions across 25 homozygotes and 9 WT controls (Table 3, Figure 2) explains *why* $-\alpha^{3.7\text{I}}$ sits on 452 distinct 400-kb haplotypes and 48 core 33-kb haplotypes distributed across the entire genealogy (Figures 8–9): recurrent non-allelic homologous recombination (NAHR) and gene conversion within the *HBA* cluster continuously place the deletion onto diverse ancestral backgrounds.
* **Crucial Demonstration of Downstream Association Reversal from Imperfect Intensity Classifiers:** Rather than stopping at classification accuracy metrics (which look deceptively acceptable for homozygotes at 85–99% specificity), the authors carry all inferred genotypes forward into the severe malaria logistic regression (adjusted for ethnicity, sex, and sickle-cell `rs334`). Table 5 and Figure 13 reveal that hierarchical clustering, `MRM`, and `CART` on probe intensities frequently flip the protective effect ($\text{OR} = 0.78$) into a spurious risk effect ($\text{OR} = 1.43\text{--}2.61, p = 10^{-8}\text{ to }10^{-15}$), providing a cautionary lesson against unvalidated CNV intensity calling.

#### Weaknesses & Methodological Audit
* **Identical Forward and Reverse Primer Sequences Listed in Table 2:** In Table 2 (line 82 and line 87), the primer sequence for `A2/3.7-F` (FWD, `16:221882-221901`) and `3.7-R` (REV, `16:227698-227718`) are both printed as `CCCCTCGCCAAGTCCACCC` (a copy-paste error in Table 2; fortunately, Table 3 line 138 prints the actual `3.7-R` sequence `CGCTGGACCCTAGAGTGCTTT`, and Table 3 line 134 prints `A2-R` as `AGACCAGGAAGGGCCGGTG` vs. its reverse complement `AAAGCACTCTAGGGTCCAGCG` in Table 2).
* **Table 3 Header vs. Text Count Mismatch and Duplicated Columns:** The caption of Table 3 states "in 25 homozygous $\alpha\text{-3.7 kb}$ and **5 normal** individuals", whereas the Methods (lines 94–100), Results (line 19), and Table 3 columns (`A` through `I`) actually include **9 normal** (wild-type) individuals. Additionally, in the top two header rows of Table 3 (`Released_key` and `Chip_id`), the 9 control IDs (`520269712` through `138218129`) are listed twice because columns `A–I` are shown first for *HBA2* and then for *HBA1*, which should be labeled clearly in the column spanners.
* **Table 4 Formatting where PPV is Duplicated from Sensitivity:** In Table 4 (lines 42–56), for all three genotype classes (`Wild-Type Homozygote`, `-α -3.7I Heterozygote`, `-α -3.7I Homozygote`), the 4th subcolumn (`PPV`) contains the exact same numbers as the 1st subcolumn (`sensitivity`) in every row (e.g., Median 70% threshold: WT sensitivity `0.770`, PPV `0.770`; Het sensitivity `0.776`, PPV `0.776`; Homo sensitivity `0.755`, PPV `0.755`), whereas Table 5 reports the actual PPVs (`87%`, `83%`, `94%`). This indicates a script indexing bug when generating Table 4 where the sensitivity column was pasted twice instead of the PPV column.
* **Use of Unnormalized Summed Raw Intensities ($X+Y$) Rather than Log R Ratio (LRR) and B-Allele Frequency (BAF):** The intensity models used raw $X + Y$ channel sums without correcting for sample-level DNA quantity/hybridization wave effects or normalizing against reference cluster centroids (standard `PennCNV`/`QuantiSNP` Log R Ratio). Normalizing each sample's *HBA* probe intensity by that sample's median chromosome 16 intensity might reduce the overlap between $-\alpha/\alpha\alpha$ heterozygotes and $\alpha\alpha/\alpha\alpha$ wild-types.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1: The $-\alpha^{3.7\text{I}}$ deletion is strongly protective against severe malaria in Kilifi ($\text{aOR} = 0.78, p = 1.1 \times 10^{-5}$), but is completely missed by flanking array SNPs due to low LD ($r^2 \le 0.16$) and distribution across hundreds of haplotypes `[Well Supported]`.** Demonstrated across 3,036 individuals and 6,072 phased chromosomes.
* **Claim 2: The Kilifi $-\alpha^{3.7}$ deletions are exclusively Type I ($-\alpha^{3.7\text{I}}$) but arose via at least two distinct crossover breakpoints `[Well Supported]`.** Proven by Sanger sequencing of 14 paralogous *HBA2*/*HBA1* sites in 25 homozygotes and 9 local wild-type controls.
* **Claim 3: Indirect genotype inference via haplotype imputation or microarray probe intensities is insufficiently reliable to replace direct PCR genotyping for $\alpha$-thalassaemia in African association studies `[Well Supported]`.** Directly demonstrated by the attenuation of association power in `IMPUTE2` and the spurious reversal of odds ratios in intensity-based classifiers (Table 5, Figure 13).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Exemplary Open Data & Code Archiving:** Individual-level GWAS data are archived in EGA (`EGAS00001001311` / `EGAD00010000904`, managed ethical access), while all underlying summary tables, intensity matrices, Sanger alignments, `IMPUTE2`/`SHAPEIT2`/`QCtool` command lines, and custom R scripts are openly deposited in Harvard Dataverse (`doi:10.7910/DVN/YTXAHR`, CC-BY 4.0) and Oxford web resources.
