### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This landmark hepatology and membrane transport study demonstrates that the multidrug resistance-associated protein (MRP) / canalicular isoform (Mrp2/cMOAT) is expressed in the canalicular and lateral plasma membrane domains of human and rat hepatocytes, where it mediates ATP-dependent hepatobiliary export of glutathione $S$-conjugates (such as leukotriene $\text{C}_4$, $\text{LTC}_4$) and amphiphilic organic anions—and that its selective absence from the bile canalicular membrane is the molecular basis of the hereditary conjugated hyperbilirubinemia and anion transport defect in $\text{TR}^-$ mutant rats (an animal model of human Dubin-Johnson syndrome). Using RT-PCR of human and normal vs. $\text{TR}^-$ mutant rat liver mRNA, immunoblotting of purified canalicular vs. basolateral hepatocyte membrane vesicles with four distinct anti-MRP antibodies (recognizing a $\sim 190\ \text{kDa}$ glycoprotein), double-label immunofluorescence and confocal laser scanning microscopy (co-staining with desmosomal/cytokeratin domain markers), and functional ATP-dependent $[3\text{H}]\text{LTC}_4$ transport assays in isolated canalicular membrane vesicles, the authors link the genetic, protein-localization, and functional transport defects in $\text{TR}^-$ hepatocytes. Only the abstract and 57-reference bibliography are preserved in the text extract (main text in a 6.4 MB PDF).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
#### STRENGTHS
- **Convergence of Genetic, Biochemical, Structural, and Functional Evidence:** The study integrates four orthogonal lines of evidence in both human and normal vs. $\text{TR}^-$ mutant rat liver: (1) RT-PCR amplification of two *MRP*/*mrp* cDNA fragments (with selective loss of one amplified fragment in $\text{TR}^-$ rat liver), (2) domain-specific immunoblotting of purified canalicular vs. basolateral membrane fractions using four independent anti-MRP antibodies, (3) confocal double-immunofluorescence microscopy demonstrating selective loss of canalicular staining while lateral membrane staining persists in $\text{TR}^-$ rats, and (4) functional ATP-dependent $\text{LTC}_4$ vesicle transport assays.
- **Careful Distinction Between Lateral MRP (MRP1) and the Canalicular Isoform (Mrp2/cMOAT):** The authors astutely observe that in $\text{TR}^-$ mutant rats, Mrp immunoreactivity is selectively lost from the canalicular membrane while remaining present in the lateral hepatocyte membrane, and cautiously conclude that *"the absence of Mrp or an isoform of Mrp from the canalicular membrane is the basis for the hereditary defect"*—accurately anticipating that canalicular Mrp represents a closely related hepatocanalicular isoform (subsequently designated MRP2/ABCC2, vs. basolateral/lateral MRP1/ABCC1) cross-reacting with C-terminal MRP antisera.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Archival Main Text Inaccessibility:** Because the main text is locked in an external PDF ("The Full Text of this article is available as a PDF (6.4 MB)"), exact RT-PCR primer sequences, $K_m / V_{\max}$ kinetic parameters for $\text{LTC}_4$ transport, and membrane purity marker enrichments (e.g., alkaline phosphatase/leucine aminopeptidase vs. $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase per Meier & Boyer) cannot be audited line-by-line.
- **Cross-Reactivity of C-Terminal MRP1 Antibodies with Canalicular Mrp2:** Because the study relies on antibodies raised against human MRP1 (including its conserved C-terminus) and RT-PCR primers designed from MRP1/ABC transporter motifs, the lateral signal (MRP1) and canalicular signal (MRP2) are detected by the same polyclonal antibody; full cDNA cloning and sequencing of the canalicular-specific *mrp2* transcript and its exact nonsense/frameshift mutation in $\text{TR}^-$ rats remain to be completed beyond the missing RT-PCR fragment reported here.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Human MRP and rat Mrp ($\sim 190\ \text{kDa}$ glycoprotein) are localized to both the canalicular and lateral plasma membrane domains of normal hepatocytes. `[Well Supported]` by immunoblotting of purified membrane subfractions with 4 antibodies and confocal double-immunofluorescence microscopy.
- **Claim 2:** In transport-deficient $\text{TR}^-$ mutant rat hepatocytes, deficient canalicular ATP-dependent $\text{LTC}_4$ transport is associated with loss of amplification of one *mrp* cDNA fragment and selective absence of Mrp from the canalicular membrane (with retention on the lateral membrane). `[Well Supported]` by concordant RT-PCR, canalicular immunoblot, confocal microscopy, and vesicle transport assays.
- **Claim 3:** Absence of Mrp or an isoform of Mrp from the canalicular membrane is the molecular basis of the hereditary hepatobiliary organic anion excretion defect in $\text{TR}^-$ rats. `[Well Supported]` and phrased with exemplary scientific precision ("Mrp or an isoform of Mrp").

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Classical methods for canalicular/basolateral membrane vesicle isolation (Meier & Boyer), $\text{LTC}_4$ ATP-dependent vesicle transport (Ishikawa et al.), desmosomal/cytokeratin domain markers (Franke et al.), and anti-MRP antibodies (Cole/Deeley, Flens/Zaman, Center) are fully cited in the bibliography, though primary figures reside in the unextracted PDF.
