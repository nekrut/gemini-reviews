### SUMMARY / 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript is a historical and translational overview book chapter ("Infectious Disease Genomics") summarizing how the Human Genome Project (HGP) catalyzed microbial, fungal, parasite, insect vector, viral, and human microbiome sequencing programs, and illustrating four primary applications of pathogen/vector genomics through selected case studies: (1) reverse vaccinology for *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup B (`4CMenB`, filtering 2,158 ORFs in strain MC58 down to 570 predicted surface-exposed proteins, 350 *E. coli*-expressible proteins, 28 protective antigens in mice, and 5 final components); (2) target-based and natural-product antibacterial discovery in the bacterial type II fatty acid synthesis (FAS II) pathway (platensimycin from *Streptomyces platensis* targeting FabF/B, and its limitation in *Streptococcus agalactiae* due to exogenous serum fatty acid salvage); (3) malaria vector genomics and genetic control in *Anopheles gambiae* (Vector Biology Network goals and *APL1* resistance locus); and (4) rapid molecular diagnostics and pathogen discovery (swine-origin H1N1 influenza A reassortment, Virochip 70-mer microarray discovery of SARS-CoV via a conserved 33-nt 3'UTR motif shared with astroviruses and IBV, and Roche/454 digital transcriptome subtraction discovery of Merkel cell polyomavirus in 8/10 MCC tumors).

### STRENGTHS
- **Accurate, balanced synthesis of milestone case studies:** Unlike superficial reviews, the chapter includes concrete quantitative details from the primary literature (e.g., the funnel of 2,158 → 570 → 350 → 28 → 5 ORFs in MenB reverse vaccinology; the 399,000 Roche/454 reads with 99.4% human host subtraction leaving 2,395 non-human reads in Merkel cell polyomavirus discovery; and the critical physiological caveat that *S. agalactiae* bypasses FAS II inhibition in human serum).
- **Useful directory of infectious disease genomic repositories:** Table 10.1 compiles major bacterial, fungal, eukaryotic pathogen (EuPathDB), vector (VectorBase), and viral (NCBI Flu, LANL HIV/HCV) genome databases.

### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT / 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Narrative book chapter without primary data or novel meta-analysis:** As a secondary review/book chapter, the manuscript performs no original statistical, bioinformatic, or experimental analyses.
- **Disconnect between the FAS II genomic rationale and how platensimycin was actually discovered:** In the "Drug Target" section (lines 61–63), the authors frame platensimycin as an example of infectious disease genomics, yet note that Merck identified it by "systematic screening of 250,000 natural product extracts" (using a target-based whole-cell antisense *fabF* sensitization screen, rather than de novo comparative genomic target discovery).
- **Outdated/unverified URLs in Table 10.1:** Several web links in Table 10.1 (e.g., `http://cmr.jcvi.org/`, `http://gemina.igs.umaryland.edu`, and static Pasteur Institute HTML pages) represent legacy resources without persistent identifiers.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
