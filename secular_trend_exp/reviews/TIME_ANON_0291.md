### 1. Executive Summary
This study identifies Myc-associated zinc finger (MAZ) as an indirect transcriptional repressor of fetal $\gamma$-globin (*HBG1/HBG2*) in human adult-type erythroid cells. Using two independent shRNAs and four CRISPR/Cas9 single-cell knockout (KO) clones in HUDEP-2 cells, as well as shRNA knockdown in primary CD34+ HSPC-derived erythroblasts from a single $\beta^0/\beta^0$-thalassemia patient (`CD41-42/CD17`), the authors show that MAZ depletion increases *HBG* mRNA, $\gamma$-globin protein, HbF percentage by HPLC, and the fraction of HbF+ cells. Re-analysis of public ChIP-seq datasets (K562 and primary erythroid cells) and new RNA-seq in MAZ-knockdown HUDEP-2 cells indicate that MAZ binds the promoter of the known $\gamma$-globin repressor *MYB* and maintains *MYB* (and downstream *BCL11A*) expression; lentiviral overexpression of *MYB* in MAZ-knockdown HUDEP-2 cells re-silences *HBG*. While the genetic perturbation and rescue chain in HUDEP-2 cells is coherent, the study is limited by $n = 1$ patient donor for primary $\beta$-thalassemia cells (with very modest effect size due to high baseline HbF), lack of direct ChIP-qPCR/CUT&RUN validation of MAZ binding at the *MYB* promoter in HUDEP-2 cells, and unadjusted multiple pairwise Student's $t$-tests.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Single Patient Donor ($n = 1$) in Primary Erythroblast Validation:** In Figure 3, primary CD34+ HSPCs were isolated from a single $\beta^0/\beta^0$-thalassemia patient (`CD41-42/CD17`). The reported `n = 3` in Figure 3A–D represents technical or culture-well replicates from this single donor rather than independent biological donors. Moreover, baseline $HBG/(HBG+HBB)$ in this patient's cells was already ~33% (38.9% HbF+ cells), and the *HBG* mRNA increase upon MAZ knockdown was minimal ("a slight induction of HBG mRNA levels").
- **Statistical Testing Without Multiple-Comparison Adjustment:** In the Quantification and Statistical Analysis section (line 149), all multi-group comparisons—including two shRNAs vs. control (Figures 1, 3, 6) and four KO clones vs. WT clones (Figure 2)—were evaluated using pairwise Student's $t$-tests without ANOVA or Dunnett/Bonferroni correction.
- **Unexplained Baseline Variability in HUDEP-2 Control Cells:** In Figure 1, the authors add a note that "The baseline level of $\gamma$-globin expression in HUDEP-2 cells varied between experimental setups" (and line 32 notes "Despite we noticed that the baseline of HBG levels might be elevated..."). Elevated baseline HbF in scrambled-shRNA HUDEP-2 controls can arise from lentiviral transduction stress or differentiation stage shifts, which can confound fold-change and %HbF+ metrics.
- **Reliance on Public ChIP-seq Across Cell Types Without Direct Local Validation:** In Figure 4, MAZ occupancy at the *MYB* promoter is inferred by intersecting published MAZ ChIP-seq from K562 (`ENCSR163IUV`) and primary erythroid cells (`GSM4135996`) with H3K4me3/H3K27ac ChIP-seq from HUDEP-2 (`GSM6199499`, `GSM5267246`) and CTCF from K562 (`ENCFF221XPQ`). The authors did not perform MAZ ChIP-qPCR or reporter assays at the *MYB* promoter in HUDEP-2 cells, nor did they test whether MAZ depletion alters H3K27ac or CTCF occupancy at the *MYB* locus.
- **Incomplete Methods Reporting for Key Assays:** The Methods section includes headers for Western blotting (only 2 sentences—stopping after RIPA extraction without listing antibodies, dilutions, or gel/transfer protocols), omits HPLC methods entirely (shown in Figure 1F), and omits intracellular HbF staining antibodies/protocols for flow cytometry (Figures 1G, 2G, 3E only list CD235a and CD71 in the Methods).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Depletion of MAZ by shRNA or CRISPR/Cas9 KO induces *HBG* expression and HbF production in HUDEP-2 cells.** `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated across two independent shRNAs and four homozygous frameshift KO clones using orthogonal readouts (RT-qPCR, Western blot, HbF flow cytometry, HPLC, and RNA-seq).
- **Claim 2: MAZ represses *HBG* indirectly by transcriptionally activating *MYB* (and downstream *BCL11A*).** `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by public ChIP-seq showing a MAZ peak at the *MYB* promoter, downregulation of *MYB* and *BCL11A* in MAZ-KD/KO HUDEP-2 cells, and the *MYB* cDNA rescue experiment (Figure 6C–D) that restores *HBG* silencing. Direct promoter binding/activity assays in HUDEP-2 would strengthen the mechanistic link.
- **Claim 3: Targeting MAZ is a viable therapeutic strategy for reactivating HbF in $\beta$-thalassemia and SCD.** `[Overstated]` — As the authors' own ChIP-seq analysis shows (Figure 4A, S4), MAZ binds the promoters of ~5,700–10,000 genes (including *HBA* $\alpha$-globin genes, which are already toxic when in excess in $\beta$-thalassemia, though *HBA* changes upon MAZ KD in $\beta$-thalassemia cells were not reported) and causes >1,500 differentially expressed genes (FC > 2) in HUDEP-2 cells. Furthermore, primary cell efficacy was only tested in a single patient with a modest shift in HbF+ cells (38.9% to 51.1%).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Sequencing Data & Accession Numbers:** Newly generated RNA-seq data are deposited in GSA-Human under accession `HRA014114`, and all re-analyzed public GEO/ENCODE accessions (`GSM4135996`, `GSM6199499`, `GSM5267246`, `ENCSR163IUV`, `ENCFF221XPQ`) are clearly cited.
- **Missing Experimental Details in Methods:** While shRNA, sgRNA, and RT-qPCR primer sequences are provided in Table S1, the Methods text fails to list catalog numbers/dilutions for all Western blot primary/secondary antibodies (MAZ, HBG, MYB, BCL11A, GAPDH), the anti-HbF flow cytometry antibody and permeabilization protocol, and the cation-exchange HPLC protocol used in Figure 1F.
