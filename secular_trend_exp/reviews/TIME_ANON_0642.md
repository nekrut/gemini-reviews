### SUMMARY / 1. Executive Summary
This canine medical genetics study screens 614 dogs across 38 breeds to characterize the mutational spectrum of canine bestrophin 1 (*cBEST1*) in canine multifocal retinopathy (*cmr*), a large-animal model of human autosomal recessive and dominant bestrophinopathies (Best vitelliform macular dystrophy). By resequencing all coding exons (exons 2–11) of *cBEST1* in affected and obligate-carrier Lapponian Herders (LH)—after excluding progressive rod-cone degeneration (*prcd*), X-linked PRA, and Collie Eye Anomaly—the authors discover two novel linked variants in exon 10 (`c.1388delC` / `p.Pro463fs` and `c.1466G>T` / `p.Gly489Val`, which creates an in-frame stop `p.Gly489X` within the shifted reading frame, truncating the 585-aa protein to 488 aa) that co-segregate recessively with a third canine multifocal retinopathy locus (*cmr3*) in 54 phenotyped LH dogs. They also confirm segregation of the known *cmr1* nonsense allele (`C73T` / `R25X`) in three European mastiff-related breeds (Dogue de Bordeaux, English mastiff, Italian cane corso) and identify 5 additional exon 10 missense variants across other breeds (including `Trp440Cys` and `Val473Gly` at $<1\%$ allele frequency in 135 Bernese Mountain Dogs). The study is methodologically rigorous, provides complete primer and RFLP/AS-PCR protocols and dbSNP `ss` accessions, and thoughtfully addresses phenotypic overlap with multifocal retinal dysplasia (MRD) and non-*prcd* PRA in the LH breed.

### STRENGTHS
- **Stringent longitudinal ophthalmic phenotyping and exclusion of confounding loci:** Because cmr lesions develop between 9 months and 2 years of age, the authors required dogs to be $\ge 3$ years of age with normal fundus exams to be classified as unaffected ($n=31$), and excluded known *prcd*, XLPRA, and CEA mutations prior to analysis.
- **Clear molecular characterization of the compound *cmr3* frameshift/stop allele:** Demonstrating that `c.1388delC` (`Pro463fs`) and `c.1466G>T` (`Gly489Val` $\rightarrow$ `Gly489X` in the $+1$ shifted frame) occur in complete linkage disequilibrium in LH and truncate the C-terminal cytosolic tail (unlike the null `R25X` *cmr1* and N-terminal missense `G161D` *cmr2* alleles) establishes a distinct C-terminal truncation model for BEST1 channel regulation.
- **Complete diagnostic assay development and transparency:** Exact PCR primers, allele-specific PCR cycling conditions, RFLP restriction enzymes (*Stu*I, *Bst*NI, *Bbs*I, *Eco*O109I, *Aat*II) with fragment sizes, and NCBI dbSNP `ss` identifiers (`ss250608388`–`ss250608401`) are fully provided.

### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT / 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Terminology error ("complete linkage equilibrium" instead of "complete linkage disequilibrium"):** On line 183, the authors write: "The two novel *cmr3* mutations found exclusively in the LH breed were in **complete linkage equilibrium** and homozygous in all animals diagnosed with the *cmr* phenotype"—an obvious slip of the pen for **complete linkage disequilibrium** (which they correctly wrote on line 155).
- **Coordinate typos in Table 2:**
  - In Table 2, variant #2 (Intron 2 `C>T`) is listed at CFA18 position `58507851` (line 109), whereas the text (line 102) and flanking exon 2/intron 3 coordinates (`57507965` and `57506423`) show the correct coordinate is `57507851` (off by 1 Mb in Table 2).
  - Similarly, variant #27 (Exon 10 silent `C>T`) is listed at `57449754` (line 134) instead of `57499754` (off by 50 kb relative to Exon 10 `57499834`).
- **Phenotypic ambiguity in two *cmr3*-homozygous LH dogs (`MRD` and `PRA`):** In Table 4 and Figure 2, out of 10 LH dogs homozygous for the *cmr3* mutant haplotype (`AA`), 8 had classic *cmr*, 1 was diagnosed with MRD (at age 7, after "rosettes" at age 5), and 1 was diagnosed with generalized PRA (at age 8, without prior eye exams). While the authors transparently discuss both cases and note that 4 of 8 MRD dogs and 1 of 4 PRA dogs are wild-type (`BB`) for *cmr3* (confirming that MRD and non-*prcd* PRA are genetically distinct from *cmr3*), their "working hypothesis" in Figure 4 suggesting that *cmr3* heterozygotes (`AB`) might develop dominant MRD is speculative given that the carrier rate among MRD dogs ($3/8 = 37.5\%$) is identical to the carrier rate among completely normal LH dogs ($13/31 = 41.9\%$, Fisher's exact $p = 1.0$).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** The linked `c.1388delC` (`Pro463fs`) and `c.1466G>T` (`Gly489Val` / `Gly489X`) variants in *cBEST1* exon 10 cause autosomal recessive canine multifocal retinopathy 3 (*cmr3*) in the Lapponian Herder `[Well Supported]`.
- **Claim 2:** The *cmr1* nonsense mutation (`C73T` / `R25X`) segregates across additional mastiff-lineage breeds including the Italian cane corso, Dogue de Bordeaux, and English mastiff `[Well Supported]`.
- **Claim 3:** Heterozygous *cmr3* carriers may occasionally manifest multifocal retinal dysplasia (MRD) via a semi-dominant mechanism `[Overstated]` — carrier frequency in MRD dogs ($3/8 = 38\%$) is no higher than in normal LH controls ($13/31 = 42\%$).
