### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This study scans the X chromosome of three pig breeds (Landrace, $n=35$ sows; Chinese Songliao, $n=34$ sows; Yorkshire, $n=44$ sows, selected from 515 pigs via PCA) genotyped on the Illumina Porcine60K SNP BeadChip (~1,130–1,163 X-linked SNPs after QC) using three between-population methods (XPEHH, XPCLR, and Gianola's Bayesian $F_{ST}$) and two within-population methods (iHS and Tajima's $D$). The authors report candidate selection regions, examine overlaps across methods, and intersect the regions with 5 hematological GWAS hits from the same cohort and candidate genes via BioMart/DAVID. However, the study suffers from severe methodological and statistical flaws: applying Tajima's $D$ to ascertained 60K SNP chip data with MAF $< 0.05$ removed, using an invalid site-shuffling permutation scheme for multi-locus and single-SNP tests, calibrating X-chromosome significance thresholds directly against autosomal 5% empirical tails without correcting for the different effective population size ($N_e$) and recombination rate of the X chromosome, and committing major numerical/table errors (e.g., Table 3 duplicating the Landrace XPCLR row at the bottom of the Yorkshire block, and Table 4 claiming an overlap for a SNP at 43.44 Mb with a selection region at 40.11–40.91 Mb).

### STRENGTHS
- **Restriction of X-chromosome selection scans to females (sows):** By restricting the analysis to 113 unrelated sows ($35$ Landrace, $34$ Songliao, $44$ Yorkshire) following PCA filtering, the authors avoid ploidy-mixing and hemizygous phasing artifacts that arise when combining boars and sows on the non-pseudoautosomal X chromosome.
- **Multi-method comparison:** Comparing haplotype-based (iHS, XPEHH), multi-locus allele frequency differentiation (XPCLR), and single-locus differentiation ($F_{ST}$) highlights the low concordance between within-breed and between-breed statistics.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Invalid application of Tajima's $D$ to ascertained SNP array genotypes with MAF filtering:** In Materials and Methods, the authors state that SNPs with $\text{MAF} < 0.05$ were removed when within-population methods were performed, and then compute Tajima's $D$ on Porcine60K chip SNPs (mean spacing ~110 kb). Tajima's $D$ compares $\hat{\theta}_\pi$ and $\hat{\theta}_W$ under an infinite-sites neutral coalescent model assuming complete, unascertained resequencing data. Ascertainment of common SNPs on commercial arrays—compounded by explicitly truncating $\text{MAF} < 0.05$—systematically eliminates rare variants, artificially inflates $\hat{\theta}_\pi$ relative to $S$, shifts Tajima's $D$ strongly positive, and makes detection of negative Tajima's $D$ (selective sweeps) mathematically impossible. Indeed, the authors find zero positive selection regions and only positive Tajima's $D$ ("balancing selection") regions, an artifact of SNP ascertainment and MAF truncation rather than biology.
- **Uncalibrated use of autosomal top-5% empirical cutoffs for X-chromosome statistics:** Because the X chromosome has $3/4$ the effective population size ($N_e$) of autosomes (or different still under breeding harem sex ratios in commercial pigs, where few boars sire many litters) and female-restricted recombination, genetic drift and baseline LD ($r^2 = 0.366\text{--}0.395$ vs $0.344\text{--}0.363$ on autosomes despite half the marker density) are substantially elevated on the X chromosome. Applying the autosomal 95th percentile cutoff directly to X-linked XPEHH, iHS, and XPCLR does not control the false positive rate under neutral X-chromosome demography.
- **Flawed permutation procedure for $F_{ST}$ and Tajima's $D$:** Shuffling allele frequencies randomly across fixed SNP positions on the X chromosome ("5000 permutation tests") merely permutes single-marker values against themselves; for a window-based statistic, breaking spatial LD creates an artificially narrow null distribution of window means because correlated neutral SNPs in high-LD blocks are broken up, inflating false positives for multi-SNP windows. Conversely, for XPCLR, shuffling allele frequencies across markers destroys LD while keeping recombination weights fixed, which the authors note yielded an unusable threshold, prompting them to abandon permutations for XPCLR.
- **Direct numerical errors and coordinate mismatches in Tables 3 and 4:**
  - In **Table 3** (Yorkshire section, final row), an extra row labeled `XPCLR | 15.20 | 1.95 | 1.21 | 0` is pasted at the bottom of the Yorkshire block—these exact numbers (`15.20`, `1.95`, `1.21`, `0`) are a copy-paste duplication of the Landrace XPCLR row.
  - In **Table 4**, the authors claim that 3 of 5 hematological GWAS SNPs fall completely into selection regions and 1 is "close to" a selection region: SNP `43443513` (43.44 Mb, platelet count) is paired with selection region `40108407–40908406` (40.11–40.91 Mb), which is $>2.5\text{ Mb}$ away, not overlapping or adjacent. Moreover, testing GWAS overlap in the exact same experimental cohort ($N=515$ pigs from Wang et al.) is circular because allele frequency/LD structure in the same animals drives both metrics.
  - In **Table 1**, the XPCLR column for `L-S` reports `59(S 41, L 18)`, whereas the text (page 6, line 133) states the reverse: "41 selection footprints were detected in Landrace when Songliao was regarded as reference population, and 18 in Songliao when Landrace was the reference population."

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1 (Tajima's $D$ identifies balancing selection regions and no positive selection on the porcine X chromosome): `[Methodologically Flawed]`** — Directly caused by commercial SNP chip ascertainment bias and explicit removal of SNPs with $\text{MAF} < 0.05$, which strips rare alleles and forces Tajima's $D$ toward positive values.
- **Claim 2 (Few selection footprints in the central 40–80 Mb region of the X chromosome are explained by X-inactivation / sex-specific dosage compensation): `[Overstated]`** — The central region of the mammalian X chromosome encompasses the centromere and pericentromeric low-recombination region where SNP density and polymorphism are historically depleted; attributing a lack of outlier test statistics in 40–80 Mb to X-inactivation silencing without modeling recombination rate or centromeric diversity is unsupported speculation.
- **Claim 3 (Candidate genes in +-400 kb windows around outlier SNPs reflect selection on meat quality, reproduction, and hematological traits): `[Fragile]`** — With ~15–19 Mb of the ~126 Mb X chromosome flagged by XPEHH/XPCLR in each breed (12–15% of the entire chromosome) and another +-400 kb added around every outlier, post-hoc cherry-picking of genes (`ACE2`, `ACSL4`, `HTR2C`) when DAVID GO enrichment showed almost no significant terms after multiple-testing correction is anecdotal story-telling.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
Raw genotype data (Porcine60K `.ped`/`.map` files) are not deposited in a public repository, genome build coordinates (Sscrofa build version) are not specified, software versions/parameters for XPEHH, iHS, XPCLR (grid size, window size $k$, weight $w$, selection coefficient steps), and Gianola's $F_{ST}$ MCMC settings are omitted, and Table 3 contains copy-paste corruption.
