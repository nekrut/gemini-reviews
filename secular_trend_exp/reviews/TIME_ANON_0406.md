### 1. Executive Summary
This integrative evolutionary genomics and biochemical study investigates cold adaptation in the alpine ant *Tetramorium alpestre* compared to four warmth-adapted congeners (*T. immigrans*, *T. parvispinum*, *T. bicarinatum*, and *T. simillimum*) and 17 outgroup Hymenopterans. The authors combine global bioclimatic niche modeling across 1,835 georeferenced records (`WorldClim` variables `bio1`, `bio5`, `bio8`, `bio10`), de novo whole-genome assembly of *T. alpestre* (245.7 Mb, 16,690 predicted genes, `BUSCO` 91.2% complete), phylogenomics of 3,261 single-copy orthologous groups (`scOGs`), branch-level $d_N/d_S$ ($\omega$) and selection-intensity (`RELAX` $k$) scans, chill-shock ($-6^\circ\text{C}$) RT-qPCR recovery time-courses for *starvin* (*stv*) and heat-shock cognate/protein genes (*Hsc70-4*, *Hsp70-like*, *Hsp83-like*), heterologous expression and Michaelis-Menten kinetics ($K_m$) of recombinant Hexokinase type 1 (`Hex-T1`) from *T. alpestre* vs. *T. immigrans* across $6–36^\circ\text{C}$, and 500 ns explicit-solvent molecular dynamics (MD) simulations of `Hex-T1`. The multi-disciplinary progression—from genome-wide positive selection scans directly to recombinant enzyme kinetics and atomistic MD showing how two substitutions (`E74G` and `N344K`) alter inter-domain salt-bridge networks and glucose binding affinity at low temperatures—is a major strength. However, the interpretation of the genome-wide terminal-branch elevation in $\omega$ and reduction in `RELAX` $k$ in *T. alpestre* as "niche-enabled relaxation of purifying selection" overlooks the classic population-genetic confounding of reduced effective population size ($N_e$) in alpine/Pleistocene-bottlenecked populations.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Confounding of Genome-Wide `RELAX` ($k$) and $d_N/d_S$ ($\omega$) by Effective Population Size ($N_e$) and Short Terminal Branches:** In the genome-wide scan of 3,261 `scOGs` (Fig. 2B), the terminal branch of *T. alpestre* (and its sister *T. immigrans*, diverged $\sim 5.1\text{ Mya}$) exhibits a markedly higher median $\omega$ ($0.38$ vs. $0.10$ in other ants) and a lower median `RELAX` selection intensity parameter $k$ ($0.47$ vs. $0.99$; values of $k < 1$ indicate relaxation of selection toward $\omega = 1$). The authors interpret this genome-wide shift primarily as ecologically driven "relaxed purifying selection when switching to a cold peripheral niche." However, under nearly neutral molecular evolution theory, a reduction in effective population size ($N_e$)—such as Pleistocene glacial bottlenecks during Alpine colonization or facultative polygyny/inbreeding—reduces the efficacy of purifying selection ($|N_e s|$) across the entire genome, elevating $d_N/d_S$ and yielding $k < 1$ in `RELAX` purely via genetic drift. Furthermore, terminal branch comparisons between closely related sister species (*T. alpestre* and *T. immigrans*) include unfixed polymorphic deleterious mutations and any residual assembly base-calling differences (especially given that the four congener assemblies were reconstructed by reference-guided mapping of `BGISEQ-500` reads onto the *T. alpestre* Illumina assembly via `Bowtie2`/`Pilon`).
- **Recombinant Hexokinase (`Hex-T1`) Kinetics & Variance Pooling (Figure 3B):** Recombinant `TalpHex-T1` and `TimmHex-T1` were expressed in *Drosophila* S2 cells and assayed at $6, 16, 26,$ and $36^\circ\text{C}$ ($n=3$ biological replicates per species and temperature). While the lower $K_m$ (higher substrate affinity / reduced thermal sensitivity) of `TalpHex-T1` across temperatures is statistically supported by linear modeling ($p < 0.0029$) and two-sided Mann-Whitney $U$-tests ($p = 0.050$ at single temperatures), the authors' $F$-test claiming lower variance of `TalpHex-T1` ($p < 0.0005$) was obtained by pooling raw $K_m$ values across $16^\circ\text{C}$ and $26^\circ\text{C}$ ($n=6$ combined). Because $K_m$ changes systematically with temperature in `TimmHex-T1` ($0.07\text{ mM}$ at $16^\circ\text{C}$ vs. $0.10\text{ mM}$ at $26^\circ\text{C}$), pooling two temperatures conflates deterministic thermal dependence (slope) with stochastic replicate variance.
- **Molecular Dynamics (MD) Rigor:** The 500 ns explicit-solvent `AMBER16` (`ff14SB`) MD simulations across three temperatures ($279, 289,$ and $299\text{ K}$) with `H++` protonation, grid inhomogeneous solvation theory (`GIST`), and principal component analysis (`PC1` capturing opening-closing cleft motion) are executed rigorously and provide a clear biophysical rationale for how `E74G` abolishes a salt bridge (`E74–K246`) to increase flexibility of the small domain while `N344K` alters the large-domain electrostatic network.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Two positively selected amino acid substitutions (`E74G` and `N344K`) in *T. alpestre* Hexokinase type 1 (`Hex-T1`) confer higher enzymatic affinity (lower $K_m$) and altered domain flexibility at cold temperatures compared to lowland *T. immigrans* `Hex-T1`. `[Well Supported]` — Directly validated by heterologous protein expression, Michaelis-Menten kinetics at $6–36^\circ\text{C}$, and 500 ns atomistic MD trajectories.
- **Claim 2:** *starvin* (*stv*) is under positive selection in *T. alpestre* and is rapidly induced (within 0.5–4.0 h) alongside *Hsc70-4* during recovery from $-6^\circ\text{C}$ chill-shock injury. `[Well Supported]` — Supported by branch-site `aBSREL` ($p = 0.0002$ after multiple-testing correction) and time-course RT-qPCR across $n=3$ colonies (18 workers).
- **Claim 3:** The genome-wide elevation of $d_N/d_S$ and reduction of `RELAX` $k$ in *T. alpestre* reflect adaptive ecological niche release rather than demographic reductions in $N_e$ or short-branch polymorphism/mapping artifacts. `[Moderately Supported]` — While the authors discuss population size, disentangling ecological release from Pleistocene bottleneck drift requires polymorphism-aware models or synonymous site frequency spectra ($\pi_N/\pi_S$).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Code Availability:** All raw sequencing reads, the *T. alpestre* genome assembly (`BioProject PRJNA532334`, `GenBank` accessions for *Hex-T1* `MK881038–MK881039` and COI), WorldClim occurrence tables, orthology alignments, and MD simulation parameters (`AMBER16`, `cpptraj`) are comprehensively documented and publicly deposited.
