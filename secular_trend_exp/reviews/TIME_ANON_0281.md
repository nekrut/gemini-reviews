### 1. Executive Summary
This submission consists solely of an abstract and a reference list (44 references) with the entire main text, Materials and Methods, Results, Tables, and Figures omitted (`"Full Text The Full Text of this article is available as a PDF (178.9 KB)"`). According to the abstract, the authors isolated *TP53* (*p53*) cDNA via RT-PCR from normal and tumor tissue of $N = 10$ canine mammary cancer patients, reporting *p53* mutations in 3 of 10 patients (tumor-associated mutations within exons 2 and 5, plus a putative germline deletion spanning exons 3 to 7) as well as a putative polymorphism at codon 69 in exon 4. From these 10 cases, the abstract claims support for a role of *p53* inactivation in breed predisposition to canine mammary cancer, proposes outbreeding strategies to eliminate inherited cancer predisposition in dogs, and speculates on analogous polymorphisms in human breast cancer. Because no primary methods, primer sequences, sequence traces, genomic DNA confirmation of what may be an RT-PCR splice artifact ("germline deletion of exons 3 to 7" detected from cDNA), or clinicopathological tables are present in the text, and the claims of germline breed predisposition and selective outbreeding are vastly overstated for an unvalidated cDNA screen of 10 dogs, the manuscript is scientifically unverifiable in its provided form.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Complete Absence of Experimental Methods, Data Tables, and Sequence Evidence:**
  - The manuscript text contains zero experimental details, primer sequences, RT-PCR cycling parameters, sequencing chemistries, breed distributions, or mutation coordinates.
- **Critical Technical Confounder — Inferring a "Germline Deletion of Exons 3 to 7" from RT-PCR cDNA Alone:**
  - The abstract states: *"Using reverse transcription and DNA amplification, we isolated p53 cDNA from normal and tumour tissue of ten canine mammary cancer patients. DNA sequencing identified p53 mutations in three of the ten patients. These included tumour-associated p53 gene mutations within exons 2 and 5 and a germ line deletion of exons 3 to 7."*
  - Amplifying cDNA by RT-PCR from normal and tumor tissue and observing a shorter amplicon lacking exons 3–7 does **not** establish a genomic germline deletion of exons 3–7; alternative splicing, aberrant exon skipping during reverse transcription, or nonsense-mediated decay/splice-site polymorphism can produce multi-exon-skipped cDNA isoforms without any genomic deletion. Furthermore, a constitutional homozygous or heterozygous deletion removing exons 3–7 of *TP53* (the entire DNA-binding core domain) would be equivalent to a canine Li-Fraumeni syndrome knockout allele, which requires Southern blotting or genomic DNA PCR/sequencing across the intron breakpoints to distinguish from an RT-PCR splicing artifact.
- **Sample Size & Statistical Power ($N = 10$):**
  - Screening $N = 10$ dogs without a control cohort of tumor-free dogs of matched breeds and ages provides no statistical basis to distinguish neutral breed polymorphisms (such as the codon 69 variant in exon 4) from pathogenic cancer predisposition alleles.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Somatic *p53* mutations in exons 2 and 5 and a codon 69 polymorphism in exon 4 occur in canine mammary tumors ($3/10$ patients). `[Fragile]` — Plausible given known *TP53* mutation spectra in canine neoplasms, but completely unverifiable because the nucleotide changes, amino acid substitutions, and chromatogram evidence are absent from the text.
- **Claim 2:** A germline deletion of *p53* exons 3 to 7 in canine mammary cancer supports inherited breed predisposition that *"could prove invaluable in the successful outbreeding of inherited predisposition to cancer in the dog."* `[Overstated]` — Inferring a constitutional multi-exon genomic deletion from cDNA amplification without genomic DNA breakpoint verification, pedigree cosegregation, or breed-level allele frequency data—and proposing selective breeding interventions on that basis—is a severe over-interpretation of a 10-animal cDNA screen.
