### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This clinical molecular genetics report screens all nine coding exons and flanking splice junctions of the serine/threonine kinase gene *STK11* (*LKB1*, chromosome 19p13.3) for germline mutations in 10 Korean Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (PJS) probands (9 sporadic cases and 1 familial case comprising two affected sisters, plus two unaffected siblings). Using radiolabeled PCR–single-strand conformation polymorphism (PCR-SSCP) followed by TA-cloning and bi-directional Sanger sequencing of conformer-shifted amplicons, the authors identify germline *STK11* variants in 5 of the 10 probands (50%): three missense variants within the conserved kinase catalytic domain—codon 232 (`TCC` $\to$ `CCC`, S232P in exon 5, cosegregating in the two affected sisters of family SNU-P5 and absent in two unaffected siblings), codon 256 (`GAA` $\to$ `GCA`, E256A in exon 6 in SNU-P2), and codon 324 (`CCG` $\to$ `CTG`, P324L in exon 8 in HYU-P2)—one frameshift mutation at codon 342 (1-bp G insertion in exon 8 leading to a premature stop at codon 359 in SNU-P6), and one canonical splice-acceptor transversion (`ag` $\to$ `cg` in intron 1 preceding exon 2 in USU-P2). None of the three missense shifts were detected by PCR-SSCP in 100 Korean control DNA samples (60 healthy peripheral blood samples and 40 normal colonic mucosa samples).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT

#### STRENGTHS
- **Population-matched control screening and familial cosegregation:** All three missense variants (exons 5, 6, and 8) were screened against 100 ethnically matched Korean control individuals (200 chromosomes), and S232P was shown to cosegregate with PJS across the two affected sisters while being absent in two unaffected siblings in family SNU-P5.
- **Clonal bi-directional sequencing:** Subcloning PCR products into pCR-TOPO vectors and sequencing at least 10 individual colonies in both directions cleanly resolves heterozygous frameshift and substitution alleles (Figs. 1 and 2).
- **Appropriate restraint on functional claims:** The authors explicitly state that aberrant splicing could not be confirmed for the intron 1 `ag` $\to$ `cg` mutation due to lack of patient mRNA, and that functional kinase assays are required to confirm the pathogenicity of the missense variants.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Sensitivity limitations of PCR-SSCP as a primary screening filter:** Only samples displaying abnormal band shifts on room-temperature 6% polyacrylamide/10% glycerol SSCP gels were sequenced. PCR-SSCP typically has a point-mutation sensitivity of 70–85% (especially when exons 4 and 5 are co-amplified and digested with *Mae*III), and both PCR-SSCP and direct exon PCR completely miss large genomic deletions or exon rearrangements (which account for a substantial fraction of *STK11*-mutation-negative PJS cases). Direct sequencing of all 9 exons in the 5 SSCP-negative probands would have been far more definitive in such a small cohort ($n=10$).
- **Lack of parental DNA to confirm de novo status in "sporadic" missense cases:** For the two sporadic missense probands (SNU-P2 with E256A and HYU-P2 with P324L), parental DNA was not tested to demonstrate that the missense mutations arose *de novo*, leaving open the possibility of rare private non-pathogenic missense variants or incomplete penetrance.
- **No in vitro kinase assay or LOH analysis in polyps:** While the authors note that codons 232, 256, and 324 are conserved in *Xenopus* XEEK1 and mouse Lkb1 within the kinase catalytic core (codons 50–337), neither loss of heterozygosity (LOH) in the patients' resected hamartomatous polyps nor in vitro autophosphorylation/kinase activity was assayed.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Five of ten Korean PJS probands harbor rare germline *STK11* sequence alterations (one frameshift, one canonical splice-acceptor site mutation, and three conserved catalytic-domain missense variants). `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated by SSCP, cloning, and Sanger sequencing chromatograms/autoradiograms, with absence in 100 controls.
- **Claim 2:** Germline missense mutations in the *STK11* kinase domain occur in PJS patients in addition to truncating mutations, though their effects on protein function require further examination. `[Well Supported]` — Appropriately hedged and supported by evolutionary conservation across human STK11, mouse Lkb1, and *Xenopus* XEEK1.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Clinical and Molecular Tables:** Table 1 provides complete patient-level clinical data (sex, age, age at first symptom, pigmentation, polyp distribution, polyp count, family history, and laparotomy count), and Table 2 summarizes exact codons, nucleotide changes, and predicted amino acid consequences.
- **Primer Reference:** PCR primers are referenced to Jenne et al. and reaction conditions (annealing at 62°C, *Mae*III digestion for exon 4–5) are fully reproducible.
