### SUMMARY
### 1. Executive Summary
This classical molecular carcinogenesis study investigates *ras* proto-oncogene activation and mutation spectra across two experimental animal models and human skin tumors: (1) murine thymic lymphomas induced by low-LET gamma radiation versus high-LET fast neutron radiation, and (2) keratoacanthomas (benign, self-regressing squamous neoplasms) induced in rabbit skin by 7,12-dimethylbenz[a]anthracene (DMBA) alongside human keratoacanthomas and squamous cell carcinomas (SCCs). The authors demonstrate that gamma and neutron radiation produce distinct *ras* point mutation spectra in murine thymic lymphomas, including a novel activating mutation at codon 146 of *K-ras* in neutron-induced tumors. In the skin model, DMBA-induced rabbit keratoacanthomas exhibit a high frequency of *H-ras* codon 61 mutations, while human keratoacanthomas harbor frequent *H-ras* mutations at both codons 12 and 61—occurring at a higher frequency in self-regressing keratoacanthomas than in malignant human SCCs, supporting a role for *H-ras* signaling in epithelial differentiation as well as proliferation.

### STRENGTHS
- **Comparative Mutagen-Specific Oncogene Spectrum Design:** Comparing high-LET neutron vs. low-LET gamma irradiation in the same target tissue (murine thymic lymphoma) and comparing chemical (DMBA) rabbit keratoacanthomas with human keratoacanthomas and SCCs links physical/chemical mutagen type to specific *ras* codon hotspots.
- **Discovery of Non-Canonical *K-ras* Codon 146 Activation:** Identifying *K-ras* codon 146 (near the guanine-ring binding pocket `SAK` motif) as a radiation-induced oncogenic hotspot expands *ras* mutational analysis beyond canonical codons 12, 13, and 61.
