### 1. Executive Summary
This study sequences and compares four partial gene regions—glycoprotein (`G`, 669 bp), non-virion (`Nv`, 366 bp), phosphoprotein (`P`, 539/541 bp), and matrix (`M`, 564 bp)—across 12 isolates of Viral Hemorrhagic Septicemia virus substrain IVb (`VHSv-IVb`) collected from Laurentian Great Lakes fishes, alongside a broader literature compilation of 19 unique `G`-gene haplotypes (`a`–`s`). Using maximum parsimony (`PAUP*`), maximum likelihood (`GARLI`), Bayesian inference (`MrBayes`), statistical parsimony haplotype networks (`TCS`), `Tajima's D`, and `OMEGAMAP` ($d_N/d_S$), the authors conclude that the Novirhabdovirus-specific `Nv` gene evolves ~7 times faster than `G` and ~15 times faster than `P` and `M`, while exhibiting star-like quasispecies diversification across Lake Erie and the Great Lakes. While characterizing multi-gene diversity beyond the standard `G` gene in `VHSv-IVb` is biologically worthwhile, the manuscript contains severe internal data contradictions (including transposed substitution rates between Table 2 and the text, and contradictory sequence lengths and amino acid substitution counts across the Abstract, Results, and Table 2) as well as invalid statistical tests that treat all $\binom{12}{2} = 66$ pairwise distances among 12 isolates as independent observations ($df = 130$).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Transposed Substitution Rates Between Table 2 and Abstract/Results Text:**
  - In the **Abstract (line 7)**, **Results (line 142)**, and **Discussion (chunk 2, line 3)**, the substitution rate ($k$, substitutions per site per year) is reported as **$2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ for `Nv`** and **$2.8 \times 10^{-4}$ for `G`** (which is the basis for the central claim that `Nv` evolves ~7x faster than `G`: $2.0 \times 10^{-3} / 2.8 \times 10^{-4} = 7.14$).
  - However, in **Table 2 (line 110)**, the digits `2.0` and `2.8` are **transposed**: `G` is reported as **$2.0 \times 10^{-4}$** and `Nv` is reported as **$2.8 \times 10^{-3}$**! Even worse, in **Discussion chunk 2, line 9**, the authors copy the transposed value from Table 2 and write *"The current study estimates the G-gene's rate of substitution at ~2.0x10-4"*—contradicting their own Abstract and Results!
- **Contradictory Sequence Lengths and Amino Acid Change Counts Across Abstract, Results, and Table 2:**
  - **`P`-gene length:** Methods (line 67) states `541 nucleotides` for the `P`-gene (`180` codons), whereas Results (line 86) and Table 2 (line 104) state `539 nucleotides`.
  - **`P`-gene and `M`-gene amino acid substitutions:** The **Abstract (line 7)** states *"four P-gene haplotypes with **two** amino acid changes, and six M-gene haplotypes with **three** amino acid differences."* Yet **Table 2 (line 115)**, **Results (lines 97, 150)**, and **Discussion (chunk 2, line 5)** state the exact opposite: **three** (`3`) amino acid changes for `P` and **two** (`2`) amino acid changes for `M` (whereas Results line 98 claims *"The M-gene had six haplotypes (a, b, h, j–l), **all varying by single amino acid changes**"*!).
- **Severe Phylogenetic Pseudoreplication in Pairwise $t$-Tests and ANCOVAs ($df = 130$):** In Results lines 135–138 and Figure 4, the authors test whether transitions ($T_i$) outnumber transversions ($T_v$) and compare substitution patterns across genes by taking all $\binom{12}{2} = 66$ pairwise comparisons among the 12 isolates and running independent two-sample $t$-tests ($t = 2.41\text{--}19.30, df = 130$, since $66 + 66 - 2 = 130$) and ANCOVAs ($F = 11.78\text{--}51.63, df = 3, 259$). Because 66 pairwise comparisons among 12 sequences are derived from only 11 independent tree branches, treating them as 66 independent observations per group inflates sample size ~6-fold and invalidates all reported $p$-values.
- **Unreliable Substitution Rate Estimation Without a Dated Coalescent Clock Model:** The authors estimate substitution rates ($k$) by dividing pairwise Jukes-Cantor distances (`MEGA5`) by sampling time differences across only 12 isolates (many collected in the same year or separated by 1–7 years) rather than fitting a tip-dated Bayesian molecular clock (`BEAST`) with confidence intervals. Furthermore, they run `OMEGAMAP` (a coalescent population genetics model designed for recombining bacterial/eukaryotic chromosomes) on a non-recombining negative-sense single-stranded RNA rhabdovirus of only $n = 12$ sequences.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Among the 12 `VHSv-IVb` isolates, the `Nv` gene exhibits higher nucleotide and amino acid polymorphism (12 unique haplotypes, 26 amino acid changes across 122 codons) than `G` (10 haplotypes, 8 AA changes), `P` (4 haplotypes, 2–3 AA changes), and `M` (6 haplotypes, 2 AA changes).* `[Well Supported]` — Despite the off-by-one discrepancy between the Abstract and Table 2 for `P` and `M`, the much higher sequence diversity of `Nv` relative to `G`, `P`, and `M` in this 12-isolate panel is clear.
- **Claim 2:** *`Nv` evolves at a substitution rate of $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ (or $2.8 \times 10^{-3}$) substitutions/site/year, ~7x faster than `G` and ~15x faster than `P` and `M`.* `[Fragile]` — As noted in the Discussion (`Question 2`, line 24), haplotypes `a` and `b` are already highly divergent in `Nv` (differing by 9 amino acids) when first detected in 2003–2005; if `a` and `b` represent pre-existing ancestral lineages introduced into the Great Lakes rather than de novo divergence since 2003, dividing their pairwise distance by collection-year difference severely overestimates the substitution rate $k$.
- **Claim 3:** *Two new 2012 Lake Erie isolates (`k` and `l`) isolated from asymptomatic fish with erratic swimming indicate that `VHSv-IVb` has evolved reduced virulence to evade host immunity.* `[Overstated]` —Observing 2 field-collected fish without external hemorrhaging does not demonstrate an evolutionary reduction in viral virulence without controlled in vivo challenge experiments comparing isolates `k`/`l` against `MI03GL` (`a`).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Positive:** GenBank accession numbers for all 19 `G`-gene haplotypes (`a`–`s`) and the new `k` and `l` sequences (`KF928905`–`KF928925`) are listed in Table 1, and software packages (`MEGA v5.0`, `PAUP* v4.0b10`, `GARLI`, `MrBayes v3.1.2`, `TCS v1.21`, `Arlequin v3.5.1.3`, `OMEGAMAP`) are cited.
- **Missing:** PCR primer sequences (`Table A`), isolate collection metadata (`Table B`), and haplotype variable site tables (`Table C`) are relegated to an external Supporting Information file (`S1 File`) not included in the main text, and the numerical contradictions between Table 2 and the main text must be reconciled.
