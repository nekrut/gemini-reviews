### 1. Executive Summary
This study (`TIME_ANON_0128`) conducts a genome-wide association study (GWAS) of perioperative myocardial infarction (`PMI`, defined as postoperative day 1 `CK-MB >= 10x ULN`) following primary isolated coronary artery bypass grafting (`CABG`) with cardiopulmonary bypass. Using the `Illumina Human610-Quad BeadChip` (`534,390` QC-passed SNPs), the authors analyzed a Stage I discovery cohort (`GeneMAGIC`/`PREVENT-IV`, $N=1,433$: 112 PMI cases and 1,321 controls) and a Stage II expanded combined cohort that pooled the 1,433 Stage I patients with 622 additional European-ancestry CABG patients (`PEGASUS`, total $N=2,055$: 225 PMI cases and 1,830 controls). Adjusting for clinical covariates and 10 `EIGENSTRAT` principal components in `PLINK 1.07`, no SNP reached genome-wide significance ($p < 9.09 \times 10^{-8}$) in Stage I or Stage II, but 8 SNPs among the 521 Stage I candidates ($p < 0.001$) achieved $p < 9.6 \times 10^{-5}$ ($0.05 / 521$) in the Stage II pooled analysis, mapping to *PAPPA2* (`rs10454444`, $p = 2.43 \times 10^{-6}$), *SEC24D* (`rs4834703`, $p = 6.80 \times 10^{-6}$; `rs6822035`, $p = 9.55 \times 10^{-6}$), *HDAC4* (`rs10200850`, $p = 8.18 \times 10^{-6}$), and two intergenic loci (`rs2303970`, `rs609418`). While this represents an important clinical cohort resource for a complex surgical phenotype, the statistical design suffers from a fundamental flaw: applying an independent-replication Bonferroni threshold ($0.05 / 521 = 9.6 \times 10^{-5}$) to a **joint Stage I + Stage II pooled dataset** in which $69.7\%$ of the samples ($1,433 / 2,055$) are the exact same discovery samples already selected for having $p < 0.001$, without ever reporting the independent association $p$-values in the 622 Stage II replication patients alone. In addition, several $p$-values are misquoted or swapped between phenotypes across the Abstract, Results, Table 2, and Discussion.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Invalid Application of the $0.05 / 521$ Replication Threshold to a Non-Independent Joint Dataset:**
  - In Stage I ($N=1,433$, 112 cases), `534,390` SNPs were tested and 521 SNPs were selected strictly because they satisfied $P_{\text{Stage I}} < 0.001$.
  - In Stage II ($N=2,055$, 225 cases), the authors did **not** test the 521 SNPs in the 622 new `PEGASUS` patients (113 cases / 509 controls) independently; instead, they pooled the 1,433 Stage I patients with the 622 new patients (`"an expanded data set in which an additional 622 patients ... were added to the discovery data set, leading to a total of 2055 patients"`, line 48) and declared statistical significance at $P_{\text{Joint}} < 0.05 / 521 = 9.6 \times 10^{-5}$ (line 63, 75).
  - **Mathematical Audit of Type I Error Inflation:** Because the 1,433 Stage I samples comprise $70\%$ of the 2,055 Stage II samples and were pre-filtered on $P_{\text{Stage I}} < 0.001$ ($|Z_{\text{Stage I}}| > 3.29$), the conditional null distribution of $Z_{\text{Joint}} \approx \sqrt{0.70}\,Z_{\text{Stage I}} + \sqrt{0.30}\,Z_{\text{Rep}}$ under $H_0^{\text{Rep}}$ ($Z_{\text{Rep}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$) has mean $\ge 0.837 \times 3.29 = 2.75$ and variance $0.30$. Even if a SNP has **zero true effect** in the 622 new patients (e.g., $Z_{\text{Rep}} = 2.1$, $p_{\text{Rep}} = 0.036$, far above $9.6 \times 10^{-5}$), its pooled statistic reaches $Z_{\text{Joint}} \approx 3.90$ ($P_{\text{Joint}} < 9.6 \times 10^{-5}$)! As established in classic two-stage GWAS theory (Skol et al., *Nat Genet* [2006]), a joint analysis of Stage I + Stage II must be evaluated against the **genome-wide significance threshold** ($p < 5 \times 10^{-8}$ or $0.05 / 534,390 = 9.35 \times 10^{-8}$), whereas the $0.05 / 521$ threshold is valid *only* for an independent test restricted to the 622 replication samples alone.
  - Indeed, inspection of **Table 1** reveals that for `rs4834703` (*SEC24D*), the Stage I odds ratio was $\text{OR} = 2.27$ ($p = 3.13 \times 10^{-5}$), whereas in the pooled Stage II dataset the effect size attenuated to $\text{OR} = 1.98$ ($p = 6.80 \times 10^{-6}$), indicating that the association in the 622 added patients alone was much weaker ($p \sim 0.03\text{–}0.05$) and did not independently pass $p < 9.6 \times 10^{-5}$. The manuscript never reports the separate association statistics for the 622 replication cohort.
- **Direct Numerical Contradictions Between Abstract, Results, Table 2, and Discussion:**
  1. **Swapped Phenotypes and Misquoted $P$-Values in the Abstract:** In line 15, the Abstract states: `"A secondary analysis using CK-MB as a quantitative trait (minimum p=1.26x10^-3 for rs609418), or a dichotomised phenotype based on extreme CK-MB values (minimum p=7.72x10^-6 for rs4834703) supported these findings."` Comparing this against **Table 2** (lines 98–105) and `Results` (line 91) reveals that the Abstract got the two phenotypes backwards: $p = 7.72 \times 10^{-6}$ for `rs4834703` is the p-value for **CK-MB as a quantitative trait** (its extreme-quartile p-value in Table 2 is $p = 0.002$), while `rs609418` has $p = 2.26 \times 10^{-4}$ for quantitative CK-MB and $p = 0.001$ for extreme CK-MB (neither of which is $1.26 \times 10^{-3}$).
  2. **Pasted Wrong $P$-Value in Discussion:** In the Discussion (line 129), the authors write: `"when CK-MB was evaluated as a quantitative trait (rs4834703, p=2.43x10^-6)"`—here $2.43 \times 10^{-6}$ is the Stage II primary PMI $p$-value of `rs10454444` (*PAPPA2*) from **Table 1**, not the quantitative CK-MB $p$-value of `rs4834703` ($7.72 \times 10^{-6}$ in **Table 2**).
- **Direction-of-Effect Anomaly in Haplotype Table 3:** In **Table 3** (line 116), for the 2-SNP window `rs6822035–rs10518325`, the reference haplotype `C-A` has frequency `0.5927` in PMI cases vs `0.685` in controls, and the `C-G` haplotype has a *lower* frequency in PMI cases (`0.009083`) than in controls (`0.02636`). Despite `C-G` being depleted in cases relative to `C-A` (crude $\text{OR} = \frac{0.009083 / 0.5927}{0.02636 / 0.685} = 0.40$), **Table 3** reports an adjusted $\text{OR} = 1.39$ ($95\%\text{ CI } 0.67\text{–}2.90$).
- **Cohort Heterogeneity & Post-Hoc Covariate Selection:** The PMI incidence jumped more than two-fold between the Stage I discovery cohort ($112 / 1,433 = 7.8\%$) and the 622 added Stage II patients ($113 / 622 = 18.2\%$), yet no cohort indicator variable (`GeneMAGIC` vs `PEGASUS`) is mentioned among the regression covariates, and clinical covariates were selected via unadjusted univariate screening within each stage.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Eight common SNPs in `PAPPA2`, `SEC24D`, `HDAC4`, and two intergenic regions are statistically significant susceptibility loci for perioperative MI after Bonferroni correction.* **`[Overstated]`** — None of the 8 SNPs reached genome-wide significance ($p < 5 \times 10^{-8}$) in the joint $N=2,055$ analysis (best $p = 2.43 \times 10^{-6}$), and the $p < 9.6 \times 10^{-5}$ threshold is invalid when applied to a pooled Stage I + Stage II sample rather than an independent replication cohort. They should be framed strictly as suggestive candidate loci ($p < 10^{-5}$).
- **Claim 2:** *Common variants at the 9p21 CAD susceptibility locus (including `rs10116277`) are not associated with PMI in patients with established CAD undergoing CABG.* **`[Well Supported]`** — The cohort has $80\%$ power for $\text{OR} \ge 2.0$ (and good power for moderate effects at high-MAF 9p21 SNPs), and the null finding aligns well with independent literature distinguishing atherosclerotic plaque burden from acute peri-procedural myonecrosis.
- **Claim 3:** *Pathway enrichment implicates extracellular matrix remodeling, IGF signaling, and ER-to-Golgi COPII transport in PMI.* **`[Moderately Supported]`** — Biologically plausible hypotheses, though pathway enrichment (`MetaCore`) run on sub-threshold GWAS hits ($p < 0.001$) without LD-clumping/permutation correction for gene length and SNP density can inflate enrichment $p$-values for large multi-SNP genes.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Summary Statistics & Metadata:** **Tables 1–3** report chromosome, rsID, physical base-pair position, MAF in cases and controls, ORs, $95\%\text{ CIs}$, and $p$-values for the top 8 SNPs, and extensive online supplementary tables (`S1–S10`) and figures (`S1–S7`) are referenced.
- **Missing Replication Breakdown & Data Availability:** Separate summary statistics (MAF, OR, $95\%\text{ CI}$, $p$-value) for the 622 Stage II replication patients alone are omitted, genome build (`NCBI36/hg18` vs `GRCh37/hg19`) and effect/reference alleles for **Tables 1 and 2** are not listed in the main tables, and no dbGaP accession number is provided for the full GWAS summary statistics.
