### 1. Executive Summary
This concise, well-controlled experimental virology manuscript characterizes the functional requirements of human and African green monkey (AGM) angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) for severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus (SARS-CoV) Spike (S)-mediated cellular entry using single-cycle `pNL4-3env*nef*-luc` lentiviral pseudotypes. Across a panel of 9 cell lines (4 permissive: Huh-7, Hep-2, Vero E6, 293T; 5 refractory: HeLa, HOS, C8166, BL41, NIH3T3), the authors demonstrate by nested RT-PCR that endogenous ACE2 mRNA expression strictly correlates with susceptibility to SARS-CoV S-driven pseudotype infection, while control pseudotypes bearing VSV-G or MLV envelope infect all lines efficiently. Ectopic expression of human or AGM ACE2 (cloned from Vero E6 cells), or a soluble human ACE2 ectodomain variant truncated at codon 740 (`sACE2`, which shows reduced but detectable cell-surface retention), rescues or enhances SARS-CoV S-driven infection without affecting VSV-G or MLV entry or human aminopeptidase N (CD13, the HCoV-229E receptor)-transfected cells. Conversely, pre-incubation of pseudotypes with concentrated culture supernatants containing `sACE2` dose-dependently blocks SARS-CoV S-mediated infection while sparing VSV-G and MLV pseudotypes. Finally, the authors construct four progressive C-terminal cytoplasmic tail truncation mutants of human ACE2 (`ACE2-1-790`, `ACE2-1-779`, `ACE2-1-775`, and `ACE2-1-771`) that sequentially eliminate consensus casein kinase II (S787) and tyrosine phosphorylation (`Y781`, `ASD777-779`) motifs, showing via flow cytometry, Western blot, and pseudotype entry assays that the cytoplasmic tail is dispensable for cell surface expression and SARS-CoV S receptor function in 293T cells. The experimental controls are clean and claims are well calibrated to the pseudotype assay system.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Experimental Design & Specificity Controls:** Every pseudotype infection experiment includes parallel negative controls (`pcDNA3` empty vector pseudotypes and mock-transfected target cells) and positive envelope specificity controls (VSV-G and MLV envelope pseudotypes, plus non-receptor control CD13/aminopeptidase N). All transduction assays were performed in quadruplicate with standardized virus inputs (`10 ng` or `50 ng` p24 antigen per well) and repeated with at least three independent virus stocks, reporting mean ± standard deviation (SD).
- **Soluble Receptor (`sACE2`) Surface Retention Paradox:** A notable technical finding appropriately investigated by the authors is that stop-codon insertion at position 740 (`sACE2`, upstream of the predicted transmembrane domain `I741–V763`) yields secreted protein in culture supernatants that blocks viral entry when pre-incubated with virions (Fig. 3A), yet transient transfection of `sACE2` into 293T cells still produces detectable cell-surface staining by FACS (Fig. 2B) and enhances S-driven entry (Fig. 2C). While the authors discuss potential GPI-anchoring or non-covalent cell-surface association, a biochemical test (e.g., phosphatidylinositol-specific phospholipase C [PI-PLC] treatment or high-salt/carbonate wash of membranes) would definitively resolve how `sACE2` remains tethered to the plasma membrane.
- **Absence of Formal Hypothesis Testing Metrics:** While the biological effect sizes across permissive vs. non-permissive lines and ACE2 vs. pcDNA3/CD13 transfections span 1.5 to 3 orders of magnitude in luciferase activity (making statistical significance self-evident), formal statistical test outputs (e.g., one-way ANOVA or Dunnett's post-hoc test comparing the four cytoplasmic tail truncation mutants against wild-type ACE2 in Fig. 4C, or $IC_{50}$ curve-fitting for `sACE2` neutralization in Fig. 3A) are not reported.
- **Pseudotype vs. Replication-Competent SARS-CoV:** All entry assays rely exclusively on lentiviral pseudotypes (`pNL4-3env*nef*-luc`) and transient overexpression in 293T cells; the authors appropriately acknowledge in the Discussion that subtle trafficking or signaling roles of the ACE2 cytoplasmic tail could still matter in polarized primary airway epithelia or under low-level endogenous receptor expression with replication-competent SARS-CoV.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Endogenous ACE2 mRNA expression across cell lines correlates with susceptibility to SARS-CoV S-mediated pseudotype infection, and both human and Vero E6 (AGM) ACE2 function as specific entry receptors.* `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated via nested RT-PCR (with `GAPDH` normalization and plasmid-free negative controls to guard against contamination), envelope-matched VSV-G/MLV controls, and non-receptor CD13 controls.
- **Claim 2:** *Soluble ACE2 ectodomain (`sACE2`, residues 1–740) secreted into culture supernatants specifically neutralizes SARS-CoV S-driven pseudotype infection in a dose-dependent manner.* `[Well Supported]` — Fig. 3A shows specific dose-dependent inhibition of SARS-CoV S pseudotypes across `0–80 ul` concentrated `sACE2` supernatant with no effect on VSV-G or MLV pseudotypes.
- **Claim 3:** *The cytoplasmic tail of ACE2 (residues 771–805), including conserved tyrosine (`Y781`) and serine (`S787`) phosphorylation motifs, is not required for surface expression or SARS-CoV S-driven entry.* `[Well Supported]` — Verified across four progressive stop-codon truncations (`1-790`, `1-779`, `1-775`, `1-771`) showing indistinguishable expression by Western blot (Fig. 4A), comparable surface geometric mean fluorescence intensity by FACS (Fig. 4B), and ~10-fold enhancement of S-driven infection matching wild-type ACE2 (Fig. 4C).

- **Primer Sequences & Construct Definitions:** Full nucleotide sequences for all 11 oligonucleotide primers (`5'huACE2`, `3'huACE2`, `3'huACE2sol`, `3'ACE2-1-790`, `3'ACE2-1-779`, `3'ACE2-1-775`, `3'ACE2-1-771`, outer RT-PCR primers `5'ACE2out`/`3'ACE2out`, and inner nested primers `5'ACE2in`/`3'ACE2in`), restriction sites (`BamHI`, `XhoI`), antibodies (`R&D Systems` anti-ACE2, `Dako` anti-CD13), and exact truncation coordinates are completely specified in Materials and Methods.
