### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the phylogeny, taxonomy, and historical biogeography of the freshwater gastropod family Pomatiopsidae (focusing on the subfamilies Pomatiopsinae and Triculinae, which include intermediate hosts of *Schistosoma japonicum*, *S. mekongi*, and *S. malayensis*). Combining new field sampling across 4 countries and 9 drainage systems (16 species across 6 genera sequenced for the first time, deposited under GenBank `KC832687–KC832728`) with GenBank records (38 taxa total), the authors analyze a concatenated 1,139 bp mitochondrial alignment (`cox1` 598 bp + `rrnL` 541 bp, plus coded indels) using maximum likelihood (`RAxML`, `PAUP*`), Bayesian inference (`MrBayes`, `BEAST`), and dynamic-homology parsimony (`POY5`). They evaluate two competing biogeographical models—the "Tibet origin" hypothesis (Indian craton rafting and West-to-East radiation) versus the "Hunan origin" hypothesis (Australasian/Eastern Gondwanan rafting and East-to-West radiation)—concluding that neither topological constraint fits the tree, while BEAST divergence time estimates favor the Hunan timeline (9/13 TMRCA comparisons). While the field collection, anatomical identification, and sequence deposition are commendable, the analytical framework suffers from relying on a single linked mitochondrial locus with severe third-codon saturation across Jurassic-to-Pleistocene timescales, *post hoc* editing of *a priori* topological constraints after initial rejection, and an artificial split of BEAST molecular clock runs ("deep" vs. "recent") that yielded five-fold incompatible substitution rate estimates.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Single-Locus Mitochondrial Inference & Saturation Across Deep Time:** Both `cox1` and `rrnL` reside on the same maternally inherited mitochondrial molecule (a single genealogical locus of 1,139 bp). Applying the Incongruence Length Difference (ILD) test to two linked mitochondrial genes is theoretically uninformative regarding locus congruence. More importantly, `cox1` third codon positions exhibit severe substitution saturation (Figure 2; BEAST third-codon rate of $25.54 \times 10^{-8}$ substitutions/site/year vs. $0.71$ and $0.09$ for first and second positions in Table 3), which degrades basal topological resolution across 110–170 Ma divergences and explains why key tribes (e.g., Jullieniini, *Tricula hortensis*, *Jullienia rolfbrandti*) jump drastically between `RAxML`, `MrBayes`, and `BEAST` trees.
- **Post-Hoc Modification of *A Priori* Constraint Topologies:** In testing the Tibet vs. Hunan hypotheses, the authors report that both unconstrained ML searches (`PAUP*` $\ln L = -11378.02$) and Shimodaira-Hasegawa (SH) tests significantly rejected the Tibet ($\ln L = -11449.67, P = 0.0024$) and Hunan ($\ln L = -11447.09, P = 0.0056$) constraint trees, with no significant difference between the two hypotheses ($P = 0.9999$). In the Bayesian topological test, $0/200,100$ posterior trees matched either *a priori* hypothesis. The authors then modified both constraint trees *post hoc* using four topological exemptions informed by the observed anomalies (e.g., forcing *Jullienia rolfbrandti* into a basal non-Pomatiopsidae polytomy and placing *Tricula xiaolongmenensis* at the root of Pomatiopsinae), after which $35/200,100$ trees ($P = 0.0002$) matched the edited Hunan constraint vs. $0/200,100$ ($P = 0.0000$) for Tibet. Even after *post hoc* editing, $P = 0.0002$ represents a decisive rejection of the Hunan topology by 99.98% of the posterior distribution.
- **Incompatible Split BEAST Clock Runs ("Deep" vs. "Recent") and Calibration Circularity:** Because including recent calibration priors produced "implausible Oligo-Miocene divergence dates" for deep nodes (Hydrobiidae/Amnicolidae), the authors split their BEAST dating into separate "Deep divergence" (using only `Root` and `Core-Group` priors) and "Most recent divergence" (adding `Malaya` at $2.0 \pm 1.01$ Ma and `Khorat` at $0.8 \pm 0.10$ Ma) runs, and then merged the resulting node ages into a single Table 4 and Figure 6. Table 3 reveals that the inferred substitution rates differ by a factor of $\sim 5\times$ between the two runs (`cox1` first codon: $0.142$ vs. $0.706$; second codon: $0.017$ vs. $0.087$; `rrnL`: $0.270$ vs. $1.287$). Furthermore, `tmrca(Khorat)` in Table 4 ($0.868$ Ma, 95% HPD $0.676\text{--}1.078$) is evaluated as supporting prediction `H15` (Hunan hypothesis), even though `Khorat` ($0.8 \pm 0.10$ Ma) was explicitly entered as a normal calibration prior on that exact divergence in the "recent" BEAST run!
- **Minor Table Formatting Artifact:** In Table 4 (`tmrca(Oncomelania)`), the standard error was scrubbed by automated year-redaction (`22.4588 ± 0.[YEAR]`).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Transfer of *Erhaia* to Amnicolidae and Non-Monophyly of *Tricula* and *Neotricula*:** `[Well Supported]` The placement of *Erhaia* sp. within an amnicolid clade alongside *Akiyoshia* and *Bythinella* (`PP = 1.0`, ML bootstrap $73\%$), as well as the paraphyly of nominal *Tricula* (splitting Yunnan *Delavaya*-allied species from northern Thai *Tricula*) and *Neotricula* (nesting *Manningiella* within the *N. aperta* clade), are robustly supported across methods and corroborated by anatomical traits.
- **Superior Support for the Hunan vs. Tibet Phylogeographical Hypothesis:** `[Fragile]` While the estimated Pliocene–Pleistocene burst of triculine cladogenesis is plausible, the claim that the molecular dating supports the Hunan hypothesis in 9 of 13 comparisons relies on splicing together two incompatible BEAST clock regimes and includes calibrated nodes (`Khorat`, `Malaya`) that are not independent of the geological/parasite-divergence assumptions being tested.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Exceptional metadata transparency in Table 5, providing exact GPS coordinates, water systems, taxonomic authorities, and GenBank accession numbers (`KC832687–KC832728`) for all newly sequenced specimens alongside all outgroup accessions. Software versions (`RAxML v7.3.2`, `MrBayes v3.2.1`, `BEAST v1.7.5`, `jModelTest v2.1.2`, `DAMBE v4.5.20`, `P4 v0.90`, `POY v5.0.0`) and prior distributions (Tables 2–3) are thoroughly documented.
- **Limitations:** Multiple sequence alignments and BEAST XML / MrBayes nexus files are not deposited in an external repository (e.g., TreeBASE or Dryad).
