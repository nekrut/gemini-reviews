### 1. Executive Summary
This study constructs a large-scale fossil-dated chloroplast megaphylogeny encompassing 36,101 angiosperm species (8,399 genera, 426 families, and all recognized orders, plus 5 gymnosperm outgroups) using two standard plastid barcoding regions: `rbcL` (`683 bp`, sampled for 26,811 species [74%]) and `matK` including `trnK` (`4,285 bp`, sampled for 31,391 species [87%]). The authors generate 820 new barcode sequences (including 468 African tree specimens) and mine the remainder from GenBank/BOLD with automated BLAST and taxonomic validation (`Taxize` / `Taxonstand`), infer a maximum-likelihood tree in `RAxML v7.4.2` using an `APG IV` ordinal backbone constraint, and time-calibrate the phylogeny via penalized likelihood in `treePL` using 56 fossil calibration points (`Table 1`). While assembling a curated 36,101-species alignment with minimal locus missingness and making the dated nexus phylogeny publicly available is a useful community resource for macroecology, the phylogenetic and molecular-clock pipeline contains several serious methodological flaws: inappropriate application of the Lewis ascertainment bias correction to full nucleotide alignments, mechanical conversion of `BEAUti` lognormal priors into uniform $+16.82\text{ Myr}$ hard maximum age bounds in `treePL`, and underestimation of node-age confidence intervals by fixing the tree topology across bootstrap dating replicates.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Misapplication of the Lewis Ascertainment Bias Correction (`RAxML` Line 47):**
  - In Materials and Methods (line 47), the authors state that ML tree inference was conducted in `RAxML v7.4.2` *"under the general time-reversible (GTR) substitution model with gamma rate heterogeneity and lewis correction."*
  - Lewis's (2001) conditional likelihood ascertainment bias correction is mathematically formulated for discrete morphological characters or SNP-only matrices where invariant sites have been stripped out prior to analysis. Applying Lewis ascertainment correction to standard contiguous DNA sequence alignments (`rbcL` + `matK/trnK`, `4,968 bp` total, where `matK` retains 364 invariant characters) distorts branch-length estimation and site-rate scaling.
  - Furthermore, the authors report (line 99) that *"Within rbcL, all characters were variable (100%), whereas for matK (incl. trnK) 3,921 characters (91.5%) were variable."* Because `rbcL` is a highly conserved housekeeping gene (much more conserved than `matK`, as the authors themselves note on line 24), observing 100% variable sites in `rbcL` vs. 91.5% in `matK` is simply a sample-size saturation effect across 26,811 GenBank sequences (potentially compounded by unmasked terminal sequencing errors in GenBank trace ends).
- **Arbitrary $+16.82\text{ Myr}$ Hard Maximum Age Constraints in `treePL` (`Table 1`, Line 54):**
  - Penalized likelihood in `treePL` requires hard minimum and hard maximum age bounds rather than soft probabilistic prior densities. To invent hard maximum bounds for all 56 fossil calibrations (`Table 1`), the authors plugged each fossil's minimum stratigraphic age (`offset`) into `BEAUti v1.10` with an arbitrary lognormal distribution (`mean = 1.0`, `SD = 1.0` in real/log space) and took the 95th percentile (`offset + 16.82 Myr`) as a **hard maximum age constraint**.
  - Inspection of `Table 1` confirms that for every single one of the 56 clades—whether a Late Miocene genus (`Valeriana`, min `11.6 Ma`, max `28.42 Ma`), an Eocene family (`Apocynaceae`, min `47.8 Ma`, max `64.62 Ma`), or an Early Cretaceous order (`Canellales`, min `125.0 Ma`, max `141.8 Ma`; `Araceae`, min `125.0 Ma`, max `141.8 Ma`)—the difference $\text{Max. age} - \text{Min. age}$ is identically $16.80\text{–}16.82\text{ Myr}$!
  - Paleobotanically and statistically, imposing a hard ceiling exactly $16.82\text{ Myr}$ above the oldest known fossil of a clade (`cr.` or `st.`) has no empirical justification: fossil preservation is notoriously patchy across clades and epochs, and treating a narrow $+16.82\text{ Myr}$ offset as an absolute hard maximum bound forces `treePL` to compress internal node ages against artificial ceilings across the entire tree.
- **Underestimated Node-Age Confidence Intervals (`TreeAnnotator`, Line 94):**
  - Only ~26% of branches in the 36,101-taxon tree achieved bootstrap support $> 75\%$ (line 99), reflecting the limited phylogenetic signal of two short plastid loci (`4,968 bp`) across 36,101 tips.
  - To compute 95% confidence intervals on node ages, the authors generated 1,000 bootstrap pseudo-replicates *"using the ML topology of the earlier heuristic analysis as constraint"* and dated each fixed-topology tree in `treePL`. Because the topology was locked to the single point-estimate ML tree, the resulting 95% CIs capture only branch-length variance and completely ignore the massive topological uncertainty across the 74% of weakly supported nodes.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **`[Well Supported]` Taxonomic and sequence curation substantially improves upon earlier GenBank-mined plant megaphylogenies:** By combining automated `Megablast` anomaly filtering, reading-frame translation checks, `Taxize`/`Taxonstand` synonym harmonization, order-by-order manual `MAFFT` profile alignment, and an `APG IV` ordinal backbone constraint, the authors avoid the gross taxonomic misplacements that plagued earlier automated supermatrices.
- **`[Moderately Supported]` Utility as a broad phylogenetic backbone for community ecology:** At the genus and family levels, the tree provides dense taxon coverage (54.6% of angiosperm genera and 94.5% of families) and appropriately acknowledges that a plastid-only dataset represents a maternal chloroplast gene tree rather than a nuclear species tree.
- **`[Fragile]` Precision of internal divergence time estimates and 95% confidence intervals:** Because every calibrated node was trapped inside an identical $16.82\text{ Myr}$ hard-bounded window (`Table 1`) and topological uncertainty was excluded from the bootstrap dating procedure, node heights and error bars should be treated as rough approximations rather than precise chronostratigraphic estimates.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Data availability and transparency are excellent: all 36,101 species and their GenBank accessions (including the 820 newly sequenced accessions), the `RAxML` `APG IV` constraint topology, the 500 `treePL` cross-validation smoothing-parameter replicates, the ML bootstrap consensus tree, and the final dated Nexus megaphylogeny are provided in `Supplementary materials 1–5`, and all 56 fossil calibrations are fully documented in `Table 1`.
