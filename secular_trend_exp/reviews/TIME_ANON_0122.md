### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript (`TIME_ANON_0122`) investigates the transcriptional regulation of terpene synthase (`SlTPS`) genes in stem trichomes of cultivated tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum* cv. Moneymaker). The authors generated a normalized cDNA transcriptome assembly from control and jasmonic acid (JA)-treated stem trichomes using Roche 454 GS FLX Titanium pyrosequencing (`979,076` reads assembled into `27,195` contigs, identifying `743` putative transcription factors [TFs]), followed by unnormalized 3'-end Illumina Genome Analyzer II (`GAII`) expression profiling (`5.63M` Control and `5.88M` JA reads). From 16 candidate TFs (11 `AP2/ERF`, 4 `WRKY`, 1 `MYC`), tissue-specific qRT-PCR ($n=3$ biological replicates) narrowed the set to 4 trichome-expressed TFs (`SlMYC1`, `SlWRKY78`, `SlWRKY28`, `SlWRKY73`). Using *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* transient transactivation assays (`ATTA`) with *SlTPS promoter:GUS* reporters in *Nicotiana benthamiana* leaves, the authors demonstrate that `SlWRKY73` transactivates *SlTPS5* (and weakly *SlTPS3* and *SlTPS7*), `SlMYC1` transactivates *SlTPS5*, *SlTPS3*, *SlTPS7*, and *SlTPS9* (but not root-specific *SlTPS8*), and co-expression of `SlMYC1` with the previously identified trichome TF `SlEOT1` nearly triples *SlTPS5* promoter activation synergistically. While the in planta `ATTA` transactivation and `SlMYC1` + `SlEOT1` synergy results are well-controlled ($n=3\text{–}4$ biological replicates, replicated across 2–3 independent experiments with ANOVA + Tukey's B post-hoc tests), the upstream NGS expression-profiling design suffers from a lack of biological replication ($N=1$ pooled sample per condition) and severe low-count quantization artifacts that resulted in a 0/4 validation rate for JA induction by qRT-PCR.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Unreplicated ($N=1$) Pooled Illumina GAII Expression Profiling and Low-Count Quantization Artifacts:**
  - In the Methods (`Hormone treatment and RNA isolation`), stems from control and 1 mM JA-sprayed plants were harvested at `30 min, 2 h, 8 h, and 24 h`, and equal amounts of RNA from the four time points were pooled into a single Control (`C`) and a single `JA` RNA sample ($N=1$ library per condition, with no biological replicates).
  - Without biological replicates, no dispersion estimation or statistical differential expression test (e.g., `edgeR`, `DESeq`, or Fisher's exact test with multiple-testing correction) was performed on the `GAII` data; instead, the authors applied a naive fold-change threshold (`>1.5x` or `>2.0x` on RPKM).
  - Inspection of **Table 5** and **Table 6** reveals extreme low-count quantization in the `Control` RPKM column: seven distinct genes (`TPS7`, `TPS24`, `TPS31`, `SlAP2_5`, `SlWRKY22`, `SlWRKY28`, `SlWRKY78`, and `SlWRKY73`) all have the exact same Control expression value of `0.18 RPKM`, and their `JA` values are small discrete multiples (`0.17`, `0.50`, `0.68`, `1.19 RPKM`). For a $\sim 1.1\text{–}1.4\text{ kb}$ transcript in a library of $\sim 5\text{M}$ mapped reads (`5.17M` JA, `4.84M` C), `0.18 RPKM` corresponds to literally **1 raw mapped read**, and `0.50` or `0.68 RPKM` corresponds to **3 to 5 raw mapped reads**!
  - Consequently, computing `"Fold JA induction"` as $0.50 / 0.18 = 2.78\text{-fold}$ (`SlWRKY28`, `SlWRKY73`) or $0.68 / 0.18 = 3.78\text{-fold}$ (`SlWRKY22`, `SlAP2_5`) is pure Poisson shot noise (1 read vs 3–4 reads). This directly explains why **0 out of 4** selected TFs showed statistically significant JA induction when tested with replicated ($n=3$) qRT-PCR at 24 h (**Figure 2**), and `SlWRKY73` actually trended *downward* by 1.7-fold ($p = 0.07$). Although the authors candidly acknowledge in the Discussion (line 266) that `"Since the numbers of sequence reads of these genes is very low both in the Control and JA samples (Table 6), the fold-induction in the Illumina experiments must have been overestimated,"` presenting unfiltered single-read RPKM ratios as quantitative fold-induction tables (**Tables 4–6**) is methodologically flawed.
- **Inappropriate RPKM Normalization for 3'-End Tag Sequencing:**
  - In `Expression profiling database construction` (line 33), the authors state that cDNA synthesis was performed `"with modifications of the adapters to enable sequencing of 3' cDNA ends."` If each transcript molecule generates a single 3'-end tag regardless of transcript length, dividing read counts by full contig length in kilobases (`RPKM`) systematically underestimates the abundance of long transcripts relative to short contigs (whereas standard RPM/CPM normalization is appropriate for 3'-tag digital gene expression).
- **Transient Transactivation (`ATTA`) Controls:**
  - The *N. benthamiana* agroinfiltration experiments (**Figures 3 and 4**) are well designed, using `35S:RFP` as a negative effector control, `35S:SlEOT1` as a positive control, $n=3$ (**Figure 3**) or $n=4$ (**Figure 4**) replicates with ANOVA + Tukey's B post-hoc testing, and independent experimental repetition (3 experiments in Fig. 3; 2 in Fig. 4).
  - However, in **Figure 4** (co-infiltration of `35S:SlEOT1` + `35S:SlMYC1` + `35S:SlWRKY73`), the Methods do not clarify whether total *Agrobacterium* $\text{OD}_{600}$ was kept constant across single, double, and triple effector infiltrations by supplementing with the `35S:RFP` filler strain. Without bacterial $\text{OD}$ balancing, co-infiltrating two or three strains either doubles/triples total bacterial load or dilutes each effector by $1/2$ or $1/3$ (which would make the observed $\sim 3\times$ synergy of `SlEOT1 + SlMYC1` even more striking, and explains why adding a third strain, `SlWRKY73`, slightly reduced activation via titration).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *`SlMYC1` and `SlWRKY73` transactivate multiple tomato terpene synthase promoters (`SlTPS5`, `SlTPS3`, `SlTPS7`, and for `SlMYC1`, `SlTPS9`) in N. benthamiana leaves.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Demonstrated across multiple `SlTPSp:GUS` constructs with appropriate negative (`35S:RFP`) and positive (`35S:SlEOT1`) controls and promoter specificity (neither activates root-specific `SlTPS8p`).
- **Claim 2:** *`SlMYC1` and `SlEOT1` act synergistically to transactivate the trichome-specific `SlTPS5` promoter.* **`[Well Supported]`** — Co-expression nearly triples GUS activity relative to either TF alone ($p < 0.05$, ANOVA with Tukey's B).
- **Claim 3:** *The four candidate WRKY TFs (`SlWRKY22`, `SlWRKY28`, `SlWRKY73`, `SlWRKY78`) are up-regulated 2.8- to 6.6-fold by JA in the Illumina transcriptome database.* **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** — Artifact of dividing 3–8 reads by 1 read (`0.18 RPKM`) in an unreplicated ($N=1$) pooled library; refuted by the authors' own $n=3$ qRT-PCR experiments (**Figure 2**).
- **Claim 4:** *Direct binding of `SlMYC1` and `SlWRKY73` to G-box/T-G-box/W-box elements or the MEME-predicted `CTAGG(T/A)(A/G)G` motif.* **`[Moderately Supported]`** — The authors appropriately caveat (line 8) that `ATTA` measures in planta transactivation (which could be indirect via endogenous *N. benthamiana* factors) and that direct binding and motif validation require further experimentation and stable tomato transformants.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Accession Numbers:** Raw 454 GS FLX Titanium and Illumina GAII reads are deposited in NCBI SRA under `SRP041373`, and the full-length `SlMYC1` ORF is deposited in GenBank under `KF430611`. SGN unigene identifiers (`SGN-U...`) are provided for all genes in **Tables 3, 4, and 6**.
- **Missing Primer & Construct Details:** Although the qRT-PCR and cloning protocols are described, no table of oligonucleotide primer sequences is provided for the 16 candidate TFs or the *ACTIN* (`SGN-U579547`) reference gene, and the assembled 27,195-contig FASTA file is not included as a supplementary file or TSA accession.
- **Anonymization Scrubbing Artifact in Table 5:** Note that in **Table 5**, the transcript lengths of `TPS9` and `TPS31` (likely 4-digit numbers between 1900 and 2025 bp) were inadvertently replaced by `[YEAR]` during automated text anonymization.
