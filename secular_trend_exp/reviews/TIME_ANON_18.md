### 1. Executive Summary
This study analyzes 158 coronavirus protein sequences retrieved from Swiss-Prot across seven protein groups (envelope, hemagglutinin-esterase, membrane, nonstructural, nucleocapsid, spike, and others/replicase) using an idiosyncratic dipeptide ("amino-acid pair") counting method. For each single protein sequence of length $L$, the authors compute the expected zero-order Markov count of each of the 400 possible ordered dipeptides $XY$ from the single-amino-acid counts ($E_{XY} = N_X N_Y / L$ for $X \neq Y$), round $E_{XY}$ to the nearest integer, and classify a dipeptide as "randomly predictable" if the observed count $O_{XY}$ exactly equals the rounded integer $\text{round}(E_{XY})$, and "randomly unpredictable" if $|O_{XY} - \text{round}(E_{XY})| \ge 1$. Comparing these proportions and differences across protein groups, the authors claim three lines of evidence showing that the coronavirus spike glycoprotein is uniquely sensitive to current, past, and future mutations.

While the biological truism that the coronavirus spike glycoprotein undergoes high sequence variation under host immune and receptor selection is well known, the mathematical and statistical framework used in this paper is fundamentally invalid. The authors' metrics ("unpredictable portion" and "magnitude of difference between actual and predicted values") are elementary mathematical artifacts of **protein sequence length ($L$)**, combined with a flawed probability derivation and unacknowledged phylogenetic/database replication of near-identical strains.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Fatal Mathematical Confounding by Sequence Length ($L$):**
  * Consider a random sequence of length $L$ with expected dipeptide count $\lambda_{XY} = (L-1) p_X p_Y \approx L / 400$. Under simple multinomial/Poisson sampling of a random sequence, the variance of the observed count $O_{XY}$ around $\lambda_{XY}$ is $\text{Var}(O_{XY}) \approx \lambda_{XY} \propto L$, so the root-mean-square deviation $|O_{XY} - \lambda_{XY}|$ scales strictly as $\sqrt{L}$, and the probability that $O_{XY}$ equals the exact rounded integer $\text{round}(\lambda_{XY})$ shrinks monotonically as $1/\sqrt{L}$.
  * Conversely, for a very short protein such as the coronavirus Envelope (E) protein ($L \approx 75\text{ aa}$), the average expected count of a dipeptide is $\lambda \approx 75 / 400 = 0.1875$ (which rounds to $0$), and at least $400 - 74 = 326$ of the 400 dipeptide types ($>81.5\%$) *must* have $O_{XY} = 0 = \text{round}(\lambda_{XY})$, guaranteeing a high "randomly predictable" fraction and a tiny $|O_{XY} - \text{round}(\lambda_{XY})|$ purely by sequence length!
  * In contrast, the Spike (S) glycoprotein is by far the longest structural protein in coronaviruses ($L = 1,255\text{ aa}$ in SARS-CoV, compared to $L \approx 75$ for E, $L \approx 220$ for M, and $L \approx 420$ for N). For $L = 1,255$, $\lambda \approx 3.1$, so Poisson sampling variance guarantees that most of the 400 dipeptides will deviate from $\text{round}(\lambda_{XY})$ by $\ge 1$, and the maximum deviations $|O_{XY} - E_{XY}|$ will be much larger (scaling as $\propto \sqrt{L}$ or $\propto L$ for compositional domains). The authors never normalize for protein length $L$, mistaking the trivial scaling of multinomial counts with sequence length $L$ for "vulnerability to mutations."
* **Invalid Probability Calculus in the *Statistics* Section (p. 63):**
  * To justify treating any integer deviation $|O_{XY} - \text{round}(E_{XY})| \ge 1$ as statistically significant at $p < 0.05$, the authors write: *"Thus, the chance of first 'TT' is $99/1,255 \times 98/1,254$ ($p = 0.0062 < 0.01$), and the chance of second 'TT' is $97/1,253 \times 96/1,252$ ($p = 0.0059 < 0.01$)... Clearly, the probability is less than 0.05 if the difference between actual and predicted values is equal to or larger than 1."*
  * This confuses the probability of drawing a specific dipeptide at a *single fixed position* ($p_T^2 \approx 0.0062$) with the probability of observing $k$ occurrences across **1,254 overlapping trials**! Across $L - 1 = 1,254$ positions, the expected number of `TT` pairs is $\lambda = 1,254 \times 0.0062 = 7.77$, and the probability that $|O_{TT} - 8| \ge 1$ under a Poisson/binomial distribution with mean $7.77$ is **$p \approx 0.86$**, not $< 0.01$! Nearly every dipeptide in a 1,255-aa random sequence will differ from its rounded expectation by $\ge 1$ purely by chance.
* **Pseudoreplication Across 158 Non-Independent Database Sequences:**
  * Performing one-way ANOVA across 158 Swiss-Prot entries (which include multiple near-identical isolates of the same coronavirus species, such as multiple human SARS-CoV or BCoV strains) treats evolutionarily and database-replicated sequences as independent observations ($N=158$), inflating degrees of freedom.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1 (Current State): Spike Is More Sensitive to Mutations Because Its "Unpredictable Portion" of Dipeptides Is Larger (Fig. 1): [Methodologically Flawed].** As proven above, the fraction of dipeptides where $O_{XY} \neq \text{round}(N_X N_Y / L)$ is a direct mathematical function of sequence length $L$.
* **Claim 2 (Past History): Dipeptides with $O_{XY} < E_{XY}$ vs. $O_{XY} > E_{XY}$ Measure Past Mutation History (Fig. 2): [Methodologically Flawed].** Static zero-order Markov deviations of dipeptide counts in a single sequence reflect local secondary structure, transmembrane helices, and codon/dinucleotide bias—not the historical number of substitutions along a phylogeny. Worse, the authors claim support because *"172 mutations have currently been documented in coronavirus proteins, of which 153 occur in spike glycoproteins"*, ignoring both that Spike is ~3–16 times longer than N, M, and E and receives intense targeted sequencing ascertainment in the literature.
* **Claim 3 (Future Potential): Magnitude of $|O_{XY} - E_{XY}|$ Predicts Future Mutation Potential and Species Susceptibility (Figs. 3–10): [Methodologically Flawed].** The range of $O_{XY} - E_{XY}$ along the horizontal axis of Figs. 4–10 scales directly with protein length $L$. Moreover, in the Discussion (p. 175), the authors dismiss multiple sequence alignment as merely *"language similarity"* that *"cannot predict the future, while our approach can predict the likelihood of future mutations"*, without a single prospective validation, simulation benchmark, or evolutionary rate test.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** The arithmetic of the dipeptide formula ($N_X N_Y / L$) is simple and explicitly illustrated with worked numbers for SARS-CoV Spike (`RS`, `AA`, `WR`, `RW`, `RT`).
* **Deficiencies:**
  1. The list of the 158 Swiss-Prot accession numbers and the group sample sizes ($n$ per protein group and species) are relegated to unavailable Electronic Supplementary Material and omitted from the main text and figure captions.
  2. Figures 4–10 contain acknowledged plotting software bugs (*"Due to the limitation of the graphic software, the filled forms are duplicated in one or two bars"*).
