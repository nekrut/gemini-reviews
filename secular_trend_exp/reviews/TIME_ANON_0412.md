### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This manuscript is a comprehensive mechanistic review of enveloped virus budding across diverse cellular membrane platforms (plasma membrane, ER, ERGIC/IC, Golgi/TGN, nuclear envelope, and multivesicular bodies). It synthesizes experimental findings across retroviruses (HIV-1, M-PMV, HTLV-1, Foamy virus), flaviviruses, coronaviruses, alphaviruses, rhabdoviruses, filoviruses, arenaviruses, paramyxoviruses, herpesviruses, and poxviruses. The review categorizes viral budding forces into "pull" (glycoprotein-driven), "push" (inner matrix/core-driven), and combined models, details membrane targeting mechanisms (N-terminal myristoylation, basic patches, PI(4,5)P2 binding with 2'-acyl chain sequestration), evaluates lipid raft/DRM and tetraspanin-enriched microdomain (TEM) hypotheses, and dissects the recruitment of host ESCRT-I/-III, ALIX/AIP1, NEDD4 ubiquitin ligases, and VPS4 via viral late domains (PT/SAP, YPDL, PPxY, FPIV).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
- **Absence of Primary Quantitative Meta-Analysis:** As a narrative review, the manuscript does not perform a systematic literature search or quantitative meta-analysis of budding efficiencies, VLP release ratios, or thermodynamic parameters across virus families.
- **Thermodynamic Approximations:** The discussion of membrane binding energies (8 kcal/mol for a myristyl anchor corresponding to $K_d \approx 10^{-4}\text{ M}$, and ~1.4 kcal/mol per lysine residue in basic patches) is cited from biophysical literature without formal derivation of standard state conventions or cooperative avidity effects during Gag multimerization.
- **Critical Appraisal of Assay Artifacts:** A notable methodological strength is the manuscript's rigorous critique of cold Triton X-100 detergent-resistant membrane (DRM) extraction assays as a flawed proxy for in vivo lipid rafts, contrasting DRM artifacts against detergent-free viral lipidomics and exclusion of flotillin-1. Similarly, the controversy surrounding HIV-1 budding in macrophages (intracellular MVB budding vs. deep plasma membrane invaginations and re-internalization) is balanced and critically evaluated.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
- `[Well Supported]` Classification of minimal budding requirements into glycoprotein-driven ("pull", e.g., flaviviruses, coronaviruses, HBV subviral particles), matrix/Gag-driven ("push", e.g., most retroviruses, rhabdoviruses, filoviruses, arenaviruses), and cooperative ("push + pull", e.g., alphaviruses, Foamy virus) mechanisms.
- `[Well Supported]` Mechanistic explanation of the "myristyl switch" and synergistic PI(4,5)P2 headgroup/2'-unsaturated acyl chain binding by the HIV-1 Matrix (MA) domain to explain both plasma membrane specificity and partitioning into saturated liquid-ordered microdomains.
- `[Well Supported]` Differentiation of viral late domain entry points into the host ESCRT pathway (PS/TAP -> TSG101/ESCRT-I; YPDL -> ALIX; PPxY -> NEDD4 family E3 ligases) converging on ESCRT-III and VPS4 ATPase while largely bypassing ESCRT-II in HIV-1.
- `[Moderately Supported]` Speculation on early ESCRT scaffolding roles in curvature induction (citing M-PMV/HTLV-1 PPxY mutants and CHMP3 lattice structures) versus late membrane stalk fission; the manuscript appropriately flags the unresolved mechanistic tension between these phenotypes.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
Because this is a conceptual and mechanistic review article rather than a primary experimental study, primary datasets, sequence accessions, and statistical tables are not applicable. The three conceptual figures (cellular budding sites, push/pull budding mechanics, and MVB/retroviral ESCRT recruitment) are clearly described and internally consistent with the text.
