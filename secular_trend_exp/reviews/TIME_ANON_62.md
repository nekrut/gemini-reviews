### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates whether the programmed $-1$ ribosomal frameshift signal of HIV-1 (the slippery heptanucleotide sequence `U UUU UUA` followed 7 nt downstream by a stem-loop structure) can be functionally recapitulated in an *Escherichia coli* S30 cell-free translation extract as well as in live *E. coli* cells using an N-terminally modified firefly luciferase (`Luc-1` vs. in-frame `Luc0`) reporter system. The authors report that: (1) wild-type bacterial ribosomes frameshift at the HIV-1 signal at $\sim 3\%$ efficiency in the S30 cell-free system (independent of mRNA concentration and competitor CAT mRNA) and $\sim 14\%$ in vivo, with non-slippery site-directed mutants (`U CCC GCG`) reducing activity to $<0.1\%$ in vitro and $<0.4\%$ in vivo; (2) deleting the downstream stem-loop (`SHS - SL`) has no significant effect in vitro, but reduces frameshifting $\sim 2$-fold in vivo; (3) chloramphenicol (peptidyl transferase inhibitor) modestly increases frameshifting independently of the stem-loop, whereas spectinomycin (translocation inhibitor) has no effect; and (4) hyperaccurate *rpsL* (S12) mutant ribosomes decrease in vitro frameshifting 2-fold—and error-inducing aminoglycosides (streptomycin, neamine) modestly increase frameshifting—only when the downstream stem-loop is present.

The experimental design is well-controlled with matched in-frame (`Luc0`) and non-slippery (`U CCC GCG`) controls, competitor mRNA titrations, and parallel in vitro and in vivo comparisons. The primary limitations are the omission of the actual numerical cells of Tables 1–3 from the manuscript text (only table captions are present), reliance on min-max range bars rather than parametric/non-parametric confidence intervals in Figure 2, and the indirect nature of enzymatic luciferase activity without direct polypeptide resolving (SDS-PAGE) to rule out differential premature termination or specific activity changes under antibiotic treatment.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Well-Constructed Internal Controls:** The use of matched `Luc0(SHS + SL)` and `Luc0(SHS - SL)` in-frame controls directly accounts for the $\sim 3$-fold reduction in firefly luciferase specific activity caused by the N-terminal peptide extension encoded by the HIV-1 insert (line 50, line 61). Furthermore, mutating the slippery heptamer `U UUU UUA` to `U CCC GCG` in all constructs provides a rigorous negative control against internal translation initiation or cryptic frameshifting ($<0.1\%$ in vitro, $<0.4\%$ in vivo).
* **Missing Tabular Data in Manuscript Body:** While the captions for Table 1, Table 2, and Table 3 are included in the text (lines 53, 56, 69) and describe $n \ge 4$ or $n = 5$ independent experiments with standard deviations $\le 15\%$ (in vitro) and $\le 20\%$ (in vivo), the actual numerical data rows of Tables 1–3 are absent from the text document, preventing independent verification of the exact relative light unit (RLU) ratios and standard deviations.
* **Statistical Testing of Modest Antibiotic Effects:** In the antibiotic titration experiments (Figure 2, line 71), the increases in frameshifting efficiency under streptomycin, neamine, and chloramphenicol are described as "modest but reproducible" and "modest but significant." However, Figure 2 plots only the mean and the "Highest and lowest values observed" across $\ge 5$ experiments rather than standard errors or $95\%$ confidence intervals, and no formal hypothesis tests ($p$-values, paired $t$-tests across extract batches, or ANOVA) are reported to substantiate statistical significance when overall translation is simultaneously inhibited by up to $50\%$.
* **Single-Reporter vs. Dual-Reporter Normalization in Vivo:** For the in vivo assays (line 39), `Luc-1` and `Luc0` are expressed from separate bacterial cultures transformed with separate pET24 plasmids and normalized by $\text{OD}_{600}$. Unlike dual-reporter constructs (e.g., GST-Luc or LacZ-Luc internal translational fusions on a single mRNA), comparing separate cultures normalized by optical density is susceptible to plasmid copy-number variation, differential mRNA stability between `Luc-1` (which experiences early ribosomal drop-off after the frameshift site in $86\%$ of translation events, exposing the downstream mRNA to endonucleolytic decay by RNase E) and `Luc0` (which is fully translated), potentially distorting the apparent $14\%$ in vivo frameshifting rate.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Recapitulation of HIV-1 $-1$ Frameshifting in E. coli S30 Extracts [Well Supported]:** The demonstration that prokaryotic ribosomes execute $-1$ frameshifting on the HIV-1 slippery heptamer at $\sim 3\%$ efficiency in vitro—abrogated to $<0.1\%$ upon mutating `U UUU UUA` to `U CCC GCG`—is solidly grounded and insensitive to mRNA concentration or competitor CAT mRNA.
* **Pre-Peptidyl-Transfer Timing Inferred from Chloramphenicol vs. Spectinomycin [Moderately Supported]:** The finding that chloramphenicol (blocking peptidyl transfer) increases frameshifting regardless of the stem-loop whereas spectinomycin (blocking EF-G-mediated translocation) has no effect logically supports slippage prior to peptide bond formation, consistent with the two-tRNA slippage model. The refined hypothesis that slippage occurs in the $A/T$ entry state (complexed with EF-Tu) is clearly framed as a plausible model refinement rather than proven fact.
* **Functional Ribosome–Stem-Loop Interaction Inferred from S12 Mutants and Aminoglycosides [Moderately Supported]:** The observation that hyperaccurate S12 mutant ribosomes decrease frameshifting 2-fold and streptomycin/neamine increase frameshifting *only* in the presence of the stem-loop is an intriguing mechanistic finding. However, the proposed conformational switch induced by a specific ribosome–stem-loop interaction remains indirect; an alternative kinetic explanation is that the stem-loop alters the dwell time of the ribosome at the slippery site just enough to unmask the altered $A$-site decoding kinetics of S12/aminoglycoside-bound ribosomes without requiring a specific structural recognition of the HIV-1 stem-loop by the 30S subunit.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Experimental Protocols:** Buffer compositions, ion concentrations ($13\ \text{mM}$ magnesium acetate, $190\ \text{mM}$ potassium glutamate, $30\ \text{mM}$ ammonium acetate), S30 preparation references, reaction volumes, and cloning strategies are thoroughly documented and highly reproducible.
* **Missing Elements:** Exact nucleotide sequences of the inserted oligonucleotide cassettes (shown in Figure 1, which is referenced as a figure caption only), the specific *rpsL* (S12) amino acid substitution(s) used in the spontaneous streptomycin-resistantRD100 mutants, and the numerical bodies of Tables 1–3 are absent from the text file, alongside several supporting validations cited as "(data not shown)" (rabbit reticulocyte lysate controls, non-slippery mutant RLU values, nonsense readthrough assays for S12 mutants).
