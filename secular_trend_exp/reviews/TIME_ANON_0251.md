### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript is a brief pedagogical review / encyclopedia chapter ("Viral Genomes and Viral Replication") introducing the diversity of viral nucleic acids, the seven Baltimore classification groups (Groups I–VII: dsDNA, ssDNA, dsRNA, (+)ssRNA, (–)ssRNA, (+)ssRNA-RT, and dsDNA-RT), and the principal strategies used by RNA viruses to express multiple proteins from compact genomes (polyprotein processing, segmentation, subgenomic mRNA transcription, internal ribosome entry sites [IRES] and termination–reinitiation, nonsense suppression / ribosomal frameshifting, and leaky scanning). Because this is an introductory educational overview containing no primary experimental data, original datasets, or mathematical modeling, it is evaluated on its conceptual rigor, biological accuracy, and taxonomic clarity.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Secondary Pedagogical Scope:** No original statistical analyses, sequence alignments, or wet-lab experiments are conducted.
- **Taxonomic & Quantitative Inaccuracies in Table 1 ("Diversity of viral genomes"):**
  - In Table 1, the first row under `DNA -> ds -> Linear -> 1 segment` lists a genome length range of `15–2500 kb` and gives `Poxviridae` as the representative family. However, as the authors state in the text ("Group I: dsDNA Viruses"), poxvirus genomes are $\sim 130\text{--}365\text{ kb}$ (and have covalently closed hairpin ends), whereas the $2,500\text{ kb}$ upper bound belongs to *Pandoravirus* (and the $15\text{ kb}$ lower bound belongs to adenoviruses/tectiviruses).
  - Similarly, the fourth row (`DNA -> ds -> Circular -> 1 segment`) lists a genome length range of `4.5–300 kb` and gives `Polyomaviridae` as the example, whereas polyomaviruses are strictly $\sim 4.5\text{--}5.5\text{ kb}$ (the $300\text{ kb}$ upper bound reflects large circular dsDNA baculoviruses/nimaviruses).
- **Nomenclatural Ambiguity Regarding HTLV-1 as an "Ambisense" Virus:** In the "Group VI" section, the authors state that *"HTLV-1, unlike other retroviruses, is really an 'ambisense' RNA virus"* because it transcribes an antisense mRNA (*HBZ*) from a promoter at the $3'$ LTR of the integrated proviral dsDNA. In standard virology taxonomy, "ambisense" is reserved for single-stranded viral genomes (such as *Arenaviridae* and *Phenuiviridae*) in which the packaged genomic nucleic acid itself contains both positive-sense and negative-sense open reading frames; in HTLV-1, the packaged genomic RNA is purely $(+)$-sense, and antisense transcription occurs exclusively from the integrated dsDNA provirus in the host chromosome.
- **Automated Redaction Artifacts in Numerical Quantities:** In the Introduction, automated year-scrubbing replaced two numerical values (likely `2000` bases and `1000` or `2000` proteins) with `[YEAR]`: *"the nucleic acids may contain as few as [YEAR] bases or more than $2\times 10^6$ bases, corresponding to coding capacities ranging from two proteins to over [YEAR] proteins."*
