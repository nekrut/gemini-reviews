### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript is a brief narrative review discussing innate immune responses (specifically type I interferon signaling via TLR3/7, RIG-I, NF-$\kappa$B, IRF3, and JAK-STAT pathways) and the historical/proposed use of convalescent sera (passive immunization) for treating and preventing SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19). While the general textbook overview of pattern recognition receptor (PRR) signaling and historical passive immunotherapy (e.g., 1918 H1N1, SARS-CoV-1, MERS, Ebola) is broadly recognizable, the manuscript contains no original data, no formal literature search or systematic review methodology, pervasive factual and numerical errors, and unsubstantiated clinical recommendations.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Absence of Review Methodology:** As a secondary literature synthesis, the manuscript specifies no search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, database queries, or quality assessment of cited studies.
- **Severe Factual and Numerical Errors in Text and Tables:**
  - **Table 2 Clinical Trial Misattributions:** Table 2 ("Some under Processing Vaccines for COVID-19") lists `NCT03721718` (DNA Phase II) and `NCT03399578, NCT03615911` (Viral vector Phase I). These ClinicalTrials.gov identifiers predate the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic (registered in the 033–037 million range, corresponding to non-COVID trials) and are erroneously presented as active COVID-19 vaccine trials.
  - **Epidemiological Exaggerations and Contradictions:** In the Introduction (line 19), the authors state that "SARS-CoV-2 affected billions of people" at a time when vaccines were still in early development, confusing the global population at risk with confirmed infections. In Section "Convalescent Sera Based Passive Immunization" (line 55), the authors state "The SARS epidemic is continued," whereas SARS-CoV-1 transmission ended in 2004.
  - **Genomic and Unit Errors:** Line 34 states that "10b, 13, and 14 are the additional gene regions reported in the genome of SARS-CoV-2," which does not correspond to standard coronavirus ORF nomenclature (ORF1a/b, S, ORF3a, E, M, ORF6, ORF7a/b, ORF8, N, ORF10). Line 60 states that in the 1918 influenza outbreak, "100 mm of convalescent serum was used to cure soldiers," confusing linear millimeters (`mm`) with volumetric milliliters (`mL`).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *"It is clear that convalescent sera prevent infection... The convalescent sera are the safe and proved way to tackle SARS-CoV-2."* — `[Overstated]`
  - The authors extrapolate from small, uncontrolled historical case series (e.g., $n=3$ in MERS, $n=80$ retrospective observations in SARS-CoV-1, and anecdotal use in COVID-19) to declare convalescent plasma a "safe and proved way" to prevent and cure SARS-CoV-2 prior to randomized controlled trial readouts.
- **Claim 2:** *"During COVID-19 the increase and decrease in total volume of neutrophils and lymphocytes, probably SARS-CoV-2 induces delayed interferon type 1 responses and thus loss of viral control occurs in prompt stage of infection."* — `[Moderately Supported]`
  - Delayed type I IFN induction and myeloid hyperinflammation (neutrophilia, lymphopenia, elevated IL-6 and IP-10) are biologically plausible mechanisms supported by early clinical cohorts ($n=99$ and $n=41$) and comparative SARS/MERS virology, though presented here purely derivatively.
- **Claim 3:** *"The innate immune responses are stronger in young age individuals than old age; therefore, the severity and fatality is more in aged individuals > 57 years."* — `[Fragile]`
  - The specific age cutoff of "> 57 years" is asserted in the Conclusion without any statistical derivation or citation in the main text.
