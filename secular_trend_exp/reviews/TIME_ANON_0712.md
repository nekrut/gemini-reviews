### SUMMARY
### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript is an authoritative reference / encyclopedia review article synthesizing the molecular biology, taxonomy, virion architecture, genome organization, replication strategy, pathogenesis, and immunology of the family *Arteriviridae* (genus *Arterivirus*: equine arteritis virus [`EAV`], lactate dehydrogenase-elevating virus [`LDV`], porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus [`PRRSV`, Lelystad and VR-2332 strains], and simian hemorrhagic fever virus [`SHFV`]) following its taxonomic placement alongside *Coronaviridae* within the order *Nidovirales*. The article clearly details the 12.7–15.7 kb polyadenylated positive-sense RNA genome, the ribosomal `-1` frameshift generating the `ORF1a/1b` replicase polyprotein, the nested set of six to nine 3'-coterminal subgenomic mRNAs possessing a common 5' leader sequence joined via conserved transcription-regulating connector motifs (`5'-UCAAC-3'` in EAV, `5'-UUAACC-3'` in PRRSV and SHFV, `5'-UAUAACC-3'` in LDV), the apparent duplication of ORFs `2a/2b/3` and `4/5/6` in `SHFV`, and the macrophage tropism and persistent infection mechanisms of arteriviruses.

---

### STRENGTHS
- **Comprehensive and Accurate Comparative Virology Synthesis:** Provides a rigorous, well-organized synthesis of arterivirus virion biophysics (50–60 nm smooth enveloped particles with 30–35 nm icosahedral nucleocapsids, buoyant density $1.13\text{–}1.17\text{ g/cm}^3$ in sucrose), replicase processing, and nested subgenomic mRNA transcription across all four canonical species.
- **Clear Mechanistic Integration of Pathogenesis and Host Genetics:** Accurately explains both the clearance defect of blood-borne LDH-5 following liver Kupffer cell destruction in LDV-infected mice and the multi-factorial pathogenesis of age-dependent poliomyelitis (`ADPM`) in immunosuppressed `C58` and `AKR` mice mediated by neurovirulent `LDV-C` quasispecies and endogenous ecotropic murine leukemia virus (`MuLV`) expression in anterior horn neurons.

### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Secondary Review Modality Without Primary Statistical Datasets:** As an encyclopedia monograph rather than an original experimental investigation, the manuscript does not present new primary datasets, statistical tests, or quantitative error estimates; figures (`Figure 1` genome organization, `Figure 2` electron micrographs, `Figure 3` Northern blot of EAV intracellular subgenomic RNAs 1–7) are adapted from prior literature.
- **Uncorroborated Host-Range Statement for Avian PRRSV Susceptibility:** In the Host Range and Virus Propagation section (line 73), the article notes without critical caveat that *"a recent report suggested that chickens and mallard ducks may also be susceptible to infection with PRRSV"*—a claim from early 1990s experimental inoculations that subsequent replication studies failed to confirm as biologically relevant avian infection.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *"Arteriviruses share a coronavirus-like replication and nested 3'-coterminal leader-joined subgenomic mRNA transcription strategy despite having a smaller 12.7–15.7 kb genome and an isometric 30–35 nm core, justifying their classification with Coronaviridae in the order Nidovirales."* — **[Well Supported]**. The molecular architecture (`ORF1a/1b` `-1` frameshift pseudoknot, papain-like and 3C-like serine proteases, polymerase/helicase motifs, and discontinuous leader-body junction sequences) is thoroughly grounded.
- **Claim 2:** *"SHFV encodes nine 3'-ORFs reflecting a duplication of the three-ORF module (`2a/2b/3` and `4/5/6`) relative to EAV, LDV, and PRRSV."* — **[Well Supported]**. Accurately reflects sequence homology between the two three-gene cassettes in SHFV.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Limited Reference List Without Inline Citations:** Consistent with an encyclopedia chapter format, the article provides a concise "See also" and "Further Reading" list of 12 foundational reviews and primary papers rather than exhaustive inline citations or GenBank accession tables.
