### 1. Executive Summary
This hybrid experimental and molecular evolutionary study investigates whether elevated interspecific divergence ($d_N/d_S$) in the rapidly evolving plastid genome of *Campanulastrum americanum* is driven by relaxed purifying selection or positive selection by comparing interspecific divergence against intraspecific polymorphism ($p_N/p_S$) across four geographically distinct populations (VA, AL, MN, OH). The authors sequenced sucrose-gradient-purified plastid DNA using Roche 454 pyrosequencing in two libraries—a single VA maternal family ($28,694$ plastid reads, used to assemble the reference genome and benchmark false-positive SNP calls) and a 4-population pool ($24,552$ plastid reads, $14.3\times$ mean coverage across 54 protein-coding genes meeting a $7\times$ minimum depth threshold)—supplemented by targeted PCR/cDNA Sanger sequencing of *clpP* and *ycf1* and `PAML v4.4` / McDonald-Kreitman (MK) analyses using *Trachelium caeruleum* as an outgroup. The study is a model of forensic experimental rigor: when initial read mapping revealed a dense cluster of 9 apparent nonsynonymous SNPs in *clpP* exon 1, the authors performed targeted genomic and cDNA Sanger sequencing, discovering that the apparent SNPs were mapping artifacts from a polymorphic partial duplication of *clpP* exon 1 in three populations. After excluding the paralogous region, they demonstrate a strong positive correlation between $\log(d_N/d_S)$ and $\log(p_N/p_S)$ ($P = 0.0005$), indicating that relaxed purifying selection explains most plastid protein-coding evolution, while *ycf1* and *clpP* exhibit both relaxed purifying and historical positive selection.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Empirical False-Positive Control via Single-Family 454 Library:** To validate their SNP calling thresholds ($\ge 7\times$ depth, $\ge 20\%$ minor allele frequency, and $\ge 2$ reads supporting the variant on both forward and reverse strands), the authors applied the exact same pipeline to the single maternal family VA library (which should be homoplasmic across the plastid genome). This yielded $0$ false-positive SNPs across all 54 analyzed protein-coding genes, providing a clean empirical validation of their filtering criteria.
- **Forensic Resolution of Paralogous Mapping Artifacts in *clpP*:** Rather than uncritically reporting a signature of balancing/diversifying selection from the 9-SNP cluster in *clpP* exon 1 (which yielded an apparent $p_N/p_S = 1.98$), the authors amplified and Sanger-sequenced both genomic DNA and cDNA across populations. They proved that AL, MN, and OH populations harbor a rearranged, non-transcribed partial duplication of *clpP* exon 1 whose divergent reads mapped onto the intact functional copy, and they properly excised the duplicated region from the polymorphism analysis (where the remaining 368 bp of *clpP* had 0 SNPs in 454 data, and full-length Sanger sequencing of 609 bp across 5 populations identified 4 nonsynonymous and 2 synonymous SNPs, $p_N/p_S = 0.562$).
- **Zero Synonymous Divergence ($d_S = 0$) in *clpP* on the Terminal *C. americanum* Branch:** In the 3-taxon `PAML` branch model (`model = 2` with *T. caeruleum* and *Helianthus annuus*), the terminal *C. americanum* branch for *clpP* accumulated $42.9$ nonsynonymous substitutions and $0.0$ synonymous substitutions ($d_N = 0.1419, d_S = 0, d_N/d_S = \infty$). To include *clpP* on the log-scale regression plot (Figure 2), the authors imputed $1$ synonymous substitution ($d_N/d_S = 5.412$). While transparently disclosed in both the main text and Figure 2 legend, point estimates of $d_N/d_S$ when $d_S = 0$ have wide confidence intervals; however, the authors also confirmed that the $\log(d_N/d_S)$ vs. $\log(p_N/p_S)$ correlation remains statistically significant across the purely 454-derived genes ($P = 0.0086$) and when using pairwise $d_N/d_S$ ($P = 0.0067$), and their MK test on *clpP* ($D_N = 162, D_S = 24$ vs. $P_N = 4, P_S = 2$; Fisher's exact $P = 0.16$) does not suffer from division by zero.
- **Unbarcoded Pooled Sequencing Caveat:** Because the 4 populations were pooled prior to 454 library preparation without population-specific barcodes, individual allele frequencies could not be estimated and variants with $<20\%$ pooled frequency were excluded to guard against pyrosequencing homopolymer errors. As the authors thoughtfully note, excluding rare alleles biases $p_N/p_S$ toward intermediate-frequency inter-population polymorphisms and reduces the power of MK tests.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Relaxed Purifying Selection as the Primary Driver of Plastid Evolutionary Rate Variation:** `[Well Supported]` The strong positive linear relationship between $\log(d_N/d_S)$ and $\log(p_N/p_S)$ across plastid functional categories (ribosomal proteins, *ndh* genes, and concatenated gene sets; $R^2$ driven by $P = 0.0005$) provides compelling evidence that heterogeneity in purifying selection intensity dominates plastid evolutionary rates in *C. americanum*.
- **Combined Relaxed Purifying and Positive Selection on *ycf1* and *clpP*:** `[Moderately Supported]` The extreme interspecific $d_N/d_S$ in *clpP* ($\sim 5.4$) and *ycf1* ($1.45$) compared to intraspecific $p_N/p_S$ ($0.56$ and $0.76$), together with the significant MK test for *ycf1* using pairwise divergence ($D_N/D_S = 232/42$ vs. $P_N/P_S = 26/11$, Fisher's exact $P = 0.04$), supports a combination of relaxed constraint and historical adaptive divergence, presented with appropriate statistical caution.
