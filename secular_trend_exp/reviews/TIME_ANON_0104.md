### 1. Executive Summary
This clinical genetics case report describes two nonconsanguineous pediatric probands presenting with autosomal recessive metabolic disorders caused by paternal uniparental isodisomy (UPiD): Patient 1 with glycogen storage disease type III (GSDIII) carrying a homozygous frameshift variant in *AGL* (`NM_000028.2:c.3423_3424delTG`, `p.Glu1142Argfs*24`) due to paternal whole-chromosome UPiD 1, and Patient 2 with Leigh syndrome carrying a novel homozygous canonical splice-site variant in *SURF1* (`NM_003172.3:c.241-1G>C`) due to paternal whole-chromosome UPiD 9 alongside concurrent maternal whole-chromosome UPiD 10. The authors initially detected both the homozygous pathogenic variants and chromosome-wide copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity (LOH) directly from proband whole-exome sequencing (WES) data (using XHMM for copy number and CNVkit for variant allele frequency plotting), and subsequently confirmed copy neutrality, whole-chromosome LOH (250 Mb on Chr1; 141 Mb on Chr9; plus Chr10), and parental origin using family-trio genome-wide SNP microarrays (Affymetrix CytoScan 750K), karyotyping, and Sanger sequencing. While limited in scope to $N = 2$ descriptive clinical cases, the multi-platform orthogonal validation is thorough and the clinical counseling implications (low recurrence risk compared to standard 25% Mendelian carrier couples, confirmed in a subsequent unaffected pregnancy in Family 2) are appropriately stated.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Orthogonal Trio Validation:** A key methodological strength is that the authors did not rely solely on WES variant allele frequency (VAF) plots; they validated both probands and all four parents using Affymetrix CytoScan 750K SNP arrays (confirming 50 informative paternal-only homozygous SNPs on Chr1 in Family 1, 30 informative paternal-only SNPs on Chr9 and 31 maternal-only SNPs on Chr10 in Family 2), Sanger sequencing of the causal variants, and G-banded karyotyping in Family 2.
- **Unexplained Double UPD in Patient 2:** Patient 2 exhibits simultaneous complete paternal UPiD of chromosome 9 and complete maternal UPiD of chromosome 10. Simultaneous whole-chromosome UPiD of two different non-homologous chromosomes from opposite parents is an extraordinarily rare cytogenetic event (implying complex meiotic/mitotic rescue or fertilization anomaly), yet the manuscript provides almost no mechanistic discussion, mosaicism evaluation (e.g., B-allele frequency inspection for low-level biparental cell lines in blood or buccal/tissue DNA), or statistical probability calculation for double UPiD.
- **Bioinformatic Pipeline Specification:** While XHMM (with $\ge 200$ matched-capture control exomes) and CNVkit are cited for WES CNV/LOH calling, quantitative thresholds for calling LOH from exome VAF (e.g., minimum read depth, B-allele frequency bounds, segmentation algorithm parameters, and total number of informative exonic SNPs per chromosome) are not reported.
- **Minor Textual & Nomenclature Inconsistencies:**
  - In the Results (line 71), the *AGL* frameshift is written as `p.Glu1142fs`, whereas on line 81 it is written as `p.Glu1142Argfs*24`, and in Figure 1 legend it is repeatedly spelled "homogenous" instead of "homozygous".
  - ACMG classification criteria `(PVS1+PM2+PP4)` are cited for both variants, which is appropriate given the null variants (frameshift and canonical `-1` splice acceptor) in genes where LOH/loss-of-function is a known mechanism, absence in gnomAD/1000G/EVS, and highly specific biochemical phenotypes (liver biopsy glycogen accumulation and hypoglycemia/elevated transaminases/CK in Patient 1; elevated lactate and Leigh neuroimaging/clinical regression in Patient 2). However, for *SURF1* `c.241-1G>C`, in silico or RT-PCR splicing verification (to confirm whether it causes exon skipping or cryptic splice activation vs. in-frame deletion) was not performed.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** "UPiD caused AR monogenic glycogen storage disease type-III (GSDIII) in patient 1 and Leigh syndrome in patient 2 through non-Mendelian inheritance of two mutant copies of a gene from each patient's father." `[Well Supported]` — Directly established by trio Sanger sequencing, WES copy-number/VAF analysis, and trio 750K SNP microarray haplotyping.
- **Claim 2:** "A single NGS-based analysis could allow us to find homozygous sequence variants and copy-neutral LOH in such cases." `[Well Supported]` — Whole-chromosome LOH (141–250 Mb) is readily visible in exome VAF plots when combined with exome read-depth CNV normalization (XHMM), and the authors appropriately caveat that small segmental LOH remains challenging due to sparse/uneven exonic SNP coverage.
- **Claim 3:** "Clinical features of patient 2 showed typical features of Leigh disease and no other typical symptoms of Leigh syndrome, which also suggests that no paternally imprinted gene on chromosome 9 and no maternal chromosome 10 have a major effect on phenotype." `[Moderately Supported]` — While consistent with the known lack of major classical imprinting syndromes on human chromosomes 9 and 10, Patient 2 died or acutely deteriorated at 18 months following hydrocephalus surgery and also had severe intrauterine growth restriction (birth weight 880 g at 32 weeks, $<3\text{rd}$ percentile) and an atrial septal defect. Attributing all features strictly to *SURF1* deficiency while ruling out subtle additive effects of other homozygous recessive variants on chromosomes 9 and 10 requires reporting all other rare homozygous coding variants on Chr9 and Chr10 from Patient 2's exome, which is omitted from the text.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Database Deposition:** The authors report submitting the variants and clinical phenotypes to the Leiden Open Variant Database (LOVD) for *AGL* and *SURF1* (`https://databases.lovd.nl/shared/individuals/AGL` and `SURF1`) and provide exact RefSeq transcript HGVS nomenclature (`NM_000028.2:c.3423_3424delTG` and `NM_003172.3:c.241-1G>C`).
- **Missing Complete Exome Variant Tables & Primers:** Raw WES fastq/BAM files are not deposited in a public repository, PCR primer sequences for Sanger confirmation are listed only as "available upon request," and the full list of other rare homozygous variants located on the isodisomic chromosomes (Chr1 in Patient 1; Chr9 and Chr10 in Patient 2) is not provided.
