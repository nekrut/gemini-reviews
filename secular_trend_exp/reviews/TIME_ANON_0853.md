### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This genomic association study investigates the genetic architecture of Brugada syndrome (BrS) in a Taiwanese cohort of 214 cases and 1,316 healthy controls from the Taiwan Biobank. The authors construct two customized imputation reference panels by combining 96 Taiwanese BrS cases genotyped on the Affymetrix TWB2 array with 1000 Genomes East Asian (1KG-EAS) haplotypes (`Merged-TWN-panel` and `Improved-TWN-panel`), benchmark imputation accuracy by masking 50% of array SNPs, and perform a logistic regression GWAS (adjusting for age, sex, and 20 PCs), GCTA-GREML SNP-heritability estimation, FUMA/MAGMA annotation, conditional SKAT rare-variant association testing (RVAT) on imputed dosages, S-PrediXcan eQTL analysis, and PennCNV/ParseCNV structural variant calling. They replicate the known *SCN10A* locus (`rs7617547`, $\text{OR} = 2.20, P = 3.78 \times 10^{-8}$) and report novel genome-wide significant low-frequency signals at *ZNF451* (`rs76107757`, $\text{OR} = 9.85, P = 6.85 \times 10^{-11}$) and *RP11-510I5* (`rs79266279` and `rs12427133`), alongside SKAT signals at *CDK17*, *CFAP54*, and *ZNF451-AS1* and a CNV duplication at *HRAS*. While replicating *SCN10A* is reassuring, the novel low-frequency and rare-variant claims are severely compromised by case-only reference panel bias, cross-array batch confounding between cases and controls, unvalidated rare-variant imputation (MAF $< 0.01$) from a small microarray reference panel, and direct numerical contradictions in the CNV methods.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
* **Case-Only Custom Reference Panel Bias & Potential Sample Overlap:** The custom `TWN-panel` used to augment 1KG-EAS was constructed exclusively from **96 BrS cases** genotyped on the TWB2 array (no controls). Furthermore, the Methods (lines 30–32) do not clarify whether these 96 BrS cases on TWB2 overlap with the 221 BrS cases in the study dataset (which were genotyped on "TPM and TWB2 arrays"). Even if non-overlapping, building a population-specific reference panel solely from cases (96 BrS patients) and using it to impute low-frequency/rare variants into a case-control study (where cases and controls were genotyped on different array mixtures, TPM + TWB2 vs. TWB) introduces differential imputation accuracy and allele frequency inflation in cases—a known source of false-positive rare/low-frequency associations such as the singleton-LD hit `rs76107757` at *ZNF451* ($\text{RAF} = 8.64\%$ in cases vs. $0.98\%$ in controls, $\text{OR} = 9.845$, with no supporting LD buddy SNPs) and the SKAT rare-variant signals (`CDK17` $P = 4.56 \times 10^{-10}$, `CFAP54` $P = 7.83 \times 10^{-8}$).
* **Invalidity of Array-Imputed Rare Variants ($\text{MAF} < 0.01$) for SKAT RVAT:** The custom `TWN-panel` was not derived from whole-genome sequencing; it was derived from 96 samples genotyped on a SNP microarray (Affymetrix TWB2, 684,436 SNPs) merged with 1KG-EAS (which contains no Taiwanese-specific sequencing samples, only CHB/CHS/JPT/CDX/KHV). As the authors' own simulation (Figure 4) demonstrates, for rare SNPs ($\text{MAF} < 0.01$) imputed with `Merged-TWN-panel`, only **21.95%** achieved a Hellinger score $\ge 0.6$ (and only 10.91% of less-common SNPs)! Running SKAT burden/kernel tests on 151–243 imputed intronic rare variants ($\text{MAF} < 0.01$) when ~78% of imputed rare variants fail basic posterior probability concordance—and where INFO $> 0.4$ is far too lenient for rare-variant association—produces severe Type I error inflation.
* **Direct Self-Contradiction in CNV Length Thresholds and Implausible Duplication Call:**
  1. On line 114, the Methods state: *"Call-level QC ensured each CNV contained at least 10 SNPs and spanned less than 50 kb. Deletions or duplications were considered valid if they were at least 100 kb in length and included at least 10 consecutive SNPs."* Requiring a CNV to simultaneously span **less than 50 kb** AND **at least 100 kb** is a mathematical contradiction.
  2. Worse, in the Results (line 222), the two highlighted significant CNVs are `chr16:1768782-1792146` (which spans **23.4 kb**) and `chr11:534197-535098` at *HRAS* (which spans only **901 base pairs**!). Calling a 901 bp duplication requiring $\ge 10$ consecutive array probes from SNP microarray intensity files without qPCR/MLPA orthogonal validation—and when cases and controls were processed from separate datasets/arrays—is highly prone to batch intensity artifacts.
* **Contradiction Between Methods and Results on eQTL Analysis:** In the Methods (line 107) and FUMA Results (line 203), the authors state that S-PrediXcan was run on genome-wide significant SNPs from the imputed dataset and that FUMA identified **four significant eQTLs at FDR $q < 0.05$** (Supplementary Table S3). Yet on line 217, the Results state that *"eQTL analysis was conducted using MetaXcan with GWAS summary statistics from the non-imputed study dataset. No significant eQTLs were identified at a threshold of P < 0.05."*
* **Underpowered GCTA-GREML Heritability Estimates ($N = 1,530$):** GCTA-GREML requires several thousand unrelated individuals to yield bounded, reliable genome-wide $h^2_{\text{SNP}}$ estimates. Here, with $N = 1,530$, the standard errors are larger than or comparable to the point estimates ($h^2_{\text{SNP}} = 0.18 \pm 0.20$), and the partitioned sums are non-additive (`Common` $0.06$ + `Less-common` $0.26 = 0.32 \ne 0.18$ for `All SNPs`).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
* **Strengths:** Rigorous clinical phenotyping of 214 Taiwanese Brugada syndrome patients (diagnosed by two independent cardiologists using consensus ECG criteria), replication of the canonical *SCN10A* locus (`rs7617547`, $P = 3.78 \times 10^{-8}$) and suggestive *SCN5A* signal, and transparent reporting of the lack of an independent replication cohort in the Limitations section.
* **Claim 1 (*SCN10A* is a genome-wide significant susceptibility locus for BrS in the Taiwanese population):** `[Well Supported]` — Supported by lead SNP `rs7617547` ($\text{OR} = 2.20, P = 3.78 \times 10^{-8}$), MAGMA gene-based significance, cardiac enhancer chromatin marks, and extensive prior literature.
* **Claim 2 (`Merged-TWN-panel` provides the highest imputation accuracy across all SNP categories):** `[Fragile]` — Contradicted by Figures 3 and 4: while `Merged-TWN-panel` had the highest raw completion rate (99.99%) and common-SNP INFO pass rate, `Improved-TWN-panel` had higher INFO pass rates for less-common/rare SNPs (Figure 3) and `1KG-EAS` had the highest Hellinger accuracy across all three MAF bins (Figure 4: 31.26%, 11.51%, 23.63%).
* **Claim 3 (*ZNF451* / *ZNF451-AS1* is a key susceptibility locus bridging common and rare-variant contributions to BrS):** `[Fragile]` — `rs76107757` is a low-frequency imputed variant ($\text{MAF} = 0.02$, $\text{RAF} = 0.0098$ in controls) with no LD support, imputed using a case-only Taiwanese panel, and unvalidated by direct genotyping or an independent replication cohort.
* **Claim 4 (A 901 bp CNV duplication at *HRAS* and rare intronic variants in *CDK17* and *CFAP54* are associated with BrS):** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Rests on low-accuracy array-imputed rare variants ($\text{HS} > 0.6$ in only 22% of rare SNPs) and a sub-kilobase microarray CNV call that violates the manuscript's own stated 100 kb length filter.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **De-Identification Leak Note:** Lines 246–249 contain partially unredacted author first names and the venue name (`Bioinformatics and Biology Insights`, DOI suffix `10.1177_11779322251385926`), which were ignored per the double-blind review protocol.
