### 1. Executive Summary
This article is a narrative conference report summarizing an International Baculovirus and Insect Cell Gene Expression Conference (26–30 March, Pinehurst, North Carolina; >200 participants from 22 countries). It surveys four topical areas of the baculovirus expression vector system (BEVS, prototyped by *Autographa californica* nuclear polyhedrosis virus [AcNPV] in *Spodoptera frugiperda* Sf9/Sf21 and *Trichoplusia ni* High 5 cells): (1) agricultural biopesticide engineering (e.g., deleting the viral ecdysteroid UDP-glucosyltransferase [*egt*] gene and inserting the scorpion sodium-channel toxin *AaIT* to create `vEGTDEL-AaIT`); (2) biomolecular expression and post-translational N-linked glycosylation limitations; (3) medical diagnostics, vaccines (HIV-1 gp160 VaxSyn, bluetongue virus), and candidate therapeutics (electroinserted RBC-rmCD4, PEGylated glucocerebrosidase) alongside FDA cGMP requirements; and (4) bioreactor scale-up (100 mL to 36–100 L vessels, oxygen demand, HARV microgravity, and dielectrophoresis cell separation). Because it is a meeting summary rather than a primary research study, it contains no original experimental protocols, primary datasets, or formal statistical analyses.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Biology of AcNPV infection, apoptosis inhibition (*p35*, *iap*), and *egt*/*AaIT* biopesticide engineering:** `[Well Supported]` — The description of polyhedrin dissolution in the alkaline lepidopteran midgut, tracheal systemic spread, anti-apoptotic viral genes (*p35*, *iap*), and accelerated larval paralysis via *egt* deletion and *AaIT* toxin expression is biologically accurate.
* **Insect vs. mammalian N-linked glycosylation differences in BEVS:** `[Well Supported]` — The report accurately highlights that lepidopteran cells predominantly assemble high-mannose/paucimannose glycans rather than complex terminally sialylated mammalian oligosaccharides, affecting therapeutic glycoprotein half-life.
* **Clinical prospects of HIV-1 gp160 (VaxSyn) and RBC-rmCD4 therapeutics:** `[Moderately Supported]` — The report appropriately notes that "It is too early to determine whether the subjects vaccinated with VaxSyn, the gp160 AIDS vaccine, will remain free of the disease," tempering enthusiasm with a realistic discussion of FDA regulatory hurdles.
