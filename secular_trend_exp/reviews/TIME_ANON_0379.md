### 1. Executive Summary
This short communication evaluates the DNA methylation status of the estrogen receptor (`ER` / `ESR1`) and epidermal growth factor receptor (`EGFR`) genes using `HpaII`/`MspI` restriction enzyme digestion and Southern blotting across 20 primary breast carcinoma specimens and 16 paired histologically normal perineoplastic breast tissue samples from the same patients, alongside cytosolic and nuclear `ER` and progesterone receptor (`PR`) enzyme immunoassays (`Abbott EIA`) and membrane `EGFR` radioligand binding assays. The authors report that `ER` gene hypomethylation (defined by the appearance of a `3.5 kb HpaII` band) is more frequent in neoplastic (`14/20`, $70\%$) than perineoplastic (`4/16`, $25\%$) tissues ($p = 0.009$), that `ER` hypomethylation correlates with higher cytosolic and nuclear `ER` and `PR` levels and lower `EGFR` levels (`Table II`), and that `EGFR` methylation (`4 kb HpaII` band) neither differs between neoplastic (`10/19`) and perineoplastic (`8/14`) tissues nor correlates with `EGFR` protein levels. While the inverse relationship between `ER` gene methylation and `ER` protein expression in breast tissue is biologically plausible, the study suffers from serious statistical and tissue-sampling flaws: paired tumor and perineoplastic samples from the same patients are treated as independent observations and pooled together in two-sample $t$-tests, skewed receptor data are analyzed parametrically, and bulk tissue cellularity differences between adipose-rich normal breast and hypercellular carcinoma are unaddressed.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Violation of Statistical Independence via Pooling Paired Tumor and Perineoplastic Samples (`Table I` and `Table II`):**
  - The study collected paired neoplastic ($n = 20$) and adjacent perineoplastic ($n = 16$) tissues from the **same 20 patients**.
  - In `Table I`, the authors compare binary hypomethylation proportions (`4/16` vs. `14/20`) using a Mann-Whitney $U$-test ($p = 0.009$), which is an unpaired rank-sum test for continuous/ordinal variables, rather than McNemar's test on the 16 matched pairs (plus accounting for the 4 unmatched tumors).
  - Worse, in `Table II`, the authors pool all 16 perineoplastic and 20 neoplastic samples together ($N = 18\text{ hypomethylated} + 18\text{ hypermethylated} = 36\text{ total ER samples}$; and $N = 18 + 15 = 33\text{ total EGFR samples}$) and run unpaired two-sample Student's $t$-tests!
  - Pooling matched tumor and normal tissues from the same patients into an unpaired $t$-test violates sample independence and introduces severe **Simpson's / group-confounding bias**: because neoplastic tissues have both much higher `ER`/`PR` levels (as the authors note from their prior study) and higher `ER` hypomethylation (`14/20` vs. `4/16`), pooling tumor and perineoplastic samples guarantees a strong apparent correlation between `ER` hypomethylation and `ER`/`PR` protein levels even if that correlation is driven entirely by the tissue-type difference (tumor vs. normal) rather than within-tissue gene regulation! Although the Abstract claims *"In both tissues, ER gene methylation was inversely correlated with protein levels,"* `Table II` only presents the pooled $N = 36$ analysis and never reports stratified statistics within neoplastic ($n = 20$) and within perineoplastic ($n = 16$) tissues separately.
- **Parametric $t$-Tests on Heavily Skewed Receptor Concentrations (`Table II`):**
  - Inspection of the means and standard errors ($\text{s.e.}$) in `Table II` reveals extreme positive skewness across all receptor measurements: for example, in the hypermethylated `ER` group ($n = 18$), `PR(C)` has a mean of $59.2$ with $\text{s.e.} = 34.8$, implying a standard deviation of $\text{SD} = 34.8 \times \sqrt{18} = 147.6$ (nearly $2.5\times$ larger than the mean, which is impossible for a non-negative variable unless driven by 1–2 extreme outliers!). Similarly, `EGFR` in the hypomethylated `ER` group ($n = 18$) has mean $143.1 \pm 58.4\text{ s.e.}$ ($\text{SD} = 247.8$). Applying parametric Student's $t$-tests to such severely skewed data without log-transformation or non-parametric rank testing invalidates the reported $p$-values (`p = 0.0447`, `0.0222`, `0.0348`).
- **Confounding by Epithelial Cellularity in Bulk Tissue Homogenates:**
  - Surgically resected perineoplastic normal breast tissue (`1 cm^3`) consists predominantly of stromal fibroblasts and adipocytes with sparse ductal/lobular epithelium (<5–10% of nuclei), whereas invasive breast carcinoma is densely packed with malignant epithelial cells. Because `ER` is expressed and regulated specifically in luminal epithelial cells, bulk DNA extraction and bulk protein homogenates (`fmol/mg total protein` or `fmol/mg DNA`) from unmicrodissected perineoplastic breast largely measure stromal/adipose DNA methylation and dilute epithelial `ER` protein against stromal collagen/albumin.
- **Unmapped `CCGG` Sites Assayed by Full-Length cDNA Probes:**
  - Hybridizing `HpaII`/`MspI`-digested genomic DNA with a `1.3 kb` `ER` cDNA probe (`pOR3`) and a full-length `EGFR` cDNA probe detects internal exonic/intronic `CCGG` sites (`3.5 kb` and `4.0 kb` bands) rather than the 5' promoter CpG island (`Ishii et al.`). As the authors acknowledge in the Discussion for `EGFR`, methylation of gene-body `CCGG` sites does not reflect 5' promoter CpG island methylation.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **`[Moderately Supported]` Inverse association between `ER` (`ESR1`) gene methylation and `ER` protein expression in breast cancer:** While subsequent bisulfite-sequencing literature confirmed that `ESR1` 5' CpG island methylation silences `ER` in `ER`-negative breast tumors, in this dataset the correlation in `Table II` is confounded by pooling tumor and normal tissues and by using a gene-body `pOR3` cDNA probe.
- **`[Overstated]` `ER` gene hypomethylation in neoplastic relative to perineoplastic breast tissue plays "a significant role in neoplastic transformation":** The apparent shift from hypermethylated perineoplastic tissue (`12/16`) to hypomethylated tumor tissue (`14/20`) largely reflects comparing paucicellular stromal/adipose perineoplastic tissue against clonal luminal-epithelial carcinoma (`ER+` luminal breast cancer), rather than active demethylation driving oncogenic transformation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Deficiencies:** No representative Southern blot autoradiographs showing the `3.5 kb` (`ER`) and `4.0 kb` (`EGFR`) `HpaII`/`MspI` digestion patterns (or verifying complete `HpaII` digestion to rule out partial-digestion artifacts) are included in the paper, and individual patient-level paired tumor/perineoplastic data are not provided.
