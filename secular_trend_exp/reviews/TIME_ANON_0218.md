### 1. Executive Summary
This methodological study develops a two-marker linkage disequilibrium (tmLD) quantitative trait mapping model that integrates two-locus and three-locus (trigenic) gametic disequilibrium parameters ($D_{12}, D_{23}, D_{13}, D_{123}$) into a Gaussian mixture likelihood estimated via a nested expectation-maximization (EM) algorithm. The authors compare `tmLD` against three existing approaches—single-marker regression (`smAT`), single-marker LD mapping (`smLD`, Hou et al. 2005), and two-marker haplotype regression (`haplo`)—using Monte Carlo simulations and apply all four methods to a genome-wide association study (GWAS) of dental caries from dbGaP (`phs000095.v2.p1`, $N = 1,843$ subjects, 443,175 SNPs). While the algebraic parameterization of the two-marker conditional QTL genotype probabilities (Table 1) and the nested EM updates are clearly derived, the manuscript suffers from a fundamental asymptotic statistical flaw regarding the null distribution of the likelihood ratio test (LRT) in mixture models, as well as unaddressed population stratification and anomalous $p$-value inflation in the real GWAS application.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Asymptotic Non-Identifiability of the Mixture Model LRT Under $H_0$:**
  - In Equation (5), the authors define the likelihood ratio test statistic $\text{LR} = -2(\ln L_0(\tilde{\mu}, \tilde{\sigma}^2) - \ln L(\hat{\mathbf{\Omega}}_q, \hat{\mathbf{\Omega}}_p))$ under the null hypothesis $H_0: \mu_0 = \mu_1 = \mu_2$ (no QTL effect) and state: *"The test statistic LR relies on three hypotheses on $\mu_2 = \mu_1 = \mu_0$, and also $D_{12} = D_{23} = D_{123} = 0$, so that LR asymptotically follows a chi-square distribution with df = 3."*
  - **Fundamental Theoretical Error:** Under $H_0: \mu_0 = \mu_1 = \mu_2$, the 3-component Gaussian mixture collapses to a single normal distribution, and the latent QTL allele frequency $q$ as well as the LD parameters ($D_{12}, D_{23}, D_{123}$) completely vanish from the likelihood (they are nuisance parameters present only under the alternative hypothesis, and furthermore constrained to a closed boundary pyramid by Fréchet bounds on haplotype frequencies $p_{ijk} \ge 0$). By classical statistical theory (Davies 1977; Self & Liang 1987; Ghosh & Sen 1985), Wilks' theorem fails for mixture models under $H_0$, and the LRT does **not** asymptotically follow a $\chi^2_3$ distribution.
  - Furthermore, the assertion that $\mu_2 = \mu_1 = \mu_0$ and $D_{12} = D_{23} = D_{123} = 0$ jointly yield $\text{df} = 3$ is self-contradictory: $\mu_2 = \mu_1 = \mu_0$ imposes 2 linear constraints on the means ($\mu_1 - \mu_0 = 0, \mu_2 - \mu_0 = 0$), while $q, D_{12}, D_{23}, D_{123}$ are 4 additional non-identifiable parameters under $\mu_2 = \mu_1 = \mu_0$. Using a naive $\chi^2_3$ threshold (`3.81e-6`, line 172) without permutation testing or parametric bootstrap invalidates extreme tail $p$-values ($P < 10^{-7}$).
- **Anomalous $p$-Value Discrepancies in the Real GWAS Application (Table 2):**
  - In Table 2 (real dental caries GWAS), several SNP pairs exhibit extreme `tmLD` significance despite having completely flat single-marker and haplotype signals. Most strikingly, on chromosome 11 (`rs10790497` and `rs10501856` in *CNTN5*), single-marker association is $P_{\text{smAT}} = \mathbf{0.88}$, single-marker LD is $P_{\text{smLD}} = \mathbf{0.82}$, and two-marker haplotype regression is $P_{\text{haplo}} = 1.7 \times 10^{-3}$, yet `tmLD` reports $P_{\text{tmLD}} = \mathbf{2.6 \times 10^{-8}}$.
  - Why does a locus where neither marker has any marginal association ($p = 0.88$) produce a $2.6 \times 10^{-8}$ signal in a 3-component Gaussian mixture with a single common variance $\sigma^2$? When phenotype residuals are skewed or zero-inflated (as dental caries counts/indices typically are!), an unconstrained 3-component Gaussian mixture EM algorithm can fit the non-normality of the trait distribution by assigning extreme component means ($\mu_0, \mu_1, \mu_2$) with weak marker coupling, inflating the mixture likelihood over the single-Gaussian null $L_0(\tilde{\mu}, \tilde{\sigma}^2)$. Because $L_0$ fits a single normal distribution to the trait while $L_1$ fits a 3-component normal mixture, **any departure of the phenotype from normality will inflate $\text{LR}$ across the genome** even when $D_{12} = D_{23} = D_{123} \approx 0$!
- **Omission of Population Stratification Adjustment in the Real Data Analysis:**
  - In the Discussion (lines 250–266), the authors outline how principal component analysis (PCA) could be incorporated into the regression model (Equations 6–7) to control for population structure. However, they admit (line 266) that *"The extension of these methods in detail are separate topic in our future validation,"* confirming that **no PCA or kinship adjustment was applied** to the multi-site dbGaP dental caries cohort (`phs000095.v2.p1`). No genomic inflation factor ($\lambda_{\text{GC}}$) or Q-Q plot is reported.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** The two-marker LD mixture model (`tmLD`) achieves higher power than single-marker and haplotype regression in Monte Carlo simulations when a causal QTL is in incomplete LD with two flanking markers. `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported within the parametric Gaussian simulation setting where the data are generated directly from the assumed 3-component normal model.
- **Claim 2:** `tmLD` discovers five novel genome-wide significant genes for dental caries (*CNTN5*, *COL4A2*, *PKD2*, *SGCG*, *LRP1B*). `[Methodologically Flawed]` / `[Overstated]` — Because the null model $L_0$ is a 1-component Gaussian whereas the alternative $L_1$ is a 3-component Gaussian mixture, non-normality of the dental caries phenotype and unadjusted population stratification can drive spurious mixture-model LRT inflation (as evidenced by $P_{\text{smAT}} = 0.88$ vs. $P_{\text{tmLD}} = 2.6 \times 10^{-8}$ at *CNTN5*).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Full mathematical derivations of the conditional genotype probabilities (Table 1) and nested EM updates (Appendix A), dbGaP study accession (`phs000095.v2.p1`), and an R package (`Journal.tmLD` on GitHub) are provided.
- **Weaknesses:** No Q-Q plots, genomic inflation factors ($\lambda_{\text{GC}}$), or permutation-calibrated null distributions on real or non-normal phenotypes are provided.
