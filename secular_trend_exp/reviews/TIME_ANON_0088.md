### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This primary experimental virology study maps the amino acid residues contributing to the four major antigenic sites (A, B, C, and D) of the transmissible gastroenteritis virus (TGEV) S (spike/peplomer) glycoprotein. Using sequence analysis of 21 monoclonal antibody (MAb) neutralization/binding escape mutants (mar mutants) combined with overlapping synthetic peptide scanning (PEPSCAN), tunicamycin/glycosidase folding vs. post-assembly glycosylation dependence assays, and pH/temperature conformational exposure assays, the authors localize subsite Aa (residue 538), Ab (residue 591), Ac (residue 543, plus peptide 537-MKSGYGQPIA-547), overlapping residue 586 (mutant 1B.H6), site B (residues 97 and 144), site C (residues 50–51, peptide 48-P-P/S-N-S-D/E-52), and site D (residue 385). While the text available in the archived record consists of the detailed abstract and reference list (with the main text body locked in a scanned PDF), the experimental logic—combining genetic escape mapping across 21 independent mar mutants with orthogonal linear peptide scanning and biophysical perturbation—is classical, mechanistically coherent, and well-grounded.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
#### STRENGTHS
- **Orthogonal Epitope Mapping Strategy:** Combining selection and sequencing of 21 MAb-resistant (`mar`) escape mutants with synthetic peptide scanning (PEPSCAN) distinguishes conformational/discontinuous epitopes (sites A and B) from linear/partially continuous epitopes (sites C and D, and subsite Ac peptide 537–547).
- **Biophysical & Folding Dissection of Glycosylation:** The study cleanly separates co-translational folding dependence on glycosylation (sites A and B require glycosylation during synthesis/folding) from direct carbohydrate participation in the mature epitope (once assembled into the virion, carbohydrate removal does not abrogate antigenicity).
- **Cryptic Epitope Exposure Controls:** Mapping site C (residues 50–51) and demonstrating that it is cryptic on native virions but exposed upon alkaline treatment (pH > 11 at 4°C) or thermal perturbation (> 45°C) provides strong physical evidence of conformational masking on the native spike trimer.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Truncation of Archival Main Text:** Because the archival text conversion captured only the abstract and bibliography, full sequence chromatograms, exact MAb neutralization titers, and binding affinity constants ($K_d$ or ELISA OD curves) cannot be audited line-by-line.
- **Escape Mutant vs. Contact Residue Ambiguity:** In MAb escape analysis ($n = 21$ mutants), single amino acid substitutions (such as residue 586 in mutant 1B.H6 affecting both Aa and Ab, or residues 97 and 144 in site B) can act either as direct antibody contact residues or via local allosteric conformational perturbation; without site-directed reverse genetics or structural determination, attribution of direct contact should remain cautious.
- **Vaccine Protection Extrapolation:** The suggestion that conserved peptide 537-MKSGYGQPIA-547 (subsite Ac) "could be of interest for protection" is not backed by in vivo swine challenge data in the abstract.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Residues 538, 591, 543, and 586 define overlapping subsites Aa, Ab, and Ac of major neutralizing site A on TGEV S glycoprotein, while residues 97/144, 50/51, and 385 contribute to sites B, C, and D. `[Well Supported]` by escape mutant sequencing and PEPSCAN concordance.
- **Claim 2:** Sites A and B depend on glycosylation for proper folding, whereas sites C and D are independent or partially independent, and mature virion antigenicity does not require the carbohydrate moiety post-assembly. `[Well Supported]` by standard tunicamycin/endoglycosidase experimental design.
- **Claim 3:** Conserved peptide 537–547 (subsite Ac) is relevant for diagnosis and potentially protection. `[Moderately Supported]` for diagnosis; `[Overstated]` if interpreted as demonstrated protective efficacy without animal challenge.
