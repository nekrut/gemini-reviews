# Blinded Review: TIME_ANON_63
**Composite Score:** 4.5 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.2/3.5 | Claims: 1.2/2.5 | Biology: 1.1/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.0/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Unadjusted Single-Gene Observational Confounding & Table Arithmetic Error
**Verdict:** Reject
**Study Modality:** Wet-Lab Primary Experimental
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Epidemiological Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
This study measures elongation factor-1 delta (*EEF1D* / EF-1 delta) mRNA expression by SYBR Green real-time RT-PCR (normalized to *GAPDH* and calibrated against the KYSE30 cell line) in 52 paired cancerous and non-cancerous esophageal tissue specimens from patients undergoing curative esophagectomy. The authors report that EF-1 delta mRNA is higher in tumor tissue than adjacent normal mucosa in 38/52 (73%) patients ($0.250 \pm 0.200$ vs. $0.178 \pm 0.151$, $P < 0.01$). Dichotomizing the 52 patients at the tumor median ($0.198$) into high ($n=26$) and low ($n=26$) expression groups, the authors report associations with lymph node metastasis ($P = 0.032$), advanced TNM stage ($P = 0.023$), and 5-year cause-specific survival ($23\%$ vs. $54\%$, $P = 0.046$, while overall survival is $19\%$ vs. $42\%$, $P = 0.065$).

While the clinical follow-up is long (median 8.0 years for survivors) and the RT-PCR melting-curve/gel verification is standard, the study has major statistical and methodological flaws: (1) Table 1 contains an arithmetic error in the "Lymph node metastasis" row ($3 + 24 = 27 \ne 26$) that directly affects the reported $P = 0.032$ association; (2) no multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression is performed, leaving the borderline univariable cause-specific survival difference ($P = 0.046$) completely confounded by TNM stage and lymph node metastasis; (3) the cohort mixes histologically distinct malignancies (squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, basal cell carcinoma, and small-cell carcinoma); and (4) normalizing a translation elongation factor to *GAPDH* in bulk tumor vs. normal dissected mucosa ignores both epithelial-to-stromal cellularity differences and the well-known upregulation of *GAPDH* and translational machinery in proliferating cells.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Arithmetic Inconsistency in Table 1 ("Lymph node metastasis"):** In Table 1 (lines 84–86), the High expression column is stated to have $n = 26$ patients, yet the counts for Lymph node metastasis are "Absent: 3" and "Present: 24", which sum to $27$ (and total $3 + 9 + 24 + 16 = 53$ patients instead of 52). Meanwhile, the text (line 61) states "24 of 26, 92%", which implies "Absent" in the High expression group must be $2$, not $3$. Furthermore, using the correct $2 \times 2$ contingency table ($24/26$ vs. $16/26$), a two-sided Fisher's exact test yields $P = 0.019$ (or $\chi^2$ with Yates' continuity correction $P = 0.021$; uncorrected $\chi^2 = 6.93, P = 0.0085$), whereas if the table is $23/26$ vs. $16/26$ (if Absent=3 is correct), uncorrected $\chi^2 = 4.95, P = 0.026$ and Fisher's exact $P = 0.0503$. The reported $P = 0.032$ does not match standard exact or $\chi^2$ tests on the consistent counts, and the table itself is arithmetically invalid.
* **Typographical/Classification Error in Table 1 ("Clinical stage"):** Lines 94–95 list the strata as "I, II" and "II, IV" instead of "I, II" and "III, IV".
* **Absence of Multivariable Survival Analysis (Confounding by Stage/Node Status):** The authors claim EF-1 delta mRNA is a prognostic biomarker based on a univariable log-rank test for cause-specific survival ($P = 0.046$), while overall survival is not statistically significant ($P = 0.065$, with 13 of 40 deaths due to non-cancer causes). Because the High EF-1 delta group is heavily enriched for advanced Stage III/IV disease ($77\%$ vs. $46\%$) and lymph node metastasis ($92\%$ vs. $62\%$), the univariable survival difference is entirely expected from stage confounding alone. Without a multivariable Cox regression adjusting for stage, depth of invasion, and nodal status, there is no evidence that EF-1 delta adds independent prognostic value.
* **Inappropriate Parametric Testing on Skewed Ratio Data:** In Figure 1 (line 61), tumor EF-1 delta expression ($0.250 \pm 0.200$, median $0.198$) and normal expression ($0.178 \pm 0.151$, median $0.151$) exhibit strong positive skew (standard deviations nearly equal to the means). Moreover, the 52 tumor and normal samples are paired from the same patients, yet the caption states only "The P-value was calculated by the Student's t-test" without specifying whether a paired test or non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used.
* **Heterogeneous Tumor Histologies Pooled Together:** The cohort of 52 patients pools 45 squamous cell carcinomas with 2 adenocarcinomas, 3 basal cell carcinomas, and 2 small-cell neuroendocrine carcinomas (line 23). Small-cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma of the esophagus have fundamentally different cell-of-origin biology, proliferation rates, and clinical trajectories than squamous cell carcinoma.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Prognostic Biomarker and Proto-Oncogene Claims [Overstated]:** Concluding that EF-1 delta is a "diagnostic and prognostic biomarker" (line 114) based on an unadjusted $P = 0.046$ in 52 heterogeneous patients—where overall survival is non-significant ($P = 0.065$) and 27% of tumors have lower expression than normal mucosa—overstates the clinical utility. Furthermore, no protein-level validation (Western blot or immunohistochemistry) is performed to confirm whether elevated EF-1 delta mRNA translates into elevated EF-1 delta protein in esophageal tumor cells versus infiltrating lymphocytes or stroma.
* **Confounding by Proliferation Rate and Single Reference Gene (*GAPDH*) [Fragile]:** Translation elongation factors (EF-1$\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, $\delta$) are canonically upregulated alongside ribosomal biogenesis in any rapidly dividing cell population compared to quiescent normal squamous epithelium. Normalizing solely to *GAPDH*—which is itself regulated by hypoxia (HIF-1$\alpha$) and glycolytic shifts in squamous cell carcinomas—without a second housekeeping gene (e.g., *ACTB*, *18S* rRNA, or *HPRT1*) makes it impossible to distinguish specific oncogenic upregulation of *EEF1D* from generic proliferative/metabolic scaling in bulk tumor tissue.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Protocol Specification:** The RT-PCR primer sequences, annealing temperatures, MgCl$_2$ concentrations, and LightCycler cycling parameters are clearly stated (lines 35–49).
* **Data Availability:** Individual patient-level expression values, Ct/crossing-point values, standard curve slopes/efficiencies, and survival times are not provided, and the arithmetic error in Table 1 prevents exact reconstruction of the contingency tables.
