### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This computational structural bioinformatics study introduces DeepGNHV, a graph neural network framework for predicting human–virus protein–protein interactions (PPIs). The authors curate 31,142 experimentally validated physical human–virus PPIs across 6,729 human and 1,154 viral proteins from six databases (BioGRID, IntAct, HPIDB, MINT, PHISTO, VirHostNet), represent each protein monomer as an $8\text{ \AA}$ contact graph built from AlphaFold2 structures with residue nodes featurized by frozen 1024-dimensional ProtT5-XL-UniRef50 embeddings, and train a two-layer weight-sharing Siamese Graph Attention Network (GATConv) followed by global max pooling, Hadamard product fusion, and a multilayer perceptron classifier. Using a 5-fold cross-validation scheme split by viral proteins—evaluating both seen viral proteins (`C1Virus`) and held-out viral proteins (`C2Virus`) at a 1:10 positive-to-negative ratio—DeepGNHV outperforms five sequence/embedding baselines (HBFormer, TransPPI, HVPPI, CAPHV, DeepViral) and an AlphaFold3 ipTM baseline ($n = 300$ pairs), achieving an AUPRC of $0.573$ on `C2Virus`. The authors also perform extensive ablation studies, label-noise robustness tests, proteome-wide HPV interaction predictions across 6 high-risk and 6 low-risk HPV subtypes, and a GNNExplainer case study on the HIV-1 gp120–human CD4 complex (`PDB: 3J70`). While the architecture and ablations are well-executed, random viral-protein splitting without sequence-identity clustering allows homolog leakage into `C2Virus`, and the gp120 GNNExplainer case study reveals weak viral interface localization (only 1 of 43 predicted key residues overlaps the true 22-residue gp120 binding interface).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
* **Homology Leakage in `C2Virus` Cross-Validation Splits:** To evaluate generalization to "novel viral proteins," the authors randomly partition the 1,154 viral proteins into 5 folds (`C2Virus`). However, because viral proteins were split **randomly** rather than clustered by sequence similarity (e.g., CD-HIT or MMseqs2 at $\le 30\text{--}40\%$ identity) or held out at the viral family/species level, closely related viral strains or subtypes (such as multiple HIV-1 isolates or HPV subtypes sharing $>70\text{--}90\%$ sequence identity and identical host partners) are split across training and `C2Virus` test folds. The authors themselves discover this exact phenomenon in the Results (line 79 and Supplementary Fig. 3): they note that HIV achieves exceptionally high `C2Virus` performance because *"HIV is characterized by a substantial number of highly similar HIV protein pairs sharing the same human host interacting partners, which is less frequently observed in other viruses, particularly in Hepatitis and SARS-CoV."* Thus, `C2Virus` overestimates true zero-shot generalization to genuinely novel, evolutionarily distant viruses.
* **Dissimilarity-Based Negative Sampling Shortcut:** Negative pairs (10 per positive) were generated using "dissimilarity-based negative sampling" (sequence identity $< 60\%$ to known interacting partners). While intended to reduce false negatives, sequence-dissimilarity filtering can make negative pairs easier to separate from positive pairs in ProtT5 embedding space compared to hard negatives (e.g., subcellularly co-localized non-interacting proteins or degree-matched host hubs).
* **Weak Interface Localization on the Viral Side in GNNExplainer Case Study (Fig. 5):** Because DeepGNHV applies global max pooling (`global_max_pool`) across each protein graph *before* computing the pairwise Hadamard product between the human and viral graph vectors, the two proteins never attend to each other at the residue level (i.e., there is no cross-attention between human and viral residues). Consequently, when GNNExplainer is applied to the CD4–gp120 complex (`PDB: 3J70`), while 10 of 22 highlighted residues on human CD4 fall within the 18-residue crystal interface, **only 1 of 43** highlighted residues on HIV-1 gp120 overlaps the 22-residue viral interface (line 106). Claiming that 9 additional residues within $< 16\text{ \AA}$ support interface localization is overly generous given that $16\text{ \AA}$ encompasses a massive fraction of a globular domain's volume.
* **Mathematical Formulas Missing From Rendered Text (Lines 39, 49–54, 62):** Several inline mathematical symbols and numbered display equations (between lines 50–54 and 61–62) were dropped during XML/Markdown text conversion (e.g., *"length [missing L]"*, *"Hadamard product [missing symbol]"*), though the architecture is clearly described in words and Figure 1.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
* **Strengths:** Comprehensive and fair benchmarking against five locally re-implemented state-of-the-art predictors and AlphaFold3 ipTM; thorough architectural ablations (Supplementary Tables 3 and 6–11 testing graph types, contact thresholds, language models [ProtT5 vs. ESM2, SaProt, TAPE], GNN layers [GAT, GCN, TransformerConv, GraphSAGE, GIN], Siamese weight sharing, max vs. mean pooling, and degree-preserving adjacency randomization); and honest analysis of dataset homology bias in HIV vs. Hepatitis/SARS-CoV.
* **Claim 1 (Integrating AlphaFold2 monomer contact graphs with ProtT5 embeddings via Siamese GAT improves human–virus PPI prediction over sequence-only baselines):** `[Well Supported]` — Supported by consistent AUPRC gains on `C1Virus` and `C2Virus` and the 16-percentage-point AUPRC drop under degree-preserving adjacency perturbation (Supplementary Table 11), which proves the contact graph provides non-trivial structural signal beyond the sequence embeddings.
* **Claim 2 (DeepGNHV generalizes effectively to newly emerging/uncharacterized viruses):** `[Moderately Supported]` — It outperforms competing methods on `C2Virus`, but performance is strongly inflated for densely sampled homologous viral strains (HIV) and drops substantially for distinct viral families (Hepatitis, SARS-CoV).
* **Claim 3 (High-risk vs. low-risk HPV subtypes cluster distinctly by predicted host GO pathways, and 22 of 23 predicted pan-HR-HPV host targets are experimentally supported):** `[Moderately Supported]` — Biologically coherent (enriching for epithelial polarity and PDZ domain binding), though the 22/23 validation rate partly reflects that known HR-HPV interactions were present in the training set (`C1` mode).
* **Claim 4 (GNNExplainer accurately pinpoints key binding interface residues without residue-level supervision):** `[Fragile]` — Works moderately on the human receptor CD4 (10/22 in interface) but fails on the viral ligand gp120 (1/43 in interface) due to late fusion after global max pooling.
