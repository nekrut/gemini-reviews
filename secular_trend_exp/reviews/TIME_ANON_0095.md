### 1. Executive Summary
This comparative genomics and molecular evolution study investigates homologous recombination and natural selection in the African swine fever virus (ASFV) serotype-specific locus encoding the C-type lectin (`EP153R`) and `CD2v` (`EP402R`) transmembrane glycoproteins across 70 paired public GenBank sequences. Using `PAL2NAL`/`MUSCLE` codon alignments, `RDP4` (7 recombination detection algorithms), `SimPlot` Bootscan, `SplitsTree5` phylogenetic networks, `SDT v1.2` pairwise identity matrices, `PAML CodeML` ($d_N/d_S$), the McDonald-Kreitman (`MK`) polymorphism test ($p_N/p_S$), and Datamonkey `FEL` site-level selection analysis, the authors identify 5 putative recombinant strains in `EP153R` and 5 in `EP402R` (predominantly from East Africa, while confirming zero recombination in negative control structural genes `B646L`/p72 and `CP204L`/p30), report global $\omega = d_N/d_S$ values of $0.50$ and $0.46$ alongside intra-population $p_N/p_S$ ratios of $2.04$ (`47/23`) and $3.17$ (`146/46`), and call 5 positively selected codons in `EP153R` and 2 in `EP402R` (`FEL p < 0.05`). While the biological premise—that sylvatic tick-warthog-pig co-infections in East Africa drive mosaicism at the immunodominant `EP153R`/`EP402R` locus—is well grounded, the evolutionary bioinformatics pipeline suffers from a fundamental methodological flaw: **performing tree-based codon selection inference (`PAML CodeML` and Datamonkey `FEL`) on unpartitioned alignments after demonstrating strong intragenic recombination**, without using `GARD` (Genetic Algorithm for Recombination Detection) to partition the alignments into non-recombinant segments with segment-specific phylogenies. Additionally, the application and interpretation of the McDonald-Kreitman test ($p_N/p_S$) are mis-specified.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Fatal Confounding of Codon Selection (`FEL` and `CodeML`) by Intragenic Recombination:**
  - It is a foundational principle of molecular evolution (established by Anisimova, Nielsen, and Yang [2003] and Kosakovsky Pond et al. [2006]) that running single-tree phylogenetic likelihood selection tests (`PAML CodeML` and `HyPhy FEL`) across an alignment containing intragenic recombination breakpoints causes severe false-positive rates for positively selected sites, because topology mismatches across recombinant segments force the maximum-likelihood model to infer spurious recurrent nonsynonymous substitutions along incompatible branches.
  - Even though the authors themselves prove in Table 1, Figure 1, and Figure 3 (`SplitsTree5` reticulate networks) that `EP153R` and `EP402R` have pervasive intragenic recombination and explicitly state in line 88 that *"in case of reticulate evolutionary events, no tree-based model can accurately model the data"*, they proceed to run `CodeML` and `FEL` across the unpartitioned 70-strain alignments without first removing the recombinant strains or partitioning the alignments at recombination breakpoints using `GARD`.
  - Furthermore, in `Table 2`, 4 of the 5 codons claimed to be under pervasive diversifying selection in `EP153R` (`codons 9, 14, 23, 98`) and both codons in `EP402R` (`codons 252, 359`) lie directly inside or adjacent to the recombinant regions mapped in `Table 1` and `Figure 2`!
- **Misapplication and Misinterpretation of the McDonald-Kreitman Test (`Table 2`, Lines 66 & 139):**
  - The classical McDonald-Kreitman (`MK`) test compares the ratio of nonsynonymous to synonymous **polymorphisms within a species** ($P_n / P_s$) against the ratio of nonsynonymous to synonymous **fixed substitutions between species** ($D_n / D_s$, requiring an outgroup) via a $2 \times 2$ contingency table ($\text{Neutrality Index } NI = \frac{P_n/P_s}{D_n/D_s}$, where $D_n/D_s > P_n/P_s$ indicates adaptive fixation).
  - Here, no outgroup species is specified, no fixed divergence counts ($D_n, D_s$) or Fisher's exact / $G$-test p-values are reported, and the authors simply divide total nonsynonymous polymorphic sites ($P_n = 47$ for `EP153R`, $P_n = 146$ for `EP402R`) by total synonymous polymorphic sites ($P_s = 23$ and $46$) without normalizing by the number of nonsynonymous vs. synonymous sites ($L_N \approx 3.5 L_S$ in the genetic code!). Because a random coding sequence has roughly $3\text{--}3.5\times$ more nonsynonymous sites than synonymous sites, an unnormalized count ratio $P_n / P_s$ of $2.04$ (`47/23`) or $3.17$ (`146/46`) actually corresponds to a per-site ratio $\pi_N / \pi_S < 1$, not positive selection! Moreover, under the MK framework, an excess of $P_n/P_s$ relative to $d_N/d_S$ ($2.04$ vs. $0.50$; $3.17$ vs. $0.46$) is the textbook signature of **segregating slightly deleterious nonsynonymous polymorphisms** (or balancing/frequency-dependent selection across serogroups), not directional positive fixation.
- **Concatenation of Non-Adjacent Loci in Bootscan (`Figure 1`):** `EP153R` and `EP402R` were artificially concatenated for `SimPlot` Bootscan (`Fig. 1`), which creates an artificial boundary in the middle of the sliding window (`200 bp` window) at the gene junction.
- **Alignment Artifacts in Hypervariable/Repeat Regions:** `EP402R` (`CD2v`) contains well-known proline-rich cytoplasmic tandem repeats and extensive length polymorphism (`indels`) across serogroups; aligning 70 divergent sequences with `MUSCLE` without masking gapped/repeat-rich regions can introduce frame-shifted or non-homologous codon columns that inflate both `FEL` site calls and `RDP4` breakpoint calls.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *East African ASFV isolates exhibit reticulate evolution and mosaic recombination breakpoints in `EP153R` (C-type lectin) and `EP402R` (`CD2v`), whereas pandemic Genotype II (Serogroup 8) strains and housekeeping structural genes (`B646L`/p72, `CP204L`/p30) are clonal/non-recombinant.* `[Well Supported]` — Supported by concordant calls across 4–7 `RDP4` methods ($p = 2.4 \times 10^{-5}$ to $8.2 \times 10^{-14}$), negative structural controls (`B646L`, `CP204L`), and `SplitsTree5` networks.
- **Claim 2:** *`p_N / p_S > 1` (`2.04` and `3.17`) from the McDonald-Kreitman test proves strong adaptive positive selection on `EP153R` and `EP402R`.* `[Methodologically Flawed]` — The authors report unnormalized site counts ($P_n / P_s = 47/23 = 2.04$ and $146/46 = 3.17$) without dividing by total nonsynonymous and synonymous sites ($L_N, L_S$) and without an outgroup fixed-divergence comparison ($D_n/D_s$).
- **Claim 3:** *Five specific codons in `EP153R` (`9G, 14N, 23S, 98R, 153K`) and two in `EP402R` (`252E, 359P`) are under pervasive positive diversifying selection (`FEL p < 0.05`).* `[Fragile]` — Inferred using single-tree `FEL` without accounting for the intragenic recombination demonstrated in the same alignments.
