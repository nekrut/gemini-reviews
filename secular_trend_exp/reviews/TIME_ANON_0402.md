### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This study investigates cotranscriptional mRNA editing of the phosphoprotein (P) gene in human parainfluenza virus type 3 (HPIV3). The authors identify an active RNA editing site within a purine-rich motif (`5'-AAUUAAAAAAGGGGG-3'`, mRNA nucleotides 791–805) that resembles an HPIV3 consensus transcription termination signal and governs access to the internal D protein reading frame. Conversely, a second putative editing motif (`5'-AAUUGGAAAGGAAAGG-3'`, mRNA nucleotides 1121–1136) upstream of the conserved cysteine-rich V open reading frame was found not to undergo editing in HPIV3. Unlike the tightly regulated insertion of 1 or 2 non-templated G residues characteristic of many paramyxoviruses (such as SV5 or Sendai virus), HPIV3 editing at nt 791–805 was indiscriminate, inserting between 1 and 12 additional G residues. Furthermore, by comparing mRNAs synthesized *in vivo* (HPIV3-infected cells) and *in vitro* (purified viral nucleocapsid complexes transcribed with or without uninfected human lung carcinoma [HLC] cell lysates), the authors demonstrate that P-gene mRNA editing occurs independently of host soluble factors and is an intrinsic activity of viral nucleocapsid proteins. ### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT

#### STRENGTHS
- **Clean Biochemical Reconstitution of *In Vitro* Editing:** Comparing mRNAs generated *in vivo* with those transcribed *in vitro* by purified viral ribonucleoprotein (nucleocapsid) complexes in the presence or absence of uninfected HLC host cell lysates directly tests whether host factors (such as cellular actin, which is required for optimal HPIV3 transcription) modulate polymerase stuttering/editing.
- **Targeted Interrogation of Two Candidate Editing Motifs:** Explicitly testing both the D-cistron junction (nt 791–805) and the V-cistron junction (nt 1121–1136) clarifies a major puzzle in paramyxovirus biology: how HPIV3 retains a conserved V ORF in its genome yet fails to express it via standard V-site editing.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Truncated Archival Text (`[Archive Limitation]`):** Because the body text and figures are absent from the extracted text file, the exact number of sequenced cDNA clones or primer-extension products used to establish the distribution of +1 to +12 G insertions and the upper bound on non-editing at nt 1121–1136 cannot be audited.
- **Potential Reverse Transcriptase Slippage Confounding:** Indiscriminate insertion of 1–12 G residues within a homopolymeric run (`AAAAAAGGGGG`) must be carefully distinguished from AMV/M-MLV reverse transcriptase slippage during cDNA synthesis using genomic RNA or synthetic unedited transcript controls; the abstract does not state whether synthetic RNA template controls were run in parallel.
- **Unexplained Silence of the Conserved V Open Reading Frame:** While the absence of editing at nt 1121–1136 is reported, the evolutionary maintenance of an intact V ORF without transcriptional access at that site raises questions as to whether editing at nt 791–805 (+2 G frame) or ribosomal frameshifting could access V, or if V is accessed only under specific host conditions.
