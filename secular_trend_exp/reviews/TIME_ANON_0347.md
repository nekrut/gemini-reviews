### 1. Executive Summary
This study proposes that hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection activates host $\text{p38}\alpha$ MAPK independently of MKK3/6 by triggering an interaction between $\text{p38}\alpha$ and TAB1 (`MAP3K7IP1`), and that activated $\text{p38}\alpha$ directly binds the N-terminal 23 amino acids of the HCV core protein and phosphorylates it at eight residues (`R9, K12, R81, S99, R101, S106, R117, S145`) to promote HCV core oligomerization and virion assembly. The authors report that pharmacological inhibition of p38 with `SB203580` or treatment with a synthetic N-terminal core peptide (`CN-peptide`, residues 1–23) disrupts the $\text{p38}\alpha$–core interaction and inhibits HCV replication in `Huh7.5.1` cells and primary human hepatocytes (PHHs). Finally, they extend their claims to assert that SFTSV, HSV-1, and SARS-CoV-2 infections also activate p38 MAPK and are inhibited by `SB203580`.

While the manuscript presents a large volume of Western blots, co-immunoprecipitations, and viral titration assays, a forensic biochemical audit uncovers a fundamental, disqualifying error at the heart of the mechanism: five of the eight "phosphorylation sites" claimed to be catalyzed by the serine/threonine kinase $\text{p38}\alpha$ on the HCV core protein (`R9, K12, R81, R101, R117`) are **arginine and lysine residues**, which are not substrates of mammalian Ser/Thr MAP kinases and whose mutation to alanine (`8A`) destroys the basic charge cluster of the HCV core RNA-binding domain. In addition, the claims regarding SARS-CoV-2 are misrepresented (only a VSV-Spike pseudovirus entry assay was performed, without a VSV-G control or any p38 phosphorylation assay), and `SB203580` is a catalytic ATP-pocket inhibitor whose reported disruption of physical binding in bacterial GST/His pull-downs lacks a coherent biochemical explanation.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Biochemically Invalid Assignment of Arginine and Lysine Phosphorylation to $\text{p38}\alpha$ MAPK:** In the Results ("P38$\alpha$ phosphorylates the HCV core protein and promotes its oligomerization", line 141), the authors incubate *E. coli*-purified GST–HCV core with immunoprecipitated Flag-$\text{p38}\alpha$ in an in vitro kinase reaction and analyze the product by LC-MS/MS, stating: *"A total of eight HCV core protein phosphorylation sites were identified: **R9, K12, R81, S99, R101, S106, R117, and S145**, suggesting that p38$\alpha$ may phosphorylate the HCV core protein (Figure 6 B-C)."*
  1. $\text{p38}\alpha$ (`MAPK14`) is a strict proline-directed **serine/threonine kinase** (`S/T-P` consensus); it cannot catalyze $N$-phosphorylation of guanidinium (arginine: `R9, R81, R101, R117`) or $\varepsilon$-amino (lysine: `K12`) side chains! Moreover, standard acidic reversed-phase LC-MS/MS mobile phases ($0.1\%$ formic acid, $\text{pH} \sim 2$) rapidly hydrolyze acid-labile phosphoramidates ($\text{pArg}$, $\text{pLys}$), indicating that the authors either misread tryptic cleavage boundary annotations (`R`/`K`) in the mass spectrometry software output or allowed uncurated false-positive search artifacts.
  2. Even worse, to test the functional role of these "phosphorylation sites," the authors mutate all five basic residues (`R9A, K12A, R81A, R101A, R117A`) plus three serines (`S99A, S106A, S145A`) to alanine (`8A` mutant, Fig. 6D–I). The N-terminal domain of the HCV core protein (residues 1–117) is a highly basic RNA-binding and nuclear/nucleolar localization domain whose positive charge is essential for viral RNA packaging and capsid assembly. Neutralizing five positively charged arginine/lysine residues (`R9, K12, R81, R101, R117`) to alanine inevitably impairs core oligomerization and JFH1 viral replication through loss of positive charge and RNA binding, completely confounding the authors' conclusion that the `8A` defect proves $\text{p38}\alpha$-dependent phosphorylation.
- **Misrepresentation of VSV-Spike Pseudovirus Entry as Live SARS-CoV-2 Replication and p38 Activation:** In the Abstract (line 11), Introduction (line 22), and Discussion (line 166), the authors claim that *"severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection also activated p38 MAPK"* and *"Consistently, SFTSV, HSV-1 and SARS-CoV-2 infections could all induce the phosphorylation of p38 in host cells."* However, checking the Materials and Methods (line 32) and Results (line 155, Fig. 9K–L) reveals that **no live SARS-CoV-2 experiments and no SARS-CoV-2 p38 phosphorylation blots were ever performed**. The authors only transduced `HEK293T-hACE2` cells with a VSV-based luciferase pseudovirus bearing SARS-CoV-2 Spike (`SARS-CoV-2 S pseudovirions`) in the presence of `SB203580`! Furthermore, they omitted a VSV-G pseudovirus specificity control (so `SB203580` inhibition of VSV-luciferase reporter expression cannot be attributed to SARS-CoV-2 Spike entry), and this claim directly contradicts their own HCV mechanism where `SB203580` had **zero effect on viral attachment or entry** (Fig. 4H–J) and acted only at late virion assembly.
- **Unexplained Cell-Free Peptide Usage Without Cell-Penetrating Tag:** In Fig. 7H–N, a synthetic 23-mer N-terminal core peptide (`CN-peptide`) is added directly to the culture medium of `Huh7.5.1` cells and primary human hepatocytes (PHHs) to disrupt the *intracellular* cytoplasmic interaction between $\text{p38}\alpha$ and HCV core, yet the Methods never report whether `CN-peptide` was conjugated to a cell-penetrating peptide (e.g., TAT) or how its sequence was synthesized, purified, or dissolved.
- **Inappropriate Statistical Testing Across Multi-Group Experiments:** Methods line 106 states that all comparisons were tested exclusively using an "unpaired, two-tailed Student's $t$-test," despite virtually every figure (Figs. 2–9) containing 4- to 10-group dose responses, time courses, or mutant panels requiring one-way or two-way ANOVA with multiple-testing correction.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **[Moderately Supported]** HCV JFH1 infection in `Huh7.5.1` cells and PHHs increases $\text{p38}\alpha$ phosphorylation via TAB1 association without altering MKK3/6 phosphorylation, and genetic or pharmacological depletion of $\text{p38}\alpha$ activity reduces HCV infectious titers.
- **[Methodologically Flawed]** The claim that $\text{p38}\alpha$ phosphorylates the HCV core protein at `R9, K12, R81, S99, R101, S106, R117, and S145` to drive capsid oligomerization is biochemically invalid due to the impossible assignment of four arginines and one lysine as $\text{p38}\alpha$ Ser/Thr kinase substrates and the charge-neutralization confounding of the `8A` mutant.
- **[Overstated]** The claim that SARS-CoV-2 infection activates p38 MAPK and that `SB203580` inhibits SARS-CoV-2 replication is unsupported by the data, which tested only VSV-Spike pseudovirus luciferase transduction without measuring p38 activation or VSV backbone controls.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** siRNA and CRISPR sgRNA target sequences (`MAPK14`, `TAB1`) and antibody catalog numbers are provided in the Methods.
- **Deficiencies:** Raw LC-MS/MS spectra, peptide fragmentation tables, and PhosphoRS/PTM localization probabilities for the claimed `R9/K12/R81/R101/R117` phosphorylation sites are absent; the sequence, synthesis, and cell-permeabilization modifications of `CN-peptide` are omitted from the Methods; and clinical liver IHC/PBMC sample sizes, patient demographics, and ethics approval identifiers are completely missing from the Materials and Methods.
