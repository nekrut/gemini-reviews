### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This histopathological and molecular study profiles the expression and cellular localization of six angiogenic factors—*VEGF-A*, *VEGF-B*, *VEGF-C*, *VEGF-D*, *Angiopoietin-1* (*Ang-1*), and *Angiopoietin-2* (*Ang-2*)—across benign postmenopausal endometrium ($n=5$), atypical complex hyperplasia (ACH, $n=5$), and endometrioid endometrial carcinoma ($n=13$: 4 Grade 1/2, 9 Grade 3) using $^{33}\text{P}$-riboprobe in situ hybridization (ISH) paired with serial-section cytokeratin (MNF116) and macrophage (CD68) immunohistochemistry. In an independent snap-frozen cohort, the authors also perform endpoint RT-PCR for *VEGF-C* and TaqMan real-time qPCR for *VEGF-B* and *Ang-2* (benign $n=7$ or $4$, ACH $n=3$, carcinoma $n=17$). They find that *VEGF-A* mRNA is detected by ISH in 13/13 carcinomas (localizing to epithelial tumor cells, especially perinecrotic zones) vs. 0/5 benign and 1/5 ACH; *VEGF-B* is detected by ISH in 5/5 ACH cases and shows significantly lower mRNA abundance in endometrial carcinoma than benign endometrium by qPCR ($p = 0.04$); and *VEGF-D* and *Ang-2* transcripts localize specifically to $\text{CD68}^+$ tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) in high-grade carcinomas.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Internal Text-Table Inconsistency in Patient Age Range:** Line 29 states: *"The age range of the patients was 51–87 years (median 57 years)."* However, inspection of Table 1 reveals three carcinoma patients younger than 51 years: Patient 11 (age 41), Patient 14 (age 46), and Patient 13 (age 48)! Thus, the actual age range in Table 1 is **41–87 years** (51–87 years is only the range of the benign/ACH subset or a misread of Patient 1's age of 51).
- **Apparent Discrepancy Between ISH and qPCR for VEGF-B in Benign Endometrium:** By in situ hybridization (Table 2), *VEGF-B* is scored as `0/5` in benign postmenopausal endometrium vs. `5/5` in ACH and `7/13` in carcinoma (implying *increase* from benign to ACH/cancer). Yet by TaqMan qPCR on an unrelated snap-frozen cohort (Figure 4A), *VEGF-B* mRNA is reported as *highest* in benign postmenopausal endometrium ($n=7$) and significantly lower in carcinoma ($n=17$, $p = 0.04$). The authors do not reconcile why benign endometrium has the highest *VEGF-B* transcript level by qPCR yet `0/5` positivity by ISH (whereas ACH has lower *VEGF-B* by qPCR yet `5/5` positivity by ISH), which may reflect diffuse low-density expression across atrophic benign stroma vs. focal epithelial concentration in ACH, or normalization distortion due to differing $\beta$-actin cellularity between atrophic postmenopausal tissue and hypercellular tumors.
- **Small Sample Sizes and Unadjusted Multiple Testing:** The ISH groups ($n=5$ benign, $n=5$ ACH, $n=4$ G1/2, $n=9$ G3) and qPCR subgroups ($n=3$ ACH, $n=4$ benign for *Ang-2*) are very small, and Fisher's exact tests across 6 transcripts $\times$ 3 histological groups are unadjusted for multiple comparisons.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality / STRENGTHS
- **STRENGTHS:**
  - High spatial resolution via $^{33}\text{P}$ in situ hybridization with sense-strand negative controls on adjacent serial sections and positive tissue controls (human B-cell lymphoma for *VEGF-C*).
  - Valuable cellular localization discovery via serial CD68 vs. cytokeratin IHC demonstrating that *VEGF-D* and *Ang-2* mRNAs in poorly differentiated endometrial carcinomas are expressed predominantly by infiltrating $\text{CD68}^+$ tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) rather than carcinoma cells.
- **Claim Evaluation:**
  - *Claim that VEGF-A mRNA is upregulated in epithelial carcinoma cells (13/13) particularly near necrosis:* `[Well Supported]` — Clear ISH signal in 13/13 carcinomas vs. 0/5 benign postmenopausal controls.
  - *Claim that VEGF-D and Ang-2 localize to CD68+ tumor-associated macrophages in high-grade tumors:* `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated by serial-section ISH and IHC (Figure 3).
  - *Claim that loss of VEGF-B contributes to endometrial carcinoma by freeing VEGFR-1 for VEGF-A:* `[Fragile]` — Speculative mechanistic hypothesis based on a marginal qPCR difference ($p=0.04$) that contradicts the directional trend of the ISH table (`0/5` benign vs. `5/5` ACH and `7/13` cancer).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Patient-level histopathological metadata are transparently tabulated in Table 1, and primer/TaqMan probe sequences for *VEGF-B* and *VEGF-C* are provided in the Methods (though *Ang-2* primer sequences are cited as a personal gift without sequences).
