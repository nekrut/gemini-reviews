### 1. Executive Summary
This clinical and molecular cancer genetics study investigates a striking 26-member extended kindred (`MON1080`) featuring 10 early-onset invasive cancers—five cases of colorectal cancer (CRC, fulfilling Amsterdam Criteria I for HNPCC/Lynch syndrome) and five cases of female breast cancer (BC)—that initially appeared to suggest a single high-penetrance BC–CRC predisposition gene. By integrating microsatellite linkage and haplotyping (across 5 chromosome 13q markers flanking *BRCA2* and 2 intragenic tetranucleotide repeats in *MSH2*, including phase confirmation via chromosome 2 somatic cell hybrids), MLH1/MSH2 immunohistochemistry (IHC), 5-marker microsatellite instability (MSI) testing, *BRCA1/2* sequencing and SSCA, *MSH2* protein truncation testing (PTT), RT-PCR cDNA sequencing, duplex TaqMan real-time qPCR copy-number analysis (normalizing *MSH2* exon 8 to *CFTR* exon 24), and long-range genomic PCR breakpoint sequencing, the authors demonstrate that two independent germline truncating mutations segregate in the family:
1. A nonsense mutation in *BRCA2* exon 3 (`c.542G>T`, `p.L105X`), carried by 9 tested/obligate individuals, which accounts for all five female breast cancers (four of which are invasive lobular carcinomas) and one papillary serous ovarian cancer.
2. A 14.9 kb *AluY*–*AluY* genomic deletion encompassing *MSH2* exon 8 (with a 45 bp identical microhomology breakpoint between intron 7 and intron 8 *AluY* elements), carried by 8 tested/obligate individuals, which accounts for the CRCs, endometrial cancer, and an MSI-H/MSH2-deficient cerebral astrocytoma.

Two family members (`II:20` and his daughter `III:25`) are confirmed/obligate double heterozygotes for both truncating mutations (`BRCA2 p.L105X` and `MSH2 Δexon 8`), and their clinical phenotypes do not differ in age of onset or tumor spectrum from single-mutation carriers. This is a thorough, multi-modality molecular pedigree investigation that resolves a classic clinical genetics question with complete breakpoint characterization. Only minor editorial inconsistencies in pedigree individual numbering between text, tables, and figure legends require correction.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Comprehensive Multi-Assay Molecular Confirmation of Both Germline Mutations:** Rather than stopping at cDNA exon-skipping or PTT, the authors rule out splice-site point mutations by genomic sequencing, confirm germline hemizygosity of *MSH2* exon 8 via duplex TaqMan qPCR against *CFTR* exon 24, use intragenic polymorphic repeat heterozygosity (`TAAA-Rpt` in intron 7) to narrow the upstream breakpoint boundary, and amplify and sequence the exact 1.9 kb junction fragment across the 14.9 kb *AluY*–*AluY* recombination site (Fig. 3). Likewise, chromosome 2 somatic cell hybrids from individual `II:18` were used to physically verify haplotype phase.
- **Pedigree Individual ID Discrepancies Between Text, Table 1, and Figure 2 Legend:**
  1. In the Methods (lines 33, 38), Results (lines 114, 132), and Discussion (line 168), the patient with the MSI-H, MSH2-negative cerebral astrocytoma is consistently identified as **patient `II: 11`**, whereas in the caption of Figure 2A and 2B (line 117) the exact same astrocytoma is attributed to **patient `II: 20`** ("Microsatellite instability at various loci in astrocytoma of patient II: 20... Immunohistochemical staining for MSH2 and MLH1 in the astrocytoma of patient II: 20").
  2. Table 1 is truncated at individual `III: 15`, omitting the rows for the right-hand branch of the pedigree (`II: 16` through `III: 25`, including the probands `III: 22` and `III: 25` and `II: 18`/`II: 20` who are explicitly referenced in Results lines 139, 157, and 159 as appearing in "Table 1").
  3. In Methods line 68 and Figure 3, the `TAAA` tetranucleotide repeat is correctly placed in *MSH2* **intron 7** (`TTTA: intron 2; TAAA: intron 7`), whereas Results line 157 refers to `TAAA-Repeat (intron 8)`. Similarly, Figure 3 legend states there are "18 Alu family sequences in intron 8", whereas Discussion line 170 states "19 Alu family sequences in intron 8."

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **[Well Supported]** Clustering of early-onset breast cancer and colorectal cancer in kindred `MON1080` is caused by independent segregation of `BRCA2 c.542G>T (p.L105X)` and a 14.9 kb *AluY*-mediated deletion of `MSH2` exon 8, rather than a single pleiotropic BC–CRC locus or cross-predisposition.
- **[Well Supported]** The 14.9 kb deletion of *MSH2* exon 8 arose via unequal homologous recombination between two *AluY* repeats sharing 83% overall sequence identity and an identical 45 bp breakpoint core.
- **[Well Supported]** Double heterozygosity for truncating mutations in *BRCA2* and *MSH2* in two individuals (`II:20` and `III:25`) is viable and does not produce a measurably accelerated or synergistic phenotype beyond the independent risks of each syndrome.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Complete primer and TaqMan probe sequences for *MSH2* exon 8, *CFTR* exon 24, long-range *MSH2* genomic breakpoint PCR (`MSH2 IVS7-5K-F`, `MSH2 IVS8-10K-R`, `TAAA-Rpt`, `H2 EX8 ALUYF`, `MSH2 IVS8 10318R`), and *BRCA2* exon 3 SSCA are printed directly in the Methods, and the exact 45 bp *AluY* breakpoint alignment is shown in Figure 3.
- **Deficiencies:** Truncation of the lower half of Table 1 (`II:16`–`III:25`) and the `II:11` vs. `II:20` typo in the Figure 2 legend should be corrected.
