### 1. Executive Summary
This study provides an integrative evolutionary genetic and functional dissection of the cis-regulatory promoter region of the human opioid neuropeptide precursor gene prodynorphin (*PDYN*). By cloning and sequencing 3 kb of *PDYN* regulatory DNA across 74 experimentally phased human chromosomes and 32 chromosomes from seven non-human primate species, the authors demonstrate that a 68-bp regulatory element 1,250 bp upstream of the transcription start site accumulated five fixed human-specific nucleotide substitutions prior to tandem duplication in humans. Phylogenetic likelihood ratio tests (molecular clock and relative ratio tests in HyPhy) confirm a ~10-fold human-lineage-specific rate acceleration ($p = 0.001\text{--}0.005$), while flanking human polymorphism shows a marked excess of high-frequency derived alleles (Fay and Wu's $H = -8.13, p = 0.004$). Crucially, the authors functionally validate the evolutionary hypothesis using human–chimpanzee chimeric luciferase reporter constructs in human neuroblastoma (SH-SY5Y) and non-neural (JAR) cells, showing that the human 68-bp element specifically increases $\text{Ca}^{2+}$/caffeine-induced transcriptional activation by ~15–20% ($p = 0.002$). Finally, by genotyping the 68-bp tandem repeat (1–4 copies) and an upstream $\text{(CA)}_{13-27}$ microsatellite across six global populations and integrating 1.2M Perlegen genome-wide SNPs and 377 microsatellites, they show elevated inter-population differentiation ($F_{\text{ST}}$) and reduced within-population microsatellite diversity ($\ln\text{RV}$ and $\ln\text{RH}$) consistent with recent geographically heterogeneous selection across the *PDYN–STK35* locus.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Phylogenetic Rate Tests & Non-Reciprocal Exchange Controls:** The authors carefully test whether the 5 fixed human substitutions in the 68-bp element (expected $\le 0.49$ under local promoter, chromosomal divergence, or germline mutation rate baselines; Poisson $p < 0.0001$) could arise from local hypermutability or biased gene conversion among tandem repeats. Using HyPhy molecular clock and relative ratio tests across 8 primate species, they show that the 68-bp element evolves neutrally across non-human primates ($p = 0.657$) and accelerates exclusively on the human branch ($A = 10.1$). Their mathematical derivation in Materials and Methods (lines 143–149) rigorously demonstrates why unbiased or even maximally biased gene conversion across $n \le 4$ tandem repeats cannot explain a 10.1-fold substitution acceleration under neutrality.
- **Population Genetic Sampling & Ascertainment Care:** Because the initial sequencing panel over-sampled rare 1- and 4-repeat alleles in the Austrian cohort, the authors properly resampled 74 haplotypes weighted by true population repeat frequencies before computing Fay and Wu's $H$ ($-8.13, p = 0.004$). In the Perlegen genome-wide $F_{\text{ST}}$ analysis of chromosome 20p, they restricted to Scheme A array-resequenced SNPs and conditioned $F_{\text{ST}}$ percentile contours on expected global heterozygosity ($2\bar{p}(1-\bar{p})$) (Figure 3G), avoiding the classic statistical pitfall where raw $F_{\text{ST}}$ is confounded by allele frequency spectrum skew. Similarly, in the microsatellite $\ln\text{RV}$ and $\ln\text{RH}$ analyses against 377 Rosenberg/Kayser autosomal microsatellites, they explicitly test for normality and positive skew (the expected signature of European ascertainment bias) and confirm significance against the conservative right tail.
- **Experimental Design of Chimeric Reporter Assays:** The 4-construct factorial design (human, chimpanzee, chimp + human 68-bp element, chimp + human exon-1 DRE site) tested with and without 10 mM caffeine ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$ release to derepress DREAM binding) in neural SH-SY5Y vs. non-neural JAR cells (5–7 independent transfections normalized to Renilla-TK) cleanly isolates the main effect of the 68-bp element ($p = 0.011$ in 3-way ANOVA; $p = 0.002$ under $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ stimulation). One minor limitation is that the reporter assays were conducted only in human cell lines (since chimpanzee neural cell lines were unavailable), leaving open the theoretical possibility of trans-acting species differences, though the JAR control supports neural specificity.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Positive natural selection drove the accelerated fixation of five substitutions in the 68-bp cis-regulatory element of the human PDYN promoter prior to the human diaspora.* `[Well Supported]` — Supported by concordant phylogenetic relative ratio tests, absence of CpG hypermutability, negative Fay and Wu's $H$ across all repeat classes, and tight purifying selection on the mature opioid peptide coding sequences ($p = 0.004$).
- **Claim 2:** *The human-specific substitutions in the 68-bp element increase inducible PDYN transcription upon intracellular calcium release.* `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated via site-swapped chimpanzee–human chimeric promoter-luciferase constructs in SH-SY5Y cells (Figure 2B).
- **Claim 3:** *Subsequent to the global human diaspora, recent positive selection favored different PDYN cis-regulatory repeat alleles in different geographical populations.* `[Moderately Supported]` — The combination of extreme $F_{\text{ST}}$ at the 68-bp repeat and flanking 170-kb Perlegen SNPs plus extreme negative $\ln\text{RH}/\ln\text{RV}$ at the linked CA microsatellite is compelling, and the authors appropriately add the caveat (lines 86–90, 108) that the 170-kb $F_{\text{ST}}$ peak also spans the divergently transcribed *STK35* gene.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- All newly generated primate and phased human *PDYN* sequences are deposited in GenBank under accession numbers `AY902542–AY902679`.
- Full haplotype matrices and 377-locus microsatellite summary statistics ($\ln\text{RV}$, $\ln\text{RH}$, $H_e$, $V$) are provided in Tables S1 and S2, and the mathematical parameterization of the selection coefficient $s$ as a function of $f_a$ and $f_d$ is clearly documented (though Tables 1–5 in the main text markdown were rendered as headers without inline cell bodies due to XML table formatting).
