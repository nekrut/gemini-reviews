### 1. Executive Summary
This primary cell and molecular biology study reports the discovery, molecular cloning, tissue-specific alternative splicing characterization, biochemical subcellular fractionation, and high-resolution morphological localization of a novel mammalian 80–84 kDa dynamin-like GTPase, termed DLP1 (dynamin-like protein 1, encoded by a 755-amino-acid ORF, later also known in the field as DRP1/*DNM1L*). Starting from an 80 kDa band co-immunoprecipitated from rat liver homogenates with a pan-dynamin GTPase-domain antibody (`MC12`), the authors microsequenced tryptic peptides, designed degenerate RT-PCR primers, screened rat brain and liver $\lambda$-ZAPII cDNA libraries, raised three affinity-purified rabbit anti-peptide antibodies (`DLP-N`, `DLP-MID`, `DLP-C`), and conducted Northern/Western blotting, two orthogonal sucrose/CsCl density gradient liver fractionations (pelleting and upward flotation), double-label immunofluorescence microscopy (against Dynamin, Clathrin, FITC-dextran, FITC-transferrin, $\alpha$-tubulin, Calnexin, and Rab1), GFP-DLP1 expression, and quantitative immunogold electron microscopy on ultrathin cryosections of rat liver. They show that DLP1 shares higher homology with yeast Dnm1p (42%) and Vps1p (40%) than with mammalian Dynamin 1 (36%), lacks the C-terminal proline-rich domain of conventional dynamins, undergoes tissue-specific alternative splicing at two cassettes (producing an 83.9 kDa brain isoform and a 78.6 kDa non-neuronal isoform), does not colocalize with endocytic markers or clathrin-coated pits, and instead associates with light microsomal vesicles and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) tubules aligned along microtubules. The biochemical and cell-biological execution is thorough and well controlled.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Orthogonal Antibody & Tagging Validation:** To rule out antibody cross-reactivity or fixation artifacts in immunofluorescence, the authors raised three independent affinity-purified rabbit polyclonal antibodies targeting distinct regions of DLP1 (`DLP-N` aa 266–294, `DLP-MID` aa 523–545, `DLP-C` aa 716–738) and corroborated endogenous antibody staining with stably expressed GFP-DLP1 fusion protein following pre-fixation digitonin permeabilization (0.01% digitonin for 30 s in microtubule-stabilizing buffer to wash out excess cytosolic GFP-DLP1).
- **Rigorous Biochemical Subcellular Fractionation Controls:** A common pitfall in differential centrifugation is that large oligomeric GTPase assemblies pellet alongside microsomal membranes as protein aggregates rather than true membrane-bound vesicles. The authors explicitly tested this (lines 98–99, Figure 6d) by performing an upward sucrose step-gradient flotation assay (loading total microsomes in 1.22 M sucrose under 1.15/0.86/0.25 M sucrose steps at $82,500g$ for 3 h) as well as CsCl rough/smooth microsome separation, proving that membrane-bound DLP1 floats upward with light vesicles and co-fractionates with rough ER (RM) alongside secretory markers (Sec23p, Rab1, $\gamma$-adaptin, Rab8) while remaining distinct from conventional dynamin (plasma membrane and heavy Golgi).
- **Quantitative Immunogold Electron Microscopy:** Rather than relying solely on representative EM micrographs, the authors quantified 10-nm immunogold labeling density (gold particles per $\mu\text{m}^2$ of organelle profile area measured on a Zeiss MOP-3 digitizer, Table I and Figure 11) on perfusion-fixed rat liver cryosections, demonstrating selective enrichment on tubular/reticular ER membranes and background-level labeling on Golgi stacks.
- **Absence of Functional Perturbation Assays:** As the authors explicitly acknowledge in the Discussion (line 134), this initial characterization is structural, biochemical, and morphological; dominant-negative GTPase mutants (e.g., K38A) or antibody microinjection assays to test functional effects on ER morphology, mitochondrial/peroxisomal division, or ER-to-Golgi transport were deferred to follow-up studies.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim: DLP1 is a novel, ubiquitously expressed mammalian dynamin-related GTPase (42% homologous to yeast Dnm1p) lacking a proline-rich tail and undergoing tissue-specific alternative splicing in brain vs. non-neuronal tissues** — `[Well Supported]`. Peptide microsequencing, cDNA cloning, Northern blots (4.6 kb ubiquitous transcript plus 3.0 kb testis transcript), multi-tissue Western blots, and RT-PCR across both alternative splice cassettes (201/180/162 bp N-terminal and 351/318/240 bp C-terminal) provide definitive proof.
- **Claim: DLP1 is excluded from clathrin-coated endocytic vesicles and instead associates with ER tubules and cytoplasmic vesicles aligned along microtubules** — `[Well Supported]`. The convergence of two liver fractionation protocols, lack of colocalization with endocytosed FITC-transferrin/dextran, hudy-1 (dynamin), or X22 (clathrin heavy chain), striking coalignment with calnexin- and Rab1-positive ER tubules, and quantitative immunogold EM strongly supports ER/cytoplasmic tubule association.
- **Claim: DLP1 may act as an ER equivalent of plasma membrane dynamin to liberate nascent secretory vesicles from ER cisternae** — `[Moderately Supported]`. Framed appropriately as a working hypothesis in the Discussion (line 134) based on the alternating punctate alignment of GFP-DLP1 and Rab1 along ER tubules (though subsequent work in the field revealed that ER-associated DLP1/DRP1 oligomers also mediate ER-coupled mitochondrial and peroxisomal tubulation/fission).
