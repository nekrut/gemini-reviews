### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates why the surface mutation H69E in HIV-1 protease (PR) severely blocks precursor autoprocessing ($>20$-fold reduction in Gag p24/total Gag processing efficiency) in a "pseudo wild-type" ($\text{wt}_{\text{pse}}$) PR backbone engineered for NMR/biochemical studies (which carries six substitutions relative to laboratory strain NL4-3: Q7K, L33I, N37S, L63I, C67A, and C95A), whereas the same H69E substitution in the native NL4-3 PR backbone causes only a moderate ($\sim 4$-fold) reduction. By systematically reverting the six differing positions individually and in combination within the pNL-PR proviral construct expressed in HEK 293T cells, the authors demonstrate that reverting A95 to the native cysteine (C95) is the primary determinant restoring Gag processing efficiency in the H69E background, while I63L and A67C provide partial, non-additive rescue. They further show that replacing H69 with aspartate (H69D, whose carboxyl side chain is one methylene group shorter than glutamate) completely abolishes autoprocessing even in the permissive NL4-3 backbone (resembling the catalytic dead D25N control), whereas H69Q behaves like wild-type and H69N matches H69E ($\text{wt} \cong \text{H69Q} > \text{H69N}, \text{H69E} \gg \text{H69D}$). Finally, a site-directed mutagenesis screen of 12 charge/polar substitutions across five surface residues (E21, D30/D31, E34, E35, F99) in an *E. coli* GST-TFR-PR-FLAG autoprocessing assay fails to identify a direct electrostatic suppressor of H69E.

This is a focused, well-controlled molecular virology study. Quantitative infrared Western blotting (Odyssey dual-laser system) across three independent biological replicates is coupled with appropriate catalytic-dead (D25N), wild-type (NL4-3 and $\text{wt}_{\text{pse}}$), and loading (GAPDH) controls, and the authors candidly report negative results from their charge-complementation screen without overclaiming. Minor weaknesses include an internal inconsistency in residue numbering between D30 and D31, lack of formal statistical hypothesis tests on the densitometric ratios, and reliance on steady-state VLP p24/total Gag ratios as an indirect proxy for N-terminal precursor autoprocessing rates in the mammalian cell experiments.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Quantitative Immunoblotting and Controls:** Unlike standard film-based ECL densitometry, the authors use IR800-conjugated secondary antibodies imaged on an Odyssey infrared scanner (unsaturated pixel volumes quantified in TotalLab) across $n = 3$ independent experiments with mean $\pm$ SD bars (Figures 2B and 3B). Inclusion of the active-site mutant D25N as a zero-processing baseline and parallel analysis of both cell lysates and pelleted virus-like particles (VLPs) confirm that the mutations do not impair Gag expression or particle budding.
* **Indirect Proxy for Autoprocessing vs. Mature Enzyme Specific Activity:** In the HEK 293T VLP assays, the metric $\text{p24} / (\text{p24} + \text{p25} + \text{p55})$ measures cumulative Gag substrate cleavage rather than direct N-terminal TFR-PR precursor self-cleavage. Although the authors support this in Figure 3A (bottom panel) by probing VLP-associated mature PR directly with polyclonal anti-PR serum (showing mature PR levels track p24 ratios), steady-state mature PR abundance in VLPs is a composite of (a) the rate of precursor autoprocessing and (b) the rate of mature PR self-degradation (autoproteolysis). Notably, three of the six $\text{wt}_{\text{pse}}$ mutations (Q7K, L33I, L63I) were specifically introduced to *block* mature PR autoproteolysis—which the authors themselves note explains why $\text{wt}_{\text{pse}}$ VLPs accumulate *more* mature PR band intensity than wild-type NL4-3 VLPs (line 53). Thus, reverting I63L or A95C could theoretically alter both precursor folding/dimerization and mature enzyme stability/catalytic turnover.
* **Absence of Formal Statistical Tests on Bar Graphs:** While Figures 2B and 3B report means and standard deviations from $n = 3$ independent transfections, no formal statistical comparisons (e.g., one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's or Tukey's post-hoc test comparing single vs. double revertants such as A95C vs. C67/C95) are provided to formally test the claim of non-additivity/lack of synergy (line 43).
* **Inconsistency Between D30 and D31 Numbering:** In the Abstract (line 9) and Methods (line 76), the mutated acidic surface residues are listed as "E21, D30, E34, E35, and F99" (or "D21, D30, E34, E35 and F99" in line 76), whereas in the Results (line 58) the constructs are listed as "E21K, E21Q, D31K, D31N...". In HIV-1 protease, position 30 is an aspartate (D30) at the active-site cleft/substrate-binding pocket, whereas position 31 is a threonine (T31). Calling the mutants D31K/D31N in the Results text and D21 instead of E21 in the Methods text is a numbering/typographical error that should be corrected.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Context-Dependence of H69E Rescue by C95 (and L63/C67) [Well Supported]:** The combinatoric reversion panel in Figure 2 cleanly isolates A95C as the major contributor restoring Gag processing in the $\text{wt}_{\text{pse}}$ H69E background, with smaller effects from I63L and A67C and negligible effects from Q7K or L33I/N37S. Structurally, C95 resides at the $\beta$-sheet dimer interface near the C-terminus (residues 95–99) directly adjacent to the surface loop containing H69 and C67, providing a coherent biophysical rationale for how C95/C67 modulate precursor dimerization geometry.
* **Severity Hierarchy at Position 69 ($\text{wt} \cong \text{H69Q} > \text{H69N}, \text{H69E} \gg \text{H69D}$) [Well Supported]:** The side-by-side comparison in the NL4-3 backbone (Figure 3) convincingly demonstrates that a negatively charged carboxylate at position 69—especially the shorter side chain of Asp (H69D)—is far more disruptive than the neutral amide isosteres (H69Q, H69N), and that H69D overcomes the compensatory effect of C95/C67/L63.
* **Discussion of Redox Regulation and Negative Suppressor Screen [Well Supported]:** The authors appropriately frame their discussion of cysteine oxidation/glutathionylation and precursor folding as working hypotheses, and transparently report that their 12-mutant surface charge screen failed to rescue H69E.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Experimental Detail:** Transfection protocols, centrifugation parameters for VLP purification ($20,800 \times g$ for 3 h), NIH AIDS Reagent Program catalog numbers (anti-p24 Cat# 3537, anti-PR Cat# 4105), IPTG induction conditions ($40\ \mu\text{M}$ at $30^\circ\text{C}$ for 4 h), and densitometric formulas are fully specified and reproducible.
* **Minor Omissions:** Only a subset of the 12 *E. coli* surface charge mutants are displayed in Figure 4B ("not all the mutants are shown here", line 58), and exact mutagenic primer sequences are stated as "available upon request" (line 76).
