### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This manuscript reports the isolation and functional characterization of *FPR3*, a novel *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* gene encoding a 413-amino-acid nuclear/nucleolar peptidylprolyl *cis-trans* isomerase (PPIase) of the FK506-binding protein (FKBP) family. The authors show that the C-terminal 111 residues share $>40\%$ sequence identity with FKBPs and that both full-length recombinant Fpr3 and its isolated C-terminal domain display catalytic PPIase activity in vitro. Through a comprehensive series of single (*fpr3$\Delta$*) and triple (*fpr1 fpr2 fpr3*) null mutants, multicopy expression, and *GAL1*-driven domain-specific overexpression experiments in wild-type and *fpr1$\Delta$* (rapamycin-resistant) backgrounds, the authors dissect domain-specific functions and toxicities. Furthermore, indirect immunofluorescence using polyclonal antibodies against either the N- or C-terminal domain localizes Fpr3 exclusively to the nucleolus. Although the full text is only available as an unextracted PDF (4.1 MB), the abstract provides a remarkably dense and logically rigorous account of biochemical, genetic, and cell-biological experiments with internal domain-swap/truncation controls.

### STRENGTHS
- **Rigorous Genetic Epistasis and Domain Dissection:** The study systematically tests null alleles (*fpr3$\Delta$*), triple immunophilin knockouts (*fpr1 fpr2 fpr3*), and *GAL1*-driven overexpression of full-length Fpr3, its acidic/basic N-terminal nucleolar-like domain, and its C-terminal FKBP catalytic domain across wild-type and *fpr1$\Delta$* strains.
- **Clever Chemical-Genetic Rescue Control:** Demonstrating that rapamycin reverses the growth toxicity of full-length Fpr3 overexpression in *fpr1$\Delta$* cells—whereas toxicity caused by overexpressing the N-terminal domain alone (which lacks the rapamycin-binding C-terminal FKBP domain) is *not* reversed by rapamycin—provides a compelling internal control proving that drug binding to the C-terminal FKBP domain titrates or alters the toxic N-terminal interaction.
- **Orthogonal Localization Validation:** Nucleolar localization is verified by indirect immunofluorescence using independent polyclonal antibodies raised against two non-overlapping regions (the N-terminal domain and the C-terminal domain) expressed in *E. coli*.

### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Archival Text Completeness Caveat:** The main text of the article is contained in an unextracted PDF ("The Full Text of this article is available as a PDF (4.1 MB)"), leaving only the detailed abstract and 88 references in the text file. Thus, enzymatic kinetic constants ($k_{\text{cat}}/K_m$ in the chymotrypsin-coupled PPIase spectrophotometric assay), plasmid copy numbers, and exact growth curve/spot dilution quantifications cannot be audited directly.
- **Overexpression Artifact Interpretation:** The rapamycin sensitivity restoration and growth inhibition phenotypes rely on strong galactose-inducible (*GAL1*) overexpression. Because *GAL1* drives massive supraphysiological protein accumulation, the observed N-terminal toxicity and C-terminal rapamycin-TOR complex formation in *fpr1$\Delta$* cells represent synthetic dosage interactions rather than endogenous physiological functions of Fpr3 (consistent with the lack of phenotype in *fpr3$\Delta$* or multicopy native-promoter *FPR3* strains).
- **Antibody Specificity Control:** While using two separate polyclonal antisera (anti-N-terminus and anti-C-terminus) is strong, confirming complete loss of nucleolar immunofluorescence staining in the *fpr3$\Delta$* null mutant is essential to rule out cross-reactivity with abundant acidic/basic nucleolar proteins like Nsr1p or Nop1p.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Full-length Fpr3 and its isolated C-terminal domain possess PPIase enzymatic activity when purified from *E. coli*. `[Well Supported]` — Standard spectrophotometric proline rotamase assay (Kofron et al. / Fischer et al. methodology cited).
- **Claim 2:** *FPR3* is non-essential, and even *fpr1 fpr2 fpr3* triple mutants are viable with unaltered baseline sensitivity to FK506 or rapamycin. `[Well Supported]` — Classic yeast one-step gene disruption and tetrad/viability analysis.
- **Claim 3:** Overexpression of the C-terminal FKBP domain (but not full-length Fpr3) restores rapamycin sensitivity to *fpr1$\Delta$* mutants, whereas overexpression of full-length Fpr3 (or its N-terminal domain) inhibits growth, with full-length toxicity rescued by rapamycin in *fpr1$\Delta$* cells. `[Well Supported]` — Internally consistent chemical-genetic domain analysis.
- **Claim 4:** Fpr3 localizes exclusively to the yeast nucleolus. `[Well Supported]` — Supported by sequence architecture (alternating acidic/basic stretches homologous to Nsr1/nucleolin) and dual-domain polyclonal immunofluorescence.
