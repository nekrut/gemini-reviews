### 1. Executive Summary
This hybrid bioinformatics and experimental plant genomics study characterizes the polygalacturonase (*PG*) gene family in cabbage (*Brassica oleracea* var. *capitata*), identifying 99 *BoPG* genes containing the glycosyl hydrolase family 28 (GH28) domain (`PF00295`). The authors classify the 99 *BoPG* genes into seven phylogenetic clades (A–G, finding complete loss of clade D in *B. oleracea* and *Arabidopsis thaliana* compared to rice), map physical chromosome locations and syntenic retention across the three whole-genome triplication (WGT) subgenomes (`LF`, `MF1`, `MF2`), analyze exon-intron structures, conserved protein motifs (MEME), and promoter *cis*-acting elements (`PlantCare`), calculate $K_a/K_s$ ratios for 21 terminal paralog pairs, profile transcript abundance across five tissues (roots, stems, leaves, floral buds, and siliques) via qRT-PCR for all 99 genes, and verify cell-wall subcellular localization for four GFP-tagged proteins (`BoPG03`, `BoPG34`, `BoPG79`, `BoPG89`) via transient expression and sucrose plasmolysis in onion epidermal cells. While the wet-lab expression profiling and plasmolysis localization add empirical value beyond a purely in silico gene-family catalog, the manuscript suffers from an arithmetic error in calculating subgenome retention rates, a structural impossibility in reported intron phase counts, and an assay flaw in which five pairs of highly similar paralogs were assayed using identical qRT-PCR primer pairs while being analyzed as distinct expression profiles.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Arithmetic Error in Subgenome Retention Rate Calculation (Section 2.2):** In Section 2.2 (lines 43–44), the authors report that 33, 23, and 20 *BoPG* genes were retained in the `LF` (least fractionated), `MF1` (moderately fractionated), and `MF2` (most fractionated) subgenomes, respectively, and state that `LF`, `MF1`, and `MF2` retained $16.2\%$, $11.3\%$, and $9.8\%$ of the *PG* genes found in *A. thaliana* ($n=68$). However, relative to the 68 *AtPG* genes in *A. thaliana*, the true subgenome retention rates are $33/68 = 48.5\%$ (`LF`), $23/68 = 33.8\%$ (`MF1`), and $20/68 = 29.4\%$ (`MF2`). The authors erroneously divided each subgenome's count by $68 \times 3 = 204$ ($33/204 = 16.18\%$, $23/204 = 11.27\%$, $20/204 = 9.80\%$), conflating the per-subgenome retention rate with each subgenome's share of the theoretical triplicated ancestral gene pool.
- **Structural Contradiction in Exon-Intron Phase Reporting (Section 2.1):** In Section 2.1 (line 34), the authors state: `"Fifteen members in clade C had 5 exons and a conserved intron phase (0, 2, and 0)"`. A coding sequence composed of 5 exons is interrupted by exactly **4** introns, not 3; reporting only three intron phases (`0, 2, and 0`) for a 5-exon gene is mathematically impossible and indicates a mismatch between the exon count and intron-phase extraction script.
- **Non-Specific Shared qRT-PCR Primers for Paralogous Gene Pairs (Section 4.3 & Table 1):** In Section 4.3 (line 119), the authors disclose that for five pairs of highly similar *BoPG* genes lacking divergent regions suitable for specific primer design, the exact same primer pair was used to represent both genes in the qRT-PCR heatmap and tissue-specificity analyses. Because a shared primer pair amplifies the pooled cDNA of both paralogs indiscriminately, it is impossible to determine how much each individual gene contributes to the signal, invalidating the individual tissue-expression assignments (Figs. 4–6) and paralog expression-divergence classifications (Categories I and II in Table 1) for any pairs sharing primers.
- **Phylogenetic & Selection Inference:** The species-level tree (Fig. 1, 267 sequences across 5 land plants) was built using Neighbor-Joining (NJ) with `p-distance` and pairwise deletion rather than Maximum Likelihood under a fitted amino-acid substitution model, though the intraspecific *BoPG* tree (Fig. 2) used ML with `WAG` in `MEGA 6.0`. In Table 1, $K_a/K_s$ values are reported without $p$-values (e.g., Fisher's exact test or likelihood ratio test against $K_a/K_s = 1$).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** The *B. oleracea* genome contains 99 *BoPG* genes distributed across clades A, B, C, E, F, and G (with complete absence of clade D), shaped by WGT and tandem duplication ($39.4\%$ in tandem clusters). `[Well Supported]` — Supported by HMM (`PF00295`) searches, synteny mapping on `Bolbase`, and chromosomal clustering criteria ($\le 5$ intervening ORFs).
- **Claim 2:** BoPG03, BoPG34, BoPG79, and BoPG89 localize to the plant cell wall rather than the plasma membrane. `[Well Supported]` — Directly verified in Figure 9 via transient GFP fusion expression in onion epidermal cells subjected to $300\text{ g/L}$ sucrose plasmolysis, showing GFP retention in the apoplastic cell wall as the protoplast retracts.
- **Claim 3:** Paralogous *BoPG* pairs fall into four distinct functional divergence categories based on independent qRT-PCR expression profiles and promoter *cis*-element similarity. `[Fragile]` — Compromised for the paralog pairs assayed with shared qRT-PCR primers, and weakened by correlating computational `PlantCare` motif counts with drought/ABA/auxin responsiveness without experimental hormone treatments.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Database & Primer Documentation:** Gene identifiers (`Bol` numbers from `Bolbase`), chromosomal coordinates, primer tables (Supplementary Table S7), and qRT-PCR thermal cycling conditions (`Bio-Rad CFX96`, `Takara SYBR Premix Ex Taq II`, three biological $\times$ three technical replicates normalized to *actin*) are thoroughly reported. The arithmetic error in subgenome retention percentages and the 5-exon/3-intron-phase discrepancy must be corrected.
