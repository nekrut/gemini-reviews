### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This manuscript dissects how radial spoke protein 3 (RSP3, 516 amino acids)—the pivotal polypeptide mutated in the *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii* paralyzed-flagella mutant *pf14*, whose absence causes failure to assemble all 17 radial spoke polypeptides onto the axoneme—attaches the radial spoke to the outer doublet A-microtubule. Combining an in vitro axoneme-binding assay using in vitro translated RSP3 with in vivo nuclear transformation rescue of *pf14* cells, the authors demonstrate that RSP3 binds specifically and saturably to spokeless *pf14* axonemes, but *not* to wild-type axonemes (where radial spoke docking sites are already occupied) or to microtubules polymerized from purified chick brain tubulin (proving that RSP3 recognizes a specific axonemal docking factor rather than bare tubulin). Deletion and chimeric fusion mapping localize the sole axoneme-binding domain of RSP3 to N-terminal residues 1–85: deleting residues 1–85 abolishes binding, whereas fusing residues 1–85 (or 42–85) to an unrelated passenger protein confers complete (or partial) *pf14* axoneme-binding activity. Confirming these biochemical results in vivo, nuclear transformation of *pf14* cells with mutagenized *RSP3* genes shows that residues 18–87 are essential for function, whereas the C-terminal 140 amino acids can be deleted with minimal effect on radial spoke assembly or flagellar motility. Although the full text is in an unextracted PDF (1.7 MB), the experimental design in the abstract is a textbook example of rigorous structure-function biology.

### STRENGTHS
- **Exquisite Specificity Controls in the In Vitro Reconstitution Assay:** Demonstrating that in vitro synthesized RSP3 binds to spokeless *pf14* axonemes, fails to bind wild-type axonemes (occupied sites), and fails to bind purified brain microtubules (ruling out generic electrostatic MAP-like tubulin binding) proves site-specific docking to an unoccupied axonemal receptor on the A-tubule.
- **Necessary-and-Sufficient Domain Mapping (Deletion + Sufficiency Chimeras):** Showing both loss of binding upon deleting residues 1–85 *and* gain of binding upon fusing residues 1–85 (or 42–85) to an unrelated reporter protein satisfies both necessity and sufficiency criteria for defining a modular targeting domain.
- **In Vitro to In Vivo Concordance:** Validating the in vitro domain map by transforming *pf14* mutant *Chlamydomonas* cells with mutated *RSP3* constructs in vivo (confirming requirement for residues 18–87 and dispensability of the C-terminal 140 residues for spoke assembly and swimming motility) bridges biochemistry and organelle physiology.
