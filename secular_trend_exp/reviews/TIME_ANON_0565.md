### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This landmark synthetic virology and experimental pathogenesis study investigates the newly discovered, previously unculturable bat influenza viruses `A/little yellow-shouldered bat/Guatemala/164/2009 (H17N10)` (`Bat09`) and `A/flat-faced bat/Peru/033/2010 (H18N11)` (`Bat10`). Using enzymatic whole-genome oligonucleotide synthesis and plasmid-based reverse genetics (`GenBank KM203345–KM203356`), the authors demonstrate that: (1) transfecting all 8 wild-type `Bat09` segments into human 293T cells generates abundant budding spherical virions by electron microscopy (Figure 1A), but these particles fail to productively infect embryonated chicken eggs or any tested mammalian, avian, or bat cell line (`MDCK, Mv1-Lu, ST, Vero, A549, Calu-3, Tb1Lu`); (2) simple `6+2` reassortment replacing `Bat09` HA and NA with `A/Puerto Rico/8/1934 (H1N1)` (`PR8`) HA and NA fails due to packaging signal incompatibility, whereas flanking the coding regions of `PR8` (`H1N1`) or `A/swine/Texas/4199-2/1998 (H3N2)` (`TX98`) HA and NA with `Bat09` or `Bat10` $3'$ and $5'$ cis-acting packaging signals (`Bat09:mH1mN1`, `Bat09:mH1mN1ss`, `Bat09:mH3mN2`, `Bat10:mH1mN1ss`) rescues high-titer replicative viruses in MDCK cells, Calu-3 cells, eggs, and BALB/c mice; (3) virulence in mice is governed both by the surface glycoproteins (`Bat09:mH1mN1` causes 75% lethality similar to mouse-adapted `PR8`, whereas `Bat09:mH3mN2` is non-lethal like `TX98`) and by classical mammalian adaptation residues in `Bat09 PB2` (`N701D` abolishes mortality; `E158G` increases mortality to 100%; native `PB2-627S` confers intermediate polymerase activity between avian `627E` and mammalian `627K` at 33°C, 37°C, and 39°C); (4) carboxy-terminal truncations of `Bat09 NS1` (`NS1-128`, `NS1-73`) impair Sendai-virus-induced IFN-$\beta$ promoter antagonism in 293T reporter assays just like `PR8 NS1` truncations, yet—unlike `PR8`—fail to attenuate viral replication in human lung epithelial `Calu-3` cells or lethality in mice (100% mortality); and (5) systematic minigenome RNP assays across 9 IAV subtypes, IBV, and `Bat10` (Figure 8), 8-plasmid reassortment matrices (Tables 1–5), and plaque-purified co-infection assays ($0/118$ reassortant plaques between `Bat09:mH1mN1` and `PR8` vs. frequent reassortment between `Bat09` and `Bat10`) prove that divergent H17N10 and H18N11 bat influenza viruses readily reassort with each other but are genetically and biochemically incompatible with canonical Influenza A and B viruses (except unidirectional `PR8-M` segment substitution).

### 2. STRENGTHS
- **Definitive Resolution of Bat Influenza Authenticity and Entry/Packaging Barriers:** Prior to this work, only metagenomic sequences of H17N10 and H18N11 existed, raising questions about whether the sequences represented functional viral genomes or sequencing artifacts. By rescuing `Bat09:mH1mN1`, `Bat09:mH3mN2`, and `Bat10:mH1mN1ss` and verifying genetic stability via Illumina MiSeq deep sequencing across cell passages and infected mouse lungs, the authors prove that all 6 internal segments (`PB2, PB1, PA, NP, M, NS`) and the terminal HA/NA packaging signals of both `Bat09` and `Bat10` are fully functional and that the sole block to culturing wild-type bat influenza in standard substrates resides in the H17/H18 and N10/N11 ectodomains/coding regions.
- **Rigorous Packaging-Signal Dissection with Synonymous Mutation (`ss`) Controls:** To rule out residual internal packaging activity within the `PR8` H1 and N1 coding sequences, the authors engineered 64 silent substitutions into the `PR8-HA` terminal coding regions (`mH1ss`) and 90 silent substitutions into `PR8-NA` (`mN1ss`), as well as chimeric `NP` and `NS` packaging-swap constructs (`Batps-PR8-NP`, `PR8ps-Bat-NP`, `Batps-PR8-NS`, `PR8ps-Bat-NS`; Tables 3–5 and Fig. S2), cleanly separating cis-acting RNA packaging blocks from protein–protein / RNP assembly blocks.
- **Multi-Level Pandemic Risk and Reassortment Assessment:** Combining quantitative RNP minigenome matrices across 11 orthomyxoviruses (`H1N1, H2N2, H3N2, H3N8, H5N9, H5N1, H7N9, IBV, Bat09, Bat10`), reverse-genetics rescue tables (Tables 1–5), and unbiased classical co-infection plaque genotyping ($n=118$ plaques) provides exceptionally strong evidence that `H17N10` and `H18N11` form a cohesive genetic group that cannot naturally reassort its internal replication machinery with canonical human, swine, or avian IAVs.
- **Discovery of Non-Canonical Innate Immune Evasion in `Bat09`:** Showing that `Bat09 NS1-73` and `NS1-128` truncations lose plasmid-based IFN-$\beta$ reporter inhibition (`Figure 4A`) yet retain full VSV-luciferase bioassay suppression (`Figure 4B`), full replication in IFN-competent human `Calu-3` cells (`Figure 4C`), and 100% lethality in BALB/c mice (`Figure 5`) demonstrates that plasmid reporter assays alone can miss viral-context innate immune antagonism mediated by the NS1 N-terminus or other Bat09 viral proteins.

### 3. WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT (Methodological & Statistical Audit)
- **Post-Hoc Selection of the "Top 4 of 6" Eggs in Figure 1E:**
  - In Materials and Methods ("Virus replication in vitro and in ovo", second section Line 41), the authors state: *"Six of 10-day-old embryonated chicken eggs were inoculated with Bat09:mH1mN1 or PR8 at $10^3\text{ TCID}_{50}$/egg... The 4 eggs with the highest titers in each virus group was used to calculate the average titer and generate the graph in Fig. 1E."*
  - Discarding the 2 lowest-titer biological replicates out of 6 in each group without a pre-defined technical exclusion criterion (such as bacterial contamination or embryo death within 24 h) upwardly biases the mean titer and artificially shrinks the standard deviation in Figure 1E. All 6 inoculated eggs per group should be included.
- **Omission of Wild-Type `PR8` Control Group in the *In Vivo* NS1 Truncation Challenge (Figure 5):**
  - While `Figure 4C` (*in vitro* `Calu-3` growth kinetics) compares `Bat09:mH1mN1ss` (WT, `NS1-128`, `NS1-73`) against both wild-type `PR8` and truncated `PR8-NS1-126` / `PR8-NS1-73`, the mouse challenge in `Figure 5` (inoculated at $10^5\text{ TCID}_{50}$) includes `Bat09:mH1mN1ss-WT`, `Bat09-NS1-128`, `Bat09-NS1-73`, and `PR8-NS1-126`, but omits wild-type `PR8` and `PR8-NS1-73`. Although the comparison between `Bat09:mH1mN1ss-WT` and its own `NS1-128`/`NS1-73` mutants clearly shows zero attenuation in the Bat09 background, including the parental `PR8` control at the same dose (or citing the $10^4\text{ TCID}_{50}$ 100% lethality from Figure 2C) would complete the $2 \times 2$ design.
- **Minor Subtype Typo in Materials and Methods:**
  - In Materials and Methods ("Complete genome synthesis and plasmid construction", second section Line 20), `A/little yellow-shouldered bat/Guatemala/164/2009` is misprinted as **`(H10N17)`** instead of **`(H17N10)`**, and in the Table 5 header `Bat10:mH1mN1ss` is misprinted as **`Bat10:mH1mH1ss`**.

### 4. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *The 6 internal protein-coding vRNAs and HA/NA cis-acting packaging signals of Bat09 (H17N10) and Bat10 (H18N11) are authentic and replication-competent in mammalian and avian cells as well as mice once paired with canonical IAV HA and NA coding regions.* — `[Well Supported]` by virus rescue, EM, multi-substrate growth kinetics, mouse histopathology/IHC, and MiSeq deep-sequencing validation (Figures 1–3).
- **Claim 2:** *Bat09 PB2 residues 701, 627, and 158 modulate polymerase activity and mammalian virulence in mice, whereas C-terminal NS1 truncations (`NS1-128`, `NS1-73`) do not attenuate Bat09 replication in Calu-3 cells or mice.* — `[Well Supported]` by site-directed mutagenesis, multi-temperature minigenome assays, VSV-luciferase IFN bioassays, and mouse survival/titer studies (Figures 4–7).
- **Claim 3:** *Divergent bat influenza viruses (H17N10 and H18N11) readily reassort with each other but have severe multi-level incompatibilities that prevent natural reassortment with canonical Influenza A or B viruses.* — `[Well Supported]` by RNP minigenome matrices, 8-plasmid rescue matrices, packaging-swap constructs, and plaque-purified co-infection assays (Figure 8, Tables 1–5).

### 5. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Sequence & Construct Accession Numbers:** All synthesized and engineered construct sequences are deposited in GenBank under accession numbers `KM203345–KM203356`, with full construct diagrams and sequence alignments provided in `Figures S1–S4`.
- **Experimental Detail:** Oligonucleotide assembly protocols, MOI and TCID50 titration conditions, Illumina MiSeq bidirectional binomial SNP-calling thresholds, dual-luciferase minigenome stoichiometry, and animal group sizes ($n=14$ mice/group: 3 euthanized at 3 dpi, 3 at 5 dpi, and 8 monitored for weight loss and survival) are documented with exemplary clarity.
