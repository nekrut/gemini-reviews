### 1. Executive Summary
This study amplifies, TA-clones, and Sanger-sequences full-length Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) genomes from five untreated chronic hepatitis B patients in Ahvaz, Iran (GenBank `MK355500`, `MK355501`, `MK693107`–`MK693109`), selects two of the five sequences (`MK355500` and `MK355501`), and subjects their translated preS/S envelope protein (389 amino acids) to an uncritical battery of ~20 generic web-server prediction tools (ProtParam, ProtScale, IEDB, BepiPred, BcePred, ABCpred, VaxiJen, AlgPred, DiANNA, SCRATCH, DISPHOS, NetPhos, GlycoEP, NetNGlyc, SOPMA, GAMMAPred, I-TASSER, Phyre2, (PS)$^2$-V2, QMEAN, and Rampage).

The manuscript exhibits severe methodological, mathematical, and biological flaws: (1) a mathematically impossible demographic breakdown for $N = 5$ patients ("five untreated patients (48.6% from females and 51.4% from males)"); (2) amplification of 3.2-kb HBV genomes for 35 cycles using non-proofreading *Taq* polymerase followed by single-clone TA sequencing, which confounds polymerase-induced errors with genuine clinical mutations; (3) arbitrary exclusion of 3 of the 5 sequenced isolates from all downstream analyses; and (4) indiscriminate application of soluble-protein web servers to a multi-pass transmembrane viral envelope protein, resulting in transmembrane helices being reported as top B-cell epitopes and poorly folded ab initio 3D models ($Q\text{-score} = -3.06$, only 69.2% favored Ramachandran residues) being over-interpreted as structural insights.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Mathematical Impossibility in Cohort Demographics (Line 34):** In Materials and Methods (Sampling, line 34), the authors state: "Sera samples were collected from five untreated patients (48.6% from females and 51.4% from males) with chronic hepatitis B virus infection..." With $N = 5$ individuals, the only possible non-zero percentages are $20\%, 40\%, 60\%, 80\%, \text{and } 100\%$. Reporting $48.6\%$ and $51.4\%$ (which correspond to $17/35$ and $18/35$, or $34/70$ and $36/70$) indicates that demographic text from a larger cohort was pasted without adjustment or that the sample description is internally inconsistent.
- **Taq Polymerase Error Confounding in TA Cloning:** Full-length 3.2-kb HBV genomes were amplified for 35 cycles using a standard non-proofreading *Taq* polymerase master mix ("2X master mix PCR which included Taq polymerase... and added 3' A overhangs... Extracted DNAs were cloned into the pTZ57 R/T vector"). Because *Taq* lacks $3'\rightarrow 5'$ exonuclease proofreading activity (error rate $\sim 1\text{--}2 \times 10^{-4}$ per nucleotide per cycle), a 3.2-kb product amplified for 35 cycles accumulates an expected 3–10 polymerase-induced substitutions per cloned molecule. Sequencing individual TA clones without direct bulk amplicon sequencing or high-fidelity proofreading polymerase makes it impossible to know whether singleton amino acid substitutions reported in `MK355500` or `MK355501` (such as $\text{L109R}$, $\text{T113S}$, $\text{T126N}$, $\text{N131T}$, $\text{P142L}$, $\text{W163R}$, $\text{R169P}$) are genuine patient variants or PCR cloning artifacts.
- **Unjustified Dropping of 60% of the Sample:** Although 5 genomes were sequenced and deposited (`MK355500`, `MK355501`, `MK693107`–`MK693109`), the authors state: "Concerning the high similarity of these sequences, we selected two sequences and performed the bioinformatics analysis on them." No pairwise identity matrix or variant table for the other three isolates (`MK693107`–`MK693109`) is shown.
- **Internal Contradictions Across Abstract, Introduction, Results, and Conclusion:**
  - In the Abstract (line 13), the authors conclude: "mutations were identified at positions T113S and N131T within the MHR region of S protein; these mutations can potentially decrease the effect of hepatitis B vaccination in vaccine recipients."
  - Yet in the Discussion (line 195) and Conclusion (line 202), they state the exact opposite: "No escape mutations were found in both detected S genes" and "No escape mutation was observed in the preS/S gene of both HBV isolates."
- **Misapplication of Globular Protein Web Servers to a Multi-Pass Integral Membrane Protein:**
  - HBsAg (L/M/S protein) is an integral ER/virion membrane protein containing four transmembrane helices that self-assembles into lipoprotein subviral particles with complex intermolecular disulfide cross-links.
  - In Table 2 ("Prediction of B cell epitopes"), the "Antigenicity prediction" column reports residues `241–262 (RRFIIFLFILLLCLIFLLVLLD)` as the predicted B-cell epitope. Inspection of the sequence (`I-I-F-L-F-I-L-L-L-C-L-I-F-L-L-V-L-L`) immediately reveals that this is the hydrophobic transmembrane helix I/II of the S domain buried inside the lipid bilayer, which is physically inaccessible to antibodies! This occurs because the Parker/Kolaskar amino-acid propensity scales on IEDB score hydrophobic/cysteine/leucine-rich stretches high when misused without membrane topology masking.
  - Similarly, running `AlgPred` (an IgE food/inhalant allergenicity predictor) to declare HBV preS/S "NON ALLERGEN" (Table 3) and running `I-TASSER` ab initio threading without a lipid bilayer (yielding a poor $Q\text{-mean}$ score of $-3.06$ and only $69.2\%$ of residues in favored Ramachandran regions, Figure 5) have no biological validity.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** "Ahvaz sequences revealed four sites (4, 112, 166, and 309) in the preS/S gene for N-glycosylation that could possibly be a potential target for anti-HBV therapy." `[Overstated]` — These four canonical `N-X-S/T` sequons (including the universal N146 site in the S domain, corresponding to residue 309 of the 389-aa L protein) are completely conserved between the wild-type genotype D reference sequence (`NC_003977.2`) and both patient sequences (Table 5). Standard conserved viral glycosylation sequons identified by a motif scan do not constitute a novel therapeutic discovery.
- **Claim 2:** "In the present study, mutations were identified at positions T113S and N131T within the MHR region of S protein; these mutations can potentially decrease the effect of hepatitis B vaccination in vaccine recipients." `[Fragile]` — No neutralization, ELISA binding, or vaccination breakthrough data are presented (all patients were untreated chronic carriers), and the variants themselves are unverified single-clone *Taq* PCR products. Furthermore, in Genotype D (subtype *ayw*), `T113S` and `N131T` (relative to a subtype *adw* or different subgenotype reference) frequently represent normal wild-type genotype/serotype polymorphisms rather than de novo escape mutations.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Sequence Accessions:** The five GenBank accession numbers (`MK355500`, `MK355501`, `MK693107`–`MK693109`) and the reference genome (`NC_003977.2`) are provided, allowing independent inspection of the deposited sequences. However, raw chromatogram trace files are unavailable, and three of the five sequences are omitted from all tables and figures.
