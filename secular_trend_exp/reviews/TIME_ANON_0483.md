### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This multi-cohort human genetics and single-nucleus transcriptomics study investigates cell-type mechanisms of abdominal obesity (WHRadjBMI) using subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) snRNA-seq from three Finnish cohorts (KOBS, $n=8$; RYSA, $n=68$; CRYO-Twin, $n=15$), bulk SAT RNA-seq and adipocyte diameter measurements from BMI-discordant monozygotic twins ($n=88$), and genotype data from METSIM ($n=6,738$ unrelated Finnish men), UK Biobank ($n=483,560$), and 1000 Genomes ($n=22$ populations). Using CELLECT/S-LDSC, the authors show that WHRadjBMI heritability is specifically enriched in SAT adipocytes across all three snRNA-seq cohorts ($p_{\text{adj}} \le 1.16 \times 10^{-3}$). They then focus on `rs10494217` (p.His156Asn), a common WHRadjBMI GWAS missense variant in the adipocyte-specific transcription factor *TBX15*, reporting elevated frequency of the derived T risk allele in Finns ($\text{MAF} = 0.28$ vs. $0.18$ in non-Finnish Europeans), a latitudinal cline via PGLS ($p = 0.0035$), a chromosome-wide standardized $\text{iHS} = -2.03$ (96th percentile) in METSIM Finns, differential expression of 13 unique adipocyte marker genes in `trans` between T-allele carriers and non-carriers, and a positive correlation between one `trans` lncRNA (`AC002066.1`), its neighboring gene *CAV2*, and adipocyte diameter. While the CELLECT heritability enrichment is robust and the multi-cohort integration is ambitious, the primary single-nucleus `trans`-gene differential expression discovery and replication suffer from severe cell-level pseudoreplication (running nucleus-level Wilcoxon tests across thousands of nuclei from $n=4$ vs. $n=4$ donors in KOBS), and the selection/LD comparisons between Finns and Brits do not adequately account for the genome-wide Finnish founder bottleneck.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS / WEAKESSSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT

#### STRENGTHS
- **Replicated Cell-Type Heritability Enrichment:** Uses CELLEX and CELLECT (S-LDSC) across three independent Finnish SAT snRNA-seq datasets (KOBS, CRYO-Twin, RYSA) to demonstrate that WHRadjBMI SNP heritability is consistently and specifically enriched in mature adipocytes rather than stromal/immune fractions.
- **Population-Structure Correction in Latitudinal Cline Analysis:** Applies phylogenetic generalized least squares (PGLS) across 22 populations from the 1000 Genomes Project using a pairwise $F_{\text{ST}}$ neighbor-joining covariance matrix and leave-one-out stability checks to test latitude and temperature predictors above background genetic drift.
- **Public Deposition of Multi-Omics Resources:** Deposits raw and processed snRNA-seq datasets in NIH GEO (`GSE269926`, `GSE236708`, `GSE274778`) and genome-wide METSIM iHS summary statistics in UCLA Dataverse.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Severe Nucleus-Level Pseudoreplication in `trans`-Gene Discovery, Replication, and Module Score Testing:** To identify downstream `trans` targets of the *TBX15* `rs10494217` T allele, the authors compare T-allele carriers ($n=4$ individuals) vs. non-carriers ($n=4$ individuals) in KOBS, and replicate in RYSA ($n=36$ carriers vs. $n=32$ non-carriers). Instead of aggregating expression per donor (pseudobulk) or fitting a donor-level mixed-effects model where the individual (the unit of genotype randomization) is the biological replicate, the authors run Seurat `FindMarkers` Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with `logfc.threshold = 0` directly across all individual *adipocyte nuclei*, and similarly run an unpaired nucleus-level Wilcoxon test on the 12-gene module score (reporting $p_{\text{KOBS}} = 1.46 \times 10^{-40}$ on $4$ vs. $4$ patients, and $p_{\text{RYSA}} = 3.53 \times 10^{-22}$). With $n=4$ vs. $n=4$ biological replicates in KOBS, the minimum possible two-sided exact permutation $p$-value at the individual level is $2 / \binom{8}{4} = 2/70 = 0.0286$—meaning *zero* genes could ever pass a Bonferroni correction across 722 tested adipocyte marker genes ($\alpha = 6.9 \times 10^{-5}$) without treating thousands of nuclei from the same 8 biopsy samples as independent observations. Remarkably, the authors *did* compute donor-level TMM-normalized pseudobulk for their gene–gene correlation analysis in RYSA (Methods line 145), proving they had the pseudobulk matrix in hand, yet used unaggregated nucleus-level Wilcoxon tests for the genotype-stratified `trans` expression claims.
- **Confounding by the Finnish Bottleneck and Reference Panel Mismatch in LD and $F_{\text{ST}}$ Comparisons:**
  - In Figure 2D and S2, the authors compare pairwise $R^2$ within $\pm 1\text{ Mb}$ of `rs10494217` between 6,738 METSIM Finns and 6,738 UKB Brits using a Wilcoxon test across SNP pairs ($p = 1.31 \times 10^{-41}$). First, Finns underwent well-documented historical founder bottlenecks that elevate LD *genome-wide* compared to outbred British populations; showing higher local $R^2$ in Finns vs. Brits without comparing against the genome-wide empirical distribution of 2-Mb window LD differences between Finns and Brits cannot distinguish positive selection from demographic history. Second, METSIM genotypes were imputed against TOPMed r2 (build hg38) whereas UKB genotypes were imputed against HRC/UK10K (build hg19 lifted over to hg38), introducing reference-panelLD differences.
  - In the $F_{\text{ST}}$ empirical $p$-value analysis (Methods line 106), the 10,000 background SNPs were matched to the allele frequency of `rs10494217` ($\text{MAF} \approx 0.28$) *in METSIM Finns*. Conditioning on high MAF in the bottlenecked focal population and comparing against populations where the derived allele is near 0% inherently selects for SNPs that drifted or swept upward in Finland. Even so, only 25 of 107 UKB populations achieved nominal $p < 0.05$ without multiple-testing correction, and the standardized iHS in Finns ($-2.03$, 96th percentile) corresponds to a modest empirical two-sided $p \approx 0.08$.
- **Thermogenic Adaptive Directionality Paradox:** The authors note that *Tbx15* knockout in mice *impairs* inguinal white adipocyte browning/thermogenesis and *increases* high-fat-diet weight gain. In humans, the `rs10494217` T allele is predicted by SIFT/PANTHER/CADD to be a *deleterious/damaging* substitution (p.His156Asn) and *increases* WHRadjBMI (abdominal obesity risk). If the T allele is a partial loss-of-function allele that impairs *TBX15* function and promotes abdominal fat accumulation/hypertrophy, it would *reduce* (not increase) *TBX15*-mediated thermogenic browning, contradicting the narrative that it was positively selected to enhance thermogenic activity in cold climates.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Abdominal obesity (WHRadjBMI) SNP heritability is specifically enriched in SAT adipocytes.** `[Well Supported]` — Replicated across three independent Finnish SAT snRNA-seq cohorts using CELLECT/S-LDSC.
- **Claim 2: The *TBX15* missense variant `rs10494217` varies along a latitudinal cline and shows a moderate extended haplotype homozygosity signal ($\text{iHS} = -2.03$, 96th percentile) in Finns.** `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by PGLS across 1000 Genomes populations and Hapbin iHS in METSIM, though local LD and $F_{\text{ST}}$ comparisons with UKB are confounded by Finland's demographic bottleneck, and the functional sign of a deleterious *TBX15* allele on thermogenesis vs. fat storage remains experimentally untested.
- **Claim 3: `rs10494217` risk allele status significantly alters SAT adipocyte expression of 13 unique adipocyte marker genes in `trans`.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Relies on nucleus-level Wilcoxon tests (`FindMarkers` with `logfc.threshold = 0`) that treat thousands of nuclei from $n=4$ vs. $n=4$ donors in KOBS (and $n=36$ vs. $n=32$ in RYSA) as independent biological replicates instead of donor-level pseudobulk or mixed-model eQTL testing.
