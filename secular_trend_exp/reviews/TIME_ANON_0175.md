### 1. Executive Summary
This simulation study evaluates the performance of Bayesian genomic selection models (`Bayes C`$\pi$ and `Bayes C` with fixed $\pi = 0.99$, implemented in `GS3`) for quantitative trait locus (QTL) mapping and individual causative-mutation zygosity (homozygous vs. heterozygous status) prediction. Using real phased genotypes (`DagPhase`, 38,277 autosomal SNPs from the Illumina BovineSNP50 array after $\text{MAF} \ge 0.05$ filtering) and a 12,142-animal pedigree for 4,387 French Holstein bulls (plus a 1,500-bull random subset), the authors simulate nine genetic architectures ($h^2 \in \{0.1, 0.4, 0.7\} \times N_{\text{QTL}} \in \{10, 100, 1000\}$, where bi-allelic SNP-QTLs explain 70% of genetic variance and pedigree polygenic effects explain 30%, masking the causal SNPs from the marker panel). They demonstrate that `Bayes C`$\pi$ frequently fails to converge on a sparse $\pi$ when heritability is low ($h^2 = 0.1$) or polygenicity is high ($N_{\text{QTL}} = 1000$), whereas fixing $\pi = 0.99$ (`Bayes C`) yields sharper posterior inclusion probability (PIP) peaks. They also show that individual QTL zygosity can be inferred by Partitioning Around Medoids (`PAM`) clustering of haplotype effect contrasts when no confounding QTLs reside within 10 Mb. The study is well grounded in realistic livestock linkage disequilibrium, though 7 of the 9 scenarios were run for only a single simulation replicate.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Realistic LD and Pedigree Simulation Design:** Using true phased genotypes from 4,387 Holstein bulls alongside a 30% residual polygenic component simulated down the true 12,142-individual pedigree, and removing the simulated causal SNPs before fitting `Bayes C` / `Bayes C`$\pi$, provides a realistic benchmark of marker-based QTL localization under long-range cattle LD.
- **Diagnosis of `Bayes C`$\pi$ Mixing Failure in Low-Power Regimes:** Figure 1 and Table 2 clearly document a critical statistical property of `Bayes C`$\pi$: because $\pi$ and the common marker variance $\sigma_a^2$ are strongly negatively correlated in the Gibbs sampler, when individual QTL effects fall below the detection threshold ($h^2 = 0.1$ or $N_{\text{QTL}} = 1000$, where even a "large" QTL explains only $0.04\% - 0.25\%$ of phenotypic variance), the chain shifts into an infinitesimal/GBLUP-like regime including $20,698 - 29,504$ of the 38,277 SNPs to capture pedigree relationships, destroying PIP localization.
- **Single-Replicate Limitation Across 7 of 9 Scenarios:** A significant weakness of the simulation design (Methods, line 34) is that only the two scenarios with $N_{\text{QTL}} = 100$ and $h^2 \in \{0.4, 0.7\}$ were replicated ($1 + 10$ runs in Table 4); the remaining 7 scenarios in Table 3 and Figures 3–5 were run **only once** ($N = 1$ simulation replicate). Because QTL locations and allele frequencies are randomly drawn, single-replicate counts of top-10 detected intervals (especially when $N_{\text{QTL}} = 10$, which has only 1 large and 2 medium QTL) are subject to high Monte Carlo sampling variance.
- **Breakdown of Null False-Positive Definition at $N_{\text{QTL}} = 1000$:** Under the 60-SNP window criterion (~3.9 Mb), the 38,277-SNP bovine genome contains ~638 non-overlapping windows. When 1,000 QTLs are randomly placed across the genome, each 60-SNP window contains on average $\sim 1.6$ true QTLs by chance alone, rendering the baseline false-positive rate (windows with zero true QTLs) nearly zero even for random window selection. The authors recognize this in the text (lines 109 and 152) and redefine false positives in Figures 4–5 as intervals containing no QTL or only small QTLs, which is a sensible pragmatic adjustment.
- **Minor Text and Equation Typographical Errors:**
  1. In Equation 1 (line 56), the linear model is written $y_i = \mu + u_i + \sum_{k=1}^K z_{ik} + e_i$, omitting the regression coefficient $a_k$ inside the sum ($\sum_{k=1}^K z_{ik} a_k$).
  2. In the Discussion (line 181), the total number of markers is misstated as `37 227` instead of `38 277` (reported in the Abstract and Methods).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** In `Bayes C`$\pi$, the hyperparameter $\pi$ fails to converge to a sparse peak under low heritability ($h^2 = 0.1$), high polygenicity ($N_{\text{QTL}} = 1000$), or reduced sample size ($N = 1500$), whereas `Bayes C` with a high fixed $\pi$ ($0.99$) produces sharper PIP peaks for QTL localization. `[Well Supported]`
- **Claim 2:** Summing PIPs over 40–60 adjacent SNP windows (~2.6–3.9 Mb) reduces false positives compared to single-SNP PIPs when $N_{\text{QTL}} \le 100$, whereas single-SNP PIPs better capture large individual QTLs when $N_{\text{QTL}} = 1000$. `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by the trends in Table 3 and Table 4, though single-replicate noise affects the $N_{\text{QTL}} = 10$ and $1000$ cells.
- **Claim 3:** Individual zygosity at the causative mutation can be accurately predicted by `PAM` clustering of the absolute difference between an individual's two phased haplotype effects when the resulting distribution is bimodal and no other QTLs lie within 10 Mb. `[Well Supported]`

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Software & Parameter Specification:** All third-party tools (`GS3`, `DagPhase`, R package `fpc` for PAM clustering) and MCMC hyperparameters (180,000 iterations, 20,000 burn-in, thinning 50, $\nu = 4.2$ prior df) are clearly specified. Because the underlying genotypes belong to a proprietary national dairy breeding cohort (French Holstein bulls genotyped by Labogena), raw genotypes are not publicly deposited, though the simulation formulas are fully specified to allow replication on any livestock SNP dataset.
