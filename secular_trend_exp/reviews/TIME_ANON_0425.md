### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This study investigates the molecular epidemiology, evolutionary dynamics, recombination history, and cell-surface receptor-binding motifs (`RGD` for $\alpha_V\beta_3/\alpha_V\beta_6$ integrins and `VP1-T132R/K` for heparan sulfate [HS] proteoglycans) of Coxsackievirus A9 (`CVA9`). The authors sequenced partial `VP1` ($358\text{ bp}$, nucleotides $2592\text{--}2949$) and `3Dpol` ($441\text{ bp}$, nucleotides $6099\text{--}6539$) regions from 54 clinical CVA9 isolates collected across Finland ($n=16$), the Netherlands ($n=30$), Denmark ($n=1$), and North America ($n=7$) between 1959 and 2012, and combined them with 21 reference GenBank complete genome sequences ($N=75$ total strains) for phylogenetic reconstruction (`MEGA6` Neighbor-Joining and `BEAST v1.8.2` Bayesian skyline coalescent analysis). While the two-locus sequencing dataset (`MN493979–MN494086`) provides useful historical surveillance data confirming complete conservation of the `VP1` C-terminal `RGD` motif and frequent discordant topologies between `VP1` and `3Dpol`, the manuscript suffers from a direct internal contradiction between the Abstract and the main text regarding the frequency of the `VP1-T132R/K` HS-binding mutation, relies solely on visual tree inspection without formal recombination breakpoint/permutation testing, and over-extrapolates Bayesian skyline `Ne` estimates from short sub-genomic fragments across highly uneven sampling epochs.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
- **Direct Internal Contradiction on `VP1-T132R/K` Prevalence (Abstract vs. Results/Discussion/Conclusions):**
  - In the Abstract (line 11), the authors state: *"The RGD motif was highly conserved among CVA9 circulating strains while the putative HS binding site was only found in one isolate."*
  - However, in Results Section 3.3 (line 103), Discussion (line 147), and Conclusions (line 152), the authors report that **10 out of 54** novel CVA9 isolates possessed the positively charged `T132R/K` substitution (`1` strain `NET-90-40132` with `T132R` and `9` strains—`FIN-97-40100`, `FIN-98-40102`, `NET-79-40119`, `NET-59-40020`, `NET-63-40035`, `NET-64-40045`, `NET-65-40057`, `NET-79-40120`, and `NET-05-40169`—with `T132K`, previously sequenced in ref. `[21]`), and that `1` sequence in the `BLAST` search (`EVB85` strain `XJ-99-292`, `JX898657.1`) carried `T132R`. The Abstract appears to conflate the single `T132R` CVA9 isolate (or single `BLAST` hit) with the overall prevalence of the `T132R/K` HS-binding site (`10/54 = 18.5%`).
- **Lack of Formal Recombination Statistical Testing:** Recombination between `VP1` and `3Dpol` is inferred purely by visual comparison of `VP1` vs. `3Dpol` Neighbor-Joining/MCC clade assignments (`A–P` vs. `1–20`) and connecting lines in Figure 2. Because the intervening `P2` and `3A–3C` genomic regions ($\sim 3.1\text{ kb}$) were not sequenced in the 54 clinical isolates, formal recombination breakpoint and permutation analyses (`RDP4`, `SimPlot`, `GARD`, or Shimodaira-Hasegawa/AU topology incongruence tests) were not performed to distinguish genuine inter-typic recombination from low bootstrap resolution on internal `VP1` branches (`VP1` fragment is only $358\text{ bp}$, where internal node support is frequently weak).
- **Bayesian Skyline Coalescent (`BEAST`) Over-Parameterization on Short Fragments:** Running a 10-group piecewise-constant Bayesian skyline plot (`SRD06` + relaxed lognormal clock) on $358\text{ bp}$ (`VP1`) and $441\text{ bp}$ (`3Dpol`) alignments of 75 sequences sampled opportunistically across 63 years ($1950\text{--}2013$) is prone to prior domination and sampling-density artifacts. Moreover, for `3Dpol`, sequences undergo frequent inter-enterovirus B recombination across different serotypes, violating the single-tree coalescent assumption of the `BEAST` skyline model and inflating the apparent `tMRCA` (`226 years`, 95% HPD `124–356` vs. `84 years` for `VP1`).
- **Geographic Count Inconsistency:** Materials and Methods (line 30) lists the 54 novel clinical isolates as `Finland (n = 16), the Netherlands (n = 30), Denmark (n = 1), and Northern America (n = 7)` ($47$ European + $7$ North American). Yet Results Section 3.3 (line 98) states: *"A set of CVA9 sequences collected during 1950–2013 from Europe (n = 48), Americas (n = 11), and Asia-Pacific (n = 15), as well as the CVA9 prototype Griggs sequence, were aligned..."* without clarifying whether a European reference sequence was added to the 47 novel European isolates.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
- **`RGD` Motif Conservation vs. In Vitro `HS` Adaptation `[Well Supported]`:** The sequencing data across 74 circulating strains and the prototype Griggs strain convincingly confirm that the `VP1` C-terminal `RGD` integrin-binding motif (along with flanking `RGD+1` leucine/methionine and `RGD+4` phenylalanine residues) is strictly conserved in clinical isolates, despite earlier laboratory passage studies showing that `RGD`-deleted mutants remain viable in cell culture.
- **Frequent Discordance Between Capsid (`VP1`) and Non-Structural (`3Dpol`) Lineages `[Moderately Supported]`:** Comparing `VP1` and `3Dpol` clustering clearly illustrates the modular evolution typical of *Enterovirus B*, where monophyletic capsid serotypes exchange non-structural `3Dpol` regions with co-circulating EV-B types.
- **Temporal Population Bottleneck in the Early 2000s (`Fig. 3C,D`) `[Overstated]`:** The apparent dip and rebound in the `VP1` Bayesian skyline plot around the early 2000s (Figure 3C) lies entirely within wide, overlapping 95% HPD intervals and likely reflects temporal sampling heterogeneity rather than a true global demographic crash of `CVA9`.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** GenBank accession numbers for all newly generated sequences are explicitly provided (`MN493979–MN494086`), primer sequences and genomic coordinates (`VP1`: `2592–2949`; `3Dpol`: `6099–6539`) are listed in Table 1, and `BEAST v1.8.2` clock/coalescent settings (`SRD06`, relaxed lognormal clock, 100M MCMC steps sampled every 1,000) are documented.
- **Weaknesses:** The reference accession list (`Table S1`) is externalized to supplementary material, ESS values for `BEAST` parameters are stated only as `>200` without reporting exact posterior means/95% HPDs for rate variation parameters, and no sequence alignments or `.xml` `BEAST` input files are deposited.
