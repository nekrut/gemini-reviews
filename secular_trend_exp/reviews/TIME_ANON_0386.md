### 1. Executive Summary
This candidate-locus genetic association study uses genotyped Illumina Human 1M-Duo data from 1,976 European-American participants (506 cases with DSM-5 cocaine use disorder [CUD] and 1,470 controls with 0–1 criteria) in the Study of Addiction: Genetics and Environment (SAGE; pooled from COGEND, COGA, and FSCD) to test 47 SNPs (MAF > 1%, spanning 22 LD bins at $r^2 \ge 0.8$) across the chromosome 8 *CHRNB3-CHRNA6* nicotinic receptor gene cluster. Using logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, study sub-cohort, maximum lifetime FTND nicotine score, and DSM-5 alcohol use disorder, the authors identify two statistically independent signals surpassing the region-wide Bonferroni threshold ($p < 0.002 = 0.05/22$): a low-frequency LD bin tagged by `rs9298626` (MAF $\approx 4\%$, OR $\approx 2.68$, $p = 2.34 \times 10^{-4}$) and a second signal revealed upon conditioning on `rs9298626`, tagged by `rs892413` ($p = 3.57 \times 10^{-4}$, OR $= 1.58$). Both associations remain significant after conditioning on the known nicotine-dependence GWAS SNP `rs1451240` and when restricting controls to cocaine-exposed unaffected individuals ($n=393$). The primary limitations are the absence of an independent replication cohort and minor inconsistencies between linear vs. logistic regression descriptions and table references.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Ascertainment Complexity and Sensitivity Analyses:** Because SAGE pools three cohorts ascertained for different primary dependencies—COGEND (nicotine, contributing 76 CUD cases and 1,005 controls), COGA (alcohol, contributing 212 CUD cases—100% comorbid with alcohol use disorder—and 389 controls), and FSCD (cocaine, contributing 218 CUD cases and 76 controls)—collider and ascertainment bias are major concerns. The authors appropriately address this by adjusting for study dummy variables, FTND score, and alcohol use disorder, and by conducting stratified analyses across sub-studies and restricting controls to the 393 cocaine-exposed non-dependent subjects ($p = 3.12 \times 10^{-3}$, OR $= 2.68$, 95% CI $1.40–5.16$).
* **Sample Size Arithmetic Discrepancy in Exposed-Control Analysis:** In the "Minor allele frequency examination" section (line 38), the text states that controls included $N=393$ exposed unaffected and $N=1,077$ never-exposed individuals ($393 + 1,077 = 1,470$ controls, plus 506 cases $= 1,976$ total). Two sentences later, it states: "When control subjects were restricted to those who had been exposed to cocaine but were unaffected ($n = 899$ vs. $1976$)"—where $899 = 506 \text{ cases} + 393 \text{ exposed controls}$ is the combined subsample size compared against the full sample size of $1,976$, phrased confusingly as "$n = 899$ vs. $1976$".
* **Linear vs. Logistic Regression Inconsistency and Symmetric CI Anomaly:** While the Methods specify logistic regression for the binary DSM-5 CUD outcome, the Abstract and line 53 refer to a "linear regression model" for the conditional analyses. Furthermore, in the Haplotype Analysis (line 48), the 4% haplotype carrying minor alleles at both `rs9298626` and `rs1451240` is reported with $\text{OR} = 3.19$, $p = 1.35 \times 10^{-4}$, and $95\% \text{ CI} = 1.64–4.73$; notably, $3.19 - 1.64 = 1.55$ and $4.73 - 3.19 = 1.54$, indicating that the authors computed an additive symmetric confidence interval on the raw OR scale ($\text{OR} \pm 1.96 \cdot \text{SE}_{\text{OR}}$) rather than exponentiating the log-odds confidence interval, which is statistically improper for odds ratios.
* **Lack of External Replication:** Although region-wide multiple testing across 22 LD bins ($p < 0.002$) is controlled, $p \approx 2.3 \times 10^{-4}$ for a low-frequency variant ($\text{MAF} \approx 4\%$) in 506 cases does not reach genome-wide significance and requires independent cohort replication to rule out winner's curse.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Association of *CHRNB3-A6* variants (`rs9298626` and `rs892413`) with DSM-5 CUD in SAGE:** `[Moderately Supported]` — Within the SAGE European-American cohort, the primary and conditional associations survive region-wide Bonferroni correction and remain consistent in cocaine-exposed controls.
* **Independence of the CUD signal from the `rs1451240` nicotine dependence signal:** `[Well Supported]` — Conditional regression and two-SNP haplotype analysis clearly show that the 18% haplotype carrying the `rs1451240` minor allele with the `rs9298626` major allele has no effect on CUD ($p=0.14$), isolating the risk effect to the 4% haplotype tagged by `rs9298626`.
* **Pleiotropic opposing effects across nicotine dependence, CUD, and bipolar disorder:** `[Moderately Supported]` — The observation that `rs4952`/`rs4953` (in LD with `rs9298626`, $r^2 = 0.94$) are associated with increased risk of CUD and bipolar disorder but reduced risk of nicotine dependence is biologically plausible via cholinergic modulation of mesolimbic dopamine signaling, though functional characterization of the causal variant is lacking.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Cohort and Genotyping Provenance:** The SAGE dataset is available through dbGaP (ethical controlled-access human cohort), and QC criteria (MAF > 1%, call rate > 0.98, Illumina Human 1M-Duo, genomic interval chr8:42,600,000–42,800,000, PLINK and Haploview parameters) are clearly documented.
* **Missing Embedded Tables:** Tables 1–5 and Figure 1 are referenced in the text for full SNP statistics but are not embedded in the manuscript text file.
