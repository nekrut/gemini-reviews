# Blinded Review: TIME_ANON_69
**Composite Score:** 3.4 / 10.0 (Rigor: 0.8/3.5 | Claims: 0.9/2.5 | Biology: 0.8/2.0 | Reproducibility: 0.9/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Severe Uncorrected Multiple Testing across 15,878 SNPs & Post-Hoc Candidate Region Overlap
**Verdict:** Reject
**Study Modality:** Dry-Lab Secondary Cohort/Database Re-Analysis
**Technology Maturity:** Mid-Stage High-Throughput
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

### 1. Executive Summary
This study re-analyzes the Collaborative Study on the Genetics of Alcoholism (COGA) family dataset distributed for Genetic Analysis Workshop 14 (GAW14; 1,614 individuals across multiplex pedigrees, with 668 classified as affected and 285 as unaffected using a composite of DSM-III-R/Feighner `ALDX1` and DSM-IV `ALDX2` criteria). The authors run nonparametric linkage (NPL in SIMWALK2) and family-based Pedigree Disequilibrium Tests (allele-based `sum-PDT` and `average-PDT`, single-locus and 2-, 3-, and 5-point `genotype-PDT` in PDT 5.1) as well as Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE adjusting for age and sex) across microsatellites and $15,878$ autosomal SNPs. Using an unadjusted nominal significance threshold of $\alpha = 0.01$, they report 489 "significant" SNPs (the top SNP being `tsc0593964` on chromosome 7 at $p = 0.000013$), map these to 74 intragenic loci, and note that 13 of these 74 genes fall within broad cytogenetic bands previously reported in schizophrenia linkage studies (e.g., `1q21-q41`, `5q21-31`, `6p22-24`, `6q21-22`, `8p21-22`, `10p11-15`). They conclude that these 13 genes are putative shared susceptibility candidates for the comorbidity of alcoholism and schizophrenia.

Evaluated on statistical genetics standards, the study's findings are indistinguishable from random noise generated by uncorrected multiple testing: testing $15,878$ SNPs across multiple statistical tests (`sum-PDT`, `average-PDT`, `genotype-PDT`, multipoint 2/3/5-SNP windows, and GEE) at $\alpha = 0.01$ is expected by chance alone to yield $\sim 159$ false positives *per test*, and even the single lowest $p$-value in the entire genome ($p = 1.3 \times 10^{-5}$) does not survive a simple Bonferroni correction for a single scan of $15,878$ SNPs ($\alpha_{\text{Bonf}} = 0.05 / 15,878 = 3.15 \times 10^{-6}$, giving $p_{\text{corrected}} \approx 0.21$ before accounting for multiple tests per SNP). Furthermore, no schizophrenia phenotypes were measured or analyzed in the dataset; intersecting random false-positive SNPs with broad cytogenetic schizophrenia linkage regions covering a substantial fraction of the human autosome without an enrichment test is pure post-hoc storytelling.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Complete Absence of Multiple Testing Correction Across >100,000 Hypothesis Tests:** The authors test $15,878$ autosomal SNPs using at least seven different test procedures: (1) allele-based `sum-PDT`, (2) allele-based `average-PDT`, (3) single-locus `genotype-PDT`, (4) GEE, (5) 2-point `genotype-PDT`, (6) 3-point `genotype-PDT`, and (7) 5-point `genotype-PDT` (plus microsatellite NPL and PDT). Under the global null hypothesis of no association anywhere in the genome:
  - A single test of $15,878$ independent SNPs at $\alpha = 0.01$ expects $15,878 \times 0.01 \approx 159$ false-positive SNPs by pure chance. Remarkably, the observed counts at $\alpha = 0.01$ are **190 SNPs** (when taking the union of *either* `sum-PDT` or `average-PDT` after pre-filtering at $0.05$), **138 SNPs** for `genotype-PDT`, and **65 SNPs** for GEE (and at $\alpha = 0.001$, $15.9$ are expected by chance vs. **18** and **16** observed!). Thus, the observed number of "significant" hits matches the null expectation of pure noise almost exactly.
  - Even the most extreme SNP across the entire genome (`tsc0593964`, $p = 1.3 \times 10^{-5}$ in `genotype-PDT`) fails to reach genome-wide significance even against a single-test Bonferroni threshold of $3.15 \times 10^{-6}$ (expected minimum $p$-value under the null across $15,878$ tests is $\sim 6.3 \times 10^{-5}$, and across 4–7 correlated tests per SNP is $\sim 1\text{--}3 \times 10^{-5}$).
* **Contradictory Reporting of the "489 Significant SNPs":** The Abstract states: "we performed genome-wide single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNP) association tests, which yielded 489 significant SNPs at the 1% significance level" (line 9). However, in the Results section (lines 70–80), the number 489 never appears! Instead, the Results report 190 SNPs (allele-PDT), 138 SNPs (genotype-PDT, of which 94 overlap with allele-PDT), 65 SNPs (GEE, of which 2 overlap with genotype-PDT), 98 SNP pairs (2-point), and 42 SNP triples (3-point). It appears the authors pooled the non-overlapping union of single-locus, GEE, and multipoint 2-SNP and 3-SNP window hits at $\alpha = 0.01$ to arrive at 489 SNPs (and 74 genes), further compounding the multiple-testing inflation.
* **Conceptual Confusion Between Linkage and Linkage Disequilibrium:** In line 65, reporting SIMWALK2 nonparametric linkage (NPL) results on microsatellites, the authors write: "Chromosome 7 showed two peaks of moderate linkage disequilibrium with alcoholism at D7S673 (30.1 cM) with -log p-value 2.077 (NPL-PAIR) and D7S820 (107.5 cM) with 2.041." `NPL-PAIR` in SIMWALK2 measures allele sharing (pedigree *linkage*), not population/gametic *linkage disequilibrium*, and a $-\log_{10} p \approx 2.07$ ($p \approx 0.0085$) in a genome-wide microsatellite scan is well below Lander-Kruglyak suggestive linkage thresholds.
* **Undefined GEE Correlation Structure and Genotype Coding:** In the GEE specification (lines 42–50), the authors write $\text{Logit}[\Pr(D_{ij})] = \beta_0 + \beta_{\text{age}}\text{age}_{ij} + \beta_{\text{sex}}\text{sex}_{ij} + \beta_{\text{geno}}\text{genotype}_{ij}$, without specifying what working correlation matrix (exchangeable across whole extended pedigrees vs. nuclear families? kinship-weighted?) was used in R, nor how the three-level genotype was parameterized (1-df additive vs. 2-df categorical). Applying an exchangeable working correlation across complex multi-generational pedigrees misspecifies the kinship covariance structure.
* **Unvalidated Post-Hoc Cytogenetic Overlap with Schizophrenia Linkage Regions:** Having selected 74 genes from unadjusted $p < 0.01$ noise, the authors intersect their cytogenetic bands with published schizophrenia linkage regions (`1q21-q41`, `5q21-31`, `6p22-24`, `6q21-22.3`, `8p21-22`, `10p11-15`) and highlight 13 overlapping genes (Table 1). Literature schizophrenia linkage intervals from the early 2000s span $15\text{--}25\%$ of the human genome; finding $13 / 74 = 17.6\%$ of random genes falling inside those bands is exactly what would occur by random uniform placement across the genome, and no hypergeometric or permutation enrichment test was performed.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Identification of 489 Alcoholism SNPs, 74 Alcoholism Genes, and 13 Shared Alcoholism–Schizophrenia Susceptibility Genes [Methodologically Flawed / Overstated]:** Every specific locus claim in the paper (`tsc0593964`, `GABRA1`, `CHRNA3`, `NTRK2`, and the 13 genes in Table 1) is based on unadjusted $p$-values between $0.0003$ and $0.009$ out of $15,878$ tested SNPs across multiple statistical tests. Furthermore, the COGA dataset analyzed here contains no schizophrenia diagnosis data whatsoever; claiming to identify candidate genes for schizophrenia–alcoholism comorbidity by intersecting $p < 0.01$ alcoholism noise with 20–40 Mb schizophrenia linkage regions has no biological or statistical validity.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Dataset Provenance:** The data come from a standardized benchmark cohort (GAW14 COGA dataset), and the phenotype recoding rule (`ALDX1 = 5 or ALDX2 = 5` vs. `ALDX1 = 1 or ALDX2 = 1`, yielding 668 affected and 285 unaffected out of 1,614 individuals) and software tools (`MEGA2`, `SIMWALK2`, `PDT 5.1`, `R`) are stated.
* **Missing Details:** Table 1 uses superscript footnotes (`a, b, c, d, e`) on the $p$-values and columns (lines 88–105), but the footnote definitions explaining which test (`a`, `b`, `c`, `d`) produced each $p$-value are omitted from the text. In addition, the exact R package/function and working correlation structure used for GEE, as well as the reconciliation of the number "489 SNPs" in the Abstract with the lower counts in the Results, are missing.
