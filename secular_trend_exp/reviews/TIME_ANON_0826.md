### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This article is a science-policy commentary/perspective written by the Director of the US National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) examining the transition of the US National Institutes of Health (NIH) from the 5-year budget-doubling period (reaching ~$26.7B–$28.6B) into the "post-doubling" era of fiscal constraint (annual budget increases of ~2.0% and ~0.5% against a projected Biomedical Research and Development Price Index [BRDPI] inflation rate of ~3.2% per year). The commentary tabulates institute-by-institute NIH appropriations (Table 1: Total NIH budget $28,035,674k to $28,740,073k; NIAID budget $4,303,040k to $4,459,395k, including ~$2.9B NIH-wide for HIV/AIDS and ~$1.7B for biodefense) and outlines NIAID strategic policy targets: maintaining a floor of $\ge 950$ competing research project grants (RPGs) per year (down from $>1,100$, with projected grant success rates dropping from ~24% to ~18–19%), eliminating annual inflationary adjustments on existing grants, restructuring AIDS clinical trials networks, and leveraging Project BioShield and the NIAID Vaccine Research Center (VRC) for translational countermeasure development.
