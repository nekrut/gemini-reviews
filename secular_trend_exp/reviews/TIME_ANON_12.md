### 1. Executive Summary
This study evaluates the mathematical and empirical properties of MinHash-based alignment-free $p$-distance estimators (Mash, BinDash, and Dashing) and their analytical transformation into evolutionary distances ($d$) for genome-scale phylogenetic reconstruction. Using 24,000 simulated genome sequence pairs (>3 Mb, incorporating realistic Zipf-distributed indels) generated under GTR and GTR+$\Gamma$ models parameterized from 142 prokaryotic genus alignments, followed by phylogenetic benchmarks across 142 multi-taxon reference trees (10–154 taxa) using `JolyTree v2.0`, the author demonstrates three main results: (1) MinHash tools maintain high linear correlation ($r > 0.99$) with true $p$-distances up to $p \approx 0.25\text{--}0.35$ when $k=26$ ($q=10^{-9}$) and sketch proportion $s \ge 0.4$, though exhibiting a systematic upward multiplicative bias ($\hat{p} \approx \beta p$ with $\beta \in [1.08, 1.15]$); (2) this upward bias partially cancels the downward bias of simple Poisson Correction (PC) and Equal-Input (EI / F81) transformations on homogeneous models; and (3) a gamma-corrected Equal-Input formula (Eq. 3) with shape parameter $a \approx 1.5$ improves exact topology recovery from 75.3% (PC) to 83.1% across the 142 simulated phylogenomic datasets.

This is a transparent, well-designed simulation and methodological study with strong open-science practices (all empirical parameter sets, simulated sequences, and reference trees are archived on Zenodo). Notably, the author also candidly identifies and mathematically explains a bug in their own previous software release (`JolyTree v1.1`, which double-transformed Mash's PC output). Minor limitations include reliance on strict binary topology identity rather than continuous tree distances (such as Robinson–Foulds or quartet distances) and tuning the empirical parameter $a \approx 1.5$ on the same 142-tree benchmark used for evaluation.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Realistic Simulation Design:** Unlike many alignment-free benchmarks that simulate short, indel-free sequences under Jukes–Cantor or K80 models, this study grounds its simulations in ML parameter estimates ($\pi$, GTR rate matrices, and $\Gamma$ shape parameters $\alpha \in [0.162, 0.422]$) inferred from 142 real prokaryotic supermatrices, and simulates 5 Mb genomes with insertions and deletions up to 50 kb (`INDELible v1.03`).
* **Characterization of Estimator Bias Cancellation:** The manuscript provides a clear mathematical diagnosis of why naive Poisson Correction in Mash works better than expected on GTR sequences: Mash/BinDash/Dashing overestimate $p$ by $\beta \approx 1.08\text{--}1.15$ (likely due to indel interruptions of $k$-mers and hash collisions/sketch saturation), which mimics the concavity of a rate-heterogeneous transformation (Fig. 4). The author also transparently documents that `JolyTree v1.1` inadvertently applied an F81 logarithm to Mash's already Poisson-corrected distance, yielding $\delta = -b_1 \ln(1 + \ln(1-\hat{p})/b_2)$, which fortuitously approximated Eq. (3) with $a = 1.208$.
* **In-Sample Tuning of Shape Parameter $a$ and Binary Tree Metric:**
  * In Fig. 5, the author sweeps $a \in [0.05, 10.0]$ across the 142 simulated tree sets and concludes that $a \in [1.0, 2.0]$ (specifically $a \approx 1.5$) maximizes the percentage of recovered reference trees (up to 83.1%). Because $a$ is selected by maximizing recovery over the same 142 trees without out-of-sample cross-validation or an independent test set (e.g., eukaryotic or viral datasets with different $\Gamma$ shape distributions or indel rates), the peak recovery rate of 83.1% is slightly optimistic.
  * Assessing phylogenetic accuracy solely via the percentage of 100% identical topologies treats a tree with a single local nearest-neighbor interchange (NNI) on a 150-taxon tree identically to a completely random topology. Reporting normalized Robinson–Foulds (nRF) or branch-score distances would provide much finer resolution.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **MinHash Tools Accurately Estimate $p$-Distances up to $p \approx 0.35$ with Appropriate $k$ and Sketch Size: [Well Supported].** Backed by 24,000 simulated genome pairs across six evolutionary scenarios and 16 $(k, s)$ parameter combinations (Figs. 1–2). The practical boundary (corresponding to ANI $> 65\%$, typical of intra-genus prokaryotic comparisons) is accurately stated.
* **Analytical EI+$\Gamma$ Transformation Approximates GTR+$\Gamma$ Evolutionary Distances: [Well Supported].** Fig. 3 and Eq. (3) demonstrate that a single shape parameter $a$ combined with empirical base frequencies captures the non-linear saturation curve of GTR+$\Gamma$ distances.
* **EI+$\Gamma$ Transformation ($a \approx 1.5$) Improves Alignment-Free Phylogenetic Reconstruction: [Moderately Supported].** Topology recovery improves from 75.3% (PC) and 76.7% (EI) to ~80–83.1% (EI+$\Gamma$, Fig. 5), though no formal paired statistical test (e.g., McNemar's test on recovered topologies or paired Wilcoxon test on Robinson–Foulds distances across the 142 trees) is reported to quantify the significance of the improvement over PC/EI.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** Reproducibility is exemplary. All 14,244 source genome assembly accessions, the 142 prokaryotic supermatrices and ML trees (`zenodo.4034244`), the 24,000 simulated sequence pairs (`zenodo.4034461`), and the 142 simulated multi-taxon datasets (`zenodo.4034643`) are permanently archived on Zenodo under CC-BY 4.0, and exact software versions (`Mash v2.2`, `BinDash v1.0`, `Dashing v0.3.4`, `INDELible v1.03`, `IQ-TREE v1.6.7.2`, `JolyTree v2.0`) and parameters are fully specified.
* **Deficiencies:** Note that in the markdown conversion of the manuscript text, the inline display equations (Equations 1, 2, and 3) were stripped during text extraction, although their functional forms and parameter definitions ($b_1, b_2, a, \delta$) are recoverable from the surrounding text and methods.
