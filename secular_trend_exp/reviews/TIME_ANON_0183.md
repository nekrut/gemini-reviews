### 1. Executive Summary
This study uses PacBio Sequel II long-read sequencing (`Canu` and `Flye`) to assemble the complete circular mitochondrial maxicircles—including both the coding region (`~15 kb`) and the notoriously repetitive variable region (`~9–19 kb`)—for four African trypanosomes (*Trypanosoma congolense* savannah and kilifi, *T. simiae*, and *T. godfreyi*), and combines them with 99 additional short-read maxicircle coding assemblies across *T. brucei* ($n=51$), *T. congolense* ($n=34$), *T. equiperdum* ($n=3$), and *T. vivax* ($n=14$). Comparative genomic and evolutionary analyses reveal: (1) independent deletions of Complex I (`ND1` in *T. godfreyi* and `ND4`/`CR4`/`ND3` in *T. theileri*, matching the non-essentiality of Complex I in bloodstream forms); (2) a strong negative correlation between pre-edited gene length and GC% across six edited genes ($R = -0.57$ to $-0.93$, reflecting historical loss of U-insertion sites in non-salivarian clades); (3) extreme AU codon bias (~80% AT) and synonymous site saturation in non-edited genes; and (4) Bayesian tip-dated molecular clock analyses (`BEAST2`) for *T. brucei* and *T. congolense*. The long-read maxicircle assemblies and comparative RNA-editing/codon-saturation analyses are high-value contributions to kinetoplastid genomics, but the statistical comparison of MCMC posterior distributions and the unvalidated extrapolation of 50-year tip dates to 100–400 kya root ages require methodological correction.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Invalid Hypothesis Testing on Autocorrelated MCMC Posterior Samples (Lines 78 & 178):** To test whether the mean molecular clock rate differs among *T. brucei* ($\mu = 1.83 \times 10^{-7}$), *T. congolense* ($\mu = 4.55 \times 10^{-7}$), and *T. vivax* ($\mu = 3.82 \times 10^{-8}$), the authors take 900 MCMC posterior draws from each `BEAST2` run and apply a frequentist Kruskal-Wallis test ($H_{(2)} = 680, P < 0.001$) followed by pairwise Wilcoxon tests ($P < 0.0001$). Applying a frequentist rank test to $n = 900$ MCMC samples is statistically invalid: sample size $n$ is determined arbitrarily by the user's MCMC chain length and thinning interval (`tracelog = 10,000`), and consecutive draws are autocorrelated realizations from a single posterior distribution, not independent biological observations. Even when the 95% HPD intervals overlap substantially (*T. brucei*: $[3.10 \times 10^{-8}, 3.69 \times 10^{-7}]$ vs *T. congolense*: $[5.48 \times 10^{-8}, 9.57 \times 10^{-7}]$), taking 900 MCMC draws forces $P < 0.0001$. Bayesian parameter comparison must be based on the posterior distribution of the rate difference ($\Pr(\mu_1 > \mu_2 \mid \text{Data})$ or Bayes factors), which here is non-significant because the 95% HPDs heavily overlap!
- **Tip-Dating Extrapolation and Absence of Date-Randomization Tests (DRT):** The `BEAST2` analyses calibrate the molecular clock solely using isolate collection years spanning a ~50-year contemporary window (1960s–2010s) and extrapolate backward by 3–4 orders of magnitude to estimate root ages of ~108 kya (95% HPD: `8–325 kya`) for *T. brucei* and ~401 kya (95% HPD: `29–1,200 kya`) for *T. congolense*. The authors neither perform a root-to-tip regression (`TempEst`) nor a date-randomization test (DRT) to verify that the 50-year sampling window contains genuine temporal signal above phylogenetic clustering, nor do they account for the Time-Dependent Rate Phenomenon (TDRP) coupled with the extreme AU synonymous saturation they themselves document in Fig. 4C.
- **Empty Table Placeholders in Manuscript Text:** In the manuscript markdown (lines 32–39), `Table 1` and `Table 2` appear as empty section headers without their tabular contents, although their values are summarized in the surrounding Results prose.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Independent losses of NADH dehydrogenase (Complex I) subunits (`ND1` in *T. godfreyi*; `ND3`/`ND4`/`CR4` in *T. theileri*) and widespread non-frameshifting indels in pan-edited genes demonstrate relaxed selection on Complex I and tolerance of pre-edited length variation in salivarian trypanosomes.* `[Well Supported]` — Directly proven by long-read single-molecule spans across the coding region and PCR/re-mapping verification (`nd1` absence in *T. godfreyi* and *T. brucei* Kenyan isolate `W913`).
- **Claim 2:** *Salivarian trypanosomes retain shorter, more GC-rich pre-edited genes (requiring more extensive U-insertion RNA editing) than non-salivarian clades, while non-edited coding sequences exhibit ~80% AT content and rapid synonymous saturation.* `[Well Supported]` — Strongly supported by linear regressions of gene length vs GC% across 6 pan-edited genes (Fig. 3, $p \le 0.002$) and codon position base composition and $d_N/d_S$ saturation curves (Fig. 4).
- **Claim 3:** *The substitution rates of *T. brucei*, *T. congolense*, and *T. vivax* are significantly different ($H = 680, P < 0.001$), and the *T. congolense* savannah–kilifi divergence dates to ~401 kya while *T. brucei* crown diversification dates to ~108 kya.* `[Fragile]` — The $P < 0.001$ rate difference is an artifact of testing $n=900$ MCMC draws (the 95% HPDs of *T. brucei* and *T. congolense* overlap across most of their range), and the deep root age estimates span 1.5 orders of magnitude (`29–1,200 kya` and `8–325 kya`) without DRT validation.
