### 1. Executive Summary
This is a thorough, mechanistically insightful functional genomics and viral epigenetics study dissecting how Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) nuclear antigens `EBNA 2` and the `EBNA 3` family (`EBNA 3A`, `3B`, and `3C`) cooperatively or antagonistically reprogram host B-cell transcription via distal enhancers and promoter-enhancer chromatin looping. By integrating `EBNA 2` and pan-`EBNA 3` ChIP-seq in latency III `Mutu III` Burkitt lymphoma (BL) cells (`GSE47629`; 21,605 `EBNA 2` peaks and 7,044 `EBNA 3` peaks at `MACS p < 10^-7`) with ENCODE `GM12878` histone/TF tracks and published transcriptomic datasets, the authors show that $75\%$ of `EBNA 2` sites and $84\%$ of `EBNA 3` sites are distal ($>4\text{ kb}$) to TSSs, $25\%$ of all peaks are co-occupied by both `EBNA 2` and `EBNA 3`s, and $80\%$ of genes nearest an `EBNA 3` peak are also targeted by `EBNA 2`. Using isoform-specific ChIP-qPCR, sequential `re-ChIP`, chromosome conformation capture (`3C`), and genetic knock-out/conditional-LCL models across five representative loci (`CTBP2`, `WEE1`, `ITGAL`, `BCL2L11/Bim`, and `ADAM28/ADAMDEC1`), they demonstrate two distinct modes of `EBNA 3`-mediated enhancer repression: (1) "anti-looping" at `CTBP2`, where `EBNA 3A/3B/3C` compete with `EBNA 2` at a `+95 kb` intronic enhancer and block active enhancer-promoter loop formation (which is restored in `EBNA 3A KO` LCLs); and (2) "repressive looping" at `WEE1` (`+27 kb` and `+39–44 kb` enhancers) and `ADAM28/ADAMDEC1`, where `EBNA 3C` actively drives enhancer-promoter loop formation required for transcriptional repression. Furthermore, they show that gene- and cell-type-specific repression by individual `EBNA 3` family members (e.g., `ITGAL` repressed by `EBNA 3B + 3C` in BL cells vs. `EBNA 3B` alone in LCLs) directly mirrors their locus- and cell-type-specific recruitment in vivo.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Transparent Characterization and Validation of Antibody Specificity (`Fig. S2` & `Fig. S8`):** The authors transparently demonstrate in `Fig. S2` that the sheep polyclonal anti-`EBNA 3C` antibody (`Abcam ab16128`) used for genome-wide ChIP-seq cross-reacts with and immunoprecipitates `EBNA 3A` and `EBNA 3B` in single-factor BJAB transfectants (while not precipitating `TBP`). Rather than over-interpreting the ChIP-seq track as `EBNA 3C`-exclusive, they treat the ChIP-seq dataset as a pan-`EBNA 3` map and systematically validate every focal locus (`CTBP2`, `WEE1`, `ITGAL`, `BCL2L11`, `ADAM28/ADAMDEC1`) in both `Mutu III` BL cells and `PER253 B95.8` LCLs by ChIP-qPCR using non-cross-reactive monospecific antibodies for `EBNA 2` (`PE2`), `EBNA 3A` (`F115P`), `EBNA 3B` (`F120P`), and `EBNA 3C` (`E3cD8`, validated in `Fig. S8`), always including flanking negative primers (`sets A, C, E, G, I`), `PPIA` background controls, and `CTBP2` positive controls on the same plates.
- **Reciprocal Sequential `Re-ChIP` Controls (`Figs. 4E–H, 6F–G, 8C–E, S11`):** To test whether co-incident `EBNA 2` and `EBNA 3` ChIP-seq peaks reflect simultaneous co-occupancy on the same DNA molecule or mutually exclusive competitive binding across different cells/alleles in the population, the authors perform primary `EBNA 2` ChIP followed by secondary `EBNA 2, 3A, 3B, or 3C` `re-ChIP` AND reciprocal primary `EBNA 3A/3B/3C` ChIP followed by secondary `EBNA 2` `re-ChIP` (`Fig. S11`), together with positive homo-re-precipitation controls for each antibody. This rigorously proves mutually exclusive occupancy at `CTBP2`, `WEE1`, and `ITGAL`.
- **Genetic Knock-Out, Revertant, and Conditional (`ER/EB 2.5`) Models:** Transcriptional and `3C` looping phenotypes are validated across multiple independent donor backgrounds and isogenic viral genetics: `EBNA 3A KO` LCLs (`mtB1, B2, B3` across donors `D2` and `D3`), `EBNA 3B KO` LCLs (`PER142` and `PER253`), `BL31` cells infected with wild-type (`wtBac-2, 3`), `EBNA 3C KO` (`3CKO-3, 6`), `EBNA 3C` revertant (`3Crev-2, 4`), or `EBNA 2 KO` BACs, and $\beta$-estradiol withdrawal/re-addition in `ER/EB 2.5` LCLs.
- **Methodological Limitations:**
  1. **Cross-Cell-Line Epigenomic Proxy (`Mutu III` BL vs. `GM12878` and `IB4` LCLs):** In Figure 2 and Figures S3–S7, `EBNA 2` and `EBNA 3` ChIP-seq peaks mapped in `Mutu III` (a Burkitt lymphoma line) are intersected with ENCODE histone modification and TF ChIP-seq tracks from `GM12878` and `IB4` (lymphoblastoid cell lines). While both express latency III EBV proteins (`Fig. S1`), the authors themselves demonstrate later in the paper (`Figs. 5 and 7`) that `EBNA 3` binding and gene expression (e.g., `CTBP2` silencing in `BL31`/`BL2`, `EBNA 3C` binding to `ITGAL` in `Mutu III` BL but not in LCLs) differ significantly between BL and LCL backgrounds.
  2. **Qualitative End-Point Gel Readout for Chromosome Conformation Capture (`3C`) and $n=2$ ChIP-qPCR Replicates:** The `3C` looping assays (`Figs. 4B–C, 6B–D, 11B, 11D`) include appropriate intervening restriction-fragment negative controls (`con`, `con1`, `con2`) and in vitro digested/ligated PCR template positive controls, but are read out as qualitative end-point agarose gels without TaqMan/SYBR `3C-qPCR` crosslinking-frequency quantification or an invariant housekeeping locus loading control. Additionally, most ChIP-qPCR bar graphs (`Figs. 3, 5, 7, 9, 10`) plot the `mean +/- range of two independent ChIP experiments` ($n=2$), which precludes formal inferential statistics on the ChIP-qPCR comparisons.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *`EBNA 2` and `EBNA 3` proteins extensively co-target distal enhancer elements in the human genome and compete for occupancy rather than binding simultaneously.* `[Well Supported]` — Supported by genome-wide ChIP-seq overlap ($25\%$ shared peaks, $80\%$ shared nearest genes) and reciprocal `re-ChIP` at `CTBP2`, `WEE1`, and `ITGAL`.
- **Claim 2:** *`EBNA 3` proteins repress target gene transcription through two distinct chromatin-looping mechanisms: preventing active enhancer-promoter looping at `CTBP2` ("anti-looping") versus actively establishing repressive enhancer-promoter loops at `WEE1` and `ADAM28/ADAMDEC1`.* `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated via `3C` assays across WT, `EBNA 3A KO`, `EBNA 3C KO`, revertant, and `BJAB-E3C` lines, correlated with RT-qPCR transcript levels.
- **Claim 3:** *Gene- and cell-type-specific regulation by `EBNA 3A`, `3B`, and `3C` is determined by their differential recruitment to target promoters and enhancers in vivo.* `[Well Supported]` — Elegantly demonstrated across `CTBP2` (`3A/3B/3C`), `WEE1` (`3C` dominant), `ITGAL` (`3B + 3C` in BL vs. `3B` only in LCL), `BCL2L11` (`3A + 3C`), and `ADAM28/ADAMDEC1` (`3A + 3C`).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Public Data Deposition & Complete Primer Tables:** All ChIP-seq raw and processed data are publicly deposited in NCBI GEO under accession `GSE47629`. All ChIP-qPCR, RT-qPCR, `3C` ligation-junction, and reporter-cloning primer sequences are provided in `Table S1`, and antibody clones, catalog numbers, and cell line provenances are thoroughly documented in Methods.
