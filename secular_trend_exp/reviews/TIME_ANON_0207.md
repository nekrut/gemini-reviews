### 1. Executive Summary
This biochemical, cytological, and genetic study in *Drosophila melanogaster* establishes the functional equivalence of the *Drosophila* Glued complex to the vertebrate dynactin complex ($p150^{\text{Glued}}$) and demonstrates its direct functional interaction with the cytoplasmic dynein motor heavy chain (*Dhc64C*). The authors show that wild-type Glued polypeptides co-sediment as a 20S complex and co-purify with microtubules, whereas the dominant *Glued$^1$* ($Gl^1$) mutation encodes a truncated polypeptide that retains microtubule binding but fails to incorporate into the 20S dynactin complex (providing a clear molecular mechanism for its dominant-negative phenotype). Immunocytochemistry during oogenesis reveals that Glued localization mirrors cytoplasmic dynein and depends on *Dhc64C* and other genes required for dynein localization. Finally, dominant genetic interaction screens show that specific *Dhc64C* alleles act as dominant suppressors or enhancers of the *Gl$^1$* rough-eye phenotype, and that a previously isolated extragenic suppressor of *Gl$^1$* (*Su(Gl)102*) is an allele of *Dhc64C*.
