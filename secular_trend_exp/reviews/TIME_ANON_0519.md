### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This plant biotechnology mini-review synthesizes strategies for engineering crop disease resistance against viral, bacterial, and fungal pathogens using CRISPR/Cas genome editing (NHEJ knockouts, HDR allele replacement/knock-in, cytosine/adenine base editing, prime editing, and Cas9/Cas13 viral targeting) alongside host-induced gene silencing (HIGS) and cross-kingdom RNAi disruption. The authors organize resistance breeding approaches into transgene-free modifications (knocking out host susceptibility genes such as `MLO`, `SWEET`, `DMR6`, and `eIF4E`; editing effector-binding cis-elements in `OsSWEET11/13/14` and `CsLOB1` promoters; synonymous codon rewriting of host defense mRNAs targeted by fungal cross-kingdom sRNAs; and amino acid substitutions at effector protease cleavage or phosphorylation sites such as `PBS1`/`BIK1`/`RIN4`) and transgenic approaches (safe-harbor HDR knock-in of NLR helper/sensor pairs or inter-family PRRs, and constitutive Cas9/Cas13 or artificial miRNA expression against plant viruses). Because this is a conceptual review rather than a primary experimental paper, it contains no original empirical data or statistical tests and is evaluated on the biological accuracy, balance, and completeness of its synthesis.

### 2. STRENGTHS
- **Nuanced Treatment of Growth-Defense Trade-Offs and Hormonal Antagonism:** Unlike superficial CRISPR reviews that present susceptibility-gene knockouts as cost-free panaceas, the authors consistently highlight pleiotropic fitness penalties (e.g., dwarfism/senescence in `MLO` and `SEC3A` knockouts, yield loss in rice `CYP71A1` knockouts, dominant-negative immunity defects in `BIK1 G230A/D231A`, and salicylic acid vs. jasmonic acid antagonism between biotrophic and necrotrophic pathogen defenses when targeting `WRKY70`/`WRKY75`).
- **Clear Mechanistic Taxonomy of Editing Modalities Beyond Simple NHEJ Knockouts:** Clearly distinguishes frameshift NHEJ knockouts from promoter cis-element editing (`TALE` effector binding sites), synonymous codon rewriting to evade fungal `AGO1`-loaded cross-kingdom sRNAs, single-residue protease/phosphorylation site editing (`RIN4 T166A`), and Cas9 vs. Cas13 viral genome cleavage.

### 3. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Optimistic Feasibility Claims for HDR and Prime Editing in Polyploid Crops:** In the Abstract and Sections 2–4 (lines 23, 35, 58, Figure 3C), the authors emphasize rewriting 20-bp cross-kingdom sRNA target sites via HDR or prime editing across crop genomes. While acknowledged briefly in passing, HDR and prime editing efficiencies in non-model polyploid crops (especially across all homeoalleles simultaneously without selectable markers) remain orders of magnitude lower than NHEJ or base editing, and no empirical example of successful CRISPR-mediated synonymous rewriting of a fungal cross-kingdom sRNA target site in a crop is cited.
- **Truncated Presentation of Table 1 in the Text:** In the Concluding Remarks (line 34), the authors state *"To date CRISPR technology has been deployed mainly to inactivate host susceptibility factors and address fungal (18 reports) viral (16 reports), and bacterial (10 reports) diseases, counting only the studies selected for Table 1"*, yet Table 1 in the manuscript text (lines 16–30 of chunk 2) displays only 10 rows from the first sub-header (*"CRISPR/Cas-approaches increasing resistance by direct targeting of viral genomes"*), omitting the remaining ~34 fungal, bacterial, and host-susceptibility entries.
- **Duplicate Bullet-Point Block in Text:** Lines 1–9 of the second text chunk repeat the four numbered comparison points (`1) miRNAs/RNAi... 4) At least some plant viruses...`) verbatim as unnumbered paragraphs immediately below the numbered list.

### 4. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **CRISPR/Cas NHEJ knockout of recessive susceptibility genes (`MLO`, `eIF4E`, `DMR6`) and promoter `TALE`-binding elements (`OsSWEET11/13/14`, `CsLOB1`) confers heritable pathogen resistance in crops:** `[Well Supported]` — Grounded in multiple replicated primary studies across hexaploid wheat, rice, tomato, citrus, and cucumber.
- **Knockout of negative immune regulators or central transcription factors (`WRKY70`, `SEC3A`, `CYP71A1`) frequently incurs growth/yield penalties or reciprocal susceptibility via SA–JA antagonism:** `[Well Supported]` — Accurately synthesized from physiological and field-trial literature.
- **Synonymous rewriting of host defense transcripts via HDR or prime editing is a practical route to block fungal cross-kingdom RNAi in crops:** `[Moderately Supported]` — Mechanistically sound in principle based on *Botrytis cinerea* and *Verticillium dahliae* sRNA effector studies, but currently a theoretical proposal without published crop proof-of-concept.
