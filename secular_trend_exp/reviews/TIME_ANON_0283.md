### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript is a brief conference report summarizing selected oral and poster presentations from the Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory Retroviruses meeting (May 23–28). It synthesizes seven primary research highlights across four areas of retrovirology: (1) envelope-mediated membrane fusion pore formation (Lorraine Albritton's demonstration that His8 in the SU subunit of ecotropic Moloney MuLV is required for the transition from hemifusion to fusion pore opening); (2) early post-entry cytoplasmic trafficking (Thomas Hope's live-cell tracking of Vpr-GFP-labeled HIV-1 cores along taxol-stabilized microtubules); (3) Gag assembly in plasma membrane lipid rafts and Late (L)-domain-mediated budding via the ubiquitin/ESCRT pathway (Eric Freed and Christopher Aiken on HIV-1 Gag/PLAP co-fractionation in detergent-resistant lipid rafts; Heinrich Göttlinger on L-domain-induced Gag ubiquitination; and Lynn VerPlank on HIV-1 PTAPP binding to the E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme homolog Tsg101 vs. RSV PPPPY); (4) structural and functional alanine-scanning mutagenesis of the HIV-1 capsid (CA) hexameric lattice (Wesley Sundquist's 43 surface alanine mutants across the NTD and CTD); and (5) human endogenous retrovirus HERV-K evolution, polymorphism, and oncogenesis (Jack Lenz on HERV-K108/108B LTR evolutionary groups and `YXDD` RT active-site variants; Annette Boese on HERV-K cORF/Rec transforming activity in nude mice and yeast two-hybrid interaction with PLZF). As a secondary meeting report rather than a primary research article, it accurately and concisely communicates landmark mechanistic discoveries in retrovirology, though it contains no primary experimental data, statistical tables, or bibliography.
