**Technology Maturity:** Commodity/Hype-Cycle Template
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript profiles the expression of Notch signaling pathway components and candidate regulatory microRNAs across five molecular subtypes of stage I (`T1N0M0`) breast cancer from 405 Polish women—luminal A ($n=130$), HER2-negative luminal B ($n=100$), HER2-positive luminal B ($n=96$), non-luminal HER2-positive ($n=36$), and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC, $n=43$)—paired with adjacent normal tissue controls. Using Affymetrix HG-U133A 2.0 mRNA microarrays, RT-qPCR, commercial tissue-homogenate ELISAs, Affymetrix miRNA 2.0 microarrays coupled with `miRDB` target prediction (score $\ge 80$), and external survival queries in `KMplotter`, the authors report that 9 Notch-related genes (`APH1A`, `CTBP1`, `HEY1`, `HEY2`, `JAG2`, `NOTCH4` upregulated; `DTX1`, `TLE2`, `TLE4` downregulated) are consistently dysregulated across all five subtypes at both the mRNA and protein levels, and propose inverse regulatory relationships with five miRNAs (`miR-145`, `miR-98`, `miR-381`, `miR-196a`, `miR-155`).

Despite a superficially large clinical sample size ($N=405$) and multi-modal assay design (mRNA microarray, RT-qPCR, ELISA, miRNA microarray), a close forensic audit of the reported data tables and text reveals severe statistical anomalies, direct internal contradictions between the Results and Discussion, and missing repository depositions for the high-throughput arrays.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Statistically Implausible / Ultra-Tight Standard Deviations in Tissue ELISA Data (Table 4):**
  * In Section 2.7, the authors state that the RT-qPCR and ELISA data were non-normally distributed (by Shapiro–Wilk test), justifying non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis and Dunn's tests. Yet in Table 4 (and Fig. 3), they report parametric `mean ± SD` values for protein concentrations (`ng/mL`) across up to 130 human tumor samples per group.
  * Inspecting the coefficients of variation ($\text{CV} = \text{SD} / \text{Mean}$) in Table 4 reveals biologically implausible uniformity across primary human breast tumors:
    * For `APH1A` in Luminal A ($n=130$ patients): $18.34 \pm 0.19\text{ ng/mL}$ (**$\text{CV} = 1.04\%$**!). Across all 5 subtypes ($n=36\text{ to }130$), the CVs for `APH1A` are $1.04\%, 1.35\%, 1.29\%, 1.66\%, \text{ and } 1.16\%$.
    * For `JAG2` in TNBC ($n=43$ patients): $10.83 \pm 0.21\text{ ng/mL}$ (**$\text{CV} = 1.94\%$**).
    * Even if the authors mislabeled Standard Error of the Mean (SEM) as SD, an SEM of $0.21$ for $n=43$ corresponds to an SD of $0.21 \times \sqrt{43} = 1.38\text{ ng/mL}$ ($\text{CV} = 12.7\%$), whereas in Luminal A ($n=130$) vs. TNBC ($n=43$) the reported `±` numbers are nearly identical ($0.19$ vs. $0.31$ for `APH1A`; $0.13$ vs. $0.21$ for `JAG2`; $0.13$ vs. $0.21$ for `NOTCH4`) instead of scaling by $\sqrt{130/43} \approx 1.74$. Furthermore, for `DTX1`, `TLE2`, and `TLE4`, all 405 tumor samples across all five subtypes are reported as uniformly `"below detection threshold *"` with $p < 0.05$, without specifying the kit limit of detection (LOD), how values below LOD were numerically imputed for the Kruskal–Wallis test, or how protein concentrations (`ng/mL` of homogenate) were normalized to total extracted tissue protein (`mg` total protein).
* **Direct Contradictions Between Results (Section 3.5) and Discussion (Section 4) on Survival Outcomes:**
  * In **Section 3.5 (Results, p. 199–207)**, the `KMplotter` overall survival (OS) findings are reported as:
    1. *"In luminal A cancer, **low** expression of CTBP1, HEY1, HEY2 was associated with worse OS (Figure 4)."* (Note: these three genes are **upregulated** in tumors in Tables 3–4, meaning tumor upregulation is associated with *better* survival in Luminal A!).
    2. *"In TNBC, **high** expression of TLE4 was associated with worse OS (Figure 8)."* (Note: `TLE4` is **downregulated** by $-5.18$-fold in TNBC in Table 3, meaning its downregulation in tumors is associated with *better* survival!).
    3. For `JAG2` in TNBC, Section 3.5 reports **no association** with OS (only `TLE4` is significant in TNBC).
  * Yet in **Section 4 (Discussion, p. 218)**, the authors write: *"Moreover, **high JAG2 levels were also associated with poorer OS in TNBC patients**"*—directly contradicting Section 3.5! They also completely ignore the paradox that the survival associations they *did* find in Section 3.5 (`HEY1`, `HEY2`, `CTBP1` in Luminal A; `TLE4` in TNBC) run in the **exact opposite direction** of their oncogenic hypothesis.
* **Improper Statistical Modeling of Paired Tumor–Normal Tissue and Unquantified miRNA–mRNA Associations:**
  * In Section 2.1, the authors state that *"Fresh tumor and adjacent non-tumor tissues"* were collected from the 405 patients, and *"Samples of adjacent healthy tissue served as controls."* However, they never state how many control samples were profiled on the microarrays/ELISAs, and they analyze the data using an **unpaired** one-way ANOVA / Kruskal–Wallis test rather than accounting for within-patient tumor–normal pairing.
  * In Section 3.4 (Table 5), the link between the 5 miRNAs (`miR-145`, `miR-98`, `miR-381`, `miR-196a`, `miR-155`) and the 3 mRNAs (`HEY1`, `JAG2`, `TLE4`) is established purely by juxtaposed group-level fold changes and an *in silico* `miRDB` score $\ge 80$. Despite having paired mRNA and miRNA microarray profiles from the same 405 patients, the authors never compute patient-level correlation coefficients (Pearson $r$ or Spearman $\rho$) between the expression of `miR-145` and `HEY1` (or `miR-98`/`miR-381` and `JAG2`) across the 405 tumors!
* **Cohort Salami-Slicing:** In Section 3.2 (p. 121), the authors note that 10 Notch pathway genes met their universal cutoff across all subtypes, but state: *"DVL3 has already been described in this cohort in another published work and was therefore omitted from this analysis [21]"*, while in Section 3.5 (p. 182) they still refer to *"combining all ten common Notch-related genes into a single signature"*.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Transcriptional and Protein Dysregulation of 9 Notch-Related Genes Across Five Subtypes: [Fragile].** While mRNA fold-changes (Table 3) and RT-qPCR primers (Table 2) are reported, the unexplained ultra-low variance in the ELISA data (Table 4), lack of total-protein normalization in tissue homogenates, and complete absence of raw array data undermine confidence in the quantitative estimates.
* **Post-Transcriptional Regulation of `HEY1`, `JAG2`, and `TLE4` by Candidate miRNAs: [Overstated].** Even as a correlative hypothesis, the authors failed to compute sample-level correlations between the candidate miRNAs and target mRNAs within their 405-patient cohort, relying solely on group-level opposing fold-changes and `miRDB` database lookups without functional validation.
* **Shared Notch-Driven Oncogenic Signature Linked to Poorer Prognosis: [Methodologically Flawed].** The multi-gene signature did not reach statistical significance for survival (`Figure S1`), and the individual gene survival trends reported in Section 3.5 (`low HEY1/HEY2/CTBP1` and `high TLE4` predicting worse OS) contradict the direction of tumor dysregulation (`high HEY1/HEY2/CTBP1` and `low TLE4`), while the Discussion fabricates or misstates a `JAG2` TNBC survival association not present in Section 3.5.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** RT-qPCR primer sequences (Table 2), Affymetrix probeset IDs (Table 3), and commercial reagent/ELISA kit catalog numbers are explicitly listed.
* **Deficiencies:**
  1. **No Public Deposition of Microarray Data:** Despite claiming to have generated hundreds of Affymetrix HG-U133A 2.0 mRNA microarrays and Affymetrix miRNA 2.0 arrays, the *Data Availability Statement* says only *"All data generated or analyzed during this study are included in this published article."* No NCBI GEO or EMBL-EBI ArrayExpress accession numbers are provided, violating standard MIAME/MINSEQE data sharing requirements.
  2. The exact number of control samples ($n$) analyzed by microarray, RT-qPCR, and ELISA is never explicitly stated in the Methods or Table 1/3/4 headers.
