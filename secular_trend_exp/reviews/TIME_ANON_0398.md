### 1. Executive Summary
This comprehensive phylogenomic and molecular evolutionary study reconstructs the evolutionary history of the vertebrate tetraspanin superfamily across >23 deuterostome genomes (identifying 38 modern bony vertebrate ortholog lineages derived from 17 pre-2R-WGD ancestral lineages, plus a 23-gene *TSPAN4* syntenic cluster in amphioxus *Branchiostoma floridae*). By integrating ME, MP, ML, and MrBayes protein phylogenies with Large Extracellular Loop (LEL) cysteine-pattern motifs (4-cys, 6-cys-a/b1/b2/c, 8-cys), SPLIGN intron positions, and UCSC genomic synteny, the authors distinguish 2R-WGD ohnologs from small-scale duplications (SSDs). They show that 76% (13/17) of ancestral lineages retained $\ge 2$ ohnologs (accounting for 32/38 bony vertebrate lineages, ~3$\times$ the vertebrate genomic average), yet total family size remained unchanged (~33 in humans vs. 39 in amphioxus and 33 in *Ciona*) because 2R-WGD ohnologs displaced lineage-specific SSDs. Applying PAML 4 branch-site Model A (LRT against null Model A with $\omega_2 = 1$, $\chi_1^2$ distribution, Hochberg multiple-testing correction), parametric `EvolveNSsite` null simulations (170 branches at $1\times$ and $1.5\times$ branch lengths to rule out $d_S$ saturation artifacts), and protein rate-shift tests (`DIVERGE2` and `RASER2`), the authors demonstrate that positive selection acted on 78% (28/36) of post-2R-WGD duplication branches vs. 31–53% of subsequent speciation branches, and identify domain-specific compensatory positive selection in non-TM regions of mammalian *ROM1* following the loss of *RDS2*.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Multi-Modal Orthology/Ohnology Resolution:** Because inter-subfamily amino acid $p$-distances reach $0.75–0.90$, sequence trees alone cannot resolve deep nodes. Combining LEL cysteine signatures (Table 1), conserved intron-phase/position mapping (`SPLIGN`), and multi-paralogon chromosomal synteny (e.g., quadruplication of the chordate *TSPAN4* cluster in Figure 3) provides a rigorous foundation for partitioning the 38 vertebrate lineages into 17 pre-2R-WGD super-lineages.
* **Stringent Branch-Site Testing and Saturation Controls:** The positive selection audit in Sections 2.9–2.10 is unusually thorough:
  1. The authors use the strict branch-site Test 2 (Model A vs. Model A with $\omega_2 = 1$ fixed) evaluated against a conservative $\chi_1^2$ distribution (rather than the $50:50$ mixture $\chi_0^2 : \chi_1^2$) with multiple starting values to avoid local optima.
  2. They verify invariance under F61 vs. F3x4 codon frequency models, removal of invertebrate outgroups, and exclusion of all deep branches with $d_S > 1.5$ or $d_S > 1.2$.
  3. They run parametric neutral/nearly-neutral simulations (`EvolveNSsite` under M1a across 13 families $\times$ 5 replicates $= 170$ branches, both at empirical branch lengths and scaled by $1.5\times$), observing 0/170 (0%) and 2/170 (1.2%) false positives, directly refuting $d_S$-saturation-driven LRT inflation.
  4. They corroborate codon-level findings with amino-acid covarion/rate-shift models (`DIVERGE2` and `RASER2`).
* **Asymmetric Family-Wise Error Rate (FWER) Grouping Across Branch Categories:** In Section 2.9 (line 101) and Table 2, Hochberg's step-up correction is applied by treating all duplication branches in a tree as one hypothesis family, all 3 serial segments of the mammalian lineage (before tetrapods, before amniotes, before mammals) as one family, and the single teleost branch as a family of size 1. Grouping 2–5 duplication branches into a Hochberg family penalizes individual duplication branches more heavily than the single unsplit teleost branch, yet when comparing the proportion of trees/families or pooled branches ($28/36 = 78\%$ vs. $27/86 = 31\%$ for individual mammal segments, Fisher's exact $p = 3.0 \times 10^{-6}$), one must keep in mind that the 3 consecutive segments leading to mammals are shorter evolutionary intervals than the unsplit teleost branch (where $16/30 = 53\%$ were significant at $p < 0.05$, Fisher's exact $p = 0.065$, and $56\%$ vs. $23\%$ at $p < 0.01$, $p = 0.012$).
* **Post-Hoc Partitioning of TM vs. Non-TM Regions in Mammalian *ROM1*:** Splitting the *RDS*/*ROM1* alignment into transmembrane (TM) and non-TM partitions was motivated after inspecting BEB sites from the full-length alignment (line 145). While biologically well justified (LEL mediates protein-protein interactions with RDS in photoreceptor outer segments, whereas TM helices undergo high $d_S$ in mammals), post-hoc partitioning after seeing BEB output slightly inflates nominal LRT significance.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **High 2R-WGD ohnolog retention (76% of ancestral lineages) reshaped family composition without expanding total family size:** `[Well Supported]` — The contrast between amphioxus (14 single-copy conserved deuterostome lineages + 25 species-specific SSDs $= 39$ genes) and bony vertebrates (32 ohnologs from 13 conserved lineages + minimal SSDs $= 33$ genes in human) nicely supports the dosage-balance hypothesis.
* **Elevated positive selection on post-2R-WGD duplication branches relative to subsequent speciation branches:** `[Well Supported]` — Supported by conservative branch-site LRTs, site proportion estimates (mean 18.4% vs. 7.2–9.6%), $d_S$-censoring sensitivity analyses, neutral simulations, and amino-acid rate-shift tests.
* **Compensatory evolution of mammalian *ROM1* triggered by loss of *RDS2*:** `[Moderately Supported]` — The temporal coincidence between *RDS2* loss on the stem mammalian branch and intense non-TM positive selection on mammalian *ROM1* (48% of non-TM sites, 42 BEB sites $>95\%$) is an intriguing and biologically plausible hypothesis given RDS/ROM1 heterotetramerization in photoreceptor discs, though functional rescue experiments would be required to prove causality.
