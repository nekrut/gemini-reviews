### 1. Executive Summary
This study provides a landmark empirical and phylogenetic stress-test of mitochondrial COI DNA barcoding for both specimen identification (assigning unknowns to a known reference library) and species discovery (delineating novel taxa via genetic distance thresholds). To overcome the severe intraspecific and sister-species undersampling of early barcoding demonstrations, the authors analyze a globally comprehensive dataset of 2,026 sequenced individuals across 263 Evolutionarily Significant Units (ESUs) and 233 traditionally recognized species (>93% global species coverage) of marine gastropods in the family Cypraeidae (cowries), supplemented by two exhaustively sampled comparative gastropod clades: *Patelloida profunda* group limpets ($n=125$, 12 ESUs) and *Astralium rhodostomum* complex turbinids ($n=278$, 30 ESUs).

The authors evaluate barcoding across a $2 \times 2$ design (Traditional morphological species vs. integrative reciprocally monophyletic ESUs $\times$ Tree-based exemplar identification vs. Distance-threshold discovery). They demonstrate that:
1. **Specimen Identification:** Against single-exemplar NJ/parsimony trees, identification is 98% accurate for ESUs (with 2% error due to three-taxon exemplar rooting artifacts and ~2% real biological error from introgression, plus 0–8% unresolved young polyphyletic species, yielding a 4–12% true empirical error floor), but drops to 80% for traditional morphological species (8% incorrect, 12% ambiguous) due to paraphyly/overlumping (10%) and polyphyly/incomplete lineage sorting (9%).
2. **Species Discovery via Thresholds:** Across cowries, intraspecific variation (measured by pairwise K2P, $\theta$, and clock-constrained GTR+$\Gamma$ maximum coalescent node depth) overlaps extensively with sister-taxon interspecific divergence, refuting the existence of a universal "barcoding gap." Minimizing cumulative false-positive + false-negative error yields a best-case 17% error rate in cowrie ESUs (at an optimal 2.6% pairwise / 0.013 nodal depth threshold) and 33% error in traditional cowrie species (at a 5% threshold). Applying the widely cited "$10\times$ mean intraspecific variation" rule (~8% threshold in cowries) results in a 34% false-negative error rate.

This is a conceptually rigorous, empirically exhaustive, and statistically transparent study that definitively demonstrates how geographical/taxonomic scale and incomplete lineage sorting govern barcoding error rates.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Sampling Depth & Bias Control:** Unlike regional checklists that artificially inflate interspecific distances by omitting allopatric sister species, this dataset covers >93% of global cowrie diversity, with $\ge 2$ individuals in 82% (216) of ESUs, $\ge 5$ in 54% (143), and $\ge 10$ in 23% (60), deliberately sampling maximally distant geographic populations. In the exemplar identification resampling test ($n=1,000$ queries), representation was capped at 10–15 sequences per taxon to prevent heavily sampled species from dominating accuracy estimates.
* **Coalescent Depth vs. Pairwise Distance Metrics:** The authors appropriately distinguish between (1) raw pairwise K2P distances (which weight heavily sampled taxa quadratically), (2) taxon-specific mean pairwise distance $\theta$, and (3) maximum coalescent node depth estimated by inserting the two most divergent haplotypes per ESU into a molecular-clock-constrained GTR+$\Gamma$ ML phylogeny (note: node depth is half the pairwise divergence, so a 0.013 node depth equals a 2.6% pairwise threshold).
* **Molecular Clock Assumption:** The authors transparently perform a likelihood ratio test (LRT) of clock-like evolution under GTR+$\Gamma$ on COI; the molecular clock was not rejected for turbinids or limpets ($p > 0.05$) but was marginally rejected for cowries ($p = 0.007$). While enforcing a global clock in PAUP* to extract ultrametric node depths is a reasonable approximation for comparing intra-ESU coalescent vs. sister-ESU split depths on the same tree, local rate variation could slightly broaden the overlap distribution; acknowledging relaxed-clock alternatives would be a minor refinement.
* **Explicit Accounting of Circularity in ESUs:** Because ESUs were defined using reciprocal monophyly at COI alongside morphology and biogeography, the authors explicitly state that ESU-based performance represents a theoretical upper bound ("best of situations") that excludes young, valid biological species still polyphyletic at COI (8% of traditional species). Reporting both traditional species and ESUs side-by-side cleanly brackets real-world performance.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1:** *"Whereas proponents envision a 'barcoding gap' between the two [intraspecific variation and interspecific divergence], we find substantial overlap, leading to minimal error rates of ~17% in cowries. Moreover, error rates double [33%] if only traditionally recognized species are analyzed."* — **[Well Supported]**. Directly demonstrated by the empirical distributions of coalescent depth and sister-taxon divergence across 263 cowrie ESUs and 233 traditional species (Figures 7 and 9) and sensitivity analyses across sample size cutoffs ($n \ge 2, 5, 10$).
* **Claim 2:** *"Taxa that lack planktonic larvae and consequently have restricted dispersal and narrow ranges, have a smaller mean theta (0.0029) than cowries that possess planktonic larvae (0.0070)."* — **[Well Supported]**. Biologically grounded in marine metapopulation genetics ($N_e$ and geographic structure) and corroborated by the lower $\theta$ values in non-feeding larval turbinids and limpets.
* **Claim 3:** *"Regional studies undersample the most closely related species, which are frequently allopatric, and thus underestimate global error rates. For instance, a barcoding gap does exist if only cowries from the island of Moorea were investigated."* — **[Well Supported]**. Provides a direct mechanistic explanation for why early regional studies (e.g., North American birds) observed an apparent barcoding gap that vanishes under global clade-wide sampling (where 79% of sister ESUs lumped at a 3% cutoff are allopatric).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
