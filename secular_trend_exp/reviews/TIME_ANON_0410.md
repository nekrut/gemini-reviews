### 1. Executive Summary
This computational and theoretical population genetics study evaluates the statistical power and false-discovery rate of single-generation selection-components analysis (comparing adult vs. pre-viability offspring allele frequencies via sliding-window kernel-smoothed $F'_{ST}$) to detect quantitative trait loci (QTLs) underlying a sexually selected male ornament. Using a custom individual-based forward C++ simulation model (`model_single-generation_selection`), the authors simulate a diploid population with four $1\text{ M}$ chromosomes ($4,000$ neutral bi-allelic markers plus 8 additive male-expressed QTLs), subjecting the population to $0–500$ generations of random mating (burn-in to vary linkage disequilibrium, $D'$) followed by a single generation of opposing viability selection (Gaussian optimum $\theta = 0, \omega_v^2 = 500$) and strong female mate choice (polygyny with Gaussian preference around male trait value $\theta = 4, \omega_s^2 = 2$). They compare three significance-thresholding procedures across parameter sweeps of LD, population size ($N$), sample size, marker density, chromosome number, QTL architecture, and environmental variance: (1) Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (`BH-FDR` at $\alpha = 0.001$) on $\chi^2$-transformed single-locus $F_{ST}$, (2) the `Stacks` 10,000-bootstrap confidence interval on kernel-smoothed $F'_{ST}$, and (3) a proposed "95% and 99% Genome-wide confidence interval (`GW-CI`)" on smoothed $F'_{ST}$. While the systematic exploration of how LD, sample size, and polygenic architecture constrain single-generation $F_{ST}$ scans is informative, the study is compromised by a mathematically misdefined "Genome-wide CI" threshold that is actually a within-scan $Z$-score outlier cutoff ($\bar{F}'_{ST} + z \sigma_{F'_{ST}}$), an extreme baseline sexual-selection regime ($\theta = 4\text{ SD}$ shift in one generation), and a generous spatial tolerance window ($\pm 50$ markers on a 1,000-marker chromosome) for scoring "true" QTL hits.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Mathematical Flaw in the Proposed "Genome-Wide Confidence Interval (`GW-CI`)" (Line 85):** The central methodological recommendation of the paper is that a parametric "95% or 99% genome-wide confidence interval" outperforms both `BH-FDR` and `Stacks` bootstrap CIs by yielding higher proportions of true peaks and lower false-discovery rates. However, inspecting the equation in line 85 reveals that the authors define their threshold as:
  $$\text{CI} = \bar{F}'_{ST} \pm \left(z \times \sigma_{F'_{ST}}\right)$$
  where $\bar{F}'_{ST}$ is the genome-wide mean of smoothed $F'_{ST}$ and $\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$ is the genome-wide standard deviation of smoothed $F'_{ST}$ across all markers in the *empirical scan itself* (not standard error of the mean, and not derived from a neutral null distribution). Statistically, $\bar{x} + 1.96 s$ or $\bar{x} + 2.58 s$ is a **parametric $Z$-score outlier threshold ($Z > 1.96$ or $Z > 2.58$)**, not a confidence interval. This formulation has two severe pathological consequences:
  1. **Guaranteed False Positives Under the Global Null (Zero Selection):** Because $\bar{F}'_{ST}$ and $\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$ are standardized directly from the empirical distribution of the scan, *every* genome scan—even under pure genetic drift with 0 QTLs and zero selection—will have $\sim 1–5\%$ of its smoothed windows exceeding $\bar{F}'_{ST} + 1.96\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$ or $+2.58\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$ simply by standardization!
  2. **Self-Masking Inflation by Strong QTLs:** When 8 strong QTLs are present on a small 4-chromosome genome and undergo intense selection ($\theta = 4$), those 8 large QTL sweeps drastically inflate the genome-wide standard deviation $\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$. Inflating $\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$ pushes the cutoff $\bar{F}'_{ST} + 2.58\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$ far above the neutral background, which artificially suppresses background noise peaks on the 4 QTL-bearing chromosomes—explaining why the authors observed low "false positive" rates in their 8-QTL simulations, and why adding neutral chromosomes or increasing QTL count to 32–128 caused `GW-CI` performance to collapse!
- **Lenient Spatial Window for Defining a "Real Peak" ($\pm 50$ Markers on a 1,000-Marker Chromosome):** In Section *Smoothing and calling peaks* (line 75), any smoothed $F'_{ST}$ peak is scored as a "true positive" (`real peak`) if a true QTL lies within $x = 50$ markers to the left or right of the peak (`window of 101 markers`). On a chromosome with 1,000 markers and 2 randomly placed QTLs, two windows of 101 markers cover up to $202 / 1,000 = 20.2\%$ of the entire chromosome! Thus, a random noise peak has an $\sim 20\%$ probability of being counted as a "real QTL detection" purely by spatial coincidence.
- **Extreme Baseline Selection Regime ($\theta = 4$ Shift in One Generation):** During the 200-generation neutral burn-in, each of the 8 QTLs has allelic effects centered at $0$ with Gaussian standard deviation $0.5$, yielding an adult male trait distribution centered at $0$ with $\sigma_z = \sqrt{16 \times 0.25} = 2.0$ (or $\sigma_z \approx 1.0–1.4$ after finite-population allele-frequency drift). Suddenly imposing a female preference optimum of $\theta = 4$ with narrow variance $\omega_s^2 = 2$ in a single generation selects exclusively for the extreme tail of males ($\ge 3\text{ SD}$ above the population mean), generating massive single-generation allele-frequency shifts ($\Delta p$) that are orders of magnitude stronger than typical polygenic sexual selection in natural populations.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** High linkage disequilibrium ($D'$, controlled by fewer generations of recombination prior to selection), larger sample sizes ($\ge 250–500$ individuals per life-history stage), and oligogenic architecture ($\le 8–16$ QTLs) are required to detect single-generation episodes of sexual selection in genome-wide $F_{ST}$ scans. `[Well Supported]` — Clearly demonstrated across the simulation parameter sweeps (Figs. 1–6) and consistent with analytical population genetics theory.
- **Claim 2:** Single-locus `BH-FDR` ($\chi^2 = 2 N F_{ST}$) yields lower power than sliding-window smoothed $F'_{ST}$ when neutral markers are only loosely linked to the causal QTL. `[Well Supported]` — Accurately reflects the variance-reduction benefit of spatial kernel smoothing across linked markers.
- **Claim 3:** The proposed 95% and 99% "Genome-wide confidence interval (`GW-CI`)" ($\bar{F}'_{ST} + z\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$) provides a statistically valid significance test that controls false positives better than resampling or FDR methods. `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Calling $\bar{x} + z s$ a "confidence interval" is a statistical misnomer; it is an empirical $Z$-score outlier rule whose apparent specificity in the 4-chromosome, 8-QTL baseline is an artifact of QTL-driven variance inflation ($\sigma_{F'_{ST}}$) and which fails under global neutrality (0 QTLs) or polygenic architectures.
