### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This veterinary field investigation and molecular surveillance study reports the first detection and isolation of Novel Goose Parvovirus (NGPV) associated with short beak and dwarfism syndrome (SBDS) in Pekin and mule duckling flocks (3–4 weeks old) across three Egyptian provinces (Giza, Behira, and Qaliobia). Out of 10 investigated farms, 4 flocks tested positive by conventional PCR targeting a 593-bp fragment of the viral *VP1* capsid gene and yielded cytopathic isolates on primary duck embryo liver (DEL) cell cultures. Sanger sequencing of the 593-bp *VP1* amplicons (`MT646477`–`MT646480`) and neighbor-joining phylogenetic analysis in MEGA6 showed 99.7–100% nucleotide identity among the four Egyptian isolates and 99.3–99.7% identity with Chinese SBDS NGPV strains (`KU844283` and `MK737642`), sharing characteristic amino acid substitutions (`Gln89Leu` and `Ala93Ser`). While the study provides useful regional surveillance data and deposited GenBank accessions, its methodological rigor is weakened by internal inconsistencies in reported mortality rates and nucleic acid type, reliance on a short 593-bp partial amplicon without full-genome or full-*VP1* sequencing, and unverified exclusion of viral/bacterial co-pathogens.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
* **Internal Contradiction in Reported Flock Mortality Rates:** In the Abstract (line 9), the authors state that across the investigated farms, *"Despite low recorded mortality rate (20%), morbidity rate was high (70%)."* However, in Results Section 3.1 (line 65), they state that the flocks *"showed a variable morbidity rate of 70% and 30% mortalities."* Furthermore, no farm-level table (listing the 10 investigated farms, province, duck breed, flock size, morbidity %, mortality %, and PCR/isolation status) is provided to reconcile these numbers.
* **Confusion Between Viral DNA and RNA in Results Section 3.2:** Goose parvovirus is a single-stranded DNA virus (*Parvoviridae*), and Section 2.3.1 describes viral DNA extraction using the QIAamp DNA Mini Kit. Yet Results Section 3.2 (line 76) states: *"Four flocks showed positive amplification of a 593 bp fragment of the VP1 gene for the RNA purified from tissue homogenates supernatants."*
* **Omission of Diagnostic Methods for Co-Pathogen Exclusion and Duck Circovirus:** In Results Section 3.1, the authors note that fibrinous perihepatitis, ascites, and hydropericardium were observed postmortem (Fig. 2)—lesions classically associated with bacterial septicemia (*Riemerella anatipestifer*, *E. coli*, *Salmonella*)—and assert that *"sampled birds were negative for Avian influenza virus, Duck hepatitis A virus, Salmonella, E. coli, Pasteurella multocida and Mannheimia haemolytica."* However, Materials and Methods contains zero description of any bacteriological culture, RT-PCR, or serological assays used to test for these agents, nor were the flocks tested for *Riemerella anatipestifer* or Duck Circovirus (despite the Introduction noting that NGPV frequently co-infects with duck circovirus in China).
* **Limited Phylogenetic Resolution from a 593-bp Partial *VP1* Amplicon:** The entire molecular and amino acid characterization rests on a 593-bp PCR fragment (~27% of the ~2.2-kb *VP1* gene and ~11% of the ~5.1-kb viral genome) analyzed solely by distance-based Neighbor-Joining (`Maximum Composite Likelihood`). Full-length *VP1* or whole-genome sequencing and maximum-likelihood phylogenetic modeling are needed to rule out recombination between classical GPV/vaccine strains and NGPV.
* **Missing Section 2.1 in Materials and Methods:** The numbering in Section 2 jumps directly from `## 2. Materials and Methods` to `## 2.2. Samples and Samples' Preparation`, omitting Section 2.1 (where ethical approval, farm sampling criteria, and bird counts per flock should have been documented).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
* **First Molecular Detection and Isolation of NGPV in Egyptian Pekin and Mule Ducks: `[Well Supported]`**  
  Clinical presentation (short beak and protruding tongue in 3–4-week-old mule and Pekin ducks, Fig. 1), primary DEL cell culture isolation with passage PCR confirmation, and Sanger sequencing of four isolates (`MT646477`–`MT646480`) clustering at 99.3–99.7% identity with reference Chinese NGPV strains establish the presence of NGPV in Egypt.
* **Identification of Shared `Gln89Leu` and `Ala93Ser` Substitutions in the Sequenced VP1 Region: `[Well Supported]`**  
  The amino acid alignment (Fig. 6) clearly demonstrates conservation of `Gln89Leu` across NGPV isolates and `Ala93Ser` shared specifically between the four Egyptian isolates and Chinese strains `M15` (`KU844283`) and `HN1P` (`MK737642`).
* **Attribution of Visceral Polyserositis (Fibrinous Perihepatitis) Solely to NGPV and Claims of Vaccine Cross-Protection: `[Overstated]`**  
  Attributing fibrinous perihepatitis solely to NGPV without documented bacteriological methods (including *Riemerella anatipestifer* testing) and inferring potential cross-protection of the commercial Derzsy LB vaccine strain solely from 95.7–96% nucleotide identity over a 593-bp fragment without cross-neutralization or challenge data overreach the presented evidence.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Sequence Accessions and Primers:** GenBank accessions `MT646477`–`MT646480`, positive control reference accession (`MF441226`), and *VP1* PCR primer sequences are fully reported.
* **Data Completeness Caveats:** A farm-by-farm epidemiological table and the missing Section 2.1 / co-pathogen diagnostic protocols must be added.
