### 1. Executive Summary
This molecular oncology study screens 22 matched normal and sporadic breast carcinoma DNA samples for loss of heterozygosity (LOH) and microsatellite instability (RER phenotype) using 42 polymorphic microsatellite markers mapped to or near six DNA mismatch-repair (MMR) genes: *hMLH1* (3p21), *hMSH2* and *hMSH6* (2p16), *hMSH3* (5q11–q13), *hPMS1* (2q32), and *hPMS2* (7p22). The authors observe frequent LOH on chromosome 3p21 (10/22 patients, 45.5%) and chromosome 5q11–q13 (5/22 patients, 22.7%), with all 5 patients deleted at 5q11–q13 also exhibiting LOH at 3p21 ($\chi^2$ test $p \approx 0.02$), while LOH at the other MMR loci occurs at baseline rates (4.7–10%). Strikingly, none of the 22 tumors display microsatellite instability across 572 locus comparisons (plus two additional tetranucleotide markers). The authors delimit shortest regions of overlapping deletion (SRO) between `D3S1298` and `D3S1266` on 3p21 and between `D5S418` and `D5S647` on 5q11–q13, proposing that *hMLH1* and *hMSH3* may contribute to breast tumorigenesis via haploinsufficiency/gene-dosage effects in non-RER pathways such as transcription-coupled repair.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Coarse Genetic Resolution of SROs (~11–18 cM) & Lack of Target Gene Validation:** In Figure 2, the 3p21 SRO between `D3S1266` (50 cM) and `D3S1298` (61 cM) spans ~11 cM; in Figure 3, the 5q11–q13 SRO between `D5S418` (63 cM) and `D5S647` (81 cM) spans ~18 cM. Intervals of 11–18 cM contain dozens to hundreds of genes. Although the authors successfully exclude *FHIT*/*PTPRG* (3p14.2) and *APC*/*MCC* (5q21) in a subset of tumors with smaller deletions, they perform no somatic mutation screening (e.g., exon-wide SSCP or sequencing of the retained allele), promoter hypermethylation assays, or RT-PCR/immunohistochemistry to show that *hMLH1* or *hMSH3* expression is actually reduced or targeted in these tumors.
- **Bulk Tumor DNA & Visual Autoradiograph Scoring:** Tumoral DNA was extracted from bulk fresh tissue without microdissection, and LOH was scored visually on $^{32}\text{P}$ polyacrylamide autoradiographs rather than by quantitative phosphorimaging or capillary electrophoresis peak-area ratios. Furthermore, aneuploidy/polysomy (amplification of one homologue) in breast carcinomas can mimic LOH on microsatellite gels.
- **Statistical Analysis of Co-deletion and RER:**
  - The co-occurrence of 3p21 and 5q11–q13 LOH in 5/10 vs. 0/12 patients is evaluated by a $\chi^2$ test ($p \approx 0.02$); given the small expected cell counts ($<5$), Fisher's exact test is required (two-sided Fisher's exact test gives $p = 0.0108$, which remains significant).
  - The power calculation showing that observing 0/22 RER+ tumors rules out a 30% population prevalence ($P_0 = e^{-0.3 \times 22} = 0.0014$, or exact binomial $(1-0.3)^{22} = 0.00039$) is sound.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Sporadic breast carcinomas in this cohort (0/22) lack microsatellite instability (RER phenotype) despite frequent LOH at 3p21 (45.5%) and 5q11–q13 (22.7%). `[Well Supported]` by 572 informative paired normal/tumor microsatellite genotypes and two additional tetranucleotide markers (`GGAA4D07`, `GGAA2E02`).
- **Claim 2:** The 3p21 and 5q11–q13 deletions define SROs distinct from *FHIT*/*PTPRG* and *APC*/*MCC* in several patients. `[Well Supported]` by the breakpoint mapping in patients 11 and 43 (3p21) and patients 13 and 29 (5q11–q13).
- **Claim 3:** Hemizygous deletions of *hMLH1* and *hMSH3* promote breast tumorigenesis via a gene-dosage effect impairing DNA repair functions other than replication error correction. `[Fragile]` — purely speculative hypothesis with no expression, functional repair, or second-hit data, though the authors appropriately caveat in the Discussion that LOH at these markers may simply reflect linkage to unidentified tumor suppressor genes within the 3p21 and 5q11–q13 intervals.
