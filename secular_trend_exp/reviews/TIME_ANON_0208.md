### 1. Executive Summary
This prospective clinical cohort study evaluates whether the absence of detectable serum hepatitis B virus (HBV) DNA by nested/Southern-blot PCR shortly after spontaneous HBeAg-to-anti-HBe seroconversion and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) normalization predicts sustained biochemical remission in chronic hepatitis B. Twenty-eight consecutive patients (24 men, 4 women; mean age 34.1 years) whose sera were tested at a mean of 4.4 months (range 2–8 months) after ALT normalization were stratified into two equal groups: PCR-positive ($n = 14$) and PCR-negative ($n = 14$), and followed prospectively for 20–66 months (mean 55.5 months) with liver function tests every 1–3 months. Using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and the generalized Wilcoxon test, cumulative biochemical reactivation rates ($\text{ALT} > 2.5\times \text{ULN}$) at years 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 were 43%, 57%, 57%, 57%, and 57% in PCR-positive patients versus 50%, 66%, 74%, 74%, and 83% in PCR-negative patients ($p = 0.79$). Most reactivations (17/19) occurred within the first 2 years of remission and were associated with the pre-core G1896A stop-codon mutation. The authors conclude that a single negative serum HBV DNA PCR test early after anti-HBe seroconversion does not guarantee sustained remission, whereas maintaining 2 years of biochemical remission is a more reliable prognostic indicator.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Sample Size & Statistical Power (`[Statistical/Design]`):** With only $n = 14$ patients per group ($N = 28$ total), the study is only powered to detect very large differences ($\Delta > 45\%$) in cumulative reactivation rates. However, because the observed cumulative reactivation rate in the PCR-negative group (50% at 1 yr, 83% at 5 yr) was numerically *higher* than in the PCR-positive group (43% at 1 yr, 57% at 5 yr), the clinical conclusion that PCR negativity fails to protect against reactivation is robust against Type II error (even a much larger study would not find PCR negativity to be protective near 0%).
- **Single-Timepoint Exposure Classification:** Serum HBV DNA status was determined from a single serum draw at $4.4 \pm 2.4$ months post-ALT normalization. Because viremia in anti-HBe-positive chronic hepatitis B fluctuates markedly, a single cross-sectional PCR measurement is subject to regression dilution / state misclassification over a 5-year follow-up.
- **Incomplete Reporting of Pre-Core Sequencing Data:** In the Materials and Methods (line 44) and Discussion (line 77), the authors state that the pre-core region of HBV at the time of reactivation was analyzed by direct PCR sequencing and that reactivations were predominantly caused by the pre-core G1896A mutant without HBeAg reversion. However, in the Results (line 59), pre-core G1896A sequencing is only explicitly shown for "a representative case" (Fig. 2), without tabulating how many of the 19 reactivation cases across the two groups were sequenced, how many reverted to HBeAg-positive WT, and how many harbored G1896A.
- **Assay Rigor:** Requiring concordance across two independent PCR-Southern blot hybridizations with a $^{32}\text{P}$-labeled full-length HBV genome probe is a sound precaution against PCR carryover contamination.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Disappearance of serum HBV DNA by PCR shortly after spontaneous anti-HBe seroconversion and ALT normalization does not predict sustained biochemical remission (50% 1-year and 83% 5-year cumulative reactivation). `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated by the prospective 55.5-month follow-up of the 14 PCR-negative patients.
- **Claim 2:** Reactivations occur predominantly within the first 2 years after ALT normalization (17/19 episodes; 46.4% at year 1, 61.3% at year 2, and $<5\%$/year thereafter), making 2-year sustained biochemical remission a better prognostic marker than a single early PCR test. `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by the Kaplan-Meier trajectory across the 28 patients, though external validation in a larger cohort is warranted.
- **Claim 3:** Reactivation in PCR-negative patients reflects persistence of dormant/latent HBV in the liver (cccDNA) or PBMCs and emergence of pre-core G1896A mutants. `[Moderately Supported]` — Biologically sound and consistent with the representative case, though the manuscript lacks a complete table of pre-core sequencing across all 19 reactivated patients.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Baseline clinical characteristics (Age, Sex, timing of initial serum draw, follow-up duration) are clearly tabulated in Table 1, and commercial RIA and PCR kits and ATCC probe (`ATCC No. 45020`) are specified.
- However, the exact nucleotide sequences of the S-gene and pre-core PCR primers, thermocycling parameters, limit of detection (in copies/mL or $\text{fg/mL}$), and numbers at risk under the Kaplan-Meier curve (Fig. 1) are not provided in the text, relying instead on prior references.
