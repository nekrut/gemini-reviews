### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This manuscript is a narrative review surveying viral diseases of aquatic animals raised in global aquaculture, covering cultivated teleost finfish, molluscs (shellfish), and crustaceans (penaeid shrimp, prawns, crabs, crayfish). Synthesizing 98 references spanning classical virology, histopathology, electron microscopy, and serology, the article highlights the major viral families driving epizootics in Asian and global mariculture—specifically baculoviruses, parvoviruses (IHHNV, HPV), reo-like viruses, and togaviruses (LOVV) in penaeid shrimp/crustaceans; herpes-like, irido-like, reo-like, and birnaviruses in bivalve molluscs; and herpesviruses (OMV, channel catfish virus, carp pox), iridoviruses (EHNV), aquareoviruses/birnaviruses (IPNV, EEV), and rhabdoviruses (IHNV, Hirame rhabdovirus, ulcerative syndrome rhabdovirus) in teleosts. Only the abstract and the 98-item bibliography are preserved in the archival text file.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
#### STRENGTHS
- **Broad Taxonomic & Geographic Synthesis:** The bibliography (98 citations) systematically spans European, North American, Australian, Japanese, Chinese, and Southeast Asian aquaculture virology literature, bridging vertebrate (teleost) and invertebrate (molluscan/crustacean) pathology.
- **Balanced Epistemic Framing:** The abstract accurately distinguishes between well-characterized teleost viruses (where cell lines, serology, and experimental transmission fulfil Koch's postulates) and invertebrate virus-like particles (primarily detected via electron microscopy or histopathology, where pathogenicity and epizootiology remain largely unknown).

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Narrative Review Without Quantitative Meta-Analysis or Primary Data:** As a descriptive review article, the manuscript contains no original experimental data, systematic search criteria, epidemiological incidence/mortality tables, or phylogenetic/statistical analyses.
- **Archival Main Text Omission:** The text extraction contains only the abstract and the 98 references, omitting the narrative body sections and any summary tables of viral taxa and hosts.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Baculoviruses are major pathogens in penaeid shrimp/prawns, while herpes-, irido-, reo-, birna-, and rhabdoviruses cause severe epizootics in farmed teleost fish. `[Well Supported]` by the cited primary literature (Lightner, Sano, Kimura, Hedrick, de Kinkelin).
- **Claim 2:** Whereas economically important teleost viral infections are relatively well understood, knowledge of the pathogenicity and epizootiology of most viruses detected in farmed molluscs and crustaceans remains sparse. `[Well Supported]` and appropriately cautious given the reliance on ultrastructural EM observations in marine invertebrates at the time.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Not applicable for primary experimental reproducibility; the 98 literature citations are complete and traceable, though the narrative text body is absent from the archival file.
