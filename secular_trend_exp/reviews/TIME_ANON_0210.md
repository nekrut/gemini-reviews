### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript is a Special Issue Editorial ("Revisiting the Ortholog Conjecture and Functional Divergence / Broadening the Framework of Orthology") introducing 11 articles arising from the 8th Quest for Orthologs (QfO) community meeting held in Montréal, Canada. The editorial synthesizes three overarching themes across the 11 contributed papers: (1) critical re-evaluation of the "ortholog conjecture" using quantitative biochemical modeling of selective optima (*Perez et al.*), pathway/interaction context (*Langschied et al.*), and protein language model (PLM) embedding-tree vs. gene-tree comparisons (*Shaw et al.*); (2) extension of orthology inference beyond whole genes to protein domain birth–death–gain dynamics (*Xiao et al.*), alternative splice isoforms, non-coding RNAs (miRNAs), and hierarchical orthologous groups (HOGs; *Sarton-Lohéac et al.*, *Majidian et al.*); and (3) practical software and database infrastructure, including *OrthoXML-tools* (*Kharrazi et al.*), *Profylo* for phylogenetic profiling (*Schoenstein et al.*), *OrthoGrafter* for PANTHER tree grafting (*Williams and Thomas*), hierarchical synteny algorithms (*Swenson et al.*), and the NCBI RefSeq Orthologs pipeline (*Oh et al.*).
