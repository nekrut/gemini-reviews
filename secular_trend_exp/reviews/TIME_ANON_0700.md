### SUMMARY
### 1. Executive Summary
This classic transgenic mouse developmental neurobiology study investigates the cis-regulatory architecture of the murine myelin basic protein (*MBP*) gene promoter and enhancer region (`-1907` to `+36`). By fusing this 1.9-kb DNA fragment to an *E. coli* *lacZ* ($\beta$-galactosidase) reporter gene in transgenic mice and assaying expression across central and peripheral nervous system glia, including nuclear run-on transcription assays and developmental time-course profiling, the authors demonstrate that the `-1907` to `+36` region drives robust, developmentally scheduled transcription in CNS oligodendrocytes at levels rivaling the endogenous *MBP* locus, but fails to drive expression in PNS myelinating Schwann cells (where endogenous *MBP* is normally expressed). From this dissociable cell-type specificity, the authors deduce a tripartite model of *MBP* cis-regulation comprising a core promoter, a proximal (`-1907` to `+36`) oligodendrocyte enhancer, and a distinct distal/intronic regulatory element required for Schwann cell transcription.

### STRENGTHS
- **Direct Transcriptional Rate Measurement via Nuclear Run-On Assays:** Rather than relying solely on steady-state $\beta$-galactosidase histochemistry or mRNA levels (which can be confounded by transcript stability or position-effect variegation), the study uses nuclear run-on assays to confirm that transcriptional initiation rates driven by the 1.9-kb transgene rival those of the endogenous *MBP* gene on the native developmental schedule.
- **Clean In Vivo Dissociation of CNS vs. PNS Glial Enhancer Modules:** Comparing transgene expression against endogenous *MBP* across CNS oligodendrocytes and PNS Schwann cells within the same transgenic animals provides a rigorous internal positive control for developmental myelination state and tissue fixation.
