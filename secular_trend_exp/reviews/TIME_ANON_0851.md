### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This preclinical cancer metabolism study investigates the mechanism by which the transcription factor NFATc1 promotes colorectal cancer (CRC) proliferation. Integrating public ChIP-seq/target databases (GTRD, hTFtarget, Cistrome DB), TCGA expression correlations, untargeted metabolomics, ChIP-qPCR, site-directed promoter luciferase reporter assays, and subcutaneous xenograft models in BALB/c-nu/nu mice ($n = 5$ per group), the authors show that NFATc1 directly binds the promoter of NAD kinase (*NADK*, at $-121\text{ to }-117$) and both the p1 ($-307\text{ to }-302$, S1) and p2 ($+976\text{ to }+981$, S2) promoters of *MDM2*. NFATc1 knockdown reduces NADK, lowers intracellular $\text{NADP}^+$ and NADPH, reduces G6PD activity and PRPP levels, elevates ROS, and stabilizes p53/p21 via MDM2 downregulation. Crucially, while NADK overexpression rescues the $\text{NAD}^+/\text{NADP}^+$ ratio, ROS, and G6PD activity, only simultaneous co-overexpression of both NADK and MDM2 fully rescues cell proliferation and xenograft tumor growth in NFATc1-depleted HCT116 cells. Finally, the authors test pharmacological inhibition with NFAT-IN-1 and nifedipine (NIFE) alone and in combination with oxaliplatin. While the dual-target genetic rescue design is thorough, the study has a direct biochemical contradiction in its reported NADPH/$\text{NADP}^+$ ratio, relies on non-specific calcium-channel blockade (nifedipine) and lacks formal synergy modeling, and uses inappropriate statistical tests for multi-group one-factor comparisons.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
* **Biochemical & Mathematical Contradiction in the Reported $\text{NADPH}/\text{NADP}^+$ Ratio and G6PD Allosteric Mechanism:** On line 117, the authors state that NFATc1 knockdown suppresses NADK, which *"led to a reduction in intracellular NADP+ levels while simultaneously elevating NAD+ levels, ultimately causing a marked increase in the NAD+/NADP+ ratio (Fig. 2C). Furthermore, we observed a concomitant decrease in NADPH levels, leading to a significant increase in the NADPH/NADP+ ratio (Fig. S2E). ... Given that the NADPH/NADP+ ratio serves as a key allosteric regulator of G6PD, the rate-limiting enzyme of the PPP, we subsequently assessed and observed a significant reduction in G6PD activity following NFATc1 suppression (Fig. 2E)."*
  1. First, if NFATc1 knockdown decreases NADPH synthesis (via lower PPP flux) and increases ROS consumption of NADPH, the $\text{NADPH}/\text{NADP}^+$ ratio should *decrease* unless $\text{NADP}^+$ drops even more drastically than NADPH.
  2. Second, and more importantly, the authors' own Discussion (line 167) claims the opposite mechanism: *"upregulation of NADK drives the phosphorylation of NAD+ to NADP+, resulting in elevated NADP+ pools that allosterically activate G6PD."* If $\text{NADP}^+$ is the substrate/allosteric activator and NADPH is the competitive product inhibitor of G6PD, an *increase* in the $\text{NADPH}/\text{NADP}^+$ ratio inhibits G6PD, whereas the Discussion attributes G6PD activation to *MDM2* upregulation (line 137: *"overexpression of MDM2 significantly rescues the decrease in glucose consumption and G6PD activity"*) even though NADK overexpression alone already completely rescued G6PD activity in Fig. 3K!
  3. Third, no actual metabolic flux measurement (such as $[1,2\text{-}^{13}\text{C}_2]\text{-glucose}$ isotope tracing to measure oxidative PPP flux into $\text{M}+1$ lactate/pentose phosphates) was performed; "PPP flux" is inferred strictly from static metabolite pools ($\text{NADP}^+$, R5P, PRPP) and an *in vitro* G6PD enzyme activity kit.
* **Inappropriate Statistical Testing and Small Sample Sizes:**
  1. Line 97 states: *"The difference between two groups was calculated using unpaired student t-test. Differences among multiple groups were calculated using two-way ANOVA."* Most multi-group experiments in the manuscript (e.g., shCon vs. shNFATc1-1 vs. shNFATc1-2 in Figs. 1G, 2C–F, 3C, 4C) have only a single experimental factor (construct/treatment) and require a **one-way ANOVA** with Dunnett's or Tukey's post-hoc multiple-comparison test, not a two-way ANOVA, and no post-hoc correction is mentioned.
  2. For the 30 paired clinical CRC and adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 1D), a paired $t$-test (or Wilcoxon signed-rank test) should be used rather than an unpaired Student's $t$-test.
  3. All *in vitro* bar graphs use $n = 3$ biological/technical replicates (parametric mean $\pm$ SD), and xenograft groups use $n = 5$ mice per group without an *a priori* power calculation.
* **Unvalidated "Synergy" Claim in Drug Combination Experiments (Fig. 6F):** The Abstract and Results claim that NFAT-IN-1 and NIFE "synergize with oxaliplatin." However, in cell culture (Fig. 6C) only a single fixed dose ($10\ \mu\text{M}$ of each agent) was tested, without a dose-response matrix or Chou-Talalay Combination Index (CI) / Bliss independence / Loewe additivity calculation. In the xenograft study ($n = 5$), the authors define an ad hoc "enhancement factor" as $[(\text{growth delay of combination}) - (\text{growth delay of NFAT-IN only})] / (\text{growth delay of OXA only})$, which measures simple arithmetic ratio over one single-agent arm rather than formal pharmacological synergy.
* **Luciferase Overexpression Paradox in NADK-mut-luc (Fig. 3G):** On line 127, the authors write: *"A luciferase reporter assay using NADK-mut-luc, an NADK-luc reporter with four mutated nucleotides in the predicted NFATc1-binding site (TTCC to GCAT), demonstrated that mutations in the binding site abolished the suppressive effect of NFATc1 knockdown and enhanced the effect of NFATc1 overexpression compared to that of wild-type NADK-luc (Fig. 3E–G)."* Mutating the transcription factor binding site must *abolish* (not *enhance*) the transcriptional activation caused by NFATc1 overexpression; either the text on line 127 is misstated or the reporter assay in Fig. 3G contradicts binding site specificity.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
* **Strengths:** The genetic dissection comparing single rescues (shNFATc1 + pcNADK in Fig. 3 and shNFATc1 + pcMDM2 in Fig. 4D–G) against the double rescue (shNFATc1 + pcNADK + pcMDM2 in Fig. 4H–K) and $p53^{-/-}$ HCT116 isogenic controls (Fig. S5E–G) is well-conceived and provides strong epistatic evidence that NFATc1 acts through both metabolic (NADK) and cell-cycle (MDM2/p53) branches.
* **Claim 1 (NFATc1 directly binds the *NADK* promoter and the p1/p2 promoters of *MDM2* to activate their transcription):** `[Well Supported]` — Supported by ChIP-qPCR and site-directed mutagenesis of binding sites S1 and S2 (with S3 serving as a negative internal binding control in Fig. 5C/G), subject to clarifying the wording of Fig. 3G.
* **Claim 2 (Both NADK and MDM2 are simultaneously required downstream of NFATc1 to fully restore CRC cell proliferation and xenograft growth):** `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated by partial rescue with single genes and complete rescue with dual overexpression in HCT116 cells and xenografts.
* **Claim 3 (NFATc1 activates PPP metabolic flux):** `[Moderately Supported]` — Static pool levels of $\text{NADP}^+$, R5P, and PRPP and G6PD activity decrease upon NFATc1 knockdown, but true pathway flux requires $^{13}\text{C}$ isotope tracing.
* **Claim 4 (NFATc1 inhibitors synergize with oxaliplatin as a targeted therapeutic strategy):** `[Overstated]` — Nifedipine is a non-specific L-type $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ channel blocker (as acknowledged in the Discussion), and formal pharmacological synergy (Bliss/Chou-Talalay) was not tested.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data Availability:** Raw untargeted metabolomics data ($182$ altered metabolites in Fig. 2I–L) are not deposited in a public repository (e.g., MetaboLights or Metabolomics Workbench); the Data Availability Statement merely says "Data will be made available on request."
* **Reagents:** Target shRNA sequences for *NFATc1* and *TP53* are explicitly provided in the Methods, and uncropped Western blots and primer/antibody tables are referenced in the Supplementary Data.
