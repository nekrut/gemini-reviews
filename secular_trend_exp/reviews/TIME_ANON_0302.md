### 1. Executive Summary
This molecular phylogenetics and evolutionary timescale study sequences 32 new complete primate mitochondrial genomes (representing 20 previously unsampled genera, including 13 Platyrrhini and 10 Strepsirrhini) via long-range PCR (8 kb + 10 kb overlapping amplicons) and barcoded Roche 454 FLX pyrosequencing (~21× coverage, supplemented by Sanger gap closure), combining them with 51 published primate mitogenomes and 4 non-primate outgroups to assemble an 87-taxon dataset spanning all 16 primate families and 57 of 78 recognized genera (78 of 480 species). Analyzing four alignment partitions (`mtDNA1`: 13,281 bp Gblocks-filtered excluding D-loop; `mtDNA2`: RY-coded `AGY` version of `mtDNA1`; `mtDNA3`: 10,773 bp 12 heavy-strand PCGs; `mtDNA4`: RY-coded `AGY` version of `mtDNA3`) under locus-partitioned Maximum Likelihood (`GARLI 2.0`) and Bayesian Inference (`MrBayes 3.1.2`), alongside relaxed-clock divergence dating (`BEAST 1.6.1`) with nine internal fossil calibrations, the authors recover a well-resolved family- and genus-level primate phylogeny, identify specific mitonuclear discordances in Cercopithecinae and Colobinae attributable to ancient hybridization, and estimate the crown primate MRCA at 66.2–69.1 Ma near the Cretaceous–Tertiary (K/T) boundary.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Compositional Bias Control via RY-Coding & Partitioned Modeling:** Recognizing the wide variation in mitogenome GC content across primate genera (37.78%–46.32%, Table S2), the authors explicitly construct C/T $\rightarrow$ Y (`AGY`) recoded datasets (`mtDNA2` and `mtDNA4`) alongside standard nucleotide datasets (`mtDNA1` and `mtDNA3`), apply BIC model selection (`jModelTest 2.1`) per locus/codon position, and monitor MCMC convergence across 4 independent 10M–25M generation runs using PSRF, `Tracer 1.5`, and `AWTY`.
- **Handling of Mitonuclear Discordances via Topology Tests & Topological Constraints:** Where the mitochondrial tree conflicts with established nuclear/retroposon clades—such as paraphyletic Lorisidae, *Erythrocebus* clustering with *Cercopithecus* rather than *Chlorocebus*, *Mandrillus*+*Cercocebus* grouping with *Macaca* rather than *Papio*/*Theropithecus*/*Lophocebus*, and *Trachypithecus* grouping with *Presbytis* rather than *Semnopithecus*—the authors apply Kishino–Hasegawa (KH) and Shimodaira–Hasegawa (SH) tests (1,000 bootstraps, Table S4). Showing that the nuclear topologies for Lorisidae, Indriidae+Lemuridae, *Saguinus*, *Erythrocebus*+*Chlorocebus*, and *Macaca* are not statistically rejected ($p > 0.05$), they constrain those nodes to the nuclear/retroposon topology in `BEAST` before estimating divergence dates. (Note: while SH is valid for a posteriori comparisons against the ML tree, the one-sided KH test is slightly anti-conservative when comparing a pre-specified tree against the data-derived ML tree, though SH is appropriately included alongside it.)
- **Root Prior vs. Posterior Divergence Age Interpretation:** A notable methodological tension occurs in the crown primate MRCA dating: in the Materials and Methods (line 79), the authors state they took the nine normal calibration priors directly from Perelman et al. [18], including "a primate MRCA of 90.0 Ma (SD = 6.0)." Yet their posterior estimate for the primate MRCA (`mtDNA1` and `mtDNA3`) is 66.22–69.05 Ma (nearly 3.5–4.0 SD below the 90.0 Ma prior mean!). While this shows that the eight younger internal primate fossil calibrations and the mitochondrial branch lengths strongly pull the crown root toward the K/T boundary (~66–69 Ma), the authors do not discuss the strong prior–posterior conflict at the root node or test sensitivity to removing the 90 Ma root prior.
- **Long-Range PCR vs. NUMT Precautions:** Amplifying the mitogenome in two large overlapping fragments (8 kb and 10 kb) and manually curating 454 homopolymer frameshifts against reading frames effectively minimizes short nuclear mitochondrial pseudogene (NUMT) co-amplification.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Complete mitochondrial genomes resolve family- and genus-level primate relationships largely congruent with nuclear and retroposon phylogenies, while revealing specific ancient hybridization events (e.g., *Trachypithecus*–*Presbytis* in Asian colobines).** `[Well Supported]` — Supported by high ML bootstrap and Bayesian posterior support across 57 genera and explicit comparison with nuclear and SINE/retroposon markers.
- **Claim 2: The crown MRCA of extant primates dates to 66.2–69.1 Ma, close to the K/T boundary.** `[Moderately Supported]` — The 66–69 Ma estimate aligns well with paleontological evidence and life-history rate models, though as noted above, it relies on a single maternal locus subject to substitutional saturation at deep Cenozoic nodes and exhibits unexamined tension with the 90 Ma (SD = 6.0) root prior imported from Perelman et al.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Exemplary Data Deposition:** All raw 454 sequencing reads are deposited in the European Nucleotide Archive (`ERP002564`), all 32 newly assembled and annotated mitogenomes are deposited in GenBank (Table S1), the complete 87-taxon MAFFT alignment is provided in `Data S1`, and all long-range PCR primer sequences, cycling conditions, calibration priors (Table S5), and software parameters are fully documented.
