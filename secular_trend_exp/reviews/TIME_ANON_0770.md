### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This neuroimmunology and viral pathogenesis study investigates whether myelin-specific autoimmune T-cell responses initiate or arise secondarily during chronic Theiler's murine encephalomyelitis virus (TMEV, `BeAn 8386` strain, $10^4\text{ PFU}$ intracranially) demyelinating disease (`TVID`). Comparing a highly susceptible inbred mouse strain (`SJL`, $N=50$, 92% [46/50] developing clinical demyelination score $\ge 3$ by 6 months) with an intermediately susceptible strain (`CBA`, $N=50$, 68% developing clinical disease by 1 year, allowing direct comparison of symptomatic [score $\ge 3$] vs. asymptomatic [score $< 3$] mice at late time points), the authors measure in vivo delayed-type hypersensitivity (DTH, 24-h ear pinna swelling to $10\ \mu\text{g}$ syngeneic whole mouse myelin vs. contralateral PBS control, $n=5\text{--}10$ mice per group tested once each) at early (4 and 8 weeks) and chronic (36–48 weeks) times post-infection. They find that neither strain exhibits myelin-specific DTH at 4 or 8 weeks post-infection (even when early demyelination and clinical signs $\ge 3$ are already present), whereas at 36–48 weeks post-infection, symptomatic SJL ($p < 0.05$) and symptomatic CBA ($p < 0.001$) mice—but **not** age-matched uninfected controls or persistently infected *asymptomatic* CBA mice at 36–48 weeks—mount significant myelin-specific DTH responses. The authors conclude with admirable restraint that myelin autoimmunity does not initiate early TMEV demyelination, but emerges late in chronically demyelinated mice via secondary determinant/epitope spreading.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES

#### STRENGTHS
- **Clever Two-Strain Comparative Design (`SJL` vs. `CBA` Symptomatic/Asymptomatic Split):** Because nearly 100% of SJL mice develop clinical disease by 16–20 weeks, SJL mice alone cannot separate *duration of persistent viral infection* from *presence of CNS myelin destruction* at 36–48 weeks. Including `CBA` mice (where 32% remain asymptomatic at 36–48 weeks despite intracranial viral inoculation) provides a critical internal control proving that late myelin DTH requires preceding clinical demyelination rather than mere viral persistence or antigenic mimicry.
- **Single-Test-Per-Mouse & Contralateral Ear Control:** Testing each mouse only once during the entire 48-week experiment (preventing prior intradermal myelin challenge from priming a de novo autoimmune response!) and subtracting contralateral PBS-injected ear swelling from the myelin-injected ear swelling (`myelin - PBS` in $10^{-3}\text{ cm}$ units), alongside blinded histological scoring of glutaraldehyde/osmium-fixed toluidine-blue spinal cord sections, reflects careful classical in vivo experimental design.
- **Balanced, Non-Dogmatic Claim Proportionality:** Unlike many papers that overstate autoimmune causality in viral demyelination, the Discussion thoughtfully notes that myelin DTH at 36–48 weeks did not correlate with clinical severity score within the 3–5 range and could represent either a contributor to chronic lesion progression or a secondary epiphenomenon of myelin debris release.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Pooling of Sham-Infected Control Time Points into a Single `Time 0` Baseline (Fig. 3):** In Figure 3a and 3b, the caption states: *"Data for the mice tested prior to infection and all of the sham-infected age-matched control mice at subsequent time-points is recorded as the 0 time-point"*. Pooling pre-infection (4–5 week old) and 4-, 8-, 36-, and 48-week age-matched sham controls into a single combined `0` bar artificially inflates the sample size $N$ of the reference group in the one-way ANOVA and obscures time-point-specific measurement variance across the 1-year experiment (Ideally, a two-way ANOVA of `Infection Status` $\times$ `Time Point` with separate age-matched control bars at each time point and Dunnett/Tukey post-hoc corrections should be reported).
- **Large Time Gap Between 8 Weeks and 36 Weeks Post-Infection:** In Figure 1, clinical disease incidence in SJL mice rises steeply between 6 and 16 weeks post-infection, yet DTH testing skips directly from 8 weeks to 36–48 weeks (a 7-month gap!). Testing intermediate time points (e.g., 12, 16, and 24 weeks) would have pinned down when epitope spreading first emerges relative to the peak of initial demyelination (as noted in the "Note added in proof" citing Miller et al. *Nat Med*, who detected peptide-specific spreading starting ~50–80 days post-infection).
- **Use of Crude Whole SJL Myelin Homogenate Without Defined Peptide Epitopes or Allogeneic Control in CBA Mice:**
  1. Whole brain myelin prepared by sucrose gradient fractionation contains all myelin proteins (MBP, PLP, MOG, MAG) as well as trace non-myelin CNS membrane proteins, preventing identification of the specific target autoantigen(s) or intermolecular cascade (`PLP 139–151` $\to$ `PLP 178–191` $\to$ `MOG`/`MBP`).
  2. Furthermore, the Methods (line 48) state that *"Myelin was prepared from the brains of adult SJL mice"* ($H\text{-}2^s$), and this SJL-derived myelin was injected into the ears of both SJL ($H\text{-}2^s$) and **CBA ($H\text{-}2^k$)** mice! While asymptomatic CBA mice and sham CBA controls did not react to a single challenge of SJL myelin, using allogeneic SJL brain tissue in $H\text{-}2^k$ CBA mice introduces a potential minor histocompatibility/alloantigenic variable if trace glial MHC/membrane proteins co-purified with myelin.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Myelin-specific peripheral DTH responses are absent during the early initiation phase of TMEV-induced demyelinating disease (4–8 weeks post-infection) in both SJL and CBA mice.* `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by negative ear-swelling assays at 4 and 8 weeks despite histological demyelination and clinical scores $\ge 3$.
- **Claim 2:** *Myelin-specific DTH develops at late chronic stages (36–48 weeks) exclusively in mice with clinical demyelinating disease (and not in asymptomatic infected CBA mice or age-matched controls).* `[Well Supported]` — Clearly demonstrated in Fig. 3a–b ($p < 0.05$ in SJL, $p < 0.001$ in CBA).
- **Claim 3:** *Late-arising myelin autoreactivity likely arises via epitope spreading secondary to virus-induced/immunopathological myelin damage and may either contribute to chronic progression or represent an epiphenomenon.* `[Well Supported]` — Mechanistically sound and appropriately caveated.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Experimental Details:** Mouse strains (`SJL` from Olac, `CBA` from Cambridge), viral strain and dose (`BeAn 8386`, $10^4\text{ PFU}$ in $20\ \mu\text{L}$ i.c.), 0–6 clinical scoring rubric, perfusion/TAAB resin histology protocol, and DTH micrometer dosage ($10\ \mu\text{g}$ myelin in $20\ \mu\text{L}$ PBS) are clearly documented, though exact numerical group means, standard errors, and exact sample sizes per time point are presented only graphically in Figures 1 and 3.
