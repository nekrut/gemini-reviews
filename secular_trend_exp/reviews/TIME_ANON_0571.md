### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This narrative review surveys the biology of SARS-CoV-2 infection in the olfactory epithelium (ACE2 and TMPRSS2 co-expression in sustentacular and basal stem cells), COVID-19 hyperinflammation, and sphingolipid/sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) metabolism (SphK1/2, S1P lyase, S1PR1–5, and transporters ABC, Spns2, Mfsd2b). The authors hypothesize that SARS-CoV-2-induced anosmia and neurological symptoms may involve disruption of S1P-mediated survival and proliferation signals in the olfactory stem cell niche, and propose S1P pathway modulators—such as the multiple sclerosis drug Fingolimod (FTY720)—as potential therapeutic candidates for COVID-19. While the review compiles useful background literature on olfactory sustentacular cell receptor expression and sphingolipid signaling in other viral infections (Table 1), it contains no original empirical data, lacks a systematic literature search methodology, and advances highly speculative therapeutic extrapolations that conflict with known immunosuppressive risks and receptor pharmacology.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
* **Absence of Systematic Review Methodology or Selection Criteria:** No database search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, date bounds, or evidence-grading framework is provided. Anecdotal single-patient case reports (e.g., Ref 36, a 57-year-old female MS patient on Fingolimod; Ref 37, a patient with COVID-19 reactivation after Fingolimod withdrawal) are discussed alongside preclinical mouse/cell studies without formal risk-of-bias or clinical evidence stratification.
* **Lack of Direct Empirical Evidence Linking SARS-CoV-2 to S1P Metabolism:** As the authors acknowledge in Section 6, there are zero experimental studies in the manuscript demonstrating that SARS-CoV-2 alters SphK1/2, S1P lyase, S1P levels, or S1PR signaling in nasal sustentacular cells, olfactory sensory neurons, or lung epithelium. Moreover, in Section 1, the authors explicitly note that *"no data have shown the expression of S1P transporter at the protein level in the olfactory system."*
* **Internal Mechanistic Contradictions in Proposed Drug Targeting:** The review simultaneously suggests inhibiting SphK1/SphK2 (citing antiviral effects in influenza and measles, Section 5.1), activating S1P/S1PR1 to stimulate olfactory neurogenesis and endothelial integrity (Sections 5.3 and 7), and administering Fingolimod/FTY720 (which induces irreversible internalization and degradation of S1PR1, acting as a functional antagonist that causes profound lymphopenia, Section 5). If S1PR1 activation is required for neuroblast migration to the olfactory bulb and stem cell proliferation (Section 5.3), functional antagonism and receptor degradation by FTY720-phosphate would inhibit rather than restore olfactory neurogenesis.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
* **Cellular Localization of ACE2 and TMPRSS2 in the Olfactory Epithelium: `[Well Supported]`**  
  The synthesis of single-cell RNA-seq and immunohistochemical evidence showing that non-neuronal sustentacular cells, horizontal basal cells, and Bowman's glands—rather than mature olfactory sensory neurons—co-express ACE2 and TMPRSS2 accurately reflects the cellular tropism underlying COVID-19 anosmia.
* **Pleiotropic Roles of SphK/S1P/S1PR Signaling in Diverse Viral Infections: `[Moderately Supported]`**  
  Table 1 and Section 5.1 accurately summarize prior literature showing that viruses such as RSV, HCMV, influenza A, measles, and dengue modulate SphK1/SphK2 activity in host cells, though these effects are highly virus-specific (e.g., SphK1 is increased in RSV/IAV but decreased in dengue and BVDV) and cannot be generalized a priori to coronaviruses.
* **Crosstalk Between S1PR1 and ACE2 Expression in Olfactory Epithelium: `[Overstated]`**  
  In Section 7, the authors extrapolate from a single study in cardiac muscle (Ref 119) to suggest that S1PR1 signaling may regulate ACE2 and TMPRSS2 expression in olfactory cells; no transcriptomic or functional data in airway or olfactory tissue support this claim.
* **Therapeutic Utility of Fingolimod (FTY720) and S1P Modulators in COVID-19: `[Fragile]`**  
  Proposing lymphocyte-sequestering immunosuppressants (FTY720) or SphK inhibitors for acute SARS-CoV-2 infection on the basis of non-coronavirus models and a single MS case report overlooks the hazard of impairing adaptive viral clearance during acute infection.
