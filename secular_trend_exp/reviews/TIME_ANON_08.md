### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the evolutionary history of the vertebrate relaxin-like peptide family (*RLN1*, *RLN2*, *RLN3*, *INSL3*, *INSL4*, *INSL5*, and *INSL6*) through TBLASTN searches of completed and draft vertebrate/invertebrate genomes and EST databases, multiple sequence alignment of the short cleaved B and A peptide domains (~50 amino acids total), gene-species tree reconciliation, pairwise $d_N/d_S$ estimation (`yn00`), and maximum-likelihood codon models (`codeml`) applied to *INSL4*. The authors report newly identified relaxin-like homologs in chimpanzee, rhesus macaque, chicken, *Xenopus*, zebrafish, fugu, and rainbow trout, confirm the absence of the previously reported *Ciona intestinalis* relaxin sequence from the completed *C. intestinalis* genome, propose that brain-expressed *RLN3* is the ancestral vertebrate relaxin under strong purifying selection ($\omega = 0.02$), and claim that the emergence of *RLN1*, *INSL4*, and *INSL6* in mammals was driven by positive Darwinian selection.

While the genomic database curation—particularly debunking the putative tunicate (*C. intestinalis*) relaxin artifact and identifying pseudogenized *INSL5* loci in rat and dog—is a useful contribution, the phylogenetic and molecular evolution analyses that underpin the manuscript's primary conclusions are severely flawed. First, because the ~50-residue B+A alignment yielded unresolved and conflicting MP, NJ, and ML trees with $<50\%$ bootstrap support, the authors manually edited the gene tree topology to minimize duplications against a reference species tree and then used that manually enforced topology to infer lineage duplication history. Second, the headline claim that *INSL6* and *RLN1* (and their B domains) are evolving under strong positive Darwinian selection in primates rests on pairwise human-chimpanzee `yn00` ratios where $d_S = 0$ (producing PAML's arbitrary division-by-zero code $\omega = 99$ from a single nonsynonymous substitution in *INSL6*) or where tiny integer counts in ~25–50 codons are interpreted without statistical testing. Third, the `codeml` analysis of *INSL4* (Tables 4 and 5) contains wrong degrees of freedom, parameter proportions that do not sum to 1.0, mislabeled null models, and conflation of overall alignment rate heterogeneity (*RLN2* + *INSL4*) with branch-specific positive selection on *INSL4*.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit

* **Division-by-Zero Artifact ($d_S = 0 \implies d_N/d_S = 99$) Misinterpreted as Positive Selection (Table 3 & Figure 4):**
  In Table 3, pairwise `yn00` comparison of human and chimpanzee *INSL6* across the concatenated B+A domains (~150 bp) yields $d_N = 0.006$ and $d_S = 0.000$, which PAML outputs as $d_N/d_S = 99$ (an arbitrary placeholder code for $\infty$ caused by dividing by $d_S = 0$). A value of $d_N = 0.006$ over ~110–120 nonsynonymous sites corresponds to **a single nonsynonymous nucleotide substitution** and **zero synonymous substitutions** between human and chimpanzee. Under strict neutrality ($\omega = 1$), observing 1 nonsynonymous and 0 synonymous changes has a binomial probability of ~0.75—it is complete statistical noise with zero power to reject $\omega \le 1$. Even worse, in Figure 4, the authors split the alignment into individual B and A domains (~24–27 codons each!), obtain the same $d_S = 0$ division-by-zero placeholder (`99`) for the B domains of *RLN1* and *INSL6*, plot bars reaching `99` on the y-axis of Figure 4, and state in the Results and Discussion: *"These comparisons show the B domains of relaxin-1 and INSL6 to be under positive selection (both estimates were 99)."* Similarly, the human-chimpanzee *RLN1* B+A estimate of $d_N/d_S = 1.4$ ($d_N = 0.055, d_S = 0.039$, representing ~1 synonymous and ~5–6 nonsynonymous differences) is never subjected to a likelihood ratio test (which gives $p > 0.40$).
* **Manual Editing of Unresolved Phylogenetic Trees and Circular Reconciliation:**
  The authors report (lines 86–88) that because the C-peptide was removed and all gaps were stripped by hand, MP, NJ, and ML (`Tree-Puzzle`) failed to resolve the phylogeny of the ~50-residue B+A alignment, with bootstrap support $< 50\%$ on most major nodes. To overcome this, the authors state: *"The Tree-Puzzle tree was resolved using topologies conserved between the MP and NJ trees and then reconciled against a species tree using GeneTree. The inferred gene trees were then edited to minimize the incongruence (the number of losses and inferred duplications) with the species tree."* In Figure 2, the majority of internal branches carry no bootstrap/quartet support at all and are labeled *"inferred."* Manually editing an unsupported gene tree to minimize duplications against a species tree and then reading the resulting reconciled tree (Fig. 3) as empirical phylogenetic evidence for the timing and number of gene duplications (e.g., classifying fish and frog `RLX3`-like sequences as `INSL5` or `RLX2` orthologs) is circular.
* **Mathematical, Degrees-of-Freedom, and Model-Specification Errors in `codeml` Analysis of *INSL4* (Tables 4 & 5):**
  1. **Failure of the Branch-Specific Test (R0 vs. R2):** When testing whether the *INSL4* branch (Branch A in Fig. 5) had an elevated $\omega$ relative to the *RLN2* background, the two-ratio model ($\omega_1 = 1.1$ vs. $\omega_0 = 0.7$) yielded $2\delta = 0.3$ ($\text{df} = 1, P = 0.60$; Table 5), showing no significant elevation of $\omega$ on the *INSL4* branch.
  2. **Conflation of Tree-Wide Site Models with *INSL4*-Specific Selection:** Site models M1, M2, M3, M7, and M8 fit $\omega$ site classes across the **entire 19-sequence alignment** (which consists of 16 mammalian *RLN2* sequences and only 3 primate *INSL4* sequences; Fig. 5). Therefore, significant LRTs for M1 vs. M2 ($2\delta = 15.7$) and M7 vs. M8 ($2\delta = 10.1$) reflect amino acid divergence across mammalian *RLN2* + *INSL4* as a whole, *not* selection specific to the *INSL4* lineage.
  3. **Incorrect Degrees of Freedom and Misuse of M3 as a Positive Selection Test:** In Table 5 and line 172, the LRT for `M0 vs. M3 (k = 3)` is reported with $\text{df} = 2$. However, M0 has 1 $\omega$ parameter whereas M3 ($k = 3$) has 5 free parameters ($p_0, p_1, \omega_0, \omega_1, \omega_2$, as correctly listed in column $\rho$ of Table 4: `1` vs. `5`), so $\text{df} = 5 - 1 = 4$, not $2$. Moreover, in Table 4, row `Discrete (M3) (k = 2)` has $\omega_0 = 0.03$ and $\omega_1 = 1.0$—neither site class has $\omega > 1$—yet the authors list *"37 sites (P > 0.99)"* in the "Positively selected sites" column!
  4. **Inconsistent Parameter Proportions and Contradictions Between Text and Tables:** In Table 4, the site-class proportions for `Neutral (M1)` are reported as $\rho_0 = 0.1, \rho_1 = 0.8$ (summing to $0.9$, not $1.0$), and for `Discrete (M3) (k = 3)` as $\rho_0 = 0.1, \rho_1 = 0.5, \rho_2 = 0.3$ (also summing to $0.9$, whereas the text on line 172 states $15\%, 54\%,$ and $31\%$). In row `Beta (M7)`, the beta shape parameter $p$ is mislabeled as $\rho_0 = 0.2$. In the text (line 174), the authors state that Branch-Site Model A *"fits the data significantly better than its null model M0 ($P = 0.02$, d.f. = 2, table 5)"*, whereas Table 5 lists the null model as `M1` (not M0) and the $p$-value as $0.002$ (not $0.02$). Furthermore, comparing old Branch-Site Model A against M1 ($\omega_0 = 0, \omega_1 = 1$) is a well-documented anti-conservative test that conflates relaxed purifying selection with positive selection.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality

* **Claim 1 — The published *Ciona intestinalis* relaxin cDNA sequence is absent from the completed *C. intestinalis* genome and no invertebrate relaxin-like genes exist in sequenced invertebrate genomes: `[Well Supported]`.**  
  Comprehensive TBLASTN searches across completed invertebrate genomes confirm that the reported tunicate sequence (~100% identical to pig relaxin) was almost certainly a porcine laboratory contamination artifact.
* **Claim 2 — *RLN3* is the ancestral vertebrate relaxin and is constrained by strong purifying selection ($\omega = 0.02$ between human and mouse, comparable to insulin): `[Well Supported]`.**  
  The high sequence conservation of the *RLN3* B and A chains across teleosts, amphibians, birds, and mammals and its low $d_N/d_S$ ($0.04 / 1.83 = 0.02$) strongly support an ancient, conserved neuropeptide function interacting with GPCR135.
* **Claim 3 — Specific non-mammalian *RLX3*-like genes are orthologs of mammalian *INSL5* and *RLN2*: `[Fragile]`.**  
  Because the raw sequence alignments of the 50-aa B+A domains had $<50\%$ bootstrap support and the tree topology was manually edited to minimize duplications, orthology assignments of divergent fish and frog paralogs to mammalian *INSL5* vs. *RLN3* remain weakly resolved without synteny analysis.
* **Claim 4 — The emergence of *RLN1*, *INSL6*, and *INSL4* in mammals/primates was driven by Darwinian positive selection: `[Methodologically Flawed]`.**  
  For *INSL6* and *RLN1*, this claim is an artifact of dividing by $d_S = 0$ ($\omega = 99$) or overinterpreting a handful of substitutions between human and chimpanzee without statistical support; for *INSL4*, the branch test failed ($p = 0.60$) and the site/branch-site models are compromised by model-specification and degrees-of-freedom errors.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
The manuscript provides GenBank and Third Party Annotation (`BK...`) accession numbers for all analyzed sequences in Table 2 and the Methods section, and includes the exact trimmed amino acid alignment in Figure 1. However, because the alignments were manually edited ("edited by hand using Seaview to remove gaps") and the phylogenetic trees were manually modified in `TreeView` and `GeneTree` to reduce incongruence, the tree inference pipeline is not algorithmically reproducible from the raw sequences alone.
