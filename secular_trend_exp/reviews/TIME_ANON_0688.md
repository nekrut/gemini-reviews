### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This study reports the cDNA cloning, genomic localization, biochemical characterization, and tissue expression profiling of mouse tenascin-X (`TN-X`), a large extracellular matrix glycoprotein encoded within the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class III region. **Critical Archive Limitation:** The digital manuscript file (`TIME_ANON_0688.md`) contains only the complete Abstract (`Line 7`) and `Selected References` (`Line 12`, with the placeholder note *"The Full Text of this article is available as a PDF (2.7 MB)"*); the main text, Materials and Methods, Results, and Figures are not extracted in the markdown file. From the detailed experimental summary in the Abstract, the authors isolated murine `TN-X` cDNA clones, mapped the gene to the mouse MHC class III locus (syntonic with human `TNXB` overlapping the steroid 21-hydroxylase `P450c21` locus), identified an $\sim 13\text{-kb}$ mRNA transcript in most mouse tissues alongside $\sim 11\text{-kb}$ and $\sim 13\text{-kb}$ transcripts in mouse cell lines (indicative of alternative splicing), raised polyclonal antibodies against bacterially expressed mouse `TN-X` fusion fragments, identified the $\sim 500\text{-kDa}$ `TN-X` subunit in heart extracts and renal carcinoma conditioned medium (predicting up to 40 fibronectin type III repeats), demonstrated specific binding of native and purified `TN-X` to heparin (but not to tenascin-C, fibronectin, laminin, or collagens), and established via Northern blot and immunohistochemistry that `TN-X` is predominantly expressed in heart, skeletal muscle, and blood vessel walls throughout the body while exhibiting a reciprocal spatial distribution to tenascin-C (`TN-C`) in skin and gastrointestinal tissues.

- **Unextracted Main Body Text in the Source File (`PDF-Only` Archive Stub):**
  - Because the full text, Materials and Methods, biochemical binding curves, Northern blots, and immunofluorescence micrographs are contained only in the unextracted 2.7 MB PDF rather than in `/Users/sergei/Projects/lit-estimation/secular_trend_exp/anon_papers/TIME_ANON_0688.md`, quantitative experimental details (such as solid-phase ELISA vs. affinity chromatography dissociation constants for heparin vs. collagen/fibronectin binding, antibody specificity controls, and cDNA sequence coverage of the $13\text{-kb}$ transcript) cannot be directly audited.
- **Inference of Fibronectin Type III Repeat Number ($\sim 40$ Repeats) From Apparent SDS-PAGE Subunit Molecular Weight ($\sim 500\text{ kDa}$):**
  - The Abstract notes that *"The subunit molecular size of TN-X is approximately 500 kD, suggesting that the protein may contain up to 40 fibronectin type III repeats."* Because large extracellular matrix glycoproteins are heavily N- and O-glycosylated (and possibly chondroitin sulfate-modified), estimating the exact number of FNIII repeats from SDS-PAGE mobility ($\sim 500\text{ kDa}$) prior to complete $13\text{-kb}$ cDNA sequencing or enzymatic deglycosylation can overestimate the polypeptide core length.
- **Negative Matrix Protein Binding Assays (`TN-C`, Fibronectin, Laminin, Collagens):**
  - While heparin binding was positively demonstrated for both conditioned-medium and purified `TN-X`, the negative binding results for collagens and laminin under *in vitro* solid-phase conditions may depend on divalent cations, native fibrillar conformation, or bridging proteoglycans (such as decorin) present *in vivo*.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality / STRENGTHS
- **[Well Supported] Identification and Characterization of Murine `TN-X` mRNA and $\sim 500\text{-kDa}$ Protein:** Concordant detection of the $\sim 13\text{-kb}$ transcript on Northern blots and the $\sim 500\text{-kDa}$ polypeptide in heart extracts and conditioned media using antibodies raised against recombinant bacterial fusion proteins provides solid primary identification of mouse `TN-X`.
- **[Well Supported] Predominant Striated Muscle / Vascular Expression and Reciprocal Distribution with `TN-C`:** Demonstrating that `TN-X`, despite residing inside the MHC class III locus, is absent from lymphoid parenchyma and instead enriched in cardiac/skeletal muscle epimysium/perimysium, perivascular matrix, and dermis/digestive tract in a pattern reciprocal to `TN-C` is a foundational biological insight (later confirmed by human Ehlers-Danlos syndrome-like `TNXB` deficiency and mouse `Tnxb` knockout phenotypes).
