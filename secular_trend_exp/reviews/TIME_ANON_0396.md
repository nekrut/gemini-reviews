### 1. Executive Summary
This livestock candidate-gene study examines tissue expression and intronic insertion/deletion (indel) polymorphisms of the long-chain acyl-CoA synthetase 1 (*ACSL1*) gene in Dezhou donkeys. First, qPCR normalized to *GAPDH* was used to compare *ACSL1* mRNA levels across seven tissues (heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, stomach, skeletal muscle) between three young (2-month-old) and three adult (>2-year-old) donkeys. Second, 450 Dezhou donkeys (206 females, 244 males) from a single breeding farm were genotyped across four intronic indels (*ACSL1-1* [+15 bp], *ACSL1-2* [+38 bp], *ACSL1-3* [-8 bp], *ACSL1-4* [-7 bp]) by 10% PAGE and Sanger sequencing, and tested for associations with 10 biometric growth traits using one-way ANOVA and SHEsis haplotype analysis. While the genotyping dataset ($N=450$) is sizable, the statistical analysis omits all covariates (age, birth season, sire/kinship structure), misreports standard deviations as standard errors, selectively omits non-significant traits without multiple-testing correction, and bases diplotype claims on tiny homozygous subgroups ($n=3$ to $n=5$).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Oversimplified Statistical Model Lacking Age and Kinship Covariates:** In line 63, the linear model for growth traits is explicitly written as $Y = \mu + e$ (omitting even the genotype effect $G_i$ in the equation), and analyzed via unadjusted one-way ANOVA stratified only by sex. No adjustment is made for individual age at measurement, birth cohort/season, or pedigree/sire relatedness (population stratification within the breeding farm), all of which strongly confound morphometric traits and body weight.
* **Selective Reporting and Uncorrected Multiple Testing in Table 4:** Ten growth traits were measured across 4 loci in 2 sexes ($10 \times 4 \times 2 = 80$ association tests). Table 4 selectively reports only the subset of trait-locus-sex combinations that achieved $p < 0.05$ (plus male body weight at *ACSL1-3*), omitting *ACSL1-2* in females, *ACSL1-4* in both sexes, and non-significant traits at *ACSL1-1* and *ACSL1-3*. No Bonferroni or FDR correction is applied; under a Bonferroni threshold for 80 tests ($\alpha = 0.05/80 = 0.000625$), none of the single-locus tests in Table 4 (lowest $p = 0.001$) would remain significant.
* **Standard Deviation (SD) Mislabelled as Standard Error (SE) in Tables 4 and 5:** Table 4 and Table 5 headers state `"Observed genotypes (mean ± SE)"`. However, for *ACSL1-1* Female withers height ($n=144$), the reported value is $135.00 \pm 5.99\text{ cm}$, and for $n=5$ (`DD`), it is $140.40 \pm 7.60\text{ cm}$. If $5.99$ were the SE for $n=144$, the SD of withers height in adult donkeys would be $5.99 \times \sqrt{144} = 71.9\text{ cm}$ (biologically impossible for donkeys averaging 135 cm tall!). Thus, the reported $\pm$ values are clearly SDs mislabelled as SEs.
* **Underpowered Homozygous Diplotype Comparisons ($n=3$ to $5$) and Post-Hoc Letter Errors:** Rather than fitting a haplotype dosage regression or including heterozygous diplotypes across the 450 donkeys, Table 5 restricts analysis exclusively to 5 homozygous diplotypes totaling only $n=36$ females (with subgroup sizes of $n=3, 4, 4, 20, 5$) and $n=53$ males ($n=3, 3, 4, 38, 5$). Moreover, in Table 5, post-hoc significance letters (`a, b, c`) are assigned to traits where the omnibus ANOVA $p$-value is non-significant (e.g., Female chest circumference $P=0.088$ has `a, b` letters; Male withers height $P=0.721$, chest circumference $P=0.095$, and chest depth $P=0.074$ all have `a, b` letters).
* **Wild-Type vs. Mutant Allele Naming Inversion and GenBank Accession Typo:** In the Abstract and Results (line 79), the deletion allele `DD` is designated as the "wild genotype" at *ACSL1-1* and *ACSL1-2*, even though in Table 2 the insertion allele `I` has a frequency of $0.839$ (83.9%) at *ACSL1-1* (`DD` is present in only 5/205 females, 2.5%). Additionally, in line 33, the GenBank accession for *ACSL1* is written as `NW_01463740.1` (missing a digit compared to `NW_014637410.1` in Table 1).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Age-related increase in *ACSL1* mRNA in lung tissue:** `[Moderately Supported]` — Although the Abstract claims *ACSL1* expression is higher across all seven tissues in adult vs. young donkeys ($n=3$ per group), the Results section (line 71) clarifies that only lung tissue reached statistical significance ($p < 0.05$).
* **Superiority of `Hap3Hap3` and `Hap6Hap6` diplotypes for Dezhou donkey breeding selection:** `[Overstated]` — Recommending `Hap3Hap3` and `Hap6Hap6` for breeding based on $n=3$ to $n=5$ homozygous animals per sex and unadjusted $p$-values overstates weak exploratory observations.
* **Intronic indels in *ACSL1* directly regulate growth traits:** `[Fragile]` — No test of whether the four intronic indels alter *ACSL1* splicing or transcript abundance is performed, and intramuscular fat or serum lipid traits (the direct physiological readout of ACSL1 enzymatic activity) were not measured.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Primer and Locus Reporting:** Table 1 provides complete forward and reverse primer sequences, annealing temperatures, amplicon sizes, indel lengths, and scaffold accession numbers (`NW_014637410.1`, `NW_014638362.1`), though exact genomic coordinates of the four indels on `NW_014637410.1` are not tabulated.
* **Closed Primary Data:** The Data Availability Statement explicitly notes: "The data are currently not publicly available. Please contact the corresponding author for further information."
