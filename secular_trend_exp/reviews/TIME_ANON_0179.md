### 1. Executive Summary
This integrative study combines comparative genomics and transcriptomic phylostratigraphy across 1,144 chelicerate species (30 annotated genomes and 1,114 de novo assembled transcriptomes) with spatial proteomics of *Trichonephila clavata* major ampullate (Ma) silk glands, single-cell RNA-seq coexpression network analysis, co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP), and wet-spinning materials testing to investigate how lineage-specific "new genes" contribute to dragline silk performance in Araneoidea spiders. The authors classify 37,599 *T. clavata* protein-coding genes across 16 phylostrata (PS1–PS16), identifying 6,868 relatively young genes (RYGs, PS13–PS16) that originated after the divergence of Araneoidea (~182 Mya). Focusing on `SpiCE-DS8` (`Tc04G175120`), a 67-aa Nephilinae-specific orphan gene (42-aa mature peptide `SpiCE-DS8-L` with a 24-aa conserved core `SpiCE-DS8-S`) highly coexpressed with major ampullate spidroins (`MaSp`), the authors demonstrate via Co-IP in 293T cells that `SpiCE-DS8` interacts with the N-terminal domain of `MaSp1b`. Incorporating synthetic `SpiCE-DS8-L` or `SpiCE-DS8-S` (12.5% w/w in HFIP dope) into wet-spun regenerated *Bombyx mori* fibroin and transgenic `SP2-KI` (`MaSp2` knock-in) silkworm silk significantly increases $\beta$-sheet content (by ATR-FTIR) and fiber fracture toughness. While the multi-modal experimental validation from gene discovery to wet-spun fiber mechanics is impressive, the manuscript suffers from a classic phylostratigraphic artifact (BLASTP homology detection failure for short, rapidly evolving secretory peptides inflating "orphan gene" counts), missing statistical test details for the mechanical assays, and several text/number errors.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Phylostratigraphic Homology Detection Bias for Short Peptides:** Genes are assigned to phylostrata using progressive `Diamond BLASTP` searches ($E\text{-value} < 10^{-3}$), and Araneoidea-specific genes lacking BLAT ($>80\%$ identity, $>20\%$ coverage) or `DupGen_Finder` ($E < 10^{-5}$) hits in non-Araneoidea spiders are classified as "orphan genes." As shown in Fig. 2e and Supplementary Fig. 3b, RYGs and orphan genes are drastically shorter than ancestral genes (e.g., `SpiCE-DS8` is 67 aa total, 42 aa mature). Short, rapidly evolving secreted proteins routinely fall below BLASTP/Diamond bit-score thresholds across deep divergences (~180–300 Myr), creating false-positive "orphan" or "de novo" assignments unless synteny-aware micro-homology or profile HMM searches are performed. Furthermore, 1,114 of the 1,144 reference species rely on incomplete de novo transcriptomes (`Trinity`), where tissue-restricted silk gland transcripts are absent if silk glands were not sampled.
- **Co-IP and In Vitro Interaction Controls:** The Co-IP in 293T cells (Fig. 5c, d) properly tests full-length `SpiCE-DS8`, signal-peptide-deleted `SpiCE-DS8-L`, and the 24-aa conserved `SpiCE-DS8-S` against `MaSp-N`, showing that interaction requires the `SpiCE-DS8-S` core. However, no non-specific negative control bait protein (other than empty vector) is included to rule out general stickiness of the hydrophobic peptide, and the in vitro phase separation / fibril formation assay (Fig. 6a) relies on qualitative bright-field micrographs without quantitative turbidity or Thioflavin T kinetics.
- **Wet-Spinning Stoichiometry and Mechanical Statistics:** In the wet-spinning dope preparation (Methods line 68, where silk fibroin is mistranslated as "freeze-dried filipin material"), $0.35\text{ g}$ of silk protein is mixed with $0.05\text{ g}$ of `SpiCE-DS8` ($12.5\%$ w/w, a massive molar excess given `SpiCE-DS8-S` is ~2.5 kDa vs ~160–350 kDa fibroin/MaSp2, far higher than native ~2.8% proteomic abundance in dragline silk). In Fiber Characterization (Methods line 78), fiber diameter is measured on only $n = 3$ segments per sample while stress-strain tests are shown for $n = 30$ fibers in Fig. 6d; the exact statistical test used to generate the $p$-values in Fig. 6c–d (e.g., one-way ANOVA with post-hoc correction vs uncorrected pairwise $t$-tests) is never stated in the Methods.
- **Internal Text and Numerical Errors:**
  1. **Corrupted Percentage Range (Results, line 89):** States *"increases in the $\beta$-sheet content (from **28.46.45** to 37.09%)"*—a garbled number (`28.46.45`).
  2. **Misclassification of `SpiCE-DS5` and `SpiCE-DS8` as RAGs in Discussion (line 104):** States *"Both SpiCE-DS1 and SpiCE-DS3 (which are both RYGs)... along with the two **RAGs** identified in this study, SpiCE-DS5 and SpiCE-DS8..."*—this is backwards; `SpiCE-DS1` (PS10) is a RAG, whereas `SpiCE-DS5` and `SpiCE-DS8` (PS15) are RYGs.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *`SpiCE-DS8` (via its 24-aa conserved core `SpiCE-DS8-S`) interacts with the N-terminal domain of `MaSp1b` and increases the $\beta$-sheet crystalline fraction and toughness of wet-spun regenerated fibroin and transgenic `SP2-KI` silk fibers.* `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated by Co-IP truncation mapping, deconvoluted ATR-FTIR amide I band quantification, and $n = 30$ stress-strain curves.
- **Claim 2:** *Lineage-specific genes in PS13–PS16 are preferentially expressed in the major ampullate gland and enriched in dragline silk proteomes.* `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by multi-tissue $\tau$ specificity indices, WGCNA module `M5`, and segmented Ma gland LC-MS/MS (`iBAQ`), though phylostratigraphic age estimates of short secreted proteins are likely biased toward younger strata.
- **Claim 3:** *The radiation of angiosperms drove insect body-size evolution, which in turn drove the emergence of new genes and foraging web toughness in Araneoidea spiders across 225 Myr of paleoclimate history (Fig. 7).* `[Overstated]` — Purely narrative macroevolutionary correlation without any comparative phylogenetic trait-evolution modeling, paleontological body-size regressions, or selection tests linking angiosperm/insect diversity to spider gene birth rates.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Positive:** Software versions and parameters (`fastp v0.21`, `Trinity v2.11`, `CD-HIT v4.8.1`, `TransDecoder v5.5.0`, `Diamond v0.9.27`, `IQ-TREE v2.1.4`, `RAxML v8.2.12`, `MrBayes v3.2.7`, `HISAT v2.2.1`, `StringTie v2.1.5`, `MaxQuant v1.3.0.5`, `WGCNA v1.73`, `AlphaFold3`) are clearly documented, and transcriptomic/single-cell data are hosted on `SpiderDB`.
- **Missing:** The custom Python scripts used for progressive phylostratigraphic assignment and longest-ORF extraction are not deposited in a public repository, and raw LC-MS/MS proteomics files (`PRIDE`/`iProX` accessions) and statistical test specifications for Fig. 6 are not provided in the main text.
