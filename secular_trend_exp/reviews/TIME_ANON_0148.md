### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This cell biology manuscript dissects the cytoplasmic $\text{NH}_2$-terminal targeting motif responsible for the intracellular sequestration of the insulin-regulatable glucose transporter GLUT-4. Building on conflicting prior reports regarding GLUT-1/GLUT-4 chimeras, the authors express a systematic panel of $\text{NH}_2$-terminal truncations ($\Delta 8$ `mGS`, $\Delta 13$ `mEP`, $\Delta 16$ `mQQ`), alanine-scanning mutants (`PS->A`, `G->A`, `F->A`, `QQI->A`, `I->A`, `SED->A`), and heterologous single-pass type II membrane receptor chimeras (fusing the GLUT-4 or GLUT-1 $\text{NH}_2$ terminus to the H1 subunit of the asialoglycoprotein receptor, ASGPR: `HUT-4A`, `HUT-4B`, `HUT-1B`) in CHO cells via a recombinant Sindbis virus vector (and verified in stably transfected CHO-K1 clones). Using four independent, quantitative localization assays—(1) expression-normalized $[3\text{H}]2\text{-deoxyglucose}$ (2DOG) uptake, (2) sonicated plasma membrane (PM) lawn quantitative immunofluorescence, (3) WGA-FeDex high-gradient magnetic affinity chromatography subcellular fractionation, and (4) cryo-immunogold electron microscopy—plus confocal colocalization with clathrin heavy chain (mAb X-22), the study proves that $\text{Phe}^5$ (within residues 2–8, $\text{PSGFQQI}$) is the core determinant of a novel phenylalanine-based autonomous sorting signal that mediates clathrin-lattice association and intracellular sequestration.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
#### STRENGTHS
- **Four-Way Orthogonal Quantitative Validation:** Rather than relying on a single qualitative microscopy readout, every key mutant (`GLUT-4`, `mEP`, `Anita`, `F->A`, `I->A`, `G->A`, `1/4D`, `4A/1/4D`) is cross-validated across functional transport (normalized 2DOG uptake, Fig. 2), PM lawn pixel intensity (Fig. 2), magnetic affinity membrane fractionation with enzymatic/endogenous markers (galactosyltransferase, $\beta$-hexosaminidase, $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ ATPase, endogenous GLUT-1, Fig. 3), and cryo-immunogold EM particle counting ($n = 10$ cell profiles per experiment across 3 experiments, Table I).
- **Control for Viral Overexpression Artifacts:** Because Sindbis virus drives rapid, high-level expression (analyzed at 6 h post-infection plus 1 h cycloheximide chase), the authors explicitly compare Sindbis-expressed GLUT-4 against stably transfected CHO cells (`GLUT-4 stable`) by cryo-immunogold EM (Table I: $20.1 \pm 1.6\%$, $18.0 \pm 1.2\%$, $14.9 \pm 1.1\%$ for Sindbis vs. $18.2 \pm 1.7\%$ and $13.7 \pm 0.9\%$ for stable transfectants; Fig. 4A vs. 4B), ruling out viral saturation or cytopathic mislocalization.
- **Autonomous Gain-of-Function Validation in a Heterologous Type II Protein:** By transplanting the GLUT-4 $\text{NH}_2$ terminus onto the single-pass ASGPR H1 subunit (`HUT-4A` and `HUT-4B`) and assaying surface exposure via Proteinase K protection (Fig. 6, $n = 5$ experiments, using ER-retained immature H1 and endogenous GLUT-1 as internal protease integrity controls), the authors prove the motif functions autonomously outside the polytopic 12-TM transporter scaffold.
- **Mechanistic Link to Endocytic Machinery:** Double-label confocal microscopy on PM lawns (Fig. 8) demonstrates that wild-type GLUT-4 colocalizes with X-22-positive clathrin lattices, whereas `mEP` and `F->A` mutants are excluded from clathrin-coated domains.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Non-Insulin-Responsive Host Cell Line (CHO):** All experiments are performed in CHO fibroblasts rather than insulin-responsive 3T3-L1 adipocytes or myocytes; thus, while constitutive sequestration and clathrin endocytosis are rigorously demonstrated, the role of $\text{Phe}^5$ in insulin-regulated exocytic translocation from specialized GLUT-4 storage vesicles (GSVs) is not directly tested.
- **Digital Enhancement of Low-Abundance Wild-Type GLUT-4 in PM Lawns (Fig. 8):** In the legend to Figure 8, the authors note that "Because the level of GLUT-4 labeling at the plasma membrane was low, images of wild-type GLUT-4 were digitally enhanced" to show clathrin colocalization; while understandable given the 4-fold lower surface abundance of wild-type GLUT-4, quantitative colocalization coefficients (e.g., Manders/Pearson) across unenhanced raw pixel intensities are not reported.
- **Incomplete Reporting of Some Point Mutants in Table I:** While `PS->A` and `QQI->A` show intermediate effects in 2DOG uptake and PM lawns (Fig. 2), they were omitted from the cryo-EM immunogold quantitation in Table I.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Deletion of the N-terminal 8 residues or mutation of $\text{Phe}^5 \rightarrow \text{Ala}$ in GLUT-4 causes a marked accumulation of the transporter at the plasma membrane, with surrounding residues ($\text{Pro}^2\text{Ser}^3$, $\text{Ile}^8$) playing a secondary role. `[Well Supported]` across Figs. 2, 3, 4 and Table I.
- **Claim 2:** The GLUT-4 $\text{NH}_2$ terminus functions as an autonomous sorting domain when transferred to the H1 subunit of ASGPR, sequestering it more efficiently than H1's native $\text{YQDL}$ motif, and this sequestration is abolished by $\text{Phe}^5 \rightarrow \text{Ala}$. `[Well Supported]` by Proteinase K protection (Fig. 6) and immunofluorescence (Fig. 7).
- **Claim 3:** The $\text{Phe}^5$-based motif mediates colocalization with cell-surface clathrin lattices. `[Well Supported]` by confocal PM lawn double-labeling (Fig. 8).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Construct designs, exact amino acid sequences (Figs. 1 and 5), base-pair coordinates on rat GLUT-4 and human H1 ASGPR cDNAs, antibody identifiers (R820, R493, X-22), and fractionation marker recoveries ($60\%$ GLUT-1, $19 \pm 3\%$ galactosyltransferase, $8 \pm 2\%$ $\beta$-hexosaminidase, $85 \pm 7\%$ $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ ATPase) are thoroughly documented.
