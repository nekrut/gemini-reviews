### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript presents a comprehensive, multi-year, multi-location quantitative genetics and genomic breeding investigation of field-based tolerance to high iron-associated (`HIA`) soil stress (iron toxicity in acid lowland soils) in rice (*Oryza sativa* and interspecific *O. glaberrima* $\times$ *O. sativa* `NERICA` derivatives) across four West African sites: three HIA-stress hotspots—Edozhigi (Nigeria, pH 4.3, extractable Fe $1,230\text{ mg/kg}$), Suakoko (Liberia, pH 5.2, Fe $489\text{ mg/kg}$), and Vallée du Kou (Burkina Faso, pH 6.1, Fe $295\text{ mg/kg}$)—and two non-stress control environments (Lofa, Liberia, and Ibadan, Nigeria, pH 7.0, Fe $84\text{ mg/kg}$). The authors integrate four complementary analytical layers:
1. **Bi-parental QTL linkage mapping** across two large $F_5$ recombinant inbred populations sharing the iron-toxicity-sensitive recipient parent `IR64-Sub1`: `Pop1` (`NERICA-L-19` $\times$ `IR64-Sub1`, $n = 445$ lines genotyped at $1,767$ polymorphic DArTseq SNPs, evaluated in a $3\text{-replicate}$ alpha-lattice design) and `Pop2` (`NERICA-L-43` $\times$ `IR64-Sub1`, $n = 310$ lines genotyped at $1,897$ DArTseq SNPs in a $2\text{-replicate}$ alpha-lattice design), identifying 31 QTLs via composite interval mapping (`R/qtl`) that coalesce into three major regions (`Region 1` and `Region 2` on chromosome 1 flanking the semi-dwarfing locus `SD1`, and `Region 3` on chromosome 3 colocalizing with the heading-date regulator `DTH3`/`OsMADS50`).
2. **Genome-wide association study (`GWAS`)** of the Rice Diversity Panel 1 (`RDP1`, $n = 296$ global accessions genotyped on the 700K High-Density Rice Array [`HDRA`]) across stress and control field trials using mixed linear models (`EMMAX` with IBS kinship and up to 4 principal components across `AllPOP`, `INDICA`, and `JAPONICA` subpanels), identifying 68 QTLs and four reproducible multi-trait/multi-site clusters (`G2–G5`).
3. **Whole-genome resequencing (`WGR`, $\sim 5\times$ Illumina NextSeq 500)** of 32 regional breeding lines and landraces ($21$ *O. glaberrima*, $10$ *O. sativa*, and `NERICA-L-19`), combined with extended (`EH1–EH4`) and nested (`NH1–NH4`) haplotype network reconstruction across the 700K `RDP1` and 3,000 Rice Genomes (`3K-RG`) panels. This genomic forensics step uncovers a major, counterintuitive discovery: the favorable HIA-stress-tolerant haplotype (`EH1`/`NH1`) donated by `NERICA-L-19` across all three bi-parental QTL regions did **not** originate from its putative *O. glaberrima* donor (`TOG5681`) nor from its recurrent parent (`IR64`), but from an undocumented outcross to a rare stress-tolerant *indica* lineage during the breeding history of `NERICA-L-19`.
4. **Multi-year agronomic validation (`2017WS` field trial)** of extreme tolerant `RDP1` accessions (`Seratoes Hari`, `Pao-Tou-Hung`, `Chiem Chanh`, and `Bahia`) alongside `NERICA-L-19` and tolerant bi-parental progeny, contextualized against a curated 20-year literature synthesis of 212 published iron-toxicity QTLs standardized to the `MSUv7` reference genome.

The study is an exemplary piece of agricultural quantitative genetics: sample sizes are well-powered ($n = 445$ and $n = 310$ RILs; $n = 296$ diversity accessions), spatial alpha-lattice field designs and broad-sense heritabilities ($h^2$) are transparently reported, unfavorable trials are candidly diagnosed rather than hidden, and the resequencing-based lineage forensics resolves a long-standing misconception regarding the interspecific origin of `NERICA-L-19` stress tolerance.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Field Experimental Design, Heritability Auditing, and Transparent Exclusion of Failed Trials:**
  - The bi-parental populations (`Pop1`, $n = 445$; `Pop2`, $n = 310$) and diversity panel (`RDP1`, $n = 296$) were evaluated in alpha-lattice incomplete block designs with 2 to 3 replications and interspersed check varieties (`IR64-Sub1`, `NERICA-L-19`, `NERICA-L-43`, `WITA 4`, `Faro 52`, `Suakoko 8`, `Gambiaka`).
  - Best Linear Unbiased Estimates (`BLUEs`) and broad-sense heritabilities ($h^2$) were estimated using `PBTools` v1.4 and restricted maximum likelihood (`REML`) in `GenStat`.
  - Crucially, the authors report exact trial-by-trait $h^2$ values and audit low-heritability environments transparently: in the 2012 wet-season trial at Edozhigi (`2012WS-Edo`), severe HIA stress caused total grain failure across $>65\%$ of `RDP1` plots, yielding an unacceptably low grain-yield heritability of $h^2 = 0.06$ (compared to $h^2 = 0.72\text{--}0.89$ in the other trials). Rather than presenting the resulting chromosome 1 GWAS peak (`G1`) as a validated biological finding, the authors explicitly flag `2012WS-Edo` as noisy and exclude `G1` from their core candidate clusters (`G2–G5`).
- **Bi-Parental Linkage Mapping (`R/qtl`) and Colocalization with Developmental Loci:**
  - Genetic maps were constructed in `MapDisto` v1.8.1 after removing markers with $>20\%$ missingness or severe segregation distortion ($\chi^2$ test), yielding $1,767$ markers spanning $1,712\text{ cM}$ in `Pop1` and $1,897$ markers spanning $1,845\text{ cM}$ in `Pop2`.
  - Composite Interval Mapping (`CIM`) was performed in `R/qtl` with 1,000 permutations per trait ($\alpha = 0.05$, yielding empirical $\text{LOD}$ thresholds of $3.2\text{--}3.4$) and a $1.5\text{-LOD}$ support interval.
  - Because `IR64-Sub1` carries the semi-dwarfing `sd1` allele whereas `NERICA-L-19` and `NERICA-L-43` are tall (`SD1`) and differ in flowering time (`DTH3`), plant height (`PH`) and days to $50\%$ flowering (`FLW`) were appropriately mapped alongside stress scores (`fe05`, `fe07`, `fe09`) and grain yield (`GY`). The authors explicitly dissect whether grain yield QTLs in `Regions 1–3` represent pleiotropic developmental escape/vigor (tall stature and early flowering) versus stress-specific tolerance, demonstrating via control-site comparisons (`2014WS-Lofa`, `2015WS-Iba`) that the `NERICA-L-19` alleles confer a strong yield advantage specifically under HIA stress ($+14.1\%$ to $+26.4\%$ in Suakoko and Edozhigi) while showing neutral or slightly negative yield effects under non-stress conditions.
- **GWAS Model Specification and Outlier Trimming Trade-off:**
  - GWAS was conducted on 296 `RDP1` accessions using `EMMAX` with an identity-by-state (`IBS`) kinship matrix and 3 (`INDICA`, `JAPONICA`) or 4 (`AllPOP`) principal components (`SNP & Variation Suite` v8.4.0), applying subgroup-specific minor allele frequency (`MAF`) filters ($\text{MAF} \ge 0.05$ in `AllPOP`, $\ge 0.08$ in `INDICA`, $\ge 0.10$ in `JAPONICA`) and requiring $-\log_{10}(p) \ge 5.0$ supported by at least two flanking SNPs with $-\log_{10}(p) > 3.5$ within $200\text{ kb}$.
  - *Minor Statistical Critique (Outlier Removal Before GWAS):* Prior to running `EMMAX`, the authors removed phenotypic observations exceeding $\pm 2.5\text{ SD}$ from the trial mean as statistical outliers. In the `2017WS` validation trial, the authors discovered that four genuinely hyper-tolerant landraces (`Seratoes Hari`, `Pao-Tou-Hung`, `Chiem Chanh`, and `Bahia`, which achieved $4.1\text{--}4.9\text{ t/ha}$ in HIA stress hotspots where the panel mean was $\sim 1.5\text{ t/ha}$) had been winsorized/excluded from the 2012–2013 grain-yield GWAS precisely because their exceptional yields exceeded $+2.5\text{ SD}$! While the authors deserve high praise for discovering, validating, and candidly reporting this statistical artifact in the manuscript, a rank-based inverse normal transformation or sensitivity GWAS retaining those four extreme right-tail accessions should be reported in the supplement to verify whether additional rare/low-frequency tolerance loci emerge when the top performers are included.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Three major bi-parental QTL regions (`Regions 1–2` on Chr 1 and `Region 3` on Chr 3) and four GWAS clusters (`G2–G5` on Chr 2, 3, and 9) govern field performance under chronic HIA stress in West Africa, largely distinct from Previously Reported hydroponic seedling bronzing QTLs.* `[Well Supported]`
  - The synthesis of 212 historical QTLs reveals that $>80\%$ of prior studies relied on acute hydroponic $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ shock at the seedling stage, which correlates poorly with reproductive-stage grain yield in acid lowland fields. The field-based mapping and multi-environment validation convincingly demonstrate that root vigor, shoot biomass architecture (`SD1`-linked `Regions 1–2`), and heading synchronization (`DTH3`-linked `Region 3`) are primary determinants of field grain yield under chronic HIA stress.
- **Claim 2:** *The favorable HIA-stress-tolerant haplotype (`EH1`/`NH1`) across `Regions 1–3` in `NERICA-L-19` was inherited from an undocumented outcross to a stress-tolerant *O. sativa* ssp. *indica* donor rather than from its *O. glaberrima* parent `TOG5681`.* `[Well Supported]`
  - Whole-genome resequencing of `NERICA-L-19`, `TOG5681`, `IR64`, and 29 additional regional lines (`PRJNA657887`), integrated with high-density haplotype clustering across `RDP1` and the `3K-RG` database, proves beyond doubt that `NERICA-L-19` does not carry the `TOG5681` (*O. glaberrima*) or `IR64` haplotype at `Regions 1–3`, but instead shares an identical rare *indica* haplotype (`EH1`/`NH1`) with classic stress-tolerant *indica* landraces (`Seratoes Hari`, `Pao-Tou-Hung`, `Pokkali`). This is a rigorous and consequential finding for rice breeding programs.
- **Claim 3:** *Candidate genes within `Regions 1–3` and `G2–G5` (including `OsMADS50`/`DTH3`, peroxidases, cytochrome P450s, and `OsVIT1`/`OsNAS` homologs) directly mediate HIA stress tolerance.* `[Moderately Supported]`
  - The authors appropriately frame specific gene assignments within the $150\text{--}1,200\text{ kb}$ linkage/LD blocks as prioritized candidates based on nonsynonymous SNPs and expression/annotation evidence rather than claiming definitive single-gene causal proof without transgenic or CRISPR validation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Raw Sequencing & Genotype Data Availability:** Whole-genome resequencing reads for all 32 rice accessions are publicly deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive under BioProject accession `PRJNA657887` (with individual sample metadata, read depths, and mapping statistics detailed in the supplementary tables). The 700K HDRA `RDP1` genotypes and `3K-RG` variant calls are publicly available community resources.
- **Phenotypic, Genetic Map, & QTL Inventory Completeness:** Detailed soil chemistry profiles across all field sites, trial-level `BLUEs`, broad-sense heritabilities, `Pop1`/`Pop2` DArTseq genetic maps, complete lists of the 31 bi-parental QTLs and 68 GWAS QTLs with exact physical (`MSUv7`) coordinates, haplotype assignments across `RDP1` and `3K-RG`, and the standardized 212-QTL historical database are thoroughly documented across the main tables and supplementary files.
