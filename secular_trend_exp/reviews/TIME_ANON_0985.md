### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This comparative genomics and evolutionary biology study sequences, assembles, and annotates the whole genome of the Manado pipefish (*Microphis manadensis*, subfamily Nerophinae; trunk-brooder with a semi-enclosed brood pouch), generating $184.95\ \text{Gb}$ of raw Illumina HiSeq 2500 short-insert ($200\text{--}500\ \text{bp}$) and mate-pair ($3\text{--}17\ \text{kb}$) reads ($102.02\ \text{Gb}$ filtered) assembled via `ALLPATHS-LG` into a $653\ \text{Mb}$ assembly (scaffold $\text{N50} = 2.75\ \text{Mb}$, contig $\text{N50} = 68\ \text{kb}$, $99\%$ CEGMA, $92\%$ BUSCO completeness) supported by 21 RNA-seq libraries ($21,003$ predicted protein-coding genes, $94\%$ transcript-supported). Comparing *M. manadensis* against three previously sequenced tail-brooding Syngnathinae genomes (*Hippocampus comes*, *Hippocampus erectus*, and *Syngnathus scovelli*) and seven outgroup ray-finned fishes across $2,634$ 1:1 orthologues ($1,162,665$ aligned amino acids; $262,619$ fourfold degenerate [4D] sites), the authors establish that: (1) accelerated molecular evolution is shared across both Syngnathidae subfamilies; (2) *M. manadensis* underwent a lineage-specific expansion of *Tc1/mariner* DNA transposons ($33\%$ of the genome, $215.98\ \text{Mb}$); (3) all four syngnathids lost the pelvic-fin determinant *tbx4* and all P/Q-rich secretory calcium-binding phosphoprotein (*SCPP*) enameloid/dentin genes except *fa93e10* in their common ancestor; (4) nonclustered protocadherins (*Pcdh8*, *Pcdh10*) expanded in syngnathids with brood-pouch upregulation; and (5) applying the symmetric "convergence at conservative sites" (CCS) method across 17 vertebrates ($2,912$ orthologues) detects 125 foreground vs. 36 background convergent amino acid substitutions between syngnathids and placental mammals ($\chi^2$, $P < 0.05$). The primary genome resource and ancestral gene-loss analyses (*tbx4* and P/Q-rich *SCPP*s) are rigorous and compelling, whereas the deep-time molecular convergence analysis across a $\sim 420\ \text{Myr}$ divergence is more speculative.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES

#### STRENGTHS
- **High-quality outgroup subfamily genome assembly and transcriptomic support:** Adding a Nerophinae (trunk-brooding) genome with $2.75\ \text{Mb}$ scaffold $\text{N50}$ and 21 staged brood-pouch and tissue RNA-seq libraries resolves ancestral Syngnathidae synapomorphies from Syngnathinae-specific changes, correcting an annotation omission in *H. comes* by discovering that *fa93e10* is actually retained across all four syngnathids (Fig. 3).
- **Symmetric control design for molecular convergence (`CCS` method + `PAML evolver` simulations):** Unlike naive pairwise convergence scans that lack background controls, the authors use a balanced 17-taxon design (4 syngnathids + 4 placental mammals vs. 4 aplacental teleosts + 4 aplacental tetrapods, rooted on elephant shark *Callorhinchus milii*) restricted to conservative sites (`CCS`), and validate ancestral state reconstruction accuracy via `PAML codeml`/`evolver` simulations under `JTT+gamma`.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Elevated syngnathid branch lengths inflate foreground convergence relative to background in the `CCS` test:**
  - In the `CCS` convergence test (Fig. 6a), a site is eligible for foreground (placental) convergence only if all 8 aplacental control species (`A1-1` to `A2-4`) retain the ancestral elephant shark amino acid $O$ ($A_{1\text{--}4} = A_{2\text{--}4} = O$), while $\ge 6/8$ placental/syngnathid species share an identical derived state $\ne O$. Conversely, a site is eligible for background (aplacental) convergence only if all 8 placental/syngnathid species retain $O$ ($P_{1\text{--}4} = P_{2\text{--}4} = O$).
  - However, as the authors prove in Figure 1c, **all four syngnathids have significantly faster amino acid and nucleotide substitution rates (longer branches) than the four aplacental control teleosts** (medaka, platyfish, tilapia, stickleback)!
  - Because syngnathid branches are much longer than aplacental teleost branches, two things happen purely by neutral/nearly-neutral rate asymmetry: (1) syngnathids substitute away from $O$ much more frequently than aplacental teleosts, generating more foreground transitions; and (2) syngnathids violate the conservation condition $P_{2\text{--}4} = O$ more often, which actively disqualifies sites from being counted in the background aplacental convergence tally! Thus, the $125$ vs. $36$ excess is at least partly an artifact of the faster evolutionary rate of Syngnathidae rather than adaptive convergence for pregnancy, consistent with the fact that 118 convergent genes yielded only a single enriched GO term (`COPII vesicle coat`) and that highlighted substitutions like `I515V` in `ADAM12`, `I206V`/`V293I` in `NDRG1`, and `D401E`/`K614R` in `ASK1` are conservative hydrophobic or charge-preserving exchanges ($\text{I}\leftrightarrow\text{V}$, $\text{D}\leftrightarrow\text{E}$, $\text{K}\leftrightarrow\text{R}$) prone to parallel homoplasy.
- **Lack of quantitative differential expression statistics in main text for brood pouch RNA-seq:** Although 20 RNA-seq libraries across pregnant and non-pregnant brood pouch stages were sequenced, the main text only mentions qualitative expression differences for *Pcdh* duplicates (referencing Supplementary Fig. S6.4) without reporting biological replicate counts per stage, $\log_2\text{FC}$, or FDR-adjusted $p$-values, and without testing whether the 118 convergent genes are differentially expressed in the pregnant brood pouch.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim that edentulism and pelvic-fin loss in the common ancestor of Syngnathidae (Nerophinae + Syngnathinae) coincided with the loss of *tbx4* and all P/Q-rich *SCPP* genes except *fa93e10*:** `[Well Supported]` — Synteny and whole-genome comparisons across all four syngnathids and outgroup teleosts (Figs. 3–4) provide definitive comparative genomic evidence.
- **Claim that *M. manadensis* has an accelerated evolutionary rate relative to non-syngnathid teleosts and a massive expansion of *Tc1/mariner* transposons ($33\%$ of genome):** `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated by 4D neutral trees (262,619 sites), Tajima's relative rate tests on 2,634 orthologues, and Kimura divergence landscapes.
- **Claim that 118 genes with parallel amino acid substitutions between syngnathids and placental mammals reflect convergent evolution for pregnancy:** `[Fragile]` — Confounded by branch-length asymmetry between fast-evolving syngnathids and slower-evolving aplacental control teleosts in the `CCS` test, and dominated by conservative $\text{I}\leftrightarrow\text{V}$ / $\text{D}\leftrightarrow\text{E}$ / $\text{K}\leftrightarrow\text{R}$ exchanges without functional validation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
The methods clearly document all bioinformatics tools and versions (`ALLPATHS-LG`, `CEGMA`, `BUSCO`, `Trinity`, `TopHat v2.0.12`, `Augustus`, `GeMoMa`, `PASA`, `EVidenceModeler`, `InParanoid v4.1`, `ClustalW v2.0.12`, `PAL2NAL v14`, `Gblocks v0.91b`, `RAxML v8.1.3`/`v8.2.4`, `MEGA v7.0.18`, `RPHAST`, `PAML codeml`/`evolver`). However, NCBI BioProject/SRA/GenBank accession numbers for the *M. manadensis* genome assembly and 21 RNA-seq libraries are not printed in the main text (deferred to Supplementary Information).
