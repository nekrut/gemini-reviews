### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This comparative metagenomics and molecular evolution study investigates the genomic neighborhood organization, biochemical substrate enantioselectivity, and adaptive sequence evolution of bacterial nitrilases (`137` environmental metagenomic clones from `GenBank AY487426–AY487562`, `19` sequences from cultivated bacterial genomes, and `17` hits mined from the Sargasso Sea shotgun metagenome). Focusing on subfamily 1 nitrilases, the authors discover that all `7` cultivated bacterial subfamily 1 nitrilases (spanning Cyanobacteria, Beta/Gammaproteobacteria, and Verrucomicrobia, plus the *Klebsiella pneumoniae* plasmid `pLVPK`) and `14` basal environmental subfamily 1 clones reside within a highly conserved 7-gene biosynthetic/detoxification operon/cluster designated `Nit1C` (`hypothetical YchN1-like ORF1` – `nitrilase` – `radical SAM COG2516` – `GNAT acetyltransferase` – `AIRS synthase` – `conserved hypothetical ORF6` – `pyridine nucleotide-disulfide oxidoreductase ORF7`, `Fig. 1–2`). Mapping genomic neighborhoods and previously published hydroxyglutaronitrile enantioselectivity data onto a `PROML` / `PAUP*` maximum-likelihood phylogeny of subfamily 1 (`Fig. 3`) reveals a sharp evolutionary transition event (`TE`): basal `Nit1C`-associated nitrilases are almost uniformly `(R)`-enantioselective, whereas the derived environmental clade loses the `Nit1C` operon, becomes associated with `MarR` transcriptional regulators, carbohydrate epimerases, and epoxide hydrolases, and shifts to high `(S)`-enantioselectivity (`>95%`). Using `HyPhy` relative-rate tests (`5.6`-fold rate acceleration on `1A21`, `P = 2 × 10^-6`), `PAML codeml` site and early branch-site models (`Model A` vs. `M1` and `Model B` vs. `M3(K=2)` on foreground `Branch 2`, `P = 0.03–0.04`, `Table 1`), and `Jackal` 3D homology modeling (`1A21` on *Agrobacterium radiobacter* `D-NCAase`, `Fig. 4B`), the authors identify 6 candidate positively selected residues (`T41`, `Q157`, `Y184` near the `E44-K126-C160` catalytic triad, and `N200`, `Q203`, `R284` at the dimer interface).

### 2. STRENGTHS
- **Elegant Synthesis of Operon Synteny, Metagenomic Phylogeny, and Biocatalytic Stereoselectivity:** Linking a major genomic neighborhood transition (loss of the 7-gene `Nit1C` cluster and acquisition of `MarR`/epimerase/epoxide hydrolase neighbors) directly to an empirical reversal from `(R)`- to `(S)`-enantioselectivity on hydroxyglutaronitrile (`Fig. 3`) is a compelling integration of comparative genomics and enzymology.
- **Structural Contextualization of Candidate Adaptive Residues:** Mapping the 6 empirical-Bayes-identified sites (`T41`, `Q157`, `Y184`, `N200`, `Q203`, `R284`) onto the `α-β-β-α` dimer homology model (`Fig. 4B`) shows that they partition cleanly into two functionally plausible clusters: three buried active-site-proximal residues adjacent to the `E44-K126-C160` catalytic triad and three surface residues at the subunit dimerization interface.

### 3. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Use of the Early `PAML` Branch-Site Formulations (`Model A vs. M1` and `Model B vs. M3(K=2)`) Vulnerable to False Positives Under Relaxed Constraint:** In Table 1 (`Lines 75–91`), the authors apply the original Yang & Nielsen (`2002`, ref `[46]`) branch-site tests: comparing `Model A` (`l = -13188.7`) against site model `M1` (`l = -13195.5`, `2Δl = 6.8, P = 0.03, df = 2`) where `M1` fixes `ω_0 = 0` and `ω_1 = 1`, and comparing `Model B` (`l = -11621.4`) against `M3(K=2)` (`l = -11627.6`, `2Δl = 6.2, P = 0.04, df = 2`). Notice that `M0` (`one-ratio`, `l = -11903.5`, `ω = 0.0418`) fits the data `1,292` log-likelihood units better than `M1` (`l = -13195.5`) because `M1` unrealistically forces purifying sites to have `ω_0 = 0` exactly! As subsequently demonstrated in the codon-modeling literature, both `Model A vs. M1` and `Model B vs. M3(K=2)` conflate branch-specific relaxation of purifying selection or rate heterogeneity with positive selection (`ω_2 > 1`), which is why the modified branch-site test (`Model A` with `ω_0` estimated in `(0,1)` vs. `Model A null` with `ω_2 = 1` fixed) was introduced. Given the marginal p-values (`P = 0.03` and `P = 0.04`, uncorrected for testing `Branch 1` and `Branch 2`) and the fact that `Branch 2` is a deep internal branch where synonymous substitutions may approach saturation, the `codeml` signal (`ω_2 = 9.7` / `129.6`) is suggestive rather than definitive without site-directed mutagenesis.
- **Internal Figure Reference Typo:** Line 72 refers to `"focusing on the genes around the transition event (Figure 6A)"`, whereas the manuscript only has 4 figures (`Figure 1` through `Figure 4`; `Figure 4A` is the tree used for `ω` estimation, as correctly stated in Table 1 and Line 92).
- **Incomplete Flanking Sequence Coverage in Environmental Clones:** Because the environmental genomic DNA inserts were short (`1–7 kb`), many environmental clones in `Fig. 3B` only captured 1 or 2 flanking genes; thus, confirming whether the entire `MarR`–`nitrilase`–`epimerase`–`epoxide hydrolase` cassette is conserved across the post-transition clade was limited by insert length.

### 4. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Basal subfamily 1 nitrilases across diverse bacterial phyla reside in a conserved 7-gene operon (`Nit1C`), whereas a derived environmental subclade lost `Nit1C` association and acquired `MarR`/epimerase/epoxide hydrolase neighbors concurrently with a switch from `(R)`- to `(S)`-enantioselectivity. `[Well Supported]` — Robustly supported by comparative synteny (`Fig. 1–3`), phylogenetic trees of both nitrilases and flanking genes (`Additional files 2–3`), and empirical stereoselectivity assays.
- **Claim 2:** Positive Darwinian selection acted along `Branch 2` at the cluster transition event at residues `T41`, `Q157`, `Y184`, `N200`, `Q203`, and `R284` to drive nitrilase neofunctionalization. `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by the `HyPhy` relative-rate test (`P = 2 × 10^-6`), `codeml` Models A/B (`P = 0.03–0.04`), and structural proximity to the catalytic triad and dimer interface (`Fig. 4B`), though the early branch-site LRT formulation (`Model A vs. M1`, `Model B vs. M3`) and lack of experimental site-directed mutagenesis warrant cautious interpretation, which the authors appropriately acknowledge (`"identified sites in the enzyme that may have been under positive selection"`).

### 5. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Alignment Availability:** All environmental nitrilase GenBank accession numbers (`AY487426–AY487562`), cultivated bacterial accessions (`Fig. 1`), and full amino acid and DNA sequence alignments (`Additional files 4–6`) are provided.
