### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This study compares bovine coronavirus (BCV) field isolates from eight US states using three phenotypic assays: one-way hemagglutination-inhibition (HI) against polyclonal antiserum raised against the reference Mebus strain (44 wild-type fecal samples and 72 cell-culture-propagated isolates), isoelectric focusing (IEF) of purified viral proteins (7 Wisconsin isolates), and in vitro susceptibility to hygromycin B in HRT-18 cells (32 isolates). Most wild-type fecal samples (39/44) showed low or no HI reactivity against Mebus antiserum ("Type III"), whereas cell-culture-adapted isolates generally exhibited higher HI titers; 28 of 32 isolates were sensitive to hygromycin B and 4 were resistant. While the multi-state collection of BCV isolates provides useful descriptive data, the study suffers from internal numerical inconsistencies (including a 100-fold discrepancy in reported hygromycin B concentration and conflicting isolate counts), reliance on a strictly one-way HI assay using a single antiserum, and unsupported speculation regarding distinct serotypes and therapeutic use of hygromycin B in cattle.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS, WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
**Strengths:**
* Evaluates a broad geographic collection of US BCV field isolates at a standardized low passage number (passage 4) in HRT-18 cells, and performs back-titration of 4–8 HA units and 3–4 technical repeats per HI assay.
* Compares paired wild-type fecal suspensions and cell-culture-propagated isolates for 21 samples, demonstrating that cell-culture adaptation shifts HI reactivity.

**Weaknesses & Methodological Audit:**
* **100-Fold Discrepancy in Hygromycin B Concentration:** The Abstract, Methods (paragraph 6), and Discussion state that hygromycin B susceptibility was tested at `0.5 mM`, whereas the Results text (paragraph 4) states that susceptibility of the 32 BCV isolates was studied at `0.05 M` (50 mM).
* **Inconsistent Isolate Counts Across Text and Figures:** The Abstract and Introduction state that `94` BCV isolates were compared, whereas the Methods (paragraph 2) states that `96` isolates were propagated (`12 + 3 + 40 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 8 + 29 = 96`). Similarly, the Methods (paragraph 4) and Results (paragraph 1) state that `72` cell-culture-propagated isolates were tested by HI (`12 + 3 + 35 + 1 + 8 + 13 = 72`), whereas the Figure 2 legend states that `77` BCV isolates were inhibited. Furthermore, Results paragraph 1 states that among the 44 wild-type isolates, 39 were Type III, 3 were Type I, and 1 was Type II (`39 + 3 + 1 = 43`, missing 1 isolate), whereas the Figure 1 legend states `4` were Type I, `1` was Type II, and `30 + 9 = 39` were Type III (`4 + 1 + 39 = 44`).
* **Table 1 Typographical Error:** In Table 1, isolate `MN-37` has a post-hygromycin B titer listed as `<23` instead of `<2` (all dilutions were two-fold starting from 1:2).
* **Absence of Two-Way Cross-HI or Cross-Neutralization:** Serotype or antigenic subtype classification requires reciprocal (two-way) cross-HI or neutralization titers. Because the authors failed to raise a high-titer antiserum against a Type III isolate (`CA-1`), all HI data are one-way against the Mebus reference serum. Moreover, in fecal ("wild-type") samples, endogenous mucosal IgA/colostral IgG1, fecal mucins, or variable viral particle-to-infectious-unit ratios can sterically alter HI titers independent of true antigenic divergence.
* **Intra-Isolate IEF Variability:** On IEF gels (Figure 3), the anodic double-band shift (`pI 4.45` vs. `4.65`) was observed not only between isolates but also between *"different fractions of the same isolate"* (`WI-1-SK, fraction b` and `WI-9, fraction 140`), indicating that the pI shift may reflect variable glycosylation, sialylation, or purification fraction heterogeneity rather than a stable genetic marker.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Majority of tested BCV isolates (28/32) are inhibited ≥4-fold by hygromycin B in HRT-18 cells, while 4 Minnesota isolates are resistant: [Moderately Supported].** Table 1 documents clear ≥4-fold HA titer reductions in 28 isolates, although host-cell cytotoxicity controls (MTT/viability of HRT-18 cells at the tested concentration) and infectious plaque/TCID50 titers are not shown.
* **Cell-culture propagation increases HI reactivity against Mebus antiserum compared to raw fecal samples: [Well Supported].** Demonstrated across paired fecal and passage-4 isolates, consistent with selection or removal of fecal inhibitors.
* **Wild-type HI differences define distinct BCV serotypes (Types I, II, and III) warranting a multivalent vaccine: [Overstated].** One-way HI reactivity of unpurified fecal suspensions against a single antiserum cannot define viral serotypes, especially when the authors note that HE gene sequencing reveals minimal amino acid divergence and virus neutralization tests in prior studies show a single serotype.
* **Hygromycin B as a candidate clinical treatment for calf scours and a direct inhibitor of BCV RNA replication: [Overstated].** Hygromycin B is an aminocyclitol translation inhibitor known to enter permeabilized virus-infected mammalian cells and inhibit protein synthesis; claiming direct action on BCV RNA replication and lack of toxicity in cattle without any in vivo safety or efficacy data is speculative.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Data Completeness:** Table 1 reports raw HA units for all 32 isolates tested with hygromycin B, and Figures 1–2 summarize the HI distributions, though individual isolate HI titers for the 72 cell-culture and 44 fecal samples are only shown as histograms, and the SDS-PAGE / Western blot / RIP results for 11 isolates are cited as "data not shown."
* **Protocol Detail:** Classical virological procedures (kaolin/erythrocyte treatment of serum, sucrose gradient ultracentrifugation, polyacrylamide IEF) are described in sufficient detail to replicate, subject to resolving the `0.5 mM` vs. `0.05 M` hygromycin B concentration typo.
