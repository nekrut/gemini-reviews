### 1. Executive Summary
This candidate-gene case-control association study investigates whether single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the human mannose receptor C-type 1 (*MRC1*) gene on chromosome 10p12—a pattern-recognition receptor implicated in mycobacterial/propionibacterial phagocytosis and T helper type 2 cytokine modulation—confer genetic susceptibility to sarcoidosis in a Japanese cohort ($n=605$ unrelated individuals: 181 sarcoidosis patients and 424 healthy controls). Following resequencing of all 30 exons in 16 individuals to confirm the absence of common novel exonic variants, the authors selected 9 tag SNPs spanning the $102\text{ kb}$ *MRC1* locus based on pair-wise linkage disequilibrium ($r^2 \ge 0.80$, MAF $> 20\%$) and genotyped the full cohort using allele-specific kinetic SYBR Green PCR or TaqMan 5'-nuclease assays. Applying multivariable logistic regression adjusted for age and sex with Bonferroni correction for 10 comparisons ($\alpha = 0.005$), they identify a significant recessive association at the 3'-UTR SNP `rs691005` (`CC` homozygotes: 18.8% in sarcoidosis cases vs. 9.2% in controls; adjusted $\text{OR} = 2.53$, 95% CI $1.47–4.37$, $P = 0.001$; organ involvement $\ge 3$ organs $\text{OR} = 3.04$, $P = 0.002$). While the inclusion of age/sex covariate adjustment and explicit Bonferroni correction is commendable for a candidate-gene study, the manuscript is undermined by a severe baseline sex and age imbalance between cases and controls, multiple internal typographical/labeling errors in SNP identifiers across tables and figures, an arithmetic error in control allele percentages in Table 3 that sums to $103.0\%$, and the lack of an independent replication cohort or functional 3'-UTR validation.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Severe Demographic Imbalance Between Cases and Controls (Table 2):** Inspection of Table 2 reveals a striking demographic mismatch in recruitment: the sarcoidosis cases ($n=181$) are predominantly female (30.9% male; median age 45, range 19–82), whereas the healthy control group ($n=424$, recruited from hospital staff and medical students) is predominantly male (65.3% male; median age 38, range 20–74). Thus, the male-to-female ratio is inverted by more than 2-fold between cases and controls ($\chi^2 \approx 61, P < 10^{-14}$), and controls include young medical students who have not yet passed through the middle-age peak of adult-onset sarcoidosis. Although the authors include linear terms for age and sex in their logistic regression models (Tables 4–5), linear covariate adjustment does not guard against non-linear age-by-sex interactions in sarcoidosis incidence (which has a well-known bimodal age-sex distribution in Japan, with a second peak in postmenopausal women) or unmeasured recruitment stratification.
- **Internal Arithmetic Error in Table 3 Control Allele Percentages:** In Table 3, under locus `111065` (labeled `rs544995`, which is actually `rs554313`), the control allele counts ($2n = 848$) are reported as `G: 522 (63.4%)` and `A: 326 (39.6%)`. First, $63.4\% + 39.6\% = 103.0\%$, which is mathematically impossible! Second, dividing the reported counts by the total control chromosomes ($522 + 326 = 848$) yields $522 / 848 = 61.6\%$ for `G` and $326 / 848 = 38.4\%$ for `A`—meaning *neither* printed percentage (`63.4%` nor `39.6%`) matches the underlying allele counts ($522 / 63.4\% \approx 823$, whereas $326 / 39.6\% \approx 823$, suggesting an inconsistent denominator of $\sim 823$ was used when computing those two percentages).
- **Widespread SNP Identifier Typos Across Table 3, Figure 2, and Discussion:**
  1. In Table 3, locus `111041` (`rs554995` in Table 4, Table 5, and text) and locus `111065` (`rs554313` in Table 4, Table 5, and text) are *both* mislabeled with the identical refSNP ID `rs544995`.
  2. In the Figure 2 legend (line 80), those two SNPs are labeled `rs544995` and `rs544313` instead of `rs554995` and `rs554313`.
  3. In the Discussion (line 162), two lung-function-associated SNPs from Tables 4–5 (`rs2477637` and `rs692527`) are misspelled as `rs26777637` (an 8-digit number) and `rs692427`.
- **Subgroup Sample Size in Pulmonary Fibrosis Analysis:** In Section *MRC1 genotypes and disease phenotypes* (lines 147–151), the authors highlight an odds ratio of $\text{OR} = 4.02$ ($P = 0.032$) for parenchymal lung involvement (radiographic Stage IV, $n=14$ total patients) at `rs691005`. With only $n=14$ Stage IV patients in the entire cohort, subgroup logistic regressions across 9 SNPs have very sparse cell counts and do not survive multiple-testing correction.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Homozygosity for the minor `C` allele at 3'-UTR SNP `rs691005` in *MRC1* is significantly associated with sarcoidosis susceptibility ($\text{OR} = 2.53, P = 0.001$) in this Japanese cohort after Bonferroni correction. `[Moderately Supported]` — The recessive association at `rs691005` passes the pre-specified Bonferroni threshold ($\alpha = 0.005$) and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium checks, though confirmation in an age- and sex-matched independent replication cohort is needed.
- **Claim 2:** The `rs691005` 3'-UTR polymorphism may alter *MRC1* mRNA stability or translation efficiency to drive exaggerated Th1 granulomatous inflammation. `[Fragile]` — Explicitly presented as a hypothesis in the Discussion without *MRC1* mRNA/protein expression measurements in patient alveolar macrophages or 3'-UTR luciferase reporter assays.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Assay & Primer Documentation:** Table 1 provides complete forward, reverse, and allele-specific primer sequences (`5'` to `3'`), genomic positions (`NT_008705.15`), and amplicon sizes for all 9 genotyped SNPs, alongside clear descriptions of the kinetic SYBR Green PCR chemistry. The duplicate/erroneous `rs` numbers in Table 3, Figure 2, and line 162, as well as the `103.0%` allele percentage sum in Table 3, must be corrected prior to publication.
