### 1. Executive Summary
This hybrid experimental and mathematical virology study characterizes the duration and probability distribution of the intracellular eclipse phase for simian/human immunodeficiency virus (SHIV-KS661) infecting cynomolgus monkey CD4+ T cells (HSC-F) *in vitro*. By conducting single-cycle (MOI $= 4.2\text{ TCID}_{50}/\text{cell}$) and multi-cycle (MOIs $= 2.1, 1.05, 0.525, 0.2625$) infection time courses sampled across 8 time points (8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, and 73 h) and measuring both intracellular SIV Nef antigen positivity via flow cytometry and extracellular viral RNA copies/mL via qRT-PCR, the authors directly fit the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of eclipse-phase completion. They show that an exponential eclipse distribution fits poorly ($\text{AIC}_c$ penalized and mean overestimated at 1.86 d), whereas Gamma/Erlang, Weibull, and Normal distributions fit well and consistently estimate a mean eclipse duration of ~1.0 day (~24 h; Erlang shape $n_E \approx 20$). Applying the linear-chain trick to embed the Erlang eclipse distribution into the full ODE viral kinetics model and simultaneously fitting all 80 data points across the 5 MOIs reveals that prior ODE models neglecting the eclipse phase overestimated the viral production rate ($p$) and infectious cell lifespan ($1/\delta$) by 2–3-fold (overestimating burst size $p/\delta$ by 7–8-fold: 2,700 vs. 19,000–22,000 RNA copies/cell) while underestimating viral infectivity ($\beta$) by 7–8-fold, leaving the basic reproductive number $R_0 \approx 44$ invariant.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Direct CDF Identification and Model Selection via $\text{AIC}_c$:** Using intracellular Nef expression at $\text{MOI} = 4.2$ to directly observe the cumulative fraction of cells exiting the eclipse phase ($F(t)$) across $N_{\text{pts}} = 8$ time points and comparing candidate 1-parameter (Exponential) and 2-parameter (Normal, Weibull, Gamma) distributions via small-sample corrected $\text{AIC}_c$ is mathematically clean and avoids the identifiability confounding between eclipse duration and viral production/clearance rates in supernatant-only fits.
* **Batch Split Across Time Points (Experimental Design Caveat):** In the Methods ("In vitro experiment", line 107), the authors disclose: "This experiment was performed in dividing into twice. One experiment was measurement for 8, 12, 16 and 20 hours after inoculation and another experiment was measurement for 24, 28, 32 and 73 hours after inoculation." Stitching two separate experimental runs (8–20 h and 24–73 h) into a single 8-point trajectory without overlapping time points (e.g., measuring 24 h in both batches) introduces potential batch discontinuity right at the steepest inflection point (20–24 h) of the ~1-day eclipse CDF.
* **Cumulative Nef+ Fraction Interpretation Under Cell Death:** In Eqs. (23)–(24), the model equates the experimentally measured fraction of Nef+ cells among viable gated cells to $[I(t) + D(t)]/T_0$, the cumulative fraction of initial cells that have entered the infectious state (including cells that subsequently died, $D(t)$). However, in flow cytometry (line 112), only *viable* HSC-F cells (gated by forward- and side-scatter) were analyzed for Nef expression. If infectious cells die rapidly ($1/\delta = 5.9\text{ h}$) and exit the viable FSC/SSC gate, the viable Nef+ fraction is $I(t)/[T(t) + E(t) + I(t)]$, not $[I(t) + D(t)]/T_0$, unless dead cells remain within the gate or cell death is negligible during the first 32 h. This distinction between viable fraction and cumulative ever-infectious fraction should be explicitly reconciled.
* **Unweighted Multi-Scale Least-Squares Objective:** In the simultaneous fit to 80 data points across 5 MOIs, the objective function sums errors from the cumulative Nef+ fraction (bounded in $[0, 1]$) and log-transformed viral RNA load ($\log_{10} V \in [5, 9]$) without variance-stabilizing weights ($\sigma_{\text{Nef}}^2$ vs. $\sigma_{\log V}^2$), though visual fits across both observables (Fig. 4) are stated to be tight.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Non-exponential (Erlang/Gamma) distribution of the SHIV-KS661 eclipse phase with mean ~24 h:** `[Well Supported]` — Mechanistically, sequential intracellular steps (reverse transcription, nuclear import, integration, transcription/splicing, Nef translation) must produce a multi-stage (Erlang-like) delay rather than memoryless exponential transition at $t=0$.
* **Systematic bias in parameter estimates ($\beta, p, \delta$) when neglecting the eclipse phase while $R_0$ remains invariant:** `[Well Supported]` — The mathematical and empirical demonstration that absorbing a 24 h eclipse delay into an ODE without an eclipse compartment forces $1/\delta$ and $p$ upward and $\beta$ downward by 7–8-fold while preserving $R_0 = \beta p T_0 / (\delta c) \approx 44$ is a major, rigorous insight for viral dynamics modeling.
* **Equivalence of intracellular Nef positivity with initiation of extracellular virion release:** `[Moderately Supported]` — Nef is an early/multiply-spliced accessory protein expressed shortly after integration prior to late Rev-dependent structural (Gag/Env) protein assembly and budding; thus, Nef positivity marks the end of pre-translational eclipse slightly earlier than actual virion release, meaning the true virion-release eclipse phase may be slightly longer than the Nef+ eclipse phase.
