### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This landmark cell biology study identifies the cell-surface glycoprotein CD44 as a primary integral membrane binding partner for the ERM (ezrin, radixin, moesin) family of actin-membrane crosslinkers. Using `[35S]`methionine metabolic labeling and sulfo-NHS-biotin surface labeling of baby hamster kidney (BHK) cells, the authors co-immunoprecipitate a major 140-kD glycosylated surface protein (and minor 85/80-kD bands) with anti-moesin `mAb CR-22` in RIPA buffer. Exploiting the selective incorporation of host ERM complexes into budding rabies virions to screen rat hybridomas raised against intact BHK cells, they isolate `mAb 30189`, screen a BHK $\lambda\text{gt11}$ cDNA expression library to clone fragment `B10`, raise a second non-overlapping rat monoclonal antibody (`mAb \alpha`) against GST-fusion subfragments (`B10b`/`B10c`), and identify the 140-kD protein by sequencing as a hamster CD44 splice variant containing exons `v9/v10` (and the 85-kD band as standard `CD44s`). They further demonstrate that all three ERM proteins (ezrin, radixin, moesin) co-immunoprecipitate with CD44 in both BHK and mouse L fibroblasts (via `mAb IM7.8.1`), and that Triton X-100-insoluble CD44 precisely colocalizes with ERM proteins at microvilli, cell-cell adhesion sites, and mitotic cleavage furrows.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
- **Orthogonal Epitope Validation via Two Independent mAbs:** A standout methodological feature is the rigorous verification that cDNA clone `B10` truly encodes the 140-kD moesin-associated protein rather than a spurious expression-library cross-reactant. Because `mAb 30189` recognized GST-`B10a` (111 bp), the authors expressed the non-overlapping `B10b`/`B10c` fragment (`v9/v10` insert) in *E. coli*, immunized rats to generate an independent monoclonal antibody (`mAb \alpha`), and showed that `mAb \alpha` re-immunoprecipitates the exact 140-kD biotinylated band eluted in high salt ($0.98\text{ M KCl}$) from `mAb CR-22` moesin immunoprecipitates (Fig. 4C).
- **"Direct" Binding vs. Detergent-Stable Complex:** The authors conclude that ERM proteins are "directly associated with the cytoplasmic domain of CD44" because `[35S]`methionine autoradiography of RIPA-solubilized `CR-22` immunoprecipitates shows only the 140-kD band and moesin (and lacks the 72-kD ankyrin-like protein previously reported by Bourguignon et al. in 1% Triton X-100). While strong evidence for a stoichiometric complex, strict biochemical proof of direct binary binding without an unlabeled or low-methionine cofactor requires in vitro reconstitution with purified recombinant ERM FERM domain and CD44 cytoplasmic tail peptides.
- **Detergent Extractability Interpretation:** The comparison of 0.1%, 0.4%, and 1.0% Triton X-100 insolubility between the 140-kD (`v9/v10`) and 85-kD (`CD44s`) isoforms (Fig. 10B) is well-executed, and the Discussion appropriately frames the oligomerization hypothesis for why `v9/v10` binds ERM more stably than `CD44s` (despite identical cytoplasmic tails) as speculative.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
- `[Well Supported]` Co-immunoprecipitation of ezrin, radixin, and moesin with CD44 across two species/cell lines (hamster BHK cells with `mAb 30189`/`mAb \alpha` and mouse L fibroblasts with pan-CD44 `mAb IM7.8.1`).
- `[Well Supported]` Molecular identification of the 140-kD BHK protein as a `v9/v10`-containing splice variant of CD44 and the 85-kD protein as `CD44s`.
- `[Well Supported]` Double-immunofluorescence demonstration that Triton X-100 extraction removes diffuse surface CD44 while leaving cytoskeleton-anchored CD44 perfectly colocalized with ERM proteins at microvilli and mitotic cleavage furrows.
- `[Moderately Supported]` Speculative model that `CD44-ERM-actin` forms a central core complex to which other cleavage-furrow proteins (`CD43`/leukosialin) laterally associate.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
Buffer compositions (RIPA, high-salt elution, Triton X-100 fractionation), antibody clones (`CR-22`, `pAb I1`, `mAb 30189`, `mAb \alpha`, `IM7.8.1`), and sequenced amino acid alignments (113 aa of `B10` aligned to mouse `CD44v` and hamster `CD44s` in Fig. 5) are completely presented in the manuscript. No GenBank accession number is listed for the 339-bp hamster `CD44v` cDNA sequence, though the translated sequence is printed in full in Figure 5.
