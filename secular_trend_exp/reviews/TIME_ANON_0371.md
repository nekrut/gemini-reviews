### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents a high-resolution next-generation sequencing (NGS) population genetics analysis of all 13 functional killer cell immunoglobulin-like receptor (`KIR`) genes, two `KIR` pseudogenes (`KIR2DP1`, `KIR3DP1`), and their cognate `HLA class I` (`HLA-A`, `-B`, `-C`) ligands across 241 unrelated healthy individuals sampled from five Iranian populations: Lurs from Khoramabad ($n = 64$), Lurs from Yasuj ($n = 81$), Lurs from Lordegan ($n = 15$), Kurds from Sanandaj ($n = 48$), and Baloch from Zahedan ($n = 33$). Using targeted oligonucleotide capture covering both `KIR` exons and introns, Illumina MiSeq ($2 \times 300\text{ bp}$) sequencing, and the custom `PING` bioinformatic pipeline, the authors resolve 159 `KIR` allelic variants (including 11 novel alleles, 7 of which they independently confirm in a >1,000,000 stem-cell donor registry), infer 170 centromeric and 94 telomeric gene-content/allelic haplotypes (including 15 structural deletion/duplication haplotypes), and compare `KIR–HLA` co-distribution patterns against global reference populations from major continental groups. The study is methodologically rigorous, technically impressive in resolving full-gene `KIR` copy number and allelic polymorphism simultaneously, and appropriately cautious in interpreting its evolutionary findings.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **High-Resolution Copy-Number and Allele Calling (`PING` Pipeline + Sanger Confirmation):**
  - Resolving `KIR` polymorphism by short-read NGS is notoriously challenging due to extreme sequence homology, paralogous gene conversion, and extensive copy-number variation (`CNV`). The authors address this by combining full-locus oligonucleotide capture (206 capture probes), read-depth ratio normalization against the invariant framework gene `KIR3DL3` to determine gene copy number, allele-specific filtering (`PING`), and independent PCR + Sanger sequencing to validate novel SNPs. Furthermore, cross-checking the 11 newly discovered alleles against >1,000,000 bone marrow registry donors typed by high-throughput exon 3/4/5/7 sequencing confirmed 7 of the novel alleles in Europeans and Caucasians.
- **Sub-Population Sample Size Imbalance (`Lordegan` $n = 15$, `Baloch` $n = 33$):**
  - While the pooled Iranian sample ($N = 241$, $2N = 482$ chromosomes) is substantial for full-resolution `KIR` NGS, two of the five subpopulations—Lurs from Lordegan ($n = 15$, $2n = 30$ chromosomes) and Baloch ($n = 33$, $2n = 66$ chromosomes)—have small sample sizes. Consequently, allele frequency point estimates in Lordegan carry wide binomial confidence intervals (e.g., an observed frequency of $0.29$ in $2n = 30$ chromosomes has a $95\%$ CI of $\approx 0.14\text{–}0.48$). When comparing subpopulation allele frequencies (such as `HLA-B*51` at $0.29$ in Lordegan or `KIR3DL1*004` at $0.015$ in Baloch, representing a single chromosome), formal exact tests (e.g., Fisher's exact test with multiple-testing adjustment) and binomial confidence intervals should be reported rather than relying solely on point estimates.
- **Statistical Haplotype Phasing Across Copy-Number Variable Segments (`PHASE 2.1`):**
  - The authors use `PHASE 2.1` separately within the centromeric (`KIR3DL3`–`KIR3DP1`) and telomeric (`KIR2DL4`–`KIR3DL2`) intervals, anchored by structural gene-content priors (`Cen-A`, `Cen-B`, `Tel-A`, `Tel-B`). While effective for single-copy `A` haplotypes, statistical phasing of duplicated paralogs (`KIR2DL5A/B`, `KIR2DS3/5`) on `B` haplotypes without long-read physical phasing remains probabilistic; the authors appropriately treat `KIR2DL5` and `KIR2DS3/5` assignment to centromeric vs. telomeric segments cautiously.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **`[Well Supported]` Iranian populations harbor high `KIR` allelic diversity intermediate between European and South Asian populations, with elevated `Cen-A / Tel-A` haplotype frequencies:** Principal component analysis (`Figure 4`) and gene-content/allele distributions clearly situate the four Zagros Mountain populations (`Yasuj`, `Khoramabad`, `Lordegan`, `Sanandaj`) near European/Southwest Asian references while placing the southeastern `Baloch` population along a cline toward the Indian subcontinent (`Tel-B` enrichment).
- **`[Well Supported]` High frequency of `KIR3DL1*001` ($0.24\text{–}0.29$) co-occurs with record-high frequencies of its cognate `Bw4` ligand `HLA-B*51` ($0.19\text{–}0.29$) in Iranian populations:** Both the receptor allele (`KIR3DL1*001`, a high-expressing, strongly inhibitory allotype formed by ancestral fusion of `*005`-like D0 and `*015`-like D1–D2 motifs) and its ligand (`HLA-B*51`) are directly measured in the same individuals, and Table 2 demonstrates that Iranian populations maintain a mean of $2.1\text{–}2.5$ viable `Bw4 + KIR3DL1` interactions per individual (exceeding European and African references).
- **`[Moderately Supported]` Co-evolutionary natural selection shaped the reciprocal frequencies of `KIR3DL1*001` and `KIR3DL1*004` relative to `HLA-B*51`:** While the functional immunology (`KIR3DL1*001` is cell-surface expressed and strongly inhibited by `Bw4`, whereas `KIR3DL1*004` is retained intracellularly) makes balancing/directional co-selection a compelling hypothesis, the authors do not perform formal neutrality or demographic simulation tests (e.g., $F_{\text{ST}}$ outlier tests or linkage/co-occurrence permutation tests conditioned on background population structure) to rule out genetic drift in small endogamous populations (`Lordegan`, `Baloch`).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** All 11 novel `KIR` allele sequences were deposited in GenBank and the IPD-KIR database with explicit accession numbers (`KP025959`–`KP025967`, `KT823613`, `KM236100`; WHO Nomenclature Committee designations `KIR2DS1*011` through `KIR2DP1*021` in `Table 1`). Oligonucleotide capture probe sequences, MiSeq read formatting, `PING` bioinformatic workflow parameters, and comprehensive allele/haplotype frequency tables (`Supplementary Figures S1–S5`) are provided.
- **Minor Additions:** Providing confidence intervals or exact $p$-values for key subpopulation frequency comparisons (`KIR3DL1*001`, `KIR3DL1*004`, `HLA-B*51`) would further strengthen the statistical presentation.
