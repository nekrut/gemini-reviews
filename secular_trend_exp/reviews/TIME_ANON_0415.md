### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This clinical case series reports 4 pediatric/adolescent patients with genetically confirmed interferon-$\gamma$ receptor deficiency (3 with `IFNGR1` mutations: homozygous `201-2A->G`, compound heterozygous `561del4`/`373+1G->T`, and dominant-negative heterozygous `817insA`; 1 with homozygous `IFNGR2` mutation `278delA,G`) and disseminated nontuberculous mycobacterial or BCG infections who also experienced severe or symptomatic viral infections (CMV, PIV-3, and RSV pneumonias requiring mechanical ventilation in Patient 1; HSV gingivostomatitis/esophagitis in Patient 2; prolonged VZV rash in Patient 3; VZV pneumonia requiring mechanical ventilation in Patient 4). The authors propose that human IFN-$\gamma$ receptor deficiency increases clinical susceptibility to herpesviruses and respiratory RNA viruses.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
- **Absence of a Denominator or Control Cohort (Ascertainment Bias):** The report presents 4 selected cases without specifying the total number of IFN-$\gamma$R-deficient patients screened at the participating centers. Without an incidence denominator or comparison to age-matched hospitalized controls with severe disseminated mycobacterial disease, it is statistically impossible to distinguish a primary defect in antiviral immunity from secondary vulnerability due to chronic debilitation, malnutrition, prolonged hospitalization, and nosocomial exposure.
- **Confounding by Concomitant Mycobacterial Organ Damage:** Patient 1 (who accounted for all three non-VZV/HSV respiratory failure events: CMV, PIV-3, and RSV, plus two culture-negative respiratory failure episodes) had preexisting disseminated MAC pneumonia and hepatosplenomegaly from 8 months of age. Structural lung damage and severe systemic illness strongly confound the attribution of severe RSV/PIV-3 pneumonia solely to `IFNGR1` deficiency.
- **Diagnostic Specificity:** Viral diagnoses were appropriately established via viral culture of BAL/lesions (Patients 1 and 2) or EM/IgM serology (Patients 3 and 4), and specific `IFNGR1`/`IFNGR2` genotypes are tabulated clearly.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
- `[Well Supported]` Detailed clinical and genetic documentation of the 4 cases, linking specific `IFNGR1` and `IFNGR2` mutations to clinical histories of disseminated mycobacterial and viral infections.
- `[Moderately Supported]` The translational parallel drawn to `Ifng` and `Ifngr1` knockout mouse models, which exhibit heightened susceptibility to HSV, MCMV, murine gammaherpesvirus, and certain RNA viruses.
- `[Fragile]` The conclusion that human IFN-$\gamma$ receptor deficiency intrinsically increases susceptibility to viral pathogens such that it "should be included in the differential diagnosis in children with severe viral infections." The authors themselves acknowledge in the Discussion that larger European cohorts of IFN-$\gamma$R-deficient children (e.g., Jouanguy et al.) recovered normally from VZV, RSV, influenza, and rotavirus and seroconverted to HSV, EBV, and CMV without clinical disease, and that concomitant mycobacterial disease and poor clinical status may favor viral disease in these 4 patients.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
Clinical case details, exact nucleotide mutations (`IFNγR1; 201-2A→G`, `IFNγR2; 278delA,G`, `IFNγR1; 561del4; 373+1G→T`, `IFNγR1; 817insA`), diagnostic modalities, and therapeutic interventions (ganciclovir, ribavirin, RSV-IG, acyclovir) are fully summarized in the text and Table.
