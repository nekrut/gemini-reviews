### 1. Executive Summary
Building on a prior PAR-CLIP dataset that identified 4,488 mRNAs bound by wild-type EWS (EWSR1), this study investigates the functional consequences of wild-type EWS loss (modeling EWSR1 haploinsufficiency in Ewing sarcoma). Using siRNA knockdown of EWS in HEK293T cells followed by Affymetrix U133 Plus 2.0 microarrays, the authors identify 116 bound and regulated transcripts ($p_{\text{adj}} < 0.05$, $\ge 50\%$ change) and focus on *CCDC6*, a cell-cycle and DNA damage regulator bound by EWS in its 3'UTR. They show that EWS knockdown reduces *CCDC6* mRNA and protein in HEK293T and MHH-ES-1 Ewing sarcoma cells, shortens *CCDC6* mRNA half-life under Actinomycin D treatment (~4.1 h to ~3.2 h), and increases apoptosis while reducing S and G2/M phase populations, with partial rescue of apoptosis by *CCDC6* overexpression. While the biological question regarding wild-type *EWSR1* haploinsufficiency is interesting, the study suffers from internal numerical contradictions between the main text and figure legends, reliance on a single siRNA without independent hairpin validation, improper linear fitting of exponential mRNA decay, and heavy reliance on HEK293T cells rather than Ewing sarcoma models for functional rescue assays.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Direct Numerical Contradiction Between Main Text and Figure 2C Legend:** In the Results section ("Downregulation of EWS affects viability, proliferation and cell cycle", line 128), the authors state: *"We recorded an increase in the percentage of apoptotic cells in EWS siRNA treated cells (63% living cells, 27% apoptotic and 10% necrotic cells) compared to controls (74% living, 13% apoptotic and 13% necrotic cells) (Fig. 2C) while the % necrotic cells remained unchanged."* However, the Figure 2C caption (line 114) states: *"Mock, scrambled and EWS KD had 10%, 11.8% and 20% apoptotic cells and 17%, 22% and 30% of necrotic cells respectively."* These two sets of numbers disagree completely on both the apoptotic fractions ($27\%$ vs. $20\%$) and whether necrotic cells remained unchanged ($13\% \to 10\%$) or increased ($22\% \to 30\%$).
- **Single siRNA Sequence and Off-Target Risk:** All EWS knockdown experiments appear to rely on a single siRNA targeting the C-terminal RRM region of *EWSR1* ("the sequence of the siRNA and the figure showing the region being targeted by the siRNA see the S1 Table and S1A Fig"). Without at least a second independent siRNA or an EWS cDNA rescue of *CCDC6* expression, off-target seed-mediated silencing of *CCDC6* or other survival genes cannot be excluded.
- **Improper Linear Regression of First-Order mRNA Decay:** In Figure 1D and the Methods (lines 17 and 74), mRNA half-life after Actinomycin D treatment was calculated by plotting percentage of remaining RNA on a *linear* scale against time ($0\text{--}12\text{ h}$) and fitting a linear regression slope ("The linear regression and slopes were calculated and the data is presented as Mean and SEM on a linear scale"). First-order mRNA decay follows $N(t) = N_0 e^{-kt}$ and must be fit log-linearly or via nonlinear exponential regression; linear regression over a 12-hour window (~3 half-lives) systematically distorts decay constants. Furthermore, the reported Actinomycin D concentration in Methods ("3$\mu$g/$\mu$l", i.e., $3\text{ mg/mL}$) is almost certainly a 1,000-fold unit error for $3\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$, and using $\beta$-actin across 12 hours of ActD treatment without accounting for global RNA loss can bias normalization.
- **Luciferase Reporter Normalization Ambiguity:** In the 3'UTR reporter assay (Fig. 1E), the authors co-transfect psiCHECK-2 containing the *CCDC6* 3'UTR with an EWS expression plasmid in HEK293T cells (which already express high endogenous EWS). If EWS stabilizes the *CCDC6* 3'UTR, testing EWS knockdown on the reporter (and mutating the PAR-CLIP binding clusters within the 3'UTR) would be far more rigorous than overexpressing EWS against an empty psiCHECK-2 control.
- **Statistical Testing Details:** Multiple multi-group and multi-timepoint comparisons (qRT-PCR across 4 genes and 3 conditions, flow cytometry across cell cycle phases, CCK-8 across timepoints) are analyzed with "two way ANOVA or unpaired t test" at $n = 3$ without specifying post-hoc multiple-comparison corrections or whether $n = 3$ represents independent biological transfections or technical triplicates.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *"EWS regulates the expression levels of CCDC6 by stabilizing it and further exerting its effect on the expression of its downstream targets."* — `[Moderately Supported]`. PAR-CLIP 3'UTR binding, qRT-PCR, and Western blot down-regulation upon EWS siRNA treatment in both HEK293T and MHH-ES-1 cells are consistent, though direct binding specificity is not tested via binding-site point mutations in the 3'UTR reporter.
- **Claim 2:** *"siRNA mediated down regulation of EWS caused an elevated apoptosis in cells in a CCDC6-dependant manner. This effect was rescued upon re-expression of CCDC6. This study provides evidence for a novel functional link through which wild-type EWS operates in a target-dependant manner in Ewing sarcoma."* — `[Overstated]`. The apoptosis, *CCDC6* rescue, proliferation, and cell-cycle experiments (Fig. 2C–F) were performed in HEK293T cells (explicitly stated in line 128 for Fig. 2C, and unspecified for Fig. 2D–F), not in Ewing sarcoma cells, and furthermore model near-complete siRNA knockdown rather than the heterozygous 50% dosage reduction (haploinsufficiency) present in Ewing sarcoma.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Antibody catalog numbers are provided (except Santa Cruz secondary antibody has a redacted/corrupted number `sc-[YEAR]`).
- Microarray accession numbers (e.g., GEO/ArrayExpress) for the Affymetrix U133 Plus 2.0 EWS knockdown experiment are not provided in the Data Availability statement.
- Key methodological errors in the text (e.g., "3$\mu$g/$\mu$l Actinomycin D", "50mg/ml of propidium iodide" in Nicoletti buffer instead of $50\text{ }\mu\text{g/mL}$, and the conflicting numbers between line 128 and Fig. 2C legend) hinder exact replication.
