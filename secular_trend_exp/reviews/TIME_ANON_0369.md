### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript applies a standard commodity codon usage bias (CUB) bioinformatics pipeline—comprising RAxML phylogenetic tree inference, nucleotide composition, relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU), correspondence/principal component analysis (PCA), effective number of codons ($\text{ENC}$), $\text{ENC}$-plot ($\text{ENC}$ vs. $\text{GC}_{3\text{s}}$), Parity Rule 2 (PR2) plot, neutrality plot ($\text{GC}_{12\text{s}}$ vs. $\text{GC}_{3\text{s}}$), codon adaptation index ($\text{CAI}$), relative codon deoptimization index ($\text{RCDI}$), similarity index ($\text{SiD}$), and relative dinucleotide abundance—to hemagglutinin (`HA`) and neuraminidase (`NA`) coding sequences of avian influenza A (`H7N9`) virus across five epidemic waves, host species (*Gallus gallus* vs. *Homo sapiens*), and virulence phenotypes (high vs. low pathogenicity) in China. Beyond being a formulaic template study that ignores phylogenetic non-independence among closely related outbreak isolates, the paper contains fatal mathematical and conceptual contradictions: the authors invert the definition of Sueoka's neutrality plot in the Methods, report regression slopes with massive standard errors ($\pm 0.45\text{ to }0.54$) as having *"extremely significant ($p < 0.0001$)"* correlations, directly contradict their own Results in the Discussion regarding whether human or chicken CAI is higher, and erroneously report intrinsic viral dinucleotide (`CpG`) frequencies as if they depend on a reference host genome.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Mathematical Contradictions and Statistical Errors in Neutrality Plot Analysis:**
  - **Inverted Definition in Methods (Line 127):** In Section 4.9, the authors state: *"A neutrality plot ($\text{GC}_{12\text{s}}$ against $\text{GC}_{3\text{s}}$) was used to evaluate the degree of mutational pressure and natural selection on the CUB... The higher the slope, the greater the effect of natural selection pressure."* This is the exact opposite of Sueoka's neutrality theory, where a slope of $1.0$ indicates $100\%$ directional mutational pressure across all codon positions and a slope of $0.0$ indicates $0\%$ mutational pressure ($100\%$ constraint/selection). Worse, in Results Section 2.6 (line 53), the authors use the *standard* formula ($1 - |\text{slope}|$) to claim that slopes of $0.0017$, $-0.251$, and $-0.117$ correspond to $99\%$, $75\%$, and $89\%$ natural selection—directly contradicting their own Methods section.
  - **Implausible Standard Errors and $p$-Values (Line 53):** Results Section 2.6 reports: *"For HP-H7N9, the slopes of the H7, N9, and whole H7N9 were $0.0017 \pm 0.4562$, $-0.251 \pm 0.5428$, and $-0.117 \pm 0.4966$, respectively, and the correlation between the indexes was extremely significant ($p < 0.0001$)."* If the standard error of a linear regression slope is $0.4562$ while the point estimate is $0.0017$, the $t$-statistic is $t = 0.0017 / 0.4562 = 0.0037$, which yields $p \approx 0.997$, **not** $p < 0.0001$. Even if the authors confused the slope standard error with the intercept or residual standard deviation, reporting $p < 0.0001$ alongside a slope of $0.0017 \pm 0.4562$ is statistically incoherent.
- **Direct Self-Contradiction Between Results and Discussion on CAI and RCDI:**
  - In Results Section 2.7 (line 57), the authors state: *"The results revealed higher CAI values in Gallus gallus compared with Homo sapiens. Moreover, the results of RCDI showed higher values for Homo sapiens than Gallus gallus."*
  - In Discussion (line 76), the authors write the exact opposite: *"In this study, we found that on the basis of host classification, the CAI value of Homo sapiens was higher than that of Gallus gallus; for RCDI, the value of Homo sapiens was lower than that of Gallus gallus."*
  - A manuscript that reverses the direction of its primary host-adaptation metrics between the Results and Discussion cannot be trusted.
- **Conceptual Error in Dinucleotide Relative Abundance (Section 2.8, Line 65):**
  - Relative dinucleotide abundance ($P_{xy} = f_{xy} / (f_x f_y)$) is an intrinsic property of the viral nucleotide sequence itself and does not use a host reference table. Yet in Section 2.8 (line 65), the authors state: *"We found the CpG content of the H7 and N9 genes was visibly lower than the other dinucleotides (Figure 7), both for Gallus gallus and for Homo sapiens ($0.33 \pm 0.015$ and $0.33 \pm 0.014$ for the H7 gene; $0.408 \pm 0.010$ and $0.409 \pm 0.009$ for the N9 gene, respectively)."* If they meant strains *isolated from* chickens versus humans, that is a classification by isolate origin (which they called "host" in Table 1), whereas in Section 2.7 "for Gallus gallus and for Homo sapiens" denoted computing $\text{CAI}/\text{RCDI}/\text{SiD}$ of the *entire* viral dataset against the chicken vs. human codon usage reference tables. Conflating isolate host metadata with reference codon tables renders the comparisons ambiguous.
- **Phylogenetic Non-Independence and Uncorrected Multiple Testing:** Treating hundreds of nearly identical epidemic outbreak sequences (mean $\text{ENC} = 48.72 \pm 0.25$ for `H7` and $52.53 \pm 0.24$ for `N9`, spanning less than 1 ENC unit across all waves) as independent observations in one-way ANOVAs and two-sample $t$-tests grossly inflates degrees of freedom and mistakes clonal founder effects across epidemic waves for adaptive evolution.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **`[Well Supported]` H7N9 `HA` and `NA` genes exhibit weak overall codon usage bias ($\text{ENC} \approx 48.5\text{–}53.5$) and AT-rich composition ($\text{A}+\text{T} \approx 57\%$):** This is a standard, well-known property of influenza A viruses.
- **`[Methodologically Flawed]` Natural selection accounts for 75%–99% of H7N9 codon usage bias based on neutrality plots:** In a clonal epidemic expansion over a few years where $\text{GC}_{3\text{s}}$ varies by less than $1\text{–}2\%$ across isolates, plotting $\text{GC}_{12\text{s}}$ against $\text{GC}_{3\text{s}}$ fits a regression line to a tight point cloud of near-identical sequences; the near-zero slope reflects lack of mutational divergence rather than intense purifying/positive selection on synonymous sites.
- **`[Overstated]` Host-specific codon adaptation inferences between *Homo sapiens* and *Gallus gallus*:** Because the Results and Discussion state opposite directions for `CAI` and `RCDI` between human and chicken reference tables, and because `H7` (`CAI` higher in one host) and `N9` (`CAI` higher in the other or identical) are Not even reported separately with clear numerical tables in the main text, all host-adaptation claims are unsupported.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Missing Primary Tables and Sample Counts:** Exact sequence counts in the Abstract and Methods were replaced by `[YEAR]` placeholders during text processing (e.g., `"[YEAR] HA and [YEAR] NA genes of H7N9 strains"`), and all strain accession numbers (`Table S1`), nucleotide compositions (`Table S2`), RSCU tables (`Table S3`), PCA loadings (`Table S4`), and numerical `CAI`/`RCDI`/`SiD` values (`Table S5`) are relegated to external supplementary files.
