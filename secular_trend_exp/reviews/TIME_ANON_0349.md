### 1. Executive Summary
This molecular pathology study investigates the topographical distribution and intratumoral homogeneity of *KRAS* (*K-ras*) codon 12 point mutations across 64 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) resected human lung carcinomas and adjacent preneoplastic/normal epithelial lesions. Using mutagenic PCR–restriction fragment length polymorphism (`BanI` PCR-RFLP, where wild-type codon 12 is cleaved while mutant codon 12 remains an uncut 110-bp band) followed by direct cycle sequencing, the authors detect *KRAS* codon 12 mutations in 14 of 61 amplifiable carcinomas (23.0%), including 12 of 41 adenocarcinomas (29.3%), 0 squamous cell carcinomas, 1 of 6 large-cell carcinomas, and 1 of 3 carcinoid tumors (`6 TGT [Cys]`, `5 GTT [Val]`, `2 GCT [Ala]`, `1 TTT [Phe]`). In the 13 single-base-substitution cases, they apply Selective Ultraviolet Radiation Fractionation (`SURF`—protecting 50–400 cells per microscopic spot with an ultraviolet-opaque black marker dot followed by 4 h UV irradiation to destroy unprotected DNA) across 115 microscopic spots (113 amplified: 71 primary carcinoma spots, 3 metastatic carcinoma spots, 15 preneoplastic lesions, and 24 normal bronchial/alveolar epithelial spots). All 74 carcinoma spots retained the patient's *KRAS* codon 12 mutation, 0 of 24 normal spots were mutated, and 1 of 15 preneoplastic lesions—a type II alveolar cell hyperplasia (`lesion 35-11`)—harbored a `GGT -> TGT` (Gly12Cys) mutation, whereas the primary adenocarcinoma in the same patient (`case 35`) harbored a `GGT -> GTT` (Gly12Val) mutation.

The SURF UV-inactivation controls (demonstrating complete ablation of PCR amplification in uncovered tissue areas up to $30\times$ larger than the inked dots, Figs. 1 and 2) and independent consecutive-section replication are technically solid. However, the authors' conclusion that finding a *discordant* *KRAS* mutation in a single type II hyperplasia lesion (`TGT` vs. `GTT` in the tumor) and observing an uncut mutant band across multi-cell (`50–400` cell) tumor spots prove that *KRAS* mutation occurs at the "very early stage of lung carcinogenesis" without intratumoral heterogeneity is overstated relative to the resolution of the assay and the discordant genotype of Case 35.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Restriction Fragment Length Arithmetic Error in Methods:** In the Materials and Methods ("PCR-RFLP and direct sequencing"), the authors state: *"PCR products can be cut by Ban I (99 base pairs and 21 base pairs) with wild-type DNA, whereas Ban I cannot cut the PCR products (110 base pairs) when the mutation in codon 12 exists."* However, $99 + 21 = 120\,\text{bp} \ne 110\,\text{bp}$! Inspection of the gel labels in Figure 2 (`110 bp` and `89 bp`) confirms that the cleaved wild-type fragment is **89 bp** ($89 + 21 = 110\,\text{bp}$), and `99 base pairs` in the Methods is a typographical error.
- **Bulk-Spot Detection Limit Confounds Claims of Complete Intratumoral Homogeneity:** Each SURF dot protected approximately $50\text{--}400$ cells (typically $100\text{--}200$ cells, with $\ge 70\%$ tumor cells and up to $30\%$ non-neoplastic stromal/inflammatory cells). Because `BanI` PCR-RFLP scores a spot as "mutation-positive" whenever an uncut 110-bp band is visible alongside the cleaved 89-bp wild-type band (which is always present from the normal allele and the $\le 30\%$ stromal cells, as seen in lanes `35-1` through `35-5` of Fig. 2), a tumor spot containing, say, 50% *KRAS*-mutant tumor cells and 50% *KRAS*-wild-type tumor cells produces an indistinguishable RFLP pattern from a 100% clonal mutant spot. Thus, 74/74 positive tumor spots demonstrates *regional* persistence of the mutant clone across 4–7 spots per tumor, but cannot exclude subclonal cellular heterogeneity within each 100–200 cell spot.
- **Logical Disconnect in Interpreting Case 35 (`TGT` in Type II Hyperplasia vs. `GTT` in Adenocarcinoma):** Across all 13 patients, 15 preneoplastic lesions were examined (`2 SQM`, `1 DYS`, `7 T2H`, `5 AAH`), and 14 of 15 (including all 5 atypical adenomatous hyperplasias [AAH]) were completely wild-type (`0/5 AAH`, `0/2 SQM`, `0/1 DYS`, `6/7 T2H`). In the single positive preneoplastic lesion (`35-11`, type II cell hyperplasia in Case 35), sequencing revealed `GGT -> TGT` (Cys), whereas all 6 spots of the patient's primary adenocarcinoma carried `GGT -> GTT` (Val). While this genotype discordance convincingly rules out cross-contamination from the tumor, it also proves that `lesion 35-11` was an independent field lesion and **not** the precursor of the patient's `GTT` adenocarcinoma—and conversely, that 0 of 15 preneoplastic lesions in these 13 resected lungs shared the actual *KRAS* mutation of the patient's carcinoma!

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **[Well Supported]** *KRAS* codon 12 point mutations are enriched in lung adenocarcinomas (29.3%, 12/41) relative to squamous cell carcinomas (0%) and are consistently detectable across all sampled geographic regions (4–7 spots per tumor) of *KRAS*-mutant primary and metastatic carcinomas.
- **[Well Supported]** Occasional non-malignant type II alveolar cell hyperplasias in carcinogen-exposed lungs can harbor clonal *KRAS* codon 12 mutations (`GGT -> TGT` in `35-11`) distinct from the synchronous primary carcinoma (`GGT -> GTT`).
- **[Overstated]** Concluding that *KRAS* codon 12 mutation "should occur in the very early stage of lung carcinogenesis" is weakly supported by the preneoplastic data, given that 14 of 15 preneoplastic lesions (including 5/5 AAHs) were *KRAS*-wild-type and the single mutated T2H lesion carried a different allele from the patient's tumor.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Strengths:** Exact PCR-RFLP primer sequences (`5'-CATGTTCTAATATAGTCACA-3'` and mutagenic `5'-CAAGGCACTCTTGCCTACGGC-3'`), `Ampliwax` hot-start cycling parameters, `BanI` digestion conditions, and a complete case-by-case lesion breakdown (Table 1) are clearly provided.
- **Deficiencies:** The total number of squamous cell carcinomas in the 61-patient cohort is not stated explicitly in the text (41 adenocarcinomas + 6 large-cell + 3 carcinoid + 3 "other" = 53, implying 8 squamous cell carcinomas by subtraction from 61).
