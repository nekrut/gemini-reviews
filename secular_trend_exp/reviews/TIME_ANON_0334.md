### 1. Executive Summary
This computational evolutionary genomics study applies a probabilistic synteny-and-orthology modeling framework (`POInT`—Polyploidy Orthology Inference Tool) to resolve the history of polyploidy and subsequent biased fractionation across three parthenogenetic root-knot nematode genomes (*Meloidogyne incognita*, *M. arenaria*, and *M. javanica*), using the sexually reproducing diploid *M. hapla* as an outgroup. Through simulated annealing synteny block optimization (identifying 5,628 homologous pillars under a whole-genome duplication [WGD] model and 5,316 pillars under a whole-genome triplication [WGT] model) paired with parametric simulation of 100 WGD datasets and 100 independent star-topology triploidies, the authors statistically reject both WGD ($p < 0.01$) and independent triploidization ($p < 0.01$), confirming a shared hypotriploid origin via two hybridization events prior to speciation. Fitting nested continuous-time Markov models of gene loss ($\text{WGT}_{\text{Null}} \subset \text{WGT}_{\text{1Frac}} \subset \text{WGT}_{\text{1FracS}} \subset \text{WGT}_{\text{1FracS2}}$, all LRT $p < 10^{-10}$), they identify a preferentially fractionated subgenome ($S_1$, loss rate $\gamma \approx 0.45$) alongside two more retained subgenomes ($S_2, S_3$). Surprisingly, synonymous divergence ($K_s$) comparisons among fully retained triplets with $\ge 95\%$ orthology confidence reveal that the most fractionated subgenome ($S_1$) exhibits slightly *lower* sequence divergence from $S_2/S_3$ than $S_2$ and $S_3$ share with each other ($p = 0.003\text{--}0.049$), challenging the assumption that the most sequence-diverged subgenome is the least expressed/most fractionated. Finally, $\chi^2$ enrichment tests against 1,988 *C. elegans* orthologs with WormBase RNAi phenotypes demonstrate that pillars reduced to a single copy are significantly depleted for genes with lethal RNAi phenotypes ($p \le 0.005$, Table 2).

This is a mathematically rigorous, well-controlled evolutionary modeling paper with full code and data deposition. The only notable limitation is the potential survivorship bias in restricting $K_s/K_a$ divergence comparisons to the $<2\%$ of pillars that retained all three homeologs with $\ge 95\%$ posterior probability.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Rigorous Parametric Bootstrap Hypothesis Testing:** Because WGD ($2^n$ orthology states) and WGT ($3^n$ orthology states) models are not trivially nested in the presence of synteny block inference, the authors appropriately simulate 100 synthetic genome sets under the fitted WGD parameters and infer WGT improvements on each simulation (Fig. 3), and likewise simulate 100 star-topology triploidizations to test shared ancestry (Fig. 1C–D). Similarly, the four nested WGT fractionation models are compared via exact likelihood ratio tests with 1 degree of freedom each ($p < 10^{-10}$, Fig. 2A).
- **Survivorship Bias in Retained Triplet $K_s$ Comparisons:** To test whether the preferentially lost subgenome ($S_1$) corresponds to the more sequence-diverged third subgenome proposed in prior literature, the authors restrict their $K_s$ and $K_a$ distributions to pillars retaining all three homeologs in a given species with $\ge 95\%$ posterior subgenome confidence—yielding only 58 (*M. arenaria*), 86 (*M. incognita*), and 34 (*M. javanica*) triplets out of 5,316 total pillars (1.6% of pillars). Because `POInT` uses syntenic continuity to assign subgenome identity, triplets retaining $\ge 95\%$ assignment confidence are by definition located inside the most conserved, least rearranged syntenic blocks. Moreover, homeologs that survived fractionation on $S_1$ may be under stronger purifying/homogenizing selection (or biased gene conversion) than the $\sim 62\%$ of $S_1$ genes that were deleted. While the authors acknowledge the small sample size, this survivorship/ascertainment filter should be explicitly discussed as a potential explanation for the lower $K_s$ on surviving $S_1$ copies.
- **Draft Assembly Fragmentation Effects:** As noted by the authors, the MIG genomes are fragmented draft assemblies where scaffold breaks and assembly gaps can mimic concerted or species-specific gene losses along internal and terminal branches.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **[Well Supported]** *M. incognita*, *M. arenaria*, and *M. javanica* share a single ancestral hypotriploidization event prior to their divergence, with extensive shared post-polyploidy gene loss along the root branch.
- **[Well Supported]** Gene loss across the three subgenomes is strongly asymmetric ($\text{LRT } p < 10^{-10}$), with one subgenome ($S_1$) experiencing roughly double the rate of single-copy retention failure and elevated loss from two-copy intermediates.
- **[Well Supported]** Pillars that have returned to single-copy status across the three MIG species are significantly depleted for orthologs of *C. elegans* genes with lethal RNAi knockdown phenotypes ($p \le 0.005$).
- **[Moderately Supported]** The preferentially fractionated subgenome ($S_1$) is not the most sequence-diverged subgenome at surviving triplet loci, suggesting that subgenome dominance and epigenetic silencing after hybridization need not track raw sequence divergence.
