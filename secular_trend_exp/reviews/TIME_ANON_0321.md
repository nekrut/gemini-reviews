### 1. Executive Summary
This study reports the chemical synthesis and in vitro antiretroviral evaluation of a series of 19 1,2-benzisothiazol-3(2H)-one benzenesulfonamides (compounds **6–24**). The lead compound **6** exhibits micromolar inhibition ($\text{EC}_{50} = 4\text{--}8\,\mu\text{M}$) against wild-type HIV-1 ($\text{IIIB}$), HIV-2 ($\text{CBL-20}$), and panels of NRTI-, NNRTI-, and protease-inhibitor-resistant HIV-1 strains in MT-4 and C8166 cell viability (MTT) assays, with moderate cytotoxicity ($\text{CC}_{50} = 86 \pm 4\,\mu\text{M}$, selectivity index $\text{SI} \approx 10$). Enzymatic assays show no inhibition of recombinant HIV-1 reverse transcriptase (RT) or integrase (IN) at $30\,\mu\text{M}$, and coculture syncytium assays show no block of gp120/CD4-mediated viral entry. Instead, compound **6** displays temperature-dependent direct virucidal activity against cell-free HIV-1 and HIV-2 at $50\text{--}100\,\mu\text{M}$ at $37^\circ\text{C}$ (but not $0^\circ\text{C}$), and serial resistance selection up to $16\times\text{EC}_{50}$ identified two amino acid substitutions (F6C and R32K) in the nucleocapsid protein NCp7. While the chemical characterization and multi-strain phenotypic screening are solid, the mechanistic target identification is preliminary—lacking direct biochemical NCp7 zinc-ejection or binding assays and lacking site-directed mutagenesis to prove that F6C/R32K confer resistance—and the analytical chemistry section contains several elemental formula and typographical errors.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Chemical Characterization & Elemental Analysis Errors:** While ${}^1\text{H}$ NMR, ${}^{13}\text{C}$ NMR, IR, and ESI-MS spectra are reported for synthesized compounds, there are multiple copy-paste and stoichiometry errors in the elemental analyses:
  1. For chloro-substituted compounds **12**, **13**, and **14** ($\text{C}_{13}\text{H}_9\text{ClN}_2\text{O}_3\text{S}_2$), the manuscript erroneously writes: *"Anal. calcd. For $\text{C}_{13}\text{H}_9\text{FN}_2\text{O}_3\text{S}_2$"* (listing fluorine instead of chlorine in the formula, although the calculated percentages $\text{C}, 45.81; \text{H}, 2.66; \text{N}, 8.22$ correspond to the chloro formula).
  2. For compound **9** ($\text{C}_{14}\text{H}_{12}\text{N}_2\text{O}_4\text{S}_2$), the formula in *"Anal. calcd. For $\text{C}_{14}\text{H}_{12}\text{N}_2\text{O}_4\text{S}$"* drops a sulfur atom, and for compound **22** ($\text{C}_{19}\text{H}_{12}\text{N}_4\text{O}_9\text{S}_3$), it writes $\text{S}$ instead of $\text{S}_3$.
  3. Typographical corruptions appear in found H percentages for **5c** (*"found C,42.02; H,4.2.56"*), **5d** (*"H,0.2.81"*), and **23** (*"H,3.2.52"*).
  4. In ${}^1\text{H}$ NMR descriptions for **4c**, **4d**, **5c**, **5d**, and **15**, signals with coupling constants ($J = 1.8$ to $7.6\,\text{Hz}$) are mislabeled as singlets (`s`) instead of doublets (`d`).
- **Narrow Selectivity Index and Virucidal Concentration:** Compound **6** has an $\text{EC}_{50}$ of $8 \pm 3\,\mu\text{M}$ and $\text{CC}_{50}$ of $86 \pm 4\,\mu\text{M}$ ($\text{SI} \approx 10.7$), while several congeners (**7**, **13**, **15**, **16**, **19**, **23**) have $\text{SI} \le 5$. Furthermore, the cell-free virucidal assay (Figures 5 and 6) requires $50\text{--}100\,\mu\text{M}$ of compound **6** to achieve substantial viral titer reduction ($>2\text{--}3\,\log_{10}$), whereas $12.5\text{--}25\,\mu\text{M}$ had marginal or no effect. Because $80\text{--}100\,\mu\text{M}$ equals the cellular $\text{CC}_{50}$ ($86\,\mu\text{M}$), carryover of compound **6** into the C8166 limiting-dilution titration wells (diluted 1:2 serially) could confound the first few dilution wells via cytotoxicity unless residual drug was removed prior to titration.
- **Inconsistency in Resistance Selection Description:** In Materials and Methods (line 270), the authors state that compound **6**-resistant virus was selected *"up to a drug concentration 8-fold greater than the $\text{EC}_{50}$"*, whereas in Results (line 12 of second chunk) and Discussion (line 34), they state *"16 times higher ($16\times$) than the $\text{EC}_{50}$"*. Moreover, the actual $\text{EC}_{50}$ value of compound **6** against the selected $\text{HIV-1}_6$ resistant strain is never reported in a table or dose-response curve.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *1,2-Benzisothiazol-3(2H)-one benzenesulfonamides inhibit HIV-1, HIV-2, and NRTI/NNRTI/PRI-resistant mutants at micromolar concentrations without inhibiting RT, IN, or viral entry.* `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated across 7 HIV-1 strains and HIV-2 in Table 1, recombinant RT/IN assays, and H9/C8166 syncytium assays (Figure 3).
- **Claim 2:** *Compound 6 targets the HIV nucleocapsid protein NCp7, where F6C and R32K mutations confer drug resistance.* `[Fragile]` — As the authors candidly acknowledge in their Discussion limitations (lines 28–38 of part 2), bulk population Sanger sequencing was restricted to *gag*, *pol*, and *env*; no biophysical NCp7 binding or $\text{Zn}^{2+}$-extrusion assay was performed, nor were F6C and R32K reintroduced into a molecular clone by site-directed mutagenesis to verify that they recapitulate resistance. Nevertheless, the electrophilic isothiazolone $\text{N-S}$ bond is chemically well-known to react with nucleophilic Cys thiolates in NCp7 CCHC zinc fingers (consistent with failure to inactivate virions at $0^\circ\text{C}$).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Synthetic procedures, yields, melting points, and spectral data are provided in full in the main text for all intermediates and final compounds **6–24**.
- However, the nucleotide sequences of the selected resistant virus ($\text{HIV-1}_6$) are not deposited in GenBank, RT-PCR primer sequences are relegated to Supplementary Data, and the phenotypic resistance curve ($\text{EC}_{50}$ fold-shift) of the selected mutant virus is omitted.
