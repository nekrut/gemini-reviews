### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This purely *in silico* COVID-19 drug-repurposing paper performs a Neighbor-Joining phylogenetic tree of 50 coronavirus genomes in MEGA 6.0, uses AutoDock Vina to dock ~77 FDA-approved drugs into the crystal structure of the SARS-CoV-2 main protease ($\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$, `PDB: 6Y2F`), and runs 10-ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations in YASARA (`AMBER14` force field) on the top three hits: "Lopinavir–Ritonavir" ($-10.6\text{ kcal/mol}$), Tipranavir ($-8.7\text{ kcal/mol}$), and Raltegravir ($-8.3\text{ kcal/mol}$). The authors claim that these drugs outperform the co-crystallized covalent peptidomimetic $\alpha$-ketoamide inhibitor `13b` ($-8.3\text{ kcal/mol}$) and propose moving directly to clinical trials. This manuscript exemplifies the commodity docking-template genre and suffers from severe chemical, biophysical, and methodological errors: treating the clinical combination drug `Lopinavir–Ritonavir` (two distinct protease inhibitor molecules co-formulated so ritonavir inhibits host CYP3A4 metabolism of lopinavir) as a single molecular entity in AutoDock Vina and 10-ns MD (`1. Lopinavir–Ritonavir (-10.6 kcal/mol)`), comparing non-covalent AutoDock Vina scores against a **covalently bound** hemithioacetal $\alpha$-ketoamide inhibitor (`13b` in `6Y2F`), docking inactive oral prodrugs (`Tenofovir-disoproxil` and `Baloxavir-marboxil`) instead of their active metabolites, running unacceptably short 10-ns single-trajectory MD simulations with an unphysical $7.86\text{ Å}$ Coulomb cutoff, and claiming in the Abstract/Conclusion that SARS-CoV-2 "does not exhibit any divergence at the genomic level."

### STRENGTHS
- **Re-docking sanity check of the crystallographic ligand geometry:** The authors re-docked the co-crystallized $\alpha$-ketoamide `13b` into `6Y2F` and reported a pose RMSD of $0.51\text{ Å}$ relative to the crystallographic coordinates (Figure 4B).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Docking the clinical co-formulation "Lopinavir–Ritonavir" as a single combined ligand (`Table 1`, `Table 2`, `Fig. 5A`, `Fig. 9B`):** Lopinavir ($M_w = 628.8\text{ Da}$) and Ritonavir ($M_w = 720.9\text{ Da}$) are two separate HIV-1 aspartyl protease inhibitor molecules combined in a pill (`Kaletra`) because ritonavir inhibits host hepatic cytochrome P450 3A4 (`CYP3A4`) to boost plasma levels of lopinavir. Throughout the manuscript, the authors download a combined two-molecule PubChem SDF record (`Lopinavir–Ritonavir`) and dock it as a single composite ligand in AutoDock Vina, yielding an artificially inflated additive Vina score of $-10.6\text{ kcal/mol}$ simply because two large molecules ($>1,300\text{ Da}$ combined) are packed into the binding cleft simultaneously!
- **Invalid comparison of non-covalent AutoDock Vina scores against a covalent $\alpha$-ketoamide warhead (`13b` in `PDB: 6Y2F`):** In `PDB: 6Y2F` (Zhang et al.), inhibitor `13b` reacts covalently with the catalytic $\text{Cys145}$ thiolate of $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$ to form a reversible hemithioacetal bond. AutoDock Vina only evaluates non-covalent empirical scoring terms; therefore, its $-8.3\text{ kcal/mol}$ score for `13b` completely ignores the covalent bond formation energy, making the claim that Raltegravir ($-8.3\text{ kcal/mol}$), Tipranavir ($-8.7\text{ kcal/mol}$), and Lopinavir–Ritonavir ($-10.6\text{ kcal/mol}$) have equal or superior binding affinity to `13b` biophysically meaningless.
- **Docking unhydrolyzed oral prodrugs (`Tenofovir-disoproxil` and `Baloxavir-marboxil`) into $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$:** Ranked #7 and #8 in Tables 1 and 2 are `Tenofovir-disoproxil` and `Baloxavir-marboxil`. Both are ester prodrugs that are rapidly hydrolyzed by host esterases upon oral absorption into tenofovir (which is then phosphorylated intracellularly to tenofovir diphosphate, a nucleotide analog) and baloxavir acid; the intact prodrug esters do not circulate at therapeutic concentrations in infected lung tissue, and adding bulky disoproxil/marboxil ester groups artificially inflates hydrophobic AutoDock Vina scores.
- **Inadequate 10-ns MD simulation protocol and false precision in Table 2:**
  - A single 10-ns MD trajectory with 101 snapshots and a short $7.86\text{ Å}$ Coulomb electrostatics cutoff (Methods, line 53) is far too short to assess ligand residence stability or binding free energy. Furthermore, Figure 7 plots the **total potential energy of the entire solvated simulation box** ($-1,280,648$ to $-1,692,992\text{ kJ/mol}$), which is dominated by thousands of bulk water molecules and box volume differences, yet the caption of Figure 7 mislabels this as *"the drugs interaction energy (kJ/mol)"* and line 153 claims *"The total binding energies were showing that drug Lopinavir–Ritonavir was showing more stable energy values, Fig. 7"*.
  - In **Table 2**, $K_i$ values derived from 2-significant-figure AutoDock Vina energies (e.g., $-10.6\text{ kcal/mol}$) via $K_i = \exp(\Delta G / RT)$ are reported to 5 significant figures (`1.6754 × 10^-8 M`), and for #9 (`Letermovir`, $-8.0\text{ kcal/mol}$) and #10 (`Maraviroc`, $-8.0\text{ kcal/mol}$), two identical docking scores produce two different $K_i$ values (`1.3533 × 10^-6` vs. `1.3236 × 10^-6`).
  - In **Table 1**, the third column (`Schematic of intermolecular interactions`) is completely blank for all 10 rows.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1 (Lopinavir–Ritonavir, Tipranavir, and Raltegravir bind SARS-CoV-2 $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$ more strongly than $\alpha$-ketoamide `13b` and can move straight to clinical trials): `[Methodologically Flawed]`** — Rests on docking the two-molecule mixture Lopinavir+Ritonavir simultaneously, ignoring covalent catalysis of `13b`, misinterpreting total water-box potential energy as drug binding energy, and performing zero biochemical or cellular validation (notably, HIV-1 aspartyl protease inhibitors lack the warhead and geometry required to potently inhibit coronavirus cysteine proteases at clinically achievable free plasma concentrations).
- **Claim 2 (SARS-CoV-2 genomes isolated from different countries "do not exhibit any divergence at the genomic level"): `[Overstated]`** — The authors aligned only 10 early pandemic genomes (Dec 2019–Feb 2020) alongside distantly related bat and MERS coronaviruses (`~79–89%` identity); on a tree scaled to inter-subgenus coronavirus divergence (`Merbecovirus` vs `Sarbecovirus`), intra-SARS-CoV-2 SNPs appear near zero branch length, which the authors misinterpret as an absence of viral mutation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
While PDB ID (`6Y2F`) and GenBank accession numbers for the 10 SARS-CoV-2 and 4 bat CoV genomes are listed (though 36 of the 50 genomes used in the tree are not given accession numbers), the docking grid box center coordinates, box dimensions, exhaustiveness settings, PubChem CIDs for the 77 compounds, and the entire third column of Table 1 are missing.
