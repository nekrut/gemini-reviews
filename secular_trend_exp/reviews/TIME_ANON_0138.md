### 1. Executive Summary
This bioinformatics and molecular phylogenetics paper introduces a "mega-phylogeny" pipeline designed to construct dense, species-rich sequence alignments and maximum-likelihood trees for targeted clades from GenBank. Rather than performing $N \times N$ all-by-all BLAST clustering as in de novo supermatrix pipelines, the method uses user-specified exemplar sequences representing clade-wide diversity ($N \times n$ BLAST comparisons) for orthology filtering, iteratively subdivides sequences along the NCBI taxonomic hierarchy whenever a Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) dispersion metric between uncorrected and Jukes–Cantor (JC69) distances exceeds a threshold ($\text{MAD} > 0.01$), and then merges the sub-alignments via profile-to-profile alignment (MAFFT) guided by a literature-grafted backbone tree before running RAxML (GTR+$\Gamma$). The authors demonstrate the pipeline on Asterales (4,954 species, 12,033 sites) and Viridiplantae *rbcL* (13,533 species, 1,401 sites). While the pipeline is practical and addresses a real bottleneck in comparative biology, the paper suffers from a missing equation in the text, unquantified topological accuracy in simulations, отсутствie of bootstrap support on the empirical trees, and internal inconsistencies regarding the number of gene regions in the Asterales dataset.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Missing Equation and Mathematical Specification of the MAD Metric:** In the "Assessing saturation" section (lines 53–55), the sentence ends with *"commonly referred to as the median absolute deviation (MAD) and given by [blank] where the median is estimated from the residual variation..."*—the mathematical formula itself was omitted or lost. Moreover, defining saturation as the one-dimensional Euclidean distance $|d_{\text{raw}} - d_{\text{JC69}}|$ across all $\binom{N}{2}$ pairwise comparisons has a structural limitation: to compute $d_{\text{raw}}$ and $d_{\text{JC69}}$ in the first place, the sequences must *already* be aligned. If the initial un-subdivided alignment of a highly divergent clade is severely misaligned due to indels and saturation, pairwise distances computed on that alignment will be distorted before MAD is evaluated.
- **Simulation Design Evaluates Dispersion Stability Rather Than Alignment/Tree Accuracy:** The Seq-Gen simulation on 20- and 100-tip trees (Figure 1) only plots MAD vs. MSE and RMSE of $|d_{\text{raw}} - d_{\text{JC69}}|$ across tree lengths $0.10$ to $2.0$ (note also that Seq-Gen simulates substitution without indels, so the true alignment is trivial and known). The authors never simulate indel evolution (e.g., via INDELible or Dawg) to test whether subdividing at $\text{MAD} > 0.01$ and profile-aligning actually improves alignment accuracy (e.g., SP-score) or topological recovery (Robinson–Foulds distance) compared to direct MAFFT alignment.
- **Internal Contradiction in Number of Loci in the Asterales Matrix:** The Abstract (line 26) and Figure 2 legend (line 79) state that the Asterales phylogeny includes **five** gene regions (*rbcL, matK, trnL-F, ndhF,* and *ITS*). However, line 72 first lists **six** gene regions (*rbcL, matK, trnL-F, trnK, ndhF,* and *ITS*), and the very next sentence lists missing-data percentages for **seven** gene regions: *"98.043% in ETS, 36.348% in ITS, 98.188% in matK, 90.338% in ndhF, 92.597% in rbcL, 98.002% in trnK, and 81.445% in trnL-F"*, before immediately reverting to *"Of the five gene regions sampled..."*.
- **Absence of Nodal Support / Bootstrap Evaluation:** Neither the 4,954-tip Asterales tree (Figure 2) nor the 13,533-tip Viridiplantae *rbcL* tree (Figure 3) reports rapid bootstrap support values or rogue-taxon stability metrics. In a matrix with $90.96\%$ missing data (Asterales), many terminal placements and deep rearrangements (such as breaking the monophyly of *Wunderlichioideae*, *Stifftioideae*, *Mutisioideae*, and *Gochnatioideae*) could be weakly supported artifacts of patchy locus coverage ("terraces" in phylogenetic tree space).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *"The mega-phylogeny approach can quickly create enormous phylogenetic matrices as more data from the same gene may be used, and the problems associated with sequence saturation are specifically attenuated."* — `[Moderately Supported]`. Taxonomic subdivision + profile alignment is a sensible heuristic for aligning fast-evolving regions like ITS across 4,242 species where global MSA fails, and the recovery of major angiosperm/Asterales clades is encouraging.
- **Claim 2:** *"The phylogeny of Viridiplantae successfully reconstructs major relationships of vascular plants that previously required many more genes."* — `[Overstated]`. In the Results (line 94), the authors themselves admit that while *rbcL* alone recovers the bryophyte grade (liverworts, mosses, hornworts), *within* vascular plants their tree places lycophytes as sister to seed plants rather than monilophytes—a well-known phylogenetic artifact contradicted by morphology, plastome inversions, and multi-gene analyses. Claiming in the Abstract that the tree "successfully reconstructs major relationships of vascular plants" directly glosses over this major topological error within Tracheophyta.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Software versions (Python 2.5, BioPython 1.48, BioSQL 1.0.1, RAxML 7.0.4, Seq-Gen 1.3.2, GenBank release 167) and the guide trees (Additional files 1 and 2) are specified.
- However, no source code repository, downloadable Python scripts, final sequence alignments, or Newick tree files for the Asterales and Viridiplantae analyses are provided or linked in the manuscript, limiting direct computational reproducibility of the pipeline.
