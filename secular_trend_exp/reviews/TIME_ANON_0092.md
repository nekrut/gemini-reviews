### 1. Executive Summary
This mechanistic innate-immunity and virology study investigates how the interferon-stimulated gene guanylate-binding protein 5 (`GBP5`) restricts the maturation of SARS-CoV-2 Spike (`S`) and other enveloped viral glycoproteins (SARS-CoV `S`, MERS-CoV `S`, influenza A virus `HA`, and HIV-1 `gp160`). Whereas prior literature attributed `GBP5`'s antiviral activity to direct inhibition of Golgi-resident furin protease, the authors demonstrate that `GBP5` (but not `GBP1`, `GBP4`, or the prenylation-defective mutant `GBP5-C583A`) reduces the apparent molecular weight (`MWa`) of full-length `S`, prevents its acquisition of `Endo H`-resistant complex glycans, induces high-molecular-weight misfolded `S` aggregates on native PAGE, and retains `S` in the endoplasmic reticulum (`ER`). By contrasting `GBP5` overexpression with pharmacological furin inhibition (`decanoyl-RVKR-CMK`, which causes accumulation of `Endo H`-*resistant* uncleaved `S`, `HA0`, and `gp160`) vs. oligosaccharyltransferase (`OST`) complex inhibition (`NGI-1`, which phenocopies `GBP5`), they show that `GBP5` acts upstream of furin at the step of ER N-linked glycosylation. Co-immunoprecipitation and domain-truncation mapping (`M1–M5`) reveal that `GBP5` binds the cytoplasmic tails of the non-catalytic OST accessory subunits (`RPN1`, `RPN2`, `DDOST`) via its middle-domain $\alpha 7\text{--}\alpha 9$ helices (`E417/K421`) and blocks substrate binding of `S` to the catalytic subunit `STT3B`. Finally, the authors perform `N-to-Q` mutagenesis across all 22 N-glycosylation sequons of SARS-CoV-2 `S` and demonstrate broad-spectrum antiviral activity of `NGI-1` against authentic SARS-CoV-2 (`WA1` and `Omicron BA.1` in `Calu-3`, `Caco-2`, and hiPSC-derived 3D lung organoids), HIV-1 (`LAI` in `MT4` cells), and VSV pseudotypes.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Clever Biochemical Distinction Between ER Glycosylation Block vs. Golgi Furin Block (`Endo H` / `PNGase F` + `CMK` vs. `NGI-1`):** The strongest experimental design element in the paper is the direct comparison of `Endo H` and `PNGase F` sensitivity between `GBP5`-overexpressing cells, `Brefeldin A`-treated cells, `NGI-1`-treated cells, and `decanoyl-RVKR-CMK` (furin inhibitor)-treated cells across SARS-CoV-2 `S` (Fig. 2b–h), IAV `HA` (Fig. 5c, g), and HIV-1 `gp160` (Fig. 5d, h). Whereas direct furin inhibition by `CMK` causes accumulation of Golgi-trafficked, `Endo H`-resistant full-length `S`, `HA0`, and `gp160` without reducing their apparent molecular weight, `GBP5` overexpression and `NGI-1` both reduce full-length `MWa` (collapsing to identical unglycosylated backbone mobility upon `PNGase F` or `Endo H` treatment) and abolish `Endo H`-resistant full-length glycoprotein accumulation.
- **Genetic & Domain-Mutagenesis Controls:** Every gain-of-function experiment includes the prenylation-defective `GBP5-C583A` point mutant in parallel with wild-type `GBP5`, and domain-deletion/point mutants (`M1` [$\Delta\text{LG}$], `M2` [$\Delta\text{LG-H}$], `M3` [$\Delta\text{LG-H-}\alpha 7\text{--}9$], `M4` [$\Delta\text{GED}$], and `M5` [`E417A/K421A`]) cleanly correlate physical binding to `RPN1/RPN2/DDOST` (Fig. 3i) with inhibition of `S` glycosylation (Fig. 3h). Crucially, loss-of-function relevance is validated in `CRISPR-Cas9 GBP5 KO Calu-3` cells and shRNA-depleted primary human bronchial epithelial cells treated with `IFN-gamma` and infected with authentic SARS-CoV-2 (Fig. 1m–n, Fig. S1j–n).
- **Methodological & Statistical Weaknesses:**
  1. **Inappropriate Use of Paired $t$-Tests and Under-Replicated Assays (`n=2`):** In Materials and Methods (`Statistical analysis`, line 226), the authors state that all data *"were analyzed using Student's t-test (paired, two-sided) with Prism 9"*, without multiple-testing correction (e.g., Dunnett's or Bonferroni test) when comparing 22 `N-to-Q` Spike mutants against WT in Fig. 4d or multiple drug concentrations against DMSO in Fig. 6. Furthermore, in Fig. 1n (`FFU` viral titer in `GBP5 KO Calu-3` cells) and Fig. 6f–g (lung organoid infections), the legends explicitly state `n = 2` (`Mean ± SD of n = 2`), yet two-sided $t$-test significance stars (`* P < 0.05; ** P < 0.01`) are plotted on $n=2$ data points.
  2. **Overstated Claim of "Viral-Specific" vs. Host Glycosylation Sparing (Line 98, Fig. S6c):** To argue that `GBP5` selectively inhibits viral glycoproteins without affecting host glycoproteins, the authors perform a crude Concanavalin A (`ConA`) resin pull-down followed by total magenta glycoprotein gel staining (`Fig. S6c`). Because bulk `ConA` staining in 293T cells measures total pre-existing steady-state host glycoproteins (synthesized prior to transient `GBP5` transfection or having long half-lives) and `GBP5` binds core OST subunits (`RPN1`, `RPN2`, `DDOST`), this bulk stain lacks the sensitivity to rule out impaired N-glycosylation of newly synthesized endogenous `STT3B`-dependent host glycoproteins.
  3. **Minor Biochemical Typo in Fig. 2d Legend (Line 53):** The legend to Fig. 2d states that *"The OST complex transfers glycans from dolichol to arginine residues in the substrate protein"* (instead of **asparagine** residues, which is correctly stated in the Introduction).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *`GBP5` is induced by SARS-CoV-2 and `IFN-gamma` and restricts SARS-CoV-2 virion assembly and release by impairing ER N-linked glycosylation and ER-to-Golgi trafficking of Spike, rather than by directly inhibiting Golgi furin.* `[Well Supported]` — Convincingly demonstrated by `Endo H`/`PNGase F` digests, `CMK` vs. `NGI-1` comparisons, native-PAGE aggregation assays, VLP/pseudovirus/live-virus assays, and `GBP5 KO Calu-3` rescue under `IFN-gamma`.
- **Claim 2:** *`GBP5` interacts via its $\alpha 7\text{--}\alpha 9$ middle domain (`E417/K421`) and C-terminal prenylation motif (`C583`) with the cytoplasmic tails of OST accessory subunits (`RPN1`, `RPN2`, `DDOST`), blocking `S` access to catalytic `STT3B`.* `[Well Supported]` — Supported by ectopic and endogenous (`IFN-gamma`-induced) co-IPs, C-terminal tail deletions of `RPN1/RPN2/DDOST` (`Fig. S5b`), and `M1–M5` domain mapping (Fig. 3).
- **Claim 3:** *`GBP5` selectively disrupts viral glycoprotein maturation without affecting host protein glycosylation, and `NGI-1` is a safe broad-spectrum antiviral.* `[Overstated]` — Bulk steady-state `ConA` staining (`Fig. S6c`) cannot resolve de novo glycosylation of individual nascent host `STT3B` substrates, and `NGI-1` directly targets the essential host catalytic `STT3A/B` subunits (inducing ER stress, as acknowledged in the Discussion).
