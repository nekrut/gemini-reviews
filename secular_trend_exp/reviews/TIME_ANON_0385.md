### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This manuscript investigates the molecular basis of the sterol requirement for Semliki Forest virus (SFV) membrane fusion and progeny virion exit. Exploiting the cholesterol auxotrophy of *Aedes albopictus* C6/36 mosquito cells depleted of cholesterol (<2% control levels) and repleted with the non-fusogenic analogue chlorocholestene, the authors select nitrosoguanidine-mutagenized SFV for sterol-independent growth, isolating three clones (`srf-1`, `srf-2`, `srf-3`). Phenotypic characterization demonstrates that `srf-3` exhibits a ~100-fold increase in primary infection, a ~1,000-fold (3-log) increase in low-pH-triggered plasma membrane fusion, and an 8-fold increase in viral exit (16% vs. 2% in pulse-chase assays) on sterol-depleted cells compared to wild-type (wt) SFV, without altering host range on cholesterol-replete BHK or C6/36 cells. Crucially, sucrose gradient ultracentrifugation reveals that `srf-3` virions budding from cholesterol-depleted cells are physically fragile to shear forces, dissociating into capsid-free membranes. Using chimeric sub-cloning of a 2.1-kb *NdeI* fragment (nt 8929–11033) into the pSP6-SFV4 infectious clone (`srf-3/ic`) and *de novo* site-directed mutagenesis (`SFV4-P226S`), the authors prove that a single nucleotide substitution (`CCC` to `UCC`), causing a proline-to-serine change at residue 226 of the E1 spike subunit (E1 P226S), is both necessary and sufficient for the cholesterol-independent fusion and exit phenotypes.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT

#### STRENGTHS
- **Definitive Reverse Genetics Causality:** The study goes beyond correlative sequencing of selected mutants by performing both fragment swap rescue (`srf-3/ic`) and *de novo* site-directed mutagenesis (`SFV4-P226S`) in a clean infectious clone background (`pSP6-SFV4`), re-sequencing the entire 2.1-kb *NdeI* fragment on both strands to rule out second-site mutations, and resolving an old database error (confirming E1 Asp323 in both parent wt and `wt/ic`).
- **Dissection of Entry, Fusion, Exit, and Virion Stability:** The authors systematically uncouple receptor binding and endocytosis (unchanged), direct low-pH-triggered plasma membrane fusion in the presence of 20 mM $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ to block secondary infection ($n=6\text{--}7$ determinations), primary infection ($n=13$ determinations), and pulse-chase budding kinetics ($n=6\text{--}7$, normalizing for entry blocks in wt via MOI 1,000 vs. MOI 50).
- **Discovery of a Structural Role for Cholesterol in Virion Integrity:** By subjecting radiolabeled `srf-3` grown in sterol-depleted cells to sucrose density gradient centrifugation (Fig. 3), the authors uncover a biophysical distinction between functional infectivity in culture and mechanical shear stability of the icosahedral spike-bilayer lattice.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Non-Independence of `srf-1`, `srf-2`, and `srf-3` Isolates:** All three clones were isolated by limiting dilution from the *same* mutagenized and passaged pool and carry the identical E1 P226S mutation (and the authors note they are likely siblings of a single mutational event), yet early figures (Fig. 1, Table I) plot them as three parallel mutant lines.
- **Lack of Formal Inferential Statistics and Error Bars in Tables:** Table I and Table II report point-estimate titer ratios (e.g., 17,860 vs. 14 in Experiment 1; 5,433 vs. 77 in Experiment 2) without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or $p$-values. Similarly, several key experiments (Fig. 1, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6) are based on $n=2$ independent experiments ("Representative example of two experiments" or "Average of two experiments").
- **Unverified Membrane Incorporation of Chlorocholestene:** While mutants were selected on chlorocholestene-repleted cells, they grew equally well on unenriched cholesterol-depleted cells, and chlorocholestene failed to stabilize virions against centrifugation shear. The authors acknowledge they did not directly quantify chlorocholestene partitioning into the C6/36 plasma membrane.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** A single amino acid substitution in the SFV E1 spike subunit, Pro226 to Ser (P226S), is sufficient to confer marked cholesterol independence for both membrane fusion and progeny virus exit. `[Well Supported]` — Directly proven by *de novo* engineered `SFV4-P226S` recapitulating the 4-log fusion increase and 5- to 8-fold exit increase of `srf-3` on sterol-depleted C6/36 cells.
- **Claim 2:** Cellular membrane cholesterol is required for the physical structural stability of SFV particles against mechanical shear forces during ultracentrifugation, even when E1 P226S bypasses the functional requirement for budding. `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated by sucrose gradient fractionation and EM of `srf-3` grown in depleted vs. control cells (Fig. 3).
- **Claim 3:** The serine 226 hydroxyl group may functionally substitute for the $3\beta$-hydroxyl group of cholesterol by filling a sterol-binding pocket. `[Moderately Supported]` — Appropriately framed in the Discussion as a speculative structural hypothesis awaiting mutagenesis of residue 226 to other amino acids (e.g., Ala, Thr, Val).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Reagent & Protocol Precision:** Mutagenesis oligonucleotide sequence (`5'-GCCCTTCAtCaGGCATGG-3'`), restriction sites (*NdeI* nt 8929–11033, *EcoNI* screening site), MOI adjustments (MOI 50 vs. 1,000 for wt exit in depleted cells), and sterol depletion protocols (colloidal silica-treated FCS passaged $\ge 4$ times) are reported with high clarity.
- **Data Availability:** Sequence alignments across 11 alphaviruses (Fig. 7) cite specific accessions where available, though several observations (EM of disrupted particles, binding/endocytosis assays, preliminary E1 homotrimerization and Sindbis assays) are cited as "data not shown" or "unpublished results."
