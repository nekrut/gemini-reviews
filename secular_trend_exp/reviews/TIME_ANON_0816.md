### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This molecular epidemiology short communication characterizes somatic *TP53* (*p53*) mutations across exons 5–8 in 44 resected hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) specimens (38 FFPE, 6 fresh-frozen) from ethnic Chinese patients in Singapore (38 males, 6 females; 31/44 [70%] HBsAg-positive). Using PCR-SSCP followed by cycle sequencing of shifted bands, as well as *Hae*III RFLP screening of exon 7 across all 44 samples, the authors detect 14 point mutations in 13 tumors (29.5%; note that the Abstract states "Twelve point mutations (27%)" whereas the Results and Table I report 14 mutations across 13 cases [29.5%], including one double mutant [case 2: codons 162 and 248] and two synonymous substitutions [cases 10 and 34]). Crucially, 0/44 tumors carried the aflatoxin B1 (AFB1)-associated codon 249 $\text{G}\to\text{T}$ hotspot mutation, and 9/14 mutations were base transitions rather than transversions, leading the authors to conclude that dietary AFB1 is not a major contributor to HCC in Singapore Chinese.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT

#### STRENGTHS
- **Orthogonal confirmation at codon 249:** Because SSCP can have incomplete sensitivity (<80–90% depending on electrophoresis temperature and glycerol conditions), the authors independently screened all 44 tumor exon 7 amplicons by *Hae*III restriction digestion alongside a homozygous codon-249 mutant positive control (Mahlavu cell line), confirming 0/44 mutations at the second/third bases of codon 249.
- **Complete mutation and clinicopathological tabulation:** Table I lists every mutated sample's sex, HBV status, exon, codon, nucleotide change, and amino acid substitution, and Table II stratifies mutation frequency by HBsAg status, Edmondson-Steiner grade, tumor size, and intravascular invasion.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Internal numerical inconsistency between Abstract and Main Text/Tables:** The Abstract states: *"Twelve point mutations (27%) were detected in tumour tissues"*. However, the Results section states: *"Of the 44 tumours examined, 13 (29.5%) showed abnormal SSCP bandshifts... Subsequent DNA sequencing confirmed that all 13 cases harboured point mutations in exons 5–8"*, and Table I lists 14 point mutations in 13 patients (because Case 2 has two mutations: codon 162 and codon 248, and Cases 34 and 10 have silent mutations at codons 284 and 291; 12/44 = 27.3% is the count of non-synonymous mutations or cases with missense/nonsense mutations, which was conflated with total point mutations in the Abstract).
- **Table II typographical error in sample counts:** In Table II under "Tumour grade I", the column "Number of tumours analysed" prints `66` with `2 (33%)` having *p53* mutation—obviously a typo for `6` ($2/6 = 33.3\%$, and $6 + 7 + 22 + 4 = 39$ cases with grade available, matching footnote $b$: *"Result not available for five cases"*, $44 - 5 = 39$).
- **Lack of matched normal tissue DNA:** Normal control DNA was taken from white blood cells of a single healthy subject rather than matched non-tumorous liver or blood from each of the 44 patients. Thus, rare germline polymorphisms (especially the two silent variants at codon 284 `ACA->ACC` in Case 34 and codon 291 `AAG->AAA` in Case 10) cannot be definitively distinguished from somatic mutations.
- **No direct measurement of aflatoxin exposure:** Aflatoxin B1 exposure was inferred purely ecologically/indirectly from the absence of codon 249 mutations rather than measured via urinary/serum AFB1-albumin or DNA adducts.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Codon 249 *p53* mutations are absent (0/44, 95% upper confidence bound ~8%) in this cohort of HCCs from ethnic Chinese patients in Singapore. `[Well Supported]`
- **Claim 2:** Risk factors other than dietary exposure to aflatoxin B1 (such as chronic HBV infection and endogenous regeneration-associated transitions) predominate in Singapore HCC aetiology. `[Moderately Supported]` — Consistent with the mutation spectrum (9/14 transitions, 0/44 codon 249), though direct biomarker measurement of AFB1 exposure was not performed.
- **Claim 3:** *p53* mutations are associated with intravascular invasion ($12/32$ [38%] vs $0/7$ [0%], Fisher's exact $P = 0.05$). `[Fragile]` — Small sample size ($n=39$ with available pathology, only 7 invasion-negative tumors) and unadjusted for multiple clinicopathological comparisons in Table II.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Primer sequences:** Exon 5–8 forward and reverse primer sequences are printed in the legend of Figure 1 (though OCR/typesetting corrupted two bases in Exon 5 F `TTCCTCYlf CCTGCAGTACTC` and Exon 7 F `GTGTfTATCTCCTAGGTTGGC`).
- **Mutation coordinates:** Exact codon and nucleotide changes are fully documented in Table I.
