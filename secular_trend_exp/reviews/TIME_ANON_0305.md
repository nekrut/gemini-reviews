### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript investigates postmortem RNA expression in human prostate tissue to evaluate its utility as a biological source for forensic postmortem interval (PMI) estimation and functional studies. The authors perform total RNA-seq (Illumina NovaSeq 6000) on 5 postmortem prostate samples collected during forensic autopsies and stored at $-20^\circ\text{C}$, stratified into low PMI ($\le 3$ days; $n = 3$: `P_B2` [68 y, ~2 days], `P_G4` [45 y, 3 days], `P_I5` [65 y, 3 days]) and high PMI ($>3$ days; $n = 2$: `P_D7` [75 y, 4 days], `P_E6` [29 y, 5 days]), alongside a secondary re-analysis of 42 GTEx prostate RNA-seq samples stratified by ischemic time ($<150\text{ min}$, $n = 23$ vs. $>1000\text{ min}$, $n = 19$). Using a threshold of $|\log_2\text{FC}| \ge 1$ and $p \le 0.05$, the authors report 252 upregulated and 245 downregulated genes in high- vs. low-PMI forensic samples, and 446 upregulated and 107 downregulated genes in longer-ischemia GTEx samples.

While the study addresses an interesting forensic question and deposits raw sequencing reads in GEO (`GSE249984`), it suffers from fatal experimental design, statistical, and biological flaws:
1. **Severe underpowering ($n = 2$ vs. $n = 3$) and unadjusted $p$-value thresholding:** The primary forensic comparison has only $n = 2$ biological replicates in the high-PMI group (confounded by extreme age disparity: 29 vs. 75 years old) and $n = 3$ in the low-PMI group, relies on unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ for differential expression and GO/KEGG enrichment, and runs GSEA with `Signal2Noise` (which mathematically requires at least $n = 3$ samples per group for stable variance estimation) at a relaxed `FDR q < 0.25`.
2. **Confounding differential RNA degradation with active de novo transcription 5 days after death:** Based on apparent relative enrichment of certain transcripts in total RNA-seq library normalization, the authors repeatedly claim that prostate cells remain "viable," "alive," and "proliferating" 5 days after human death. Total RNA-seq measures relative transcript abundance in a fixed library pool: when labile cytoplasmic mRNAs rapidly degrade postmortem, stable, short, GC-rich, or nuclear-retained transcripts (such as small nuclear ribonucleoproteins and non-coding RNAs) artificially increase in relative TPM/CPM fraction, creating the illusion of active upregulation.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Sample Size, Age Confounding, and Statistical Testing:**
  - The forensic cohort comprises only $N = 5$ total individuals split into $n = 3$ (low PMI: 2, 3, and 3 days) vs. $n = 2$ (high PMI: 4 and 5 days). Note that the difference between the two groups is as small as 1 day (3 days vs. 4 days), and the causes of death (`hanging`, `car accident`, `gunshot wounds`, `natural dynamic`, `sharp force injury`), ambient environmental temperatures prior to autopsy, and subject ages (ranging from 29 to 75 years) are completely uncontrolled.
  - Because `DESeq2` or `edgeR` cannot reliably estimate gene-wise dispersion from $n = 2$ vs. $n = 3$ heterogeneous human autopsy samples, the authors filter differentially expressed genes using unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ (`-0.05 <= p-value <= 0.05`), yielding 497 nominal hits that are indistinguishable from false positives and compositional degradation artifacts.
  - Similarly, GO (` enrichGO`) and KEGG (`enrichKEGG`) over-representation analyses use `pAdjustMethod = "none"` with `pvalueCutoff = 0.05`, guaranteeing spurious functional enrichments.
  - In the GSEA analysis (`GSEA v4.2.3`), the authors use the `Signal2Noise` ranking metric (`(mean_A - mean_B) / (sd_A + sd_B)`) on a group of size $n = 2$; GSEA documentation explicitly warns against using `Signal2Noise` with fewer than 3 samples per class because two-point standard deviations are noisy and unstable.
- **Compositional Normalization Artifact in Postmortem Total RNA-seq:**
  - Standard RNA-seq normalization assumes that total cellular RNA content per cell is constant across conditions and that most transcripts are invariant. In postmortem tissues undergoing progressive autolysis over 2–5 days, bulk cytoplasmic mRNA degrades extensively. Transcripts that are structurally resistant to endonucleases or sequestered in nuclear ribonucleoprotein complexes (`SNRPA1`, `TERC`, snoRNAs, miRNAs, long non-coding RNAs) survive longer and therefore occupy a larger share of the sequenced reads, producing positive $\log_2\text{FC}$ values without any de novo RNA synthesis.
  - Without exogenous spike-in controls (e.g., ERCC spike-ins calibrated to tissue mass or DNA content) or metabolic labeling (e.g., 4sU/EU incorporation), relative RNA-seq abundance cannot distinguish active transcription from differential decay resistance.
- **Omission of RNA Integrity Numbers (RIN):**
  - Although Methods (line 75) state that RNA quality was assessed with an `Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (RNA 6000 Nano Kit)`, no RIN or DV200 values are reported anywhere in Table 2 or the text for the 5 forensic samples.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *"Furthermore, the activation of mechanisms that positively regulate biological processes, cellular processes, and cell proliferation suggests that the cells were still viable. All in all, we demonstrated that the transcription machinery remains active in prostate tissue even after five days postmortem"* / *"our analysis confirmed the high persistence of active transcriptional machinery, leading to cells being alive in high PMI prostate tissue"* `[Methodologically Flawed]`
  - Claiming that somatic cells in a human cadaver remain "alive," "viable," and actively "proliferating" 5 days after death (including storage at $-20^\circ\text{C}$) based on relative GO term enrichment of residual transcripts violates basic physiology and molecular biology. Even though the authors briefly concede on line 23 that *"genome-wide experiments do not allow the evaluation of RNA degradation, therefore the marked changes in the gene expression may be attributed to the combination of a reduced RNA synthesis and/or to the RNA degradation,"* they contradict this caveat throughout the Abstract, Results, and Conclusions by asserting that cells are alive and actively transcribing at 5 days postmortem.
- **Claim 2:** *"SNRPA1 is significantly upregulated (p-value = 0.0029), whilst the RBM38 gene is significantly downregulated (p-value = 0.0096) in high PMI samples compared to low PMI samples, making them forensic biomarker candidates for the estimation of the PMI."* `[Overstated]`
  - Proposing candidate forensic biomarkers from an unadjusted $p$-value in an $n = 2$ vs. $n = 3$ discovery comparison with no orthogonal validation (not even qRT-PCR on the same 5 samples or an independent autopsy cohort) is statistically unsupported. Moreover, in the GTEx dataset ($n = 42$), ischemia duration spans up to ~27 hours ($<2$ days), which does not overlap the 3–5 day forensic PMI window.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw and processed forensic prostate RNA-seq data are deposited in NCBI GEO under accession `GSE249984`. GTEx v8 prostate data were obtained from `gtexportal.org`.
- **Metadata & Reporting Gaps:** Individual sample RIN/DV200 scores, total read counts, mapping rates, rRNA depletion efficiency (`TruSeq Stranded Total RNA`), and the exact differential expression software (`DESeq2` vs. `edgeR` vs. `limma`) used prior to filtering are not clearly specified in the Methods.
