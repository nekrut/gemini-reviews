### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This genomics and quantitative genetics study integrates pooled single-cell RNA sequencing (10X Genomics Chromium 3' scRNA-seq of 18,233 log-phase cells in rich YPD media; BioProject `PRJNA1022775`) with an existing barcoded bulk genotype-phenotype map (`BB-QTL`) of `BY4741` $\times$ `RM11-1a` F2 *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* segregants, focusing on Batch 1 ($n = 4,489$ F2 segregants). Because the lineage DNA barcodes are not expressed, the authors adapt a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) to infer segregant genotypes across 41,594 SNPs from sparse scRNA-seq exonic reads ($\sim 0.2\times$ depth, $3\%$ SNP breadth per cell), assigning $72.2\%$ ($13,069 / 18,233$) of cell barcodes to reference segregants ($65.7\%$ of the 4,489 lineages recovered). They then apply the `DISCERN` autoencoder neural network to denoise/impute single-cell expression profiles, use `GCTA-GREML` to partition 30°C YPD fitness variance into genotype-only ($20.9\%$), genotype-expression shared ($47.8\%$), and expression-only ($0.69\%$) components, and perform BIC-penalized forward stepwise regression to map `cis`- and `trans`-sc-eQTLs across 6,240 genes. While the HMM-based lineage recovery from ultra-low-coverage scRNA-seq reads and the replication of the 29 bulk fitness QTLs are technical strengths, the transcriptomic variance-partitioning and sc-eQTL claims are compromised by algorithmic and statistical artifacts: (1) `DISCERN` autoencoder imputation (coupled with uncentered gene standardization) collapses 99.6% of the variance of the entire 6,240-gene transcriptome onto a single principal component (PC1), which is then used as the univariate response in GCTA-REML to claim 75.7% transcriptome heritability; (2) individual gene-level GCTA-REML heritabilities exhibit an implausible floor of $\min = 31.0\%$ ($\sigma = 4.51\%$) across all 6,240 genes; (3) the "consensus" expression profile per segregant is actually taken from a single cell (the cell with maximum read count), failing to average out cell-cycle variation; and (4) omitting cross-validation in favor of standard BIC ($\lambda = \log n$) during forward stepwise selection across $p = 41,594$ SNPs overfits the eQTL models (calling a mean of 34.3 eQTLs per gene).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Artifactual Collapse of 99.6% of Imputed Transcriptome Variance onto PC1 and Conflation of PC1 Heritability with Total Transcriptome Heritability:**
  - On line 56 (Results) and in `Methods: Estimating the expression heritability from scRNA-seq` (Offset 46080, line 60), the authors report that after denoising the expression matrix with the `DISCERN` neural network, **the first principal component (PC1) explains 99.6% of the total variance across all 6,240 yeast genes** (*"we orthogonalized the gene expression matrix using principal component analysis (PCA) and used its first PC, which explains 99.6% of the variance, as a response variable of the model"*).
  - In authentic biological transcriptomes across 6,240 genes and ~3,000 genetically diverse yeast segregants, a single linear axis (PC1) never accounts for 99.6% of transcriptome variance. This 99.6% PC1 collapse is a classic mathematical artifact of either: (a) performing SVD/PCA on an uncentered matrix after dividing standardized counts by total cell counts (so PC1 simply captures the mean gene-to-gene expression vector across cells), or (b) severe rank collapse by the `DISCERN` autoencoder reconstructing all cells from a low-dimensional bottleneck. Using this single collapsed PC1 score as the response vector in `GCTA-REML` to claim that *"genotype explains 75.7% of expression variance"* (line 81) does not measure multi-dimensional transcriptome heritability.
- **Biologically and Statistically Implausible Floor ($\min = 31.0\%$, $\sigma = 4.51\%$) in Gene-Level Expression Heritability Across All 6,240 Genes:**
  - On line 81, the authors state: *"At the individual gene level, the genotype explains, on average, 35.4% of transcript level variation (σ=4.51%; min = 31.0%; median = 33.4%; max = 100%)."*
  - Across the entire *S. cerevisiae* genome of 6,240 genes—which includes hundreds of genes that are not expressed in YPD log phase (e.g., sporulation/meiosis genes), lowly expressed transcripts (median 1,311 genes detected per cell prior to imputation; Table 1), and tightly buffered housekeeping/cell-cycle genes—it is biologically impossible for **every single gene in the genome** to have a narrow-sense expression heritability $\ge 31.0\%$ with a standard deviation across 6,240 genes of only $4.51\%$! By comparison, in `Albert et al.`'s bulk RNA-seq study of 1,012 BY $\times$ RM segregants, gene-level expression heritability spans the full range from $0\%$ to $>90\%$. This artificial floor ($\min = 31.0\%$) indicates that either the `DISCERN` neural network imputed missing gene counts as a deterministic function of shared global transcriptomic covariates (leaking genetic kinship across all imputed genes) or the `GCTA-REML` solver hit a lower boundary constraint.
- **Contradiction Between Claiming to "Average Out" Cell-Cycle Effects and Selecting the Single Maximum-Read Cell per Segregant:**
  - On line 98, the authors state: *"Because our cells are not synchronized, differences in the cell cycle could create transcriptomic variation that could obscure the association with genotypic variation. To control for this, we averaged out the effect of cell cycle by reconstructing the consensus expression and genotype profiles of each segregant significantly associated to the cells."*
  - Yet in the Figure 3 legend (line 86) and Methods (Offset 46080, line 55), the authors reveal how this "consensus" expression profile was actually constructed: *"Because each segregant had multiple representatives/cells in the scRNA-seq data, the consensus profile of these lineages has been obtained from the cell with the highest transcript level"* (`the cell with the maximum read count`). Picking a **single cell** (the highest-UMI cell) per segregant does **not** average across cells at different cell-cycle stages; rather, it discards the other ~3.8 cells per segregant and biases selection toward large late-G2/M cells with maximal total RNA content!
- **Overfitting of Stepwise Forward eQTL Selection Under Standard BIC ($\lambda = \log n$) When $p = 41,594 \gg n$:**
  - For the 30°C fitness QTL mapping, the authors used 10-fold cross-validation to tune the $L_0$ penalty $\lambda \ge \log(n)$ and identified 29 QTLs (matching the 31 QTLs found in bulk). However, for eQTL mapping across all 6,240 genes (Offset 46080, line 72), they bypassed cross-validation to save compute time and fixed $\lambda = \log(n)$ (standard BIC). In high-dimensional feature selection where the number of candidate SNPs ($p = 41,594$) vastly exceeds the number of segregants ($n \approx 2,950$), standard BIC ($\lambda = \log n$) without a model-space multiplicity penalty (such as Extended BIC, $\text{EBIC}_\gamma = \log n + 2\gamma \log p$) is notoriously anti-conservative, explaining why the greedy forward search selects an average of **34.3 eQTLs per gene** (median 21, nearly $4\times$ higher than `Albert et al.`) even though the effective sample size of unique recovered segregants (~2,950) at $5,158$ UMIs/cell has lower total transcript count per strain than `Albert et al.`'s deep bulk RNA-seq of 1,012 segregants.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality / STRENGTHS
- **Strengths:**
  - **Clever HMM Genotyping and Permutation-Validated Lineage Assignment from Sparse ($0.2\times$) scRNA-Seq Reads:** Re-estimating reverse-transcriptase error and index-swapping parameters directly from non-polymorphic and multi-mismatch reads (Fig. 1—figure supplement 1) increased lineage recovery from $58.6\%$ to $65.7\%$ and raised median genotype concordance ($R^2$) with reference bulk sequencing from $0.64$ to $0.81$ ($0.87$ for median consensus genotypes; Fig. 2A), accurately reproducing narrow-sense fitness heritabilities across 15 environments (Fig. 2B) and the 30°C fitness QTL map ($86.2\%$ similarity score).
  - **One-Pot Pooled Growth Design:** Culturing all 4,489 segregants in a single pooled flask prior to 10X Chromium capture eliminates flask-to-flask environmental batch effects that required growth-rate regression in earlier multi-batch bulk RNA-seq studies.
- **Claim Proportionality:**
  - `[Well Supported]` Pooled 10X Chromium scRNA-seq of F2 yeast segregants coupled with HMM genotype imputation accurately recovers segregant genotypes ($\text{median } R^2 = 0.87$) and replicates bulk fitness heritability and fitness QTL locations.
  - `[Moderately Supported]` Known major fitness QTL hubs (`MKT1`, `HAP1`, `IRA2`, `VPS70`, `TDH3`) colocalize with trans-eQTL regulatory hotspots, and trans-eQTLs cumulatively account for more expression variance than individual large-effect cis-eQTLs (Fig. 4).
  - `[Methodologically Flawed]` Claims that PC1 of `DISCERN`-imputed expression explains 99.6% of biological transcriptome variance, that genotype explains 75.7% of total expression variance via PC1, and that individual gene expression heritability has a minimum of $31.0\%$ across all 6,240 yeast genes.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Data and code availability are exemplary: raw scRNA-seq reads are deposited in NCBI BioProject (`PRJNA1022775`), processed expression matrices (`Matrix_gene_expression_barcodes_1_to_9000.csv.tar.gz` and `9001_to_18233.csv.tar.gz`) and all Python/R pipelines for HMM genotyping, lineage assignment, variance partitioning, and sc-eQTL mapping are publicly archived on GitHub (`https://github.com/arnaud00013/sc-eQTL`).
