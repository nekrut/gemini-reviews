### 1. Executive Summary
This study reports the positional cloning, molecular characterization, and developmental genetic analysis of *warthog* (`wrt`), a *Drosophila melanogaster* locus originally isolated as an enhancer of a sensitized *Notch* eye phenotype that also causes a recessive macrochaete bristle defect on the head, notum, and scutellum. Through P-element complementation testing (`P2352` / `l(2)08232`), a 25-kb genomic walk at `33C–D` on chromosome `2L`, cDNA library screening, P-element excision mutagenesis (generating 14 homozygous lethal alleles including the `1.8-kb` genomic null deletion `wrt^D23D`, 9 viable hypomorphic bristle alleles, and 13 precise-excision wild-type revertants), and transgenic genomic/cDNA rescue, the authors prove that `wrt` encodes `Drab6`, the *Drosophila* homologue of the Golgi-to-TGN vesicular trafficking GTPase `Rab6` (89% identical to human `Rab6`). They further show that the original screen alleles (`wrt^AM4`, `wrt^ER1`, `wrt^AS1`) and a spontaneous allele (`wrt^MEF`) carry an identical hypomorphic `R62C` missense mutation adjacent to the `DX2G` GTP-binding motif derived from the parental `w^1118` stock, that complete loss of `Drab6` (`D23D` and `P2352`) is larval lethal even in the absence of maternal contribution (`ovo^D FLP-FRT` germline clones), that `FLP-FRT` somatic mosaic clones of `P2352` affect macrochaete and microchaete bristle shafts in a **cell-nonautonomous** manner without perturbing eye, wing, or leg morphogenesis, and that pulsed heat-shock overexpression of a constitutively active GTP-locked mutant (`Drab6-Q71L`) redirects bristle shaft growth orientation while rescuing `R62C` at basal expression levels. The genetic analysis is rigorous, self-correcting, and insightful.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Exemplary Genetic Dissection and Allelic Series Construction:**
  - The authors construct a complete allelic series spanning hypomorphic missense (`R62C`), P-element insertion (`P2352`, inserted ~30 bp upstream of the `Drab6` start codon), imprecise excision genomic null (`D23D`, deleting the entire intronless `Drab6` ORF), precise excision revertants (`13` independent lines confirming that the `P2352` insertion causes the phenotype), and dominant-active GTPase-deficient (`Q71L`) alleles.
  - Because the `1.8-kb` `D23D` deletion extends to 72 bp upstream of the adjacent kallikrein-like serine protease gene `Phaedra1` (`Phae1`), the authors rigorously verify using nested genomic rescue constructs (`9-6`, `X-X5` lacking the C-terminal `CAC` isoprenylation motif, `SacII-RI`, `RX11`) and heat-shock `Drab6` cDNA transgenes that both the larval lethality and the bristle defects of `D23D` and `P2352` are fully rescued by the `Drab6` ORF alone (`Figure 4`).
- **Candid Resolution of Shared `R62C` Lesion Across Mutagenesis Screens:**
  - Sequencing of `wrt^AM4`, `wrt^ER1`, `wrt^AS1` (>4 independent PCR clones each) revealed the exact same `C313T` (`R62C`) transition despite originating from separate EMS and X-ray screens. Rather than over-interpreting this as a mutational hotspot, the authors sequenced a spontaneous allele (`wrt^MEF`) isolated from the same `w^1118` parental background, confirmed it carried `R62C`, and transparently concluded that `R62C` was segregating at low frequency in the starting `w^1118` stock.
- **Unshown Biochemical and Double-Mutant Data (`"Data Not Shown"`):**
  - In the Discussion (line 147), the authors state that *"in vitro studies revealed R62C mutant protein was capable of binding and hydrolyzing GTP (data not shown),"* and on line 142 they note that *"no additive or synergistic interactions were seen with many mutations known to affect bristle structure (e.g., Sb, sn^3, f^36a, Pr^1, ss, Bsb, Bsb Pr, and Pr Dr; data not shown)."* Showing the quantitative GTP-binding and hydrolysis curves for recombinant `Drab6-R62C` vs. wild-type and `Q71L` would have substantially strengthened the biochemical characterization of this novel `R62C` mutation.
  - In the somatic mosaic analysis (`Figure 6d`), the finding of cell non-autonomy (`P2352/P2352` homozygous tissue containing wild-type bristles and vice versa) is intriguing; specifying the exact cuticular markers used to score clone boundaries (`yellow` `y` and `pawn` `pwn` or `multiple wing hairs` on the thorax) and quantifying the distance (in cell diameters) over which non-autonomous rescue occurs would make this observation more precise.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **`[Well Supported]` `warthog` encodes `Drab6` and is essential for larval viability in *Drosophila* without a maternal requirement for embryogenesis:** Proven by molecular cloning, precise excision reversion, `D23D` null deletion rescue by `Drab6` cDNA (`Figure 4–5`), and `ovo^D FLP-FRT` maternal germline clone analysis showing 100% survival of `P2352/P2352` embryos to the larval stage.
- **`[Well Supported]` Post-larval `Drab6` loss-of-function selectively disrupts thoracic/head mechanosensory bristle shaft elongation and circumferential actin-ridge morphology while sparing general eye, wing, and leg development:** Demonstrated by scanning electron microscopy (`Figures 2, 3, 6, 7`) across both viable `R62C` homozygotes and `P2352/P2352` somatic clones, supporting substantial functional redundancy in Golgi-to-TGN transport across most adult tissues.
- **`[Moderately Supported]` `Drab6` modifies *Notch* signaling by delaying TGN-to-cell-surface transport or altering furin-like (`S1`) proteolytic processing of the Notch receptor in the TGN:** These two mechanistic models (`Discussion` lines 131–133) are biologically compelling and well-grounded in mammalian `Rab6` trafficking kinetics, though direct immunolocalization or pulse-chase cleavage analysis of Notch in `wrt` mutant cells is left for future work.
