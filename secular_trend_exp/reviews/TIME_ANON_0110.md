### 1. Executive Summary
This computational evolutionary genomics study adapts the `ConSpeciFix` framework—originally developed to delineate bacterial biological species via the ratio of homoplastic ($h$) to non-homoplastic ($m$) polymorphisms—coupled with `CoreSimul` forward sequence simulations to quantify the contribution of homologous recombination versus convergent mutation across *Betacoronavirus* subgenera (*Embecovirus*, *Merbecovirus*, and *Sarbecovirus*). Analyzing 168 dereplicated genomes ($D \ge 0.0001$, downsampled from 1,422 VIPR genomes) and 218 early SARS-CoV-2 genomes, the authors report that (1) recombination accounts for ~40% of standing polymorphisms within the three major subgenera after subtracting simulated mutational homoplasies; (2) genetic exchange occurs exclusively within subgenera (and within specific subclades of *Embecovirus*), with between-subgenus $h/m$ drops matching simulated non-recombining lineages; (3) gene-by-gene analysis across *Sarbecovirus* shows that excess recombination ($h/m$ significantly above simulated null, $p < 10^{-5}$) is strongly concentrated in the Spike coding region; and (4) no recombination is detectable among the 218 early SARS-CoV-2 isolates.

While pairing $h/m$ resampling with explicit null simulations matched for tree topology, transition/transversion ratio ($\kappa$), and total polymorphism count is a thoughtful approach to addressing mutational saturation, the pipeline suffers from several algorithmic and biological modeling artifacts—most notably an unexamined gene-segmentation artifact that splits the ~30-kb *Sarbecovirus* genome into "43 conserved genes" including multiple "genes encoding subunits of the spike protein," and a site-homogeneous codon simulation model that conflates diversifying positive selection in Spike with recombination.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Gene Annotation / Orthology Fragmentation Artifact ("43 Genes" in *Sarbecovirus*):** In the Results (line 54) and Figure 2, the authors state: "We quantified the ratios of homoplastic to non-homoplastic polymorphisms for each of the 43 genes conserved among members of this subgroup [*Sarbecovirus*]... Nearly all the genes encoding subunits of the spike protein display highly significant (>1.2-fold, $p < 10^{-5}$...) increases in $h/m$ ratios. Aside from those in the spike protein region, only three CDSs—CDS6, CDS28 and CDS33—which are located within the large ORF1ab polyprotein..." Biologically, *Sarbecovirus* genomes (~29.9 kb) contain only ~10–14 ORFs (with ORF1ab cleaved into 16 non-structural proteins) and a single gene (`S`) encoding the Spike glycoprotein (which is proteolytically cleaved into S1 and S2 subunits post-translationally, not encoded by multiple separate genes). In Figure 2, the pipeline has evidently partitioned the *Sarbecovirus* core genome into 43 numbered segments (`CDS1` through `CDS43`, with `CDS34`–`CDS38` representing fragments of Spike) due to how `ConSpeciFix` or the annotation parser split mature peptides/domains or sliding orthologous blocks, yet the authors refer to them throughout the text as "the 43 genes conserved among members of this subgroup" and "genes encoding subunits of the spike protein."
- **Confounding of Positive/Diversifying Selection and Site-to-Site Rate Variation with Recombination in `CoreSimul`:**
  - In the Methods (line 106), `CoreSimul` evolves sequences without recombination by fixing relative substitution rates across codon positions 1, 2, and 3 at `0.1, 0.05, and 0.85` uniformly across all sites in a gene, rescaled to match the total polymorphism count of that gene.
  - However, in real *Sarbecovirus* evolution—especially in the Spike receptor-binding domain (RBD) and N-terminal domain (NTD)—nonsynonymous substitutions are intensely concentrated at a small subset of hypervariable, surface-exposed loops under strong diversifying selection ($\omega > 1$) and host-receptor adaptation, while core structural residues are invariant. When a gene's total polymorphism count is redistributed by `CoreSimul` across all sites using uniform rates within codon positions 1, 2, and 3 (without a continuous Gamma distribution $+\Gamma$ of site-to-site rate heterogeneity or site-specific $\omega > 1$), the simulation spreads mutations across the entire gene length rather than concentrating them at a few hot-spot codons. Consequently, `CoreSimul` systematically underestimates true mutational convergence (homoplasy $h$) at adaptive hotspots, causing any region under strong diversifying selection to exhibit an artificially elevated observed-to-simulated $h/m$ ratio.
- **Persistent Taxonomic Misspelling:** Throughout the entire manuscript (Abstract, Introduction, Results headings, Figures 1, S2–S5, Discussion, and Methods), the ICTV-recognized *Betacoronavirus* subgenus *Embecovirus* is repeatedly misspelled as *"Embevovirus"* (19 occurrences).
- **Ambiguous Interpretation of the SARS-CoV-2 Inclusion Test (Line 42 & S5 Fig):** The authors report that adding a single SARS-CoV-2 genome (`MT106054`) to *Embecovirus* lowers $h/m$, whereas adding it to *Sarbecovirus* or *Merbecovirus* "did not substantially modify the $h/m$ ratios of these clades (S5 Fig), which can be interpreted as evidence of recombination between SARS-CoV-2 and other *Sarbecoviruses* and/or *Merbecoviruses*." Because SARS-CoV-2 *is* a native member of *Sarbecovirus*, keeping $h/m$ unchanged within *Sarbecovirus* is expected; however, the lack of $h/m$ drop in *Merbecovirus* ($n=17$) when adding one divergent sequence is primarily a sample-size and saturation artifact, which the authors partially acknowledge in the following sentence ("cannot fully rule-out the possibility that these patterns are due to convergent mutations") and later refute via simulation ($p \approx 0.05$ for the 11 clustered homoplasies in NSP12/NSP13).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** "Gene exchange occurs almost exclusively among strains belonging to the same subgenus." `[Well Supported]` — The cross-subgenus addition tests paired with simulated non-recombining control sequences (S2 and S3 Figs) convincingly demonstrate reproductive isolation between *Sarbecovirus*, *Merbecovirus*, and *Embecovirus*, as well as subclade isolation within *Embecovirus* (S4 Fig).
- **Claim 2:** "We find that recombination accounts for nearly 40% of the polymorphisms circulating in populations..." `[Moderately Supported]` — While the direction of the excess $h/m$ over the `CoreSimul` baseline is clear (Fig. 1), the exact ~40% point estimate depends heavily on the lack of within-codon-position Gamma rate variation in the null simulator, which underestimates mutational homoplasies at hypervariable sites.
- **Claim 3:** "Nearly all the genes encoding subunits of the spike protein display highly significant (>1.2-fold, $p < 10^{-5}$...) increases in $h/m$ ratios... Although experimental studies have shown that recombinational exchanges occur at random along the coronaviral genome, in nature, they are vastly overrepresented in regions controlling viral interaction with host cells." `[Moderately Supported]` — Recombination breakpoints and Spike RBD mosaicism in sarbecoviruses are well established in the literature, and positive selection favors the retention of recombinant Spike variants; however, the authors' test cannot fully disentangle excess homoplasies driven by homologous recombination from excess homoplasies driven by intense diversifying selection at Spike contact residues, and the description of Spike domains as separate "genes" is a bioinformatic parsing error.
- **Claim 4:** "So far, no evidence of recombination among SARS-CoV-2 genomes." `[Well Supported]` — In the early pandemic sample of 218 nearly identical genomes (Fig. 3), sequence diversity was far too low to detect or accumulate homoplastic recombinants, as the authors correctly note in the Discussion.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Software & Accessions:** The authors cite `ConSpeciFix`, `CoreSimul`, and `RAxML v8` (`GTR + GAMMA`), specify the dereplication threshold ($D < 0.0001$) and orthology thresholds (50% nucleotide identity, 80% length conservation), and list all 1,422 initial VIPR genomes, 168 dereplicated genomes, and 218 SARS-CoV-2 accessions in Supporting Tables S1–S3. However, the exact mapping of the 43 numbered "genes" (`CDS1`–`CDS43` in Fig. 2) to standard coronavirus genomic coordinates and nsp/ORF names is not provided in the main text or figure legend.
