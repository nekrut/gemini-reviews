### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This manuscript is a brief editorial introduction to a special issue of *Current Opinion in Virology* surveying animal models for human viral pathogens with narrow host tropism. It summarizes nine commissioned review articles covering dengue virus (DENV) in interferon-receptor-deficient mice, hepatitis B, C, and delta viruses (HBV, HCV, HDV) in humanized/adapted mouse models, lymphotropic viruses (EBV, HCMV, HIV) in human immune system (HIS) and bone-marrow/liver/thymus (BLT) engrafted mice, influenza virus transmission in ferrets, mice, and guinea pigs, respiratory syncytial virus (hRSV and cognate bovine bRSV in cotton rats and cattle), and epidemic coronaviruses (SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV). Because this is a non-empirical editorial overview rather than a primary research study or systematic review, it contains no original datasets, formal search protocols, or quantitative analyses, but accurately summarizes the biological scope and translational limitations of each model system.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS, WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
**Strengths:**
* **Balanced Appraisal of Model Fidelity:** The editorial clearly distinguishes between Permissive/Immunocompromised models (e.g., IFN-$\alpha/\beta/\gamma$ receptor knockout mice for DENV), chimeric humanized platforms (HSC-engrafted HIS and fetal-tissue BLT mice for EBV/HCMV/HIV), and surrogate natural host-pathogen pairs (bRSV in cattle vs. hRSV in rodents), noting specific immunological defects such as weak humoral class-switching in BLT mice and lack of respiratory symptoms in influenza-infected guinea pigs.

**Weaknesses & Methodological Audit:**
* **Non-Primary Editorial Format:** As an issue preface, the text provides no systematic literature methodology, comparative summary tables, or quantitative synthesis of viral titers, transmission efficiencies, or immunological endpoints across animal models.
* **Lack of Primary Citations for Historical and Epidemiological Claims:** Broad epidemiological figures (e.g., ">600 million patients are infected with one or more of the five known hepatitis viruses") and historical milestones (Wilson Smith's 1933 human-to-ferret influenza transmission; enhanced aerosol spread of modified avian influenza in ferrets) are stated without direct primary bibliographic references, relying entirely on references to the nine articles within the issue.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1 (HIS and BLT mice enable mucosal HIV transmission and T-cell response modeling but exhibit weak humoral/isotype-switching responses): `[Well Supported]`** — Accurately reflects established immunological characteristics of HSC- and fetal thymus/liver-engrafted immunodeficient mice.
* **Claim 2 (Cognate bovine RSV in cattle recapitulates TH2 skewing, IL-4/IL-13/IgE production, and incomplete protection against reinfection seen in human RSV): `[Well Supported]`** — Biologically accurate comparison of natural host-pathogen immunopathology relative to non-permissive mouse adaptation.
* **Claim 3 (No single animal model faithfully recapitulates both SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV pathogenesis due to distinct receptor/tropism constraints): `[Well Supported]`** — Reflects the divergence between human ACE2 and DPP4 receptor usage in rodent models (prior to transgenic/knock-in DPP4 models).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Editorial Format:** No primary data, code, or tabular syntheses are generated or applicable. References are limited to the nine in-issue review articles.
