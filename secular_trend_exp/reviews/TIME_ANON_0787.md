### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This paper is an early-pandemic narrative mini-review compiling candidate pharmacological treatments proposed for COVID-19 (chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine, lopinavir/ritonavir, danoprevir/ritonavir, favipiravir, remdesivir, camostat/nafamostat mesylate, ivermectin, dexamethasone, convalescent plasma, and anticoagulants). While the paper appropriately notes pharmacokinetic limitations of *in vitro* ivermectin concentrations (citing Momekov & Momekova that $5\ \mu\text{M}$ is $17\times$ human $C_{\max}$), reports the negative randomized trial of lopinavir/ritonavir (Cao et al., $N=199$), and summarizes the RECOVERY trial benefit of dexamethasone in ventilated/oxygen-supported patients, it lacks any systematic review methodology, uncritically repeats claims from tiny unblinded case series ($N=3$ for danoprevir/ritonavir; $N=5$ for convalescent plasma) and flawed studies (Gautret et al., Gao et al., and unverified traditional Chinese medicine claims), and introduces unsupported nutritional prevention claims in the conclusion.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS, WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
**Strengths:**
* **Pharmacokinetic Critique of *In Vitro* Ivermectin:** The review correctly highlights that the $5\ \mu\text{M}$ concentration used by Caly et al. *in vitro* is approximately $50\times$ clinical plasma levels and $17\times$ literature $C_{\max}$ ($247.8\ \text{ng/mL}$), making single-dose *in vivo* extrapolation pharmacologically unfeasible.
* **Inclusion of Key Controlled Trial Outcomes:** Correctly summarizes the null 28-day mortality result for lopinavir/ritonavir ($N=199$, Cao et al.), the lack of clinical benefit in the Wang et al. remdesivir trial alongside the uncontrolled compassionate-use Grein et al. cohort, and the respiratory-support-stratified mortality reduction in the UK RECOVERY dexamethasone trial ($N \approx 2100$).

**Weaknesses & Methodological Audit:**
* **Complete Absence of Systematic Review Methodology:** The manuscript specifies no literature databases searched, no search strings or date cutoffs, no inclusion/exclusion criteria, no summary comparison table of trial designs/sample sizes/endpoints, and no risk-of-bias or evidence-tier grading (e.g., GRADE).
* **Uncritical Reliance on Tiny, Uncontrolled Case Series:** In the "Danoprevir/Ritonavir" section, the authors state as fact that *"the danoprevir/ritonavir therapy can suppress viral replication in less than a week and is effective in reducing ground-glass opacity (GGO) areas. Viral nucleic acids detected through nasal swabs become negative about 2–3 days after the beginning of the treatment"* based on an uncontrolled report of **three** patients (Chen et al.). Similarly, convalescent plasma efficacy is described from a 5-patient uncontrolled series (Shen et al.) confounded by concomitant lopinavir/ritonavir and interferon.
* **Inconsistent Evaluation of Chloroquine/Hydroxychloroquine & TCM:** The text states that *"The addition of Azithromycin to the treatment, concomitant with the use of hydroxychloroquine, was significantly more efficient in eliminating the virus (Gautret et al.)"* and cites Gao et al. without noting the methodological flaws, non-randomization, or attrition in Gautret et al., before appending the June 17 WHO statement at the end of the section. In the Introduction, the authors assert without evidence grading that *"many results in clinical practice have shown that traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) plays a significant role in the treatment of COVID-19."*
* **Unsupported Non-Sequitur in Conclusion:** The final sentence of the Conclusion asserts: *"However, having healthy eating habits and adequate levels of vitamins D and B help the maintenance of the immune system, which is one of the many suggestions to prevent the disease"*—a claim neither reviewed nor cited anywhere in the body of the manuscript.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1 (Dexamethasone reduces mortality by ~1/3 in mechanically ventilated patients and ~1/5 in oxygen-supported patients, with no benefit in patients not requiring respiratory support): `[Well Supported]`** — Accurately summarizes the RECOVERY trial findings and immunopathological phase rationale.
* **Claim 2 (*In vitro* antiviral activity of $5\ \mu\text{M}$ ivermectin cannot be achieved with safe clinical dosing): `[Well Supported]`** — Grounded in quantitative human pharmacokinetic parameters ($C_{\max} = 247.8\ \text{ng/mL}$).
* **Claim 3 (Danoprevir/ritonavir suppresses SARS-CoV-2 replication in <1 week and clears nasal swabs in 2–3 days): `[Overstated]`** — Inferred from an uncontrolled 3-patient observation where spontaneous viral clearance cannot be distinguished from drug effect.
* **Claim 4 (Traditional Chinese medicine prevents progression to severe COVID-19 and Vitamins D and B prevent COVID-19): `[Methodologically Flawed]`** — Asserted in the Introduction and Conclusion with zero clinical trial evidence or mechanistic data presented in the review.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **No Search Protocol or Summary Tables:** Because no search strategy, flow diagram, or structured table of clinical studies is provided, the literature selection is non-reproducible.
