### 1. Executive Summary

This manuscript attempts to characterize the intrinsic, selection-free mutation rates and mutational spectra of HIV-1 reverse transcriptase (RT) on native viral gene templates (*Gag*, *protease*, wild-type *RT p66*, and a synonymously codon-mutated *RT p66*). The authors transiently transfect pTT5 expression plasmids encoding each gene into mammalian EXPI293F cells, extract total cellular RNA after two days, perform in vitro cDNA synthesis using recombinant HIV-1 RT (p51/p66), amplify the cDNA via 35 cycles of Q5 PCR, clone the products into blunt-end TOPO vectors, and Sanger-sequence 571 to 801 clones per gene. They then perform Two Sample Logo flanking-sequence analyses around observed $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ mutations and in silico protein stability ($\Delta\Delta G$) predictions using FoldX and Rosetta `Cartesian_ddg`.

While examining template-specific effects on viral polymerase fidelity is a reasonable biological goal, the study's central conclusions rest on a fundamentally confounded experimental design and uncalibrated statistical interpretations. First, because the RNA templates fed to recombinant HIV-1 RT were transcribed inside mammalian EXPI293F cells rather than generated by defined in vitro transcription, mutations introduced in vivo by host RNA Polymerase II and host adenosine deaminases (ADAR1/2) are conflated with errors made in vitro by recombinant HIV-1 RT. Second, bulk cDNA was amplified for 35 PCR cycles prior to TOPO cloning without unique molecular identifiers (UMIs), meaning the 571–801 sequenced colonies per gene are non-independent amplicons of the same template pool rather than independent RT events. Third, the central biological claim that HIV-1 RT "avoids crucial functional sites" is an artifact of severe under-sampling: across ~600 clones at a mutation rate of $\sim 6 \times 10^{-5}\text{ bp}^{-1}$, the expected number of mutations in a 9-bp active site is $\lambda \approx 0.35$ ($P(X=0) \approx 0.70$ under a uniform Poisson null). These foundational flaws invalidate the primary claims.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit

1. **Conflation of Cellular Transcription/RNA Editing with Recombinant HIV-1 RT Error Rates:**
   * In the Abstract and Table 1, the authors report HIV-1 RT error rates of $4.71 \times 10^{-5}$ (*Gag*), $6.03 \times 10^{-5}$ (*protease*), $7.09 \times 10^{-5}$ (*RT p66*), and $1.88 \times 10^{-4}$ (*codon-mutated RT p66*) mutations/bp *inclusive* of $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ hypermutations, while simultaneously arguing in Section 2.3 and the Discussion (lines 101–102) that these $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ hypermutations were elicited by host cell ADAR deaminases inside the transfected EXPI293F cells prior to RNA extraction.
   * In the Discussion (line 95), the authors dismiss host cell contributions by citing the *genomic DNA replication* error rates of *E. coli* ($5.4 \times 10^{-10}$) and mammalian cells ($5.0 \times 10^{-11}$). However, the template extracted from EXPI293F cells is **mRNA** transcribed by host RNA Polymerase II, whose transcription error rate ($\sim 10^{-5}\text{ to }10^{-6}\text{ bp}^{-1}$) is five orders of magnitude higher than mammalian DNA replication and comparable to the background mutation rates measured here.
   * No parallel control was performed using a high-fidelity reverse transcriptase on the same EXPI293F RNA extracts to quantify baseline cellular mRNA errors/editing, nor was an in vitro T7-transcribed RNA control run to isolate true HIV-1 RT polymerase errors from host-cell ADAR activity. Consequently, attributing the 2.7-fold higher overall mutation rate in codon-mutated *p66* ($1.88 \times 10^{-4}$ vs. $7.09 \times 10^{-5}$, driven largely by 139 vs. 27 $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ hypermutations) to HIV-1 RT template fidelity is invalid.

2. **Clonal Non-Independence from 35-Cycle Bulk PCR Amplification:**
   * Following a single bulk in vitro cDNA synthesis reaction from $3\,\mu\text{g}$ of total RNA (Section 4.2), the authors perform 35 cycles of PCR amplification before ligating products into TOPO vectors and picking 571–801 bacterial colonies per gene (Section 4.3).
   * Because each cDNA molecule (or single ADAR-edited mRNA molecule from a transfected cell) is exponentially amplified during 35 cycles of PCR, multiple colonies picked from the TOPO plate can derive from the exact same original cDNA molecule. Without single-molecule barcoding (UMIs) or limiting-dilution cDNA synthesis prior to PCR, clone counts cannot be treated as independent Bernoulli/Poisson trials, distorting both point-mutation frequencies and "hypermutation" clone counts.

3. **Failure to Compute Poisson Null Expectations for "Avoidance of Crucial Functional Sites":**
   * The Abstract (line 7), Section 2.2 (lines 57–62), and Discussion (lines 107, 111) claim that HIV-1 RT exhibits a "general avoidance of crucial functional sites" and "in-built sequence barriers of self-preservation" because zero mutations were observed in the *protease* active site ($\text{25-DTG-27}$, 9 bp), the *RT p66* catalytic triad ($\text{D110, D185, D186}$, 9 bp), the p51-RNH cleavage site ($\text{AETF440}\downarrow\text{Y441VD}$, 21 bp), and four of five *Gag* cleavage sites (21 bp each).
   * Simple Poisson math shows that the study has virtually zero statistical power to detect depletion at individual motifs of this size:
     * For the *protease* active site (9 bp across 640 clones = $5,760\text{ bp}$ sequenced) at the gene-wide rate of $6.03 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mutations/bp}$, the expected number of mutations under a completely uniform random distribution is $\lambda = 5,760 \times 6.03 \times 10^{-5} = 0.347$, yielding $P(X = 0) = e^{-0.347} = 0.707$ ($70.7\%$ probability of observing zero mutations by chance alone).
     * For the *RT p66* catalytic triad (9 bp across 571 clones = $5,139\text{ bp}$ sequenced) at $7.09 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mutations/bp}$, $\lambda = 0.364$, yielding $P(X = 0) = e^{-0.364} = 0.695$.
     * For a 21-bp cleavage site in *Gag* across 801 clones ($16,821\text{ bp}$ sequenced) at $3.36 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mutations/bp}$ (excluding hypermutations), $\lambda = 0.565$, yielding $P(X = 0) = e^{-0.565} = 0.568$. Across five 21-bp *Gag* cleavage sites ($84,105\text{ bp}$ total), the expected total number of mutations is $2.83$, and the authors actually observed $2$ mutations (A431D and F433L in the NC/p1 cleavage site), which is indistinguishable from the uniform expectation ($P = 0.80$).
   * Likewise, domain-level rate variations in Figure 4 are presented as point estimates without confidence intervals or hypothesis tests, even though several domains contain only 0 to 5 mutations.

4. **Uncorrected Multiple Testing in Flanking-Sequence Logo Analysis & Missing Tests in $\Delta\Delta G$ Comparisons:**
   * In Figure 5C (Section 2.3), the authors test 8 flanking positions ($\pm 4\text{ nt}$) $\times 4$ bases $= 32$ hypotheses per gene across 4 templates ($128$ tests total) using a "two sample *t*-test without Bonferroni correction" at $p < 0.05$, and selectively highlight individual positions that match published ADAR1/2 preferences while ignoring positions that conflict (e.g., in codon-mutated *p66*, $+1$ is enriched for $\text{G}$ at $p < 0.01$, whereas ADAR1/2 preference in Fig. 5B is $\text{G}$ only for ADAR2 while $-1$ depletion of $\text{G}$ is the primary ADAR signature). Moreover, because multiple $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ mutations occur on the same hypermutated clones, the flanking windows are not independent observations if PCR duplicate clones are present.
   * In Section 2.4 (Figure 6), claims that wild-type *p66* and *Gag* are structurally buffered against $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ mutations compared to other mutations or codon-mutated *p66* are based on visual inspection of density peaks and mean values ($1.13\text{ vs. }1.75\text{ kcal/mol}$) without any two-sample statistical test (e.g., Mann-Whitney $U$ or permutation test) or variance estimates.

5. **Internal Arithmetic Inconsistencies:**
   * **Gag:** Table 1 lists **40** mutations excluding hypermutations and **56** including hypermutations. However, Section 2.1 (line 31) reports $24$ transitions + $12$ transversions $= 36$ substitutions excluding hypermutations, and $40$ transitions + $12$ transversions $= 52$ substitutions including hypermutations (matching Fig. 2A's $n = 52$). Adding the 4 frameshift mutations from line 31 gives $36 + 4 = 40$ and $52 + 4 = 56$, confirming that Table 1 includes frameshifts; however, Figure 3A reports $n = 54$ amino acid mutations ($30\text{ missense} + 15\text{ silent} + 5\text{ nonsense} + 4\text{ frameshift} = 54$), leaving 2 substitutions unaccounted for between Table 1/Fig. 2A and Fig. 3A.
   * **RT p66:** Line 50 states that when hypermutations are excluded ($n = 29$ substitutions: $18$ transitions + $11$ transversions), the substitution counts are: $\text{T}\to\text{C}$ ($n=8, 27.6\%$), $\text{G}\to\text{T}$ ($n=8, 27.6\%$), $\text{G}\to\text{A}$ ($n=6, 20.7\%$), $\text{C}\to\text{A}$ ($n=4, 12.5\%$), $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ ($n=2, 6.9\%$), and $\text{C}\to\text{T}$ ($n=2, 6.9\%$). First, $8+8+6+4+2+2 = 30 \neq 29$. Second, $4/29 = 13.8\%$ (not $12.5\%$). Third, in Fig. 3A, amino acid mutations sum to $n = 64$ ($31 + 19 + 2 + 12$), which is 4 fewer than Table 1's 68 mutations ($56\text{ substitutions} + 12\text{ frameshifts}$).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality

* **Claim that native HIV-1 sequences possess "in-built sequence barriers" that prevent RT from mutating active/cleavage sites:** **Methodologically Flawed / Overstated.** As shown in the Poisson calculation above, observing 0 mutations in 9-bp catalytic motifs when only $\sim 0.35$ mutations are expected by chance provides no evidence of mutational avoidance. Furthermore, the single-base genetic code probabilities that the authors themselves cite in Discussion line 97 show that $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ transitions inherently produce mostly conservative missense or synonymous changes (e.g., $\text{AAA [Lys]} \to \text{AGA [Arg]}$, $\text{GAA [Glu]} \to \text{GGA [Gly]}$) and can never generate stop codons ($\text{TAA, TAG, TGA}$) because they deplete rather than create $\text{A}$ bases—a property of the universal genetic code rather than an evolved structural buffer specific to HIV-1 *Gag* or *p66*.
* **Claim that codon usage of wild-type *RT p66* protects against HIV-1 RT mutagenesis:** **Confounded.** The codon-mutated *p66* construct is 63 bp shorter than the wild-type *p66* construct in Table 1 ($1,617\text{ bp}$ vs. $1,680\text{ bp}$, an unexplained 21-codon truncation), uses different PCR primers and annealing temperatures (Section 4.3), and its elevated mutation rate is overwhelmingly driven by $\text{A}\to\text{G}$ hypermutations ($139/213$ mutations) occurring in the mammalian transfection host prior to HIV-1 RT addition.
* **Speculative extrapolations in Abstract and Discussion:** Claims that this low-throughput TOPO-cloning assay provides "transferable applications to how new viral diseases and drug resistances can emerge," "calculate the mutational events leading to the zoonotic transmission of SIV to HIV," and "predict viral tropism changes (as evident in current COVID19 spike changes)" (lines 7, 111, 117) are unsupported by the data.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness

* **Positives:** Plasmid backbones, GenBank accessions for reference *protease* (`AY622223.1`) and *RT p66* (`K03455.1`), recombinant HIV-1 RT catalog numbers (`40244-V07E` and `40244-V07E1`), PCR primer sequences, cycling parameters, and PDB ID (`3T19`) for RT p66 structure modeling are clearly specified in Section 4.
* **Deficiencies:**
  1. The exact length discrepancy between wild-type *RT p66* ($1,680\text{ bp}$) and codon-mutated *RT p66* ($1,617\text{ bp}$) in Table 1 is never explained in the Methods, nor is it clear how priming regions were excluded from the denominator of base counts.
  2. Raw Sanger chromatograms or aligned clone sequences are not deposited in a public repository; only summary lists in Supplementary Tables are referenced.
  3. The custom script used to extract and align 9-nt windows for Two Sample Logo analysis (Section 4.4) is not provided.
