### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This forensic phylogenetic audit re-evaluates published clinical oncology studies that reported high rates of somatic mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) mutations or cancer-predisposing germline mtDNA variants across hepatocellular, papillary thyroid, prostate, bladder, lung, head and neck, ovarian, and esophageal carcinomas, glioblastomas, medulloblastomas, and myelodysplastic syndromes. By mapping the reported "tumor-specific somatic mutations" and "cancer-associated polymorphisms" onto the global human mtDNA phylogeny (>2,100 complete mtDNA genomes and the SWGDAM forensic database), the authors demonstrate that blocks of purported somatic mutations in landmark cancer papers actually correspond to exact evolutionary pathways separating distinct human mtDNA haplogroups (e.g., African L1b/L1c2, West Eurasian W, K1a, U4a, J1c, U5a1, and East Asian M7c, F1b, B4b, D5a, M8a, Z). Because somatic mutagenesis in a tumor cannot independently recreate 5 to 18 linked diagnostic substitutions that define pre-existing human continental lineages, these "hypermutated tumors" and "heteroplasmic instabilities" are conclusively shown to be laboratory sample mix-ups, cross-contaminations, reference sequence errors (e.g., scoring errors in the original `J01415` CRS), or population stratification artifacts. The phylogenetic forensics are airtight and provide a major corrective to the cancer mtDNA field, with only minor overreach where the authors state that ">80% of the studies... are based on faulty data" without presenting a formal systematic review denominator table.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
#### Strengths
* **Irrefutable Phylogenetic Proofreading of Somatic Mutation Claims:** The core analytical method—mapping paired normal vs. tumor mtDNA mutation lists onto the rooted human mtDNA tree (Figure 1)—leverages the near-absence of recombination in human mtDNA and the known hierarchy of haplogroup-defining motifs. Showing, for instance, that Patient 2 in Kirches et al. has 11 glioblastoma "somatic" mutations matching subhaplogroup U4a while 6 normal-tissue mutations match subhaplogroup J1c (`A5198G`), or that Case 21 in esophageal cancer has a D5a normal esophagus vs. an M8a tumor, provides mathematical certainty of sample switch/contamination rather than somatic evolution.
* **Identification of Reference Sequence Scoring Artifacts and Shift Errors:** The authors catch subtle bioinformatic and curation errors across published datasets, including the use of the unrevised `J01415` sequence (containing the three historical CRS errors `4985`, `11335`, `14766` plus `A750G`) by Nishikawa et al., +/-1 bp coordinate shift errors (`G246A` $\to$ `G247A`, `G317A` $\to$ `G316A`, `T15904C` $\to$ `T15905C`) in Wong et al., and nucleotide swap errors (`A16183G` for `A16183C`).
* **Biophysical Critique of Artifactual Mutational Spectra:** In auditing Reddy et al. (Table III), the authors point out that 25 transversions and 18 indels vs. only 9 transitions (plus mutations inside primer annealing sites) violate the well-established ~10:1–20:1 transition:transversion bias of mammalian mtDNA and absence of coding frameshift indels across >1,700 complete human mitogenomes.
* **Clear Demonstration of Population Stratification and Non-Independence in Case-Control Studies:** For Yeh et al. and Petros et al., the authors show that treating linked haplogroup-defining variants (`T710C`–`T3308C`–`T7389C` on L1b; `A10398G`–`G7521A` on L0/L1/L2; `T6253C` on H15/D5/M13) as independent alleles in unmatched case-control cohorts violates statistical independence and conflates ancestry with cancer risk.

#### Weaknesses & Methodological Audit
* **Unquantified Denominator for the ">80%" Meta-Claim:** In the Discussion (line 88), the authors state: "We have found that the vast majority (>80%) of the studies dealing with potential functional implications of the mtDNA molecule in tumorigenesis (and providing data for inspection) are based on faulty data with surreal findings." However, the manuscript does not provide a systematic literature search protocol, inclusion/exclusion table, or the exact numerator and denominator of inspected studies (roughly 12–15 specific papers are dissected in the text).
* **Lack of Formal Coincidence Probability Calculation:** While qualitatively obvious to any population geneticist that 8–17 linked haplogroup-defining mutations cannot arise de novo by somatic mutation, providing a simple binomial/multinomial probability bound (e.g., $P < 10^{-25}$ that $k$ random somatic substitutions in a $16.5\text{-kb}$ genome hit the exact $k$ branch-defining sites of an existing human haplogroup) would make the statistical refutation self-contained for clinical readers.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Claim 1: Arrays of homoplasmic or heteroplasmic "somatic" mtDNA mutations reported in numerous high-profile cancer studies are actually laboratory sample mix-ups or cross-contaminations between distinct human mtDNA haplogroups `[Well Supported]`.** Figure 1 and the case-by-case reconstructions leave no doubt that these mutation sets trace pre-existing human genealogical branches rather than somatic tumor evolution.
* **Claim 2: Case-control mtDNA association studies that count polymorphisms relative to rCRS without haplogroup matching confound population structure and phylogenetic linkage with disease predisposition `[Well Supported]`.** Directly demonstrated by showing that purported thyroid and prostate cancer risk alleles (`A10398G`, `G7521A`, `T6253C`) are high-frequency continent- or haplogroup-defining polymorphisms.
* **Claim 3: Over 80% of inspectable cancer mtDNA studies are based on flawed data `[Moderately Supported]`.** Plausible given the pervasive errors demonstrated across the major cited papers in the field, but lacks a formal systematic census table showing the exact list of all inspected studies.

* **Exact Position-by-Position Audit Trail:** Every critiqued study, patient ID, nucleotide position, reference database match (Herrnstadt et al., Ingman et al., Kong et al., Coble et al., SWGDAM IDs such as `USA.CAU.001306`, `CHN.ASN.000113`, and GenBank `AY495278.1`), and phylogenetic branch assignment is explicitly documented in the text and mapped onto Figure 1, allowing any reader to verify the haplogroup assignments immediately.
