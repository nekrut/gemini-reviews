### 1. Executive Summary
This viral surveillance and characterization study reports the isolation and whole-genome sequencing of two low-pathogenicity avian influenza A (H1N1) strains—`A/Greater white-fronted goose/South Korea/KNU18-64(H1N1)` and `A/wild bird/South Korea/WKU19-4(H1N1)`—from 1,800 wild bird fecal samples collected in South Korea. Using BLAST identity comparisons, MEGA 6.0 maximum-likelihood gene trees, MDCK replication kinetics, and intranasal challenge of BALB/c mice ($10^4\text{--}10^6\text{ EID}_{50}$) alongside pandemic `A/California/04/09(H1N1)` as a positive control, the authors infer multi-step Eurasian and Alaskan–American reassortment histories and evaluate mammalian replication and lung histopathology. While the primary surveillance sequences (deposited in GenBank) and basic virological phenotyping in MDCK cells and mice are useful, the manuscript is undermined by blatant self-contradictions regarding which isolate is more virulent and how specific amino acid substitutions (`PB1-F2 N66S`, `PA N321I`, `PA F277S`/`F227S`, `PA K328R`) relate to the in vitro and in vivo phenotypes, as well as ad hoc narrative reassortment reconstructions lacking formal phylogeographic or molecular clock analysis.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Direct Self-Contradictions on Virulence, Growth Kinetics, and Mutational Markers:**
  - *MDCK Growth Kinetics Contradiction:* In the Abstract (line 7) and Section 3.4 (line 127), the authors state that `WKU19-4` showed *"a dramatic increase in the viral titer after 12 h post-inoculation compared with that in the control group H1N1 (CA/04/09(pdm09))"* and that `KNU18-64` *"has similar pattern with A/California/04/09."* Yet in the immediately preceding sentence of Section 3.4 (line 127), they write the exact opposite: *"The control H1N1 strain replicated more efficiently in MDCK cells than KNU18-64 or WKU19-4 (Figure 4)."* Furthermore, in the Discussion (line 151), they conflate the two strain names into a nonexistent chimera (`WKU19-64`).
  - *In Vivo Mouse Virulence vs. Mutational Prediction Flip-Flop:* In Section 3.3 (line 120), the authors argue that because `WKU19-4` carries `PB1-F2 N66S` while `KNU18-64` carries a `PA` mutation at position 328, this implicates *"the higher virulence of WKU19-4 over KNU18-64."* However, in the mouse challenge experiment (Section 3.5, line 136), **neither** avian isolate caused significant weight loss or mortality, and **`KNU18-64` replicated to higher lung titers than `WKU19-4`** ($2.71 \pm 0.16$ vs. $0\text{ log}_{10}\text{ TCID}_{50}/\text{mL}$ at 3 dpi; $3.71 \pm 0.25$ vs. $2.75 \pm 0.51\text{ log}_{10}\text{ TCID}_{50}/\text{mL}$ at 6 dpi). Then, in the Discussion (line 153), without acknowledging their opposite prediction in Section 3.3, the authors reverse their narrative and claim: *"The KNU18-64 isolate appeared more pathogenic in mice... This result appears to corroborate the results of the molecular characterization section; the KNU18-64 isolate carries two individual mutations in the PA protein (phenylalanine is substituted with serine at position 227, and lysine is substituted with arginine at position 328), which lead to increased virulence in and the adaptation to mammals."*
  - *Contradiction on PA F277S / F227S Presence:* In Section 3.3 (line 122), the authors explicitly state that `PA-F277S` and `PA-K356R` *"were not found in both isolates, supporting their still low pathogenic traits,"* yet in the Discussion (line 153) they claim `KNU18-64` carries a phenylalanine-to-serine substitution in PA (`position 227` [or 277]) that explains its higher mammalian virulence!
- **Ad Hoc Reassortment and Ancestral Transmission Narrative (Figure 3):** In Section 3.2, the authors propose specific year-by-year, strain-by-strain donor–recipient reassortment chains (e.g., claiming `A/duck/Mongolia/520/[YEAR](H1N1)` donated HA and NA to `A/hooded crane/Korea/1176/[YEAR](H1N1)` during a specific migration season) based solely on top BLAST percent identities ($98.07\%\text{--}99.64\%$) and uncalibrated MEGA 6.0 ML trees (substitution model not even specified). Identifying the closest sampled sequence in GenBank/GISAID does not establish direct ancestor–descendant transmission or exact reassortment dates without time-calibrated coalescent phylogenetics (e.g., BEAST) and formal reassortment testing.
- **Inconsistent Statements on NA Closest Relatives:** In Section 3.1 (line 102), the authors state that the `WKU19-4` NA gene *"most closely resembles that of the avian H1N1 subtype isolates obtained from Mongolia,"* whereas in the Discussion (line 147) they state that the `WKU19-4` NA gene *"closely resembles that of a Bangladesh strain,"* and in line 149 they state that American H6N1 strains appear closest to `KNU18-64` NA (after stating in line 102 and 147 that Korean H6N1 is closest).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *"Two avian H1N1 isolates (KNU18-64 and WKU19-4) were isolated from wild bird feces in Korea, harboring low-pathogenicity HA cleavage motifs (PSIQRS↓GLF) and segments related to Eurasian and North American (Alaskan) avian lineages."* — `[Well Supported]`. Whole-genome sequences (`MN584878`–`MN584885` and `MT821115`–`MT821122`) and segment phylogenies support inter-flyway reassortment involving an Alaskan-lineage MP segment in `KNU18-64`.
- **Claim 2:** *"The mutations in the PB1-F2 and PA protein of WKU19-4(H1N1) indicate increasing polymerase activity [and] KNU18-64(H1N1) isolate carries mutations indicating an increase in mammal adaptation; this characterization was confirmed by the animal study in mice."* — `[Overstated]` / `[Methodologically Flawed]`. No minigenome polymerase assay or reverse-genetics mutagenesis was performed, and the authors' post-hoc rationalizations of which mutations explain which phenotype directly contradict each other between Sections 3.3, 3.4, and 4.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- GenBank accession numbers for all 16 segments of both isolates (`MN584878`–`MN584885`, `MT821115`–`MT821122`), GPS collection coordinates, and ethics approval (`WKU19-64`) are provided.
- However, the specific nucleotide substitution model used in MEGA 6.0 is omitted, and the multiple internal contradictions in text descriptions of mutations and growth kinetics severely compromise clarity.
