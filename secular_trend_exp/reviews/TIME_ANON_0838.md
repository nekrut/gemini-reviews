### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This computational biology and molecular evolution study (`TIME_ANON_0838`) introduces `PSPE` (Phylogenetic Simulation of Promoter Evolution), a forward-time sequence simulator built upon `Dawg` that incorporates nucleotide substitution models (`JC`, `F81`, `K80`, `HKY`, `TrN`, `GTR` with $\Gamma+\text{I}$ rate heterogeneity), geometric/negative-binomial/Zipf indel processes, 3rd-order Markov promoter background sequences trained on ~25,000 human RefSeq promoters, and explicit JASPAR position weight matrix (PWM) functional constraints (score thresholds, translocation distance windows $W$, and module spacing constraints). Using `PSPE`, the authors quantify the replacement turnover rate (RTR)—where an ancestral transcription factor binding site (TFBS) is lost only after a functionally equivalent site arises nearby—for E2F, Myc, and NF$\kappa$B across divergence distances ($d = 0.01$ to $5.0$ substitutions/site), indel rates ($0$ to $0.2$ indels/substitution), and translocation windows ($W = 0$ to $300\text{ bp}$), fitting analytical Poisson turnover models. They then benchmark five widely used multiple sequence alignment (MSA) tools (`CLUSTALW`, `DIALIGN`, `AVID/MAVID`, `LAGAN/MLAGAN`, and `MUSCLE`) on 1,000 simulated 5-species mammalian promoter sets ($3,000\text{ bp}$) across 8 tree-scaling factors ($0.25\times$ to $5\times$), evaluating both ancestral nucleotide alignment sensitivity and functional TFBS alignment accuracy. This is a thoughtful, rigorously designed simulation and benchmarking paper with only minor modeling limitations.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Non-Mechanistic Rejection Sampling vs Population-Genetic Fixation Probabilities:** As described in Figure 1, `PSPE` simulates neutral mutations along a branch of small length $\Delta d$ (or a single substitution/indel step) and checks whether all user-specified functional constraints (e.g., PWM score $\ge 0.92$ within window $W$) are satisfied; if not, it discards the mutated sequence and regenerates from the parent (`rejection sampling` up to a maximum retry count). While computationally convenient, uniform rejection sampling over macroevolutionary branch chunks ($\Delta d$) does not correspond to a Wright-Fisher or Haldane-Kimura population-genetic fixation process ($2N_e s / (1 - e^{-2N_e s})$), and the inferred replacement turnover rate depends on the step size $\Delta d$ over which a compensatory site and a deleterious mutation in the original site are allowed to co-occur before constraint checking. The authors do not explicitly specify the discretization step size $\Delta d$ used in the rejection loop or test sensitivity of RTR to $\Delta d$.
- **Minor Discrepancy in Human RefSeq Training Set Size Between Methods Sections:** On line 291 (`Background model of ancestral sequences`), the authors state that the 3rd-order Markov model was trained on `25,088` human promoter sequences of length `500 bp` from NCBI RefSeq build 35, whereas on line 359 (`Simulation of alignment benchmark data`) they state it was trained on `24,649` human promoter sequences of length `5,000 bp` from NCBI RefSeq build 35. While this reflects the two different simulation lengths (`500 bp` for single-site RTR vs `3,000 bp` for MSA benchmarking), the reason for the two separate training extractions could be stated more clearly.
- **Minor Typo in Equation 314 (GTR Rate Matrix $Q$):** In line 314, the third row of $Q$ writes $\mu_{gg}\pi_a$ instead of $\mu_{ga}\pi_a$, and the fourth row writes $\mu_{tt}\pi_a$ and $\mu_{tg}\pi_t$ instead of $\mu_{ta}\pi_a$ and $\mu_{tg}\pi_g$, and line 316 omits the minus sign on the diagonal $q_{ii} = -\sum_{j\neq i} \mu_{ij}\pi_j$. These are clearly typesetting typos in the manuscript text rather than code bugs (since `PSPE` adapts `Dawg`'s rate matrix engine).
- **Non-Default `MUSCLE` Flags:** In the MSA benchmark (line 354), `MUSCLE` (v3.6) was run with `-diags -maxiters 2` for speed; even under these fast settings, `MUSCLE` outperformed the progressive aligners (`CLUSTALW`, `MAVID`, `MLAGAN`), so this does not bias the conclusions against `MUSCLE`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality / STRENGTHS
- **Distinction Between Sequence Homology (`TFBS Sensitivity`) and Functional Homology (`TFBS Detection Accuracy`) `[Well Supported]`:** Equations 370–396 cleanly separate whether an aligner aligns nucleotides descending from the same ancestral bases (`TFBS sensitivity`, Benchmark `SimuALN`) versus whether it aligns functionally equivalent TFBSs after replacement turnover (`TFBS detection accuracy`). Showing that `MUSCLE` can occasionally achieve higher `TFBS detection accuracy` than `SimuALN` (by aligning turned-over sites that share sequence similarity at the motif level even when not orthologous at the nucleotide level) is an insightful observation.
- **Analytical Poisson Approximation of Turnover Kinetics `[Well Supported]`:** Fitting $y = \alpha(1 - e^{-\beta x})$ across divergence distances ($x = d$), translocation windows ($W$), and indel rates ($r_{\text{indel}}$) with 1,000 replicates per point (Tables 1–4; Figures 4–8) demonstrates high statistical precision and reveals how multi-site modules (`E2F + Myc + NFκB`) deviate from independent single-site Poisson kinetics due to steric/window exclusion.
- **Demonstration That Adding Divergent Species Can Degrade Progressive Non-Coding Alignments `[Well Supported]`:** Figure 12 provides a compelling demonstration that adding mouse, dog, and cow to a human–baboon promoter alignment markedly degrades TFBS alignment accuracy in strictly progressive aligners (`MAVID`, `CLUSTALW`, `MLAGAN`) due to early gap-placement errors frozen by the "once a gap, always a gap" rule, whereas iterative refinement (`MUSCLE`) and segment-based local anchoring (`DIALIGN`) preserve accuracy.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **High Reproducibility:** All simulation parameters (Table 5), JASPAR motif cutoffs and window constraints (Table 6), substitution/indel parameters, tool versions (`CLUSTALW v1.83`, `DIALIGN v2.2.1`, `AVID v2.1 / MAVID v2.0.4`, `LAGAN/MLAGAN v1.21`, `MUSCLE v3.6`), and exact mathematical definitions of all five evaluation metrics are reported, and `PSPE` source code was made publicly available.
