### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript performs an *in silico* sequence re-analysis of publicly available GenBank VP1 coding sequences of Foot-and-Mouth Disease Virus (FMDV) serotypes A ($n=29$ unique), O ($n=35$ unique), and SAT2 ($n=24$ unique) from Egypt. The authors run ClustalW/MUSCLE alignments, SDT pairwise identity plots, MEGA11 maximum-likelihood trees and substitution matrices, TempEst root-to-tip regressions, BEAST molecular clock analyses, and AlphaFold2 3D structure visualization on a reference UniProt sequence (`P03305`).

While monitoring FMDV VP1 genetic diversity in Egypt is epidemiologically relevant, the computational and evolutionary analyses in this manuscript contain pervasive technical errors, software version contradictions, impossible substitution rate matrices, and a 1,000-fold internal contradiction in the evolutionary rate of serotype SAT2 that directly invalidates the paper's central conclusion.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
* **Direct 1,000-Fold Self-Contradiction on Serotype SAT2 Evolutionary Rate:**
  * In the Abstract, Table 1, and the *Molecular clock analysis* results section, the BEAST substitution rates are reported as **$2.23 \times 10^{-3}$** (serotype A), **$1.85 \times 10^{-3}$** (or $1.84 \times 10^{-3}$ in text, serotype O), and **$4.48 \times 10^{-6}$ substitutions/site/year** (serotype SAT2). Note that $4.48 \times 10^{-6}$ is **~500-fold slower** than serotypes A and O!
  * Conversely, in the *Temporal signal analysis* (TempEst root-to-tip regression, Fig. 8), the slopes are reported as $1.406 \times 10^{-3}$ (A), $1.239 \times 10^{-3}$ (O), and **$5.2704 \times 10^{-3}$ subs/site/year** (SAT2)—where SAT2 is the fastest.
  * Then, in the Discussion and Conclusion, the authors write: *"This study showed that serotype SAT2 has the highest evolutionary variability per year, followed by serotype A and then serotype O"*, completely contradicting their own BEAST estimate ($4.48 \times 10^{-6}$) highlighted in the Abstract and Table 1! (Moreover, in the previous section they simultaneously write: *"Serotype SAT2 was revealed as the most stable, other than serotypes A and O"*).
* **Misreporting the Fixed Empirical JTT Matrix as a Dataset-Specific Maximum Likelihood Estimate (Table 2):**
  * In Section 3.4.1 (*Maximum likelihood estimate of substitution matrix*, Table 2), the authors present a $20 \times 20$ amino acid rate matrix and amino acid frequencies (7.69% A, 5.11% R, etc.) and write: *"This result was the same in the three serotypes (Table 2)."*
  * It is identical across all three serotypes because **MEGA11 simply outputs the hardcoded, pre-computed empirical Jones–Taylor–Thornton (1992) matrix** when the user selects the JTT model! The authors mistook the fixed empirical JTT reference matrix for a newly estimated ML parameter matrix of their Egyptian FMDV sequences.
* **Degenerate / Corrupted Nucleotide Substitution Matrix for Serotype O (Table 4) and Model Contradictions:**
  * In Section 3.4.2, the authors state that under the Tamura–Nei model, *"Each of the nucleotide frequencies is equal to 25.00%."* By definition, the Tamura–Nei (TN93) model uses empirical (unequal) base frequencies (a model with equal base frequencies and two transition rates is Kimura 3-parameter / Tamura 1992, not TN93).
  * Immediately afterward, in Section 3.4.3 (Table 4, Serotype O), the authors report the MCL substitution matrix for Serotype O where the **$\text{A} \leftrightarrow \text{G}$ purine transition rate is $0$** (`A->G = 0`, `G->A = 0`) while the $\text{T} \rightarrow \text{C}$ transition rate is **58.28** and $\text{C} \rightarrow \text{T}$ is **37.51**! For an RNA virus (where $\text{A} \leftrightarrow \text{G}$ transitions are ubiquitous), an estimated $\text{A} \leftrightarrow \text{G}$ transition rate of exactly 0.00 indicates either a severe alignment corruption (e.g., frame-shifted or unaligned sequences) or numerical optimization failure in MEGA11.
* **Alignment Length Anomaly in Serotype SAT2 (Fig. 7):**
  * FMDV VP1 is ~633–648 nucleotides (~211–216 amino acids) long. In Figs. 5 and 6, the nucleotide alignment lengths are 770 and 710 positions, respectively. However, in Fig. 7 (Serotype SAT2), the caption states: *"The final dataset comprised a total of 1279 positions"*—nearly double the length of the VP1 gene! This indicates either inclusion of flanking capsid genes (VP3/2A) without trimming or a major frameshift/gap inflation artifact in the alignment.
* **Contradictory Software Versions and Misapplied AlphaFold2 Modeling:**
  * The Abstract states that time-calibrated phylogenetics were conducted using **BEAST v2.6**, whereas the Methods (p. 69) and Results (p. 6) state **BEAST.X (v10.5.0-beta5)** (the BEAST 1.10 / BEAST X branch, which is a completely different software architecture from BEAST 2.6).
  * In *Materials and methods*, the authors state that they retrieved a single reference protein from UniProtKB (`P03305`, which is a historical European FMDV O1 strain polyprotein) to predict the 3D structure in AlphaFold2, rather than modeling the actual Egyptian serotype A, O, and SAT2 field strain sequences containing the mutations of interest.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
* **Phylogenetic Clustering of Egyptian FMDV Serotypes A, O, and SAT2 into Known Topotypes: [Moderately Supported].** Grouping Egyptian VP1 sequences from GenBank into known regional topotypes (A: Asia, Africa, Euro-SA; O: EA-3, ME-SA, Euro-SA; SAT2: VII) is straightforward and generally consistent with prior WRLFMD surveillance reports, though the SAT2 alignment length (1,279 bp) requires verification.
* **Bayesian Molecular Clock Evolutionary Rate Estimates ($4.48 \times 10^{-6}$ subs/site/year for SAT2): [Methodologically Flawed].** A substitution rate of $4.48 \times 10^{-6}\text{ subs/site/year}$ for FMDV VP1 is biologically implausible (3 orders of magnitude below standard picornavirus VP1 substitution rates of $10^{-3}\text{--}10^{-2}\text{ subs/site/year}$), directly contradicts the authors' own root-to-tip slope ($5.27 \times 10^{-3}\text{ subs/site/year}$), and is contradicted by the authors' own Discussion claiming SAT2 evolves the fastest.
* **Discovery of "Conserved Patterns PWG and SWR" Replacing the RGD Integrin-Binding Motif in SAT2 (`MH732984` and `MZ097482`) and `G136S` in Serotype A (`MG552839`): [Fragile].** In Table 1 and p. 88, the authors claim that the strictly essential integrin-binding `RGD` tripeptide at positions 144–146 of SAT2 is replaced by `"PWG"` and `"SWR"` in two GenBank sequences. Because `PWG` and `SWR` share zero amino acids with `RGD` (and `PWG`/`SWR` can arise from a $-1$ or $+1$ nucleotide frameshift or misalignment in the hypervariable G-H loop), the authors fail to verify whether these GenBank submissions contain sequencing indels/frameshifts or alignment register shifts rather than viable biological receptor-binding motifs.
* **Structural Stability Claims Based on Cysteine Counts across Serotypes: [Overstated].** The authors repeatedly assert that Serotype SAT2 and O are structurally "more stable" than Serotype A simply by counting cysteine residues (4 in SAT2, 2 in O, 1 in A) in a 1D sequence alignment without checking whether those cysteines are spatially proximal in the 3D capsid structure to form intramolecular or inter-protomer disulfide bonds.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Strengths:** GenBank accession numbers are visible in the text/figures for several discussed isolates (`MG552839`, `ON569816`, `MH732984`, `MZ097482`, `KF112932`, etc.).
* **Deficiencies:**
  1. BEAST XML input files, tree files, and trimmed sequence alignments are not deposited.
  2. No 95% Highest Posterior Density (HPD) credible intervals are reported for any of the BEAST clock rates or TMRCA estimates.
  3. The manuscript contains numerous typographical errors (e.g., *"with approximately 0.5–1.0.5.0% of the genome"*, *"displayed as the ligand described"* instead of legend, *"Maximum Composite Likelihood Estimate of the stitution of Serotype APattern of Nucleotide Sub"*).
