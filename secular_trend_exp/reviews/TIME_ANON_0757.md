### 1. Executive Summary / SUMMARY
This manuscript is a narrative literature review summarizing the applications of reverse genetics (infectious cDNA clones driven by bacteriophage T7/T3/SP6 promoters for in vitro transcription or CaMV 35S promoters for in planta agroinfiltration/biolistics) in the plant virus genus *Potyvirus* (*Potyviridae*). The review catalogs published mutagenesis, chimera, and vector-engineering studies across four thematic tables: (1) point mutations and deletions affecting biological properties and cross-protection (Table 1), (2) mapping of viral genetic determinants for symptom severity, seed transmission, movement, host range, and resistance-breaking (Table 2), (3) reporter gene tagging (`GUS`, `GFP`, `mCherry`, `Rosea1`, `His-tag`) for tracking infection (Table 3), and (4) expression of biologically active heterologous polypeptides in planta (Table 4). While the four summary tables compile a useful reference catalog of ~100 primary potyvirus papers, the manuscript contains no original data, no systematic search methodology or meta-analysis, numerous factual and typographical errors in the text and tables (e.g., confusing TVMV with TuMV, misstating 5' vs. 3' UTR stem-loops, and conflating deletions with point mutations), and superficial synthesis that reads largely as an uncritical list of individual paper abstracts.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit / STRENGTHS & WEAKNESSES

#### STRENGTHS
- **Structured Tabular Compilation of Classical Potyvirus Reverse Genetics:** Tables 1–4 organize a wide array of potyviral species (*PVY*, *TuMV*, *TEV*, *PPV*, *ClYVV*, *PVA*, *SMV*, *PRSV*, *ZYMV*, *PSbMV*, *LMV*, *PepMoV*, *TVBMV*, *PLDMV*) by specific genome manipulation, target cistron (`P1`, `HC-Pro`, `P3`, `6K1`, `CI`, `6K2`, `VPg`, `NIa-Pro`, `NIb`, `CP`, `5'/3' NCR`), and phenotypic outcome.

#### WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT
- **Factual & Internal Contradictions in Text and Tables:**
  1. **TVMV vs. TuMV Confusion (Table 1 & Table 2):** In Table 1 row 1, the virus is listed as *"Tobacco vein mottling virus TVMV-WT"* with amino acid substitutions at Lys307, Thr-Ser283/284, and Thr-Ala368/369 in HC-Pro, but the Findings column states: *"TuMV-307, TuMV-283/284 and TuMV-368/369 mutants-infected plants had no symptoms..."*—erroneously replacing `TVMV` with `TuMV` (*Turnip mosaic virus*). Conversely, in Table 2 row 1, the virus is *"Turnip mosaic virus TuMV"* (`pXBS7` / `pXBS8`), yet the Findings column states: *"pXBS7 induced symptoms in infected tobacco plants that are indistinguishable from those produced by native TVMV RNA"*—again confusing `TuMV` and `TVMV`!
  2. **5' vs. 3' UTR Stem-Loop Contradiction (Section 2.3.2):** In Section 2.3.2 ("Structural Importance"), the authors write that nucleotides 8–42 in the **3' UTR** of `TVBMV-HN39` were deleted, and that mutants lacking positions 1–20 and 15–35 within the **3' UTR** *"were found to have restored the 5'-end SL like structures... Hence, the 5'-terminal stem loop was proposed to be necessary"*. This muddles the 5'-end of the 3'-UTR with the 5'-terminal UTR of the viral genome.
  3. **Categorization Errors (Deletions & Chimeras Listed Under "Point Mutation"):** Section 2.1 is titled *"Point Mutation"* and defined as *"a single base pair alteration in the DNA sequence"*, yet both the text of Section 2.1.1 and Table 1 extensively list large multi-hundred-nucleotide deletions (e.g., `TEV-2del` losing 207 nt of HC-Pro, `PPV` lacking nt 39–145 in the 5' NCR, `pClYVVdel` 3' NCR deletions, `pSAHPdelF1` deleting almost the entire HC-Pro ORF) and whole-gene replacements (`PVA B11-U CP` replacing the entire CP gene).
- **No Systematic Review Methodology or Comparative Quantitative Synthesis:** As a secondary literature review, there is no description of literature search databases, inclusion criteria, or temporal coverage. More importantly, the manuscript offers no mechanistic synthesis of *why* certain insertion sites (`P1/HC-Pro` vs. `NIb/CP` vs. `P3N-PIPO` constraints) or vector architectures succeed or fail (e.g., genome size packaging limits, proteolytic cleavage site context, or RNA recombination instability rates across serial passages).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Reverse genetics via infectious cDNA clones has enabled mapping of potyviral determinants for aphid transmission (CP DAG motif and HC-Pro KITC/PTK/FRNK motifs), recessive eIF4E/VPg resistance breaking, and symptom development.* `[Well Supported]` — Accurately reflects decades of primary experimental plant virology literature.
- **Claim 2:** *Potyviruses are less restrictive to large foreign gene inserts due to their flexuous rod morphology and can stably express multiple foreign proteins.* `[Moderately Supported]` — While filamentous virions lack the rigid icosahedral capsid volume limit of isometric viruses, potyviral vectors are well known to suffer from high homologous and non-homologous RNA recombination that deletes foreign inserts over serial passages (as seen in the `uidA` loss at 15 dpt in Table 3 row 2), a limitation that is under-discussed in the text.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Citation & Table Traceability:** All entries in Tables 1–4 cite specific primary references (`[1]`–`[106]`), allowing readers to trace the underlying experimental studies, though multiple grammatical errors and OCR/transcription typos (`TuMV` vs `TVMV`, `VP06` vs `VP60` in Table 4 row 2) reduce the reliability of the tables as a standalone reference.
