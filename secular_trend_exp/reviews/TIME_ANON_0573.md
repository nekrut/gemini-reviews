### 1. Executive Summary (SUMMARY)
This narrative and scoping review compiles in vitro, animal, and clinical literature on the putative antiviral activities of nine oral disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) and immunosuppressants—chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine (CQ/HCQ), baricitinib, cyclosporine A (CsA), mycophenolate mofetil/mycophenolic acid (MMF/MPA), thalidomide, leflunomide, tofacitinib, hydroxyurea, and minocycline—against SARS-CoV-2 (Part I) and other RNA/DNA viruses including HIV, HCV, HBV, HSV, CMV, BKV, JEV, and dengue (Part II). While the review provides a broad catalog of proposed biochemical mechanisms (Table 3) and clinical/preclinical citations (Table 4), its clinical recommendations (Table 2) rest on uncritical synthesis of single-patient case reports, small open-label non-randomized cohorts, and high-concentration Vero E6 in vitro assays without formal risk-of-bias assessment or pharmacokinetic feasibility analysis.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit (WEAKNESSES & METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT)
* **Absence of Formal Risk-of-Bias Assessment or Evidence Grading:** Although the authors include a PubMed search query up to 13 May `[YEAR]` and an article selection flowchart (Fig. 1), they apply no standardized methodological quality or risk-of-bias tool (e.g., Cochrane RoB 2, ROBINS-I, or GRADE). Consequently, Table 1 and Table 2 treat an $n=1$ case report of thalidomide in COVID-19 (Ref 15), an $n=4$ uncontrolled case series of baricitinib (Ref 13), and heavily confounded open-label HCQ cohorts (Refs 7, 9) as positive clinical evidence (`•` or `✓`) alongside randomized trials.
* **Failure to Evaluate Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic ($C_{\max}$ vs. $\text{EC}_{50}$) Feasibility:** Throughout Parts I and II, in vitro antiviral activity in Vero E6 or microglial cultures is cited as evidence of clinical therapeutic potential without comparing in vitro $\text{EC}_{50}$ values against unbound plasma or tissue drug concentrations achievable at safe human dosing.
* **Contradictory Clinical Recommendations Ignoring Severe Toxicity and Mortality Signals:** In Table 2, the authors recommend MMF/MPA (`• In vitro`) and thalidomide (`• Case report`) as treatment considerations for SARS-CoV-2, and hydroxyurea (`•`) and MMF (`•`) for HIV. Yet in the text, they acknowledge that MMF caused 100% severe and fatal disease with higher viral loads in MERS-CoV-infected marmosets (Ref 96), that hydroxyurea combined with didanosine causes bone marrow suppression and CD4+ T-cell depletion in HIV patients (Refs 65–69), and that minocycline worsened encephalomyelitis and increased mortality in rabies-infected animals (Ref 92). Framing broad-spectrum cytotoxic/immunosuppressive agents as beneficial dual-purpose antivirals on the basis of cell-culture nucleotide depletion (IMPDH/DHODH/dNTP inhibition) conflates host-cell cytostasis with selective antiviral efficacy.
* **Editing Errors in Text:** The Introduction contains a broken, unedited sentence fragment: *"For other viruses, including We also conducted a two-step research as follows: common ones in daily practice and those without specific target therapy, but life threatening, evidence is covered in part II"* (line 16), and the Cyclosporine A section states *"organ transplants to prevent injection"* instead of *"rejection"* (line 76).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality (STRENGTHS)
* **Mechanistic Summary of Host-Targeted Cyclophilin and Nucleotide Synthesis Inhibition: `[Moderately Supported]`**  
  The review accurately summarizes established biochemical mechanisms whereby CsA binds host cyclophilins (blocking HCV NS5A/B replication complex assembly and HIV Gag incorporation) and leflunomide/MMF/hydroxyurea deplete intracellular nucleotide pools (DHODH, IMPDH, ribonucleotide reductase) in vitro.
* **Clinical Efficacy of Hydroxychloroquine, Thalidomide, and MMF in COVID-19: `[Overstated]`**  
  Presenting HCQ + azithromycin open-label series (Refs 7, 9), a single patient given thalidomide (Ref 15), and Vero E6 MMF assays (Ref 28) as actionable clinical evidence for COVID-19 treatment overstates flawed, underpowered, or strictly preclinical observations.
* **Clinical Selection Matrix for DMARDs in Concomitant Viral Infections (Table 2): `[Methodologically Flawed]`**  
  Table 2 advises clinicians on selecting DMARDs during active viral infections using symbols (`•`, `△`) that mix human RCTs with single case reports (`n=1`) and cell-culture studies (`In vitro`), which is clinically unsafe without rigorous evidence stratification.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
* **Search Query Documentation:** The boolean PubMed search string and search cutoff date (13 May `[YEAR]`) are stated in the Methods section.
* **Incomplete Screening Transparency:** The exact number of records retrieved, screened, and excluded at each stage of Figure 1 is not stated in the text, and no supplementary table of excluded studies or quality scores is provided.
