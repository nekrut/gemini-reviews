### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates circadian regulation of the herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) receptor NECTIN-1 in neuronal cells. Re-analyzing public mouse circadian microarray data (GSE54652) from brainstem and hippocampus with JTK_CYCLE, the authors identify *Nectin-1* among 559 overlapping rhythmically expressed genes. In serum-shocked human neuroblastoma SH-SY5Y cells, *NECTIN-1* mRNA oscillates in phase with *BMAL1* and correlates with HSV-1 viral DNA load when cells are infected at 4-hour intervals post-synchronization. Overexpression of BMAL1 and CLOCK increases *NECTIN-1* mRNA/protein and HSV-1 DNA copy number, and luciferase reporter and ChIP-qPCR assays indicate that BMAL1 and CLOCK bind an E-box-like motif in the *NECTIN-1* promoter. Finally, the CLOCK inhibitor CLK8 reduces NECTIN-1 protein, viral plaque formation in Vero cells, and HSV-1 DNA/protein (ICP0, ICP5) levels in SH-SY5Y cells. While the core transcriptional link between BMAL1/CLOCK and *NECTIN-1* is plausible, the experimental design conflates viral entry with 12–24 h replication, lacks cytotoxicity controls for CLK8, and contains several methodological contradictions.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
- **Conflation of Viral Entry with Viral Replication (`[Technical/Assay]`):** Throughout the manuscript (Fig. 2C–D, Fig. 3C, Fig. 5C), the authors claim to measure "HSV-1 entry" by quantifying viral DNA copy numbers at **12 h or 24 h post-infection** (noting a direct contradiction between Methods line 46, which states "viral DNA was extracted at 24 h post-infection," and the Fig. 2C–D legends, which state "12 h post-infection"). Because the HSV-1 lytic replication cycle initiates DNA synthesis within 3–6 hours and completes a full replication cycle by 12–18 hours, measuring viral DNA at 12–24 h post-infection in culture supernatant/lysate measures cumulative viral replication and egress, not viral entry (which requires a 30–120 min attachment/penetration assay or $\beta$-lactamase/luciferase entry reporter prior to viral DNA replication).
- **Viral DNA Extraction Protocol Error (`[Technical/Assay]`):** In Materials and Methods (line 51), the authors state that viral DNA was extracted from "200 $\mu$L of supernatant... transferred to FastPure® RNA columns" to measure "HSV-1 entry." Measuring extracellular supernatant DNA at 12–24 h post-infection reflects progeny virion release, not cellular entry; furthermore, if total cellular DNA was actually used (as suggested by line 58 "Total DNA from the samples"), the Methods section is internally inconsistent.
- **Missing Cytotoxicity Controls (`[Statistical/Design]`):** In Figure 5, CLK8 is applied at up to $20\ \mu\text{M}$ for 12 h (SH-SY5Y) or 72 h (Vero plaque assay), yielding a 52% reduction in plaques. No cell viability/cytotoxicity assay (CCK-8/MTT/LDH) or $\text{CC}_{50}$ determination is presented to demonstrate that $20\ \mu\text{M}$ CLK8 over 72 h does not simply impair host cell viability or proliferation.
- **Promoter Mutagenesis & ChIP Controls:** In the luciferase reporter assay (Fig. 4A) and ChIP-qPCR (Fig. 4C), the authors identify predicted E-box-like motifs in the *NECTIN-1* promoter (Fig. 4B), but they do not perform site-directed mutagenesis of the E-box motif in the luciferase reporter to prove that activation depends on that specific motif. Moreover, in Fig. 4C, ChIP-qPCR was performed in SH-SY5Y cells *transfected with BMAL1 and CLOCK overexpression plasmids* rather than on endogenous BMAL1/CLOCK, and no negative control genomic locus (off-target region) was tested.
- **Statistical Testing:** In Fig. 3A, 3C, and 4A, multiple groups (e.g., 5 clock genes vs. vector in Fig. 3A; BMAL1, CLOCK, and BMAL1+CLOCK vs. vector in Fig. 3C and 4A) are compared using Student's $t$-tests without multiple-testing correction instead of one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's or Tukey's post-hoc test. Similarly, JTK_CYCLE analysis of GSE54652 uses raw $P < 0.05$ without reporting Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted $q$-values across thousands of genes.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** BMAL1 and CLOCK transcriptionally upregulate *NECTIN-1* in SH-SY5Y cells via promoter binding. `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by RT-qPCR, Western blot, dual-luciferase reporter, and ChIP-qPCR, though lacking E-box point-mutation reporter controls and endogenous ChIP validation.
- **Claim 2:** Circadian oscillations in *NECTIN-1* directly drive synchronized fluctuations in "HSV-1 entry." `[Overstated]` — Because viral DNA was harvested 12–24 h post-infection (and in supernatant), the assay cannot distinguish NECTIN-1-mediated viral entry from downstream circadian effects on intracellular viral transcription and DNA replication (which the authors themselves note involves 9 other rhythmic HSV-1 pathway genes in Fig. 1C, and which conflicts with prior reports that $Bmal1^{-/-}$ enhances HSV-1 replication).
- **Claim 3:** Pharmacological inhibition of CLOCK with CLK8 suppresses HSV-1 infection specifically via NECTIN-1 downregulation. `[Fragile]` — Lacks cytotoxicity controls ($\text{CC}_{50}$) and receptor-rescue experiments (e.g., showing that constitutive NECTIN-1 overexpression rescues HSV-1 from CLK8 inhibition).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- Microarray dataset accession (`GSE54652`), cell line ATCC identifiers (`CCL-81`, `CRL-2266`), antibody catalog numbers (`sc-21722`, `sc-56989`, `sc-53070`), and qPCR/ChIP-qPCR primer sequences (Tables 1 and 2) are clearly provided.
- However, the multiplicity of infection (MOI) used for HSV-1 infections in SH-SY5Y and Vero cells is never stated anywhere in the Materials and Methods or figure legends, and the exact genomic coordinates of the cloned *NECTIN-1* promoter luciferase construct and E-box motifs are omitted from the main text.
