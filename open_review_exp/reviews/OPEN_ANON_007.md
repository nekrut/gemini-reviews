# Blinded Review: OPEN_ANON_007
**Composite Score:** 7.1 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.1/3.5 | Claims: 1.9/2.5 | Biology: 1.6/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.5/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Statistical/Design
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Hybrid Computational + Wet-Lab
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical

### 1. Executive Summary
This prospective 2-year observational cohort study (`COVIH` follow-up) evaluates longitudinal SARS-CoV-2 ancestral spike (S1)-specific IgG binding antibody kinetics (DiaSorin LIAISON TrimericS IgG) and breakthrough infection incidence over 24 months following primary COVID-19 vaccination in 448 adults with HIV (PWH) across three Dutch treatment centers, stratified by baseline $\text{CD4}^+$ T-cell count $< 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$ ($n = 16$, 77 longitudinal samples) versus $\ge 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$ ($n = 432$, 2,065 longitudinal samples). The authors find that PWH with baseline $\text{CD4}^+ < 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$ maintain significantly lower S1-specific IgG concentrations across 24 months ($p < 0.001$), whereas cumulative breakthrough infection rates (~55%) and booster uptake (median 2 boosters) are comparable between groups. While the clinical cohort is valuable and the central conclusion is biologically sound and relevant to vaccination guidelines, a forensic audit of the reported numbers reveals two glaring statistical/reporting errors: (1) the 95% confidence intervals for the mixed-effects model predictions at months 6, 12, 18, and 24 have their standard errors **swapped** between the $n = 16$ and $n = 432$ groups (giving the $n = 16$ group a 5.2-fold tighter log-scale CI than the $n = 432$ group, matching $\sqrt{432/16} = 5.20$), and (2) Table 1 reports a baseline post-vaccination geometric mean of `65` BAU/mL with a 95% CI of `(188–236)`, where the point estimate lies completely outside its own confidence interval.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Swapped Standard Errors / 95% Confidence Intervals Between the $n = 16$ and $n = 432$ Groups in the Linear Mixed-Effects Model (Abstract, Line 21; Results, Line 140):** Look at the predicted S1-specific IgG concentrations (BAU/mL) and 95% CIs reported for the baseline $\text{CD4} < 200$ group ($n = 16$, 77 samples) versus the baseline $\text{CD4} \ge 200$ group ($n = 432$, 2,065 samples):
   - **Month 6:** `168 (95% CI 151–187)` for $\text{CD4} < 200$ ($n=16$) vs. `1504 (95% CI 858–2636)` for $\text{CD4} \ge 200$ ($n=432$).
   - **Month 12:** `514 (95% CI 456–578)` for $\text{CD4} < 200$ ($n=16$) vs. `2758 (95% CI 1488–5110)` for $\text{CD4} \ge 200$ ($n=432$).
   - **Month 18:** `879 (95% CI 784–987)` for $\text{CD4} < 200$ ($n=16$) vs. `3660 (95% CI 2008–6671)` for $\text{CD4} \ge 200$ ($n=432$).
   - **Month 24:** `839 (95% CI 732–959)` for $\text{CD4} < 200$ ($n=16$) vs. `3505 (95% CI 1712–7175)` for $\text{CD4} \ge 200$ ($n=432$).
   On the $\log_{10}$ scale (the scale on which the `nlme` model was fit), the 95% CI width $\log_{10}(\text{Upper}/\text{Lower})$ at Month 12 is $0.1030$ for the $n = 16$ group (`456–578`, i.e., $\pm 12\%$) and $0.5358$ for the $n = 432$ group (`1488–5110`, i.e., $\times/\div 1.85$). The ratio of the log-scale CI widths is:
   $$\frac{\log_{10}(5110 / 1488)}{\log_{10}(578 / 456)} = \frac{0.5358}{0.1030} = 5.20, \quad \text{which equals } \sqrt{\frac{n_{\ge 200}}{n_{< 200}}} = \sqrt{\frac{432}{16}} = \sqrt{27} = 5.196!$$
   The exact same $\approx 5.2\times$ ratio holds at Months 6, 18, and 24. In a linear mixed-effects model with homogeneous error variance, the group with $27\times$ *more* participants ($n = 432$) has a standard error that is $\sqrt{27} = 5.20\times$ *smaller*, not $5.20\times$ *larger*! When computing the marginal predictions and 95% CIs from `nlme`, the authors inverted the group standard errors—assigning the tiny standard error of the $n = 432$ group ($\approx 0.026\text{ log}_{10}\text{ units}$) to the $n = 16$ group and the large standard error of the $n = 16$ group ($\approx 0.137\text{ log}_{10}\text{ units}$) to the $n = 432$ group. This must be corrected in the Abstract, Results, and code.
2. **Impossible Confidence Interval in Table 1 for Post-Primary Vaccination Antibody Levels (Results, Table 1, Line 124):** In the bottom row of Table 1 (*"S1-specific IgG antibodies 4–6 weeks after primary vaccination, BAU per mL"*), the value for the $\text{CD4} < 200$ group ($n = 16$) is reported as **`65 (188–236)`**. A point estimate of `65` cannot lie outside its own 95% CI (`188–236`)—and if `188–236` were an IQR instead of a 95% CI, a median of `65` still cannot lie outside its own 25th–75th percentile (`188–236`). Furthermore, in Table 1 Line 112 (*"Primary series + 2 boosters"*), the subgroup counts are $4$ ($<200$ group) and $136$ ($\ge 200$ group), which sum to $140$, whereas the total column reports $139$.
3. **Retrospective Inclusion of 50% of the Low-CD4 Group Introducing Immortal-Time / Survivorship Bias (Methods, Line 52; Results, Line 80):** Only 8 participants in the original prospective cohort had a baseline $\text{CD4} < 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$; therefore, between October 2023 and April 2024 (after the 2-year follow-up period had already concluded), the authors retrospectively enrolled 16 additional patients with $\text{CD4} < 500$ from Erasmus MC, including 8 with $\text{CD4} < 200$. Because retrospective enrollment in 2023–2024 required patients to be alive and in active clinical follow-up at Erasmus MC 2–3 years after their 2021 primary vaccination with stored biobanked sera available from routine semi-annual HIV visits, these 8 patients (comprising 50% of the $\text{CD4} < 200$ group) are subject to survivorship/retention bias. A sensitivity analysis restricting the mixed-effects model to the prospectively enrolled cohort ($n = 8$ vs. $n = 424$) should be reported in the main text alongside `S1 Fig`.
4. **Conditioning on Baseline Outcome (Month 1 Antibody Level) in the Longitudinal Mixed-Effects Model (Methods, Line 67; Results, Line 151):** In the second mixed-effects model assessing variables associated with antibody kinetics over 1, 6, 12, 18, and 24 months, the authors include *"S1-specific IgG antibody levels measured 4–6 weeks after the primary vaccination series"* (i.e., Month 1) as a fixed-effect covariate while Month 1 is also the first timepoint of the primary outcome vector, and while simultaneously testing baseline $\text{CD4} < 200$, age, and vaccine type (which are the upstream determinants of the Month 1 response). Conditioning on the Month 1 post-vaccination antibody level blocks the direct causal path from baseline $\text{CD4}$ count and primary vaccine platform to overall antibody levels and can induce collider/regression-to-the-mean bias on longitudinal slopes.
5. **Time-Varying CD4 Count Recovery and Quadratic Time Parameterization (Results, Line 80; Fig 2):** Over the 24-month follow-up, 9 of the 16 participants (56.3%) in the baseline $\text{CD4} < 200$ group experienced immune reconstitution to $\text{CD4} \ge 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$, while 3 of 432 in the $\ge 200$ group dropped below $200$. Moreover, antibody levels in both groups *increased* from Month 1 to Month 18 (`65 -> 879` BAU/mL and `1210 -> 3660` BAU/mL) due to repeated mRNA booster doses and Omicron breakthrough infections. The authors should clarify why CD4 count was not additionally evaluated as a time-varying covariate and note that the smooth quadratic curve in Fig 2 averages across discrete boosting and infection events rather than representing intrinsic post-vaccination antibody decay.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *PWH with a baseline $\text{CD4}^+$ T-cell count $< 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$ have persistently lower S1-specific IgG binding antibody responses over two years following primary vaccination compared to PWH with $\text{CD4}^+ \ge 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$.* `[Well Supported]` — Despite the small sample size ($n = 16$) and the swapped CI reporting error that needs correction, the ~4- to 9-fold lower geometric mean S1-IgG concentration across all timepoints in the $< 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$ group is large, biologically consistent with impaired CD4 T-follicular helper activity in advanced HIV, and supported by the longitudinal mixed-model LRT ($p < 0.001$).
- **Claim 2:** *Incidence of self-reported SARS-CoV-2 breakthrough infection (~55%) was comparable between $\text{CD4} < 200$ and $\ge 200$ groups.* `[Moderately Supported]` — Appropriately reported as an exploratory secondary endpoint, though with $n = 16$ in the $< 200$ group (95% CI for infection-free survival: 27.2%–80.2%), the log-rank test has very low statistical power to detect moderate differences in infection risk.
- **Claim 3:** *National COVID-19 vaccine guidelines should specifically emphasize booster vaccinations for PWH with $\text{CD4}^+ < 200\text{ cells/\mu L}$.* `[Moderately Supported]` — Proportionate and clinically sensible recommendation, tempered by the acknowledgement that ancestral S1-binding IgG rather than variant-specific Omicron neutralization or T-cell immunity was measured.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Individual participant data and R (`nlme`) analytic code are available upon request to the Erasmus MC HIV Eradication Group (`eheg@erasmusmc.nl`), which is standard for clinical HIV cohorts.
- **Reporting & Registry Completeness:** The trial registration (`EUCTR2021-001054-57-N`), ethics approval (`MEC-U 20.125`), immunoassay specification (`DiaSorin LIAISON TrimericS IgG`, LLoQ = 4.81 BAU/mL), and missing data denominators are clearly documented, but the numerical errors in Table 1 (`65 (188–236)` and `4 + 136 = 139`) and the swapped 95% CIs in the text must be rectified prior to publication.
