### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents the pharmacological characterization of six ROR$\gamma$t inverse agonists from an acetamide series (Compounds 1–6, including the clinical candidate AZD0284 [Compound 5]), evaluating whether a therapeutic window exists between the desired suppression of peripheral Th17 IL-17A production and the mechanism-based toxicity of thymocyte apoptosis (which leads to thymic lymphomas in rodents). The authors characterize human and murine ROR$\gamma$t LBD radioligand binding, human ROR$\gamma$t SRC-1 co-activator displacement, selectivity against ROR$\alpha$ and ROR$\beta$, IL-17A inhibition in primary human Th17 cells, ex vivo apoptosis of primary murine thymocytes, in vivo thymic involution (depletion of $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD8}^+$ double-positive thymocytes) in mice for Compounds 3 and 4, and in vivo anti-inflammatory efficacy in the imiquimod (IMQ)-induced ear inflammation mouse model for Compound 3.

The experimental assays are classically sound and the Discussion is unusually candid regarding the clinical termination of ROR$\gamma$t inhibitors and the translational hurdles of thymic lymphoma risk. However, the study's central quantitative conclusion—that inducing thymocyte apoptosis requires significantly higher ROR$\gamma$t inhibition than suppressing Th17 IL-17A release—is confounded by cross-species and cross-media assay differences (serum-free human Th17 culture for 96 h vs. 10% FBS murine thymocyte culture for 26 h) without free-drug concentration correction, and is contradicted by the in vivo pharmacokinetic data showing that the efficacious anti-inflammatory dose of Compound 3 produces steady-state blood exposures equal to the high dose that causes severe thymic depletion.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Assay Protein-Binding Confounding in the In Vitro Margin Comparison (Table 1, Fig. 4):** The primary in vitro margin is calculated by comparing nominal compound potencies between primary human Th17 cells ($\text{pIC}_{50} = 7.2\text{--}8.2$) and ex vivo murine thymocytes ($\text{pEC}_{50} = 5.6\text{--}7.5$). However, the human Th17 assay is conducted in serum-free X-Vivo15 medium for 4 days (Line 72), whereas the murine thymocyte apoptosis assay is conducted in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated FBS for 26 hours (Line 79). Because these lipophilic compounds exhibit high plasma/serum protein binding (Table 1 shows mouse plasma unbound fractions of only 0.5% for Compound 1, 5.1% for Compound 6, and 6.2% for Compound 3), the presence of 10% FBS in the thymocyte medium substantially reduces the free drug concentration relative to the serum-free human Th17 medium. While the authors acknowledge protein binding qualitatively for Compound 1 (Line 252) and color-code points by % unbound in FBS in Fig. 4, all potency comparisons in Table 1 and Fig. 1 are reported in nominal total concentrations rather than unbound (free) concentrations.
2. **Absence of a Species-Matched Murine Th17 Assay:** To isolate target biology (Th17 cytokine transcription vs. double-positive thymocyte survival) from species differences and media composition, the compounds should have been tested in a primary *murine* Th17 (or murine $\gamma\delta$ T cell) IL-17A release assay using the identical 10% FBS RPMI-1640 medium used for murine thymocytes. Comparing human Th17 cells directly to mouse thymocytes conflates species-specific cofactor interactions, cellular permeability, and assay duration (96 h cumulative cytokine accumulation vs. 26 h acute apoptosis).
3. **In Vivo Exposure Overlap Between Efficacy and Thymic Toxicity (Lines 225, 235, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, S4 Fig):** In the 48-hour thymus involution study, Compound 3 dosed at 50 mg/kg BID and 150 mg/kg BID caused a dose-dependent, statistically significant loss of $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD8}^+$ double-positive thymocytes (Fig. 2A). In the 7-day IMQ skin inflammation study, Compound 3 was dosed at 50 mg/kg BID, and the authors explicitly note that "due to accumulation, [this] led to an exposure at steady state similar to the high dose group (150 mg/kg) in the thymus involution study (S4 Fig)" (Line 235). Even at this high thymotoxic exposure, ear thickness was only modestly reduced from 0.37 mm to 0.33 mm ($p = 0.04$, Line 237). Thus, in vivo, no therapeutic window was achieved for Compound 3 between anti-inflammatory efficacy and double-positive thymocyte depletion.
4. **Low or Single Replication ($n = 1$) in Table 1:** Several biochemical and selectivity entries in Table 1 are based on $n = 1$ or $n = 2$ independent test occasions (e.g., Compound 6 has $n = 1$ for human ROR$\gamma$t binding, $n = 1$ for murine ROR$\gamma$t binding, $n = 1$ for ROR$\gamma$t inverse agonism, and $n = 2$ for thymocyte apoptosis; Compound 1 has $n = 1$ for ROR$\gamma$t inverse agonism; Compound 2 has $n = 1$ for murine ROR$\gamma$t binding; all mouse PPB values have $n = 1$). Summary potency comparisons across compounds and assays require at least $n \ge 3$ independent biological replicates to estimate variance reliably.
5. **Inconsistent Dosing Regimen and Control Group Design in the Thymus Involution Study (Line 90):** For Compound 4, three dose groups ($n = 10$ each) were compared against a vehicle control group (0.5% HPMC / 0.5% F127). For Compound 3, sample sizes were unbalanced ($n = 8$ at 50 mg/kg; $n = 4$ at 150 mg/kg), the 150 mg/kg group received only 3 doses instead of 4 over the 48-hour period ("got only one dose at 7.30 the final day"), and the control group was dosed with "an inactive compound from the same series" rather than vehicle alone, without specifying the identity, structure, or off-target profile of this "inactive compound" (also used as the control in the IMQ study, Line 97).
6. **Typographical Error in the Cheng-Prusoff Equation (Lines 43, 129):** In Lines 43 and 129, the Cheng-Prusoff equation is printed with a missing opening parenthesis in the denominator: `Ki = IC50 / 1+[L]/Kd)` instead of $K_i = \text{IC}_{50} / (1 + [L]/K_d)$ (which was correctly written on Line 38).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Compounds 1–6 are potent, selective ROR$\gamma$t inverse agonists that inhibit IL-17A production in primary human Th17 cells without cytotoxicity.* `[Well Supported]` — Radioligand displacement, SRC-1 cofactor recruitment, ROR$\alpha$/ROR$\beta$ counter-screens, primary human Th17 IL-17A dose-response curves, and parallel CellTiter-Glo ATP viability assays robustly establish on-target inverse agonism in vitro.
- **Claim 2:** *Oral dosing of Compound 3 reduces IL-17A-producing $\gamma\delta$ T cells and ear swelling in the murine IMQ skin inflammation model.* `[Well Supported]` — Flow cytometric quantification of total $\text{CD45}^+\text{IL-17A}^+$ cells ($p = 0.0002$), $\text{IL-17A}^+$ $\gamma\delta$ T cells ($p = 0.002$), and micrometer ear thickness measurements ($p = 0.04$) support in vivo target engagement and partial anti-inflammatory efficacy.
- **Claim 3:** *Thymocyte apoptosis requires a greater degree of ROR$\gamma$t inhibition than Th17 IL-17A inhibition, indicating a potential biological margin between effector T cell suppression and thymic toxicity.* `[Fragile]` — As detailed in the methodological audit, the apparent 5- to 50-fold potency shift between human Th17 IL-17A inhibition and mouse thymocyte apoptosis is confounded by serum protein binding differences (0% FBS vs. 10% FBS), assay duration (96 h vs. 26 h), and species differences, and was not reproduced as an exposure margin in vivo. The authors appropriately temper this claim in the Discussion (Lines 13–17 of page 2).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Reagent & Protocol Specificity:** Assay buffers, protein construct boundaries (`hRORγ 258–518`, `mRORγ 256–516`, `hRORβ T221–K470`), radioligand identities and concentrations, bead types, plate catalog numbers, flow cytometry antibody clones and catalog numbers, and animal housing/ethics approvals are documented with exceptional industrial thoroughness.
- **Missing Chemical Identity of Control Compound:** While the structures of Compounds 1–6 are provided in S2 Fig and prior medicinal chemistry publications [31–33], the chemical structure and assay data for the "non-active compound from the same series" used as the negative control in the Compound 3 in vivo thymus involution and IMQ studies (Lines 90, 97) are not disclosed and should be added.
