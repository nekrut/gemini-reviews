### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript introduces `SpaMI`, a graph neural network (GCN) autoencoder framework for integrating spatial multi-omics data (spatial transcriptome + proteome or spatial transcriptome + epigenome/ATAC) measured on the same tissue slice. `SpaMI` constructs a $k$-nearest neighbor spatial graph ($r = 3$ or $6$), applies 2-layer modality-specific GCN encoders to both the true spatial graph and a feature-shuffled corrupted graph, optimizes a Deep Graph Infomax (DGI)-style local contrastive loss ($L_{\text{contrast}}$) alongside a cross-modality cosine similarity correspondence loss ($L_{\text{corr}}$) and an L2 feature reconstruction loss ($L_{\text{rec}}$), and fuses modality embeddings via a learned attention layer. The authors benchmark `SpaMI` against `Seurat` v4 (WNN), `MOFA+`, and `SpatialGlue` across simulated datasets (RNA+ADT and RNA+ATAC) and four real spatial multi-omics datasets: mouse embryonic brain E15.5 (MISAR-seq), juvenile mouse brain P22 (spatial ATAC-RNA-seq), mouse thymus (Stereo-CITE-seq), and human lymph node (10x Visium RNA+Protein). While the architectural combination is computationally fast and achieves competitive clustering metrics (ARI, AMI, NMI, Homogeneity, and Moran's I), the manuscript suffers from dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning of five loss weights and learning rates without an unsupervised selection protocol, mathematical notation errors in the Methods equations, incomplete baseline comparisons (omitting `PRESENT` and `spaMultiVAE` discussed in the Introduction), and unvalidated gene-level "denoising" claims derived by projecting low-dimensional PCA/LSI reconstructions.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Dataset-Specific Hyperparameter Tuning on Unsupervised Benchmarks:** `SpaMI` relies on five loss weighting hyperparameters $[\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \lambda_1, \lambda_2, \mu]$ plus the spatial neighbor count $r$, learning rate, and training epochs. In "Implementation details and parameter selection" (line 41 of second chunk), the authors disclose that these were tuned differently for every real dataset:
   - MISAR-seq: $r=3$, weights $[1, 1, 10, 15, 3]$, $\text{lr}=0.01$, $\text{epochs}=800$
   - Spatial ATAC-RNA-seq: $r=6$, weights $[1, 1, 10, 15, 5]$, $\text{lr}=0.001$, $\text{epochs}=400$
   - Stereo-CITE-seq: $r=3$, weights $[1, 5, 10, 25, 1]$, $\text{lr}=0.0001$, $\text{epochs}=600$
   - Simulations: $r=3$, weights $[1, 1, 10, 10, 5]$, $\text{lr}=0.001$, $\text{epochs}=1000$
   Because spatial domain clustering is an unsupervised task where ground-truth manual annotations are unavailable a priori, tuning seven hyperparameters per dataset can overfit the benchmark annotations relative to competing methods run with default parameters. Although the authors mention testing default parameters on one dataset (Spatial ATAC-RNA-seq, Fig G in S1 Text), a fair benchmark requires reporting default hyperparameter performance across *all* datasets and providing a principled unsupervised heuristic for setting $[\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \lambda_1, \lambda_2, \mu]$.
2. **Mathematical and Matrix Dimension Errors in Equations:**
   - **Symmetric Normalized Adjacency Matrix (lines 171–187):** The GCN propagation rule is written as $D^{-1/2} \tilde{A} D^{1/2}$ instead of the standard symmetric normalization $D^{-1/2} \tilde{A} D^{-1/2}$. Note that $D^{-1/2} \tilde{A} D^{1/2}$ is a similarity transform that is asymmetric and amplifies high-degree column nodes rather than normalizing degree variance.
   - **Attention Fusion Equation (lines 198–226):** The authors define $Z_{\text{concat}} = [Z_1 \parallel Z_2] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 2d}$, pass it through $V = \tanh(W_v Z_{\text{concat}} + b_v)$ and $U = W_u V + b_u$ (where left-multiplying $Z_{\text{concat}}$ by $W_v$ is dimensionally transposed unless $Z_{\text{concat}}$ is $2d \times N$), and then write the fused embedding as $Z = Z_{\text{concat}} \times \text{diag}(\alpha)$. If $Z_{\text{concat}}$ is concatenated feature-wise ($N \times 2d$) and $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ is the weighted sum of the two modalities $Z_1, Z_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$, then $Z = \text{diag}(\alpha_1) Z_1 + \text{diag}(\alpha_2) Z_2$, *not* $Z_{\text{concat}} \times \text{diag}(\alpha)$.
   - **Correspondence Loss (line 24 of chunk 2):** Written as $L_{\text{corr}} = \mu \left(1 - \frac{Z_1 \cdot Z_2}{\|Z_1\| \|Z_2\|}\right)$ without specifying whether the cosine similarity is computed row-wise per spot $i$ and averaged over $N$ spots ($\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{z_{1i}^\top z_{2i}}{\|z_{1i}\|_2 \|z_{2i}\|_2}$) or Frobenius inner product over the entire matrix.
3. **Reconstruction in PCA/LSI Space vs. Gene-Level Denoising Claims:** In the Methods (line 129 & line 15 of chunk 2), the input features $x_{1i}$ and $x_{2i}$ fed into the GCN encoder and reconstructed by the decoder ($y_{1i}, y_{2i}$) are 50-dimensional PCA (for RNA) or LSI (for ATAC) vectors (or 21 dimensions for mouse thymus). However, in Figs 3E, 4E, and 6D, the authors plot "reconstructed spatial expression of marker genes" (e.g., *Myh3*, *Ccr7*, *Klf2*, *Rag1*). First, reconstructing a 50-dimensional PCA vector via a 2-layer GCN decoder applies spatial Laplacian smoothing on top of a rank-50 linear projection—which inherently acts as a low-pass spatial filter that forces any gene's spatial map to look smooth and regionally localized, without proving that the smoothed values reflect true biological expression rather than over-smoothing across domain boundaries. Second, the exact inverse projection from the 50D decoder output $Y_1$ back to individual gene space (presumably multiplying by $W_{\text{PCA}}^\top$) is omitted from the Methods.
4. **Incomplete Benchmarking Against Recent Spatial Multi-Omics Methods:** In the Introduction (line 21), the authors discuss `PRESENT` and `spaMultiVAE` alongside `SpatialGlue`, but only benchmark against `Seurat`, `MOFA+` (both non-spatial), and `SpatialGlue`. Including at least one additional spatially aware multi-omics method (or `MultiVI`/`totalVI` as stronger single-cell baselines) and reporting mean $\pm$ SD across multiple random seeds for `SpaMI` and `SpatialGlue` (since GNN initialization and feature corruption are stochastic) are essential to establish statistical significance of the reported ARI/NMI gains.
5. **Caption Error in Fig 6F:** For the mouse thymus Stereo-CITE-seq dataset (RNA + Protein/ADT), the Fig 6F caption (line 97) erroneously states *"from left to right, RNA modality data alone, ATAC modality data alone, and both modality data combined"* when the second modality is ADT (protein), not ATAC.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: `SpaMI` outperforms non-spatial (`Seurat`, `MOFA+`) and single-layer GCN (`SpatialGlue`) baselines in spatial domain identification across RNA+ATAC and RNA+ADT platforms.** `[Moderately Supported]` The visual parcellation of cortical layers in P22 mouse brain and cortical/medullary zones in mouse thymus is compelling, though the quantitative margin over `SpatialGlue` needs multi-seed variance estimates and uniform (non-dataset-tuned) hyperparameters to confirm robustness.
- **Claim 2: `SpaMI` effectively denoises spatial gene expression profiles.** `[Fragile]` The assessment of denoising is purely qualitative (visual inspection of marker heatmaps in Figs 3E, 4E, 6D) with no quantitative holdout/dropout recovery benchmark or guard against spatial over-smoothing.
- **Claim 3: `SpaMI` embeddings enable downstream cis-regulatory link inference via `FGOT` validated by H3K4me1 signals in cerebral cortex layers.** `[Moderately Supported]` The enrichment of layer-specific H3K4me1 ChIP/CUT&Tag signal around FGOT-predicted enhancer-gene links (e.g., at the *Pcp4* locus in Fig 5) provides a nice biological application, though it primarily reflects the quality of the cluster parcellation and the external `FGOT` tool.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Code Availability:** An open-source Python repository is provided, along with clear hardware runtime and memory benchmarks (e.g., $\sim 3$ minutes and $2,200\text{ MB}$ VRAM on an RTX 3090 for 9,215 spots).
- **Data Availability:** While the four real datasets are from published studies (MISAR-seq, Spatial ATAC-RNA-seq, Stereo-CITE-seq, 10x Visium Lymph Node), the manuscript does not list their direct GEO/SRA/Zenodo accession numbers in the Data Availability statement, and the exact Leiden clustering resolution parameters used for each method should be explicitly documented.
