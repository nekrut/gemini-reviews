### 1. Executive Summary
This study adapts and optimizes a fourplex serotype-specific TaqMan qRT-PCR targeting the E gene of dengue virus (DENV-1 to DENV-4) by introducing degenerate bases into the DENV-3 reverse primer (`DEN-3-eDyn`) and probe (`MxDEN-3Pdegenerated`) to rescue detection of contemporary DENV-3 genotype I isolates from New Caledonia, and by pairing the four probes with Atto425, Atto590, FAM, and Cy5 fluorochromes on a Roche LightCycler 480 platform. The authors characterize the analytical performance of the multiplex assay (limits of detection of $1.19\text{--}7.52\text{ copies}/\mu\text{L}$, efficiencies of $89.8\text{--}99.3\%$, $R^2 \ge 0.9985$, inter-assay CVs of $2.17\text{--}6.34\%$), evaluate specificity/sensitivity across 87 clinical sera and 9 non-DENV arbovirus/flavivirus controls, validate proportional quantification in artificial two-serotype viral mixes (`100:0` to `0:100`), and implement an in vitro competition assay in human hepatoma (HuH7) cells comparing clinical isolates of DENV-1 (`AVS94`), DENV-2 (`DWL089`), and DENV-3 (`DWL039`) at `50:50` and `10:90` FFU MOI ratios ($n = 3$ biological replicates). While the molecular assay optimization and competition workflow are practical and clearly presented, the statistical analysis of the competition and mixture assays relies on an inappropriate statistical test (Fisher's exact test applied to continuous qRT-PCR percentage/concentration data), the DENV-2/DENV-3 fluorescence channels suffer from residual spectral crosstalk requiring subjective manual threshold manipulation, and fitness comparisons are based on a single endpoint (72 hpi) using only one viral isolate per serotype.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Misapplication of Fisher's Exact Test to Continuous qRT-PCR Concentrations and Mean Percentages (Lines 190, 243, 256–258):**
   In the Methods (line 190) and Results (lines 243, 256–258), the authors state that *"Fisher's exact test was used for testing the difference between observed and expected proportions in mixes of viral isolates or between the inoculum and the supernatant in competition assays."* Fisher's exact test is strictly valid only for independent discrete counts in a $2 \times 2$ contingency table (e.g., number of individual plaques or sequenced clones), NOT for continuous RT-qPCR genome copy concentrations ($\text{copies}/\mu\text{L}$) or normalized percentages ($97.5\%$ vs. $14.8\%$) averaged across $n = 3$ independent biological replicates! Treating relative percentages (out of 100) or arbitrary $\text{copies}/\mu\text{L}$ numbers as integer counts in a $2 \times 2$ Fisher's exact test makes the $p$-value entirely dependent on the arbitrary scaling unit (e.g., $\%/100$ vs. copies per $\mu\text{L}$ vs. copies per mL) and ignores the inter-replicate variance ($\text{SD}$ across the 3 triplicates shown in Fig 4C–G). Proper statistical comparison between inoculum and 72 hpi supernatant ratios across $n = 3$ biological replicates requires a paired $t$-test on the log-transformed competitive index $\ln(\text{RNA}_A / \text{RNA}_B)_{\text{72h}} - \ln(\text{RNA}_A / \text{RNA}_B)_{\text{inoculum}}$, a linear mixed-effects model, or a beta-regression/Dirichlet model.
2. **Residual Spectral Crosstalk Between DENV-3 (FAM) and DENV-2 (Atto590) Channels and Manual Thresholding (Lines 217, 236–238):**
   Even after running a LightCycler 480 color compensation calibration, DENV-3 positive templates generate false-positive amplification curves in the DENV-2 channel (with an average $\Delta\text{Ct} = 7.95 \pm 3.88$ across 25 experiments, line 217), and DENV-2 Atto590 exhibits weak maximum fluorescence ($0.64\text{ AU}$, line 206). To suppress this crosstalk, the authors manually elevated the fluorescence threshold in every run based on single-serotype positive controls (line 217) or applied an ad hoc $\Delta\text{Ct} > 7.95$ rule in clinical samples (line 238). While $\Delta\text{Ct} \approx 7.95$ corresponds to a $\sim 250$-fold concentration difference (acceptable for `50:50` or `10:90` mixes), in competition assays where DENV-3 outcompetes DENV-2 by $>100$-fold, DENV-3 bleed-through into the DENV-2 Atto590 channel could bias the quantification of the minority DENV-2 population unless explicit matrix spillover subtraction is performed.
3. **Discrepancy Between Clinical Specificity/Sensitivity Numbers in Text (Line 236):**
   In line 236, the authors test 87 patient sera: 32 DENV-1, 20 DENV-2, 13 DENV-3, 7 DENV-4 (total = 72 mono-infections), 5 co-infections, and 10 DENV-negative samples ($72 + 5 + 10 = 87$). They state that the optimized qRT-PCR correctly assigned 30/32 DENV-1 ($93.75\%$ sensitivity), 19/20 DENV-2 ($95.0\%$ sensitivity), 13/13 DENV-3 ($100\%$ sensitivity), and 7/7 DENV-4 ($100\%$ sensitivity), and then state *"no false positives, 9 samples identified as DENV-3 positive while being typed as negative by the routine technique and three false negatives, yielding... 86.95% specificity for DENV-3"*. First, if 9 samples typed as negative by the reference assay were called DENV-3 positive by the new assay, calling them "no false positives" while simultaneously computing DENV-3 specificity as $60 / (60 + 9) = 86.95\%$ (which treats those 9 calls as false positives relative to the reference standard!) is internally contradictory. Second, without sequencing confirmation of those 9 discordant negative samples, they cannot be definitively claimed as true DENV-3 infections missed by the reference assay rather than low-level FAM channel background amplification.
4. **Large Genome-Copy-to-FFU Ratio Asymmetry in the Competition Inocula (Fig 4D, 4F & Table 1):**
   Although competition infections were set up at `50:50` or `10:90` based on infectious titers ($\text{FFU/mL}$ in C6/36 cells), the measured RNA genome copy percentages in the `50:50` inocula (Fig 4D) were heavily skewed against DENV-1 (`14.8%` DENV-1 in DENV-1:DENV-2 and `9.9%` DENV-1 in DENV-1:DENV-3). Curiously, looking at Table 1, the stock genome-copy-to-FFU ratios are:
   - DENV-1 `AVS94`: $6.03 \times 10^5\text{ copies}/\mu\text{L} = 6.03 \times 10^8\text{ copies/mL}$ vs. $3.93 \times 10^7\text{ FFU/mL}$ ($\text{ratio} = 15.3\text{ copies/FFU}$)
   - DENV-2 `DWL089`: $3.74 \times 10^8\text{ copies/mL}$ vs. $1.24 \times 10^7\text{ FFU/mL}$ ($\text{ratio} = 30.2\text{ copies/FFU}$)
   - DENV-3 `DWL039`: $4.94 \times 10^8\text{ copies/mL}$ vs. $1.67 \times 10^7\text{ FFU/mL}$ ($\text{ratio} = 29.6\text{ copies/FFU}$)
   Based on Table 1, a 1:1 FFU mixture of DENV-1 and DENV-2 should have had $15.3 / (15.3 + 30.2) = 33.6\%$ DENV-1 RNA copies, whereas Figure 4D measured $14.8\%$ (and $9.9\%$ for DENV-1:DENV-3). Moreover, because the inoculum was collected *after* the 2-hour adsorption period on HuH7 cells (line 185: *"After two hours of incubation at 37°C... the inoculum was retrieved and stored"*), measuring the post-adsorption residual supernatant rather than the pre-adsorption input mix confounds input viral ratio with differential viral attachment/entry into HuH7 cells during the 2h incubation!

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Degenerate primer/probe optimization (`DEN-3-eDyn` and `MxDEN-3Pdegenerated`) restores detection of contemporary New Caledonia DENV-3 genotype I isolates and enables linear, sensitive fourplex quantification across all four DENV serotypes. `[Well Supported]` — The sequence alignments (Fig 1), standard curves ($R^2 > 0.998$, efficiencies $89.8\text{--}99.3\%$), and artificial viral mix validations (`100:0` to `0:100`, Fig 4A–B) solidly validate the analytical performance of the assay.
- **Claim 2:** In HuH7 competition assays, DENV-1 (`AVS94`) exhibits higher replicative fitness than DENV-2 (`DWL089`) and DENV-3 (`DWL039`), and DENV-3 (`DWL039`) outcompetes DENV-2 (`DWL089`). `[Moderately Supported]` — The large shift in relative RNA abundance from inoculum to 72 hpi supernatant (e.g., DENV-1 rising from $14.8\%$ to $97.5\%$ against DENV-2 at `50:50`, and from $1.3\%$ to $41.2\%$ at `10:90`) strongly supports a replicative advantage for the tested DENV-1 isolate over DENV-2 in HuH7 cells. However, as only a single isolate per serotype was tested at a single timepoint (72 hpi) without measuring infectious progeny titers ($\text{FFU/mL}$) at 72 hpi, the findings reflect isolate-specific RNA yield in HuH7 rather than general serotype-level fitness.
