### 1. Executive Summary
This computational study re-analyzes publicly available triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) single-cell RNA-seq data (GSE176078, $n = 9$ samples) and two bulk transcriptomic cohorts (GSE25066, $n = 178$ training; GSE86166, external validation) to characterize ferroptosis-associated tumor microenvironment (TME) subpopulations and construct a 23-gene prognostic risk score for recurrence-free survival (RFS) and drug sensitivity. Using non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) on FerrDb genes, the authors partition T/NK cells into three subclusters and macrophages into two subclusters (`FOLR2+` and `TREM2+`), then take 8,371 differentially expressed subpopulation markers into univariate Cox and LASSO regression on GSE25066, followed by ESTIMATE, CIBERSORT, TIDE, and `pRRophetic` analyses. While the single-cell macrophage subclustering recapitulates known `FOLR2+` vs. `TREM2+` macrophage biology in breast cancer, the manuscript suffers from fundamental methodological and conceptual flaws: the 23 genes in the prognostic model are not actually ferroptosis genes, microarray datasets are mischaracterized as bulk RNA-seq, the risk score formula contains a mathematical syntax error, and the model exhibits substantial overfitting alongside negative ESTIMATE and TIDE results that contradict the overarching TME claims.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Disconnect Between "Ferroptosis-Related Genes" Framing and the 23-Gene Risk Model (Section 2.4, Figure 4):** The manuscript repeatedly states that the prognostic model was constructed from "23 ferroptosis-related genes" (e.g., Figure 4a caption: *"Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression of 23 ferroptosis-related genes"*). However, Section 2.4 reveals that univariate Cox regression was actually performed on "8371 specific markers of ferroptosis-related subpopulations"—i.e., general differentially expressed genes across the NMF-derived T-cell and macrophage subclusters, rather than the 471 FerrDb ferroptosis genes. Consequently, almost none of the 23 selected model genes (*TMEM160, EWSR1, BCAT2, PNKP, MLEC, SAP30BP, TBL1XR1, STAG1, NR4A1, TNFRSF9, PSD3, BAD, MIIP, HIST3H2A, CDC25B, TCEB1, HMGCS1, SPC25, TKT, PTTG1, ADA, AK1, AIMP2*) are ferroptosis regulators; instead, they are generic cell-cycle/mitotic proliferation genes (*SPC25, CDC25B, PTTG1*) and general metabolic/housekeeping transcripts. Labeling these 23 genes as "ferroptosis-related genes" is factually incorrect.
2. **Misidentification of Affymetrix Microarray Cohorts as "Bulk RNA-seq":** Throughout the Abstract, Introduction, Discussion, and Materials and Methods (lines 8, 21, 119, 123, 131, 173), the authors describe GSE25066 (Hatzis et al., 2011, Affymetrix Human Genome U133A Array) and GSE86166 (Prabhakaran et al., 2017, Affymetrix HuRSTA-2a520709 Array) as "bulk RNA-seq data." Mischaracterizing legacy hybridization microarray platforms as RNA sequencing reflects a lack of familiarity with the underlying primary data and their normalization/distribution properties.
3. **Mathematical Typo in the Risk Score Equation (Section 2.4, Line 71):** The reported linear predictor formula contains an algebraic operator error for `TNFRSF9`, writing `+ TNFRSF9 + (–0.0503)` with an addition sign instead of multiplication (`+ TNFRSF9 * (–0.0503)`).
4. **In-Sample Feature Selection Overfitting and Steep Validation Drop:** The authors pre-screened 8,371 candidate genes via univariate Cox regression ($p < 0.01$) across all 178 patients in GSE25066 without multiple-testing correction, and then fit a 23-gene LASSO Cox model on the exact same 178 patients. Fitting a 23-parameter model on a modest event count after in-sample pre-filtering causes severe optimism bias, evidenced by the drop in 3-, 4-, and 5-year ROC AUCs from 0.87, 0.88, and 0.88 in the training cohort (Fig 4h) down to 0.65, 0.67, and 0.71 in the external cohort GSE86166 (Fig 4i). Furthermore, standard time-dependent ROC confidence intervals and C-indices are not reported.
5. **Internal Contradictions in Single-Cell QC and Cluster Counts (Section 2.1, Figure 1):** Line 29 states: *"After quality control (QC), 38,985 genes from 29,733 cells per sample were finally selected for subsequent analysis."* Having 29,733 cells *per sample* across 9 samples would equal 267,597 cells; in reality, 29,733 is the *total* cell count pooled across all 9 TNBC samples (comprising 11,784 T/NK cells, 3,671 macrophages, and other lineages). Additionally, the text and Figure 1a caption state the cells were divided into "9 major cell clusters," whereas the Figure 1c caption and line 40 refer to "12 major cell clusters" because the myeloid cluster was subdivided into 4 clusters without updating the nomenclature consistently.
6. **Conflation of Pre-Clustered Cell-Type Composition with Immune Infiltration (Section 2.2):** In the single-cell T-cell analysis, NK cells and NKT cells were pooled with CD4+ and CD8+ T cells prior to NMF clustering. The authors then report that "the proportion of NK cells in the T_C2 subpopulation was significantly more than the other two subpopulations" (Fig 2c) and interpret this in the Discussion (line 121) as "The T_C2 subpopulation was accompanied by a greater infiltration of NK cells." That NMF cluster T_C2 captured NK cells from a mixed T/NK object simply means the NMF decomposition separated NK cells from T cells by their lineage transcriptomes, not that patients with T_C2 cells have higher tissue infiltration of NK cells.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: At the single-cell level, T cells and macrophages form ferroptosis-related subpopulations whose infiltration degrees significantly correlate with clinical outcomes.** `[Overstated]` First, the single-cell cohort (GSE176078) contains only $n = 9$ TNBC patients and no survival outcomes are analyzed at the single-cell level. Second, the authors themselves note in Section 2.2 (line 53) that Hallmark pathway enrichment scores did not differ significantly between the T-cell subpopulations. Third, the macrophage subclusters (`FOLR2+` vs. `TREM2+`) represent well-established resident vs. lipid-associated tumor macrophage states (Nalio Ramos et al., 2022) rather than novel ferroptosis-specific states.
- **Claim 2: The risk score delineates ferroptosis-associated immune microenvironment heterogeneity in bulk TNBC cohorts.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` In Section 2.6 (Figure 6a–f) and Section 2.7 (Figure 7c), the authors found **no statistically significant difference** between the high- and low-risk groups in ImmuneScore, StromalScore, ESTIMATEScore, TumorPurity, or TIDE score ($p > 0.05$ for all). Claiming that the risk score captures TME immune heterogeneity based on a few unadjusted CIBERSORT fractions while global immune/stromal and TIDE metrics are completely null is unsupported.
- **Claim 3: High-risk patients exhibit enhanced sensitivity to 27 therapeutic agents (including ABT-263 and erlotinib), providing actionable guidance for clinical medication.** `[Overstated]` These "clinical responses" were not measured in patients; they were imputed computationally using `pRRophetic` (ridge regression trained on GDSC cancer cell lines) and correlated against the 23-gene proliferation-heavy score. Extrapolating in vitro cell-line IC50 imputations to clinical treatment recommendations without experimental or clinical trial validation is unwarranted.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** All three datasets are publicly accessible in NCBI GEO under accessions `GSE176078`, `GSE25066`, and `GSE86166`.
- **Code & Parameters:** Figure-level R scripts (`fig1-code1.zip` through `fig7-code1.zip`) are provided as source code supplements, and package versions (Seurat v4.1.1, NMF v0.26, Monocle v2.28.0, SCENIC v1.3.1, irGSEA v2.1.5, glmnet v4.0-2, pRRophetic v0.5) are documented in Materials and Methods.
- **Reporting Gaps:** The Key Resources Table is nearly empty (listing only a single row for Seurat with source incorrectly listed as "Microsoft"). Exact random seeds for NMF, t-SNE, and LASSO cross-validation (`cv.glmnet`), as well as the specific optimal penalty parameter $\lambda$, are not reported in the text.
