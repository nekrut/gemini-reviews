### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript resolves a fundamental developmental question in Congenital NAD Deficiency Disorder (CNDD): since biallelic loss-of-function mutations in *KYNU*, *HAAO*, or *NADSYN1* disrupt embryonic organogenesis between E7.5 and E12.5 in mice—prior to the functional maturation of the embryonic liver—which tissue performs de novo NAD synthesis from L-tryptophan during early organogenesis? Combining *Haao* knockout mouse models (`Haao+/+`, `Haao+/-`, `Haao-/-`), HAAO enzymatic assays, RT-qPCR, UHPLC-MS/MS targeted metabolomics of embryonic liver, placenta, visceral yolk sac, and whole embryos, a refined 3-day (E7.5–E10.5) maternal dietary vitamin B3 restriction regimen (`NW3.5` vs. `NW15`), micro-CT phenotyping of E18.5 embryos, and reanalysis of eight public mouse and human scRNA-seq/bulk RNA-seq datasets, the authors convincingly demonstrate that the extraembryonic visceral yolk sac endoderm (and not the placenta) is the exclusive site of de novo NAD synthesis during early organogenesis before handing over this function to the developing embryonic liver around E12.5–E14.5. The experimental design is elegant—particularly the `Haao-/-` dam $\times$ `Haao+/-` sire cross under E7.5–E10.5 `NW3.5` restriction, where `Haao+/-` littermates with intact yolk sac HAAO activity develop normally while 86.7% of `Haao-/-` littermates develop multi-organ CNDD malformations. Only minor statistical and methodological clarifications are needed.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Factorial ($2 \times 2$) Design Analyzed via One-Way ANOVA Instead of Two-Way Mixed-Effects ANOVA (Results, Lines 100–119; Figs 4 & 5; Methods, Line 114):** In the E10.5 metabolic experiment comparing `NW15` vs. `NW3.5` diets across `Haao+/-` vs. `Haao-/-` conceptuses (Figs 4A–H and 5A–H), the data form a $2 \times 2$ factorial design (Diet $\times$ Genotype) with littermate conceptuses nested within pregnant dams. Analyzing the four combinations via a one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post-hoc test obscures the formal main effects of Diet, Genotype, and the Diet $\times$ Genotype interaction, and does not account for litter-level clustering (random intercept for dam/litter). Fitting a two-way linear mixed-effects model (`Metabolite ~ Diet * Genotype + (1|Litter)`) would strengthen the statistical inference, especially for E10.5 embryo weight (Fig 4H) where the main effect of Diet is clear while Genotype within Diet is null.
2. **Pairing of Pooled Yolk Sacs with Single Individual Embryos in Correlation Analyses (Results, Line 113; Fig 5I–K):** At E10.5, 2–3 genotype-matched yolk sacs had to be pooled per UHPLC-MS/MS sample due to low tissue mass, whereas each corresponding embryo was analyzed individually (one embryo matched to the pool of 2–3 yolk sacs that contained its own yolk sac plus 1–2 littermate yolk sacs). While this practical constraint is transparently disclosed in Line 113, correlating an individual embryo against a 2–3 yolk sac pool introduces classical measurement error (attenuation bias toward zero for within-litter variance and inflation of between-litter correlation if multiple pools come from the same dam). The authors should report the number of independent litters and pooled samples ($n$) underlying Fig 5I–K.
3. **Reference Annotation Typo in Human Bulk RNA-Seq Reanalysis Methods (Methods, Line 107):** In the *Analysis of published RNA-seq datasets* section (Line 107 of the second text block), the authors state that bulk RNA-seq fastq files (from the human yolk sac dataset of Cindrova-Davies et al., 2017, `PRJEB18767`) were *"aligned to hg38 genome using the STAR aligner (two pass method). Gene counts were generated from the STAR aligner using gencode vM10 as the reference gene model."* `GENCODE vM10` is a **mouse** (`GRCm38`) annotation release, whereas `hg38` is the human genome. This appears to be a typographical error for the corresponding human GENCODE release and should be corrected.
4. **Dietary Background Difference in Figure 1—Figure Supplement 2B:** The E14.5 HAAO enzymatic assay comparing embryo-without-liver, isolated liver, placenta, and yolk sac was performed on mice maintained on the AIN93G diet rather than the Breeder diet used in Figure 1C, I, J and Figure 1—figure supplement 2A. Although transparently noted in the figure caption, reporting the niacin and L-tryptophan content of AIN93G alongside the Breeder diet (90 mg/kg niacin, 3.7 g/kg L-tryptophan) in the Methods would aid comparison.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *The visceral yolk sac endoderm exclusively performs de novo NAD synthesis from L-kynurenine during early mouse organogenesis (E8.0–E12.5) prior to the onset of embryonic liver activity (E12.5–E14.5), whereas the chorioallantoic placenta lacks this activity.* `[Well Supported]` — Supported by three orthogonal lines of evidence: cell-type-resolved scRNA-seq and RT-qPCR time-courses, direct HAAO enzymatic activity assays across tissues and genotypes, and UHPLC-MS/MS metabolic block signatures (18–20-fold 3HAA accumulation and loss of QA/NAMN in `Haao-/-` yolk sacs and E14.5 livers).
- **Claim 2:** *Restricting maternal NAD precursor supply for a 3-day window (E7.5–E10.5) depletes yolk sac and embryonic NAD+ and induces CNDD malformations specifically in `Haao-/-` embryos while `Haao+/-` littermates capable of yolk sac de novo NAD synthesis are protected.* `[Well Supported]` — The littermate comparison within `Haao-/-` dams (where maternal circulation is identical for `Haao+/-` and `Haao-/-` conceptuses) provides definitive genetic proof that conceptus-autonomous (yolk sac) de novo NAD synthesis rescues embryogenesis when maternal vitamin B3 is limiting.
- **Claim 3:** *Expression dynamics of the de novo NAD synthesis pathway in the human secondary yolk sac endoderm (3–8 PCW) and fetal hepatoblasts parallel those in the mouse.* `[Moderately Supported]` — Grounded in reanalyses of human bulk and single-cell transcriptomic atlases (Fig 6E–H), appropriately framed as transcriptional conservation since direct enzymatic/metabolic assays in early human yolk sacs were not performed.
