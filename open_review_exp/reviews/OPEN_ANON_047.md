### 1. Executive Summary
This study compares free amino acids and their derivatives between rhizomes of two *Polygonatum* species—*Polygonatum kingianum* var. *grandifolium* (`PzP`, $n=3$) and *Polygonatum sibiricum* Redouté (`PS`, $n=3$)—using commercial broad-targeted UPLC-MS/MS metabolomics (`Metware`), *de novo* RNA-seq assembly (`Trinity`/`Corset`), Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (`WGCNA`), and qRT-PCR validation of 10 candidate unigenes. Out of 72 detected amino acid-related metabolites, 37 are reported as differentially accumulated (34 higher in `PzP`, 3 higher in `PS`), and 2,539 unigenes are clustered via `WGCNA` into 6 modules (`MEblue` and `MEturquoise` correlating with the 34 and 3 metabolites, respectively). While characterizing metabolic differences between medicinal *Polygonatum* species is of practical agronomic interest, the study is undermined by fundamental statistical and bioinformatic errors: (1) executing `WGCNA` and `OPLS-DA` on a two-group dataset of only $N=6$ total samples ($n=3$ vs. $n=3$), which violates the minimum sample-size requirement of WGCNA ($N \ge 15$) and reduces network clustering to a trivial binary contrast; (2) comparing *de novo* `Trinity` unigene FPKMs across two different plant species without accounting for inter-species sequence divergence or orthology mapping; (3) reporting enantiomers (`DL-Methionine` vs. `L-Methionine`) and structural isomers (`L-Leucine`, `L-Isoleucine`, `L-Norleucine`) with identical achiral C18 MRM transitions as distinct quantified compounds; and (4) omitting public deposition of raw RNA-seq data or assembled unigene sequences, rendering the opaque `Cluster-XXXXX` gene identifiers uninterpretable.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Invalid Application of `WGCNA` to $N = 6$ Samples ($n = 3$ vs. $n = 3$ Design, `Lines 25, 127–136`, `Fig 6`)**:
   - The entire study consists of only **6 samples** total (3 biological replicates of `PzP` and 3 biological replicates of `PS`).
   - `WGCNA` requires at least 15–20 independent samples to estimate robust pairwise gene-gene topological overlap matrices; running `WGCNA` on $N=6$ samples in a two-group ($3\text{ vs. }3$) design is statistically invalid because Pearson correlations across 6 points partitioned into two tight groups are governed entirely by the between-species mean difference.
   - This mathematical artifact is directly visible in `Fig 6C` (Line 129): `MEblue` correlates ($r = 0.86$ to $1.00$) with all 34 metabolites that are higher in `PzP`, whereas `MEturquoise` correlates ($r = 0.95$ to $0.98$) with the exact 3 metabolites (`L-Valyl-L-Leucine`, `S-(5'-Adenosyl)-L-homocysteine`, and `N-Acetyl-L-tyrosine`) that are higher in `PS`! Thus, `WGCNA` did not uncover regulatory co-expression modules; it simply partitioned genes into those upregulated in `PzP` (`MEblue`) versus those upregulated in `PS` (`MEturquoise`).
2. **Cross-Species *De Novo* Transcriptome Assembly and Unspecified Differential Expression Statistics (`Lines 36–38`)**:
   - `PzP` (*P. kingianum* var. *grandifolium*) and `PS` (*P. sibiricum*) are two distinct species. In Section "Transcriptome analysis" (Line 37), reads were assembled with `Trinity` and clustered with `Corset`, and expression was quantified as `FPKM`.
   - First, when reads from two different species are co-assembled in `Trinity`, sequence divergence (SNPs/indels) between orthologous transcripts frequently causes `Trinity`/`Corset` to split orthologs into species-specific unigene clusters (`Cluster-XXXXX`), creating artificial "differential expression" where one unigene appears expressed only in `PzP` and its ortholog only in `PS`.
   - Second, `FPKM` is not a valid between-sample normalization metric for differential expression across divergent transcriptomes (where `DESeq2` or `edgeR` TMM normalization on raw counts is required), and Line 38 states *"differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined"* without specifying what statistical test, p-value/FDR threshold, or fold-change cutoff was used!
3. **Isobaric and Enantiomeric Duplication in Achiral C18 UPLC-MS/MS (`Table 1`, `Lines 31, 81–101`)**:
   - Chromatography was performed on a standard achiral reverse-phase `Agilent SB-C18` column (`2.1 mm x 100 mm`, Line 31).
   - Yet `Table 1` lists both **`DL-Methionine`** (`Q1 = 150.10 Da`, `Q3 = 61.00 Da`, `MW = 149.0510`) and **`L-Methionine`** (`Q1 = 150.06 Da`, `Q3 = 61.00 Da`, `MW = 149.0511`) as separate differentially accumulated metabolites! An achiral C18 column and triple-quadrupole MRM ($150.1 \to 61.0$) cannot physically resolve D- and L-enantiomers without chiral derivatization or a chiral stationary phase.
   - Similarly, `L-Leucine` (`132.10 -> 86.20`), `L-Isoleucine` (`132.10 -> 86.00`), and `L-Norleucine` (`132.10 -> 86.00`), as well as `NG,NG-Dimethyl-L-arginine` (`203.15 -> 70.07`) and `N,N'-Dimethylarginine;SDMA` (`203.15 -> 70.07`), share identical parent/product ion transitions; without retention times and authentic standard chromatograms, counting duplicate MRM library entries inflates the reported number of distinct differential metabolites (37 DAMs).
4. **Inappropriate Statistical Tests (`OPLS-DA` and `Duncan's test` on 2 Groups, `Lines 66, 105`)**:
   - Fitting supervised `OPLS-DA` on $n=3$ vs. $n=3$ samples (`Fig 2A`) without reporting permutation test $p$-values overfits the group separation, and the caption to `Table 1` (Line 105) states *"Significance was assessed using Duncan's test. Different letters indicate significant differences ($P < 0.05$)"*—whereas Duncan's multiple range test is a multi-group post-hoc procedure that is inappropriate for a two-group comparison and lacks false discovery rate control across 72 metabolites.
5. **Internal Numerical, Figure Reference, and Primer Orientation Errors**:
   - **Conflicting gene and enzyme counts**: The Abstract (Line 8) reports *"271 genes are responsible for encoding 37 enzymes"*, the Results (Line 118) reports *"271 unigenes (S4 Table) encoding 47 enzymes"*, and a duplicate `## Results` summary section appended after the Discussion (Line 180) reports *"272 genes encoding 47 related enzymes"*.
   - **Mislabeled figure calls**: In Line 140 (under `Fig 7`), the text refers to `Fig 7A` and then mistakenly writes `Fig 5B` and `Fig 5C` when describing the contents of `Fig 7B` and `Fig 7C`.
   - **Primer orientation error**: In Line 47, the ubiquitin reverse primer is written in $3'\to 5'$ orientation (`3'- AATTACCAGGGATACAGCACC-5'`) rather than standard $5'\to 3'$, and the gene ID `Cluster-29705.11` is misspelled as `luster-29705.11` in Line 129.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim that `PzP` has broadly higher free amino acid accumulation than `PS` (`Figs 1–3`, `Table 1`)**: `[Moderately Supported]` — The UPLC-MS/MS profiles show clear separation between the two species grown in the same substrate, though duplicate isobaric/enantiomeric MRM entries in `Table 1` must be deduplicated and verified with retention times.
- **Claim that `WGCNA` identified 10 hub genes (`Cluster-39698.1`, `Cluster-27133.0`, etc.) regulating amino acid biosynthesis (`Figs 6–7`)**: `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Derived from an invalid 6-sample ($3\text{ vs. }3$) `WGCNA` where any gene differentially expressed between the two species correlates with the species-specific metabolite levels; furthermore, the biological annotations/enzyme identities of these 10 `Cluster-XXXXX` IDs are never even named in the main text!
- **Claim that qRT-PCR validates the transcriptome sequencing (`Fig 8`, `Line 151`)**: `[Moderately Supported]` — 8 of the 10 tested transcripts show directional agreement between RNA-seq FPKM and qRT-PCR, though using a single reference gene (*ubiquitin*) across two different plant species without verifying inter-species primer efficiency and Ct stability is a limitation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Severe Data Availability Deficit**: Although Line 188 states *"All relevant data are within the paper and its Supporting Information files"*, the raw Illumina RNA-seq reads have **not** been deposited in NCBI SRA/CNCB, nor is the assembled `Trinity` unigene FASTA file provided. Because `Trinity`/`Corset` identifiers (`Cluster-39698.1`, `Cluster-27133.0`, etc.) are arbitrary run-specific strings, external researchers cannot identify what genes these candidate hubs actually represent without the sequences and functional annotations.
