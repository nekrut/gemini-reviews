### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript reports a mixed-methods process evaluation of the UKHSA Winter Pressures 2022–23 pilot sub-study ($N = 7,774$ healthcare workers recruited between November 2022 and March 2023), which expanded an existing fortnightly SARS-CoV-2 PCR cohort to multiplex PCR testing for SARS-CoV-2, influenza, and RSV across three operational pathways: Pathway A (on-site hospital swabbing and local hospital laboratory testing; 33 sites, $n = 3,713$), Pathway B (on-site swabbing sent to a centralized UKHSA-commissioned laboratory; 6 sites, $n = 576$), and Pathway C (at-home postal swabbing returned to a centralized UKHSA-commissioned laboratory; $n = 3,485$). Using study fidelity metrics (recruitment, retention, assay switchover dates, multiplex coverage), an 11-item participant experience survey ($n = 1,204$ respondents, $15.5\%$ response rate), and thematic analysis of a 20-person internal research team workshop, the authors evaluate pathway agility and acceptability. They transparently report that while overall recruitment exceeded the $N = 7,500$ target, delayed and heterogeneous switchover to multiplex testing in local hospital laboratories (Pathway A: only $45.6\%$ of returned swabs were multiplexed, with switchover dates spanning 28 Nov 2022 to 16 Mar 2023—Only 13/33 sites even responded to confirm their switchover date) versus $100\%$ multiplex coverage in centralized laboratories (Pathways B and C) caused the study to miss the winter influenza peak and prevented the planned seasonal influenza vaccine effectiveness analysis.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Typographical and Numerical Errors in Reported Statistics:**
   - **Negative $p$-value in text (line 150):** When comparing withdrawal due to moving sites/leaving the healthcare workforce between site-based ($24/152; 15.8\%$) and postal ($32/420; 7.6\%$) pathways, the text reports `$p = -0.006$` (whereas Table 2 correctly reports `0.006`). A $p$-value cannot be negative.
   - **Inconsistent Swab Return Rate for Pathway C:** In the Results (line 186), the swab return rate for Pathway C is reported as `80.5%` compared to `73.7%` and `73.6%` for Pathways A and B. However, in the Discussion (line 285), the exact same comparison is reported as `80.7% vs 73.7%`.
   - **Truncated Quote in Results (line 221):** The participant quote on line 221 cuts off mid-sentence: *“having the swabs sent out earlier and in bulk as [the] first samples were hit by a”* (presumably "postal strike").
2. **Confounding Between Consent Mechanism, Cohort Eligibility, and Pathway Assignment:** Pathways A/B and Pathway C were not randomized or contemporaneously recruited under equivalent consent models:
   - Pathways A and B enrolled active participants at participating hospitals via *opt-out (implied) consent* (except for a subset of re-recruited inactive participants), starting 28 November 2022.
   - Pathway C recruited inactive participants at non-participating sites via *active opt-in online survey* ($3,485 / 12,549 = 27.8\%$ uptake), starting 13 December 2022 (with first swabs taken 22 December 2022).
   Consequently, comparisons of retention ($96.5\%$ site-based vs. $87.9\%$ postal), swab return rates ($73.7\%$ vs. $80.5\%$), and median test counts per participant (Pathway A: 6, Pathway B: 4, Pathway C: 5) are heavily confounded by (a) opt-out inertia vs. active opt-in self-selection, and (b) unequal follow-up windows (Pathway C started nearly a month later, giving participants fewer possible fortnightly testing rounds before 31 March 2023). Normalizing test completion by the number of *eligible fortnightly testing windows per enrolled participant* rather than raw test counts (median 6 vs. 4 vs. 5) would provide a much more accurate measure of pathway adherence.
3. **Unverified Proxy for Multiplex Switchover and Data Linkage Loss in Pathway A:** Only 13 of the 33 Pathway A sites responded to retrospective questioning regarding when they switched from monoplex SARS-CoV-2 to multiplex PCR; for the remaining 20 sites, the authors used the date Participant Information Leaflets (PILs) were sent as an unverified proxy (line 89, 194). Furthermore, in the Discussion (line 297), the authors note that local laboratories in Pathway A may have actually performed multiplex testing, but centralized data linkage failures (name spelling discrepancies and date-of-birth errors between hospital LIMS and UKHSA) prevented retrieval of influenza/RSV results. Distinguishing true laboratory assay delay from health informatics/linkage failure is critical for process evaluation recommendations.
4. **Severe Non-Response Bias in the Participant Experience Survey:** Only $1,204 / 7,774$ ($15.5\%$) of participants completed the feedback survey, and the survey was only sent to participants who *completed* the sub-study (excluding the 572 who withdrew). Thus, the reported $98.2\%$ willingness to participate again reflects a highly selected subset of completers.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Centralized UKHSA-commissioned laboratory testing (Pathways B and C) achieved 100% multiplex coverage compared to 45.6% in decentralized local hospital laboratories (Pathway A), which suffered severe implementation delays.** `[Well Supported]` The administrative and assay coverage records clearly demonstrate the operational bottleneck of relying on 33 local hospital laboratories during winter pressures.
- **Claim 2: The postal testing pathway (Pathway C) is effective, adaptable at pace, and acceptable to healthcare worker participants for pandemic preparedness.** `[Moderately Supported]` While operational agility and 100% multiplex assay standardization are clearly demonstrated, only $27.8\%$ of invited participants opted into Pathway C, postal withdrawal was nearly 3.5-fold higher than site-based withdrawal ($12.1\%$ vs. $3.5\%$), and participant satisfaction metrics (feeling valued, respected, results management) were statistically significantly lower in the postal arm ($p < 0.001$).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Completeness:** Summary tables of withdrawal reasons (Table 2), participant survey qualitative themes (Table 3), and team workshop themes (Table 4) are included, along with the survey instrument in S1 File.
- **Missing Granular Tables:** The exact underlying counts and denominators for Figure 1 (fortnightly recruitment and monoplex/multiplex test counts by pathway) and Figure 2 (exact numerators/denominators for each Likert category across the 11 survey items by pathway) are not provided in tabular form in the main text and should be included in Supporting Information to ensure full numerical reproducibility.
