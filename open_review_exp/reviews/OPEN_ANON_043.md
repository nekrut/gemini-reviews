### 1. Executive Summary
This study conducts a genome-wide association scan and constructs a polygenic risk score (PRS) for acute rejection (AR) following living-donor kidney transplantation based on donor-recipient autosomal identity-by-state (IBS) mismatch scores ($M_{IBS(ij)} = |D_{ij} - R_{ij}|$). Using 784 European-ancestry living donor-recipient pairs (161 AR events) from the DeKAF Genomics cohort for discovery and 352 pairs (61 AR events) from the GEN-03 cohort for validation, the authors select 1,307 non-HLA SNPs ($p < 1 \times 10^{-3}$, $r^2 < 0.2$) and report a validation hazard ratio of 1.54 (95% CI: 1.07–2.22, $p = 0.019$) per SD of the PRS. Despite the clinical relevance of non-HLA histocompatibility, the study suffers from fundamental statistical and immunogenetic flaws: (1) the IBS mismatch metric and linear PRS signed weighting allow genetic mismatches with negative regression coefficients ($\beta < 0$) to *reduce* the calculated rejection risk score, which contradicts the immunology of allorecognition; (2) living-donor cohorts mix biologically related (siblings, parent-child) and unrelated donor-recipient pairs, yet donor-recipient kinship/IBD is omitted from the Cox regression covariates; (3) `GCTA-GREML` is inappropriately applied to dyadic $|D_{ij} - R_{ij}|$ scores on a censored time-to-event outcome to claim a "heritability" of 0.53; and (4) LD clumping failed to remove closely linked duplicate signals (e.g., two *PIWIL2* variants 29 kb apart in Table 2).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Biologically Contradictory Signed Weights ($\beta < 0$) and Symmetric Mismatch Metric ($|D_{ij} - R_{ij}|$)**:
   - In Section 2.2 (Line 81), the donor-recipient mismatch at variant $j$ is defined symmetrically as $M_{IBS(ij)} = |D_{ij} - R_{ij}| \in \{0, 1, 2\}$. Immunologically, allograft rejection is driven by directional allorecognition (when the donor graft expresses an antigen absent in the recipient, i.e., $D_{ij} > 0$ while $R_{ij} = 0$), whereas a recipient carrying an allele absent in the donor ($D_{ij} = 0, R_{ij} > 0$) does not create a foreign graft epitope.
   - More critically, when constructing the PRS from the discovery Cox regression estimates (Line 90, Table 2), the authors use signed log-hazard ratio weights ($\hat{\beta}_j$), including large **negative** weights (e.g., `rs12798364` in *ANO1* with Effect size $= -0.887$, and `rs190087598` in *PTPRM* with Effect size $= -1.068$ in Table 2). Under a signed sum $\text{PRS}_i = \sum_j \hat{\beta}_j |D_{ij} - R_{ij}|$, a negative weight implies that **having a genetic mismatch between donor and recipient at that locus actively protects against acute rejection**, canceling out positive mismatch risks elsewhere in the genome. In histocompatibility genetics, true minor histocompatibility mismatches can only increase risk ($\beta \ge 0$); negative coefficients on $|D - R|$ at $p < 10^{-3}$ are classic estimation noise (Winner's curse) and should not be summed as "protective mismatches."
2. **Unadjusted Confounding by Donor-Recipient Pedigree Relatedness (Kinship / pIBD)**:
   - In living-donor kidney transplantation, pairs comprise a mixture of first-degree genetic relatives (parent-offspring, full siblings sharing ~50% IBD), more distant relatives, and genetically unrelated individuals (spouses, friends sharing 0% IBD). Consequently, across any set of 1,307 autosomal SNPs, the sum of IBS mismatches $|D_{ij} - R_{ij}|$ is strongly colinear with overall donor-recipient genetic relatedness (kinship).
   - However, the Cox regression model (Line 85) adjusts only for recipient age, gender, PRA status, 2-digit HLA matching code, and prior non-kidney transplant—omitting both genome-wide donor-recipient kinship/pIBD and ancestry principal components (PCs).
   - In Line 102, the authors state: *"Because most of the donor-recipient pairs are related, we carried out a sensitivity analysis for the PRS after removing 6 unrelated donor-recipient pairs (kinship coefficient < 0.1) in GEN-03."* Claiming that only 6 out of 352 living-donor pairs (1.7%) in a multicenter US transplant trial (GEN-03) are genetically unrelated (kinship $< 0.1$) is highly implausible given that spousal and unrelated living donation accounts for 35%–50% of US living kidney transplants; this suggests either a calculation error in the kinship coefficient (e.g., conflating IBS sharing with kinship) or an undocumented pre-filtering of the cohort. Moreover, even among related pairs, full siblings (including 0-HLA-mismatch siblings) vs. parent-child vs. second-degree relatives have different genome-wide IBS mismatch distributions and clinical immunosuppression protocols that must be adjusted for directly via genome-wide pIBD as a covariate.
3. **Invalid Application of `GCTA-GREML` to Dyadic Mismatch Scores (`Lines 85, 102`)**:
   - The authors report: *"We also calculated the AR heritability as the variance proportion explained by genome-wide IBS mismatching scores and estimated it in the DeKAF Genomics cohort by Genome-wide Complex Trait Analysis (GCTA-GREML) method... we estimated the AR observed-scale heritability as 0.53 with P-value = $4.18 \times 10^{-3}$."*
   - `GCTA-GREML` assumes an individual-level additive polygenic model where the genetic similarity matrix is constructed from standardized allele counts of unrelated individuals. Feeding pairwise absolute genotype differences $|D_{ij} - R_{ij}|$ from a cohort containing related donor-recipient pairs into `GCTA-GREML` for a right-censored survival outcome violates the foundational assumptions of GREML (specifically, the bimodal variance of genome-wide $|D - R|$ between related and unrelated pairs violates the infinitesimal random-effects covariance structure), and the resulting variance component is not "heritability."
4. **LD Clumping Failure in `Table 2`**:
   - Section 2.3 states that variant clumping was performed in `PLINK 1.9` with $r^2 < 0.2$ and a 200 kb window. Yet Table 2 ("Top 10 weighted SNPs in the PRS") lists two variants in *PIWIL2* on Chromosome 8 separated by only **29.4 kb**: `rs6557986` (pos 22,167,613; A1 freq = 0.084; Mean IBS = 0.051) and `rs60263168` (pos 22,197,019; A1 freq = 0.083; Mean IBS = 0.052). Why did two variants 29 kb apart with virtually identical allele frequencies and mean IBS scores both pass a 200 kb / $r^2 < 0.2$ clump? Notably, if LD clumping was run on individual genotypes rather than on the analysis variable $M_{IBS(ij)}$, or if multi-allelic/indel identifiers (`C/CA` for `rs60263168`) were mishandled in the reference panel, redundant correlated signals were double-counted in the PRS.
   - Additionally, Table 2 is labeled "Top 10 weighted SNPs in the PRS," yet it is ordered by chromosome (Chr 2 to Chr 18) and its effect sizes ($|\hat{\beta}|$ from 0.887 to 1.191) are not explained in relation to the remaining 1,297 SNPs in the PRS.
5. **In-Sample Threshold Selection and Missing Multi-Threshold Validation**:
   - In Section 2.3 (Line 90), five p-value thresholds ($5\times 10^{-2}, 1\times 10^{-2}, 1\times 10^{-3}, 1\times 10^{-4}, 1\times 10^{-5}$) were evaluated in DeKAF before selecting $1\times 10^{-3}$ for GEN-03. Because evaluating a PRS in its own training discovery cohort (DeKAF) is trivially overfitted (in-sample $R^2$ increases monotonically with SNP count), selecting $1\times 10^{-3}$ based on "statistical significance in the training data and the number of SNPs" is arbitrary. Standard PRS reporting requires showing validation performance in GEN-03 across all five pre-specified p-value thresholds.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim that non-HLA donor-recipient mismatching has an observed-scale heritability of $0.53$ ($p = 4.18 \times 10^{-3}$)**: `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Derived from an invalid application of `GCTA-GREML` to dyadic $|D_{ij} - R_{ij}|$ scores in a mixed-relatedness living-donor cohort.
- **Claim that the 1,307-SNP non-HLA mismatch PRS independently predicts acute rejection (`HR = 1.54`, $p = 0.019$)**: `[Fragile]` — The validation p-value ($p = 0.019$, based on 61 AR events in GEN-03) is unadjusted for multiple p-value thresholds, does not control for genome-wide donor-recipient kinship/pIBD or high-resolution (4-digit/eplet) HLA mismatching in the Cox model, and relies on a biologically incoherent signed sum where mismatches at negative-weight loci reduce risk.
- **Claim that pathway analysis implicates `cell morphogenesis (GO:0000902)` in allograft rejection**: `[Overstated]` — Mapping 1,307 noise-dominated SNPs ($p < 10^{-3}$ in an underpowered GWAS of 161 events) within 20 kb to nearest genes without controlling for gene length (large genes such as *PTPRM* or morphogenesis/adhesion genes span hundreds of kilobases and harbor far more SNPs by chance in unadjusted `PANTHER` gene-list tests) produces classic gene-length bias in GO over-representation testing.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability**: GEN-03 is available via dbGaP (`phs001667.v1.p1`), whereas DeKAF Genomics individual-level data is restricted by consent. The 1,308 SNP summary statistics and weights are provided in `S1 Appendix`.
- **Missing Details**: The exact formula used to compute the kinship coefficient in GEN-03 (which implausibly flagged 346 of 352 pairs as related with kinship $\ge 0.1$), the definition of the "two-digit HLA matching code" covariate (e.g., whether HLA-A, -B, -DR, -DQ were summed linearly or treated categorically), and the GEN-03 validation results for the other four p-value thresholds ($5\times 10^{-2}, 1\times 10^{-2}, 1\times 10^{-4}, 1\times 10^{-5}$) are absent.
