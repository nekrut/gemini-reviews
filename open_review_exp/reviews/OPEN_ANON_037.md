### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript follows a multi-step bioinformatics template to investigate "cuproptosis-associated genes" (CAGs) in Alzheimer's disease (AD) using public peripheral blood microarray data (`GSE63060`), external cortical brain bulk expression datasets (`GSE33000`, `GSE122063`), peripheral blood single-cell RNA-seq (`GSE181279`, 3 AD vs. 2 controls), molecular docking (`PyRx`), and a small clinical RT-qPCR cohort ($n = 6$ amyloid-PET-positive AD patients and $n = 4$ controls). The authors intersect differentially expressed genes in `GSE63060` with 46 CAGs to obtain 19 "DECAGs", perform `CIBERSORT`, `ConsensusClusterPlus`, `GSVA`, and train four machine-learning classifiers (GLM, RF, SVM, XGB) to select 5 hub genes (*FDX1*, *GLS*, *PDK1*, *MAP2K1*, *SOD1*), build a diagnostic nomogram, dock 4 compounds from DGIdb, run `CellChat` on peripheral blood scRNA-seq, and measure the 5 genes by RT-qPCR. Forensically, the manuscript exhibits severe methodological flaws, internal contradictions across virtually every analytical step, data leakage in feature selection, opposite directions of gene expression between their bulk/qPCR data and their single-cell analysis, false claims regarding drug-target pairings (docking a SYK inhibitor to FDX1), and physically impossible molecular docking scores ($-7.7\text{ kcal/mol}$ for a 9-heavy-atom fragment, dichloroacetic acid).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Severe Internal Contradictions in Sample Sizes, Thresholds, and Clustering Cohort:**
   - **Clustering Sample Size Discrepancy:** In the Methods (line 47 & line 84), `GSE63060` is correctly described as containing **145 AD patients**, 80 MCI subjects, and 104 healthy controls (total 329 blood samples). In the Results (line 194, Fig 5D), the two consensus clusters of AD patients contain $n = 90$ (Cluster 1) and $n = 55$ (Cluster 2), which sums to $90 + 55 = \mathbf{145}$ AD patients. Yet in the Methods ("Unsupervised clustering", line 72), the authors state: *"Cluster analysis of **310 patients with AD** was performed using the ConsensusClusterPlus software package."* Notice that 310 is the number of AD brain samples in `GSE33000` (line 84), not `GSE63060`!
   - **Implausible DEG Threshold on Blood Microarray Data (`GSE63060`):** In the Methods (line 52), the authors claim they identified 19 DECAGs out of 46 CAGs in `GSE63060` peripheral blood using `limma` with **$\lvert\log_2\text{FC}\rvert > 1$ (a $>2$-fold change) and Bonferroni $p < 0.05$**. However, in Fig 2A (the boxplot of the 46 CAGs in `GSE63060`), the expression differences between AD and control whole blood are tiny ($\lvert\log_2\text{FC}\rvert \ll 0.3$, typical of peripheral blood microarrays in AD where almost no genes exceed a 2-fold change). Furthermore, the Fig 2D caption (line 172) refers to *"16 DECRG"* instead of 19 DECAGs.
   - **R Version Contradiction:** Line 47 states *"All datasets were processed utilizing R Software (v4.4.1)"*, whereas line 155 states *"Bioinformatics analyses were performed using the mentioned packages in the R (version 4.3.1)"*.
2. **Data Leakage and Missing Control Group in Machine Learning Risk Model:**
   - First, the 19 DECAGs were selected across the *entire* `GSE63060` dataset before splitting into a 70% training and 30% test set, constituting feature-selection data leakage.
   - Second, in the Methods ("Construction of cuproptosis-related AD risk models", line 84), the authors write: *"Firstly, the 145 AD patients in GSE63060 dataset were randomly divided into a test cohort (30%, n = 44) and a training cohort (70%, n = 101)."* If only the 145 AD patients (and 0 healthy controls) were split into training ($n=101$) and test ($n=44$), a binary disease classifier (AD vs. Control) cannot be trained or evaluated via ROC curves because every sample has the same outcome label (AD)! Even if controls were included without being mentioned, evaluating decision curve analysis (DCA) and calibration curves on the *training* dataset (line 216) overstates clinical utility.
3. **Direct Directional Contradiction Between Bulk/qPCR and Single-Cell RNA-seq Results:**
   - In the bulk `GSE63060` dataset (line 163) and in the authors' clinical RT-qPCR validation (line 21 & line 299), **`FDX1` and `GLS` are significantly downregulated (decreased)** in AD peripheral blood, while **`PDK1` is upregulated** and **`MAP2K1` is downregulated** in bulk (and unchanged in qPCR; note also that line 310 in the Discussion falsely claims RT-qPCR confirmed *FDX1*, *PDK1*, and *MAP2K1*, contradicting line 299 and line 11 of chunk 2 which state *FDX1*, *GLS*, and *PDK1* were significant in qPCR!).
   - Yet in the single-cell RNA-seq analysis of AD peripheral blood (`GSE181279`, Abstract line 21 and Results line 283), the authors claim the exact *opposite*: *"in the single-cell analysis of AD peripheral blood, all hub-genes except SOD1 were found to be **up-regulated** in B cells, NK cells, and CD4+ T cells"*. How can *FDX1*, *GLS*, and *MAP2K1* be upregulated across all major lymphocyte populations in AD blood scRNA-seq while being significantly *downregulated* in bulk peripheral blood and RT-qPCR? The authors never notice or address this fundamental contradiction.
4. **Biologically False Drug-Target Pairings and Physically Impossible Docking Energies:**
   - **False DGIdb Target Mapping:** The authors claim they queried DGIdb for drugs targeting their 5 hub genes and identified **Lanraplenib as a drug targeting FDX1** (Table 3, Fig 10C). In reality, Lanraplenib (GS-9876) is a selective **SYK (spleen tyrosine kinase)** inhibitor (which the authors themselves state in the Discussion, line 9 of chunk 2!)—it has no known pharmacological interaction with mitochondrial ferredoxin 1 (FDX1).
   - **Physically Impossible AutoDock Vina Binding Affinity for Dichloroacetic Acid:** In Table 3 (line 261), the authors report a PyRx (AutoDock Vina) binding energy of **$-7.7\text{ kcal/mol}$** for **Dichloroacetic Acid ($\text{CHCl}_2\text{COOH}$)** docked to PDK1. Dichloroacetic acid has a molecular weight of $128.9\text{ Da}$ and only **6 non-hydrogen heavy atoms**. In AutoDock Vina's empirical scoring function, maximum ligand efficiency per heavy atom is $\sim -0.5$ to $-0.6\text{ kcal/mol/atom}$, meaning a 6-heavy-atom fragment has a theoretical maximum Vina score of roughly $-3.0$ to $-3.8\text{ kcal/mol}$ (verified in crystal structures of DCA bound to PDK1/PDK2 where DCA is a weak millimolar allosteric binder). A Vina score of $-7.7\text{ kcal/mol}$ for a 6-heavy-atom molecule is physically impossible unless a much larger molecule or cofactor was docked by mistake.
5. **Conflation of *PDK1* (Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Kinase 1) with *PDPK1* (3-Phosphoinositide-Dependent Protein Kinase-1):**
   - Throughout the manuscript, the authors confuse two completely different human genes that share the alias "PDK1": mitochondrial **pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase 1 (*PDK1*, HGNC:8809)**—which phosphorylates PDHA1 in the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (lipoic acid/cuproptosis pathway) and is inhibited by dichloroacetic acid—versus cytoplasmic **3-phosphoinositide-dependent protein kinase 1 (*PDPK1*, HGNC:8816)**, a serine/threonine PI3K/AKT signaling kinase!
   - Specifically, in the Discussion (chunk 2, line 3), the authors define their hub gene *PDK1* as: *"Serine/threonine kinase 3-phosphatidylinositol-dependent protein kinase 1 (PDK1) is important for multiple types of immune cell development..."* (which is *PDPK1*), and then 6 lines later (chunk 2, line 9) state that Dichloroacetic Acid acts *"by inhibiting pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase (PDK)"* (which is mitochondrial *PDK1*). Checking their qPCR primer in Table 2 (`5'-TGGTTTTGGTTATGGATTGCC-3'`), it targets mitochondrial *PDK1*, meaning their immunological interpretation of *PDPK1* is built on gene-symbol confusion.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Cuproptosis is a key driver of peripheral immune cell alterations in AD, mediated by 5 hub cuproptosis genes (*FDX1*, *GLS*, *PDK1*, *MAP2K1*, *SOD1*).** `[Methodologically Flawed]` First, *MAP2K1* (MEK1) and *SOD1* are not core cuproptosis execution genes (per Tsvetkov et al. 2022, which identified *FDX1*, *LIAS*, *LIPT1*, *DLD*, *DLAT*, *PDHA1*, *PDHB*, *MTF1*, *GLS*, *CDKN2A*), and the authors conflate mitochondrial *PDK1* with *PDPK1*. Second, peripheral blood leukocytes do not undergo Alzheimer's neuropathology, and the direction of *FDX1* and *GLS* expression in single-cell analysis contradicts both the bulk dataset and the authors' own qPCR data.
- **Claim 2: Tetracycline, AZD8330, Lanraplenib, and Dichloroacetic Acid bind strongly ($<-7.0\text{ kcal/mol}$) to SOD1, MAP2K1, FDX1, and PDK1 as candidate AD therapeutics.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` Lanraplenib is a SYK inhibitor with no relation to FDX1, the $-7.7\text{ kcal/mol}$ docking score for the 6-heavy-atom fragment dichloroacetic acid is physically impossible in AutoDock Vina, and inhibiting FDX1 or SOD1 would exacerbate mitochondrial and oxidative dysfunction rather than treat AD.
- **Claim 3: RT-qPCR in 6 AD patients and 4 controls validates *FDX1*, *GLS*, and *PDK1* as peripheral blood diagnostic biomarkers of AD.** `[Overstated]` An unblinded convenience sample of $n = 6$ vs. $n = 4$ using unadjusted Student's $t$-tests across 5 genes is severely underpowered ($N = 10$ total) to support clinical biomarker claims, and the text contradicts itself on which three genes were validated (line 299 says *PDK1*, *FDX1*, *GLS*; line 310 says *FDX1*, *PDK1*, *MAP2K1*).

- **Public Accessions:** GEO accessions (`GSE63060`, `GSE33000`, `GSE122063`, `GSE181279`) and qPCR primer sequences (Table 2) are provided.
