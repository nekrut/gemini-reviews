### 1. Executive Summary
This study employs dual-RNA sequencing to dissect the contrasting antagonistic behaviors of two *Trichoderma afroharzianum* isolates—Th19A (which completely overgrows the soybean sudden death syndrome pathogen *Fusarium virguliforme* Mont-1) and Th4 (which forms a mutual-inhibition zone without overgrowth)—across three developmental stages of confrontation on cellophane-overlaid PDA plates: S1 (pre-contact, 10 mm apart), S2 (initial contact for Th19A or onset of inhibition zone for Th4), and S3 (5 mm overgrowth by Th19A or complete inhibition zone for Th4), with self-confrontation controls ($n = 4$ biological replicates per condition, each replicate pooling 6 plates). Reads were aligned independently to the *T. afroharzianum* T6776 and *F. virguliforme* Mont-1 reference genomes using STAR v2.7.8a (confirming $<0.8\%$ cross-species misalignment in single-species controls), quantified with `featureCounts`, and analyzed via `DESeq2` and `GOEnrichment`. The authors uncover a striking non-monotonic transcriptional response in *F. virguliforme* (high DEG counts at pre-contact S1 and late S3, but minimal DEGs at S2) alongside a monotonic increase in *T. afroharzianum* DEGs from S1 to S3, and contrast oxidative stress defenses (catalases/chloroperoxidases against Th4 vs. SDR/flavoprotein monooxygenases against Th19A) and secreted CAZymes, CBM1 proteins, hydrophobins, and a Kp4 killer-toxin domain protein uniquely induced in Th19A during S3 mycoparasitism.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Well-Controlled Dual-RNA-Seq Experimental Design and Cross-Mapping Validation:** The use of self-confrontation controls (*Fv* vs. *Fv*, Th19A vs. Th19A, Th4 vs. Th4) rather than single-plug colonies properly controls for nutrient depletion and colony-proximity effects on the plate. Moreover, explicitly checking cross-species read mapping in the single-species controls ($<0.8\%$ cross-mapping, line 59) validates the strategy of mapping dual-harvested S3 overgrowth samples to each reference genome.
2. **Inconsistent Reporting of DEG Filtering Thresholds Across Text and Figures:**
   - In the Methods (line 43) and Results (line 64), DEGs are defined as $\lvert\log_2\text{FC}\rvert > 1$ (which corresponds to $\text{FC} > 2$) and FDR-adjusted $p < 0.05$.
   - In the Fig 3 caption (line 76, for *F. virguliforme*), the threshold is stated as *"adjusted p-value < 0.05 and absolute fold change > 2"* (matching $\lvert\log_2\text{FC}\rvert > 1$).
   - However, in the Fig 8 caption (line 124, for *T. afroharzianum*), the threshold is stated as *"adjusted p-value < 0.05 and absolute fold change > 1"* (likely a typo for $\lvert\log_2\text{FC}\rvert > 1$), while in the Supporting Information caption for S4 Fig (line 170), it is stated as *"DEGs were determined using DESeq2 (False discovery rate (FDR) < 0.1)"*. The authors should clarify whether $\text{FDR} < 0.05$ with $\lvert\log_2\text{FC}\rvert > 1$ was used uniformly across all figures and tables.
3. **Single-Reference Alignment Bias Between Isolates Th19A and Th4:** Both *T. afroharzianum* field isolates (Th19A and Th4, isolated from Midwest US soybean seedlings) were aligned to the single reference genome *T. afroharzianum* T6776. While contrasting each isolate at S1/S2/S3 against its *own* self-confrontation control (`~treatment` within isolate) cancels out baseline sequence divergence and copy-number variation relative to T6776, accessory genes present in Th19A or Th4 but absent from the T6776 reference assembly cannot be detected by reference-based mapping alone.
4. **Time-of-Harvest (Colony Age) Potentially Confounded with Interaction Stage:** Because *F. virguliforme* plugs were placed 3 days prior to *Trichoderma* plugs and harvested at morphologically defined stages (S1: 10 mm apart; S2: contact/inhibition onset; S3: 5 mm overgrowth/full inhibition zone), the chronological age of the colonies increases from S1 to S2 to S3. The authors do not specify whether the self-confrontation controls (*Fv*-vs-*Fv*, Th19A-vs-Th19A, Th4-vs-Th4) were harvested at a single time point or matched to each of the three stages (S1, S2, S3). If controls were harvested at only a single time point (e.g., matching S2), then colony developmental age could explain why *F. virguliforme* showed the fewest DEGs at S2 (when colony age matched the control) and many more DEGs at S1 (younger colony) and S3 (older colony). This critical experimental detail must be clarified in the Methods.
5. **Minor Text/Formatting Artifacts:**
   - Abstract line 8: *"the intra-species variability i frequently observed"* (stray "i").
   - Introduction line 13: *"inhibit each other's growth. other's In fact"* (duplicated word).
   - Methods line 38: stray quotation marks `""` and `""""` around the STAR mapping sentences.
   - Results line 140: `Th19A` is repeatedly misspelled as `Th9A` (five times in one paragraph) and `amidohydrolase` is misspelled as `amidrohydrolase` (lines 145 and 161).
   - Software versions (`DESeq2` v1.12.3 in R v3.6.3) are quite dated, though functionally sound for standard negative binomial GLM contrasts.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Pre-contact (S1) transcriptional reprogramming in both *F. virguliforme* and *T. afroharzianum* indicates early, likely volatile- or diffusible-metabolite-mediated mutual recognition.** `[Moderately Supported]` At S1 (10 mm separation on PDA), *F. virguliforme* already exhibits $7.2\%\text{–}9.5\%$ DEGs and *T. afroharzianum* upregulates catalytic and polysaccharide catabolic genes. Note that on shared PDA medium overlaid with cellophane, both volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in the headspace *and* water-soluble secreted metabolites diffusing through the agar below the cellophane can mediate S1 signaling; therefore, attributing S1 recognition solely to volatiles (as in the Abstract) should be broadened to "volatile or diffusible secreted compounds".
- **Claim 2: Th19A induces mycoparasitism-associated CAZymes, CBM1 proteins, and a Kp4 killer-toxin domain protein during S3 overgrowth, whereas Th4 triggers an oxidative/peroxidase defense response in *F. virguliforme* across an inhibition zone.** `[Well Supported]` The divergence in secreted effector/CAZyme induction in Th19A versus catalase/chloroperoxidase induction in *F. virguliforme* confronting Th4 is biologically coherent and well-grounded in fungal antagonism literature.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw sequencing reads are publicly deposited in NCBI SRA under BioProject `PRJNA1052097`, and reference genome sources (*T. afroharzianum* T6776 from EnsemblFungi and *F. virguliforme* Mont-1 from Dryad) are clearly cited.
- **Completeness of Supplementary Tables:** While S1–S3 Tables summarize mapping rates, GO terms, and S2 *Fv*-vs-Th4/Th19A DEGs, providing the complete gene-level `DESeq2` output tables (gene ID, baseMean, $\log_2\text{FC}$, lfcSE, $p$-value, padj, functional annotation) for all 6 stage contrasts in both species as a supplementary spreadsheet would substantially enhance reuse. Also, the exact timing (days post-inoculation) of S1, S2, S3 and control harvests should be explicitly stated.
