### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates early-stage (35-day-old) breast muscle melanin deposition in Muchuan Black Bone Chickens by comparing a high-melanin group (MH) and a low-melanin group (ML) selected from 60 randomly sampled birds using bulk RNA-seq ($n = 5$ per group), untargeted UPLC-QTOF-MS metabolomics ($n = 6$ per group), and RT-qPCR validation. The authors report 73 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and 234 differential metabolites, highlighting *CREB5* and *GIPC2* alongside metabolites such as CDP-ethanolamine, m-trigallic acid, UDP-4-dehydro-6-deoxy-D-glucose, glucoiberverin, and UDP-glucuronic acid as key regulators of pectoral muscle melanogenesis. Forensically, this study is undermined by physically impossible biochemical measurements for melanin content, use of an unvalidated commercial "double-antibody sandwich ELISA" for an insoluble polymer (eumelanin), complete absence of any canonical melanocyte or melanogenesis genes (*TYR*, *TYRP1*, *DCT*, *PMEL17*, *MITF*, *EDNRB2*, *MLPH*) among the 73 DEGs, sex-chromosome confounding (*MHM2* is the #1 DEG because bird sex was neither controlled nor reported), internal contradictions in DEG and metabolite thresholds, and high-ppm mass spectrometry identification tolerances ($100\text{ ppm}$ precursor / $50\text{ ppm}$ product) that yielded plant/microbial xenobiotics rather than endogenous avian melanin intermediates.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Physically Impossible Melanin Concentrations and Unvalidated "Sandwich ELISA" Assay:**
   - In the Methods (line 28), 0.1 g ($100\text{ mg}$) of breast muscle was homogenized in $900\ \mu\text{L}$ ($0.9\text{ mL}$) of PBS buffer (a $1\text{ mL}$ total homogenate containing at most $100\text{ mg/mL}$ of *total wet tissue*, of which $\sim 75\%$ is water and $\sim 20\%$ is myofibrillar/sarcoplasmic protein), centrifuged, and the aqueous PBS supernatant was assayed with a commercial "chicken melanin double-antibody sandwich ELISA kit" (Shanghai Enzyme Biotech Co., Ltd., YJ500392).
   - First, eumelanin is an insoluble, heterogeneous, non-proteinaceous indolequinone polymer packaged inside melanosomes; it is insoluble in neutral aqueous PBS (so centrifugation pellets the melanosomes into the debris) and lacks defined, non-overlapping protein epitopes required for a two-site "double-antibody sandwich ELISA".
   - Second, in the Results (line 91), the authors report breast muscle melanin concentrations of **$103.193 \pm 3.598\text{ mg/mL}$ (ML) and $121.731 \pm 3.69\text{ mg/mL}$ (MH)**. A concentration of $121.7\text{ mg/mL}$ in an extract made from $100\text{ mg}$ of wet tissue in $0.9\text{ mL}$ PBS means the mass of melanin in the supernatant exceeds $120\%$ of the entire wet mass of the muscle tissue! Even if the units were meant to be $\mu\text{g/mL}$ or $\text{ng/mL}$, the narrow $18\%$ difference between ML ($103.2$) and MH ($121.7$) from an unspecific colorimetric kit does not validly quantify eumelanin (which in poultry science requires spectrophotometric alkali solubilization or HPLC quantification of PTCA/AHP degradation products).
2. **Uncontrolled Sex Confounding Driving the Top Differentially Expressed Gene (*MHM2*):** In Table 4 (line 144), the single most strongly upregulated gene in the MH group is **`MHM2` (male hypermethylated region 2, $\log_2\text{FC} = 3.03$)**. In chickens (*Gallus gallus*), the *MHM* locus on the Z chromosome is transcribed almost exclusively in females (ZW) and is hypermethylated/transcriptionally silent in males (ZZ). A $\log_2\text{FC}$ of $3.03$ ($8.2$-fold difference) in *MHM2* between $n=5$ MH and $n=5$ ML birds indicates that the MH and ML groups had an unbalanced ratio of female vs. male chicks! The Methods section ("Sample collection", line 23) never states the sex of the 60 chickens or the $5\text{ vs. }5$ RNA-seq subset.
3. **Internal Contradictions in Differential Expression Methods and Thresholds:**
   - In the Methods (line 33), DEG calling is described as using *"DESeq2-edgeR methods, with an FDR threshold of 0.05 and a fold change (FC) of 1.5"* ($\lvert \log_2\text{FC} \rvert > 0.585$). Later (line 83), it is described as *"DESeq2_EBSeq with an FDR threshold of 0.05 and a fold change cutoff of 1.5"*.
   - However, in Table 5 (lines 176–182), the authors list supposed "DEGs" from their KEGG pathways with **$\log_2(\text{FC}) = 0.06$ for *FGF1* ($\text{FC} = 1.04$, a $4\%$ change!)** and **$\log_2(\text{FC}) = 0.34$ for *DUSP1* ($\text{FC} = 1.27$)**. Neither gene meets the stated $\text{FC} \ge 1.5$ ($\log_2\text{FC} \ge 0.585$) cutoff.
   - Similarly, in the Results (line 159), the authors claim that *"the RNA-seq expression level of the CREB5 gene in the MH group was 5-fold higher compared to the ML group"*, but Table 4 and Table 5 both report *CREB5* $\log_2(\text{FC}) = 1.37$, which is $2^{1.37} = \mathbf{2.58\text{-fold}}$, not 5-fold.
4. **Flawed Metabolomics Mass Accuracy Thresholds and Xenobiotic Misannotations:**
   - In the Methods (line 57), the authors state: *"Mass accuracy thresholds were maintained at 100 ppm for precursor ions and 50 ppm for product ions."* For a high-resolution QTOF mass spectrometer (Waters Xevo G2-XS QTof, which operates at $<5\text{–}10\text{ ppm}$ mass accuracy), using a $100\text{ ppm}$ precursor window leads to rampant false-positive database matches.
   - Consequently, the "key differential metabolites" highlighted in the Abstract, Results, and Discussion are plant/bacterial secondary metabolites not synthesized by vertebrate muscle: **glucoiberverin** (a cruciferous plant glucosinolate), **m-trigallic acid** (a plant hydrolysable tannin), **gossypol** (a cottonseed sesquiterpene polyphenol, line 230), **semivioxanthin** (a fungal polyketide naphthopyrone, line 230), and **UDP-4-dehydro-6-deoxy-D-glucose** (a bacterial/plant deoxy-sugar intermediate, which the authors themselves admit on line 241 has no known function in animals).
   - Furthermore, the differential metabolite screening criterion is stated on line 57 as `FC > 1, P < 0.05, and VIP > 1`. Since $\text{FC} > 1$ merely means any positive change (or no fold-change threshold at all if bidirectional), this provides zero fold-change stringency, and no FDR adjustment was applied to the metabolite $p$-values.
5. **Underpowered WGCNA ($N = 10$):** Running WGCNA on only $N = 10$ RNA-seq samples ($5\text{ MH} + 5\text{ ML}$) and $N = 12$ metabolome samples ($6\text{ MH} + 6\text{ ML}$) violates the minimum sample size requirements recommended by the WGCNA authors ($N \ge 15\text{–}20$), producing unstable module eigengenes driven by between-group mean shifts.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: *CREB5* and *GIPC2* are key genetic regulators of breast muscle melanin synthesis in Muchuan Black Bone Chickens.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` Not a single canonical melanogenesis or melanocyte marker gene (*TYR*, *TYRP1*, *DCT*, *PMEL17*, *MLPH*, *MITF*, *EDN3*, *EDNRB2*, *MC1R*) was differentially expressed (as admitted in line 243). Moreover, in the authors' own O2PLS integrative model (line 230), they note that *TPM2*, *CREB5*, *FGF1*, and *DUSP1* had negligible loading values ($\lvert\text{Loading } 1\rvert < 0.01$ and $\lvert\text{Loading } 2\rvert < 0.01$).
- **Claim 2: CDP-ethanolamine, m-trigallic acid, UDP-4-dehydro-6-deoxy-D-glucose, glucoiberverin, and UDP-glucuronic acid are intimately linked to phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan biosynthesis and melanin accumulation.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` In the Abstract (line 8), the authors claim these five metabolites *"were intimately linked to phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan biosynthesis associated with melanin accumulation."* Yet none of those five compounds are intermediates of the phenylalanine/tyrosine/tryptophan pathway; three of them (glucoiberverin, m-trigallic acid, UDP-4-dehydro-6-deoxy-D-glucose) are plant/microbial compounds likely misannotated due to the $100\text{ ppm}$ mass tolerance or derived from feed, and in the Discussion (line 241) the authors explicitly contradict the Abstract by stating *"neither Glucoiberverin nor UDP-glucuronic acid played a crucial role in key melanogenic processes, particularly in facilitating the bioconversion of tyrosine to melanin pigments."*

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw sequencing reads are deposited in the CNGB Sequence Archive (`CNP0006904`), and qPCR primer sequences/amplicon sizes (Table 1), UPLC gradient parameters (Table 2), and sequencing QC metrics (Table 3) are provided.
- **Missing Metadata & Tables:** Complete lists of the 73 DEGs (with raw/normalized counts, $\log_2\text{FC}$, nominal $p$-values, and FDR $q$-values) and the 234 differential metabolites (with exact $m/z$, retention time, adduct, ppm error, MS/MS fragmentation scores, VIP, FC, and $p$-values) are absent from the main text and supporting files, and bird sex metadata is omitted.
