### 1. Executive Summary
This longitudinal secondary cohort study of the US Health and Retirement Study (HRS, 2008/2010 baseline waves with up to 10 years of biennial follow-up through 2018/2020) evaluates the independent and interactive associations of a PCA-derived Subjective Neighborhood Disadvantage Index (7 items) and an Alzheimer's disease polygenic score (PGS-AD, excluding *APOE*) with incident cognitive impairment ($N = 6,826$: $6,123$ European ancestries [EA], $703$ African ancestries [AA]), cognitive impairment non-dementia (CIND, $N = 6,746$), and dementia ($N = 7,760$). Using ancestry-stratified Cox proportional hazards models and Relative Excess Risk Due to Interaction (RERI), the authors find that in EA participants, a 1-SD higher neighborhood disadvantage index is associated with higher hazards of any cognitive impairment ($\text{HR} = 1.09$, 95% CI: $1.03\text{--}1.15$), CIND ($\text{HR} = 1.08$, 95% CI: $1.02\text{--}1.14$), and dementia ($\text{HR} = 1.13$, 95% CI: $1.02\text{--}1.24$), with zero multiplicative or additive interaction with PGS-AD. While the study uses a well-established national cohort and evaluates both multiplicative and additive gene-environment interaction scales, it is compromised by the exclusion of proxy respondents at every follow-up wave (which systematically censors severe incident dementia cases), use of an unpruned PGS-AD that fails to predict incident dementia ($\text{HR} = 1.05, p = 0.30$), conditioning on future trajectory in the CIND exclusion rule, and a direct contradiction between the Results and Conclusions regarding whether PGS-AD predicts dementia.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Exclusion of Proxy Respondents Across All Follow-Up Waves Induces Informative Censoring of Incident Dementia:** In the Methods ("Cognitive status", line 59), the authors state: *"Proxy respondents were excluded from our study sample at each wave. To categorize cognitive status in the Health and Retirement Study, we used the Langa-Weir approach, which uses a composite score of 0–27..."* In the HRS, participants who develop moderate-to-severe incident dementia frequently cannot complete the 27-point self-respondent telephone interview and instead transition to proxy interviews—which the validated Langa–Weir algorithm explicitly incorporates via a 0–11 proxy cognitive scale. By excluding proxy respondents at every wave during the 10-year follow-up, the authors selectively dropped participants at the very wave they developed severe cognitive impairment/dementia. This informative attrition biases dementia hazard estimates toward the null and likely explains why PGS-AD was completely unassociated with incident dementia in the EA cohort ($\text{HR} = 1.05$, 95% CI: $0.95\text{--}1.16, p = 0.30$).
2. **Unpruned Polygenic Score Construction (`pT = 0.01` Without LD Clumping/Pruning) and Null Association with Dementia:** In the Methods ("Polygenic score for Alzheimer's disease", line 68), the authors report that they *"used a p-value threshold of pT = 0.01 and did not include linkage disequilibrium in the scores (i.e., no pruning algorithms)."* Summing genome-wide imputed SNPs at $p_T = 0.01$ without linkage disequilibrium clumping/pruning (or Bayesian LD shrinkage) over-weights high-LD blocks and degrades signal-to-noise ratio. Notably, while *APOE* $\varepsilon 4$ carrier status strongly predicted dementia ($\text{HR} = 2.04$, 95% CI: $1.68\text{--}2.48$), the non-*APOE* PGS-AD did not predict incident dementia in either continuous ($\text{HR} = 1.05, p = 0.30$, Table 2) or top-quartile dichotomous ($\text{HR} = 1.20$, 95% CI: $0.92\text{--}1.57$, `S3 Table`) specifications.
3. **Direct Contradiction Between Results/Abstract and Conclusions on PGS-AD and Dementia:**
   - The Abstract (line 21) and Results (line 193, Table 2) explicitly state that PGS-AD was associated with any cognitive impairment and CIND **"but not dementia (HR: 1.05; CI: 0.96–1.16)"** ($p = 0.30$).
   - However, the Conclusions section (ContentOffset 46080, line 21) states: *"We found that living in a more disadvantaged neighborhood and having a higher genetic risk for Alzheimer’s disease were significantly associated with an increased risk of developing any cognitive impairment, CIND, and dementia among European ancestries participants."* The authors must correct the Conclusions to accurately reflect that PGS-AD was not significantly associated with incident dementia.
4. **Outcome-Dependent Exclusion of Rapid Progressors in the CIND Analysis:** In the Methods ("Sample selection", line 80), for the CIND-specific analysis ($N = 6,746$), the authors *"further excluded participants who developed dementia during follow-up without prior records of CIND (N = 80)."* Because HRS cognitive assessments occur only once every 2 years, participants who progressed from normal cognition through CIND to dementia between two biennial waves were retroactively excluded from the baseline risk set based on their future outcome, violating survival analysis risk-set principles. Instead, these 80 individuals should either be retained in the risk set and censored/competing-risk modeled at dementia onset, or treated as having experienced cognitive progression during the interval.
5. **Discrete Biennial Interval Censoring and Unmodeled Competing Risk of Death:** Outcomes were only observed at discrete 2-year waves (up to 5 waves over 10 years) in an older cohort (mean baseline age $\sim 67$ years). Continuous-time Cox models without interval-censored survival methods or competing-risk adjustment for mortality over a 10-year window can distort hazard ratios, especially when neighborhood disadvantage and age also increase mortality risk.
6. **Truncated Table 2 Rows and Typographical Errors in Figure Captions:**
   - In Table 2 (African Ancestry panel, lines 223–238), the bottom rows (`APOE E4 status`, `Social Ladder`, `Baseline wave`, `PGS-AD`, `Neighborhood * PGS-AD`, and `RERI`) are omitted/truncated from the main table display.
   - In the captions to **Fig 1** (line 83) and **Fig 2** (line 185), the baseline wave is misprinted as `"Health and Retirement Study 2018 and 2010 wave"` instead of `"2008 and 2010 wave"`.
   - In Results line 263, the RERI African ancestry dichotomized model is cited as `(S2 Table)` instead of `(S3 Table)`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Higher perceived neighborhood disadvantage is independently associated with a modest ($\sim 8\text{--}13\%$ per SD) increase in the hazard of incident cognitive impairment and dementia in older adults of European ancestry. — `[Moderately Supported]`. Consistent across continuous and dichotomized models and robust to sensitivity adjustments for health behaviors and chronic conditions (`S4 Table`), though attenuated for CIND ($\text{HR} = 1.05$, 95% CI: $1.00\text{--}1.11$) after adjusting for health/depression covariates.
- **Claim 2:** There is no multiplicative or additive (RERI) gene-by-environment interaction between subjective neighborhood disadvantage and PGS-AD on incident cognitive impairment. — `[Well Supported]` (for European ancestry, where the interaction HR is tightly bounded around the null: $\text{HR} = 0.99$, 95% CI: $0.94\text{--}1.04$, $\text{RERI} = -0.02$) / `[Moderately Supported]` (for African ancestry, where $N = 703$ and poor cross-ancestry transferability of European-derived IGAP weights limit statistical power, as the authors appropriately note).
- **Claim 3:** "Having a higher genetic risk for Alzheimer's disease [PGS-AD] was significantly associated with an increased risk of developing ... dementia." — `[Overstated]`. Only *APOE* $\varepsilon 4$ predicted dementia ($\text{HR} = 2.04$); PGS-AD did not ($\text{HR} = 1.05, p = 0.30$).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** All phenotypic and genetic data are available via the University of Michigan HRS data portal (`https://hrs.isr.umich.edu/`) and NIAGADS (`ng00153`), and the inclusion/exclusion cascade from $N = 14,562$ down to the analytic samples is well documented in Fig 1 and `S1 Table`.
- **Analytic Details:** While software versions (`SAS 9.4`, `R 4.3.3`, `epiR` package) and covariate definitions are clearly stated, the exact PCA factor loadings and variance explained by the first principal component of the 7-item subjective neighborhood disadvantage index are not reported in the text, and analysis scripts are not deposited in a public repository.
