### 1. Executive Summary
This cross-sectional observational study characterizes the sputum bacteriome, functional pathways, resistome, and phageome of 63 adults with cystic fibrosis (pwCF) stratified by oral glucose tolerance status: 29 with cystic fibrosis-related diabetes (CFRD), 12 with impaired glucose tolerance (IGT), and 22 with normal glucose tolerance (NGT). Deep shotgun metagenomic sequencing (median 84.5 million non-host reads/sample) and 16S rRNA qPCR were performed alongside bioBakery3 (MetaPhlAn4, HUMAnN3), ShortBRED (CARD/MEGARes), and Marker-MAGu viral profiling. The authors report lower alpha diversity (Inverse Simpson $p = 0.033$ unadjusted, $p = 0.052$ adjusted) and altered beta diversity (Bray-Curtis PERMANOVA $R^2 = 0.17, p = 0.011$) in CFRD vs. NGT, enrichment of *Streptococcus gordonii* ($q = 0.01$) and tyrosine metabolism pathways ($q = 0.002$), and an association between a sparse Partial Least Squares (sPLS) CFRD virome score and lower $\text{FEV}_1\%$ predicted. While the deep shotgun metagenomic dataset with negative/mock controls in an important clinical population is a clear strength, the manuscript has substantial statistical and internal consistency issues—most notably numerical discrepancies between the Abstract, Results text, and Table 2, in-sample overfitting of the sPLS virome score (which has a near-random AUC of 0.61 for CFRD), unadjusted confounding by pancreatic insufficiency/CFTR genotype severity, and overstatement of non-significant results (*Pseudomonas aeruginosa* $q = 0.449$).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Internal Numerical Contradictions Between Abstract, Results Text, and Table 2:** The primary clinical effect size reported for the sPLS virome score on $\text{FEV}_1\%$ predicted is inconsistent across three sections of the manuscript:
   - **Abstract (line 21):** Reports `−6.4 [95% CI −10.2, −2.6]%, p = 0.001 for each 10% increase in virome score` in multivariate models adjusting for age, sex, antibiotic use, and modulator therapies.
   - **Results text (line 175):** Reports a `5.7% decrease in % predicted FEV1` in Table 2, Model 3.
   - **Table 2 (line 184):** Neither $-6.4\%$ nor $-5.7\%$ appears anywhere in Table 2; instead, Model 1 reports `-6.1 (-9.2, -3.0)***`, Model 3 reports `-5.6 (-8.8, -2.4)**`, and Model 5 reports `-3.8 (-7.3, -0.3)*`.
   - Furthermore, the Results text (line 175) states that Model 3 adjusted for "age, male sex, CFTR modulator use" and that the association "persisted even after adjusting for CFRD status (Model 5)," implying Model 3 did not include CFRD status or antibiotics; yet Table 2 displays estimates for both `CFRD status` and `Antibiotics` inside Model 3 (and labels the sex covariate `Female sex` rather than `Male sex`).
2. **sPLS Model Overfitting, Near-Random Discrimination (AUC 0.54 and 0.61), and In-Sample Reuse:** In Section 3 (line 162), differential abundance testing of the phageome found *no significant differences in any viral species* between CFRD and NGT. When sPLS models were trained on the 51 CFRD/NGT samples to classify CFRD status, the cross-validated AUCs were **0.54 for the microbiome score** and **0.61 for the virome score** (line 167)—indicating that neither score meaningfully discriminates CFRD from NGT beyond chance. Moreover, selecting "the best-performing repetition of the model" (line 71) and then plugging the in-sample predicted sPLS scores from the same 51 patients into a linear regression predicting $\text{FEV}_1\%$ predicted (Table 2) introduces post-selection inference bias. Because the dominant phages in CF sputum are *Caudoviricetes* targeting *Pseudomonas* and *Streptococcus* (Fig 5B), the sPLS projection captures latent airway pathogen/inflammation burden strongly tied to $\text{FEV}_1$ rather than a specific "CFRD virome signature."
3. **Reporting Non-Significant Taxa and Resistome Features as Positive Findings:** In Section 2.5 (line 69), the authors pre-specify an FDR threshold of $q < 0.10$ for differential abundance testing. However, both the Abstract and Results (line 124) claim that *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* had higher relative abundance in CFRD vs. NGT based on an unadjusted $p = 0.056$ and an **FDR $q = 0.449$**, which fails both nominal ($p < 0.05$) and FDR ($q < 0.10$) significance thresholds. Similarly, in the antimicrobial resistance section (line 151), phenicol, lipopeptide, tetracycline, and beta-lactam resistance genes are described as only "nominally associated" with CFRD, without reporting their FDR-adjusted $q$-values in the main text.
4. **Uncontrolled Confounding by Pancreatic Insufficiency and Underlying CFTR Genotype Class (Table 1):** Table 1 reveals a striking baseline imbalance: **100% of CFRD (29/29) and IGT (12/12) participants had pancreatic insufficiency, compared to only 50% of NGT participants (11/22, $p < 0.001$)**. In CF, pancreatic sufficiency is a strong marker of milder Class IV/V *CFTR* mutations, which also explains why only 50% of NGT patients were receiving CFTR modulators compared to 82% of CFRD patients ($p = 0.022$). Because severe *CFTR* genotypes independently drive both pancreatic endocrine destruction (CFRD) and severe airway dysbiosis/lower $\text{FEV}_1$, omitting pancreatic insufficiency and *CFTR* genotype severity from the multivariate regression models leaves substantial residual confounding.
5. **Sample Size Verification in Multivariate Models:** The Methods state that IGT participants ($n = 12$) were included in diversity analyses ($N = 63$) but excluded from differential abundance and sPLS training ($N = 51$). It is unclear whether Table 2 regressions ($N$ not stated in Table 2) included only the 51 CFRD/NGT participants or projected the sPLS scores onto all 63 participants; the exact sample size $N$ and $R^2$ for each model in Table 2 should be explicitly stated.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: CFRD is associated with altered global sputum bacterial community composition (beta diversity) and enriched *Streptococcus gordonii* and tyrosine metabolism pathways compared to NGT.** `[Moderately Supported]` The Bray-Curtis ($R^2 = 0.17, p = 0.011$) and Horn-Morisita ($R^2 = 0.19, p = 0.0067$) PERMANOVA results and FDR-significant enrichment of *S. gordonii* ($q = 0.01$) and tyrosine metabolism ($q = 0.002$) are supported by deep metagenomic data, though residual confounding by pancreatic insufficiency/CFTR genotype severity should be addressed.
- **Claim 2: *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* relative abundance and multiple antimicrobial resistance gene classes are elevated in CFRD vs. NGT.** `[Overstated]` *P. aeruginosa* did not reach statistical significance ($p = 0.056, q = 0.449$), and total bacterial load by 16S qPCR was virtually identical between groups ($4.32$ vs. $4.45\text{ ng}/\mu\text{L}, p = 0.80$).
- **Claim 3: A CFRD-specific airway virome signature independently predicts lower lung function ($\text{FEV}_1\%$ predicted), whereas the microbiome signature does not.** `[Fragile]` Because no individual phage species differed significantly between CFRD and NGT and the sPLS virome classifier achieved an AUC of only 0.61 for CFRD, labeling this component a "CFRD virome signature" is tenuous; its correlation with $\text{FEV}_1$ likely reflects bacteriophage turnover associated with general airway disease severity rather than glycemic status per se.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw shotgun metagenomic sequencing data are deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under BioProject accessions `PRJNA861321` and `PRJNA1078869`.
- **Controls & Experimental Rigor:** Inclusion of reagent-only negative extraction controls, Zymo D6300 mock community positive controls, and triplicate 16S TaqMan qPCR (`Ba04230899_s1`) with *E. coli* JM109 standard curves is commendable—though the sequencing results of the negative and mock controls are not summarized in the Results text.
- **Software & Code:** Software tools and database versions (bioBakery3, MetaPhlAn4 `mpa_v30_CHOCOPhlAn_201901`, HUMAnN3, CARD v3.0.7, ShortBRED, Marker-MAGu, R v4.4.1) are well documented, but custom R scripts used for the CLR linear models, PERMANOVA covariate adjustments, and `mixOmics`/Caret sPLS cross-validation are not deposited in a public repository.
