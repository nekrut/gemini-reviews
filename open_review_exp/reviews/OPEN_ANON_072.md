### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents the first comprehensive pangenome investigation of two basal chordate (lancelet/amphioxus) species, *Branchiostoma belcheri* ($N = 99$ whole-genome resequencing samples after removing 1 misclassified sample, plus 91 RNA-seq datasets) and *B. floridae* ($N = 41$ unrelated resequencing samples, a 98-sample parent-offspring pedigree comprising 2 parents at $\sim 60\times$ and 96 progeny at $\sim 7\times$, and 61 RNA-seq datasets). Using coverage-based presence-absence variation (PAV) calling benchmarked against reference gonad libraries (gene false-positive rate $\sim 0.05\text{--}0.09\%$) and de novo assembly of unmapped non-reference sequences (NRSs), the authors identify 1,112 and 1,281 shell/cloud dispensable genes in *B. belcheri* and *B. floridae*, respectively (each representing 4.4% of protein-coding genes). They demonstrate that dispensable genes are strongly enriched in innate immunity and apoptosis/signaling domains (Death, Lectin C-type, TIR, AIG1/GIMAP, and APEC), follow Mendelian inheritance across generations, and include a $\sim 150\text{ kb}$ integrated malacoherpesvirus-like provirus (`BelcheriHV-1`) present in 20/99 *B. belcheri* individuals alongside a second related viral genome (`BelcheriHV-2`) in a single individual. The study is well-conceived and biologically insightful, though minor numerical inconsistencies between the text and tables and methodological nuances regarding low-coverage progeny mapping warrant correction.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Benchmarking and Pedigree Validation of Coverage-Based PAV Calling:** The authors validate their $1/8\times$ BUSCO-normalized depth threshold using the two gonad DNA samples used to assemble the *B. belcheri* reference genome (estimating a region-level FPR of $0.23\%$ and a gene-level FPR of $0.05\text{--}0.09\%$) and further validate transgenerational inheritance in 96 full-sib *B. floridae* offspring. For Class I genes absent in both parents ($-- \times --$, $n = 282$), a median of $97.9\%$ were confirmed absent in progeny, providing strong empirical support for the PAV calls.
2. **Downward Bias in Offspring Absence Rates Due to Multi-Mapping and Low Progeny Depth ($\sim 7\times$):** In the Mendelian inheritance analysis (Table 3, Fig 8), observed median absence rates in the 96 offspring are systematically lower than theoretical Mendelian expectations for segregating loci: Class II ($+- \times --$, expected $50\%$ absence) exhibited $39.6\%$ (Table 3) / $40\%$ (text) absence, and Class III ($+- \times +-$, expected $25\%$ absence) exhibited $17.7\%$ (Table 3) / $19\%$ (text) absence. In Materials and Methods, `bwa mem` was run "allowing read multi-mapping" against a non-haplotype-resolved reference genome. Cross-mapping of short reads from closely related paralogs or non-reference alleles onto reference dispensable gene models, combined with wide hemizygosity coverage windows ($15\text{--}45\times$ in F and $14\text{--}42\times$ in M, leaving 420/1,281 [32.8%] shell/cloud genes unclassified as "undetermined"), accounts for this systematic deviation toward apparent gene presence. Testing unique-mapping filters (e.g., `MAPQ >= 20`) on the Class II and Class III genes would clarify how much of the $10\%$ and $7.3\%$ deficit is driven by paralogous cross-mapping.
3. **Internal Numerical and Species-Label Discrepancies Between Text and Tables:**
   - **BelcheriHV-1 coding density:** Results line 82 states that "72.7% of the provirus region length was covered by protein-coding genes," whereas Table 2 reports a coding density of `70.4%` for BelcheriHV-1 (note that the weighted average of the three blocks in Table 2 is $(46 \times 82.8 + 21 \times 37.1 + 75.5 \times 75.6) / 142.5 = 72.2\%$, or $102.96\text{ kb} / 149.856\text{ kb} = 68.7\%$, while block lengths $46,000 + 21,000 + 75,500 = 142,500\text{ bp}$ leave $7,356\text{ bp}$ of inter-block sequence unaccounted for).
   - **BelcheriHV-2 ORF count:** Results line 102 states that the three BelcheriHV-2 contigs "encoded 72 ORFs," whereas Table 2 lists `74` predicted genes for BelcheriHV-2.
   - **Big defensin absence rate:** Results line 144 states that "the big defensin gene was absent in 77% of the individuals," whereas Discussion line 188 states that the same gene "was absent in 88% of *B. belcheri* samples."
   - **Class III offspring absence percentage:** Results line 153 reports "absent in 19% of the offspring," whereas Table 3 reports `17.7%` for Class III.
   - **Species misattribution in Discussion:** Discussion line 188 states "By analysing the genotypes of paired parent/offspring data in *B. belcheri*, we further demonstrated that the inheritance of lancelet dispensable genes conformed to Mendelian expectations," whereas the parent-offspring dataset is from *B. floridae* (Results line 149, Table 3).
   - **Figure 8 caption table reference:** The caption to Fig 8 refers to "(see Table 2)" for the four classes of *B. floridae* dispensable genes, which are actually in **Table 3**.
4. **Effect of Collapsed Haplotype Reference Assemblies in Highly Heterozygous Genomes:** Amphioxus genomes exhibit extreme heterozygosity ($\sim 1$ SNP per $34\text{--}174\text{ nt}$). When a reference assembly contains unpurged haplotigs or divergent allelic scaffolds, reads from individuals homozygous for the alternative haplotype may fail to map to the secondary scaffold, mimicking PAV. While the authors acknowledge this limitation in the Conclusions, quantifying whether shell/cloud genes show lower baseline coverage (~$0.5\times$) in the reference individual or reside preferentially near scaffold ends would strengthen the distinction between true structural hemizygosity/absence and allelic divergence.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Lancelet pangenomes contain over a thousand shell/cloud dispensable genes per species that segregate according to Mendelian inheritance rules. — `[Well Supported]`. Backed by 140 resequenced genomes, BUSCO depth normalization, and direct tracking across 96 F1 progeny.
- **Claim 2:** PAV preferentially targets multigene families involved in innate immune recognition, apoptosis, and intracellular signaling (e.g., GIMAP/AIG1, APEC, Death, TIR, C-type lectin domains), paralleling prokaryotic defense islands and viral "gene accordions." — `[Well Supported]`. Hypergeometric enrichment tests with strict fold-change thresholds ($>4\times$ for Pfam, $>2\times$ for GO, $\text{FDR} < 0.01$) and maximum-likelihood phylogenetic analyses (IQ-TREE with `WAG+F+R5` and `WAG+F+R3`) consistently demonstrate lineage-specific expansions and high dispensability across both species and de novo assembled NRSs.
- **Claim 3:** A 35-gene dispensable block in *B. belcheri* represents an integrated malacoherpesvirus-like genome (`BelcheriHV-1`, $\sim 150\text{ kb}$) with low SNP density ($1\text{ SNP}/5,958\text{ nt}$ vs. $1\text{ SNP}/174\text{ nt}$ in the host), extending the host range of Malacoherpesviridae-related viruses to chordates. — `[Well Supported]`. Supported by concordant PAV across 20/99 samples, `geNomad` and viral ORF reannotation (62 ORFs, 27 homologous to malacoherpesviruses), $\sim 0.7\text{--}1.2\times$ relative sequencing depth, bimodal $50\% / 100\%$ SNP allele frequencies, and discovery of a second viral variant (`BelcheriHV-2`) in NRS assemblies from `SRR14996628`.

- **Code & Pipeline:** Software versions and exact parameters (`fastp v0.23.1`, `RepeatModeler v2.0.5`, `RepeatMasker v4.1.2`, `BUSCO v5.2.2`, `InterProScan v5.57-90.0`, `SPAdes v3.15.5 --isolate`, `BRAKER v3.0.8`, `geNomad v1.7.4`, `OrthoFinder v2.5.4`, `IQ-TREE`) are thoroughly reported, and custom PAV/enrichment scripts are deposited in public GitHub/GitLab repositories, with presence/absence matrices provided in `S1 File` and `S2 File`.
