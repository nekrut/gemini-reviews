# Blinded Review: OPEN_ANON_076
**Composite Score:** 5.4 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.5/3.5 | Claims: 1.1/2.5 | Biology: 1.2/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.6/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Statistical/Design
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Wet-Lab Primary Experimental
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

---

### 1. Executive Summary
This open-label, pilot randomized controlled trial (`jRCTs041210165`) evaluated the short-term (4-day) administration of low-dose risperidone ($0.5\text{ mg/day}$) versus no-treatment control on glucose homeostasis (primary outcome: Day 5 change in HOMA-IR), plasma and urinary $\text{D/L}$-serine and $\text{D/L}$-alanine levels (measured by 2D-HPLC), kidney function (eGFR, UACR), and gut microbiome composition (16S rRNA sequencing) in healthy Japanese adults. Out of 8 randomized participants (4 per group), 1 participant in the risperidone arm withdrew, leaving an analyzed sample of **$n = 3$ in the risperidone group and $n = 4$ in the control group** ($N = 7$ total). While two-dimensional HPLC chiral amino acid quantification is a technical strength, the study is severely limited by its micro-sample size ($n = 3$ vs. $n = 4$), mathematical impossibility of achieving exact two-sided Mann–Whitney $U$ significance ($p < 0.05$) at $n_1 = 3, n_2 = 4$, the fact that the between-group difference in plasma $\text{D}$-serine was driven by a significant within-group decrease in the unblinded control group ($p = 0.02$) rather than a significant within-group increase in the risperidone group ($p > 0.07$), and multiple unit errors in reporting urinary $\text{D}$-amino acid ratios.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Extreme Micro-Sample Size ($n = 3$ vs. $n = 4$) and Mislabeling of Per-Protocol Analysis as Intention-to-Treat (ITT):** In the Methods ("Study endpoints"), the authors state that *"Intention-to-treat analyses were performed according to the randomly assigned groups."* However, 8 participants were randomized (4 risperidone, 4 control), and 1 participant in the risperidone group declined to complete the study and was excluded from all tables and analyses (leaving $n = 3$ vs. $n = 4$). Excluding a post-randomization dropout is a complete-case/per-protocol analysis, not ITT. Moreover, with $n = 3$ vs. $n = 4$, the trial is severely underpowered to evaluate its primary safety endpoint (change in HOMA-IR: $+0.88 \pm 0.58$ in risperidone vs. $+0.42 \pm 1.00$ in control, $p = 0.51$; fasting blood glucose: $+4.0 \pm 2.0\text{ mg/dL}$ vs. $-1.3 \pm 3.0\text{ mg/dL}$, $p = 0.048$), rendering non-significant $p$-values uninformative regarding metabolic or renal safety.
2. **Mathematical Impossibility of Exact Two-Sided Mann–Whitney $U$ $p < 0.05$ for $n_1 = 3$ and $n_2 = 4$:** To address the non-normality and small sample size of $n = 3$ vs. $n = 4$, the authors performed two-sided Mann–Whitney $U$ tests and state in the Results (line 128) and `S1 Table` that the differences in plasma $\text{D}$-serine and urinary $\text{D}$-serine/creatinine ratio remained statistically significant ($p < 0.05$). However, for $n_1 = 3$ and $n_2 = 4$, there are only $\binom{7}{3} = 35$ possible rank configurations. Even when the two groups have zero overlap (ranks $\{1,2,3\}$ vs. $\{4,5,6,7\}$, $U = 0$), the exact two-sided Mann–Whitney $U$ $p$-value is $2/35 = 0.0571 > 0.05$. Any reported Mann–Whitney $p < 0.05$ (such as $p = 0.034$) is an artifact of Stata's uncorrected asymptotic normal approximation, which is invalid at $N = 7$.
3. **Between-Group $\text{D}$-Serine Difference Driven by Washout in the Control Group ($p = 0.02$) Rather Than Significant Elevation in the Risperidone Group ($p > 0.07$):** In the Results (line 133), the authors report that *"Within-group paired comparisons showed that the control group had a significant decrease in d-serine from baseline to day 5 (p = 0.02), while other amino acids showed no significant within-group changes across any group (all p > 0.07)."* Thus, within the risperidone arm ($n = 3$), the change in plasma $\text{D}$-serine ($+0.24 \pm 0.36\text{ nmol/mL}$) was **not** statistically significant from baseline ($p > 0.07$). Instead, because all participants were instructed to abstain from fermented foods (natto, miso, yogurt, pickles, kimchi—major dietary sources of $\text{D}$-serine) starting 1 week before Day 1, the control group experienced a dietary washout decrease ($-0.32 \pm 0.13\text{ nmol/mL}$, paired $p = 0.02$), which created the apparent between-group difference ($p = 0.03$).
4. **Unit Errors for Urinary $\text{D}$-Amino Acid-to-Creatinine Ratios in the Abstract and Results Text:**
   - In Table 1 (lines 115–118) and Results line 128, urinary amino acid-to-creatinine ratios are reported in units of **`mmol/g Cr`** (e.g., urinary $\text{D}$-serine/Cr change: `38.2 ± 19.8 and −25.8 ± 23.4 mmol/g Cr`).
   - However, in the Abstract (line 8), the exact same urinary $\text{D}$-serine/creatinine ratio changes are reported with concentration units of **`nmol/mL`** (`38.2 vs. −25.8 nmol/mL, p = 0.01`).
   - Likewise, in Results line 133, the change in urinary $\text{D}$-alanine-to-creatinine ratio is reported with units of **`nmol/mL`** (`24.98 vs. −9.42 nmol/mL, p = 0.20`) instead of `mmol/g Cr`.
5. **Baseline HOMA-IR Inclusion Criterion Discrepancy and BMI Imbalance:** Inclusion criterion #3 required $\text{HOMA-IR} \ge 1.6$ (line 33). Yet at baseline (Table 1), the control group had a mean HOMA-IR of $1.6 \pm 0.6$, indicating that individual control participants had Day 1 $\text{HOMA-IR} < 1.6$ (likely due to regression to the mean between screening and Day 1). Furthermore, despite minimization randomization by age and sex, baseline BMI was significantly higher in the risperidone group than in the control group ($23.8 \pm 1.4$ vs. $21.2 \pm 0.8\text{ kg/m}^2$, $p = 0.03$).
6. **Typographical Error in Microbiome Beta-Diversity Metric:** Results line 180 refers to `"the Gray-Curtis distances"` instead of **Bray–Curtis** distances.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** "Short-term administration of risperidone at 0.5 mg over 4 days effectively increased plasma d-serine concentrations in healthy participants." — `[Fragile]` / `[Overstated]`. In $n = 3$ treated subjects, the within-group change from baseline ($+0.24 \pm 0.36\text{ nmol/mL}$) was not statistically significant ($p > 0.07$), and the between-group comparison ($p = 0.03$ parametric; exact Mann–Whitney $p \ge 0.057$) was heavily driven by a $-0.32\text{ nmol/mL}$ decline in the $n = 4$ control group undergoing dietary fermented-food restriction.
- **Claim 2:** "Short-term risperidone affects d-serine metabolism without instigating acute adverse effects on kidney or glucose homeostasis in healthy individuals." — `[Overstated]`. A 4-day exposure in $n = 3$ healthy volunteers has negligible statistical power to rule out metabolic or renal toxicity; notably, 2 of 3 risperidone participants (67%) experienced somnolence, 1 of 3 (33%) experienced hypertension ($>140/90\text{ mmHg}$), and fasting blood glucose rose by $+4.0 \pm 2.0\text{ mg/dL}$ vs. $-1.3 \pm 3.0\text{ mg/dL}$ in controls ($p = 0.048$ by Student's $t$-test).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Registration:** The trial was prospectively registered with the Japan Registry for Clinical Trials (`jRCTs041210165`), the study protocol and CONSORT checklist are included in `S1 Protocol` and `S1 Checklist`, and the participant-level dataset is publicly deposited on Figshare (`https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.30000049.v1`).
- **Assay Documentation:** The 2D-HPLC chiral amino acid assay (`DASH 27B3X00322000001`), eGFR formula, and 16S rRNA QIIME2 (`v2021.2`, sampling depth 10,000 reads) parameters are well described, though raw 16S sequencing FASTQ accession numbers are not provided.
