### 1. Executive Summary
This study introduces a metagenome-to-constraint-based metabolic modeling (CBM) framework—including an iterative Microbial Community Succession Module (`MCSM`)—to infer hierarchical trophic exchanges in the apple rhizosphere microbiome associated with Apple Replant Disease (ARD). Starting from ~2 billion shotgun metagenomic reads across 6 rootstock/soil-amendment treatments (`PRJNA779554`), the authors binned 2,233 high-quality metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs, $\ge 90\%$ completeness, $<5\%$ contamination), dereplicated them at 99% ANI into 395 unique MAGs, reconstructed draft Genome-Scale Metabolic Models (GSMMs) without gap-filling via `CarveMe v1.5.1`, and retained 243 stoichiometrically consistent GSMMs after `MEMOTE` filtering. By simulating iterative community growth on 33 literature-derived apple root exudates and 60 inorganic compounds (plus 31 organic phosphorus compounds added at Iteration 4), they connect 84 GSMMs and 203 organic metabolites into a 9,773-edge directed bipartite exchange network and analyze 45,972 3-node (`PM`) and 571,605 5-node (`PMM`) motifs across healthy (`H`, seed-meal amended) vs. sick (`S`, unamended ARD) differential abundance categories. The framework is well-documented and generates concrete, testable biological hypotheses (e.g., 4-hydroxybenzoate $\to$ `GSMM_091` $\to$ catechol), though key algorithmic assumptions in `MCSM` (unbounded `1000 mmol/gDW/h` accumulation of any FVA-feasible secretion and manual injection of 31 organic-P compounds at Iteration 4) and statistical testing on combinatorially expanded path counts warrant careful qualification.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unconstrained Mass-Balance Accumulation and Combinatorial FVA Union in `MCSM`:** In each iteration of `MCSM` (Methods, line 46), each GSMM is evaluated independently using regular Flux Variability Analysis (FVA) with the biomass objective constrained to $\ge 90\%$ of its optimum. Every metabolite with a positive maximum FVA secretion flux is added to the shared medium for the subsequent iteration with an upper uptake bound of **`1000 mmol/gDW/h`**. This design introduces two modeling artifacts:
   - **Mutually Exclusive FVA States & Thermodynamic Loops:** Because FVA computes the extremum of each exchange reaction independently across the 90%-optimal polytope, a single microbe is assumed to simultaneously secrete *all* metabolites that are individually feasible in mutually exclusive flux distributions (including high-value cofactors such as Coenzyme A, FAD, FMN, vitamins, and 11 amino acids), and those secreted compounds become available in unlimited (`1000 mmol/gDW/h`) amounts to all other community members without mass-balance conservation between producer secretion rates and consumer uptake rates. Notably, while the authors compared loopless FVA to regular FVA on a subset of models in static media (`Figure 3—figure supplement 1B`), regular FVA was used in `MCSM`.
   - **Ad-Hoc Addition of 31 Exogenous Organic Phosphorus Compounds at Iteration 4:** Trophic succession on root exudates + inorganic nutrients terminated after Iteration 3 with 71 growing GSMMs (Fig 4B). At the start of Iteration 4, the authors manually injected 31 organic phosphorus compounds (which contain carbon backbones as well as phosphorus) mined from the `COBRApy minimal_medium` dictionaries into the shared medium, enabling 13 additional GSMMs to grow in Iterations 4–5 (reaching 84 GSMMs). Because these 31 organic-P compounds were neither part of the root exudate profile nor secreted by community members in Iterations 1–3, edges and paths arising in Iterations 4–5 reflect exogenous nutrient supplementation rather than endogenous root-driven trophic succession.
2. **Loss of 38.5% of High-Quality MAGs (152/395) at the `MEMOTE` Stoichiometric Consistency Filter:** The 395 dereplicated MAGs met strict quality standards ($\ge 90\%$ completeness, $<5\%$ contamination), yet 152 of the 395 `CarveMe` models failed `MEMOTE` stoichiometric consistency checks and were discarded, leaving 243 GSMMs, of which 84 grew in `MCSM` ($21.3\%$ of the 395 MAGs, where the 395 MAGs collectively mapped $30\text{--}36\%$ of raw metagenomic reads). Because `CarveMe` carves sub-models from a curated universal bacterial reaction database (`BiGG`), the authors should clarify which specific reaction classes caused 38.5% of models to fail stoichiometric consistency when gap-filling was disabled, and whether specific phyla were disproportionately filtered out.
3. **Parametric ANOVA on Highly Skewed, Combinatorially Expanded `PMM` Path Counts (Fig 6A):** In Fig 6A, the authors compare the number of 5-node `PMM` paths initiated by primary consumer GSMMs classified as Healthy (`H`), Sick (`S`), or Non-Associated (`NA`) using a parametric one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post-hoc test (`*** p <= 0.005`). However:
   - Only 27 GSMMs grew in Iteration 1 directly on root exudates (with 84 GSMMs total in the network across all 5 iterations), and the main text does not state the exact sample sizes ($n_H, n_S, n_{NA}$) of GSMMs occupying the first position of the `PMM` paths in Fig 6A.
   - As shown in `Figure 6—figure supplement 1B` and the boxplot outliers in Fig 6A, the number of `PMM` paths per GSMM spans more than two orders of magnitude ($398$ to $50,628$ paths) due to combinatorial multiplication across intermediate metabolites and secondary consumers. Applying parametric ANOVA to combinatorially exploded, heavily skewed degree products can inflate significance driven by a few hub GSMMs; a non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis / Dunn test or permutation test at the GSMM level should be reported to confirm robustness.
4. **Decoupling of Literature Exudate Metabolomics from Treatment-Specific Metagenomes:** The 33 root exudates (Table 1) were compiled from previously published metabolomics studies of G935 and M26 rootstocks grown in sterile sand (Leisso et al., 2017, 2018), whereas the MAGs were assembled from G210 and M26 rootstocks grown in ARD orchard soil with or without Brassicaceae seed-meal amendment. Consequently, the simulated chemical environment is identical for `H` and `S` communities, and the functional differentiation in Figs 5 and 6 is driven entirely by projecting `edgeR` read-mapping differential abundance labels onto the shared network. The authors transparently discuss this limitation in the Conclusions.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Constructing un-gapfilled GSMMs directly from native rhizosphere MAGs and iteratively simulating FVA-based secretion-uptake dynamics (`MCSM`) captures multi-step trophic successions (such as primary exudate consumers enabling secondary growth of *Elusimicrobiota*, *Chlamydiota*, *Fibrobacterota*, *Rhizobiales*, and *Burkholderiales*, as well as multi-step cellulose degradation). — `[Well Supported]`. The qualitative succession patterns and the cellulose degradation benchmark (`Figure 4—figure supplement 3`) demonstrate that `MCSM` recovers biologically coherent metabolic dependencies.
- **Claim 2:** `S`-associated (ARD-conducive) primary consumer GSMMs initiate more `PMM` trophic paths and preferentially utilize phenolic root exudates such as gallic acid and caffeic acid (3,4-dihydroxy-trans-cinnamate), whereas `H`-associated paths are enriched for antifungal/nematicidal secretions (L-sorbose, phenylacetaldehyde) and catechol production (`4-hydroxybenzoate -> GSMM_091 -> catechol`). — `[Moderately Supported]`. Framed appropriately by the authors as computational hypothesis generation supported by independent literature on ARD root phenolics and catecholamine tolerance, while explicitly acknowledging that experimental validation is required.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Exemplary Open Data & Code Availability:** Raw metagenomic reads are available under NCBI BioProject `PRJNA779554`, all 395 MAG FASTA files and all 243 SBML/COBRApy GSMMs, media definitions, and the complete three-stage Python pipeline (`MCSM`, `NETWORK.py`, `PATHS.py` using `COBRApy`, `CPLEX v12.8`, `networkx`) are archived on GitHub/Zenodo (`FreilichLab, 2023a, 2023b`), and comprehensive source data tables (`Figure 2—source data 1–5` through `Figure 6—source data 1–2`) are provided.
