### 1. Executive Summary
This cross-sectional survey study ($N = 5,843$ healthcare workers across four grouped global regions, collected between November 1 and December 5, 2023) evaluates self-reported fear of emerging SARS-CoV-2 variants using the 7-item Fear of COVID-19 Scale (FCS) and vaccine confidence among vaccinated respondents ($n = 4,251$) using the 16-item ARAB-VAX-CONF scale. While the sample size is large and spans multiple countries, a forensic audit reveals severe internal mathematical and statistical contradictions that directly invalidate several headline conclusions in the Abstract and Discussion. Specifically, in Table 3, the crude and adjusted odds ratios for chronic disease are inverted relative to the raw contingency counts ($30.9\%$ extreme fear in those with chronic diseases vs. $48.5\%$ in those without, which yields a true $\text{COR} = 0.475$, yet is reported as $\text{COR} = 2.1$ and $\text{AOR} = 1.2$), leading the authors to claim the exact opposite direction of effect. Multiple confidence intervals in Table 3 are mathematically impossible (e.g., point estimate $\text{AOR} = 1.2$ outside its own 95% CI of $0.9\text{--}1.14$) or miscalculated from the 2x2 counts. Furthermore, the primary outcome dichotomization relies on a cited FCS cutoff of $17.5$ that has an AUC of $0.52$ (sensitivity $55.1\%$, specificity $49.6\%$), which is no better than random guessing.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Inverted Odds Ratio and Reversed Direction of Effect for Chronic Diseases (Table 3):** In Table 3, among respondents with chronic diseases (`Yes`, $n = 2,668$), $1,843$ ($69.1\%$) have normal fear and $825$ ($30.9\%$) have extreme fear. Among those without chronic diseases (`No`, $n = 3,175$, set as the reference category `1`), $1,634$ ($51.5\%$) have normal fear and $1,541$ ($48.5\%$) have extreme fear. Calculating the crude odds ratio of extreme fear for `Yes` vs. `No` yields $\text{COR} = (825 \times 1,634) / (1,843 \times 1,541) = 0.475$ (95% CI: $0.427\text{--}0.528$). However, Table 3 reports $\text{COR} = 2.1\ (1.8\text{--}2.3)$—which is the reciprocal $1 / 0.475 = 2.107$—and $\text{AOR} = 1.2\ (1.1\text{--}1.4)$. While the text on line 155 notes that extreme fear was higher among "those who didn't suffer from any chronic disease (48.5%)", the Abstract, multivariable results, and Discussion all claim that having chronic diseases increases the odds of extreme fear.
2. **Miscalculated and Impossible Confidence Intervals in Tables 3 and 4:**
   - In Table 3 (`Level of monthly income: Just enough`), the reported $\text{AOR}$ is $1.2$ with a 95% CI of $(0.9\text{--}1.14)$; the point estimate lies outside its own confidence interval.
   - In Table 3 (`Eastern Mediterranean region` vs. `Western region`), the raw counts ($1,969$ normal, $512$ extreme fear vs. $598$ normal, $245$ extreme fear) yield $\text{COR} = 0.635$ with $\text{SE}(\ln \text{OR}) = 0.0906$ and an exact 95% CI of $(0.531\text{--}0.758)$, which is highly significant ($p < 0.001$). Yet Table 3 reports $\text{COR} = 0.63\ (0.53\text{--}1.15)$ and $\text{AOR} = 0.61\ (0.55\text{--}1.13)$, with typographical errors in the upper bounds making a strongly protective effect appear non-significant.
   - In Table 3 (`Field of practice: Nursing` vs. `Administrative`), the raw counts ($483$ normal, $488$ extreme fear vs. $177$ normal, $139$ extreme fear) yield $\text{COR} = 1.287$ with a 95% CI of $(0.996\text{--}1.662)$, yet Table 3 reports a 95% CI upper bound of $12.32$ (`1.29 (0.86-12.32)`).
   - In Table 4 (`Number of family members = 4` vs. reference `≥5`), the proportion with "Good" confidence is $584 / 1,340 = 43.6\%$ for family size 4 vs. $457 / 1,434 = 31.9\%$ for family size $\ge 5$. The crude OR for Good vs. Intermediate/Poor confidence is $(584 \times 977) / (756 \times 457) = 1.65$ (95% CI: $1.41\text{--}1.93$), yet Table 4 reports $\text{COR} = 1.02\ (0.65\text{--}1.9)$ and $\text{AOR} = 0.78\ (0.69\text{--}0.89)$, claiming that a family size of 4 is associated with *lower* confidence than a family size of $\ge 5$.
3. **Internal Sample Size and Percentage Discrepancies:**
   - Line 79 states that "$45.7\%$ had been infected themselves. Of those infected ($n = 3,291$), $81.6\%$ reported that their infection occurred over a year ago." However, $3,291 / 5,843 = 56.3\%$, not $45.7\%$ ($45.7\%$ appears to have been duplicated from line 74: "$45.7\%$ had no chronic health problems").
   - Moreover, line 74 states that $45.7\%$ had no chronic health problems, whereas in Table 3, $2,668 / 5,843 = 45.7\%$ are listed as `Chronic diseases: Yes` and $3,175 / 5,843 = 54.3\%$ are listed as `No`.
   - In Table 3, the `Marital status` row counts sum to $5,835$ ($1,969+1,480+1,393+770+114+109$) rather than $5,843$, with no explanation of missing data.
4. **Psychometrically Invalid Dichotomization of the FCS Outcome:** The Methods (line 46) justify dichotomizing the FCS score at $\ge 17.5$ based on a cited validation with a sensitivity of $55.1\%$, specificity of $49.6\%$, and $\text{AUC} = 0.52$. An $\text{AUC}$ of $0.52$ with specificity below $50\%$ represents near-zero discriminative validity (indistinguishable from chance). Analyzing the continuous FCS score via linear mixed-effects regression would avoid this arbitrary and noisy dichotomization.
5. **Unmodeled Hierarchical Clustering and Contradictory Eligibility Criteria:** Data were collected across 19 countries via convenience and snowball sampling, yet the regression models do not account for country-level clustering (e.g., via random intercepts or cluster-robust standard errors). Additionally, line 31 states that printed hard copies were used "to target HCWs who don't have internet access," directly contradicting inclusion criterion (4) on line 36 ("having a smartphone or computer with access to the internet"). Finally, Table 4 displays a 3-category ordinal outcome (Poor, Intermediate, Good) but applies binary stepwise logistic regression without defining the collapsed binary outcome in the Methods.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Having chronic diseases is a significant predictor of increased extreme fear of emerging variants ($\text{AOR} = 1.2$).** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Directly contradicted by the raw data in Table 3, where respondents with chronic diseases had a substantially *lower* rate of extreme fear ($30.9\%$) than those without chronic diseases ($48.5\%$), and the reported $\text{COR} = 2.1$ is the mathematical reciprocal of the true $\text{COR} = 0.475$.
- **Claim 2: $40.5\%$ of HCWs experience "extreme fear" of emerging SARS-CoV-2 variants.** `[Overstated]` — Relies on convenience/snowball sampling and a dichotomization cutoff ($17.5$) whose cited diagnostic performance ($\text{AUC} = 0.52$, specificity $49.6\%$) has no discriminative validity.
- **Claim 3: Neurobiological activation of the amygdala, insula, and prefrontal cortex explains the observed fear and vaccine confidence patterns (Discussion, line 266).** `[Overstated]` — The study collected no neuroimaging, physiological, or cognitive task data; invoking neural substrates in a self-report survey is unsupported speculation.
- **Claim 4: Prior COVID-19 infection ($\text{AOR} = 0.61$) and post-vaccination side effects ($\text{AOR} = 0.63$) are associated with lower confidence in the received vaccine.** `[Moderately Supported]` — The directional association in Table 4 is consistent with the raw proportions ($36.8\%$ vs. $48.9\%$ good confidence for prior infection; $35.2\%$ vs. $51.0\%$ for side effects), though subject to convenience sampling and stepwise selection biases.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** The Data Availability statement claims "All relevant data are within the paper and its Supporting information files," but no raw individual-level de-identified dataset (e.g., CSV/SAV file) or statistical analysis script is provided in a public repository, preventing independent verification and correction of the miscalculated odds ratios and confidence intervals in Tables 3 and 4.
- **Survey Instruments & Parameters:** The two questionnaires (FCS and ARAB-VAX-CONF) and their Cronbach's $\alpha$ values ($0.912$ and $0.802$) are described, and the IRB approval number ($00012098$) is reported. However, the exact entry/exit p-value thresholds for the stepwise logistic regression and the exact coding of the collapsed binary outcome in Table 4 are omitted.
