### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents a rigorous retrospective benchmark of real-time variant frequency nowcasting and short-term (+30 day) forecasting models for SARS-CoV-2 across 8 countries (Australia, Brazil, Japan, South Africa, Trinidad and Tobago, the UK, the USA, and Vietnam, plus Denmark in downsampling experiments) over 24 bi-monthly analysis dates in 2022. The authors compare four fitness/renewal models implemented in `evofr` (Multinomial Logistic Regression [MLR], Piantham et al., Fixed Growth Advantage [FGA], and Growth Advantage Random Walk [GARW]) and a hierarchical multi-country MLR extension against a 7-day moving average naive baseline. The study stands out for its strict enforcement of real-time data availability (`submission_date <= analysis_date`), explicit sensitivity testing of real-time vs. retrospective Nextclade lineage designations, and realistic downsampling experiments that preserve empirical submission delays. Only minor numerical/typographical discrepancies in Table 1 and the text require correction.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Strict Temporal Discipline and Zero Lookahead Leakage:** A major strength of the study design is its faithful reconstruction of real-time surveillance conditions. For each of the 24 semi-monthly analysis dates $T_{\text{obs}}$ in 2022, sequences were filtered strictly by `submission_date <= T_obs` and `collection_date >= T_obs - 90 days`. Furthermore, the authors explicitly evaluate the impact of retrospective vs. contemporary taxonomic definitions (`S9–S17 Figs`) by comparing forecasts built on modern Nextclade clade assignments against those built using the October 2022 real-time Nextclade reference tree during the emergence of clade 22E (BQ.1).
2. **Realistic Downsampling Protocol Preserving Turnaround Time Bottlenecks:** In the UK and Denmark downsampling experiments (100 to 2,000 sequences/week, 5 replicates per threshold), the authors subsample sequences by collection week *prior* to filtering by `submission_date <= T_obs`. This preserves each country's empirical submission delay distribution and recent-week backfill taper, providing a realistic estimate of the sequencing throughput required to bound forecast error ($\sim 1,000$ sequences/week for full saturation; $\sim 200$ sequences/week or $\ge 50$ sequences in the prior 30 days to maintain mean AE $\lesssim 10\%$).
3. **Candid Diagnosis of Probabilistic Under-Coverage (`S8 Fig`):** The authors evaluate 95% posterior latent and posterior predictive intervals using stochastic variational inference in NumPyro, demonstrating that predictive coverage drops below 50% in high-sequencing countries (USA, UK). They accurately diagnose this over-confidence as arising from multinomial sampling over-dispersion (where large $N$ shrinks multinomial variance faster than real-world spatial/clustering noise) and time-varying sublineage fitness within coarse clades (partially mitigated by GARW).
4. **Minor Internal Table 1 and Text Discrepancies:**
   - **Missing cell in Table 1:** Under `30 Lead from date of estimation`, the `Mean Absolute Error` entry for **Brazil – GARW** is blank (`| Brazil | ... | 9.70% |  |`).
   - **Mean AE range at 0-day lead:** Results line 44 states that "mean absolute error increases for the MLR model from 0.4–4.2% at −30 days, to 2.2–8.6% at 0 days," whereas Table 1 reports Japan's 0-day MLR Mean AE as `2.10%` (giving a range of `2.1–8.6%`).
   - **30-day sequence count rounding discrepancy:** Results line 118 reports median 30-day available sequence counts of `173` (South Africa, MAE 7.0%), `31` (Vietnam, MAE 11.6%), and `44` (Trinidad and Tobago, MAE 12.0%), whereas Discussion line 156 cites `170` (MAE 7%), `30` (MAE 11%), and `43` (MAE 12%) for the same quantities.
   - **Nextstrain Clade Nomenclature Typo in Methods:** Methods line 191 refers to "the initial Omicron strain (clade 21L, lineage BA.1)," whereas in Nextstrain nomenclature lineage BA.1 is clade **21K** (as correctly labeled in the Fig 4 caption, line 105) and clade 21L is BA.2.
   - **Unformatted Citation Key:** Discussion line 154 contains an uncompiled BibTeX key: `As observed in Susswein et al. susswein2023leveraging`.
5. **Sensitivity of Absolute Error to Emerging Low-Frequency Variants:** Because $\text{AE}_t^{m,d} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{v \in V} |f_v^d(t) - \hat{f}_v^{m,d}(t)|$ averages absolute frequency differences across all circulating clades, errors during the earliest phase of variant emergence (e.g., when a variant is at $0.5\%$ true frequency vs. $2.5\%$ predicted) contribute very little to total AE compared to errors near $50\%$ prevalence, even though relative growth rate uncertainty is highest during emergence (Fig 4). Discussing relative/logit-scale error or Kullback–Leibler divergence alongside raw frequency AE would provide helpful context for early-warning surveillance applications.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Simple population-genetic fitness models (MLR) perform comparably to more complex renewal-equation models incorporating case counts (FGA, GARW, Piantham), achieving $\sim 0.5\text{--}1.9\%$ median AE and $\sim 5.8\text{--}12.0\%$ mean AE at +30 days and substantially outperforming naive moving-average baselines. — `[Well Supported]`. Demonstrated consistently across 8 countries and 24 bi-monthly evaluation windows.
- **Claim 2:** A sequencing cadence of $\sim 1,000$ sequences/week is sufficient to saturate short-term forecast accuracy, while $\sim 200$ sequences/week (or $\ge 50$ available sequences collected in the preceding 30 days) suffices to keep mean +30 day forecast error below $\sim 10\%$. — `[Well Supported]`. Directly supported by cross-country correlations (Fig 5) and systematic downsampling in both the UK (population 67M) and Denmark (population 6M) (Fig 6).
- **Claim 3:** Hierarchical pooling of variant growth advantages across countries ($\delta_{v,g} \sim \text{Normal}(\bar{\delta}_v, \sigma)$) substantially improves forecast accuracy in lower-surveillance settings (e.g., Trinidad and Tobago, Vietnam) without degrading accuracy in data-rich countries. — `[Well Supported]`. Clearly established in Fig 7 across all forecast horizons from $-30$ to $+30$ days.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Code & Software:** All models are implemented in the open-source `evofr` package using `NumPyro`, and the complete analysis pipeline, figure generation scripts, GISAID accession lists, and derived daily clade/case count matrices are deposited in a public GitHub repository.
- **Mathematical Specification:** Equations for the MLR model, transmission advantage $\Delta_v = \exp(\delta_v \tau)$, hierarchical prior structure, and absolute error metric are clearly and accurately specified.
