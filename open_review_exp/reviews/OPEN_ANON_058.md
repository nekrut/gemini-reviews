### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the transcriptional response of two Brazilian common bean (*Phaseolus vulgaris*) cultivars—resistant `Ouro Vermelho` (OV) and susceptible `BRS Estilo` (E)—to inoculation with *Colletotrichum lindemuthianum* race 65 (isolate Lv134) across three timepoints: 0, 48, and 96 hours after inoculation (hai). Using 12 Illumina NextSeq 500 RNA-seq libraries (2 cultivars $\times$ 3 timepoints $\times$ 2 biological replicates), the authors perform `DESeq2` differential expression analysis across six pairwise contrasts, define intersection sets of putative resistance (`R48`, `R96`) and susceptibility (`S48`, `S96`) candidate genes, conduct `topGO` and `KEGG` pathway enrichment, construct cultivar-specific gene co-expression networks (`igraph`, Pearson $r \ge 0.9$ + Highest Reciprocal Rank), map DEGs within 500 kb of known anthracnose resistance loci (`Co-1`, `Co-4`, `Co-6`), and validate 10 DEGs by RT-qPCR.

While the biological pathosystem is agricultural-economically important and the wet-lab inoculation and RT-qPCR validation are well executed, the transcriptomic experimental design and statistical network analyses suffer from three major methodological flaws: (1) RNA-seq was performed with only $n = 2$ biological replicates per condition and without time-matched mock-inoculated controls at 48 and 96 hai; (2) the six pairwise contrasts omit the baseline `OV 0 hai vs. E 0 hai` comparison, confounding constitutive genetic background differences between two distinct cultivars with pathogen-induced resistance; and (3) constructing genome-wide Pearson co-expression networks ($r \ge 0.9$) separately within each cultivar using only $N = 6$ samples (3 timepoints $\times$ 2 replicates) is statistically invalid and dominated by spurious correlations.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Underpowered Replication ($n = 2$) and Absence of Time-Matched Mock Controls in RNA-Seq (Lines 40, 50, 60–75):**
   - Although the greenhouse phenotypic trial used 3 replicates (Line 33) and included water-inoculated control plants (Line 40), RNA-seq was performed on only **2 biological replicates** per cultivar per timepoint (12 libraries total, Line 50) and included **no mock-inoculated controls at 48 and 96 hai**.
   - During the experiment, inoculated plants were placed in a dark/mist chamber (95% RH, $25^\circ\text{C}$) for the first 48 hours and then transferred to a greenhouse (85% RH, $24^\circ\text{C}$) from 48 to 96 hai (Line 40). Without time-matched mock controls at 48 and 96 hai, the huge number of DEGs detected in Contrast 2 (`OV 48 vs OV 0`: 6,474 DEGs, representing 29% of all 22,344 expressed genes!) and Contrast 3 (`E 48 vs E 0`: 4,855 DEGs) heavily reflects the environmental shift into the 95% RH mist chamber and leaf development rather than pathogen-specific transcriptional reprogramming.
2. **Confounding of Constitutive Cultivar Differences with Induced Resistance in Contrasts 1–6 (Table 1, Lines 63–81):**
   - `Ouro Vermelho` and `BRS Estilo` are two genetically distinct commercial cultivars, not near-isogenic lines (NILs). However, the authors never compute or filter against the baseline contrast `OV 0 hai vs. E 0 hai`.
   - Under the authors' intersection logic (Table 1; Fig. 3C, 3H), any gene that is up-regulated in Contrast 1 (`OV 48 vs E 48`) and down-regulated in Contrast 3 (`E 48 vs E 0`) is classified as a "putative R48-gene" ($n = 108$ genes, Line 130)—even if that gene was **not induced at all** in the resistant cultivar (`OV 48 vs OV 0`) and simply had a higher constitutive baseline expression in `Ouro Vermelho`! A proper factorial generalized linear model in `DESeq2` (`~ Cultivar + Time + Cultivar:Time`) testing the interaction term $\text{Cultivar} \times \text{Time}$ (or explicitly subtracting baseline `OV 0 vs E 0` differences) is required to isolate true pathogen-induced differential responses.
3. **Statistically Invalid Co-Expression Networks Constructed from $N = 6$ Samples per Cultivar (Lines 89–91, 155–170):**
   - In Section "Gene coexpression networks" (Line 90), the authors build separate co-expression networks for the resistant (`Ouro Vermelho`) and susceptible (`BRS Estilo`) genotypes using pairwise Pearson correlations ($r \ge 0.9$) and Highest Reciprocal Rank (HRR). Because each cultivar has only 3 timepoints $\times$ 2 replicates, each network is computed across **only $N = 6$ samples** (comprising just 3 distinct biological states!).
   - With $N = 6$ observations and 3 timepoints, any two genes that share the same directional change at 48 or 96 hai (e.g., low at 0 and 48 hai, high at 96 hai) will automatically exhibit a Pearson correlation $r > 0.90$. Computing Kleinberg hub centrality scores and degrees (100–130 connections) on a 6-sample correlation matrix across thousands of genes merely rediscovers clusters of genes that peak at the same timepoint (e.g., 96 hai) and does not provide valid statistical evidence of gene regulatory network topology.
4. **Inaccurate Reporting of the `DESeq2` Fold-Change Metric (Line 60):**
   - Line 60 states: *"Significance was set at FDR-adjusted p < 0.05 and log2 fold-change >= 2.0 (TPM-based)."* `DESeq2` estimates $\log_2$ fold changes directly from raw read counts using median-of-ratios size factor normalization and negative binomial GLM dispersion shrinkage—not from Transcripts Per Million (TPM). The authors should clarify whether $\log_2\text{FC} \ge 2.0$ was filtered using `DESeq2`'s native count-based `log2FoldChange` column or an external post-hoc TPM ratio.
5. **Single-Reference Housekeeping Gene in RT-qPCR Without Efficiency Correction (Line 96):**
   - RT-qPCR validation normalized $C_q$ values against a single reference gene (`Phvul.011G064500.1`, actin) using simple $\Delta C_q$ without reporting primer amplification efficiencies ($E$) or verifying expression stability of actin across fungal infection timepoints (where cytoskeletal remodeling occurs).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Ouro Vermelho exhibits complete phenotypic resistance (score 1.27) whereas BRS Estilo is highly susceptible (score 8.78, plant death by day 7) to C. lindemuthianum race 65 isolate Lv134.* `[Well Supported]` — Clearly demonstrated by standard visual symptom scoring and Student's $t$-test ($p < 0.001$, Fig. 1).
- **Claim 2:** *Incompatible interaction in Ouro Vermelho involves transcriptional activation of PRRs (LRR-RLKs, malectin-like kinases near Co-4), NBS-LRRs, phenylpropanoid/lignin biosynthesis, lipoxygenases (JA pathway), and cell wall remodeling, whereas compatible interaction in BRS Estilo involves auxin (SAUR) and sugar transporter induction.* `[Moderately Supported]` — The biological pathways identified align well with established hemibiotrophic plant-pathogen biology and RT-qPCR confirmed the directional expression of the 10 tested DEGs; however, the specific lists of "putative R/S genes" require re-filtering with a proper `Cultivar × Time` interaction model to remove constitutive cultivar differences.
- **Claim 3:** *Gene co-expression networks identify master hub regulators (e.g., GDSL-like lipase Phvul.002G106300, matrix metalloproteinase Phvul.002G076400, UDP-glucosyl transferase Phvul.002G096500) driving the resistance and susceptibility networks.* `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Co-expression networks inferred from $N = 6$ samples (3 timepoints) lack statistical degrees of freedom to distinguish genuine regulatory co-expression from simple temporal co-upregulation at 96 hai.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw sequencing reads for all 12 RNA-seq libraries are publicly deposited in NCBI SRA under BioProject `PRJNA1219552`, and the full expression matrix and DEG tables are provided in Supporting Information (`S1 Table`–`S14 Table`).
- **Parameter Transparency:** `Trimmomatic` and `STAR v2.7.10b` command-line parameters, `Primer3Plus` design constraints, and RT-qPCR primer/probe sequences (`S1 Table`) are reported with commendable completeness. Minor typographical errors in the text (e.g., `"preciously mapped"` on Line 174, `"stydy"` on Line 183, `"do producing"` on Line 203, and a duplicated sentence on Line 197) should be cleaned up.
