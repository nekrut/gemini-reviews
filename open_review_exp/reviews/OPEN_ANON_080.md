### 1. Executive Summary
This study combines experimental evolution, molecular microbiology, quantitative competition assays, and mathematical modeling to resolve why antimicrobial resistance (AMR) progressively went extinct in *Escherichia coli* populations evolving with the conjugative IncFII plasmid R1 under regular influx of plasmid-free recipients. By reanalyzing Illumina read-depth profiles across the 99.4 kb R1 sequence and performing repeat 32-day evolution experiments, the authors discover that homologous recombination between flanking direct-repeat `IS1a` and `IS1b` elements spontaneously deletes the entire ~35.6 kb AMR region (including `bla`), generating "streamlined" conjugative plasmids ($s$). Through a rigorous series of vertical competition, horizontal invasion, monoculture growth-rate, and barrier/exclusion assays, they show that streamlined R1 variants alleviate the fitness cost of high plasmid copy number (`copA*`), displace ancestral AMR plasmids vertically (when high-copy) and horizontally (even at wild-type low copy number), and act as a superior long-term barrier (24 h) against AMR plasmid invasion compared to canonical Type II restriction-modification (RM) systems (`EcoRI`, `EcoRV`) via entry exclusion. The experimental execution is outstanding; only a discrepancy between the text description and displayed equations of the ODE model regarding co-infection displacement and a minor mixed-effects degree-of-freedom issue in the growth-rate ANOVA require revision.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Forensic Rigor in Ruling Out *In Vitro* PCR Recombination Artifacts (`S2 Fig`):** When screening colonies with `R1_del` primers flanking the 35.6 kb AMR region, the authors observed a faint ~1 kb deletion band even in ancestral R1 colonies. Rather than misinterpreting this as ubiquitous *in vivo* deletion, they systematically designed three additional primer sets (`R1_del_short`, `R1_del_mid`, `R1_del_long`), tested fresh transconjugants in a homologous-recombination-deficient $\Delta recA::\text{frt}$ background (`S2C Fig`), and varied PCR annealing temperatures and elongation times (`S2D Fig`), conclusively proving that the faint band in full-length R1 was an *in vitro* PCR template-switching/recombination artifact across the `IS1` repeats.
2. **Discrepancy Between the Text Description of Co-Infection Displacement and the Displayed ODE System (Materials and Methods, Lines 46–78):**
   - In Materials and Methods ("Model of plasmid competition", ContentOffset 46080 line 46), the text explicitly states: *"If a plasmid transmits to an already co-infected cell, the incoming variant displaces the other variant with probability 1/2 (this is necessary to ensure structural neutrality [59])."*
   - However, in the displayed ODE system (lines 56–75):
     $$\frac{dN_W}{dt} = N_W[(1 - c)\rho(1 - T/K) - \gamma] + N_0 \lambda_W - (1 - k) N_W \lambda_M + N_{WM} s_M (1 - c)\rho(1 - T/K)$$
     $$\frac{dN_M}{dt} = N_M[(1 - c)\rho(1 - T/K) - \gamma] + N_0 \lambda_M - (1 - k) N_M \lambda_W + N_{WM} s_W (1 - c)\rho(1 - T/K)$$
     $$\frac{dN_{WM}}{dt} = N_{WM}[(1 - s_W - s_M)(1 - c)\rho(1 - T/K) - \gamma] + (1 - k)[N_W \lambda_M + N_M \lambda_W]$$
     the terms corresponding to conjugation of $W$ or $M$ into already co-infected cells $N_{WM}$—namely $+\frac{1}{2}(1 - k)N_{WM}\lambda_W$ into $N_W$, $+\frac{1}{2}(1 - k)N_{WM}\lambda_M$ into $N_M$, and $-\frac{1}{2}(1 - k)N_{WM}(\lambda_W + \lambda_M)$ out of $N_{WM}$—are **missing from the displayed equations**! Without those terms (or the corresponding homologous superinfection terms), the displayed ODE system violates the structural neutrality criterion of Lipsitch et al. [59] when $\beta_W = \beta_M$ and $s_W = s_M$ (because a rare variant $M$ enters $N_{WM}$ at per-capita rate $(1-k)\beta N_W$ and exits to $N_M$ and $N_W$ at equal rates $s_W = s_M$, creating frequency-dependent bias). Additionally, the total cell density $T = N_0 + N_W + N_M + N_{WM}$ appearing in the logistic term $(1 - T/K)$ should be explicitly defined beneath the equation block (especially since $T$ is used earlier on line 32 to denote transconjugant density in $\gamma = T/DRt$). The authors should verify whether the displacement terms were included in the Mathematica notebook (`S2 Data`) and update the displayed equations accordingly.
3. **Pseudoreplication of Technical Plate Wells in Fixed-Effects Growth Rate Linear Model (`df = 518`):** In the growth rate analysis (Results line 64 and `S3 Table`), the authors ran $n = 3$ independent overnight biological cultures per strain (`MG Δlac` and `MG RifR`), each split into 12 technical replicate wells on a 96-well plate, and fit a fixed-effects linear model `lm(exponential growth rate ~ strain * plasmid * rep_nb)` with `518` residual degrees of freedom (`F_{1, 518} = 1,917`, `F_{7, 518} = 443`) followed by `TukeyHSD`. Treating the 12 technical replicate wells per overnight culture as independent error degrees of freedom inflates residual `df` by 12-fold; fitting a linear mixed-effects model (`lmer(growth_rate ~ strain * plasmid + (1 | biological_replicate))`) or performing Tukey contrasts on the $n = 3$ biological replicate means per strain ($n = 6$ total per plasmid) is more statistically appropriate.
4. **Confounding Secondary Mutation in Low-Copy Streamlined Variant $s_{w\_e}$:** As the authors transparently acknowledge in the Discussion (line 128) and `S1 Table`, the low-PCN streamlined variant $s_{w\_e}$ also carries a single-nucleotide G deletion in a polyG tract within the leading region. While all five streamlined variants tested ($s_{w\_e}, s_{w90c}, s_{w90d}, s_{m68c}, s_{m97f}$) invaded faster horizontally than ancestral R1 (Fig 5), constructing a clean engineered `IS1`-mediated deletion on the ancestral R1 backbone would definitively isolate the effect of the 35.6 kb size reduction from the leading-region polyG mutation.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Apparent loss of R1 plasmids during experimental evolution with immigrant plasmid-free hosts is actually driven by the evolution and sweep of streamlined plasmids that spontaneously delete the ~35.6 kb `IS1`-flanked AMR region via homologous recombination. — `[Well Supported]`. Conclusively proved by Illumina coverage profiles (Fig 1B, `S3 Fig`), Sanger sequencing of the `IS1b`/`IS1a` junction, and repeat 32-day evolution experiments screened by non-selective colony PCR (Fig 2).
- **Claim 2:** Deletion of the AMR region alleviates the growth cost of high-copy-number (`copA*`) plasmids (`m97e` vs. $s_{w90d}, s_{m97f}$), and streamlined variants out-compete full-length AMR plasmids vertically (when high-copy) and horizontally. — `[Well Supported]`. Directly demonstrated by growth assays (Fig 3), 8-day intracellular segregation assays (Fig 4), and 8-day horizontal invasion assays with fluorescently/metabolically marked donor, recipient, and competing strains (Fig 5).
- **Claim 3:** Streamlined conjugative plasmids provide a more durable barrier against AMR plasmid invasion over 24 h than plasmid-borne Type II restriction-modification systems (`EcoRI`, `EcoRV`), which suffer from methylation escape once rare transconjugants form. — `[Well Supported]`. Elegantly demonstrated by comparing 1 h vs. 24 h conjugation kinetics in Fig 6A–B.
