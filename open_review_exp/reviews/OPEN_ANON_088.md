### 1. Executive Summary
This study benchmarks the basal microRNA (miRNA) expression profiles of three widely used undifferentiated immortalized neuronal cell lines—mouse hippocampal HT22, mouse neuroblastoma N2A, and human neuroblastoma SH-SY5Y ($n = 3$ independent passages/cultures per line)—against embryonic day 18 (E18) mouse primary hippocampal neurons cultured for 10 days in vitro (DIV10), as well as published adult mouse hippocampal Ago2-immunoprecipitated miRNAs and human post-mortem hippocampal small RNA-seq data. Profiling ~750 miRNAs via the TaqMan OpenArray qPCR platform, the authors detect 310 miRNAs across the four cell types (98 shared by all four), confirm that primary hippocampal neurons express higher levels of mature neuron-enriched miRNAs (`miR-124, miR-128, miR-132, miR-134, miR-218`) and lower levels of proliferative miRNAs (`miR-17/92` cluster) than immortalized lines, and provide a comparative catalogue to guide cell line selection for neuroscience research. While the resource addresses a practical need for experimental neurobiologists, the manuscript contains a major cross-species assay limitation (profiling human SH-SY5Y cells on a rodent-specific TaqMan OpenArray panel), a direct contradiction between the main text and the Figure 4 caption regarding whether HT22 or SH-SY5Y has the highest correlation with primary neurons, questionable cross-platform differential expression between rescaled RNA-seq counts and qPCR Ct values, and several figure/text mislabelings.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Cross-Species Assay Mismatch: Profiling Human SH-SY5Y Cells on a Rodent-Specific TaqMan OpenArray Panel (Section 2.3):** According to Materials and Methods (line 39), all four cell types were assayed using the **OpenArray Rodent microRNA Panel (750 mouse/rat miRNAs, Cat. No. 4461105)**. While primary hippocampal neurons, HT22, and N2A are murine (*Mus musculus*), **SH-SY5Y is a human (*Homo sapiens*) cell line**. Because TaqMan stem-loop RT-qPCR assays rely on exact 3' end complementarity for reverse transcription and sequence-specific internal hydrolysis probes, assaying human RNA on a mouse/rat panel introduces severe species bias: human-specific miRNAs are absent from the panel, human orthologs with 1–2 nucleotide divergences or shifted canonical 3' isomiR ends will fail to amplify or exhibit artificially high Ct values, and rodent-specific probes may cross-react unpredictably. This technical confound is not disclosed when interpreting why SH-SY5Y formed a separate cluster with 61 "unique" miRNAs (Fig 2B–C) or when comparing SH-SY5Y against mouse lines and human RNA-seq tissue (Fig 3C, 3E, 3H).
2. **Direct Numerical Contradiction Between Results Text and Figure 4 Caption on Which Cell Line Best Correlates with Primary Neurons:** A central claim of the Abstract and Results (Section 3.5 header: *"High correlation between miRNA profile in HT22 cells and primary mouse hippocampal neurons"*) is that HT22 had the highest correlation with primary hippocampal neurons. However, the correlation coefficients reported in the main text (line 114) contradict those printed in the Figure 4 caption (line 117):
   - **Main Text (line 114):** Reports HT22 as **`r = 0.735, MSE = 8.515 (Fig 4A)`**, N2A as `r = 0.564, MSE = 13.444 (Fig 4B)`, and SH-SY5Y as **`r = 0.715, MSE = 8.443 (Fig 4C)`**.
   - **Figure 4 Caption (line 117):** Reports HT22 as **`r = 0.700, MSE = 8.515`**, N2A as `r = 0.564, MSE = 13.444`, and SH-SY5Y as **`r = 0.721, MSE = 8.443`**.
   Under the Figure 4 caption values, **SH-SY5Y actually has both a higher Pearson correlation ($r = 0.721 > 0.700$) and a lower mean squared error ($\text{MSE} = 8.443 < 8.515$) than HT22**! Even using the main text values, SH-SY5Y has a lower MSE ($8.443 < 8.515$) than HT22. The authors must reconcile which $r$ values are correct and revise their conclusions accordingly.
3. **Cross-Platform Differential Expression Artifact Between Small RNA-seq and TaqMan qPCR (Sections 2.5 & 3.4, Fig 3H):** To compare SH-SY5Y (profiled by OpenArray qPCR) against post-mortem human hippocampus (profiled by small RNA-seq in an external study [46]), the authors filtered RNA-seq counts ($>10$), transformed $\log\text{CPM}$ via a linear rescaling onto the $[10, 35]$ Ct numeric interval, merged the rescaled RNA-seq values with qPCR Ct values, applied quantile normalization (`qpcrNorm`), and ran `limma` differential expression (Fig 3H). First, the Methods do not clarify whether the linear transformation inverted the sign of $\log\text{CPM}$ (since high $\log\text{CPM}$ means *high* expression whereas high Ct means *low* expression). Second, small RNA-seq and stem-loop RT-qPCR have fundamentally different platform biases, primer/ligation efficiencies, and variance-mean relationships; forcing them onto a synthetic Ct scale via quantile normalization and testing for differential expression with `limma` conflates platform technology differences with biological differences between SH-SY5Y and human brain.
4. **Multiple Text–Figure Mismatches and Nomenclature Errors:**
   - **Figure 2C Mislabeling (line 89):** The Figure 2C caption describes the panel as *"C) Volcano Plot represents overlap between the four cell lines"*, whereas Figure 2C is a **Venn/Euler diagram** showing the overlap of 310 miRNAs across the four cell types (volcano plots appear in Fig 3C–H).
   - **Incorrect miRNA Cluster Membership and Figure Reference (line 96):** Section 3.3 refers to *"the miR-17/92 cluster (miR-27, miR-18 and miR-92) [47] (Fig 2B)"*. `miR-27` is part of the `miR-23/27/24` cluster, not the `miR-17/92` cluster (likely a typo for `miR-17` or `miR-20a`), and the citation to `Fig 2B` (PCA plot) should be `Fig 3B`.
   - **Figure 5 Text vs. Caption Discrepancy (lines 123 & 129):** Section 3.5 states that *"miR-106b and **miR-28** have similar levels only between N2A and neurons (Fig 5C) and miR-16 and **miR-29** have similar expression levels between SH-SY5Y and neurons (Fig 5D)"*, whereas the Figure 5 caption labels these panels *"C) miR-106b and **miR-26**. D) miR-16 and **miR-29b**."*
   - **Minor Percentage Discrepancy (lines 59 vs. 136):** Line 59 reports that **78%** of miRNAs in primary mouse neurons were present in adult mouse hippocampal Ago2-RIP, whereas the Discussion (line 136) states **79%**.
5. **Single-Value Imputation and Normalization Details (Section 2.4):** For miRNAs detected in 2 of 3 biological replicates, the missing third replicate was imputed as the exact mean of the other two replicates prior to `limma` analysis. Imputing a missing value as the group mean artificially reduces the within-group variance by 50% for that miRNA in `limma` (shrinking the standard error and inflating the $t$-statistic). `limma` natively handles `NA` values without requiring mean imputation.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Cultured DIV10 mouse primary hippocampal neurons share ~78–79% of their expressed miRNAs with Ago2-loaded miRNAs in the adult mouse hippocampus and express high levels of canonical neuronal miRNAs (`miR-128, miR-132, miR-134, miR-218`).** `[Well Supported]` The comparison confirms that DIV10 primary hippocampal cultures capture the core mature neuronal miRNA repertoire while immortalized lines under-express several activity- and synapse-related miRNAs (`miR-129a, miR-132, miR-134`).
- **Claim 2: HT22 cells exhibit the highest overall miRNA profile correlation with mouse primary hippocampal neurons among the three tested cell lines.** `[Fragile]` Because the Figure 4 caption reports a higher correlation and lower MSE for SH-SY5Y ($r = 0.721, \text{MSE} = 8.443$) than for HT22 ($r = 0.700, \text{MSE} = 8.515$), this claim depends on resolving the numerical discrepancy between the text and Figure 4.
- **Claim 3: Cross-species differences between human SH-SY5Y and mouse lines are minor compared to cellular function.** `[Overstated]` Because human SH-SY5Y cells were profiled on a rodent OpenArray panel that cannot detect human-specific or sequence-divergent human miRNAs, the study is technically blind to human-specific miRNA divergence.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw OpenArray Ct data are included in the Supporting Information (`Open array Cells file`), and external human/mouse reference datasets are cited.
- **Assay & Protocol Reporting:** Cell culture conditions, seeding densities, mycoplasma testing, and TaqMan OpenArray catalog numbers (`4461105`) and quality thresholds (`AmpScore >= 1.24`, `Cqconf >= 0.8`, `10 < Ct < 35`) are well documented.
- **Code Availability:** R analysis scripts (`qpcrNorm`, `limma`, and the custom binomial bootstrap script in `S1 Methods`) are not deposited in a public code repository, and exact accession numbers for the re-analyzed human post-mortem hippocampal small RNA-seq dataset [46] should be stated explicitly in Section 2.5.
