### 1. Executive Summary
This study evaluates the cross-species reliability of three widely used subcellular localization prediction algorithms—TargetP 2.0, Localizer 1.0.4, and WoLF PSORT (WPS v0.2)—across photosynthetic eukaryotes. First, the authors compare *in silico* predicted plastid and mitochondrial proteomes against experimentally verified (*in vivo* mass spectrometry/tagging) organelle proteomes in four representative Chloroplastida species spanning eudicots (*Arabidopsis thaliana*), monocots (*Zea mays*), bryophytes (*Physcomitrium patens*), and chlorophyte algae (*Chlamydomonas reinhardtii*). Second, they evaluate TargetP 2.0 across 171 photosynthetic eukaryotes using OrthoFinder orthology-inferred organelle proteomes as a reference. The analysis demonstrates that prediction performance degrades sharply outside model angiosperms—especially for mitochondria and dual-targeted proteins—and links cross-organelle mispredictions to evolutionary shifts in N-terminal targeting sequence (NTS) net charge and serine/threonine content. While the biological insights into organelle targeting evolution and angiosperm training bias are compelling, the quantitative precision estimates in non-model species are confounded by incomplete experimental proteome coverage in moss/algae and circularity in the orthology-inferred ground truth across 171 species.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Confounding of Algorithmic "False Positives" with Incomplete Experimental Mass Spectrometry Coverage in Non-Model Species:** In the Methods section ("Evaluation of algorithms," line 131), the authors define any protein predicted by an algorithm to be organellar but absent from the published experimental organelle proteome as a **False Positive (FP)**, and calculate $\text{Precision} = \text{TP} / (\text{TP} + \text{FP})$. While *Arabidopsis thaliana* has undergone decades of exhaustive sub-organellar proteomics and fluorescent tagging, experimental organelle proteomes for *Physcomitrium patens*, *Zea mays*, and *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii* are derived from far fewer studies and capture only a fraction of the true *in vivo* organelle proteome (especially low-abundance or conditionally expressed mitochondrial proteins). Treating unmeasured true organellar proteins in under-sampled species as algorithmic "False Positives" systematically underestimates precision in bryophytes and algae relative to *Arabidopsis*. Sensitivity ($\text{TP} / (\text{TP} + \text{FN})$), which conditions on experimentally verified organelle proteins, is far less biased by proteome completeness and shows a much more moderate decline across species (Figure 2C–D) than Precision does.
2. **Circularity in the 171-Species Orthology-Inferred Benchmark (Figure 3):** To benchmark TargetP 2.0 across 171 photosynthetic eukaryotes lacking experimental organelle proteomes (lines 51–58, Figure 3), the authors define the reference organelle proteome of each species by sequence-based orthology (OrthoFinder protein family clustering) to the experimentally verified organelle proteomes. However, as the authors themselves demonstrate in Figure 5, over half of organelle proteins in a given species belong to species- or clade-specific "pan-proteome" families that lack orthologs in distant lineages. Because the experimental reference set is heavily anchored by *Arabidopsis*/angiosperms, any true lineage-specific plastid or mitochondrial protein predicted by TargetP in an alga or bryophyte will lack an experimental angiosperm ortholog and be scored as a "False Positive" in Figure 3! Thus, part of the negative slope in Figure 3C reflects the decay of orthology conservation with evolutionary distance rather than purely algorithmic failure.
3. **Omission of Specificity Despite Claims in the Abstract and Figure 2 Caption:** Both the Abstract (line 8: *"Specificity, sensitivity and precision analyses highlight cross-organelle errors"*) and the Figure 2 caption (line 45: *"Sensitivity, specificity and precision of individual algorithms and their combinations..."*) state that **specificity** ($\text{TN} / (\text{TN} + \text{FP})$) was analyzed. However, the Methods section (lines 131–147) and Figure 2C–D only compute and plot **Sensitivity** and **Precision**; specificity is neither defined nor reported.
4. **Exclusion of TargetP 2.0 "Thylakoid" Predictions in Main Figure 2A/C:** TargetP 2.0 distinguishes stromal chloroplast transit peptides (`cTP`) from luminal thylakoid transfer peptides (`luTP`). In the main plastid evaluation (Figure 2A/C), the authors excluded ~100 proteins predicted by TargetP 2.0 as `thylakoid` from the predicted plastid set (relegating the combined `chloroplast + thylakoid` analysis to S4 Fig, which is also mis-cited in line 149 as *"compared to Fig 1A"* instead of Fig 2A). Because experimental whole-plastid proteomes include thylakoid proteins, excluding `luTP` predictions in the main text unfairly penalizes TargetP 2.0.
5. **Missing Statistical Reporting and Phylogenetic Non-Independence in Figure 3C:** For the linear regression of TargetP precision against cophenetic evolutionary distance across 171 species (Figure 3C, line 52 & 164), the text does not report the regression slope, $R^2$, Pearson/Spearman $r$, or $p$-value, nor does it account for phylogenetic autocorrelation among the 171 species (e.g., via phylogenetic independent contrasts or PGLS), where densely sampled eudicot clades form a non-independent cluster of points.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Current localization predictors (TargetP 2.0, Localizer, WoLF PSORT) are trained overwhelmingly on eudicot (plastid) and metazoan/fungal (mitochondrial) sequences, causing systematic cross-organelle mispredictions when applied to bryophytes and green algae.* `[Well Supported]` — Figure 1A, S5 Fig, and the physicochemical analysis of the first 20 N-terminal residues (Figure 4C–D) convincingly show that angiosperm pNTS/mNTS divergence (higher net positive charge in mNTS, higher Ser/Thr content in pNTS) is less pronounced in early-branching plants and algae, causing angiosperm-trained classifiers to mis-sort organellar cargo.
- **Claim 2:** *All three algorithms fail to reliably identify dual-targeted (plastid + mitochondrial) proteins.* `[Well Supported]` — Figure 4E–F and S7–S8 Figs demonstrate negligible overlap between *in silico* dual-targeting predictions and experimentally verified dual-localized proteins across all tested tools.
- **Claim 3:** *The match between predictions and experimental data drops as low as 2% and borders on random sampling for plant mitochondria as evolutionary distance increases.* `[Moderately Supported]` — While WoLF PSORT performs near random baseline and mitochondrial prediction is substantially worse than plastid prediction across all tools, the apparent drop in TargetP precision to <10% in distant species is partially inflated by incomplete experimental mitochondrial proteome references in algae/moss and orthology ascertainment bias in the 171-species analysis.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Code Availability:** Very good. All custom Python and R scripts (`Algorithm_predicted_proteins.py`, `Sensitivity_precision.py`, `charge_phospho.py`, `cophenetic_distance.r`), supplementary tables, and source data are publicly archived on Zenodo (`13924211`).
- **Parameter & Version Transparency:** Exact software versions and flags (`TargetP 2.0 -pl`, `Localizer 1.0.4 -p`, `WoLF PSORT 0.2 plant`, `OrthoFinder v2.5.4`) and probability cutoffs for dual targeting ($>0.35$ for both organelles in TargetP 2.0) are clearly documented.
