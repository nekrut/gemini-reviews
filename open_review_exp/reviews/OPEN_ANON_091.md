### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript introduces Dropout Augmentation (DA), a training-time regularization technique that injects synthetic Bernoulli zero-masking into single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) expression matrices, and DAZZLE, a variational autoencoder (VAE)-based structural equation model (SEM) for unsupervised gene regulatory network (GRN) inference building on DeepSEM. The authors benchmark single-run (1x) and 10-run ensemble (10x) versions of DAZZLE against DeepSEM, GENIE3, GRNBoost2, and PIDC across seven BEELINE scRNA-seq datasets (hESC, hHep, mDC, mESC, mHSC-E, mHSC-GM, mHSC-L; 383–1,071 cells and 692–1,620 genes) evaluated against three reference networks (STRING, non-cell-type-specific ChIP-Seq, and cell-type-specific ChIP-Seq), and present a scalability/biological case study on mouse microglia across five lifespan stages (GSE121654; 49,972 cells and ~13,929–15,673 genes). The engineering improvements—particularly delaying the $L_1$ sparsity penalty by 5 epochs to prevent early collapse of the adjacency matrix $A$, simplifying the prior to a closed-form Normal distribution, and regularizing training trajectories against late-epoch degradation via DA—are practical and well-demonstrated. However, the mathematical formulation contains an inverted ratio in the AUPRC Ratio equation, an autoencoding objective that reconstructs corrupted data $X'$ rather than clean data $X$, unablated label contamination in the auxiliary dropout classifier, and a biological case study that conflates co-expressed non-transcription-factor marker genes with causal transcriptional regulators.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Inverted Fraction in Equation 12 (AUPRC Ratio Definition):** In Section 3.4 (lines 296–300), the text correctly states that the AUPRC Ratio is computed by *dividing* the discretized AUPRC score by the expected AUPRC of a random predictor ($\text{Positives} / \text{Total Instances}$). However, Equation 12 writes:
   $$\text{AUPRC Ratio} \approx \frac{\text{Positives}}{\text{Total Instances}} \cdot \sum_{n=1}^{N} P_n \cdot (R_n - R_{n-1})$$
   which *multiplies* by the positive class prevalence instead of dividing by it (the factor should be $\frac{\text{Total Instances}}{\text{Positives}}$).
2. **Reconstruction of Corrupted Input $X'$ vs. Denoising Reconstruction of $X$ (Equations 4–10):** In Section 3.2, Equations 4–7 formulate the SEM and ELBO entirely in terms of the dropout-augmented matrix $X'$, so that the decoder $p_\theta(X'|Z')$ is trained to reconstruct the synthetically zeroed-out matrix $X'$ rather than the uncorrupted observed expression matrix $X$ (as a standard denoising autoencoder would). Moreover, Equation 10 reverts to unprimed notation ($X$ and $Z$) and introduces hyperparameter $\gamma$ for $L_{\text{BCE}}$, contradicting line 256 which names the scaling hyperparameter $\chi$. The authors should clarify whether the reconstruction loss in code is evaluated against $X$ or $X'$ and harmonize the notation across Equations 4–10.
3. **Label Contamination and Missing Ablation for the Auxiliary Noise Classifier $C_{\text{DA}}$:** In Equations 8–9, the 3-layer MLP classifier $C_{\text{DA}}(Z')$ is trained via binary cross-entropy against the synthetic mask $M_{\text{DA}}$. However, scRNA-seq matrices $X$ already contain 57–92% zeros (comprising both true biological zeros and pre-existing technical dropouts), all of which are assigned $M_{\text{DA}} = 0$ (negative class), while genes that were already zero in $X$ and randomly selected by $M_{\text{DA}} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$ receive $M_{\text{DA}} = 1$ despite undergoing no change ($E_{ij} = 0$). Furthermore, while Sections 2.3 and 2.4 isolate the effects of DA probability $p$ and delayed $L_1$ regularization, there is no ablation isolating the contribution of the auxiliary classifier $C_{\text{DA}}$ ($\gamma > 0$ vs. $\gamma = 0$) or comparing closed-form prior/single-optimizer changes independently.
4. **Near-Random Performance on Cell-Type-Specific ChIP-Seq Networks (Table 1):** Across all seven BEELINE datasets, AUPRC Ratios on the cell-type-specific ChIP-Seq ground truth range from 0.95 to 1.10 (with standard deviations of 0.00–0.03), where 1.00 is random guessing. While the authors candidly note this limitation in Section 2.2 (line 114), claiming superiority in cell-type-specific GRN recovery when all methods perform at the random baseline requires tempering.
5. **Ad-Hoc Edge Thresholding and Cross-Sectional Slicing in the Microglia Case Study:** In Section 2.5 (line 149), edges in the ~15,000-gene microglial networks are extracted using a fixed arbitrary weight threshold ($|A_{ij}| > 0.001$) across five time points with differing cell counts (7,704 to 13,316 cells) and gene counts (13,929 to 15,673 genes), without false discovery rate calibration or permutation testing to account for network size and sample size differences across developmental stages.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *DAZZLE stabilizes GRN inference relative to DeepSEM by delaying $L_1$ sparsity regularization and applying Dropout Augmentation, achieving comparable accuracy in a single run (1x) to 10-run ensembles while reducing parameter count by 21.7% and runtime by 50.8%.* `[Well Supported]` — Figure 3 clearly demonstrates that immediate $L_1$ regularization in DeepSEM-1x causes bimodal convergence where adjacency weights prematurely collapse to zero, and delaying the sparse loss by 5 iterations resolves this instability.
- **Claim 2:** *Dropout augmentation prevents overfitting to dropout noise and maintains AUPRC stability across extended training iterations.* `[Moderately Supported]` — Figure 2 and S1 Fig show improved post-peak stability on several datasets (e.g., hESC, hHep), though the optimal DA rate varies substantially across datasets (0% to 20%) and degrades accuracy or convergence speed in mHSC lineages at higher rates.
- **Claim 3:** *DAZZLE identifies biologically meaningful transcriptional regulators of microglial identity and aging (Tmem119, Apoe) in the Hammond dataset.* `[Overstated]` — In Section 2.5 (lines 161–167) and Figure 4b, the authors interpret genes such as *C1qc*, *Cd81*, *Cx3cr1*, *Hexb*, *Lgmn*, *P2ry12*, *Selplg*, *Ftl1*, *Tmsb4x*, and *Itm2b* as "predicted regulators" of *Tmem119* and *Apoe*. Biologically, these are canonical microglial surface receptors, complement components, lysosomal enzymes, and iron-storage/cytoskeletal proteins—not transcription factors. Because DAZZLE fits an unconstrained all-by-all adjacency matrix $A$ on all ~15,000 genes without restricting sources to transcription factors, it captures strong co-expression modules among cell-type marker genes rather than direct cis/trans transcriptional regulation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Excellent. All BEELINE source GEO accessions (GSE81252, GSE75748, GSE98664, GSE48968, GSE81682) and the Hammond mouse microglia GEO accession (GSE121654) are explicitly provided, along with a Zenodo archive (`15762315`) containing processed data and a source code repository.
- **Parameter & Reporting Completeness:** Basic hyperparameters (learning rates $1 \times 10^{-4}$ for NN and $2 \times 10^{-5}$ for $A$, batch size 64, 120 iterations, 5-step $L_1$ delay, 10% default DA probability) are reported, though exact values of $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ in Equation 10 for the microglia analysis vs. BEELINE benchmarks should be explicitly tabulated in the main Methods section alongside Equation 12's typographical correction.
