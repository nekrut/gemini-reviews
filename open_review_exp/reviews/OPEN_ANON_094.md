# Blinded Review: OPEN_ANON_094
**Composite Score:** 7.1 / 10.0 (Rigor: 2.2/3.5 | Claims: 1.7/2.5 | Biology: 1.6/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.6/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Technical/Assay
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Hybrid Computational + Wet-Lab
**Technology Maturity:** Mid-Stage High-Throughput
**Epistemic Stage:** Translational/Preclinical/Biomarker

### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the role of endogenous hydrogen sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) biogenesis (via CBS, CTH, and 3-MST) and pharmacological supplementation with slow-releasing $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ donors (GYY4137 and Na-GYY4137) during SARS-CoV-2 infection. Using VeroE6, HEK-ACE2, and Calu-3 cell lines infected with HK and Delta variants, human nasopharyngeal swabs, shRNA knockdowns (CTH, CBS, 3-MST, Keap1), genetically encoded subcellular redox biosensors (cyto- and mito-Grx1-roGFP2), bulk RNA-seq, untargeted LC-MS metabolomics, Seahorse XF extracellular flux assays, and *in vivo* challenge models in BALB/c mice (MA-10 variant) and Syrian hamsters (Delta/US isolate) with unrestrained whole-body plethysmography (uWBP), the authors report that SARS-CoV-2 suppresses endogenous $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ production and that GYY4137 inhibits viral replication while restoring Nrf2/Keap1 signaling, redox balance, and mitochondrial respiration. While the multi-modal experimental scope is broad and the *in vitro*/*in vivo* viral suppression phenotypes are consistent across multiple models, the mechanistic link between the transcriptomic/antiviral assays and the metabolomic/bioenergetic assays is undermined by an unexplained 500-fold drug concentration discrepancy (5 mM vs. 10 µM GYY4137), pseudoreplicated metabolomics ($n = 1$ biological experiment with 3 technical replicates), and an inconsistent double-infection protocol in the plethysmography cohort.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unexplained 500-Fold Discrepancy in GYY4137 Concentration Across Mechanistic Assays (5 mM vs. 10 µM):** In all antiviral plaque/RT-qPCR assays (Figure 2E–F), redox biosensor experiments (Figure 5D), Keap1 shRNA epistasis assays (Figure 5G–H), and bulk RNA-seq experiments (Figure 3), cells are treated with **5 mM** (5,000 µM) GYY4137 or Na-GYY4137 (which releases ~600–700 µM $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ over 48 h; Figure 2D). However, in the untargeted LC-MS metabolomics (Figure 4, line 85) and Seahorse XF mitochondrial flux analysis (Figure 5E, line 110), cells are treated with **10 µM** GYY4137—a 500-fold lower dose (which would release only ~1–1.4 µM $\text{H}_2\text{S}$)—and infected at MOI 0.1 for 24 h instead of MOI 0.01 for 48 h. No antiviral dose-response curve is presented showing whether 10 µM GYY4137 suppresses SARS-CoV-2 replication at all, nor is any rationale given for integrating 5 mM RNA-seq data with 10 µM metabolomics and Seahorse data.
2. **Pseudoreplication and Lenient Thresholding in Untargeted Metabolomics (Figure 4):** The Figure 4 caption (line 91) explicitly states: *"Results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation of data from single experiment, performed in three technical replicates. * p < 0.05; ** p < 0.01; *** p < 0.001; **** p < 0.0001, by student's t-test or one-way ANOVA"*, and the main text (lines 85, 93, 99) defines significantly altered metabolites using an unadjusted $p < 0.1$ threshold. Performing parametric $t$-tests/ANOVAs across three technical LC-MS injections from a single biological experiment ($N = 1$ biological replicate) constitutes technical pseudoreplication and does not measure biological variance.
3. **Inconsistent Double-Infection Protocol in Whole-Body Plethysmography (uWBP) Mice:** In the standard mouse efficacy experiment (Figure 6A–E; Methods line 126 of part 2), 10–12-week-old female BALB/c mice are infected once intranasally with $5 \times 10^4$ PFU of SARS-CoV-2 MA-10. However, in the uWBP experiment (Methods line 130 of part 2), 18-week-old female BALB/c mice are infected with $10^5$ PFU of MA-10 and then given *"A second dose of infection... 48 h after the first dose"*, while Na-GYY4137 dosing ends at 24 h post-first-infection (prior to the second viral challenge). No explanation is provided for why animals were re-challenged at 48 h during plethysmography or how intranasal fluid instillation at 48 h affected respiratory pause parameters (Penh, EEP, RT). Additionally, Penh in unrestrained whole-body plethysmography is a known indicator of breathing pattern/nasal conditioning rather than a direct measure of lower airway resistance.
4. **Confounded Chemical Epistasis in the DTT Co-Treatment Assay (Figure 5H):** In Figure 5H (line 116), the authors co-treat VeroE6 cells with **10 µM DTT** and **5 mM GYY4137** (a 500:1 molar excess of GYY4137 over DTT) and interpret the partial reduction in GYY4137's antiviral activity as specific reversal of Keap1 disulfide/persulfide formation. Thermodynamically, 10 µM DTT cannot stoichiometrically reduce protein disulfides or persulfides in the presence of 600–700 µM continuous $\text{H}_2\text{S}$/persulfide generation from 5 mM GYY4137, suggesting either direct chemical reaction in the pre-incubation media or off-target effects on viral surface proteins during inoculation.
5. **Under-Specified Clinical Swab Cohort (Figure 1H):** The comparison of *cbs*, *cth*, and *mst* mRNA in nasopharyngeal swabs from COVID-19-positive versus COVID-19-negative respiratory distress patients reports only "$n > 10$" without exact sample sizes, patient age/sex demographics, symptom duration, or swab cellularity/RNA quality normalization controls.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *SARS-CoV-2 infection downregulates endogenous $\text{H}_2\text{S}$-producing enzymes (CBS, CTH, 3-MST) in cultured cells, and genetic/chemical depletion of CTH enhances viral replication.* `[Well Supported]` — Time- and MOI-dependent suppression of *cbs*, *cth*, and *mst* transcripts and proteins across VeroE6, Calu-3, and HEK-ACE2 cells, reduced lead-acetate $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ production, and increased viral titers upon CTH shRNA knockdown and PAG inhibition (Figures 1–2) provide solid converging evidence.
- **Claim 2:** *Slow-releasing $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ donors (GYY4137 and Na-GYY4137) suppress SARS-CoV-2 replication in vitro and reduce lung viral load and histopathology in mouse and hamster models.* `[Well Supported]` — Both morpholine-salt GYY4137 and sodium-salt Na-GYY4137 consistently reduce viral RNA and infectious plaque titers across three cell lines, two viral variants, BALB/c mice (MA-10), and Syrian hamsters.
- **Claim 3:** *GYY4137 blocks SARS-CoV-2 replication by restoring steady-state TCA/redox metabolites and potentiating mitochondrial OXPHOS via the Keap1/Nrf2 pathway.* `[Moderately Supported]` — While the Grx1-roGFP2 biosensor, RNA-seq Nrf2 regulon induction, and shKeap1 knockdown (Figure 5G) support involvement of the Keap1/Nrf2 axis at 5 mM GYY4137, the metabolomics (Figure 4) and Seahorse OXPHOS restoration (Figure 5E) were performed at 10 µM GYY4137 (and the metabolomics lacks biological replicates), leaving a gap between the concentrations used for bioenergetic/metabolic profiling and those required for antiviral activity.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Code Availability:** Bulk RNA-seq data are deposited in NCBI GEO (`GSE283665`), and DGE/pathway analysis scripts are hosted on GitHub. However, raw mass spectrometry files for the untargeted LC-MS metabolomics experiment (Figure 4) do not have a public repository accession (e.g., MetaboLights or Metabolomics Workbench).
- **Reagent & Protocol Transparency:** Virus BEI catalog numbers (`NR-52282`, `NR-55671`, `NR-55329`), antibody clone numbers, shRNA TRC identifiers (S1 Table), and RT-qPCR primers (S2 Table) are thoroughly documented. Clarifying the 5 mM vs. 10 µM GYY4137 dose discrepancy and providing biological replicates and raw data deposition for the metabolomics assay are essential for full reproducibility.
