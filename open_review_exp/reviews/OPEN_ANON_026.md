### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript presents a formulaic genome-wide gene family characterization of basic leucine zipper (`bZIP`) transcription factors in *Zanthoxylum bungeanum* (`Zb`, an allotetraploid Rutaceae crop, $2n = 4x$). Using `BLASTp` and `Pfam` (`PF00170`) searches via `TBtools`, the authors identify 275 putative `ZbbZIP` genes across 50 chromosomes, classify them into 13 subfamilies against *Arabidopsis thaliana*, and run a standard suite of web-server/point-and-click bioinformatic analyses (MEME motifs, PlantCARE promoter *cis*-elements, Cell-PLoc 2.0 localization, CodonW codon usage bias, MCScanX synteny, STRING homology-transferred PPI, eggNOG GO/KEGG enrichment, and re-analysis of three public abiotic stress RNA-seq datasets [`PRJNA597398`, `PRJNA1107841`, `PRJNA784034`]), followed by qRT-PCR of 4 candidate genes under drought, cold, and salt treatments. Beyond adhering to a low-depth commodity bioinformatics template, the manuscript exhibits severe methodological errors, internal numerical and unit contradictions (e.g., comparing drought RNA-seq measured in **days** [0, 3, 9, 15 d] against qRT-PCR reported in **hours** [9 h], or mislabeling cold stress hours as days), a reversed definition of Effective Number of Codons ($N_c$) vs. gene expression, unadjusted FPKM thresholding without differential expression statistics, and contradictory qRT-PCR vs. RNA-seq trends.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Severe Time-Scale, Unit, and Experimental Design Contradictions in Stress Expression and qRT-PCR Validation (Methods vs. Results vs. Fig 7):**
   - **Drought Stress Days vs. Hours:** In the Methods (line 62), the authors state that "Drought stress was simulated by ceasing irrigatio [sic], and leaves were collected at 0, 3, 9, and 15 d." Yet in the Results (line 158), when comparing RNA-seq (`PRJNA784034`) to their qRT-PCR data (Fig 7), they write: "Both expression profiles demonstrated downregulated expression of core genes subjected to drought stress. The expression of the core genes determined by qPCR increased at **9 h** after drought treatment." Withholding irrigation in potted 1-year-old woody trees for 9 *hours* does not induce physiological soil drought, and directly comparing a 9-hour qRT-PCR timepoint (or mislabeling 9 days as 9 hours) against a multi-day RNA-seq time course invalidates the comparison. Even more concerning, the sentence "Both expression profiles demonstrated downregulated expression... The expression of the core genes determined by qPCR increased at 9 h" directly contradicts itself within two consecutive sentences!
   - **Cold Stress Hours vs. Days:** In the Methods (line 62), cold treatment at $4\,^\circ\text{C}$ is sampled at `0, 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 h`. Yet in the Results (line 142), the authors state that cold stress expression groups showed peaks during "late-phase (12–24 **d**) and early-phase (0–1 **d**) stress treatments."
   - **Lack of Concordance Between RNA-seq and qRT-PCR:** As the authors themselves admit on line 158 ("there were differences in the overall expression trends between the two methods. This error may have been caused by the plant materials and experimental methods used"), the qRT-PCR curves in Fig 7 disagree with the RNA-seq FPKM bars because the public RNA-seq datasets (`PRJNA597398`, `PRJNA1107841`, `PRJNA784034`) were generated by three different external studies using different cultivars, developmental stages, and stress protocols.
2. **Inverted Definition of Effective Number of Codons ($N_c$) in Optimal Codon Identification:** In the Methods ("Codon usage pattern and synteny analysis", line 52), the authors state: "The top 10% of genes with NC values were classified into the low-expression group, and the bottom 10% were classified into the high-expression group." While lower $N_c$ (closer to 20) indicates stronger codon bias, using $N_c$ of a single transcription factor family (275 genes) as a proxy for "high expression" and "low expression" when actual empirical RNA-seq expression data (`FPKM`) are available in the very same paper is a misapplication of Wright's (1990) method (which compares ribosomal/highly expressed housekeeping genes against low-expressed genes genome-wide). Worse, partitioning 275 genes by their own $N_c$ extreme tails (~27 genes per tail) and defining $\Delta\text{RSCU} > 0$ as "optimal codons" is circular: it merely identifies codons enriched in the subset of `ZbbZIP` genes with the lowest $N_c$ (highest AT3 compositional bias), rather than translationally optimal codons driven by tRNA abundance. Indeed, on line 122, the authors note that `CAI`, `CBI`, and `Fop` are *negatively* correlated with `T3s` and *positively* correlated with `C3s`, which proves that C-ending codons are the translationally optimal ones (`CAI`/`Fop`), directly contradicting their conclusion that T-ending codons are favored by natural selection for expression potential!
3. **Absence of Differential Expression Statistics and Arbitrary `FPKM > 10` Filtering:** To analyze stress transcriptomes across three distinct BioProjects (`PRJNA597398`, `PRJNA1107841`, `PRJNA784034`), the authors simply filtered for genes with `FPKM > 10` ("Genes with FPKM > 10 were defined as the high marker genes for analysis") and plotted unadjusted FPKM heatmaps (Fig 5). No formal differential expression analysis (`DESeq2`, `edgeR`, fold-change thresholds, or Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-adjusted $p$-values comparing treated vs. 0h controls) was performed, and no cross-batch normalization was applied before claiming that salt stress globally "inhibited" `ZbbZIP` expression compared to drought and cold stress (which could simply reflect lower library sequencing depth or tissue differences in `PRJNA1107841`).
4. **Internal Numerical Inconsistencies:**
   - **Total Gene Count:** The total number of `ZbbZIP` genes is stated as `275` throughout the paper, but on line 82 the isoelectric point fraction is reported as `63.4% (175/276)` (where the denominator suddenly becomes `276`, and $175/275 = 63.64\%$, $175/276 = 63.41\%$).
   - **Subcellular Localization Percentage:** On line 82, nuclear localization is reported as `92.63% (252)`, with `23` genes in other compartments ($252 + 23 = 275$). However, $252 / 275 = 91.64\%$, not `92.63%`!
   - **Exon Range Contradiction:** On line 106, the authors state: "Analysis of the gene structure revealed that the number of ZbbZIP exons ranged from **2 to 12**... However, Subfamily S had the lowest abundance of exons, with **only one**." If Subfamily S has 1 exon, the range cannot be 2 to 12.
   - **Incomplete Identification Criteria:** In the Methods (line 32), `BLASTp` ($E < 10^{-5}$) and `PfamScan` (`PF00170`) are used, without checking `SMART`/`CDD` for completeness of the basic region and leucine heptad repeats or removing splice isoforms/allelic haplotigs in the highly heterozygous/allotetraploid `PRJNA524242` assembly.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: The *Z. bungeanum* genome contains 275 `ZbbZIP` genes across 13 subfamilies expanded primarily by WGD/segmental duplication (76%) with zero tandem duplications.** `[Moderately Supported]` — The polyploid expansion aligns with the known allotetraploid history of *Z. bungeanum*, though the complete absence of tandem duplications and the inflation to 275 members (~3.5$\times$ *Arabidopsis* and ~5.5$\times$ closely related *Citrus sinensis* [50 members]) likely partly reflects uncollapsed homologous/homeologous contigs in the assembly.
- **Claim 2: Codon usage in `ZbbZIPs` is driven by natural selection favoring T-ending optimal codons.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Directly contradicted by the authors' own Fig 4B showing that `CAI`, `CBI`, and `Fop` correlate positively with `C3s` and negatively with `T3s`; the T3 preference reflects mutational AT-bias typical of dicot coding sequences, and the $N_c$-based $\Delta\text{RSCU}$ analysis is circular.
- **Claim 3: *Z. bungeanum* prioritizes drought and cold adaptation over salt tolerance because fewer `ZbbZIPs` have FPKM > 10 under salt stress.** `[Overstated]` — Comparing raw FPKM counts across three independent public BioProjects without batch correction or differential expression testing against matched controls cannot support evolutionary claims about stress prioritization.
- **Claim 4: Four core genes (`EVM0033673.1`, `EVM0081289.1`, `EVM0001090.1`, `EVM0023876.1`) significantly contribute to abiotic stress responses via ABA signaling.** `[Fragile]` — Identified purely by homology to *Arabidopsis* `HYH`/`DPBF`/`ABF` and `FPKM > 10` thresholds, with qRT-PCR profiles that conflict with the RNA-seq trajectories and lack any functional genetic validation.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Public NCBI BioProject accessions (`PRJNA524242`, `PRJNA597398`, `PRJNA1107841`, `PRJNA784034`) and supplementary tables (`S1–S4 Tables`) are referenced.
- **Methodological Transparency Gaps:** The Statistical Analysis section (line 74) consists of a single sentence ("Statistical analyses and standard deviation calculations were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics 26") without specifying any statistical test, sample size, or significance threshold used for Fig 7 or the enrichment analyses. No custom scripts, command-line parameters for `MCScanX` or `TBtools` RNA-seq alignment/quantification (which aligner/quantifier inside TBtools?), or raw Ct values for the qRT-PCR experiments are provided.
