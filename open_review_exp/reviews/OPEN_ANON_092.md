### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents PaaSc (Pathway activity analysis of Single-cell), a computational method that projects cells and genes into a shared latent coordinate space via Multiple Correspondence Analysis (MCA, using the CelliD implementation) and scores single-cell pathway activity by regressing gene coordinates on pathway membership and background pathway frequency. The authors benchmark PaaSc against single-cell (CelliD, GSDensity, AUCell, VAM, scGSEA) and bulk-adapted (ssGSEA/GSEA, GSVA) tools across human PBMC REAP-seq data, five tissue scRNA-seq atlases, >100 tumor immune scRNA-seq datasets from TISCH, cellular senescence bulk and single-cell datasets, GWAS trait-associated cell-type enrichment in mouse tissues, IFN-stimulated lupus PBMCs (GSE96583), 10x Multiome PBMC scATAC-seq, and spatial transcriptomics datasets (Visium mouse brain, human DLPFC, and CosMx SMI human NSCLC). While the empirical breadth of benchmarking is impressive and the regression-based dimension selection improves discrimination over unweighted MCA approaches on perturbation and senescence datasets, the manuscript exhibits mathematical discrepancies between the text and equations regarding dimension weighting, lacks multiple-testing correction across MCA dimensions, and contains several internal dataset count inconsistencies.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Contradiction Between Text/Figure 1D and Methods Equation 7 on Secondary Dimension Weighting:** In the Results section (line 29) and Figure 1D caption (line 35), the authors state that dimensions with $P < 0.05$ are weighted using *both* $t$-statistics as the primary weight and *"the proportion of variation explained by each dimension [as] a secondary weight."* However, in the Methods section ("Calculation of gene pathway activity at the single-cell level," Equation 7 and line 71), the weight matrix is defined purely from the $t$-statistics:
   $$W_{ij} = \begin{cases} t_{ij}, & \text{if } p < 0.05 \\ 0, & \text{if } p > 0.05 \end{cases}, \quad w_{ij}^* = \frac{w_{ij}}{\sum_i w_{ij}}$$
   with no inclusion of singular values $D_\alpha$ or variance explained. The authors must reconcile whether variance explained is actually multiplied into $W_{ij}$ in the software implementation or omitted.
2. **Division-by-Zero and Sign-Cancellation Vulnerabilities in Weight Normalization (Line 71):** In Methods (line 71), weights are normalized across dimensions via $w_{ij}^* = w_{ij} / \sum_i w_{ij}$, and the final score is standardized against background scores via $\text{score} = \frac{C W_1 - \text{mean}(C W_2) 1_N}{\text{sd}(C W_2)}$. First, because Equation 6 ($p_{ij} = P(t_{M-3} > |t_{ij}|)$) is a two-sided $t$-test, retained dimensions can have both positive and negative $t_{ij}$ values (since MCA axes have arbitrary sign orientation), meaning $\sum_i w_{ij}$ can approach zero or flip sign depending on axis orientation, and multiplying cell coordinates $C$ by $w_{ij} / \sum_i w_{ij}$ when $\sum_i w_{ij} < 0$ inverts the direction of pathway activity! Second, if no MCA dimension reaches $p < 0.05$ for either $S_1$ or $S_2$, $\sum_i w_{ij} = 0$ and $\text{sd}(C W_2) = 0$, causing a division-by-zero undefined score.
3. **Unadjusted $p < 0.05$ Thresholding Across $K \in [20, 50]$ MCA Dimensions:** For every pathway tested, Equation 6 performs $K = 20\text{–}50$ independent linear regressions across MCA dimensions and retains any dimension with unadjusted $p < 0.05$. Under a complete null (a random gene set with no true biological signal), $50 \times 0.05 = 2.5$ MCA dimensions will be falsely selected by chance, generating spurious non-zero pathway scores and artificially bimodal z-score distributions after background standardization.
4. **Internal Dataset Count Discrepancies Across Text and Figures:**
   - In Section "Benchmarking of PaaSc for large-scale cancer scRNA-seq data," line 59 states *"Among the 118 datasets with raw UMI counts..."*, whereas the Figure 3 caption (line 62) states *"Evaluation of the performance of PaaSc on 136 annotated scRNA-seq datasets"*, and line 82 states *"We tested 140 datasets from the TISCH database"*.
   - In the senescence analysis, line 72 states *"we analyzed a curated cohort comprising 41 bulk RNA-seq datasets... across 70% (29/41) of the analyzed datasets (Fig 4A)"*, whereas the Figure 4A caption (line 78) states *"A volcano plot showing the enrichment of senescence signatures across 50 bulk RNA-seq datasets."*
   - In the REAP-seq PBMC analysis (line 42), the text states *"identifying nine distinct cell types"* but only lists eight cell types (CD4+ T, CD8+ T, NK, pDCs, DCs, CD14+ monocytes, CD16+ monocytes, and megakaryocytes—omitting B cells, whereas S2B Fig lists ten populations).
5. **Discretization Sensitivity in Spatial Mutual Information (SMI, Equations 8–11):** The spatial mutual information $I((X,Y); Z)$ in Equations 8–11 discretizes continuous 2D spatial coordinates $(x, y)$ and continuous pathway scores $z$ into histogram bins without specifying the number of spatial or expression bins or correcting for finite-sample Miller–Madow bias, which strongly affects absolute SMI values when spot/cell density varies across tissue sections.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *PaaSc accurately scores cell-type-specific marker sets and dynamic pathway perturbations (such as IFN stimulation in SLE PBMCs and progressive cellular senescence in IMR90/WI-38 fibroblasts), outperforming unweighted MCA tools (CelliD, GSDensity).* `[Well Supported]` — The GSE96583 IFN-stimulation control vs. stimulated PBMC benchmark (Figure 6C–D) is a particularly well-designed test that separates cell-identity scoring (B-cell markers, AUC ~ 0.50 across treatment batches) from condition-specific signaling activation (IFN pathway, PaaSc AUC = 0.96 vs. CelliD 0.60 and GSDensity 0.56).
- **Claim 2:** *PaaSc maintains accuracy in the presence of batch effects.* `[Moderately Supported]` — While Figure 6 shows that rank-normalized B-cell scores remain high in B cells across control and IFN-stimulated conditions, IFN stimulation in GSE96583 is a biological perturbation rather than a technical multi-center/multi-platform batch effect. Testing technical batch effects (e.g., 10x v2 vs. Smart-seq2 or multi-donor/multi-site batches) would be required to fully substantiate broad batch-effect invariance.
- **Claim 3:** *PaaSc scores exhibit a distinct bimodal distribution that enables objective classification of cells into positive and negative states for GWAS traits.* `[Fragile]` — As shown in S6B Fig, when comparing PaaSc against CelliD and GSDensity across the 22 GWAS traits (Figure 5D), the authors abandoned bimodal thresholding and instead defined positive cells arbitrarily as the top 5% quantile for each method.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Code Availability:** Very strong. All public GEO accessions (GSE175533, GSE102090, GSE119807, GSE115301, GSE100501, GSE96583), TISCH, 10x Multiome links, and Zenodo deposits for processed spatial datasets (`8201825`), source data (`17138447`), and analysis scripts (`17136774`) are provided.
- **Methodological Reporting:** Software versions (Seurat v4.3.0, Monocle2 v2.22.0, Scanpy v1.10.4, STAR, FastQC v0.11.8, Trim Galore v0.6.2) are well documented. The authors need only clarify the exact equation used for $W_{ij}$ in code and specify the bin counts used for SMI discretization in Equations 9–11.
