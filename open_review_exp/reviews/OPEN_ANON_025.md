### 1. Executive Summary
This study combines molecular virology, biophysics, cell biology, transcriptomics, and comparative genomics to investigate how the Chinese rufous horseshoe bat (*Rhinolophus sinicus*) diversifies its antiviral interferon-induced transmembrane protein (IFITM) repertoire despite possessing only a single immune-related *IFITM* locus (`LOC109436297`). The authors demonstrate that alternative first-exon splicing generates two endogenous protein isoforms—`rsIFITMa` (containing the N-terminal $^{20}\text{YEML}^{23}$ endocytic sorting motif) and `rsIFITMb` (an N-terminally truncated isoform lacking the $\text{YXX}\Phi$ motif)—that are induced by poly(I:C) and VSV infection in native *R. sinicus* kidney epithelial (`RsKT.01`) cells. Using circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy, NBD-cholesterol fluorescence intensity/polarization assays, and chimeric constructs, they show that the conserved amphipathic helix retains helical structure and cholesterol-binding capacity. Through pseudotyped and replication-competent infection assays across IAV, SARS-CoV, MERS-CoV, HCoV-229E, SARS-CoV-2 variants (Wuhan, Delta, Omicron), and Nipah virus, paired with confocal colocalization (`CD63`) and reciprocal N-terminal mutagenesis (`Y20E` and `rsIFITMb [Nt]`), they establish that subcellular localization dictates entry-route-specific restriction (endosomal restriction by `rsIFITMa` vs. plasma membrane Nipah restriction by `rsIFITMb`). Extending this to *R. affinis* (`IFITM3a`/`3b`) and a bioinformatic survey of 206 mammalian RefSeq transcriptomes, the authors show that alternative splicing is a recurring evolutionary strategy for *IFITM* diversification (present in ~36% of surveyed mammals, with 11/206 generating $\text{YXX}\Phi$-distinct isoforms). The experimental design is thorough and mechanistically cohesive, with minor statistical and bioinformatic caveats regarding annotation depth bias and RNA-seq repository deposition.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Annotation Depth and Assembly Quality Confounding in the 206-Mammal Comparative Splicing Analysis (Fig 7D–E):** In Fig 7D–E, the authors analyze NCBI RefSeq RNA annotations across 206 mammalian species, concluding that 87% exhibit *IFITM* gene duplication, 36% (75/206) exhibit alternative splicing, and that gene duplication and alternative splicing are positively associated by Fisher's exact test (Fig 7E). However, RefSeq transcript models across non-model mammals vary wildly in underlying short-read/long-read RNA-seq tissue coverage and genome assembly contiguity (especially across the repetitive, tandemly duplicated *IFITM* locus, which frequently collapses in short-read assemblies—as evidenced by the shift from 1 *IFITM* gene in the older *R. sinicus* RefSeq assembly `GCF_001888835.1` to 4 *IFITM* genes in the reference-quality Bat1K *R. affinis* assembly). Well-annotated model or biomedically prioritized species with deeper multi-tissue RNA-seq and higher-quality assemblies will simultaneously have more resolved paralogs (duplication) and more annotated splice isoforms (alternative splicing). Testing whether the Fisher's exact test association in Fig 7E remains significant after controlling for total proteome/transcriptome annotation depth (e.g., total annotated splice isoforms per gene genome-wide in each species' RefSeq build) is essential to distinguish true evolutionary coupling from annotation completeness bias.
2. **Pseudoreplication vs. Biological Replication in Confocal Image Quantification (Fig 4B–C vs. Fig 5A–B) and Multiple Testing:**
   - In Fig 4B–C, surface FLAG percentage and JACoP Pearson colocalization coefficients with CD63 are analyzed across "at least 30 cells" using an unpaired *t*-test, treating individual cells (rather than independent biological transfections) as the unit of replication ($n \ge 30$). In Fig 5A–B, the unit of analysis shifts to "10 images, each capturing 1–3 cells" across 5 construct groups, analyzed by multiple pairwise unpaired *t*-tests rather than a one-way ANOVA with post-hoc multiple-comparison correction (such as Tukey's or Šidák's test).
   - Similarly, across Fig 3B–H, Fig 5C–D, and Fig 7C, comparisons against vector control properly use one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's test, but comparisons between `rsIFITMa` and `rsIFITMb` (or mutant pairs) are performed via standalone unpaired *t*-tests on $N=3$ independent biological experiments without adjusting for multiple comparisons or pooling variance across the ANOVA model.
3. **RNA-seq Differential Expression Thresholds and Unreported Replicate Structure (Fig 1B–D):** In the Methods ("RNA sequencing and analysis", line 137), differential expression in `RsKT.01` cells (untreated vs. poly(I:C) or VSV) is performed in `edgeR` using an unadjusted nominal `p-value < 0.05` (`fold-change > 2`) rather than false discovery rate (FDR / Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted $q < 0.05$), and the number of biological replicates sequenced per condition in Fig 1D is not explicitly stated in the Methods or Fig 1 caption. Furthermore, `STAR v2.7.2b` is a genome aligner that outputs BAM files; the specific tool used to quantify isoform-level abundances (`XM_019714804.1` vs `XM_019714805.1`) prior to `edgeR` TMM normalization (e.g., RSEM, Salmon, Kallisto, or featureCounts on exon-1 junctions) is omitted.
4. **Sequence Alignment Identity Discrepancy (`rsIFITMa` vs. `rsIFITMb`):** On line 41, the authors state that pairwise alignment of `rsIFITMa` and `rsIFITMb` shows 79.4% sequence identity because they are produced by alternative first-exon splicing of a 2-exon gene (`LOC109436297`), sharing exon 2. Yet on line 118 (Discussion), they note that "codon 70 of rsIFITMa and rsIFITMb encodes a proline and a threonine respectively." Wait—if `rsIFITMa` and `rsIFITMb` are splice variants of the same single gene `LOC109436297` that only differ in their alternative first exon (N-terminus), how can codon 70 (or aligned position 70, if falling in the shared region) differ between two transcripts of the same genomic locus unless position 70 falls within Exon 1 just upstream of the splice junction? Clarifying the exact exon-1/exon-2 boundary amino acid coordinates in `S1B Fig` would resolve this.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: *R. sinicus* `LOC109436297` produces two endogenous, interferon/virus-inducible splice isoforms (`rsIFITMa` and `rsIFITMb`) at both the mRNA and protein levels.** `[Well Supported]` — Convincingly demonstrated in native `RsKT.01` cells via RNA-seq, absolute standard-curve RT-qPCR, poly(I:C) Western blotting showing two distinct molecular-weight bands, and isoform-specific CRISPR/Cas9 knockout targeting exon 1 of `rsIFITMa` (`S2D Fig`), which selectively ablates the upper protein band.
- **Claim 2: The amphipathic helix of `rsIFITMs` retains $\alpha$-helical secondary structure, cholesterol binding, and functional competence.** `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated by CD spectroscopy (56.0–63.1% helicity), NBD-cholesterol fluorescence intensity and polarization curves (with rotavirus NSP4 negative control), and functional rescue of IAV restriction by a human `IFITM3-AH [R. sinicus]` chimera.
- **Claim 3: `rsIFITMa` and `rsIFITMb` localize to endolysosomes (`CD63+`) and the plasma membrane, respectively, driving opposing restriction potencies against endosomal viruses (IAV, HCoV-229E, SARS-CoV-2 Omicron) vs. surface-fusing viruses (Nipah) and pro-viral enhancement of Wuhan/Delta SARS-CoV-2 by `rsIFITMb`.** `[Well Supported]` — This is the standout mechanistic centerpiece of the paper: the reciprocal gain/loss of function via `rsIFITMa [Y20E]` and `rsIFITMb [Nt]`/`rsIFITMb [Nt_Y20E]` (Fig 5C), the functional validation in native `RsKT.01` cells (Fig 3H) and with *R. affinis* `IFITM3a`/`3b` (Fig 7C), and the Nipah virus reversal (Fig 5D) provide compelling, multi-layered biological proof.
- **Claim 4: Alternative splicing is a recurring evolutionary strategy across mammals that converges with gene duplication to diversify the IFITM repertoire.** `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by the survey of 206 mammals and experimental validation in two *Rhinolophus* species, though the exact comparative frequencies (87% vs 36%) and positive correlation between duplication and splicing require controlling for genome/transcriptome assembly quality across non-model taxa.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Missing Raw RNA-seq Accession Number:** While the authors generated primary RNA-seq data from `RsKT.01` cells across three conditions (untreated, poly(I:C)-treated, and VSV-infected; Fig 1B–D), the Data Availability statement only states that "All relevant data are within the paper and its supporting data files" and provides a GitHub repository (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`) for the comparative bioinformatic scripts. Raw RNA-seq FASTQ files and processed count tables from the `RsKT.01` experiment must be deposited in NCBI SRA / GEO (or ENA) with a public accession number.
- **Reagents & Sequences:** RT-qPCR primer sequences, CRISPR sgRNA sequence (`CTGCGGATGTTAACCACGG`), antibody catalog dilutions, and NCBI RefSeq transcript/protein accessions (`XM_019714804.1`, `XM_019714805.1`, `GCF_001888835.1`) are thoroughly documented. The exact peptide sequences synthesized for the CD and NBD-cholesterol assays (including the NSP4 control peptide) and the exact *R. affinis* sequences cloned for Fig 7C should be explicitly listed in a supplementary table.
