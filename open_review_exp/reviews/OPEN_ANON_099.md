### 1. Executive Summary
This observational study profiles the V3–V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene in oral (periodontal pocket or tongue) and fecal samples from 38 patients with metastatic urothelial carcinoma (UC) and 20 healthy controls at Yamaguchi University Hospital, and evaluates associations between the relative abundances of *Veillonellaceae* and *Prevotellaceae*, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), C-reactive protein (CRP), overall survival (OS), and progression-free survival (PFS). While identifying non-invasive oral and gut microbiome biomarkers of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) efficacy in UC is of clinical interest, the study has fundamental design and analytical flaws: (1) extreme, unadjusted demographic confounding between cases and controls (the UC cohort is 73.7% male with median age 74 years, whereas the control cohort is 80.0% female with median age 61 years); (2) inconsistent oral sampling anatomical sites (subgingival periodontal pockets in dentate subjects vs. tongue swabs in edentulous subjects); (3) three contradictory numbers for how many UC patients actually received ICI therapy (27 in the Abstract, 38 in Methods text, and 30 in Table 1); and (4) unadjusted univariate survival analyses without defined cutoffs or multiple-testing correction.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Severe Uncontrolled Sex, Age, and Smoking Confounding Between UC Cases and Controls (Table 1):** In Table 1 (lines 59–62), the 38 UC patients are **73.7% male** ($28/38$) with a median age of **74 years** (range 60–89), whereas the 20 healthy controls are **80.0% female** ($16/20$, only 4 men; Fisher's exact $p = 0.0001$) with a median age of **61 years** (range 46–82). Furthermore, smoking history—the primary epidemiological risk factor for both urothelial carcinoma and periodontal dysbiosis—and antibiotic/proton-pump-inhibitor use are completely unrecorded. Comparing oral and fecal taxa between 74-year-old predominantly male cancer patients and 61-year-old predominantly female healthy donors via unadjusted univariate ALDEx2 tests (`we.ep`, `wi.ep`) confounds disease status with sex, age, and smoking.
2. **Anatomical Site Confounding in Oral Microbiome Sampling (Periodontal Pocket vs. Tongue, Line 99):** In the Methods section ("DNA extraction from oral specimens," line 99), the authors state: *"In the edentulous jaw, we collected bacteria only from the tongue, whereas in the case of the teeth, we collected bacteria from the periodontal pockets."* Subgingival periodontal pockets (an anaerobic crevice enriched in *Prevotellaceae* and *Porphyromonadaceae*) and the dorsal tongue mucosa harbor fundamentally distinct microbial communities, and edentulism/periodontal pocket depth varies strongly with age (74 vs. 61 years). Mixing periodontal pocket samples and tongue swabs without recording or stratifying by dentition status or periodontal disease invalidates the salivary/oral comparisons.
3. **Three Contradictory Sample Sizes for ICI-Treated Patients ($N = 27$ vs. $N = 38$ vs. $N = 30$):**
   - The Abstract (line 16) states: *"A total of 20 healthy individuals and 38 patients with UC were analyzed. Among them, **27** patients with UC underwent ICI treatment."*
   - The Methods text (line 47) states: *"Samples were collected from **38** patients with metastatic UC (38 patients) treated with ICIs at Yamaguchi University Hospital..."*
   - Table 1 (lines 66–72, `Prior systemaic [sic] therapy before IO`) lists: `0: 1 (2.6%)` (misaligned under the `NORMAL` column), `1: 20 (52.6%)`, `2: 6 (15.8%)`, `3: 3 (7.9%)`, `>4: 0 (0%)`, and `no IO: 8 (21.1%)`. Subtracting the 8 `no IO` patients from 38 yields **30** patients treated with IO, which contradicts both 27 and 38! Moreover, the manuscript never states whether the OS and PFS Kaplan–Meier curves in Figures 3 and 4 analyze $N = 27$, $N = 30$, or all $N = 38$ patients (including the 8 who never received immunotherapy).
4. **Unspecified Dichotomization Cutoffs, Lack of Multivariable Survival Adjustment, and Panel Mismatches (Figures 3–4):**
   - The Methods and Figure 3–4 captions never state how patients were stratified into "High" vs. "Low" *Veillonellaceae* and *Prevotellaceae* abundance (e.g., median split vs. post-hoc optimal cutpoint search) or what the group sizes ($n$) are on each survival curve.
   - Across the 8 Kaplan–Meier log-rank tests (2 bacterial families × 2 sample sites × 2 survival endpoints), only 3 reach nominal $p < 0.05$ (stool *Veillonellaceae* OS $p = 0.0251$ and PFS $p = 0.0125$; saliva *Prevotellaceae* OS $p = 0.0475$, with saliva *Prevotellaceae* PFS non-significant and stool *Prevotellaceae* OS $p = 0.4712$). None survive Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the 8 tests, and no multivariable Cox regression adjusting for ECOG performance status (21.1% had ECOG PS $> 2$), metastatic burden, or line of therapy was performed.
   - In Results line 159, panel citations do not match the figure captions: line 159 cites `(Fig 3a, b)` for OS and `(Fig 3d)` for stool *Veillonellaceae* PFS, whereas the Figure 3 caption (lines 128 and 138) states that survival curves are in panels `(a, c)` and `(e, g)` while panels `(b, d)` and `(f, h)` display NLR and CRP Mann–Whitney comparisons.
5. **Implausible PCR Annealing Temperature and Outdated Reference Database (Lines 109–111):** Line 109 reports that 16S V3–V4 PCR amplification was performed with *"annealing at the temperature of 72°C for 30 seconds"* (standard Illumina V3–V4 341F/805R protocol uses 55°C annealing, as used in the index PCR on line 111), and taxonomic assignment was performed using the obsolete GreenGenes database via Illumina BaseSpace 16S Metagenomics v1.1.0 (which still uses the deprecated family *Porphyromonadaceae* and outdated OTU clustering rather than ASV denoising with SILVA/GTDB).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Patients with UC have significantly elevated oral and intestinal Veillonellaceae and Prevotellaceae compared to healthy individuals, implicating dysbiosis in urothelial carcinogenesis.* `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Because the control group is 80% female and 13 years younger while the UC group is 74% male, and oral sampling mixed subgingival periodontal pockets with tongue swabs, the observed taxonomic differences cannot be attributed to urothelial carcinoma.
- **Claim 2:** *Oral and fecal Veillonellaceae and Prevotellaceae serve as predictive biomarkers of ICI efficacy and survival in UC.* `[Overstated]` — With an unclear number of ICI-treated patients (27 vs. 30 vs. 38), no non-ICI control arm to distinguish prognostic from predictive effects, no adjustment for ECOG performance status or NLR in a multivariable Cox model, and inconsistent significance between saliva and stool (e.g., saliva *Veillonellaceae* OS $p = 0.2094$; stool *Prevotellaceae* OS $p = 0.4712$), biomarker claims for ICI personalization are unsupported.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** The Data Availability statement (line 190) provides an NCBI GEO accession (`GSE293354`) for both the 16S sequencing data and original code (though raw 16S amplicon FASTQ files are normally deposited in NCBI SRA/BioProject rather than GEO).
- **Reporting Gaps:** Exact patient counts per survival curve, threshold values used to define High/Low bacterial abundance, exact definitions of survival time zero ($t_0$), and reconciliation of the 27 vs. 30 vs. 38 ICI patient counts are missing.
