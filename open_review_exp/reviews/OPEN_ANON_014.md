### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript presents a rigorous, clear, and insightful theoretical and empirical investigation into why gene-by-environment (GxE) models at single variants often underperform simple additive models in GWAS, and how polygenic covariance modeling overcomes this limitation. The authors frame single-variant effect estimation as a bias-variance trade-off, deriving a closed-form mean squared error (MSE) decision rule that weighs the bias of a pooled additive estimator ($\hat{\beta}_{A \cup B}$) against the increased estimation variance of context-stratified GxE estimators ($\hat{\beta}_A, \hat{\beta}_B$). Applying this framework to sex-stratified GWAS across 27 quantitative physiological traits in the UK Biobank (with 80/20 out-of-sample validation), they show that genome-wide, $<0.5\%$ of variants are better estimated by sex-stratified OLS individually, whereas a substantial fraction of genome-wide significant loci (e.g., $100\%$ for testosterone, $62\%$ for creatinine) favor context-specific estimation. Extending the analysis to polygenic traits, the authors persuasively demonstrate via simulations and a `mash` reanalysis of *Drosophila melanogaster* GxDiet longevity data (`PRJNA725602`, 271K SNPs) that classifying GxE based on per-variant significance ("top hits") conflates differential statistical power under pervasive amplification with context-specific ("cryptic") genetic variation. Finally, they show that multivariate empirical Bayes shrinkage (`mash`) improves polygenic score prediction when sample sizes are sufficient to learn cross-context covariance matrices. Only minor mathematical clarifications and text-method parameter reconciliations are needed.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Implicit Context-Demeaning Assumption in the Derivation of Equation 3 (Methods, Line 209):** In proving $\hat{\beta}_{A \cup B} = \omega_A \hat{\beta}_A + \omega_B \hat{\beta}_B$, line 209 assumes $\sum_{i=1}^n y_i = 0$ and $\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+m} y_i = 0$ separately within each context, stating that "mean-centering is inconsequential for effect estimation." However, the additive model in Equation 2 ($y_i \sim N(\alpha_{A \cup B} + \beta_{A \cup B} g_i, \sigma^2_{A \cup B})$) has only a single global intercept $\alpha_{A \cup B}$, which guarantees only $\sum_{i=1}^{n+m} (y_i - \bar{y}_{A \cup B}) = 0$, not $\bar{y}_A = \bar{y}_B = 0$. If $\bar{y}_A \neq \bar{y}_B$ (the context has a non-zero main effect) and $\bar{g}_A \neq \bar{g}_B$ (allele frequencies differ across contexts), the omitted cross-term $\frac{(\bar{g}_A - \bar{g}_{A \cup B}) n \bar{y}_A + (\bar{g}_B - \bar{g}_{A \cup B}) m \bar{y}_B}{\sum_{i=1}^{n+m} (g_i - \bar{g}_{A \cup B})^2}$ is non-zero (omitted-variable confounding). Conversely, if the additive model includes a context main effect (centering $y$ within each context), then $g$ is also centered within each context in the OLS normal equations, making the denominator $\sum_{i=1}^n (g_i - \bar{g}_A)^2 + \sum_{i=n+1}^{n+m} (g_i - \bar{g}_B)^2$ so that $\omega_A + \omega_B = 1$ holds exactly even when $\bar{g}_A \neq \bar{g}_B$. When $\bar{g}_A = \bar{g}_B$ (as in autosomal GxSex), $\bar{g}_A - \bar{g}_{A \cup B} = 0$ identically and Equation 3 holds without requiring $\bar{y}_A = \bar{y}_B = 0$. Clarifying this distinction will make the proof in Methods completely watertight and connect it seamlessly to Appendix 1 Section 4.
2. **Minor Parameter Discrepancies Between Main Text and Methods in Simulations:**
   - *Drosophila GxDiet simulation:* Line 157 of the main text states "we sampled from a mixture of 40% of variants having no effect under either diet and 60% of variants having an effect under both diets—but exactly 1.4x larger under a high-sugar diet." However, the Methods (line 310) state the reverse mixture proportions: "A random 60% of variants were set to have no effect under either diet, with the effects of the remaining 40% of variants sampled as $[\beta_{cj}, \beta_{hj}]^T \sim N([-0.125, -0.15]^T, 0.01 \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1.4 \\ 1.4 & 1.96 \end{bmatrix})$." Additionally, while the covariance matrix has a $1.4\times$ standard deviation ratio ($\sqrt{1.96} = 1.4$), the mean vector $[-0.125, -0.15]^T$ has a ratio of $-0.15 / -0.125 = 1.2\times$ rather than $1.4\times$ ($-0.175$).
   - *Polygenic score simulation:* The main text (line 167) and Figure 5 caption (line 170) state that amplified effects were "$1.4\times$ larger in one of contexts," whereas the Methods equation (line 345) specifies the covariance matrix $\begin{bmatrix} 3/2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2/3 \end{bmatrix}$ and explicitly states "$1.5\times$ the standard deviation in context A" (matching Appendix 1—figure 5). Also, Figure 5 caption states "11,000 simulations" whereas Appendix 1—figure 5 states "1000 simulations."
3. **Typographical Note in Appendix 1—figure 3 Caption:** The caption states "The dashed line shows the decision boundary for effect estimation in females," whereas the focal context plotted on the y-axis and described in the header is males (as in Figure 3B).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: At individual variants genome-wide, context-stratified GxE estimation almostalways has higher MSE than pooled additive estimation due to the variance penalty, except at strongly associated loci in traits with marked context specificity (e.g., testosterone, creatinine, WHR, SHBG).** `[Well Supported]` — Analytically proven under OLS and empirically validated via out-of-sample splitting across 27 UK Biobank physiological traits.
- **Claim 2: Per-variant significance classification ("top hits") in GxE studies conflates differential statistical power under polygenic amplification with context-specific ("cryptic") genetic variation.** `[Well Supported]` — The side-by-side comparison of the Pallares et al. (2023) *Drosophila* longevity data and the pervasive amplification simulation (Figure 4), reinforced by the `mash` covariance estimation assigning $92\%$ weight to shared amplified effects and $0\%$ to diet-specific effects, is a textbook demonstration of how thresholding bivariate normal estimates creates illusory specificity.
- **Claim 3: Jointly modeling polygenic covariance across contexts (`mash`) improves polygenic score accuracy over both additive and stratified GxE models when GWAS sample sizes are sufficiently powered.** `[Well Supported]` — Clearly demonstrated in Figure 5B ($N = 50,000$) vs. Figure 5A ($N = 1,000$), accurately reflecting the variance cost of learning prior covariance matrices at small sample sizes.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** All empirical datasets used are publicly available and properly cited (`PRJNA725602` for *Drosophila* GxDiet data; Zenodo deposits for UK Biobank additive and sex-specific summary statistics).
- **Code Availability:** While the mathematical formulations and simulation generative models are thoroughly documented in the Methods and Appendix 1, the manuscript does not provide a link to a public code repository (e.g., GitHub or Zenodo) containing the R/Python scripts used to run the simulations, `ash`/`mash` pipelines, and figure generation. Adding a code repository link would further strengthen reproducibility.
