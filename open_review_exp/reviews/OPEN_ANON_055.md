### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents a large-scale retrospective genomic and epidemiologic surveillance analysis of persistent SARS-CoV-2 infections ($\ge 21$ days between sequenced specimens) in California from January 2021 through July 2023. Screening 987,973 sequenced SARS-CoV-2 infections (from 812,563 patients) in the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) Integrated Genomic Epidemiology Dataset (IGED), the authors identify 217 candidate cases meeting epidemiologic criteria and confirm 69 persistent infections via phylogenetic placement (UShER/Augur), consensus mutation tracking, and intra-host single nucleotide variant (iSNV) read-level phasing. The authors compare demographic and clinical severity metrics against the baseline sequenced population, review clinical charts for a subset of $n = 14$ cases with Kaiser Permanente Northern California medical records (confirming immunocompromising conditions, particularly B-cell lymphomas and solid organ transplants, in 13 of 14 cases), test for genomic enrichment of nonsynonymous mutations across 500-nt bins (identifying Spike RBD Bin 46 at the consensus level and Spike S2 Bin 50 at the sub-consensus level, plus convergent substitutions at `S:444`, `S:484`, `ORF1b:662`, and `ORF1a:T1638I`), and detail two notable longitudinal infections (a 103-day BA.5.11 infection with oscillating viral subpopulations and a 400-day B.1.429 Epsilon infection).

This is a rigorous, well-executed, and biologically insightful public health genomic surveillance study. The bioinformatic filtering of sequencing artifacts (masking problematic sites, primer regions, strand bias $>10\times$, indel alignment artifacts, and lineage-defining mutations) and read-level verification of subpopulation phasing are exemplary. Minor revisions are recommended to address ascertainment bias in the unadjusted $\chi^2$ comparisons for hospitalization/death, refine the null model in the binned binomial enrichment test, and correct a date typo in the text.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Ascertainment and Reporting Bias in Hospitalization and Mortality $\chi^2$ Comparisons (Table 1, Lines 53, 175, 208):** In Table 1, the authors report highly significant $\chi^2$ differences in hospitalization (37.7% [26/69] vs. 3.5% [28,233/812,563], $\chi^2 = 230.4, \text{df} = 1$) and mortality (11.6% [8/69] vs. 0.5% [4,457/812,563], $\chi^2 = 134.5, \text{df} = 1$) between persistent infections and all sequenced California cases. Two structural biases inflate these unadjusted statistics:
   - First, by study design, identifying a persistent infection requires at least two sequenced positive specimens collected $\ge 21$ days apart with no intervening negative PCR test. Immunocompromised or severely ill hospitalized patients undergo routine serial PCR testing and longitudinal viral load monitoring in inpatient/oncology settings, whereas mild outpatient infections are rarely re-tested and re-sequenced after 21 days.
   - Second, persistent cases were substantially older than the baseline sequenced population (46.3% aged $\ge 65$ years vs. 10.5% in the baseline cohort, $\chi^2 = 81.4, \text{df} = 3$). Because hospitalization and death strongly depend on age, an age-stratified or age-adjusted logistic regression (or Mantel-Haenszel test) should be reported alongside the crude $\chi^2$ statistics to quantify how much of the excess hospitalization and mortality is explained by age vs. persistent infection status.
   - Third, as noted in the Methods (Line 208), unknown hospitalization or death statuses in CDPH registries were coded as "No", and mandatory hospital reporting only began on July 27, 2021.
2. **Denominator Discrepancy Between Text and Table 1 for Ethnicity Percentages (Line 53 vs. Table 1):** In the text (Line 53), the authors state: *"Of cases with known ethnicity (n = 59), 27 (45.8%) were White and 18 (30.5%) were Latino."* In Table 1 (Lines 82, 84), the percentages for the exact same counts (`18` Latino and `27` White) are listed as `26.1%` and `39.1%` because Table 1 uses total persistent cases ($n = 69$, including 10 Unknown) as the denominator. Explicitly noting in the text that Table 1 percentages use $n = 69$ as the denominator will prevent reader confusion.
3. **Uniform Null Assumption in the 500-nt Binned Binomial Enrichment Test (Lines 142–146, 28–31 of page 2):** To test for regional enrichment of nonsynonymous mutations (Fig. 3), the genome was divided into 500-nt bins and evaluated with a one-tailed binomial test (`MutationalPatterns`). A standard uniform binomial test assumes an equal probability of observing a nonsynonymous substitution in every 500-nt bin. In reality, bins vary in (a) the number of nonsynonymous sites (due to codon composition and non-coding UTR/intergenic regions at the $5'$ and $3'$ ends), and (b) effective sequencing depth (amplicon dropout in Spike often reduces callable coverage). While lower coverage in the Spike RBD makes the detected enrichment in Bin 46 (adjusted $p = 0.005$) conservative, the authors should briefly note that the binomial null is uniform across genomic length rather than normalized by callable nonsynonymous sites per bin.
4. **Exclusion of Rebound Cases with Intervening Negative PCR Tests (Lines 193, 220):** Filtering out patients who had any intervening negative PCR test between two positive sequenced specimens $\ge 21$ days apart is a conservative specificity choice to avoid misclassifying reinfections, but it systematically excludes persistent infections with fluctuating viral loads near the limit of detection (e.g., Paxlovid/remdesivir viral rebound or intermittent upper-respiratory shedding). The authors appropriately acknowledge this limitation on Line 193.
5. **Typographical Error in Collection Date (Line 185):** In the Discussion (Line 185), the text refers to *"nucleotide positions 7086 and 7104 in the 2025-07-11 sample from Case 60"*. Per the Results (Line 151) and Fig. 4, this sample was collected on `2023-07-11`, not `2025-07-11`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Persistent SARS-CoV-2 infections in California are strongly enriched among older, hospitalized, and immunocompromised individuals (particularly those with B-cell lymphomas and organ transplants).* `[Well Supported]` — Despite surveillance ascertainment effects, the clinical chart review of the Kaiser Permanente subset ($n = 14$, where 13/14 had documented immunocompromising conditions, including 8/9 cancer patients having B-cell lymphomas and 55.6% receiving active chemo/CAR-T/radiation) aligns with established viral immunology showing that impaired humoral/B-cell and cellular immunity drives prolonged intra-host SARS-CoV-2 replication.
- **Claim 2:** *Intra-host evolution during persistent infection exhibits strong convergent selection on immune-escape residues in the Spike RBD (`S:444`, `S:484`) and `ORF1b:662` / `ORF1a:T1638I`, anticipating mutations seen in circulating variants (e.g., BQ.1).* `[Well Supported]` — Supported by rigorous artifact masking, per-patient deduplication of mutation counts, and clear temporal alignment showing `S:K444R/N` accumulating in persistent infections prior to and during the rise of BQ.1.
- **Claim 3:** *Persistent infections can harbor complex, competing intra-host viral subpopulations (Case 60, BA.5.11) and divergent intra-host lineages evolving from an unsampled common ancestor over $>1$ year (Case 33, B.1.429).* `[Well Supported]` — Direct read-level phasing at positions 7086 and 7104 in Case 60 and phylogenetic placement of the 400-day B.1.429 sequences 10 months after Epsilon extinction in the community provide compelling empirical support.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** GISAID, NCBI GenBank, and SRA accession numbers for all 69 persistent infection cases (along with sequencing platforms and amplicon schemes) are provided in S1 Table under BioProject `PRJNA750736`.
- **Code & Workflow Transparency:** The bioinformatic workflows (`TheiaCoV`, `Augur_PHB`), software versions (`Nextclade v2.14.0/v3.8.2`, `UShER`, `MutationalPatterns v3.14.0`, `R v4.4.1`), variant calling parameters (`min_depth = 20`, `variant_min_freq = 0.01`, iSNV frequency 5–60%, total depth $\ge 100\times$, alt depth $\ge 20\times$, strand bias $<10\times$), and manually masked artifactual sites (S5 Table) are thoroughly documented.
