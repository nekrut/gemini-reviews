### 1. Executive Summary
This study tests whether size-grading juvenile winter-run steelhead (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) from 15 full-sibling families (initial $n = 375$ fish per tank across 4 tanks; 25 fish/family/tank) after 143 days of rearing into small (<97 mm fork length [FL]), medium (97–113 mm FL), and large (>113 mm FL) groups—followed by differential feeding and density manipulation for ~5 months—reduces the total variance and among-family variance in size at release compared to ungraded control tanks. While the biological question is practical for conservation hatchery management and the empirical observation that initially small juveniles failed to achieve compensatory growth under low density and excess ration is noteworthy, the study suffers from severe experimental design and statistical flaws. Most critically, the analysis treats individual fish ($n_1 = 615, n_2 = 556$) rather than tanks as the unit of replication (textbook pseudoreplication), uses only a single unreplicated tank for the high-density/feed-restricted large size group, removes tank variation by ad hoc mean-centering before re-pooling, and reports an internally impossible test statistic ($\chi^2 = 40.51, P = 0.076$) for the two-group Fligner-Killeen test of family means.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Severe Pseudoreplication and Ad Hoc Mean-Centering of Tanks (Methods, Lines 53 & 72):** Two tanks were assigned to the ungraded control and two tanks were graded into two small tanks, two medium tanks, and one pooled large tank. The authors explicitly state that *"individual fish (not tanks) are the unit of replication"* and then manually adjust individual fish fork lengths so that replicate tanks within each size bin have identical means before pooling all graded fish into a single synthetic cohort ($n_2 = 556$) to compare against the pooled control cohort ($n_1 = 615$). Because fish within the same tank share water quality, social hierarchy, feeding delivery, and pathogen history, treating 1,171 individual fish as independent observations massively inflates the degrees of freedom ($df = 1167$ for the $t$-test; $n_1 = 615, n_2 = 556$ for the Fligner-Killeen test). Furthermore, the "large" grading treatment was pooled into a single 380 L tank ($n = 1$ tank), making it statistically impossible to separate the treatment effect of feed restriction/high density from random tank effects for the exact subgroup that drove the entire variance reduction.
2. **Mathematically Impossible Fligner-Killeen Test Statistic for Family Means (Results, Line 111):** The authors report comparing the variance among the 15 family means in the graded group ($\text{Var} = 0.39, n_2 = 15$) versus the control group ($\text{Var} = 0.64, n_1 = 15$) via a Fligner-Killeen median test, reporting $P = 0.076$ and $\chi^2 = 40.51$. A two-group Fligner-Killeen test has $df = 2 - 1 = 1$; a test statistic of $\chi^2 = 40.51$ with $df = 1$ yields $P = 1.96 \times 10^{-10}$, not $P = 0.076$. Conversely, $\text{pchisq}(40.51, df = 29, \text{lower.tail} = \text{FALSE}) = 0.0760$. This proves that the test was executed with $df = 29$ (i.e., 30 groups), which occurs in R (`fligner.test`) either when testing across all $15 \times 2 = 30$ family-by-treatment combinations on individual fish (testing homogeneity of *within-family* variance rather than variance *among family means*) or when passing a continuous vector as the grouping factor. The proper analysis requires a linear mixed-effects model (LMM) specifying family and tank as random effects and testing whether heterogeneous family variance components ($\sigma^2_{\text{family, control}}$ vs. $\sigma^2_{\text{family, graded}}$) improve model fit via a likelihood ratio test.
3. **Confounding of Rearing Density, Ration Level, and Fasting Schedule (Methods, Lines 45 & 55):** In the graded treatment, the large group was simultaneously subjected to doubled rearing density (pooling two tanks into one), reduced target ration (7 FPP vs. 6 FPP), and two days per week of complete fasting, while the small group experienced both lower density and increased ration (4 FPP). Without unmanipulated graded controls (graded into size bins but reared at standard ration and density), it is impossible to disentangle the effect of size-segregation per se from starvation/fasting in the large group.
4. **Swapped Control and Graded Coefficient of Variation (CV) Values (Discussion, Line 139):** The authors state: *"we estimate a 20.90% reduction in the opportunity for selection among individuals, and a 35.50% reduction in the opportunity for selection among families (CV among individuals = 7.98 and 8.97, and among families = 3.34 and 4.16 for control and graded, respectively)."* Since $I = (\text{CV}/100)^2$ and $1 - (7.98/8.97)^2 = 20.86\%$ and $1 - (3.34/4.16)^2 = 35.54\%$, the smaller CVs (7.98 and 3.34) belong to the **graded** group and the larger CVs (8.97 and 4.16) belong to the **control** group—opposite to the *"for control and graded, respectively"* text.
5. **Unexplained Sample Size Attrition and Family Exclusion (Methods, Lines 33, 38, 111):** Line 33 states 16 full-sibling families (A–P) were spawned across two dates (March 25 and April 7, 2022, with chilled water used on the first spawn group), but Line 38 states 25 fish from each of 15 families were placed into each of 4 tanks ($375 \times 4 = 1,500$ fish total). At the end of the experiment, only $615 + 556 = 1,171$ fish are analyzed (a 21.9% loss of 329 fish, with 59 fewer fish in the graded group than the control group), yet Line 62 claims *"Mortality was negligible."* The manuscript never accounts for the missing 329 fish (whether due to mortality during bacterial cold water disease / *Ichthyobodo* outbreaks, jumping, or failed microsatellite parentage exclusion at $\ge 5$ of 6 loci) or which of the 16 families was dropped.
6. **Typographical Error in Size Bin Definition (Methods, Line 45):** The small size bin is written as *"small (97 mm FL)"* instead of `<97 mm FL` or `≤97 mm FL`.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Initially small fish failed to catch up in growth to medium or control fish despite low rearing density and high food availability, indicating inherent physiological/behavioral growth limitations rather than competitive suppression by larger fish.* `[Moderately Supported]` — This is the strongest and most biologically informative finding of the study (Fig 3), demonstrating that removing large competitors and providing excess feed is insufficient to rescue slow-growing steelhead juveniles.
- **Claim 2:** *Grading significantly reduced total variance in size at release by 25% and reduced the opportunity for selection among individuals by 20.9%.* `[Methodologically Flawed]` — The reduction in right-tail variance was achieved by starving and crowding the initially large fish in a single unreplicated tank to such a degree that they ended up *smaller* than the medium and control groups (Fig 3), significantly reducing the mean fork length of the entire graded cohort by 0.49 cm ($P = 2.61 \times 10^{-7}$). Because marine survival in salmonids is positively correlated with absolute size at release, artificially stunting the fastest-growing fish reduces expected cohort survival rather than equalizing fitness.
- **Claim 3:** *Grading reduced among-family variance by 39% (P = 0.076) and ICC from 0.21 to 0.15.* `[Fragile]` — Neither metric is statistically significant (95% CIs for ICC broadly overlap: 0.073–0.35 vs. 0.040–0.27), and the reported $P = 0.076$ is derived from a misspecified Fligner-Killeen test ($\chi^2 = 40.51, df = 29$).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Code Availability:** A public GitHub repository is linked in the Data Availability statement, and feed guidelines are referenced in Supporting Information (`S1 File`).
- **Missing Metadata:** Per-tank sample sizes at grading (day 143) and at harvest (day 297), per-tank mean fork lengths *prior* to mean-centering adjustment, microsatellite genotyping exclusion rates per tank, and the spawn-group identity (March 25 chilled vs. April 7 ambient) of families A–O are not reported and should be tabulated to allow proper mixed-model reanalysis.
