### 1. Executive Summary
This methodological study evaluates "T-shaped alignments"—multiple sequence alignments combining near full-length (nFL, $\sim 9\text{ kb}$) HIV-1 genomes with partial *pol* (PRRT, $\sim 1\text{--}1.3\text{ kb}$) sequences where non-*pol* sites in the partial sequences are masked as missing data (`-`/`?`)—to determine how the proportion of nFL sequences ($0\%$ to $100\%$) affects maximum-likelihood phylogenetic reconstruction (`IQ-TREE`, GTR+F+I+G4) and midpoint-rooted bootstrap cluster inference (bootstrap thresholds $70, 80, 85, 90, 95, 99$). Using two empirical HIV-1 subtype B datasets from the Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) database (a global full dataset of $N = 1,196$ sequences and a single-study subset of $N = 116$ sequences), the authors generate 100 random taxon-order permutations across 13 nFL:pol mixture ratios ($13,000$ total alignments) and compare each inferred tree and cluster partition against the $100\%$ nFL (`wgs100`) reference using Robinson-Foulds (RF) distance, Clustering Information Distance (PCoA), Adjusted Mutual Information (AMI), and cluster overlap coefficients. They find a striking non-monotonic behavior: in the full dataset, adding $10\%\text{--}40\%$ nFL sequences fails to improve (and at `wgs20` degrades) topological and clustering congruence relative to a $100\%$ *pol* alignment, with improvements emerging only when $>50\%$ of sequences are nFL (`wgs50+`). While the empirical observation of non-monotonic accuracy in patchy/T-shaped alignments is important and practically relevant to molecular HIV surveillance, the study leaves the core phylogenetic mechanism unresolved, uses a pure bootstrap-only clustering definition without genetic distance or branch-length criteria, and overlooks a classic phylogenetic artifact of unpartitioned ML inference on patchy alignments (branch-length distortion and bootstrap inflation/deflation across taxa with $85\%$ missing characters).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unpartitioned GTR+F+I+G4 Model on Unaligned/Masked Multi-Gene Concatenations and Missing-Data Branch-Length Distortion:**
   In `IQ-TREE`, the authors fit a single unpartitioned `GTR+F+I+G4` model across the entire $\sim 9\text{ kb}$ HIV-1 genome where `pol`-only taxa have $\sim 85\%$ missing sites (`-`). While the authors tested fixing the global GTR substitution rates and $\Gamma$ shape parameter to the `wgs100` estimates (lines 164–166) and found no change in topology, they overlooked the two primary ways `IQ-TREE` is affected by T-shaped alignments:
   - **Edge-length scaling and rate heterogeneity across genomic regions:** Because *pol* is significantly more conserved than *env* and accessory genes, the mean substitution rate per site in *pol* is much lower than the genome-wide average. In an unpartitioned ML tree, branch lengths are estimated in expected substitutions per site scaled to the global alignment rate. For two `pol`-only sequences, their likelihood only evaluates sites in *pol* (low divergence), whereas for two `wgs` sequences, their likelihood evaluates the whole genome (high divergence in *env*/*gag*/*nef*). Without a partitioned edge-linked proportional model (`-p` / `-spp` in IQ-TREE, which estimates separate region-specific rate multipliers $r_{\text{pol}}$ vs. $r_{\text{non-pol}}$), the ML optimizer systematically underestimates branch lengths for `pol`-only tips relative to `wgs` tips and distorts internal branch lengths where `wgs` and `pol` lineages coalesce! This directly explains why `pol` sequences preferentially cluster together (`0.64` proportion at `wgs50`, line 156) and why midpoint rooting (`midpoint clustering algorithm`, line 50) shifts erratically when a minority ($10\text{--}30\%$) of tips have longer genome-wide branch lengths (`wgs`) than the rest of the tree (`pol`)!
   - **Non-parametric vs. Ultrafast Bootstrap Resampling of T-Shaped Alignments:** The Methods (line 50) state that IQ-TREE was used *"to generate phylogenies with bootstrapping"*, without specifying whether standard non-parametric bootstrap (`-b`) or Ultrafast Bootstrap (`-bb` / `-B`) was run, nor whether resampling was partitioned by region. In unpartitioned bootstrap resampling of a $9\text{ kb}$ alignment where $85\%$ of columns are completely empty (`-`) for `pol`-only taxa, random bootstrap replicates have highly variable numbers of non-missing *pol* columns ($\text{Binomial}(9000, p_{\text{pol}})$), which artificially deflates bootstrap support for splits involving `pol`-only taxa relative to a pure $1.3\text{ kb}$ *pol* alignment where 100% of resampled columns fall within *pol*!Look at Figure 6A: as soon as `wgs` increases from `wgs0` (`pol`, length $1.3\text{ kb}$) to `wgs10`–`wgs50` (length $9\text{ kb}$, $85\%$ gaps for most taxa), the number of clusters at bootstrap $\ge 90\text{--}99\%$ plummets! That drop occurs because switching from a $1.3\text{ kb}$ alignment (`wgs0`) to a $9\text{ kb}$ padded alignment (`wgs10`) dilutes the bootstrap resampling of the informative *pol* columns by a factor of $\sim 7\times$ for the $90\%$ of taxa that only have *pol* data!
2. **Wait—Did `wgs0` (`pol`) Have 85% Gaps or Was It Trimmed? Critical Experimental Control Question:**
   In line 48, the authors state: *"Eleven of the datasets had mixtures in increments of 10% of the taxa being nFL in the alignment starting from 0%, i.e. from 10% up to 100%..."* If `wgs0` (`pol`) had 100% of sequences masked outside PRRT with gaps (`-`), `IQ-TREE` automatically strips 100%-gap columns during alignment reading, reducing `wgs0` to $\sim 1,300$ columns, whereas `wgs10` retains all $\sim 9,000$ columns because $10\%$ of taxa have non-gap characters outside PRRT! Consequently, in `wgs0`, every bootstrap replicate draws $1,300$ columns from PRRT, whereas in `wgs10`, each bootstrap replicate draws $9,000$ columns from the whole genome, so the $90\%$ `pol`-only taxa only get $\sim 1,300$ informative draws on average (with Poisson variance) while $7,700$ resampled columns are pure missing data for $90\%$ of the tree, destabilizing `UFBoot`/`bootstrap` support and creating rogue-taxon behavior! Testing a **partitioned bootstrap** (where bootstrap resampling is stratified within the `pol` partition and `non-pol` partition separately, `-p` in IQ-TREE) would directly test and likely resolve this exact phenomenon.
3. **Omission of Genetic Distance / Branch-Length Criteria in HIV Cluster Definition:**
   In public health HIV molecular surveillance (e.g., CDC HIV-TRACE, Cluster Picker, PhyloPart), transmission clusters are never defined by bootstrap support alone; they require both clade support AND a maximum pairwise genetic distance or patristic branch-length threshold (typically $0.5\%\text{--}4.5\%$ in *pol*), because a deep phylogenetic clade uniting hundreds of unrelated subtype B sequences from different decades can easily have $99\%$ bootstrap support without representing an epidemiological transmission cluster! Using a pure bootstrap-only midpoint-clustering rule on a global LANL database ($N = 1,196$ mostly unlinked subtype B sequences collected globally up to 2018) groups deep phylogenetic sub-lineages rather than actual epidemiological transmission clusters.
4. **Statistical Testing Across 13 Mixture Levels Without Multiple-Testing Adjustment or Effect Sizes (Line 68):**
   The authors apply one-tailed paired $t$-tests across $n = 100$ random masking permutations without reporting adjusted $p$-values across the multiple mixture levels and bootstrap thresholds, and without reporting the exact numerical RF distances and AMI values in the main text alongside the $p$-values.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Under unpartitioned ML inference in IQ-TREE, T-shaped alignments with $<50\%$ nFL sequences in the global dataset ($<30\%$ in the single-study subset) do not improve topological or bootstrap-clustering congruence with the $100\%$ nFL reference tree relative to a *pol*-only alignment. `[Well Supported]` — Within the exact pipeline tested by the authors, the empirical U-shaped/threshold behavior across 100 random replicates is clearly demonstrated by RF distance, PCoA, AMI, and overlap coefficients.
- **Claim 2:** The failure of low-proportion T-shaped alignments is not caused by fast-evolving `gp120` sites or global GTR+F+I+G4 substitution rate parameters, but involves preferential clustering of `pol` tips. `[Moderately Supported]` — The `gp120`-masking and fixed-GTR-parameter experiments cleanly rule out those two hypotheses, and the observation that `pol` sequences are overrepresented in clusters ($64\%$ at `wgs50`) is accurate. However, as noted in the audit above, the authors missed the underlying algorithmic cause: unpartitioned branch-length scaling between conserved `pol` and faster-evolving `non-pol` regions combined with unstratified bootstrap column resampling across $85\%$-gapped alignments.
- **Claim 3:** T-shaped alignments improve HIV-1 transmission cluster inference for public health when $>50\%$ of sequences are nFL. `[Fragile]` — Because `wgs100` on a global LANL dataset lacks known ground-truth transmission pairs, and because bootstrap-only clustering without a genetic distance threshold identifies deep clades rather than recent transmission clusters, extrapolating this $50\%$ rule to real-world public health contact-tracing networks requires validation on simulated transmission trees with known ground truth or partitioned ML models.
