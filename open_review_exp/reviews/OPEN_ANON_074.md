### 1. Executive Summary
This retrospective observational cohort study uses the US Veterans Health Administration (VHA) COVID-19 Shared Data Resource ($N = 499,396$ veterans tested for SARS-CoV-2 between March 15 and November 30, 2020) to compare 3-year incident ICD-10 cancer diagnoses between patients who tested positive ($n = 85,680$) and those who tested negative ($n = 413,716$). Using multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression starting 30 days post-test, the authors report a 25% reduction in incident cancer hazard among COVID-19-positive veterans ($\text{aHR} = 0.75$, 95% CI: $0.73\text{--}0.77$), with reductions across all 10 major cancer types and progressively larger reductions in elderly strata (reaching $\text{aHR} = 0.63$ in ages 85–99). They attribute this uniform pan-cancer reduction to putative anticancer immunity from SARS-CoV-2 ssRNA-induced non-classical monocytes. However, the study suffers from fatal epidemiological design flaws—including conditioning on future exposure status in the control group definition (or massive unmeasured cross-over), diagnostic/pre-procedural testing ascertainment bias in 2020, failure to account for the competing risk of death in elderly veterans, and multiple glaring arithmetic and sample-size contradictions between the Abstract, Results, and Tables.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Conditioning on the Future / Immortal Exposure Selection Bias in Control Group Definition:** In the Methods ("Study design, data sources, and study population"), the authors state: *"The control group included Veterans who had a negative test result in this time frame and did not report positive test results in the next three years."* Defining a baseline control cohort by conditioning on remaining free of a positive test over the subsequent 3-year follow-up period introduces severe immortal-time/selection bias. Directly contradicting the Methods, the Discussion ("Strengths and limitations") states: *"Veterans were maintained in this initial grouping for the three-year follow-up period, even if those in the initial COVID-19 negative group may have subsequently developed an infection at a later period during the follow-up window."* Both cannot be true: either the control cohort was conditioned on future non-infection over 3 years, or massive unmeasured exposure crossover occurred during the 2021–2023 Delta and Omicron waves.
2. **Pre-Procedural Diagnostic Testing Bias (Reverse Causation / Ascertainment Bias):** Between March and November 2020 (the first wave), SARS-CoV-2 testing in the VHA was not performed at random; negative tests were heavily enriched among patients undergoing mandatory pre-procedural or pre-admission SARS-CoV-2 screening prior to colonoscopies, bronchoscopies, biopsies, imaging, or oncologic surgeries for suspected malignancies. Because follow-up started just 30 days after the index test, patients tested negative during pre-diagnostic workups were subsequently coded with their incident ICD-10 cancer diagnosis shortly after day 30, artificially inflating cancer incidence in the COVID-19-negative comparator group. Conversely, patients who tested positive for acute COVID-19 had elective screenings and diagnostic workups deferred. Providing a landmark sensitivity analysis excluding the first 6 or 12 months after testing, or comparing against an untested active VHA primary-care cohort matched on healthcare utilization, was not performed.
3. **Failure to Model Competing Risk of Death in Elderly Strata:** Overall cohort mortality during the 3-year follow-up was $16.9\%$ ($84,542$ deaths after already excluding deaths within the first 30 days). The authors used standard cause-specific Cox regression where patients were simply censored at death, rather than a Fine–Gray competing-risks subdistribution hazard model. In older veterans (ages 75–84 and 85–99), post-COVID-19 morbidity, frailty, and competing mortality strongly reduce the probability of undergoing routine cancer screening (e.g., PSA testing, colonoscopy, low-dose CT) and surviving long enough to receive a new cancer diagnosis. This directly explains why the apparent "protective" association is completely absent in younger adults (ages 18–24: $\text{aHR} = 1.43$ [0.44–4.58]; ages 24–34: $\text{aHR} = 0.86$ [0.66–1.14]; ages 35–44: $\text{aHR} = 0.93$ [0.80–1.10]) and monotonically increases with advancing age (ages 75–84: $\text{aHR} = 0.73$; ages 85–99: $\text{aHR} = 0.63$, Table 2).
4. **Multiple Internal Arithmetic and Sample-Size Contradictions:**
   - **Abstract vs. Table 1 / Results Cohort Numbers:** The Abstract states: *"499,396 patients were included in this study, with 88590 (17.2%) COVID-19 positive, 427566 (82.8%) COVID-19 negative."* First, $88,590 + 427,566 = 516,156 \ne 499,396$. Second, the Results text and Table 1 report entirely different group counts: $413,716$ COVID-19 negative and $85,680$ COVID-19 positive ($413,716 + 85,680 = 499,396$).
   - **Table 1 Age Group Percentages Sum to 121.6%:** In Table 1 under `COVID-19 Positive (n = 85,680)`, age group `19-64` is reported as `51980 (60.7%)` and age group `65` is reported as `33700 (60.9%)` ($60.7\% + 60.9\% = 121.6\%$; $33,700 / 85,680$ is actually $39.3\%$).
   - **Table 1 Mortality Count Discrepancy:** In Table 1, COVID-19 Negative deaths (`69,520`) plus COVID-19 Positive deaths (`14,890`) equals $84,410$, which does not match the reported Total Mortality of `84,542` (off by 132 deaths).
   - **Table 5 Missing Cancer Events and Denominator Mismatch:** In Table 2 (across all age groups) and Table 3 ($52,729$ in men + $3,111$ in women), the total number of incident cancer events is $55,840$. Yet in Table 5 (stratified by COVID-19 severity), total events sum to $47,614 + 5,831 + 1,079 + 329 = 54,853$—leaving $987$ cancer events unaccounted for. Furthermore, $47,614$ events in $413,716$ COVID-19-negative patients is $11.51\%$, not `11.7%` as printed in Table 5.
   - **Direct Self-Contradiction Regarding the Asian Subgroup:** The Abstract and Discussion state that the reduction in cancer hazard was similar across races *"except in Asians."* However, the Results section (line 118) states: *"Regarding race and ethnicity, the reduction in new cancer diagnoses was similar between white, black, Asian, and Hispanic populations (Table 4)."* In Table 4, the adjusted hazard ratio for Asians is $\text{aHR} = 0.73$ (95% CI: $0.47\text{--}1.15$, $n = 251$ events)—a point estimate identical to White ($\text{aHR} = 0.74$), Hispanic ($\text{aHR} = 0.76$), and Black ($\text{aHR} = 0.78$) veterans. The authors conflated lower statistical power in the smallest racial subgroup ($1.2\%$ of the cohort) with absence of an effect, while contradicting their own Results text.
5. **Overlapping Age Category Labels in Table 2:** Table 2 lists overlapping age intervals `18-24` and `24-34` (whilst Table 1 starts at age `19-64`).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** SARS-CoV-2 infection during the first wave is associated with a 25% biological reduction in 3-year incident cancer risk across all major solid and hematologic malignancies ($\text{aHR} = 0.57\text{--}0.84$). — `[Methodologically Flawed]` / `[Overstated]`. Solid tumors (prostate, lung, colorectal, pancreatic, bladder, liver, kidney, oral, melanoma) develop over 5–30+ years of somatic clonal evolution. Observing an immediate, uniform 20–43% drop in new diagnoses across every solid and hematologic organ system within 36 months—coupled with an inverse severity gradient where mild outpatient cases show the largest drop ($\text{aHR} = 0.72$) and severe hospitalized cases show no significant drop ($\text{aHR} = 0.92$, 95% CI: $0.82\text{--}1.02$, likely because hospitalized patients receive intensive inpatient imaging and laboratory workups)—is characteristic of diagnostic ascertainment and pre-procedural testing bias rather than viral oncolysis or immune surveillance.
- **Claim 2:** Acute COVID-19 ssRNA-induced non-classical monocytes and TLR7/HLA-DR upregulation provide a plausible pan-cancer protective mechanism in humans. — `[Overstated]`. No immunological, monocyte, or tumor microenvironment data were collected in this EHR study; extrapolating preclinical murine lung-metastasis monocyte findings to a 3-year reduction in human primary prostate, bladder, and oral cancer incidence is speculative.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw clinical data reside behind the VHA VINCI firewall and cannot be shared publicly, which is standard for VHA studies.
- **Analytic & Protocol Transparency:** The manuscript does not provide the exact ICD-10 code lists used to define incident cancer outcomes or baseline comorbidities, does not report how BMI and categorical "Overweight" ($6.9\%$ in Table 1, an implausibly low prevalence for a US Veteran cohort unless defined by administrative ICD codes rather than measured BMI) and "Obesity" were simultaneously parameterized in the Cox models without collinearity, and does not provide R scripts or Schoenfeld residual diagnostic plots.
