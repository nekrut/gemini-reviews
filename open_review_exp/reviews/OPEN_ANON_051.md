### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript presents iSORT, a transfer-learning computational framework that integrates single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptomics (ST) data by combining Seurat reference-based co-embedding ($z = h(x)$), Kullback-Leibler Importance Estimation Procedure (KLIEP) density ratio weighting ($w(z) = p_{\text{sc}}(z)/p_{\text{st}}(z)$), and a multi-layer perceptron regression network ($y = g(z)$) to map single-cell gene expression vectors directly to continuous 2D spatial coordinates ($y = f(x) = g(h(x))$). From this learned mapping, the authors define two downstream quantities: (1) a Spatial-Organizing Gene (SOG) index based on the input-output Jacobian sensitivity ($I_g = \|\partial_{x_g} f(x)\|$), and (2) "SpaRNA velocity" ($v_{\text{st}} = f(x + v_{\text{RNA}}) - f(x)$), which projects scTour-inferred transcriptomic RNA velocity vectors into physical space. The authors benchmark iSORT on public datasets (human DLPFC, mouse embryo seqFISH, mouse brain, Drosophila embryo FISH, human MTG, and developing human heart) and generate new wet-lab scRNA-seq ($n = 2$) and 10x Visium ST ($n = 3$) datasets from normal and atherosclerotic human coronary arteries.

While density-ratio reweighting is a sensible approach to handle cell-type compositional shifts between dissociated scRNA-seq and ST slices, the framework suffers from three fundamental methodological and conceptual flaws: (1) formulating spatial reconstruction as a deterministic point regression $y = f(x)$ trained under mean squared error (MSE) loss collapses identically expressed cells in non-contiguous spatial locations to their conditional spatial expectation $\mathbb{E}[y|z]$; (2) the "in-silico knockout validation" of SOGs is mathematically circular, testing whether zeroing out the highest-gradient input features of the neural network $f(x)$ alters the output of $f(x)$ more than zeroing out low-gradient features; and (3) applying "SpaRNA velocity" to adult post-mortem human DLPFC cortical layers and interpreting the resulting vectors as physical cell migration/growth trajectories from white matter to Layer 1 is biologically invalid.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Deterministic Regression Collapse vs. One-to-Many Spatial Mapping (Eqs. 4–7):** In Eq. 4, the authors postulate $p_{\text{st}}(y|z) = p_{\text{sc}}(y|z)$ and then replace this conditional probability distribution with a deterministic neural network mapping $y = g(z) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ trained via MSE loss $L(z, y; g) = \|g(z) - y\|^2$ (Eq. 7). Mathematically, minimizing weighted MSE yields the conditional expectation $g^*(z) = \mathbb{E}[y|z]$. In biological tissues where cells sharing a transcriptomic state $z$ (e.g., vascular endothelial cells, microglia, or dispersed interneurons) are distributed across multiple non-contiguous regions or concentric rings, $\mathbb{E}[y|z]$ maps all such cells to the geometric center of mass of their spatial distribution rather than reconstructing their true spatial dispersion. This explains why the authors had to apply a manual polar coordinate transformation prior to running iSORT on the circular human artery sections (Line 119; Note F), because an unmodified MLP minimizing Euclidean MSE on a ring collapses cells into the lumen or onto a single arc.
2. **Circularity of the In-Silico Knockout Validation for SOGs (Lines 109–115, Eq. 13):** The SOG index is defined as the average $L_1$/Jacobian sensitivity of the trained neural network with respect to gene $g$: $I_g = \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p=1}^2 \left|\frac{\partial y_p}{\partial x_g}\right|\right]$ (Eq. 13). To "validate" that SOGs—rather than spatially variable genes (SVGs) from Moran's $I$ or SpatialDE—drive tissue organization, the authors zero out ("knock out") the top 20 or 300 SOGs in the input vector $x$ and evaluate the MSE of the neural network reconstruction $f(x_{\text{-KO}})$ on DLPFC slice ID151674 (Fig. 5a–f). By definition of a first-order Taylor expansion $f(x - \Delta x_g) - f(x) \approx -\nabla_{x_g} f(x) \cdot x_g$, removing the features with the largest partial derivatives $\|\partial_{x_g} f(x)\|$ will maximize the perturbation to $f(x)$. This experiment merely confirms that $I_g$ measures the sensitivity of the fitted neural network $f$; it provides zero independent biological evidence that those genes causally drive morphogenesis or spatial organization in vivo.
3. **Homologous Self-Reconstruction and Coarse-Grained Leakage in Benchmarks (Lines 50, 58, 94, 179–184):** In the mouse embryo seqFISH (Line 58) and Drosophila embryo FISH (Line 94) benchmarks, the "scRNA-seq" query is created by stripping the spatial coordinates from the single-cell FISH dataset, while the "low-resolution ST reference" is generated by aggregating the *exact same cells* from the *exact same specimen* onto a regular spatial grid (Lines 183–184). Because the query cells are the exact constituents of the reference spots without any biological, donor, or technical platform variation, a neural network interpolator can trivially invert the grid aggregation. Similarly, in the DLPFC benchmark (Fig. 2a), the query (ID151674) is a 10x Visium spot dataset (not single-cell RNA-seq) stripped of coordinates and mapped against an immediately adjacent serial section (ID151675) from the same brain donor.
4. **Unfair Benchmarking Against Spot Deconvolution Methods (Lines 50–51, Fig. 2a–b):** In Fig. 2a–b, iSORT is compared against five spot deconvolution algorithms (RCTD, CARD, Redeconve, CytoSPACE, Celloc) by assigning each spot a single discrete label corresponding to the dominant deconvolved cell type ("selected the cell type with the highest proportion in each spot as the dominant cell type") and then evaluating spatial clustering metrics ($S_C, \rho_C, A_C, P_C$). Spot deconvolution methods estimate cell-type proportions *within* fixed reference spots (ID151675), whereas iSORT predicts continuous coordinates for query spots (ID151674). Converting continuous proportion vectors into hard argmax labels on a mismatched reference slice geometry artificially penalizes deconvolution methods.
5. **Lack of Biological Replication in the Human Artery Cohort (Lines 117–120):** The primary wet-lab validation in human atherosclerosis comprises only $n = 1$ normal artery scRNA-seq sample, $n = 1$ atherosclerotic (AS) artery scRNA-seq sample, $n = 2$ normal ST slices, and $n = 1$ AS ST slice. With $n = 1$ AS patient per modality, individual donor genetics, plaque stage, and technical batch effects are completely confounded with disease status, precluding statistical inference on differential SOGs between normal and AS arteries.
6. **Gaussian Noise Simulation on Preprocessed Data (Lines 89, 186–206):** In the noise sensitivity analysis, Gaussian noise $N_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma \cdot \text{Var}(X_j))$ is added to expression values followed by $\max(X_{ij} + N_{ij}, 0)$ (Line 202). However, the preprocessing pipeline (Eq. 2) applies $z$-score standardization $x_{ij} \leftarrow (x_{ij} - \mu_i)/\sigma_i$, which yields negative values for below-average expression. Applying $\max(\cdot, 0)$ after $z$-scoring truncates all below-mean expression values to zero, whereas if applied to raw counts, Gaussian noise violates the discrete overdispersed count nature (negative binomial/Poisson) of scRNA-seq UMI data.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *iSORT accurately reconstructs continuous spatial organization across homologous and heterologous ST references.* `[Moderately Supported]` — On DLPFC serial sections and mouse brain datasets, iSORT recovers cortical lamination patterns and shows reasonable robustness across rotated and multi-slice references (Fig. 3), though continuous predictions remain restricted to smooth manifolds dictated by the MLP architecture.
- **Claim 2:** *SOGs identified by $I_g = \|\partial_{x_g} f(x)\|$ represent genes with "dynamical causality" that causally drive tissue spatial patterning, as validated by in-silico knockouts.* `[Methodologically Flawed]` — A partial derivative of a static regression network mapping expression to coordinates measures predictor salience (how strongly the MLP relies on gene $g$ to predict $(y_1, y_2)$), not causal developmental morphogenesis.
- **Claim 3:** *SpaRNA velocity reconstructs physical "pseudo-growth trajectories" in the human DLPFC from white matter through Layers 6–2 to Layer 1 (Lines 126, 132, Fig. 6f).* `[Overstated]` / `[Methodologically Flawed]` — First, the human DLPFC dataset (Maynard et al.) consists of *adult post-mortem* human brain tissue; adult cortical neurons are post-mitotic and are not actively migrating from white matter to Layer 1. Second, even during embryonic cortical neurogenesis, Layer 1 (the marginal zone containing Cajal-Retzius cells) is formed *first* (prior to Layer 6), whereas the inside-out migration of radially migrating excitatory neurons proceeds from the ventricular zone to Layer 6 $\to$ Layer 5 $\to$ Layer 4 $\to$ Layer 3 $\to$ Layer 2. Claiming that adult DLPFC cells grow sequentially from WM through L6–L2 into L1 misrepresents both adult neurobiology and embryonic corticogenesis.
- **Claim 4:** *SOGs identified in the human artery dataset (e.g., TNN, RFLNA, SPN, EZH2) are atherosclerosis biomarkers that regulate vascular spatial structure.* `[Fragile]` — Derived from $n = 1$ AS scRNA-seq sample without independent cohort validation or experimental perturbation.
