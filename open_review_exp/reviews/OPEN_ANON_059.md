### 1. Executive Summary
This field surveillance study investigates the occurrence and molecular diversity of trypanosomatid parasites in urban phyllostomid bats captured in Mangabeiras Municipal Park (MMP), Belo Horizonte, Minas Gerais, Brazil, across six sampling nights in 2022. From 56 captured bats representing seven species (predominantly *Artibeus lituratus*, $n = 28$, and *Sturnira lilium*, $n = 12$), the authors inoculated 149 blood, tissue, and organ samples from 52 bats into biphasic NNN/LIT culture medium (yielding 0 positive cultures and 32.2% fungal/bacterial contamination) and screened 186 samples by nested PCR targeting the V7V8 hypervariable region of the 18S SSU rRNA gene followed by Sanger sequencing. Nested PCR detected trypanosomatid DNA in 11 samples from 9 bats, identifying *Leishmania infantum*, *Leishmania braziliensis*, *Trypanosoma* sp. Neobat 3, and *Trypanosoma* sp. Neobat 4, including a putative mixed infection (*L. infantum* in heart and *L. braziliensis* in blood) in one *Platyrrhinus lineatus* individual.

While the study provides useful baseline molecular surveillance data from an urban park endemic for visceral and cutaneous leishmaniasis, the manuscript contains multiple internal arithmetic errors in its sample counts and summary tables (Table 2 conflates infected animal counts with positive sample counts across rows and columns so that none of the marginal percentages match their numerators/denominators), provides an incomplete accounting of the 186 PCR-screened samples vs. 149 cultured samples, and relies solely on a single conserved 561 bp 18S rDNA amplicon (without confirmatory species-specific targets such as `hsp70` or `ITS1`) to differentiate *Leishmania* species when all four *Leishmania* reference strains were run concurrently as nested-PCR positive controls.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Severe Internal Arithmetic and Denominator Contradictions in Table 2 and Results Text (Lines 92, 101–117, 132):** Table 2 and the accompanying text mix up **positive bats ($N = 9$)**, **positive infections ($N = 10$, counting the mixed infection twice)**, and **positive tissue samples ($N = 11$)**, resulting in mathematically inconsistent numbers throughout:
   - **Discrepancy between text and Table 2 for *Artibeus lituratus*:** Line 92 states: *"Artibeus lituratus presenting DNA of Leishmania infantum (N = 3) and Trypanosoma sp. Neobat 3 (N = 1)"* (total = 4 detections). However, Table 2 (Line 109) lists `2 H,L,S` under *L. infantum* and `1 B,L` under *Trypanosoma* sp. Neobat 3, with a row total of `3 (45,4)`.
   - **Inconsistent denominators in the `Total (%)` column of Table 2:** In the rightmost column of Table 2, the integer counts are `1, 3, 1, 1, 2, 1`, which sum to **9** (`9 (100)` in the bottom right cell). However, the percentages in parentheses next to those exact integers are `(9,1)`, `(45,4)`, `(9,1)`, `(9,1)`, `(18,2)`, and `(9,1)`—which are calculated by dividing `1, 5, 1, 1, 2, 1` by a denominator of **11** ($1/11 = 9.1\%$, $5/11 = 45.4\%$, $2/11 = 18.2\%$), not 9!
   - **Inconsistent denominators in the bottom `Total (%)` row of Table 2:** In the bottom row of Table 2 (Line 114) and Abstract/Discussion (Line 132), the column totals across the four parasite taxa are listed as: *L. infantum* = `5 (50.0)`, *L. braziliensis* = `1 (12.5)`, *T.* sp. Neobat 3 = `2 (25.0)`, *T.* sp. Neobat 4 = `1 (12.5)`, and Total = `9 (100)`. First, the integer counts in the *L. infantum* column above it (`0 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0`) sum to **5**, which means the four parasite column totals sum to $5 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 9$ detections (8 single infections + 1 mixed bat counted in two columns = 9 positive detections across 8 bats, or if *A. lituratus* had 3 *L. infantum* bats as stated on Line 92, then $6 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 10$ detections across 9 bats!). Second, $1/9 = 11.1\%$ (not $12.5\%$), $2/9 = 22.2\%$ (not $25.0\%$), and $5/9 = 55.6\%$ (not $50.0\%$). The percentages `12.5%` and `25.0%` correspond to a denominator of **8** ($1/8$ and $2/8$), whereas `50.0%` corresponds to $4/8$ or $5/10$! Table 2 must be completely audited and reconstructed with separate, unambiguous columns for **Number of Positive Bats ($N = 9$)** and **Number of Positive Tissue Samples ($N = 11$, Corresponding to `OR656589`–`OR656599`)**.
2. **Arithmetic Error and Missing Sample Breakdown for Culture vs. PCR (Lines 42, 90, 92, 119):**
   - On Line 90, the authors state that **149 samples** were cultured in NNN/LIT medium, of which **48 samples (32.2%)** were contaminated and *"the remaining 104 samples (67.8%) showed no trypanosomatid growth"*. However, $48 + 104 = 152 \neq 149$! Since $101 / 149 = 67.79\% \approx 67.8\%$ and $48 + 101 = 149$, `104` is an arithmetic error for `101`.
   - Furthermore, Line 42 states that six sample types were collected per euthanized bat: blood, spleen, heart, liver, ear skin, and wing membrane. Yet the culture breakdown on Line 90 lists only five sample types: *"blood (N = 32), tissues (ear skin and wing membrane, N = 18 each), and organs (liver, N = 40; heart, N = 41)"* ($32 + 18 + 18 + 40 + 41 = 149$), omitting **spleen** entirely—even though Table 2 reports *L. infantum* detections in spleen (`S`) and Line 119 states that 7 of the 11 PCR-positive samples were culture-negative, 1 was contaminated, and 3 were not collected for culture. Meanwhile, Line 92 states that **186 samples** were tested by V7V8 nested PCR (giving $11 / 186 = 5.9\%$ sample positivity), without ever providing the breakdown of those 186 samples by organ/tissue type or bat species.
3. **Reliance on a Single Conserved 18S rDNA Marker and Nested-PCR Contamination Risk for *Leishmania* Species Identification (Lines 54–59, Fig. 3B):**
   - While the V7V8 SSU rRNA region reliably distinguishes *Trypanosoma* clades (such as *T.* sp. Neobat 3 and Neobat 4 within the *T. wauwau* clade, Fig. 3A), the 18S rRNA gene is highly conserved across *Leishmania* species, where *L. infantum*, *L. amazonensis*, *L. braziliensis*, and *L. guyanensis* differ by only a few nucleotides across the 561 bp SSU561 amplicon (as reflected by the short branch lengths and modest bootstrap support within Leishmaniinae in Fig. 3B).
   - Critically, in all nested PCR runs, reference DNA of *L. amazonensis*, *L. braziliensis*, *L. infantum*, and *L. guyanensis* was amplified simultaneously as positive controls (Line 56). Because two-round nested PCR is exceptionally prone to carryover aerosol contamination from positive control amplicons and 0 of 149 cultures grew flagellates, confirming *L. infantum* and *L. braziliensis* tissue infections (especially the mixed infection in *P. lineatus*) with an independent polymorphic locus (such as `hsp70`, `ITS1`, or `cytb`) would substantially strengthen diagnostic certainty.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Bats in Mangabeiras Municipal Park harbor Trypanosoma sp. Neobat 3 (in A. lituratus and for the first time in S. lilium) and Trypanosoma sp. Neobat 4 (in A. caudifer).* `[Well Supported]` — The V7V8 SSU rRNA marker is the standard barcoding locus on which the *Trypanosoma* spp. Neobat 1–6 MOTUs are defined, cannot be attributed to laboratory reference control contamination (as only *Leishmania* strains were used as positive controls), and shows clear phylogenetic placement in the *T. wauwau* clade (Fig. 3A).
- **Claim 2:** *Four phyllostomid bat species in MMP carry L. infantum DNA and P. lineatus carries a mixed L. infantum / L. braziliensis infection.* `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by Sanger sequencing of the 561 bp 18S rDNA nested amplicon and endogenous host gene (`cytb`/`gamma-actin`) quality controls, though unconfirmed by parasitological isolation or secondary species-specific *Leishmania* markers (`hsp70`/`ITS1`).
- **Claim 3:** *Urban bats act as reservoirs maintaining L. infantum and L. braziliensis transmission cycles in MMP.* `[Overstated]` — Detecting parasite DNA in tissues by nested PCR (with 0% culture recovery and 0% skin/ear membrane positivity) demonstrates *host exposure/infection*, not *reservoir competence* (which requires demonstrating viable parasites capable of infecting sand fly vectors via xenodiagnosis or skin parasitism). While the Abstract uses cautious language ("potential reservoirs", "hosts"), the Discussion (Lines 134, 152) should clearly distinguish incidental/dead-end infection from proven reservoir competence.
