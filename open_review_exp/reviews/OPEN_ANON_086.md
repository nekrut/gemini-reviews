### 1. Executive Summary
This cross-sectional observational study characterizes the fecal 16S rRNA microbiota of 44 post-menopausal Brazilian women (aged 45–60 years) stratified by duration since menopause: Group A ($n = 34$, $\le 10$ years post-menopause, mean age $54.7 \pm 3.26$ years) and Group B ($n = 10$, $\ge 10$ years post-menopause, mean age $57.3 \pm 2.86$ years). Using V3–V4 16S rRNA metabarcoding processed via QIIME2/DADA2, the authors evaluate alpha diversity (Shannon, Simpson), beta diversity (Bray-Curtis, Jaccard PCoA, PERMANOVA, ANOSIM), and the top 10 taxa at the phylum, family, and genus levels. They report no significant differences in Shannon diversity ($p = 0.07$), beta diversity (PERMANOVA $p = 0.492$, ANOSIM $p = 0.98$), or any of the top 10 phyla, families, or genera, alongside a marginally higher Simpson diversity index in Group B ($p = 0.04$), concluding that the post-menopausal gut microbiota is resilient regardless of time elapsed since menopause. While the study addresses an underrepresented demographic cohort, it suffers from severe technical batch confounding (two different sequencing facilities, library prep kits, forward primers, sequencing platforms, and single-end vs. paired-end read modes were merged), arithmetic errors in Table 1 where Group B medication percentages were calculated using the wrong denominator ($N = 44$ instead of $n = 10$), and severely limited statistical power in Group B ($n = 10$).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Severe Technical Batch Confounding Across Sequencing Centers, Library Kits, Primers, Platforms, and Read Modes (Sections 3.3 & 3.4):** Section 3.3 reveals that the 44 samples were split across two incompatible wet-lab and sequencing workflows:
   - **38 samples** were prepared by *Neoprospecta Microbiome Technologies* using two-step amplicon PCR with primers **341F (`CCTACGGGRSGCAGCAG`) and 806R (`GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT`)** and sequenced **paired-end** on an **Illumina MiSeq (V2, 500 cycles)**.
   - **6 samples** were prepared by *Proteimax Biotecnologia Ltda* using the **Illumina Nextera XT DNA library prep kit** and primer **314F (`CCTACGGGNGGCWGCAG`, mislabeled 314F instead of 341F with different degenerate bases)** and sequenced **single-end** on an **Illumina NovaSeq 6000**.
   - In Section 3.4, single-end and paired-end samples were processed into separate DADA2 ASV tables and then merged. Because single-end reads and merged paired-end V3–V4 amplicons differ in length and primer sequence, their exact ASV sequences cannot overlap directly in a merged DADA2 ASV table (only post-taxonomic collapsing can merge them), and differences in read length, error models, and primer degeneracy directly bias ASV richness and Simpson dominance. Crucially, the manuscript never discloses how the 6 single-end NovaSeq samples were distributed between Group A ($n = 34$) and Group B ($n = 10$). If those 6 samples were concentrated in Group B, the platform/read-mode batch effect alone would explain the difference in Simpson's index ($p = 0.04$).
2. **Denominator Calculation Error in Table 1 for Group B Medication Percentages:** In Table 1, Group B has a sample size of $n = 10$, meaning any percentage of Group B participants must be a multiple of $10.0\%$ ($0\%, 10\%, 20\%, 30\%, \dots$). While disease prevalences in Group B are correctly reported as multiples of 10% (Hypertension 30%, Diabetes 20%, Osteoporosis 20%, Thyroid 30%, Neuro-psychiatric 40%), the specific medication class percentages for Group B are erroneously reported as:
   - `Antihypertensive: 4.5%` (actually $2 / 44 = 4.55\%$, which is $2 / 10 = 20.0\%$ of Group B)
   - `Antidiabetic: 4.5` ($2 / 44 = 4.55\%$, actually $20.0\%$ of Group B)
   - `Lipid-lowering agents: 2.2` ($1 / 44 = 2.27\%$, actually $10.0\%$ of Group B)
   - `Antidepressant/anxiolytic: 6.82` ($3 / 44 = 6.82\%$, actually $30.0\%$ of Group B)
   - `Thyroid agents: 4.5` ($2 / 44 = 4.55\%$, actually $20.0\%$ of Group B)
   - `Bone diseases: 2.2`, `Bronchodilators: 2.2`, `Others: 2.2` ($1 / 44 = 2.27\%$, actually $10.0\%$ of Group B)
   The authors divided Group B's medication counts by the *total study sample size ($N = 44$)* instead of Group B's sample size ($n = 10$), while dividing Group A's counts by $n = 34$. This error must be corrected in Table 1.
3. **Confounding Between Menopause Duration and Age at Menopause (Early Menopause in Group B):** In Table 1, Group A ($n = 34$) and Group B ($n = 10$) have nearly identical chronological ages ($54.7 \pm 3.26$ vs. $57.3 \pm 2.86$ years, differing by only 2.6 years), whereas their post-menopause duration differs by 9.4 years ($4.82 \pm 2.68$ vs. $14.2 \pm 5.37$ years). This occurs because Group B experienced menopause at a much younger age (**Last menstrual cycle: $43.8 \pm 5.88$ years in Group B vs. $50.6 \pm 3.14$ years in Group A**). Thus, Group B is heavily enriched for women with early or premature menopause (<45 years) and also exhibits a lower BMI ($26.98 \pm 5.04$ vs. $30.90 \pm 6.30\text{ kg/m}^2$) and lower income (60% vs. 17.6% earning $<1$ minimum wage). None of these baseline differences (nor p-values for Table 1 baseline variables) were tested or adjusted for.
4. **Unequal Sample Size Rarefaction Artifact in Table 3 ("Taxonomic Inference Found in Each Group"):** Table 3 reports that Group A ($n = 34$) contained 14 phyla (100%) and 150 putative species (97%), whereas Group B ($n = 10$) contained 10 phyla (71%) and 95 putative species (61%). Pooling 34 samples will inevitably capture more rare taxa than pooling 10 samples; presenting unrarefied pooled group taxon totals without sample-size standardization is misleading.
5. **Internal Inconsistencies in Database Version and Metadata:**
   - Section 3.4 (line 71) states taxonomic classification used the **SILVA 132** reference database, whereas the captions for Figures 6, 7, and 8 state **SILVA 138** was used.
   - Supporting Information line 285 contains a copy-paste contradiction: *"Spearman’s correlation... Parametric data was tested using the Shapiro-Wilk test"* (labeling non-parametric Spearman correlation as parametric).
   - Line 319 lists *"Collection date 2025"*, contradicting Section 1 (line 43) which states sample collection occurred between December 15, 2020 and March 5, 2021.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Women who have been post-menopausal for $\ge 10$ years have greater microbiota diversity and lower species dominance according to Simpson's index ($p = 0.04$).** `[Fragile]` This single marginal unadjusted p-value ($p = 0.04$ in an unbalanced $n = 34$ vs. $n = 10$ comparison, while Shannon diversity is non-significant at $p = 0.07$) is vulnerable to the unadjusted technical batch effect (6 single-end NovaSeq vs. 38 paired-end MiSeq samples), multiple testing across diversity metrics, and confounding by BMI and age at menopause.
- **Claim 2: The absence of differences in beta diversity and predominant taxa between women $\le 10$ and $\ge 10$ years post-menopause demonstrates that the post-menopausal gut microbiota is "resilient, resistant, and stable."** `[Overstated]` Equivalence/resilience cannot be established from failing to reject the null hypothesis in an underpowered cross-sectional comparison ($n = 10$ in Group B, no pre-menopausal control group, and no longitudinal follow-up).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw 16S sequencing reads are deposited in NCBI SRA under BioProject `PRJNA1223961`, and the bioinformatics pipeline is hosted in a public GitHub repository.
- **Metadata & Reporting Gaps:** Individual sample metadata mapping each of the 44 SRA accessions to Group A vs. Group B and specifying which 6 samples were sequenced single-end on the NovaSeq 6000 (Proteimax) vs. paired-end on the MiSeq (Neoprospecta) must be explicitly provided to allow independent evaluation of the technical batch effect.
