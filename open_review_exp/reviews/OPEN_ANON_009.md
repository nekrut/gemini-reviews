### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the transcriptomic and genetic basis of local adaptation in Japanese cedar (*Cryptomeria japonica*) by performing RNA-seq on current-year shoots harvested on hot summer days ($\ge 30^\circ\text{C}$) from 24 trees per site across three common gardens in northern (Miyagi, MYG), central (Ibaraki, IBR), and southern (Kumamoto, KMT) Japan (72 samples total representing 35 unique clones from 12 source populations spanning the *omote-sugi*, *ura-sugi*, and *yaku-sugi* lineages). Using genome-guided transcript assembly (`SUGI v1`, 77,212 transcripts from 56,203 genes), RNA-seq variant calling (16,620 LD-pruned SNPs), `pcadapt` outlier scans (151 outlier SNPs), and consensus WGCNA (25 modules across 8,255 variable transcripts), the authors report candidate adaptive SNPs and two co-expression modules (`grey60` enriched for defense genes, higher in *omote-sugi*; `darkred` containing terpenoid synthase *TPS03*, higher in *ura-sugi*) that consistently correlate with genetic differentiation ($\text{PC1}_{\text{SNP}}$) across all three gardens. While the multi-garden clonal design is a valuable forestry resource, the study has major statistical and methodological weaknesses: (1) none of the reported SNP–climate (40 SNPs) or expression–climate (9 genes) associations survived multiple-testing correction ($\text{FDR} < 0.1$), yet the authors reverted to an unadjusted $p \le 0.001$ threshold where the 9 hits match the expected number of false positives under the null; (2) Table 2 contains multiple internal mathematical inconsistencies in FDR values, transcript counts, and correlation $p$-values; and (3) common-garden site effects are confounded with sampling year (2020, 2021, 2022) and calendar month (June vs. August).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Reverting to Unadjusted $p \le 0.001$ After Complete Null Results Under Multiple-Testing Correction (Methods, Line 99; Results, Lines 142 & 144; Abstract, Line 8):** The authors tested 151 `pcadapt` outlier SNPs against 20 bioclimatic variables (`bio1`–`bio19` and `CTD`, i.e., $151 \times 20 = 3,020$ Kruskal-Wallis tests for genotype–climate association and $151 \times 20 \times 3 = 9,060$ Spearman correlation tests for gene expression–climate association across the three gardens). As stated in Methods Line 99: *"Although multiple testing correction was applied, no SNPs remained significant at FDR < 0.1. Therefore, we adopted an unadjusted p-value threshold of $\le 0.001$."* In 9,060 expression–climate correlation tests, an unadjusted threshold of $p \le 0.001$ is expected to produce $9,060 \times 0.001 \approx 9.1$ false-positive hits by chance alone—which is the exact number of genes (9) reported in the Abstract and Results (none of which replicated across all three gardens). Presenting these 40 SNPs and 9 genes in the Abstract as climate-associated without noting that zero survived $\text{FDR} < 0.1$ overstates the statistical evidence.
2. **Internal Mathematical and Tabular Inconsistencies in Table 2 and Discussion (Results, Table 2, Lines 171–198; Discussion, Line 8 of block 2):**
   - *Non-monotonic BH-FDR in Table 2 (Lines 175–177):* Within the `IBR` $\text{PC1}_{\text{SNP}}$ block of Table 2, `MEdarkred` has $r = -0.66, p = 0.0005$ and is assigned $\text{FDR} = 0.0400$, whereas `MEgreen` has a **larger** raw $p$-value ($r = -0.57, p = 0.0032$) yet is assigned a **smaller** FDR ($\text{FDR} = 0.0170$, identical to `MEgrey60` which has $p = 0.0003$). Under Benjamini–Hochberg step-up adjustment across the 25 WGCNA modules, FDR must be a strictly non-decreasing function of the raw $p$-value ($p = 0.0003 \to \text{FDR} = 0.0063$; $p = 0.0005 \to \text{FDR} = 0.0063$; $p = 0.0032 \to \text{FDR} = 0.0267$).
   - *Contradictory module size for `MEgreenyellow` in Table 2 (Line 179 vs. Line 188):* Because `blockwiseConsensusModules` assigns each transcript to a single consensus module across all three gardens, every module has a fixed transcript count (e.g., `MEgrey60 = 59`, `MEdarkred = 43` across IBR, KMT, and MYG). Yet `MEgreenyellow` is listed as having **`152`** transcripts in IBR (Line 179) and **`395`** transcripts in KMT (Line 188, which duplicates the `395` count of `MEgreen`!).
   - *Impossible $p$-value and FDR for `CTD` in Table 2 (Line 197):* Under `MYG` `CTD`, `MEgrey60` is reported with $r = 0.58, p = 0.0819, \text{FDR} = 0.0819$. First, for $N = 24$ samples in MYG, a Spearman correlation of $r = 0.58$ yields $p \approx 0.0030$, not $p = 0.0819$. Second, when testing 25 modules, the BH-adjusted FDR can only equal the raw $p$-value ($0.0819$) for the *largest* (rank-25) $p$-value of all 25 modules!
   - *Sign inversion in Discussion text (Line 8 of block 2):* Under *"Ura-sugi had higher expression of terpenoid biosynthesis genes"*, the text states: *"The one of key gene of darkred module that showed a positive correlation with PC1 SNP was involved in terpenoid biosynthesis gene."* Throughout the Abstract, Results (Lines 164, 207), and Table 2 ($r = -0.66, -0.64, -0.65$), `darkred` is **negatively** correlated with $\text{PC1}_{\text{SNP}}$.
   - *Minor SNP count mismatch:* Methods Lines 94 & 99 report `16,620 SNPs` after LD pruning, whereas Results Line 133 reports `16,622 representative SNPs`.
3. **Population-Level Pseudoreplication and Single-Population Confounding on $\text{PC1}_{\text{SNP}}$ (Table 1; Results, Lines 133–145):** All 35 trees come from just 12 geographic source populations (with only $N = 12$ unique WorldClim climate vectors), and the *yaku-sugi* genetic group comes from a **single island population** (Yakushima, `YKS`, the southernmost, warmest, and wettest site; note also that in KMT, two shoots were sampled from clonal ramets of the exact same genotype `YKS005`, Table 1 footnote b). Because $\text{PC1}_{\text{SNP}}$ primarily separates the single Yakushima population from mainland Japan (`S3 Fig`), any drift-driven or lineage-specific allele/expression difference in Yakushima automatically correlates with southern/warm WorldClim variables (`bio1, bio6, bio11`) when tested across individuals without accounting for population structure or clonal relatedness.
4. **Confounding of Common Garden Location with Sampling Year and Season (Methods, Line 74):** RNA-seq sampling was performed on August 8, 2020 at KMT, August 2, 2021 at MYG, and June 28, 2022 at IBR. Because photoperiod, shoot developmental maturity (late June vs. early August), inter-annual weather, and days since last rainfall (2, 6, and 9 days) all differ across the three single-timepoint collections, cross-site differences in module correlations cannot be attributed purely to geographic site climate.
5. **RNA-Seq Variant Calling Bias (Methods, Line 89):** Calling SNPs from RNA-seq reads (`bcftools mpileup` with cohort `DP >= 100`) in a highly heterozygous conifer is susceptible to allele-specific expression (ASE) dropout, where individuals with low expression of a transcript or cis-silencing of one allele are miscalled as homozygous or filtered out, creating artificial correlation between SNP genotypes and expression levels.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Clonal ramets grown across three different common gardens show higher transcriptome similarity to one another than to unrelated individuals from the same source population grown in the same garden.* `[Well Supported]` — This is a compelling empirical result (`S5 Fig`) demonstrating strong broad-sense heritability / genetic control of baseline shoot transcriptomes in *C. japonica*.
- **Claim 2:** *Constitutive expression differences between genetic lineages across all three common gardens involve biotic defense genes (`grey60` module, higher in omote-sugi) and terpenoid biosynthesis genes (`darkred` module containing TPS03, higher in ura-sugi).* `[Moderately Supported]` — The replication of `MEgrey60` ($r = 0.67\text{ to }0.78$) and `MEdarkred` ($r = -0.64\text{ to }-0.66$) across all three sites/years is robust, and the discussion appropriately considers both pathogen-mediated selection (e.g., *Fomitiporia torreyae*) and the growth–differentiation balance hypothesis.
- **Claim 3:** *Of 151 outlier SNPs, 40 are associated with climatic variables and 9 show gene expression correlated with climatic variables.* `[Overstated]` — None of these associations survived multiple-testing correction ($\text{FDR} < 0.1$), and they are confounded by the single-population *yaku-sugi* axis.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** All 72 new raw RNA-seq datasets (`DRR668422–DRR668493`) and 24 previously generated datasets (`DRR196892–DRR196915`) are deposited in DDBJ DRA, and extensive supplementary tables (`S1–S11 Tables`) document the bioclimatic variables, outlier SNPs, WGCNA module memberships, and GO annotations.
- **Code & Pipeline Reporting:** Software versions and parameters (`Trimgalore v0.6.7`, `hisat2 v2.2.1`, `psiclass`, `stringtie v2.2.1`, `bcftools v1.16`, `PLINK v1.9`, `pcadapt`, `WGCNA`) are well described, though the custom Perl script used to filter discordant ramet genotypes and the exact `R` script used to compute the Table 2 FDR values should be deposited in a public repository.
