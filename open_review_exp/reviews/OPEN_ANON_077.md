### 1. Executive Summary
This study develops a 3D radially symmetric reaction-diffusion partial differential equation (PDE) model to investigate locoregional delivery of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells (intratumoral injection vs. intracavitary surface administration) across four solid tumor phenotypes varying in proliferation rate ($a$), diffusivity ($D_T^*$), and density-dependent diffusion threshold ($u^*$). The model couples logistic tumor growth and step-function density-dependent tumor invasion with de Pillis–type saturating fractional CAR T-cell killing, lysis-driven CAR T-cell proliferation, and mass-action T-cell exhaustion. The authors demonstrate that the model can reproduce longitudinal bioluminescence imaging (BLI) trajectories from two preclinical murine mesothelioma studies, evaluate the impact of ex vivo CAR T-cell exhaustion, compare diameter-based (RECIST) vs. density-incorporating (Choi) clinical response criteria, and show that at a fixed detectable diameter, lower average tumor cell density (rather than total tumor burden or volume doubling time) best predicts the minimum eradicating intratumoral CAR T-cell dose ($R^2 = 0.83$). While the modeling framework is insightful and well-motivated, several mathematical typographical errors in the governing equations, a 100-fold discrepancy in the reported value of $D_C$ between text sections, and radius-vs.-diameter labeling inconsistencies must be corrected.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Typographical Error in the CAR T-Cell PDE (Equation 3):** In Equation (3) (line 73), the reaction-diffusion equation for CAR T-cell density $v(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is written as:
   $$\frac{\partial v}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot (D_C \nabla u) + F_2(u, v)$$
   The diffusion operator is erroneously applied to the *tumor* density $\nabla u$ (which would represent cross-diffusion/chemotaxis) rather than the CAR T-cell density $\nabla v$. (In the radially symmetric formulation in Equation 6b, it is correctly written with $\partial v / \partial r$).
2. **Incorrect Spherical Volume Jacobian ($r$ instead of $r^2$) in the Exhausted CAR T-Cell Integral (Equation 7):** In Equation (7) (line 119), the total number of exhausted CAR T cells $V_E(t)$ across the 3D sphere of radius $r_{\max}$ is written as:
   $$V_E(t) = V_E(0)\exp(-mt) + \int_0^t \exp(-m\tau) \int_0^{r_{\max}} 4\pi q v(r, \tau) u(r, \tau) r \, dr \, d\tau$$
   In 3D spherical coordinates with radial symmetry, the volume element of a spherical shell is $4\pi r^2 \, dr$, **not** $4\pi r \, dr$. Additionally, line 115 refers to the initial exhausted cell count as $v_E(0)$ (lowercase) whereas Equation (7) uses $V_E(0)$ (uppercase).
3. **100-Fold Inconsistency in the Reported CAR T-Cell Diffusion Constant ($D_C$) and Dimensional Inconsistency in Time-Step Formula:**
   - In the "Parameter estimation" section (line 128), the CAR T-cell diffusion constant derived from Mullazzani et al. is reported as $D_C = 1.38\text{ cm}^2/\text{day}$.
   - However, in Materials and Methods ("Process for fitting model to murine data", ContentOffset 46080 line 37), the text states: *"We fixed the CAR T-cell diffusion constant at $D_C = 0.0138\text{ cm}^2/\text{day}$ as estimated based on Mullazanni et al. [75]."* The authors must clarify which value ($1.38$ vs. $0.0138\text{ cm}^2/\text{day}$) was used in the baseline simulations vs. murine fits.
   - In "Numerical simulations" (ContentOffset 46080 line 32), the time step is written as $\Delta t = \min(a \Delta r / D_C, \Delta r / 5)$ days. Dimensionally, $a \Delta r / D_C$ has units of $\text{day}^{-1} \cdot \text{cm} / (\text{cm}^2/\text{day}) = \text{cm}^{-1}$ rather than days (and parabolic stability scales with $\Delta r^2 / D_C$).
4. **Discontinuous Heaviside Tumor Diffusivity and Degraded Crank–Nicolson Convergence:** Because $D_T(u)$ in Equation (6a) is a discontinuous step function at $u = u^*$, the solution has a jump in spatial derivatives at the moving threshold interface. As the authors transparently demonstrate using the Method of Manufactured Solutions (MMS), standard centered Crank–Nicolson finite differences drop from second-order $O(\Delta t^2, \Delta r^2)$ convergence to $O(\Delta t^{0.3}, \Delta r^{0.5})$ near the boundary and $O(\Delta t^{1.0}, \Delta r^{0.6})$ at $r = 0.5$. Replacing the discontinuous step function with a smooth, steep hyperbolic tangent or logistic transition $D_T(u) = \frac{D_T^*}{2}\left(1 + \tanh\frac{u - u^*}{\epsilon}\right)$ would eliminate non-smooth numerical artifacts while retaining the exact same macroscopic behavior.
5. **Radius vs. Diameter Confusion and Figure Caption Copy-Paste Errors:**
   - The baseline tumor size at treatment is a **radius of 1 cm (diameter of 2 cm)** (Results line 136: *"until the detectable tumor radius reached 1 cm"*; line 161: *"tumor of diameter 2 cm"*; line 202: *"detectable diameter of 2 cm"*). However, the caption to Fig 6 (line 210) and Results line 214 erroneously state *"when the detectable tumor radius reached 2 cm"* / *"Assuming a constant detectable tumor radius of 2 cm"*.
   - **Figure 4 caption error:** Figure 4 displays **intracavitary** treatment outcomes (`Fig 4. Intracavitary treatment outcomes`), but the caption text for panels (A–B) and (C–D) erroneously states `"Treating tumor type I with an intratumoral dose..."` and `"Intratumoral injection of 1 x 10^9 CAR T cells..."` (copy-pasted from the Fig 3 caption).
   - **Figure 8 caption typo:** Panel `(D)` is listed twice in the Fig 8 caption (once for cooperativity $l$ and again at the end for death rate $m$, which should be `(H)`).
6. **Identifiability of Preclinical Murine Fits (Fig 5):** The intratumoral fit to Zhao et al. (Fig 5A–C) is based on a single mouse ($n = 1$) without CAR T-cell kinetic measurements, and the intrapleural fit to Skovgard et al. (Fig 5D–F) has no pre-treatment tumor growth curve and tunes 4 CAR T-cell parameters via Latin hypercube sampling against 4 post-treatment timepoints. While useful as an illustrative sanity check, parameter values from these fits are not uniquely identifiable, as the authors appropriately note.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Assuming equal detectable tumor diameter at treatment, low average tumor cell density (and the ratio $a / D_T^*$) is a much stronger predictor of the minimum eradicating intratumoral CAR T-cell dose ($R^2 = 0.83\text{--}0.84$) than total tumor cell burden or volume doubling time ($R^2 < 0.40$). — `[Well Supported]` (within the mathematical model). The mechanistic explanation—that lower local tumor density $u(\mathbf{x}, t)$ increases the local effector-to-target ratio $v/u$ in the Hill-type killing term $D(\mathbf{x}, t)$, enabling faster outward proliferation and expansion before exhaustion $-quv$—is clear and mathematically sound.
- **Claim 2:** Diameter-only evaluation criteria (RECIST) can misclassify hollowing out of the tumor core after intratumoral CAR T-cell injection as stable or progressive disease, whereas density-aware criteria (Choi) detect partial responses at lower doses. — `[Well Supported]`. Directly follows from the spatial geometry of central intratumoral injection vs. peripheral intracavitary delivery (Fig 7).
- **Claim 3:** Pre-infusion ex vivo CAR T-cell exhaustion can cause abrupt treatment failure even at high total cell doses, and CAR T-cell diffusivity $D_C$ exhibits a non-monotonic optimum for tumor nadir. — `[Well Supported]`. Consistently demonstrated across the parameter sweeps in Figs 6 and 8.

- **Code & Data Availability:** Digitized murine BLI datasets from Zhao et al. and Skovgard et al. are provided in the supporting information, all model parameter tables (`Tables A and B`) and nondimensionalizations are provided in `S1 Text`, and the MATLAB (`R2021b`) simulation code is deposited in a public GitHub repository.
