### 1. Executive Summary
This molecular epidemiology study investigates the spatiotemporal dispersal and transmission dynamics of the HIV-1 sub-subtype A6 epidemic in Poland, focusing on the impact of war-related displacement from Ukraine after February 2022. The authors assemble a dataset of 13,696 unique HIV-1 *pol* sequences (positions 2253–3554 of HXB2), comprising 1,889 Polish sequences (1,219 from Polish-born individuals and 670 from Ukrainian-born migrants sampled between 1997 and November 2023) and 11,807 international background sequences from 48 countries via LANL. Using a multi-stage phylogenetic workflow—IQ-TREE 2.3.6 (GTR+G4) for maximum-likelihood topology, TempEst 1.5.3 for temporal signal outlier filtering, TreeTime 0.11.1 with a fixed external clock rate ($0.00323$ substitutions/site/year) for time calibration, and BEAST 1.10 discrete (BSSVS with Tip-State Swap adjusted Bayes Factors, $BF_{\text{adj}} \ge 3$) and continuous (Relaxed Random Walk) phylogeographic models—the study infers 670 independent introductions into Poland (334 occurring after 2022) and contrasts large male/MSM-dominated domestic clusters against female-enriched singleton/dyad introductions among Ukrainian migrants. While the clinical surveillance cohort is substantial and the application of $BF_{\text{adj}}$ to mitigate regional sampling bias is a methodological strength, the study suffers from severe sampling bias in the source location (Ukraine post-2022), reliance on a fixed single-point evolutionary rate without uncertainty propagation, arbitrary jittering of first-level administrative coordinates for continuous diffusion, and restricted public data availability (only 10% of sequences deposited).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unpropagated Clock Rate Uncertainty and Fixed Tree Topology:** Time calibration in TreeTime 0.11.1 was conducted on a fixed IQ-TREE ML topology using a single fixed evolutionary rate ($0.00323\text{ s/s/y}$) taken from an external study of 426 FSU sequences (1997–2016). Fixing both the tree topology and point substitution rate across 13,696 tips collapses phylogenetic and molecular clock uncertainty. Consequently, the reported 95% HPD interval for lineage introductions (663–678) only reflects stochastic mapping variance on a fixed time-tree rather than true posterior uncertainty in coalescence times or branch lengths.
2. **Severe Post-2022 Source-Sink Sampling Asymmetry:** The discrete phylogeographic model assigns internal nodes to "Poland" vs. "non-Poland" and further stratifies domestic nodes by nationality ("born in Poland" vs. "born in Ukraine"). Because sequencing in Ukraine dropped precipitously after the 2022 invasion while Polish clinical genotyping captured newly linked Ukrainian refugees, ancestral state reconstruction is heavily biased toward inferring "migrant-to-migrant" transitions *within* Poland (reported as rising to 21.1% after 2022) for transmissions that may have actually occurred in Ukraine prior to displacement. Although the authors acknowledge this possibility qualitatively in the Discussion, the statistical model treats nationality/country-of-birth as a spatial/demographic state transitioning along branches inside Poland, conflating pre-migration transmission in Ukraine with post-arrival domestic transmission in Poland.
3. **Continuous Phylogeography on Coarsely Jittered Administrative Polygons:** In the continuous Relaxed Random Walk (RRW) analysis, exact patient residential coordinates were unavailable; the authors assigned coordinates by randomly selecting a point within each sample's first-level administrative region (voivodeship). Because Polish voivodeships span tens of thousands of square kilometers, uniform random point assignment injects artificial spatial variance that inflates intra-regional diffusion rates and distorts wavefront velocity estimates in `Seraphim`.
4. **Internal Numerical Discrepancies in Sample Counts and Percentages:**
   - In the Abstract, the male count is stated as $76.1\%\ (n = 1,437)$, whereas in the Results ("Sequence data overview", line 59) it is stated as $n = 1,438\ (76.1\%)$.
   - In the Abstract, singletons and dyads among Ukrainian migrants are reported as $69.5\%\ (n = 473; p < 0.0001)$, whereas in the Results (line 67) the exact same count ($n = 473$) is reported as $69.4\%$. Furthermore, 473 out of 670 Ukrainian migrant sequences is $70.6\%$, while 473 out of 682 total singleton/dyad sequences is $69.35\%$ ($69.4\%$)—indicating the text conflates the denominator (proportion of singletons/dyads that are Ukrainian vs. proportion of Ukrainian migrants who are in singletons/dyads).
   - In line 99, the authors state: "many sequences were classified as singletons ($n = 498, 74.3\%$) or dyads ($n = 92, 13.8\%$)" alongside large clusters ($n = 6, 0.9\%$) and networks ($n = 74, 11.0\%$). Here, $6 + 74 + 498 + 92 = 670$ *introductions/clusters*, not *sequences* ($498\text{ singletons} + 92 \times 2\text{ dyads} = 682\text{ sequences}$, matching line 67). Calling the 92 dyads "sequences" in line 99 is a unit-of-analysis error.
5. **Short MCMC Chain Length for Preliminary Discrete Trait Analysis:** Running the preliminary discrete phylogeographic MCMC on a 13,696-tip tree for only $1,000,000$ iterations (sampling every 1,000 steps) is extraordinarily short for a tree of this size, even when keeping topology fixed.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: The Polish HIV-1 A6 epidemic exhibits dual dynamics—large male/MSM-dominated domestic clusters versus female-balanced singleton/dyad introductions among displaced Ukrainian migrants.** `[Well Supported]` The demographic stratification across cluster sizes ($95.5\%$ Polish-born in large clusters $n > 14$ vs. $69.4\%$ of singletons/dyads being Ukrainian migrants with $47.1\%$ female representation) is strongly supported by the national clinical cohort data.
- **Claim 2: After 2022, migrant-to-migrant transmissions inside Poland accounted for 21.1% of domestic migration events, and displaced PLWH demonstrated high inter-regional mobility driving viral circulation.** `[Overstated]` Because "born in Ukraine" is a static patient attribute and recent background sequences from Ukraine are severely undersampled due to the war, phylogenetic sister-taxa sampled from Ukrainian refugees in different Polish voivodeships (e.g., dyads across West Pomerania and Masovia) can reflect pre-war or wartime transmission chains in Ukraine followed by dispersal to different Polish reception regions, rather than active viral transmission across Polish regions post-arrival.
- **Claim 3: Relevance of A6 introductions to Cabotegravir/Rilpivirine (CAB/RPV) resistance via L74I.** `[Moderately Supported]` While the authors motivate the study and conclusions with the A6 signature integrase polymorphism L74I and CAB/RPV failure risk, their sequence dataset spans HXB2 positions 2253–3554 (protease and partial reverse transcriptase) and *excludes the integrase gene entirely*, plus strips 43 drug-resistance codons prior to tree building. Thus, no empirical integrase or drug resistance data are analyzed in this study.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Sequence Data Availability:** Only a random 10% subset of the 1,889 Polish HIV-1 A6 sequences was deposited in GenBank (Table I in S1 Text), citing patient privacy. While common in clinical HIV molecular epidemiology, withholding 90% of the primary Polish sequences prevents independent replication of the cluster definitions, TreeTime calibration, and introduction counts.
- **Pipeline & Code Availability:** A GitHub repository link is provided for BEAST XMLs and phylogeographic scripts, and software versions (IQ-TREE 2.3.6, TempEst 1.5.3, TreeTime 0.11.1, BEAST 1.10) are clearly documented. However, exact criteria for outlier removal in TempEst (how many sequences were pruned before arriving at 13,696) and the exact list of 11,807 LANL background accession numbers should be provided in full machine-readable tables.
