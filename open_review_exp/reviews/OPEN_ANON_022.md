### 1. Executive Summary
This study experimentally and theoretically investigates how ancestral mutation rates shape the speed of antibiotic resistance evolution in *Escherichia coli*. Using the IS-free *E. coli* MDS42 wild-type strain and 12 engineered single- and double-knockout mutator strains ($\Delta mutS$, $\Delta mutH$, $\Delta mutL$, $\Delta mutT$, $\Delta dnaQ$, and seven double mutants), the authors first quantify baseline synonymous base-pair substitution (BPS) and intergenic indel rates across 39 mutation accumulation (MA) lineages (13 strains $\times$ 3 replicates; 23–69 passages) via whole-genome sequencing. They then perform a 9-day automated serial transfer evolution experiment across 260 populations (13 strains $\times$ 5 antibiotics [chloramphenicol, trimethoprim, amikacin, cefixime, ciprofloxacin] $\times$ 4 replicates) in 2-fold drug dilution gradients, complemented by whole-genome sequencing of 24 evolved populations and a deterministic ODE pharmacodynamic-population dynamics model. The experimental execution—combining an IS-free background, synonymous-only MA rate estimation ($dN/dS \approx 1$), 5-day vs. 9-day reproducibility checks, and robotic serial transfers—is high quality. However, the theoretical modeling relies on a deterministic ODE framework that cannot properly capture stochastic mutant arrival times or Muller's ratchet, and the non-monotonic downturn at extreme mutation rates rests on a single outlier double-mutant strain ($\Delta mutL\Delta dnaQ$, `LQ`).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Deterministic ODE Formulation vs. Stochastic Bottlenecks and Fractional Cell Counts:** The population dynamics model in Eq. (1), $\frac{dM_i}{dt} = \mu p_b M_{i-1}(1 - \frac{M}{K}) + \mu(1 - p_b)M_i(1 - \frac{M}{K}) - \gamma_i M_i$, is fully deterministic and continuous. In a serial transfer protocol starting at $M_0 = 3 \times 10^{-4}$ ($\text{OD}_{620}$, corresponding to $\sim 2 \times 10^5$ cells) with $M_i(0) = 0$ for $i \ge 1$, a deterministic ODE immediately generates a continuous, non-zero density of $i=1, 2, \dots, k$ multi-mutant cells at $t > 0$ via cascading flux terms ($\mu p_b M_{i-1}$), allowing fractional cells ($M_i \ll 1\text{ cell}$, i.e., $\text{OD}_{620} < 1.5 \times 10^{-9}$) to deterministically grow and survive serial dilution. In reality, adaptive evolution in populations of $2 \times 10^5$ cells is governed by the stochastic waiting time for discrete beneficial mutations to arise ($M_i \ge 1\text{ cell}$) and survive drift during the 100-fold daily bottleneck. While the ODE captures the qualitative interplay between baseline growth rate $\mu(p) = \mu_0 e^{-\delta p}$ and the pharmacodynamic Hill coefficient $\beta$, the absence of a stochastic cutoff or Wright-Fisher/tau-leaping simulation means that the fitted parameters ($\epsilon$, $b_0$, $\beta$) absorb continuum artifacts.
2. **Non-Identifiability of Beneficial Mutation Fraction ($\alpha$) and Unreported Fixed Value:** In Eq. (1), the beneficial mutation rate is defined as $p_b = \alpha p$. On line 166, the authors note that "Because of the difficulty in experimentally estimating parameter $\alpha$, which represents the ratio of beneficial mutations, we assigned an arbitrary value and confirmed that the simulated results remained qualitatively unchanged within a certain range." However, the exact numerical value assigned to $\alpha$ in Figs 4C, 4D, and 5 is never reported in the text, Table 2, or Methods! Moreover, in the deterministic ODE, changing $\alpha$ directly rescales the flux between compartments $M_{i-1} \to M_i$, trading off directly with $\epsilon$ (the MIC benefit per mutation) and $\beta$.
3. **Reliance on a Single Double-Mutant Strain (`LQ`) for the Non-Monotonic Downturn:** The central claim that adaptation speed peaks and then declines at excessive mutation rates relies entirely on a single strain: `LQ` ($\Delta mutL\Delta dnaQ$), which has a mutation rate $\sim 10$-fold higher than the next cluster of mutators (`T`, `HT`, `LT`, `ST`, `QT` at $p \approx 0.1$) and exhibits a severe baseline growth defect in drug-free media ($\mu \approx 0.45\text{ h}^{-1}$ vs. $0.90\text{ h}^{-1}$ in WT). Because no strains bridge the gap between $p \approx 0.1$ and $p \approx 1.0$, and because Eq. (2) ($\mu = \mu_0 e^{-\delta p}$) is heavily anchored by `LQ` alone, the model's sharp downturn is driven by the pre-existing physiological growth defect of the `LQ` ancestor rather than dynamic accumulation of deleterious mutations during the 9-day experiment (indeed, Eq. (1) keeps $\mu$ constant over time and only tracks beneficial mutations $i$).
4. **Statistical Power and Multiple Testing in Bacteriostatic vs. Bactericidal Comparisons:** On line 92, the authors test for a downturn in `LQ` by comparing `LQ` ($n=4$ replicates) against the pooled 5 mutator strains with $p \approx 0.1$ ($n=20$ data points across 5 distinct genotypes) using a Mann-Whitney U-test ($p < 0.01$ for CP and TP). Pooling 4 technical replicates across 5 distinct genetic backgrounds into a single sample of $n=20$ violates independence assumptions (pseudoreplication across genotypes). Furthermore, claiming a general mechanistic dichotomy between bacteriostatic (2 drugs: CP, TP) and bactericidal (3 drugs: AMK, CFIX, CPFX) classes based on $N=5$ total drugs is statistically underpowered, especially since AMK showed almost no evolution across any strain.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Mutation rates in single and double DNA repair knockouts span a 6- to 400-fold range above WT with distinct mutational spectra that persist during antibiotic selection.** `[Well Supported]` — The MA experiment (Table 1, Fig 1) and evolved population resequencing (24 lines, S7 Table) cleanly demonstrate expected mutational signatures (e.g., $\text{A:T}\to\text{C:G}$ transversions in $\Delta mutT$ backgrounds and transitions/indels in MMR knockouts) with neutral accumulation ($dN/dS \approx 1$).
- **Claim 2: The rate of MIC adaptation increases with mutation rate up to $p \approx 0.1$ BPS/genome/generation, but drops in the hypermutator $\Delta mutL\Delta dnaQ$ (`LQ`) strain under chloramphenicol and trimethoprim selection.** `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by 9-day and 5-day replicate robotic serial transfer experiments across 260 lines.
- **Claim 3: Differences in the steepness of the pharmacodynamic killing curve ($\beta$) between bacteriostatic and bactericidal drugs govern the mutation-rate dependency of adaptation speed.** `[Moderately Supported]` — The hypothesis that shallow dose-response curves ($\beta \approx 0.5$) allow partially resistant subpopulations to proliferate and accumulate secondary mutations is biologically plausible and consistent with the data, but the deterministic model was fitted directly to the MIC trajectories rather than independently measured pharmacodynamic kill curves.
- **Claim 4: The estimated mutational benefit $\epsilon$ correlates with antibiotic molecular weight via efflux pump sensitivity.** `[Overstated]` — Speculating on a physical relationship between $\epsilon$ and drug molecular weight across only 5 compounds (where target-site mutations like *gyrA* for CPFX or *folA* for TP coexist with efflux mutations) is unsupported by direct experimental evidence, as the authors themselves acknowledge.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Whole-genome sequencing reads for the MA lines and evolved populations are publicly deposited in the DDBJ Sequence Read Archive under accessions `DRA016357` and `DRA019544`. Detailed mutation call tables, colony diameters across MA rounds, and generation counts are provided in Supplementary Tables S1–S8.
- **Code & Modeling Completeness:** The custom C code used to integrate Eq. (1) and run the genetic algorithm parameter estimation is not deposited in a public repository (e.g., GitHub/Zenodo). Furthermore, the exact value of the fixed parameter $\alpha$ (beneficial mutation fraction) and the exact integration timestep / boundary conditions for Eq. (1) must be explicitly stated to allow independent reproduction of Figs 4 and 5.
