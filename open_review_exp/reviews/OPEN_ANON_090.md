### 1. Executive Summary
This study applies a "non-canonical metatranscriptomics" workflow—repurposing non-human reads from host total RNA-seq libraries—to compare transcriptionally active microbes (TAMs, classified via Kraken2/Bracken with `minikraken2`) and antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs, profiled via CARD RGI `bwt`/KMA and BLASTx against NCBI-NR) across 251 hospitalized COVID-19 patients (mild, moderate, severe, mortality) and 112 dengue patients (mild, moderate, severe) in New Delhi, India. The authors report 1,344 unique ARGs across 6,897 microbial species in COVID-19 compared to 497 ARGs across 5,805 species in dengue, concluding that COVID-19 exhibits a higher ARG burden (especially in mortality cases) driven by pandemic antibiotic misuse and respiratory dysbiosis (*Klebsiella pneumoniae, Escherichia coli, Acinetobacter baumannii*), whereas dengue exhibits a more conserved resistome with *Bacillus cereus* in severe cases. However, the study is undermined by fundamental experimental design and bioinformatic flaws: (1) the cross-disease comparison directly confounds viral disease with biological specimen type, comparing high-biomass upper respiratory **nasopharyngeal swabs** in COVID-19 against ultra-low-biomass **whole blood** in dengue; (2) no negative extraction or reagent controls were included, resulting in massive "kitome" and k-mer false-positive artifacts (claiming ~6,000 microbial species in blood, including fish herpesviruses, insect parasitoid wasp viruses, *M. tuberculosis*, and *B. pseudomallei*); (3) Table 1 mislabels the median $\text{SpO}_2$ values as the patient sample sizes; and (4) the Procrustes analysis reports a mathematically impossible machine-epsilon $M^2$ of $4.441 \times 10^{-16}$.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Fatal Specimen-Type and Library-Kit Confounding Between COVID-19 (Nasopharyngeal Swabs) and Dengue (Whole Blood):** Every comparative analysis between COVID-19 and dengue in this manuscript (alpha diversity in Fig 4a, Bray-Curtis PERMANOVA in Fig 4b, total ARG counts in Fig 2a, and taxonomic differences in Fig 4c–e) compares **nasopharyngeal swabs in viral transport medium** (COVID-19, prepared with Illumina TruSeq Total RNA Gold) against **peripheral whole blood** (dengue, prepared with modified erythrocyte lysis and Illumina TruSeq Total RNA Globin depletion). The human nasopharynx is a densely colonized mucosal surface naturally inhabited by *Streptococcus, Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, Moraxella,* and *Corynebacterium* (yielding 36–62% non-human reads, ~8.3M unmapped reads/sample), whereas peripheral blood is a physiologically sterile, ultra-low-biomass compartment (yielding 92% human reads, ~1.6M unmapped reads/sample). Attributing the higher number of detected bacterial taxa and ARGs in nasopharyngeal swabs vs. whole blood to "COVID-19 vs. dengue viral pathogenesis" or "pandemic antibiotic misuse" is scientifically invalid; the comparison primarily measures the difference between a nasal swab and a blood draw, compounded by a $5\times$ difference in non-human read depth.
2. **Severe Sample Size Contradictions and Table 1 Column Header Error (Section 2.1 vs. Table 1):**
   - **Section 2.1 (line 33):** States that the 251 COVID-19 patients comprised `mild (n = 128), moderate (n = 33), severe (n = 24) and mortality (n = 60)`. Note first that $128 + 33 + 24 + 60 = 245 \ne 251$ (and the Gender row in Table 1 sums to $128 + 33 + 24 + 59 = 244$).
   - **Table 1 Column Headers (line 53):** Lists the subgroup sample sizes as **`Mild (n = 97) | Moderate (n = 95) | Severe (n = 80) | Mortality (n = 91)`** (which would sum to 363). Inspection of the second row of Table 1 (`SpO2`: `97 | 95 | 80 | 91.5`) and Results line 46 (*"SpO2 levels were lowest in severe (80) and mortality (91) patients compared to the mild (97) and moderate (95)"*) reveals that the authors **pasted the group median $\text{SpO}_2$ oxygen saturation percentages (`97`, `95`, `80`, `91`) into the Table 1 headers as the patient sample sizes `n`**!
   - Additionally, for dengue, Results line 33 and Table 1 list `Mild (n = 45)` and `Moderate (n = 46)`, whereas Methods line 79 states `mild; n = 46` and `moderate; n = 45`.
3. **Absence of Negative Controls and Severe Taxonomic False Positives in Low-Biomass Blood RNA-seq (Sections 2.3 & 2.4):** As acknowledged in the Limitations (line 56), no extraction blanks, VTM blanks, or library reagent negative controls were sequenced. Running `Kraken2`/`Bracken` with the downsampled 8 GB `minikraken2` database on unmapped RNA-seq reads from blood without negative controls or strict genome coverage validation produced extreme false-positive inflation:
   - The authors claim to detect **5,805 transcriptionally active microbial species from 58 phyla in the blood of dengue patients** (and 6,897 species in COVID-19 swabs; line 127).
   - Among the "dengue-associated TAMs" in human blood, the authors report *Burkholderia pseudomallei* (the Tier 1 select agent of melioidosis), *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, soil antibiotic-producing actinomycetes (*Streptomyces pristinaespiralis* and *Streptomyces sviceus*), **Cyprinid herpesvirus 1** (a herpesvirus specific to carp fish), and **Glypta fumiferanae ichnovirus** (a polydnavirus specific to a North American parasitoid wasp of the eastern spruce budworm) (lines 166–168)! These are unmistakable bioinformatic false positives (host/low-complexity RNA k-mer collisions in `minikraken2`) and reagent "kitome" contaminants.
   - Similarly, the ubiquitous detection of *TEM-116* in 248/251 COVID-19 and 111/112 dengue samples (line 117) is a well-documented signature of recombinant Taq polymerase / molecular biology reagent vector contamination (`pBR322`/`pUC` blaTEM-116 backbone) rather than universal extended-spectrum $\beta$-lactamase carriage across all community patients at admission.
4. **Mathematically Impossible Procrustes Statistic ($M^2 = 4.441 \times 10^{-16}$, Section 2.3):** In line 135, the authors report a Procrustes correlation between the ARG ordination and TAM ordination of **`M2 = 4.441e-16, p = 0.001 in COVID-19; M2 = 8.882e-16, p = 0.001 in dengue`**. In IEEE 754 double-precision arithmetic, machine epsilon is $\epsilon = 2^{-52} \approx 2.2204 \times 10^{-16}$; thus $4.441 \times 10^{-16} = 2\epsilon$ and $8.882 \times 10^{-16} = 4\epsilon$, meaning $M^2 = 0$ to within floating-point rounding error! Two genuinely distinct high-dimensional biological matrices (ARG abundances vs. species abundances across 251 patients) cannot have a Procrustes residual sum of squares of zero ($10^{-16}$); this indicates a coding bug where the same ordination matrix was compared to itself (`protest(X, X)`).
5. **Compositional Closure Artifact in the `GCPM` Formula (Section 4.5, Line 100):** The authors define `GCPM(x)` by dividing the read count per base of ARG $x$ by the sum of read counts per base *across only the $n$ predicted CARD ARGs in that sample*, multiplied by $10^6$. As the authors state on line 104: *"The sum of the GCPM values for all the predicted genes in each sample was one million."* Because every sample is forced to sum to $10^6$ GCPM *within the resistome alone* (without normalizing to total sequencing depth or total bacterial read depth), a sample with only 1 detected ARG has a GCPM of $1,000,000$ for that gene, whereas a sample with 200 highly expressed ARGs has an average GCPM of $5,000$ per gene! Using this closed within-CARD ratio to claim that dengue has higher individual ARG abundance (Fig 4e.ii) or to compare total ARG burden across groups (line 51) is mathematically flawed.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: COVID-19 patients harbor a more diverse and abundant resistome than dengue patients due to pandemic antibiotic misuse and viral-induced dysbiosis.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` Because COVID-19 was sampled via nasopharyngeal swabs and dengue via whole blood, the comparison is completely confounded by anatomical site and microbial biomass.Moreover, as the authors note in the Discussion (line 36), samples were collected on day 1 of hospital reporting *before* in-hospital antibiotic administration, and in their recorded clinical data, mortality patients had the *lowest* antibiotic exposure (0% $\beta$-lactams and 0% tetracyclines).
- **Claim 2: Dengue patients harbor beneficial *Streptomyces* species as well as *Cyprinid herpesvirus 1* and *Glypta fumiferanae ichnovirus* in their blood microbiome.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` Identifying carp herpesvirus, spruce-budworm wasp ichnovirus, and soil *Streptomyces* in human dengue blood without negative extraction controls is a clear k-mer database misclassification / reagent contamination artifact.
- **Claim 3: *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, and *Acinetobacter baumannii* are the definitive bacterial hosts of the detected mobile ARGs.** `[Overstated]` Assigning bacterial species hosts to short RNA-seq reads mapping to conserved plasmid-borne ARGs (*TEM, sul1, tet*) simply by taking the top `blastx` hit of the ARG consensus sequence against NCBI-NR cannot resolve horizontal gene transfer or true host provenance in a mixed metatranscriptome.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw sequencing reads are deposited across six NCBI SRA BioProjects: `PRJNA676016`, `PRJNA678831`, `PRJNA868733`, and `PRJNA952815` (COVID-19), and `PRJNA1071729` and `PRJNA1279769` (Dengue).
- **Software & Protocol Documentation:** Software tools and versions (`bcltofastq` v1.8.4, `Trimmomatic` v0.39, `HISAT2` v2.2.1, `Kraken2` v2.1.3 with `minikraken2_v1_202003`, `Bracken` v2.9, `metagenomeSeq` v1.46.0, CARD v3.3.0, `RGI` v6.0.3) are clearly listed.
- **Critical Missing Controls & Code:** No negative extraction/reagent control sequencing data are available, and custom Python/R analysis scripts (including the code that produced the $M^2 = 4.44 \times 10^{-16}$ Procrustes output and the Table 1 summary statistics) are not deposited in a public repository.
