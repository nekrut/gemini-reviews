### 1. Executive Summary
This study develops and validates a BSL-1 genomic surveillance workflow to detect and whole-genome sequence clade 2.3.4.4b H5N1 highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI, genotype B3.13) viral RNA directly from commercial pasteurized retail dairy products. Over a 2-month period (April–June 2024), the authors sampled 66 retail dairy products (representing 29 processing plant codes across 20 US states) purchased in Madison, Wisconsin. Using 6-replicate MagMAX Wastewater Ultra extractions pooled and concentrated via Zymo RNA Clean & Concentrator, an H5 2.3.4.4b-specific RT-qPCR assay, and a custom 250-bp tiled-amplicon PrimalScheme coupled with QIAseq Direct RT-PCR and Oxford Nanopore MinION R10 sequencing, they detected H5N1 vRNA in 13/66 cartons (from CO, IN, KY, MI, and TX; 1.92–4.50 $\log_{10}$ copies/mL) and achieved $>90\%$ genome coverage at $\ge 20\times$ depth in 5 of 10 sequenced cartons. Consensus sequences clustered within the North American cattle B3.13 outbreak tree on Nextstrain, and `augur traits` correctly inferred the processing state for 3 of 5 samples. The manuscript is transparent, practical, and exemplary in its open-science reproducibility, requiring only minor revisions to address a few arithmetic/reporting discrepancies in the Results and methods.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Arithmetic Inconsistency in Segment-Level vs. Overall Sequencing Depth (Line 140):** In the Results ("Tiled-amplicon sequencing covers all H5N1 gene segments", line 140), the authors state: "The method yielded an overall average sequencing depth of **8,176x** across all samples, with individual samples varying from **325x to 29,256x**... The average depth across all samples was lowest for the neuraminidase (NA) segment, with a depth of **199x**. The average depth was highest for the polymerase-basic 1 (PB1) gene segment with a depth of **419x**." Mathematically, if the lowest segment-wide average depth across all samples is $199\times$ (NA) and the highest segment-wide average depth is $419\times$ (PB1), the overall average sequencing depth across all segments and samples must lie between $199\times$ and $419\times$—it cannot possibly be $8,176\times$! This >20-fold discrepancy suggests that $8,176\times$ (and the range $325\times\text{–}29,256\times$) is either the sum of depths across amplicons, the unnormalized read count per segment, or calculated before `rasusa` downsampling, whereas $199\times\text{–}419\times$ is post-downsampling or median depth. The authors should reconcile these numbers.
2. **Sample Size and Input Variable for Spearman Correlation ($r_s = 0.946, p = 3 \times 10^{-7}$, Line 140):** The authors report a Spearman correlation of $r_s = 0.946$ ($p = 3 \times 10^{-7}$) between input vRNA amount and sequencing depth. However, Table 2 lists 10 sequenced cartons, of which only $n = 9$ have quantifiable $\log_{10}$ copies/mL (`Carton 33` was negative upon re-testing and has a blank value in Table 2). For a two-sided Spearman rank correlation with $n = 9$ (or even $n = 10$), a coefficient of $r_s = 0.946$ yields $t = r_s \sqrt{(n-2)/(1-r_s^2)} = 0.946 \sqrt{7 / 0.105} \approx 7.72$, which corresponds to $p \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-4}$ (for $n=9$) or $p \approx 3.5 \times 10^{-5}$ (for $n=10$), **not** $p = 3 \times 10^{-7}$ (unless the correlation was computed across individual segment data points, e.g., $9 \text{ cartons} \times 8 \text{ segments} = 72$ pseudoreplicated points, or included the 4 negative controls). If 8 segments per carton were pooled into a single Spearman test, this violates independence because all 8 segments share the same carton-level HA RT-qPCR copy number.
3. **Quantification Bias from dsDNA Standards in 1-Step RT-qPCR:** As noted on lines 51 and 110, viral RNA concentrations ($1.92\text{–}4.50\text{ }\log_{10}$ copies/mL) were interpolated against a synthetic double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) standard curve rather than in vitro transcribed single-stranded viral RNA. Because dsDNA does not undergo the reverse transcription step (whose efficiency in milk matrices is typically $<50\text{–}80\%$) and provides two template strands in the first PCR cycle, dsDNA standards systematically underestimate absolute vRNA copy numbers by at least $2\times$ to $5\times$ ($0.3\text{–}0.7\text{ }\log_{10}$). Additionally, the explicit conversion formula from "copies per reaction" (5 µL RNA input from a 50 µL elution extracted from 400 µL neat or 1:1 diluted dairy product) to "$\log_{10}$ copies per mL of dairy product" in Table 2 should be written out in the Methods.
4. **Pooled Milk Metagenomic Chimera and Intra-Host Variant Calling Caveats:** Because commercial processing plants pool milk from hundreds to thousands of cows across multiple farms, a single carton is a metagenomic mixture of viral populations. Calling a "consensus sequence" across 250-bp tiled amplicons can stitch together mutations from distinct farm lineages into an artificial chimeric genome, which may explain why `Carton 05` (processed in Colorado) clustered with Texas sequences or why `augur traits` misassigned 2 of 5 samples. Similarly, calling "sub-consensus" variants at a 10% threshold (such as `HA:P324L` at 36% in `Carton 24`) on Oxford Nanopore R10 amplicon reads at $\ge 20\times$ minimum depth reflects inter-animal/inter-farm mixture frequencies rather than within-host minor variants, and could be affected by amplicon dropout/bias when vRNA concentrations are low ($\sim 10^2\text{–}10^3$ copies/mL).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Short (250-bp) tiled-amplicon sequencing recovers $>90\%$ H5N1 genome coverage at $\ge 20\times$ depth from pasteurized retail milk when viral RNA titers exceed $\sim 2.5\text{ }\log_{10}$ copies/mL.** `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated across 10 sequenced cartons with four stage-specific negative water controls (`Controls A–D`), all of which showed 0% coverage at $20\times$ depth (104–171 background reads vs. 331,732–1,950,789 mapped reads in passing cartons).
- **Claim 2: Retail milk consensus sequences cluster within the clade 2.3.4.4b B3.13 cattle outbreak phylogeny.** `[Well Supported]` — Confirmed by Nextclade placement on both the H5Nx clade 2.3.4.4 HA tree and the concatenated 8-segment cattle outbreak genome tree.
- **Claim 3: Retail dairy surveillance provides an accessible, BSL-1 One Health complement to on-farm bulk-tank testing, with clear limitations regarding pooled chimerism and non-fluid dairy supply chains.** `[Well Supported]` — The Discussion is exceptionally balanced and candidly details the limitations of milk pooling, interstate milk transport, Wisconsin's 90% cheese allocation, and short-amplicon inability to phase haplotypes.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Protocol Availability:** Exemplary open-science execution. Raw sequencing reads for all 10 cartons are deposited in NCBI SRA (`SRR31094751`–`SRR31094760`), wet-lab RNA extraction and tiled-amplicon RT-PCR protocols are published with DOIs on `protocols.io`, and the entire Nextflow bioinformatics pipeline (`Dorado`, `CutAdapt`, `minimap2`, `iVar`, `snpEff`) with Docker containerization and `pyproject.toml` dependencies is publicly available on GitHub (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`).
- **Primer & Metadata Completeness:** RT-qPCR primers/probe (`GGGAAGCTATGCGACCTAAAT`, `CATTCCGGCACTCTGATGAA`, `ACATTGGGTTTCCGAGGAGCCATC`), reference genome accession (`A/Bovine/texas/24-029328-01/2024(H5N1)`), and full carton metadata (`S3 Table`) are completely documented. Ensuring the complete 250-bp PrimalScheme + manual 5'/3' NCR terminal primer sequences are included in `S1 File` / `S1 Table` will guarantee full wet-lab reproducibility.
