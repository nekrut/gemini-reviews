### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript introduces `kMermaid`, a Python-based tool for assigning shotgun metagenomic short reads to taxa-agnostic clusters of homologous bacterial proteins using a two-layer nested hash map of amino acid 5-mer frequencies. The reference database is built by clustering 1,793,361 NCBI RefSeq microbial proteins into 32,308 clusters via a two-step CD-HIT procedure, with remote homology re-annotation (`HHblits`) applied to 8,617 hypothetical cluster representatives. The authors benchmark `kMermaid` against `BLASTX`, `DIAMOND`, and `MMseqs2` on simulated reads, newly deposited 2025 RefSeq sequences (reverse-translated), and 29 human fecal metagenomes from an ulcerative colitis cohort (LOTUS trial). While grouping homologous proteins into clusters trivially resolves multi-mapping ambiguity across orthologous bacterial proteins, the core scoring formulation suffers from unnormalized additive length and multi-frame biases, the database covers a surprisingly narrow set of 32,308 clusters, and several benchmarking choices (forcing `max_target_seqs = 1` on aligners, coarse step-function memory profiling, and conditioning AUROC only on reads jointly classified by both tools) tilt comparisons in `kMermaid`'s favor.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unnormalized Additive Scoring Across Pooled Reading Frames (`s_{r,C}`)**: In the assignment algorithm (Lines 29, 122–130), a query nucleotide read $r$ undergoes six-frame translation, all frames lacking a stop codon are retained, and the cluster score $s_{r,C} = \sum_{w \in r} \text{frequency}_{C_w}$ sums 5-mer frequencies across *all* non-truncated frames simultaneously. Because short reads (e.g., 100–150 bp) frequently lack stop codons in multiple non-coding or antisense frames, pooling 5-mers across all surviving frames injects out-of-frame noise into the sum. Furthermore, because $s_{r,C}$ is an unnormalized cumulative sum rather than a length-normalized or frame-specific likelihood/score, longer reads accumulate higher scores under the null and require different decision thresholds—a flaw directly manifested in the unseen RefSeq experiment (Line 70), where the authors had to raise the score threshold from the default $>3$ to $6.3$–$8.0$ across 125–200 bp reads to maintain $\text{FPR} \le 0.05$, and in Fig 3b, where performance degrades for sequences $>500$ nt.
2. **Inconsistency in the Two-Step CD-HIT Clustering Pipeline**: In Lines 105–106, Step 1 clusters 43,176 RefSeq proteins using `CD-HIT` at a 65% similarity threshold ($k=5$) to form 32,308 cluster representatives. Step 2 then uses `CD-HIT-2D` at a *stricter* 70% similarity threshold to recruit 1,793,361 out of 1,797,426 RefSeq microbial proteins into those 32,308 representatives. First, applying a higher sequence identity threshold (70%) in the expansion step than in the seed-clustering step (65%) is inverted relative to standard hierarchical clustering logic. Second, it is biologically implausible that 99.77% (1,793,361 / 1,797,426) of the entire RefSeq microbial protein universe shares $\ge 70\%$ sequence identity with just 32,308 seed proteins; this indicates that the 1.79M protein set was already restricted to homologs of the 43,176 seed set rather than representing the full diversity of bacterial RefSeq (which spans millions of protein families).
3. **Selection bias in AUROC Evaluation on Unseen 2025 RefSeq Proteins**: In the evaluation of 22,435 newly deposited RefSeq proteins (Line 70; Methods offset Line 1), AUROC values of 0.93–0.96 are computed *only* on the subset of reads classified by both `BLASTX` and `kMermaid`, where a true positive is defined as cluster agreement with `BLASTX`. Because overall classification coverage on this dataset is only 11.7%–13.6% for `kMermaid` (and 13.7%–14.9% for `BLASTX`), conditioning AUROC on the doubly-classified subset excludes ~86% of reads (including reads classified by `kMermaid` that have no `BLASTX` hit or vice versa) and overstates classification accuracy on novel sequences.
4. **Simulated Read Benchmarking Design and Single-Hit Restriction**: In Fig 3a and Lines 52 and 149, simulated reads were generated from 1,383 coding sequences already present in the `kMermaid` model, and `BLASTX` and `DIAMOND` were evaluated under `max_target_seqs = 1` at the single-protein level, where random tie-breaking among identical homologs artificially depresses their accuracy. When those exact same single-protein alignments from `DIAMOND` and `BLASTX` are mapped onto the `kMermaid` cluster definitions ("cluster level" in Fig 3a and Fig 2a), `DIAMOND` and `BLASTX` resolve >93% of multi-mapping and match `kMermaid`'s cluster-level accuracy. Thus, the accuracy gain comes from post-hoc grouping of proteins into CD-HIT clusters rather than from the 5-mer frequency model itself.
5. **Coarse Resource Benchmarking Methodology**: In Lines 154–156, maximum RAM usage was not measured via process profiling (`/usr/bin/time -v` or `cgroup`/`sacct` `MaxRSS`), but by resubmitting SLURM jobs with 1–2 GB memory increments (500 MB, 1 GB, 2 GB, 4 GB, ..., 16 GB) until the job did not crash with an out-of-memory error, and wall-clock runtime was computed "manually using the date function in Linux." Furthermore, `DIAMOND` defaults to a large memory block size optimized for multi-core server throughput; benchmarking `DIAMOND` on 1 CPU without adjusting `--block-size` (which directly controls `DIAMOND`'s memory footprint) overstates `DIAMOND`'s minimum memory requirements.
6. **Arbitrary Tie-Breaking**: As noted in Line 95, ties in maximum cluster score $s_{r,C}$ are broken by selecting whichever cluster first reaches the maximum during iteration, introducing dictionary/hash-order dependence for low-complexity or conserved domain reads.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim that grouping proteins into homologous clusters resolves >93% of short-read multi-mapping ambiguity (`Fig 2a`)**: `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrating that >93% of top `BLASTX`/`DIAMOND` hits for a given read fall within a single 65–70% identity CD-HIT cluster is a solid empirical observation that justifies cluster-level functional aggregation.
- **Claim that `kMermaid` matches `BLASTX` sensitivity while running 1,600x faster on 100K reads and classifying 40M reads in 3.3 hours (`Fig 3c–d`)**: `[Moderately Supported]` — The speed and fixed ~2 GB memory footprint of a hash-table lookup over 2.57M 5-mers are credible, though `DIAMOND` is faster (1.9–2.2 hours on 40M reads) and achieves comparable cluster-level accuracy when its hits are mapped to the same cluster dictionary.
- **Claim that `kMermaid` accurately classifies unseen microbial proteins (`AUROC = 0.93–0.96`, `Fig 4a`)**: `[Overstated]` — Only 11.7%–13.6% of reverse-translated reads from the 2025 RefSeq proteins were classified at all, AUROC was restricted to the jointly mapped subset, and the default score threshold ($>3$) yielded high false positives unless manually raised to $6.3$–$8.0$ in a read-length-dependent manner.
- **Claim of remote homology detection on reads unclassified by `BLASTX` (`Fig 4d`)**: `[Moderately Supported]` — The authors validate reads in 6 clusters with very high `kMermaid` scores ($>20$) using `PSI-BLAST` and `HHblits3`, showing that composite cluster k-mer profiles can capture remote homologs missed by pairwise `BLASTX`, though this is demonstrated anecdotes/case-wise on 6 selected clusters rather than systematically quantified for false discovery rate across all `BLASTX`-unclassified reads.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Code Availability**: The LOTUS clinical trial metagenomic dataset is publicly available under SRA accession `PRJEB50699`, and the `kMermaid` Python package and precomputed model are hosted on a public GitHub repository (redacted for review).
- **Missing Reproducibility Details**: The exact selection criteria that reduced NCBI RefSeq microbial proteins to 43,176 sequences in Step 1 versus 1,797,426 sequences in Step 2 (and why 99.77% clustered into 32,308 groups at 70% identity) require explicit script and accession documentation. Additionally, normalizing $s_{r,C}$ by frame length and selecting the single best reading frame (rather than summing across all non-stop frames) should be evaluated and documented.
