# Blinded Review: OPEN_ANON_023
**Composite Score:** 5.8 / 10.0 (Rigor: 1.8/3.5 | Claims: 1.5/2.5 | Biology: 1.2/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.3/2.0)
**Bias Category:** Statistical/Design
**Verdict:** Major Revision
**Study Modality:** Wet-Lab Primary Experimental
**Technology Maturity:** Mature/Classical
**Epistemic Stage:** Clinical/Observational

### 1. Executive Summary
This multicenter prospective study (`NCT03402776`) evaluated the humoral immunogenicity and safety of a 3+1 pediatric hexavalent vaccine schedule (diphtheria, tetanus, poliomyelitis, hepatitis B, *Haemophilus influenzae* type b [Hib], and pertussis; Infanrix Hexa or Hexyon) administered at M0, M1, M2, and M12 in 104 adult allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) recipients enrolled 6–24 months post-transplant across 5 French centers. Originally designed as a randomized trial to test an extra dose (between M3 and M5) in poor responders (defined as seroprotection against $<4$ of 5 pathogens at M3) assuming a 50% suboptimal response rate, the trial was terminated early for futility after 104 enrollments because only 6 patients met the randomization criterion. Pivoting to an observational single-arm analysis of the remaining cohort, the authors report high seroprotection rates at M3 and M13 (97.5% tetanus, 97.5% diphtheria, 92.7% polio, 95.0% Hib, and 84.1% HBV at M13). While the clinical message is reassuring, the manuscript suffers from severe study design and measurement limitations: (1) upper-limit assay censoring (`1001 IU/L` for HBV and `9.1 µg/mL` for Hib) that distorts parametric geometric means and multivariate linear regressions; (2) internal mathematical contradictions in Table 1 and Table 3; (3) uncentralized serology across 5 centers without cross-laboratory standardization; (4) substantial unadjusted protocol schedule deviations (M13 sampling ranging from 25 to 542 days post-booster) and 20–35% attrition by M13; and (5) confounding by prior CRM-197-conjugated pneumococcal vaccines and pre-existing baseline seroprotection.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Internal Numerical Contradictions in Tables 1, 2, and 3:**
   - **Table 1 Impossible Median/IQR:** For the row "Time (days) between booster at month 12 and last antibody measure (theoretically 1 month)*", the reported value is `29 [34 – 46] Range: 25–542`. A median of `29` cannot lie outside its own interquartile range of `[34 – 46]`!
   - **Table 2 vs. Table 3 Baseline Seroprotection Contradiction:** In Table 2 ("At inclusion"), seroprotection rates are 25.3% for Diphtheria (meaning 74.7% are unprotected), 100% for Tetanus, 42.6% for Hepatitis B (57.4% unprotected), 32.6% for *H. influenzae* (67.4% unprotected), and 71.6% for Poliomyelitis (28.4% unprotected). By the pigeonhole principle, with 74.7% unprotected against Diphtheria, 67.4% unprotected against Hib, and 57.4% unprotected against HBV at inclusion, a large majority of patients (>75%) must be unprotected against at least 1 pathogen, and a substantial fraction must be unprotected against $\ge 2$ or $\ge 3$ pathogens at inclusion. Yet Table 3 ("At inclusion") reports that only `29.0% (20.3; 39.5)` of participants were "Not protected against at least 1 pathogen," and `0% (0; 4.9)` were unprotected against 2, 3, 4, or 5 pathogens! This is mathematically impossible given Table 2; it appears the authors either inverted "Protected" vs. "Not protected" in the M0 column of Table 3 or miscalculated the boolean intersection in R.
   - **Sample Size Discrepancies and Attrition at M13:** Of 104 enrolled patients (minus 3 randomized to the extra dose = 101 eligible for M13), only 81–82 were analyzed at M13 for Diphtheria, Tetanus, Hib, and Polio (~20% loss to follow-up), and only **68** (33% loss to follow-up) were analyzed for Hepatitis B at M13. No explanation is given for why 13 patients who had M13 sera analyzed for Diphtheria/Tetanus/Hib lacked M13 Hepatitis B measurements, nor whether selective dropout biased the M13 HBV seroprotection rate (84.1%).
2. **Right-Censoring Distortion of Geometric Means and Linear Regression Models:** In Table 2 footnotes, the authors state that "values above 1 000 IU/L were given an arbitrary value of 1 001" for Hepatitis B, and "values above 9 µg/mL were given an arbitrary value of 9.1" for *H. influenzae* b. At M3 and M13, the median and entire interquartile range for Hib is `9 [9 – 9]`, and at M13 the median for HBV is `1000 [89 – 1000]`, indicating that >50% of observations hit the upper limit of quantification (ULOQ). Truncating right-censored data at the ULOQ (`9.1` or `1001`) makes the reported geometric means (`6.52` and `6.54 µg/mL` for Hib; `110` and `181 IU/L` for HBV) and their 95% CIs severely downward-biased and biologically meaningless. Even worse, fitting ordinary least squares (OLS) multivariate linear regression models (line 71 and line 174) to a dependent variable where >75% of values are tied at a ceiling of `9.1 µg/mL` violates normality and homoscedasticity assumptions; Tobit (censored) regression or logistic regression on seroprotection thresholds should have been used.
3. **Lack of Pre-Booster (M12) Timepoint and Extreme Timing Heterogeneity:** Antibody titers were measured at M0, M3, and M13, but **not** at M12 immediately prior to the fourth dose. Consequently, the study cannot evaluate antibody waning between M3 and M12 or distinguish the true booster effect of the 4th dose. Furthermore, time from M0 to the booster ranged from 12 to 37 months, and time from the booster to the "M13" blood draw ranged from 25 to 542 days (1.5 years), introducing massive unmodeled decay variance into the M13 endpoint.
4. **Decentralized Assays and Confounding by Concurrent/Prior Vaccines:** Serological assays were performed locally across 5 centers without centralized re-testing, reference standard calibration, or center-level random effects in the regression models (moreover, poliovirus seroprotection mixed two different assay units: dilution titer $\ge 1/8$ or $\ge 0.5\text{ IU/mL}$). In addition, all patients received three doses of 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV13, which uses CRM-197, a non-toxic mutant diphtheria toxin, as its carrier protein) prior to inclusion, directly confounding both baseline (25.3%) and post-vaccination anti-diphtheria responses.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: A 3+1 pediatric hexavalent vaccination schedule initiated 6–24 months post-HSCT achieves >92% seroprotection for tetanus, diphtheria, Hib, and polio, and 84.1% for HBV in selected adults off immunosuppression.** `[Moderately Supported]` — The high post-vaccination seropositivity rates are empirically clear, though restricted to a favorable subset of patients (requiring $\text{CD19}^+ \ge 0.1\text{ G/L}$, $\text{IgG} \ge 4\text{ g/L}$, and no immunosuppressive therapy except ruxolitinib/photopheresis) and partially inflated by high baseline seropositivity (100% tetanus, 71.6% polio, 42.6% HBV at M0).
- **Claim 2: Anti-Hib antibody levels at M13 are significantly associated with baseline gammaglobulins and $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells in multivariate linear regression.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Because >75% of M13 anti-Hib values are right-censored at the assay ceiling (`9 µg/mL`, median `9 [9–9]`), standard linear regression on M13 anti-Hib levels is statistically invalid and driven by the few uncensored low outliers, compounded by unadjusted multiple testing across 5 pathogens $\times$ 10 covariates.
- **Claim 3: Apart from hepatitis B, it may not be necessary to assess post-vaccine antibody levels.** `[Overstated]` — Given that this cohort excluded patients with low B cells ($\text{CD19}^+ < 0.1\text{ G/L}$), hypogammaglobulinemia ($<4\text{ g/L}$), or active immunosuppressive therapy, and did not measure long-term waning beyond M13, generalizing that serology monitoring is unnecessary across adult HSCT recipients overstates the scope of the inclusion criteria.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Clinical trial registration (`NCT03402776`, EudraCT `2017-003523-30`) and an open-access OSF repository (`https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/KYDU6`) are provided.
- **Reporting Gaps:** The manuscript lacks the full regression output tables (regression coefficients $\beta$, standard errors, exact $p$-values, and $R^2$ for the multivariate models discussed on line 174), the breakdown of how many patients received Infanrix Hexa vs. Hexyon (or mixed regimens), and the exact patient numbers in the Fig 1 flowchart (which is referenced only as a stub caption in the text).
