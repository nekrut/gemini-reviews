### 1. Executive Summary
This study evaluates portable Oxford Nanopore sequencing (R10.4.1 chemistry, `SQK-LSK114`, Dorado v1.0.2 SUP v5.0.0 basecalling) against Illumina NovaSeq 6000 ($2 \times 150\text{ bp}$) and camera trapping for vertebrate environmental DNA (eDNA) metabarcoding (`12SV05` ~97 bp and `16Smam1/2` ~95 bp primers) across five freshwater bodies in Luambe and Lukusuzi National Parks, Zambia. Using a split-pool design where the exact same cleaned amplicon pool (48 tagged PCR reactions from 15 biological samples and negative controls) was sequenced on both platforms and processed through an identical bioinformatics pipeline (`OBITools4`, `Cutadapt`, `VSEARCH`, `MIDORI2`), the authors identify 26 vertebrate taxa across platforms ($18$ shared, $7$ Nanopore-only, $1$ Illumina-only). They also quantify NCBI reference database completeness for Zambian terrestrial vertebrates and successfully execute an end-to-end *in situ* mobile laboratory workflow inside a tent at Mwendi Lagoon over six consecutive days. The split-pool experimental design, transparent reporting of negative controls and database gaps, and successful field deployment are strong contributions. Minor revisions are needed to account for sequencing depth differences between platforms and to correct minor internal numerical and typographical inconsistencies.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Uncontrolled Sequencing Depth Asymmetry Between Nanopore and Illumina (Results Lines 99 & 103):** In the platform comparison, mean sequencing depth per sample was $5.65\times$ higher on Nanopore than Illumina for the 12S marker ($273,710 \pm 543,585$ vs. $48,459 \pm 41,615$ reads) and $1.87\times$ higher for the 16S marker ($227,434 \pm 309,771$ vs. $121,653 \pm 89,537$ reads), with maximum Nanopore yields reaching $2.42 \times 10^6$ reads per sample vs. $1.61 \times 10^5$ on Illumina. Because reads were not rarefied or downsampled to equal depth prior to OTU clustering and taxonomic comparison, the detection of $7$ additional taxa by Nanopore ($25$ vs. $19$ taxa) is confounded by substantially deeper sequencing on the MinION flow cell. Including a rarefaction or depth-normalized comparison would clarify whether the extra detections arise from sequencing depth or platform characteristics.
2. **Internal Arithmetic Inconsistency in Taxon Counts (Lines 103 & 111):** Line 103 reports that $18$ taxa were detected by both technologies, $7$ only by Nanopore, and $1$ only by Illumina, which sums to $26$ total unique taxa across both eDNA platforms ($25$ for Nanopore, $19$ for Illumina). On line 111, the text states that "eDNA-based methods yielded 25 detections across all four primary terrestrial vertebrates, out of which 14 were at the species level and 5 at the genus and 7 at the family level, respectively." However, $14 + 5 + 7 = 26$, not $25$. The text should clarify whether the total is $26$ across both eDNA platforms (with $25$ detected by Nanopore).
3. **Contamination in Initial Negative Controls and Equal-Volume PCR Pooling:**
   - Line 51 notes that all visualized PCR products from the 2023 campaign, "including the negative controls," showed an amplified band of the expected size, and line 103 notes that *Xenopus* sp. was detected in the negative controls and excluded. Additionally, pooling equal volumes ($10\ \mu\text{L}$, line 56) rather than equimolar quantities of the 48 PCR products explains the extreme variance in per-sample read depth ($3$ to $161,352$ reads on Illumina 12S; $18$ to $2,424,297$ reads on Nanopore 12S). While the 2025 *in situ* field protocol substantially improved contamination control (using enclosed Smith-Root $1.2\ \mu\text{m}$ PES filter packs, blank extraction controls, and human blocking oligos, yielding zero local vertebrate detections in negative controls), the authors should briefly discuss the risk of excluding true environmental amphibians (*Xenopus*) when subtracting taxa found in negative controls, as well as how many low-read-count samples fell below a reliable detection depth.
4. **Minor Typographical Error on Line 74:** Line 74 states "We only focused our subsequent analyses on primary non-terrestrial vertebrate classes," whereas the rest of the manuscript (e.g., lines 101, 103, 111, 119) refers to "primary terrestrial vertebrate classes" (mammals, birds, reptiles, and amphibians, excluding fish).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: R10.4.1 Nanopore sequencing with Dorado SUP basecalling recapitulates Illumina short-read metabarcoding of ~95–97 bp vertebrate 12S/16S amplicons.** `[Well Supported]` — $18$ of $19$ Illumina-detected taxa were recovered by Nanopore from the same split PCR pool; however, the claim that Nanopore "surpasses" Illumina should be tempered unless confirmed after normalizing for the $1.9\text{--}5.7\times$ higher Nanopore read depth.
- **Claim 2: Freshwater eDNA and camera trapping detect largely complementary subsets of the vertebrate community (e.g., semi-aquatic species, amphibians, and reptiles via eDNA vs. antelopes and brief water-visiting birds/mammals via camera traps).** `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by paired multi-site and longitudinal 6-day observations (Figures 3–5) and ecologically well reasoned.
- **Claim 3: Complete end-to-end eDNA metabarcoding (from water filtration to GPU basecalling and taxonomic assignment) can be executed in a mobile tent laboratory in a remote national park.** `[Well Supported]` — Demonstrated on-site across 6 consecutive sampling days at Mwendi Lagoon with clean negative controls.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data & Code Availability:** Raw sequencing data are publicly available in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive under BioProject accession `PRJNA1195924`, and the bioinformatic analysis scripts are available in a public GitHub repository.
- **Protocol Completeness:** Primer and human-blocker sequences, PCR cycling parameters, reagent kits, software versions (`Dorado v1.0.2 SUP v5.0.0`, `OBITools4`, `Cutadapt v4.9`, `VSEARCH v2.28.1`, `MIDORI2 v266`), and geographical coordinates of all sampling sites are thoroughly documented. Clarifying in line 58 whether the two libraries sequenced consecutively on the flow cell corresponded to the 12S and 16S amplicon pools will complete the wet-lab documentation.
