### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript proposes the Haplotype Function Score (HFS) framework for genome-wide association studies (GWAS), fine-mapping, biological pathway enrichment, and cross-ancestry polygenic risk prediction. Using imputed genotypes from the UK Biobank ($n = 350,587$ White British training set across 14 complex traits, plus non-British European, South Asian, East Asian, and African validation cohorts), the authors partition the hg38 genome into 617,378 non-overlapping 4,096 bp loci (590,959 polymorphic), reconstruct phased haplotype sequences (`SHAPEIT4`), and apply the sequence-based deep learning model `Sei` to compute 39 sequence class scores per haplotype. Defining a locus's HFS as the individual's mean haplotype score for the hg38 reference sequence class, the authors perform marginal GLM association testing, apply `SuSiE` across 1,361 LD blocks for individual-level fine-mapping, run OLS regressions of `SuSiE` posterior inclusion probabilities (PIPs) against `cS2G` pathway, `EpiMap` tissue, and `scATAC-seq` cell-type annotations, and integrate block-wise HFS scores with `LDAK-BOLT` SNP polygenic risk scores (PRS) via LASSO regression.

The core idea of aggregating local phased common and rare variants through a deep-learning sequence-to-chromatin model to reduce local LD and capture rare-variant burden is innovative and timely. Moreover, the authors commendably report negative results where HFS underperforms (e.g., marginal association power is lower than SNP GWAS at 95% of loci, and HFS misses 84% of exonic causal SNPs identified by `PolyFun`). However, several methodological asymmetries and statistical assumptions—including comparing individual-level HFS `SuSiE` against summary-statistic SNP fine-mappers, simulating phenotypes directly from HFS in the benchmark simulation, discarding 38 of 39 `Sei` sequence classes when half of all loci switch dominant class across haplotypes, shifting sequence frames via 3' truncation/padding of indels, and tuning 1,362 LASSO weights in small target-ancestry cohorts—require careful qualification and methodological refinement.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Asymmetric Comparison in Fine-Mapping (Individual-Level `SuSiE` with Rare Variants vs. Summary-Statistic `PolyFun`/`SBayesRC` on Common SNPs, Lines 70–71, 81):** The authors report that HFS + `SuSiE` identifies a median of 63 more causal signals ($\text{PIP} > 0.95$) per trait than SNP-based `PolyFun` or `SBayesRC`, and note that 55.3% of HFS causal signals disappear when rare variants ($\text{MAF} < 0.01$, down to allele count $\ge 10$, $\text{MAF} \approx 1.4 \times 10^{-5}$) are excluded (Line 81). This comparison confounds three distinct factors: (a) individual-level genotype `SuSiE` vs. summary-statistics fine-mapping; (b) inclusion of rare variants ($\text{MAC} \ge 10$) in HFS vs. common-variant MAF thresholds in `SBayesRC`/`PolyFun`; and (c) prior rounding of HFS values to 2 decimal places (`round(HFS - HFS_ref, 2)`, Page 2 Line 6), which collapses haplotypes with small predicted differences into identical zero values and alters the empirical correlation matrix passed to `SuSiE`.
2. **Generative Model Circularity in the Fine-Mapping Simulation (Page 2 Lines 40–43):** To benchmark `HFS + SuSiE` against `REGENIE + PolyFun`, the authors simulate quantitative traits on chromosome 1 ($n = 50,000$) by sampling 500 causal loci and generating the genetic liability *directly as a linear combination of the locus HFS values* ($y = \sum \beta_k \text{HFS}_k + \epsilon$, $h^2 = 0.1$). Because the simulated phenotype is generated directly from the exact HFS variable tested by `HFS + SuSiE` (rather than from underlying causal SNPs with realistic functional vs. non-functional architectures), `HFS + SuSiE` is guaranteed by construction to have a correctly specified model, whereas single-SNP `PolyFun` faces synthetic allelic heterogeneity across all rare and common variants within each 4,096 bp window.
3. **Information Loss from Selecting Only the Reference Sequence Class and Ad Hoc Rounding (Line 61, 63; Page 2 Line 6):** `Sei` outputs 39 sequence class scores per 4,096 bp haplotype. The authors define HFS exclusively using the single sequence class that attains the maximum score in the *hg38 reference haplotype* (Line 63), even though they observe on Line 61 that **49.7% of all loci** contain haplotypes whose dominant sequence class differs from the reference haplotype (e.g., a variant creating a *de novo* transcription factor binding site or enhancer in a reference heterochromatin/transcribed locus). Furthermore, rounding `HFS - HFS_ref` to two decimal places (`0.01`, Page 2 Line 6) to suppress "`Sei` prediction noise" applies a uniform absolute cutoff across sequence classes that have different variance scales, arbitrarily zeroing out real small-effect regulatory variants in low-variance classes.
4. **Positional Frame-Shift Artifacts from Indel Truncation/Padding in 4,096 bp Windows (Line 87, 181):** When constructing haplotype FASTA sequences (`bcftools consensus`), insertions longer than the reference cause base pairs at the $3'$ end of the 4,096 bp window to be truncated, while deletions are padded with `N`s at the $3'$ end (Line 181). Consequently, an insertion or deletion near the $5'$ end of a window shifts the coordinate index of every downstream base pair relative to the reference sequence, And removes or adds sequence at the $3'$ boundary. Because `Sei` is a convolutional neural network whose predictions depend on sequence composition across the full 4,096 bp receptive field, $3'$ boundary truncation/padding and positional frame shifts can induce artificial score changes unrelated to the biological effect of the indel—consistent with the authors' observation that removing indels significantly reduces the genomic inflation factor $\lambda_{\text{GC}}$ ($p = 0.0001$, Line 87).
5. **OLS Linear Regression on Bounded, Zero-Inflated `SuSiE` PIPs in Enrichment Analysis (Page 2 Lines 11–29, 46–49):** To test pathway, tissue, and cell-type enrichment, the authors fit a standard multivariate ordinary least squares (OLS) regression with the locus `SuSiE` $\text{PIP} \in [0, 1]$ as the dependent variable against binary functional annotations and baseline covariates. Because $>99\%$ of loci have $\text{PIP} \approx 0$ and causal loci cluster near $\text{PIP} = 1$, the residuals are severely non-normal and heteroscedastic, violating OLS assumptions and producing extreme $t$-statistics ($p < 10^{-30}$) and widespread false-positive confounding (requiring the authors to manually discard pathways and cell types that reached significance in $>50\%$ of traits, Page 2 Line 29). A logistic/probit regression (e.g., on $\text{PIP} > 0.95$ or quasibinomial GLM on PIP) or stratified LDSC-style jackknife standard errors across the 1,361 blocks should be used to calibrate Type I error.
6. **Degrees-of-Freedom Asymmetry in Cross-Ancestry PRS Tuning (Lines 136–144; Page 2 Lines 35–38):** In the cross-ancestry height prediction comparison (Fig. 5C), `HFS + LDAK` fits a LASSO regression with **1,362 predictors** (1,361 block-wise HFS scores + 1 `LDAK` genome-wide SNP PRS) inside the target-ancestry tuning split (50% of NBE, SAS, EAS, or AFR samples), whereas the comparator `Polypred` (`LDAK + PolyFun-pred` or `LDAK + SBayesRC`) tunes only **2 global mixing weights**. Allowing LASSO to re-weight 1,361 local genomic blocks directly in the target ancestry provides `HFS + LDAK` with 1,360 additional degrees of freedom to capture local ancestry/LD differences. A fair comparison should either combine the single genome-wide HFS PRS with `LDAK` PRS (2 parameters, matching `Polypred`) or allow `LDAK`/`SBayesRC` to also be tuned across the 1,361 blocks via LASSO.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *HFS reduces LD between adjacent genomic units compared to SNPs and captures rare-variant regulatory burden.* `[Well Supported]` — Adjacent 4,096 bp HFS blocks show low median $R^2 = 0.013$ (rising to $0.14$ when rare variants are excluded), confirming that integrating rare alleles within functional windows breaks up long-range common-variant LD.
- **Claim 2:** *HFS outperforms SNP-based fine-mapping in non-coding regulatory regions (identifying 2,699 causal locus-trait associations and resolving MHC signals), whereas SNP-based fine-mapping is superior in coding exons.* `[Moderately Supported]` — The biological enrichment of HFS causal loci in promoters ($\text{OR} = 2.33$) and bivalent enhancers ($\text{OR} = 2.22$), the replication of `PolyFun` causal SNPs, and the transparent demonstration that HFS misses 84% of exonic causal SNPs (Line 131) are well-grounded, though the raw count advantage over `PolyFun`/`SBayesRC` is partially inflated by comparing individual-level vs. summary-level inputs.
- **Claim 3:** *Integrating HFS with LDAK PRS improves cross-ancestry polygenic prediction by 16.1–39.8%.* `[Fragile]` — While the predictive gain over single-score `LDAK` is statistically significant in the held-out half of each ancestry group, this gain is only demonstrated for a single trait (height) and is confounded by tuning 1,361 block weights via LASSO in the target ancestry vs. 2 global weights for `Polypred`.
