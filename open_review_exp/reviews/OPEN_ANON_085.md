### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates the transcriptomic and neuroendocrine effects of long-term $17\alpha$-estradiol administration (14.4 ppm in diet for 6 months, from 24 to 30 months of age) on the hypothalamus of aged male Brown Norway (BN) rats. The authors generated single-nucleus RNA-seq (snRNA-seq) libraries from pooled hypothalami of young control ($Y$, 4 months old), aged control ($O$, 30 months old), and $17\alpha$-estradiol-treated aged ($O.T$, 30 months old) male rats, supplemented by serum hormone enzyme immunoassays ($n = 3$ rats/group), testicular H&E histology ($n = 8$ testes/group), and two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) across 204 human plasma pQTL datasets. By defining 209 "supervised" neuronal subsets based on non-zero expression of neuropeptides, hormones, or receptors, the authors report that $17\alpha$-estradiol counteracts age-related elevations in neuronal oxidative phosphorylation/mTORC1 signaling, restores synaptic pathways, increases oxytocin (*Oxt*) and *Gnrh1* activity alongside serum testosterone, and induces a stress signature in *Crh* neurons. While the biological question—how $17\alpha$-estradiol mediates male-specific lifespan extension via hypothalamic neuroendocrine circuits—is of high interest, the study is substantially weakened by an unreplicated pooled snRNA-seq design ($N = 1$ library per group), pseudoreplication in differential expression and testis histology, overlapping single-UMI cell definitions, and an unadjusted human pQTL MR analysis that does not establish neuroendocrine causality in rats.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unreplicated Pooled snRNA-seq Design ($N = 1$ Library per Condition) and Single-Cell Pseudoreplication:** As stated in Materials and Methods ("snRNA-seq data processing"), two ($O$) or three ($Y$ and $O.T$) hypothalami were physically pooled and homogenized into a single 10x Chromium library per group without sample-level multiplexing/hashtagging. Consequently, the biological sample size for all snRNA-seq comparisons is $N = 1$ pool per condition. Every cell-type proportion shift (Fig 1C, Fig 6A), differential expression test (`FindMarkers(test.use = "bimod")` treating thousands of nuclei from one library as independent biological replicates), and `Augur` perturbation ranking (Fig 3C–D) completely confounds inter-animal biological variation, dissection variability (e.g., capture of pars tuberalis, ependymal layers, or specific hypothalamic nuclei like PVN/SON/ARC), and 10x library preparation batch effects with treatment effects. Furthermore, running `Harmony` batch correction across three unreplicated condition libraries risks distorting genuine group differences while failing to estimate biological variance.
2. **Inconsistent Animal Age Reporting and Testicular Pseudoreplication:** In Materials and Methods ("Animals, treatment, and tissues"), the text states: *"Twelve Norway brown male rats (12-months-old) were acquired from Charles River, including 8 12-months-old and 4 1-month-old (Beijing)... Four aged rats treated with 17α-estradiol ... starting at 24 months of age for 6 months."* This implies the 8 older rats were housed from 12 to 24 months before starting the 6-month treatment (sacrificed at 30 months, as noted in Results line 154), which should be stated clearly. More importantly, with only 4 rats in $O$ and 4 rats in $O.T$, the "Seminiferous tubule inflammation test" (Methods line 21) states *"Eight testes were obtained from each sample group"*—meaning both left and right testes from the same 4 rats were treated as $n = 8$ independent biological replicates in the statistical test (Fig 7C, $p = 0.15$).
3. **"Supervised Clustering" via Non-Mutually-Exclusive Single-Transcript Thresholding (`expression > 0`):** The 209 neuronal "subtypes" (104 peptide/hormone-expressing and 105 receptor-expressing) were defined by subsetting any neuron with target gene expression $> 0$ (i.e., $\ge 1$ UMI count), down to a minimum of 6 or 10 cells per subset. This creates two major artifacts: (i) individual hypothalamic neurons co-express multiple neuropeptides and receptors, so the same cells are counted repeatedly across many of the 209 subsets (as evidenced by the barcode overlap in Figure 3—figure supplement 1), violating independence across subtypes; and (ii) in pooled hypothalamic nuclear homogenates, highly expressed transcripts (*Oxt, Avp, Pomc*) contribute to ambient RNA soup, causing false-positive "expression $> 0$" calls at a 1-UMI threshold unless ambient RNA removal (e.g., SoupX/CellBender) and stricter expression thresholds are applied.
4. **Circularity in Defining and Validating `c1-up-signature` and `c4-up-signature` (Section 2.2, Fig 2C–E):** The authors partitioned neurons into quartiles (`c1`–`c4`) based on the expression of six selected metabolic and synaptic pathways, identified genes differentially expressed in `c1` (19 genes) and `c4` (12 genes) using `FindAllMarkers` on those exact cells, and then correlated the resulting 19-gene and 12-gene signatures back against the same pathways in the same cells (Fig 2E) to claim that the signatures validate the opposing regulatory networks. This is statistically circular.
5. **Underpowered Serum ELISAs ($n = 3$/group), Uncorrected Multiple $t$-Tests, and Steroid Cross-Reactivity:** Serum hormone assays were performed on only $n = 3$ animals per group and analyzed with pairwise two-tailed unpaired $t$-tests across the three groups without one-way ANOVA or multiple-testing correction. Several key claims rely on statistically non-significant p-values ($p = 0.078$ for cortisol in Fig 5E; $p = 0.096$ for Gnrh $O.T$ vs. $O$ in Fig 7B; $p = 0.052$ for testosterone $O.T$ vs. $Y$; $p = 0.15$ for testis inflammation in Fig 7C). Additionally, animals were chronically fed high-dose $17\alpha$-estradiol (14.4 ppm); commercial competitive immunoassays for serum estradiol ($17\beta$-estradiol) and testosterone in unextracted rodent serum are prone to cross-reactivity and matrix interference, requiring validation by LC–MS/MS.
6. **Methodologically Flawed Human pQTL Bidirectional MR Analysis (Fig 6F, Fig 7F–G):** To infer downstream targets of hypothalamic Oxt and Gnrh, the authors ran bidirectional two-sample MR between human peripheral blood SomaLogic aptamer levels of `OXT` (`prot-a-2159`) and `GNRH1` (`prot-a-1233`) and 204 endocrine traits using a relaxed IV threshold ($P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$) and unadjusted $p < 0.05$. First, across $2 \times 204 \times 2 = 816$ directional MR tests, nominal $p < 0.05$ without FDR or Bonferroni correction guarantees dozens of false positives. Second, selecting pQTL instruments at $P < 10^{-5}$ mixes *trans*-pQTLs (which reflect pleiotropic blood protein factors, clearance, or aptamer binding artifacts) with *cis* variants, explaining why 14 traits were significantly causal in *both* directions with OXT (Fig 6—figure supplement 2). Third, human peripheral blood aptamer levels do not reflect pulsatile hypothalamic portal GnRH or central paraventricular oxytocin signaling in aged rats.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: $17\alpha$-estradiol attenuates aging-associated hypermetabolism (OXPHOS/mTORC1) and restores synapse-related transcriptomic programs in aged male hypothalamic neurons.** `[Moderately Supported]` The global shift in neuronal metabolic vs. synaptic gene expression between $O$, $O.T$, and $Y$ is biologically plausible and consistent with prior studies on $17\alpha$-estradiol and mTOR/anorexigenic signaling, though independent biological replicates (e.g., qPCR, RNAscope/immunohistochemistry on individual rat hypothalami) are required to confirm findings from a single pooled snRNA-seq library per group.
- **Claim 2: $17\alpha$-estradiol activates the HPG axis (elevating plasma GnRH and testosterone while lowering estradiol) and increases oxytocin secretion.** `[Fragile]` With only $n = 3$ serum samples per group, GnRH ($O.T$ vs. $O$, $p = 0.096$) and testis inflammation ($p = 0.15$) did not reach statistical significance, and dramatic shifts in apparent *Oxt* neuron proportion in the pooled snRNA-seq library may simply reflect variation in paraventricular/supraoptic nucleus inclusion during dissection of the pooled tissue.
- **Claim 3: Bidirectional Mendelian randomization in human pQTLs establishes that GnRH1 and OXT causally mediate the endocrine effects of $17\alpha$-estradiol on POMC, glucagon, BDNF, and testosterone.** `[Methodologically Flawed]` Uncorrected $p < 0.05$ screening across 204 traits using $P < 10^{-5}$ trans-pQTL instruments in human plasma does not establish causal mediation of central $17\alpha$-estradiol action in the rat hypothalamus.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw and processed snRNA-seq data are publicly deposited in NCBI GEO under accession `GSE248413`.
- **Reagent & Assay Reporting:** Commercial kit catalog numbers for Macklin Biochemical $17\alpha$-estradiol (`E834897`), RayBiotech ELISAs (`EIAR-OXT`, `EIAR-CRF`, `EIAR-GNRH`), and Enzyme-linked Biotechnology kits (`ml002868`, `ml002891`, `ml002876`, `ml002874`) as well as OpenGWAS dataset IDs are clearly documented.
- **Reporting Gaps:** Custom R scripts for the supervised clustering, quartile signature generation, and MR filtering are not deposited in a public code repository. Additionally, the exact number of valid nuclei passing QC in each of the three libraries ($Y$, $O$, $O.T$) and the reference genome build used in Cell Ranger v3.1.0 (e.g., `Rnor_6.0` vs. `mRatBN7.2`) are not stated in the Methods text.
