### 1. Executive Summary
This retrospective observational cohort study uses US Veterans Health Administration (VHA) Corporate Data Warehouse electronic health record (EHR) data (1 October 2021 – 1 October 2023) to evaluate associations between Long COVID documentation (ICD-10 code `U09.9`) and eight clinical outcomes (primary care visits, new glucose-lowering drug classes, weight change, mean post-index HbA1c, insulin initiation, dialysis initiation, VA hospitalization, and all-cause mortality) among $N = 1,896,080$ Veterans with prevalent diabetes ($20,438$ [1.08%] with `U09.9` and $1,875,642$ without `U09.9`). While the national VHA sample size is large, the manuscript is undermined by severe internal numerical contradictions (including claiming that 75,944 diabetic Veterans had `U09.9` without a prior positive test when only 20,438 individuals in the entire cohort had `U09.9`), direct contradictions between Table 3 and the Discussion regarding mortality significance among insulin users, unit mislabeling (pounds vs. kilograms), failure to account for differential follow-up time or competing mortality risks in logistic/Poisson models, and lack of adjustment for baseline kidney function (eGFR/CKD stage) and baseline HbA1c.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Mathematically Impossible Subcohort Count in Results (Line 58):** In Results (line 58) and Table 1, the total number of Veterans with `U09.9` documentation in the cohort is **20,438** (out of $1,896,080$, or 1.08%). However, at the end of the very same paragraph (line 58), the authors write: *"A total of 75,944 individuals (0.75%) had U09 without a prior positive test for SARS-CoV-2.”"* (complete with a stray closing quotation mark). It is mathematically impossible for 75,944 individuals in the study to have `U09.9` without a positive test when only 20,438 individuals in the study have `U09.9` in total (and $75,944 / 0.0075 \approx 10.1\text{ million}$, indicating this sentence was erroneously pasted from an unstratified VHA-wide query).
2. **Direct Contradiction Between Table 3 and the Discussion/Conclusions on Mortality in Insulin Users:** In the Discussion (line 185) and Conclusions (line 193), the authors state twice: *"Among individuals using insulin at baseline, associations of Long COVID documentation with dialysis and mortality were not statistically significant."* However, Table 3 (line 175, `Odds of mortality`) reports that among current insulin users ($n = 521,812$), the adjusted odds ratio for mortality is **OR = 0.84 (95% CI: 0.78, 0.90)**, with an interaction $p < 0.001$. An OR of 0.84 with a 95% CI of $0.78\text{–}0.90$ excludes 1.00 by a wide margin and is highly statistically significant.
3. **Multiple Internal Inconsistencies in Numbers, Units, and Interpretations:**
   - **Abstract vs. Table 2 IRR Percentage (Line 21 vs. Line 113):** The Abstract states that Veterans with `U09.9` had *"39% more primary care visits per year after the index date (incidence rate ratio [IRR] 1.36, 95%CI 1.36; 1.37)"*, whereas an IRR of 1.36 corresponds to **36%** more visits (as reported in Results line 113).
   - **Unit Error in Table 3 (Pounds vs. Kilograms, Line 171):** Table 2 reports `Change in weight, kg` as $\beta = 0.62\text{ kg}$ ($95\%\text{ CI}: 0.52, 0.72$). Table 3 (line 171) labels the row `Change in weight, pounds` and reports $\beta = 0.51$ ($0.33, 0.69$) in insulin users and $\beta = 0.65$ ($0.53, 0.77$) in non-users. Because the sample-size-weighted average of 0.51 and 0.65 equals $0.61\text{ kg}$ (matching Table 2's $\beta = 0.62\text{ kg}$), the values in Table 3 are in **kilograms**, not pounds.
   - **Contradictory Interpretation of Weight Change:** Unadjusted descriptive statistics (line 111) show that both groups *lost* weight during follow-up, with `U09.9` patients losing less weight ($-1.5\text{ kg}$ vs. $-4.2\text{ kg}$ [printed with a typo as `-4.2.0 kg`]). In the adjusted linear regression, $\beta = +0.62\text{ kg}$ therefore represents 0.62 kg *less weight loss*, yet Results line 113 calls it *"a 0.62 kg greater weight gain"* while the Conclusions (lines 26, 185, 193) state the opposite (*"Individuals with Long COVID documentation did not have more weight gain"*).
   - **Missing BMI Rate Discrepancy:** Methods line 53 states that multiple imputation was used for BMI *"due to approximately 20% missing values"*, whereas Table 1 (line 90) shows BMI is missing in 12.8% ($240,420 / 1,875,642$) of unexposed and 4.6% ($930 / 20,438$) of exposed patients.
4. **Denominator Error for Incident Outcomes and Lack of Competing-Risk / Time-to-Event Modeling:**
   - **Prevalent Users Included in "Initiated Insulin" Model (Table 2):** Table 2 reports logistic regression for `Initiated insulin` with $n = 1,896,080$ in the header, even though $521,812$ patients (27.5%, Table 3) were already using insulin at baseline and therefore were not at risk of *initiating* insulin (nor was prevalence of baseline dialysis excluded or reported).
   - **Logistic Regression vs. Survival/Competing-Risk Analysis:** Follow-up durations varied across the 2-year window, and 12–13% of the cohort died during follow-up. Note that raw mortality was higher in the `U09.9` group (13% vs. 12%, line 111), yet adjusted logistic regression flipped the odds ratio to protective ($\text{OR} = 0.87$). Modeling incident dialysis, insulin initiation, and hospitalization via static logistic regression without accounting for person-time at risk or the competing risk of death (12–13%)—and modeling primary care visits via ordinary Poisson regression despite extreme overdispersion—is methodologically suboptimal.
5. **Unmeasured Confounding by Baseline eGFR/CKD Stage and Baseline HbA1c:** All models adjust only for the composite Charlson comorbidity index, hypertension, and dyslipidemia, without adjusting for baseline eGFR/CKD stage, baseline HbA1c, or baseline diabetes medication intensity. Because `U09.9` patients had substantially higher baseline comorbidity (Charlson 3.5 vs. 2.5, hypertension 74% vs. 52%, prior $\ge 10$ primary care visits 34% vs. 16%), omitting baseline eGFR when modeling dialysis initiation ($\text{OR} = 1.34$) and omitting baseline HbA1c when modeling post-index HbA1c leaves severe residual confounding.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Long COVID documentation (U09.9) in Veterans with diabetes is associated with higher subsequent primary care utilization, medication intensification, and VA hospitalization.* `[Moderately Supported]` — The effect sizes for healthcare utilization ($\text{IRR} = 1.36$) and hospitalization ($\text{OR} = 2.02$) are large, though partly affected by differential care-seeking behavior among patients receiving a specific `U09.9` diagnosis code compared to unexposed controls assigned a random index date within a visit month.
- **Claim 2:** *Long COVID documentation is independently associated with progression to incident dialysis (OR = 1.34), driven by non-metabolic mechanisms since HbA1c and weight did not worsen.* `[Fragile]` — Without adjusting for baseline eGFR, albuminuria, CKD stage, or prior acute kidney injury during acute COVID-19 hospitalization, the association between `U09.9` coding and dialysis initiation cannot be separated from pre-existing renal impairment at the index date.
- **Claim 3:** *Among insulin users, Long COVID documentation is not significantly associated with mortality.* `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Directly contradicted by Table 3 ($\text{OR} = 0.84$, $95\%\text{ CI}: 0.78\text{–}0.90$).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Restricted due to VHA patient privacy regulations (standard for VA CDW studies), with institutional contact details provided.
- **Code & Protocol Completeness:** The manuscript does not provide Stata analysis code, exact ICD-10/CPT code lists used to define incident dialysis, baseline diabetes, or glucose-lowering medication classes, or the exact sample sizes after exclusions for each outcome model in Table 2.
