### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents a continuous mathematical model implemented in COMSOL Multiphysics that couples a five-compartment cancer cell cycle ODE system ($G_1, S, G_2, M, G_0$), a reaction-diffusion equation for oxygen transport ($c_{ox}$), a Linear Quadratic (LQ) radiation cell-kill model modulated by an oxygen enhancement ratio ($OER$), and finite-deformation continuum tumor growth mechanics ($\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{F}_e \mathbf{F}_g$). The authors calibrate the mitosis transition rate ($k_M$) and dead-cell clearance rate ($C_r$) to published preclinical tumor growth datasets in murine HPV-positive head-and-neck cancer ($2 \times 6\text{ Gy}$) and murine prostate cancer ($2\text{ Gy}$ and $5\text{ Gy}$ schedules), as well as clinical volumetric trajectories from 10 human head-and-neck cancer patients. They then simulate conventionally fractionated radiotherapy (CFRT), moderately hypofractionated radiotherapy (MHRT), and stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) for prostate cancer under low ($S_v = 5000\text{ m}^{-1}$) and high ($S_v = 25000\text{ m}^{-1}$) functional vascular densities. While the multiphysics integration is well-motivated, the mathematical formulation contains dimensional inconsistencies, physically unguarded linear parametrizations for oxygen dependence, an unphysical initial condition placing 100% of tumor cells synchronously in $G_1$, and an internal mass-balance mismatch between cell division in Eq. (1) and volumetric growth in Eq. (14).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Mass-Balance Discrepancy Between Cell Cycle ODEs (Eqs. 1–5) and Volumetric Growth Stretch Equation (Eq. 14):**
   Summing the five normalized cell-cycle compartment equations (Eqs. 1–5) yields the rate of change of total viable tumor cells $T = G_1 + S + G_2 + M + G_0$:
   $$\frac{dT}{dt} = k_M M + (1 - \tilde{\alpha} k_{G_1} - \tilde{b} k_{G_1 G_0})\text{?}$$
   Wait: look closely at Eq. (1) vs. Eqs. (2) and (5)! In Eq. (1), the transition out of $G_1$ to $S$ is $-\tilde{\alpha} k_{G_1} G_1$ where $k_{G_1} = 0.035\text{ h}^{-1} = 0.84\text{ day}^{-1}$, and the transition out of $G_1$ to $G_0$ is $-\tilde{b} k_{G_1 G_0} G_1$ where $k_{G_1 G_0} = 0.09\text{ day}^{-1}$. These two terms cancel exactly with $+\tilde{\alpha} k_{G_1} G_1$ in Eq. (2) and $+\tilde{b} k_{G_1 G_0} G_1$ in Eq. (5). However, look at the radiation-killed cells $R_c c$: in Eqs. (1–5), radiation kills cells at rate $-\sum_c R_c c$, which simultaneously enters the dead cell pool $N_{tot}$ in Eq. (9) at rate $+\sum_c R_c c$. Yet in Eq. (14), the volumetric growth rate is defined as:
   $$\frac{3}{\lambda_g}\frac{d\lambda_g}{dt} = -\mu_{G_0} G_0 - \mu_{G_1} G_1 - \mu_S S - \mu_{G_2} G_2 - \mu_M M + k_M M - C_r N_{tot}$$
   Notice two critical flaws in Eq. (14) and Eq. (9):
   - First, in Eq. (9), $\frac{dN_n}{dt} = \sum_c R_c^n c$ has **no sink term** $-C_r N_{tot}$! If $N_{tot}$ is not depleted as dead cells are cleared at rate $C_r$, then $N_{tot}$ remains permanently positive and constant after radiotherapy finishes ($t > t_{\text{therapy}}$), causing $-C_r N_{tot}$ in Eq. (14) to remain a permanent negative constant forever, driving $\lambda_g \to 0$ (unbounded negative volumetric shrinkage even after all dead cells have been cleared)!
   - Second, during radiotherapy ($t = \text{therapy}$), viable cells $c$ are removed from Eqs. (1–5) by $-R_c c$, whileEq. (14) does not include $-\sum_c R_c c$ directly (which makes physical sense if dead cells still occupy volume until cleared by $-C_r N_{tot}$), *provided* $N_{tot}$ obeys $\frac{dN_{tot}}{dt} = \sum_c R_c c - C_r N_{tot}$. Writing Eq. (9) without the $-C_r N_{tot}$ clearance decay term is a fundamental ODE formulation error.
2. **Dimensional Inconsistency and Unboundedness of the Linear $OER$ Formula (Eq. 21):**
   Equation (21) defines the oxygen enhancement ratio as:
   $$OER = -10 c_{ox} + 3$$
   where $c_{ox}$ is the oxygen concentration in $\text{mol m}^{-3}$ with initial/vessel concentration $c_{i,ox} = 0.2\text{ mol m}^{-3}$ (Table 1). While this linear interpolation happens to give $OER = 1$ at $c_{ox} = 0.2\text{ mol m}^{-3}$ and $OER = 3$ at $c_{ox} = 0$, it deviates starkly from the standard biophysical Alper and Howard-Flanders hyperbolic relationship ($OER(pO_2) = \frac{m \cdot K + pO_2}{K + pO_2}$), which saturates rapidly at low oxygen tensions ($K \approx 3\text{ mmHg} \approx 0.004\text{ mol m}^{-3}$). Under the authors' linear formula Eq. (21), a moderate drop in oxygen from $0.2$ to $0.1\text{ mol m}^{-3}$ (still well within normoxic physiological tissue levels, $\sim 75\text{ mmHg}$) artificially inflates $OER$ to $2.0$, vastly exaggerating the radiosensitivity difference between $S_v = 5000\text{ m}^{-1}$ and $S_v = 25000\text{ m}^{-1}$!
3. **Unphysical Initial Cell-Cycle Synchronization and Constant Normalized Density Assumption:**
   In line 96, the authors state that *"the initial value of the sum of all five phases was set to 1 for the tumor region and to 0 for the host tissue. Particularly, cells were assumed to be in the $G_1$-phase at the start of every simulation."* Initializing 100% of tumor cells in $G_1$ at $t=0$ creates artificial transient cell-cycle oscillations across compartments instead of initializing at the ergodic steady-state phase distribution of the transition matrix. Furthermore, Eqs. (1–5) are written as pure ODEs without spatial advection ($\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{v} c)$) or dilution from volumetric expansion ($\frac{3}{\lambda_g}\frac{d\lambda_g}{dt} c$), meaning the local cell density $T$ is not coupled to the mechanical deformation $\mathbf{F}_g$ consistently.
4. **Inconsistent Initial Tumor Size vs. Preclinical/Clinical Validation Volumes:**
   Line 300 states: *"The model features a spherical tumor with an initial diameter of $500\ \mu\text{m}$"* (which corresponds to a volume of $\frac{\pi}{6}(0.5\text{ mm})^3 \approx 0.065\text{ mm}^3$). However, in the murine prostate validation (line 364), tumors start treatment at $100\text{ mm}^3$ and grow to $400\text{ mm}^3$, and human head-and-neck tumors are tens of cubic centimeters. Because oxygen diffusion length ($\sqrt{D_{ox} / (\text{uptake})} \approx 100\text{--}200\ \mu\text{m}$) is scale-dependent, simulating a $500\ \mu\text{m}$ sphere and rescaling relative volume does not preserve the spatial hypoxia core-to-rim ratio of a $100\text{ mm}^3$ or $30\text{ cm}^3$ tumor unless the physical radius was actually matched to each tumor—which is never clarified.
5. **Unreported Modified Parameter Values in Figures 9–11:**
   In Figures 9B, 9C, 10B, 10C, 11B, and 11C, the authors simulate "increased radioresistance" by "a decrease in parameter $\alpha$" (panels B) or "a higher $\alpha/\beta$ ratio" (panels C), but the exact numerical values of $\alpha$ and $\alpha/\beta$ used in panels B and C are never stated in the main text, Table 6, or the figure captions.
6. **Over-Interpretation of Deterministic Curves Killing "All Tumor Cells" (Fig 8):**
   In lines 443 and 481, the authors claim that *"all radiotherapy schemes manage to eliminate all tumor cells, by the end of the treatment. As a result, tumor recurrence does not occur in any of the cases."* In a continuum ODE model governed by exponential survival fractions ($Sf_c = e^{-\alpha D - \beta D^2}$), the normalized continuum variable $T(t)$ approaches zero on a linear plot (e.g., dropping below $1\%$ of initial density) while still representing $10^5\text{--}10^6$ surviving clonogenic cells per $\text{cm}^3$ unless a stochastic tumor control probability ($\text{TCP} = \exp(-N_{\text{clonogens}} \cdot Sf)$) is calculated. Equating visually small percentage survival on a linear scale (Figs 8–11) with complete tumor eradication ("kill all tumor cells") is a classic continuum modeling fallacy.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** Higher functional vascular density ($S_v$) and oxygenation increase radiation cell kill, with a larger relative impact on surviving fraction in more radioresistant tumors. `[Moderately Supported]` — Within the model's deterministic equations, reducing $OER$ increases $\alpha_{\text{reduced}}$ and $\beta_{\text{reduced}}$, which mathematically shifts survival fractions more noticeably on a linear percentage scale when baseline cell kill is incomplete (i.e., lower $\alpha$). However, the magnitude of this effect is heavily amplified by the unrealistic linear $OER(c_{ox})$ relationship in Eq. (21).
- **Claim 2:** All three prostate radiotherapy schedules (CFRT 78 Gy, MHRT 60 Gy, SBRT 36.25 Gy) eliminate all tumor cells and prevent recurrence, making SBRT optimal because it achieves maximum cell kill fastest. `[Overstated]` — Reaching a small percentage of initial cell density on a linear scale does not mean zero surviving clonogenic cells ($N=0$), and prostate cancer parameters ($k_M, C_r$) were chosen ad hoc without clinical prostate validation data or normal tissue complication probability (NTCP) modeling.
- **Claim 3:** Hyperfractionated radiotherapy provides a pronounced advantage over conventional fractionation in rapidly proliferating head-and-neck tumors (5x baseline $k_M$). `[Moderately Supported]` — This qualitative behavior aligns with standard radiobiological repopulation theory, though in the model it is driven by a single patient parameter set (`HN15`) with arbitrarily scaled $k_M$.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Model Equations & Parameters:** Most governing equations and baseline parameter tables (Tables 1–6) are written out, and the Data Availability statement notes that a COMSOL Multiphysics model report is included in the Supporting Information.
- **Missing Numerical Specifications:** Exact values of $\alpha$ and $\alpha/\beta$ for the radioresistant perturbations in Figs 9B–C, 10B–C, and 11B–C are omitted from the manuscript text and tables. Furthermore, the bulk modulus of the tumor in Table 1 is listed as $10.40 \times 10^7\text{ kPa}$ ($104\text{ GPa}$, comparable to solid steel!), whereas host tissue is $6.67\text{ kPa}$—almost certainly a unit conversion typo for $10.40\text{ kPa}$ or $104\text{ kPa}$ (since $\mu_{\text{tumor}} = 10.4\text{ kPa}$).
