### 1. Executive Summary
This manuscript reports a five-laboratory international ring trial across three continents evaluating the inter-laboratory reproducibility of synthetic microbial community (SynCom) assembly, rhizosphere exometabolite profiles, and host plant phenotypes using the model grass *Brachypodium distachyon* Bd21-3 grown in sterile EcoFAB 2.0 devices. Each participating laboratory ($n = 5$, Labs A–E) tested four treatments ($n = 7$ biological replicates per treatment per lab: axenic plant control, 16-member SynCom, 17-member SynCom containing the dominant colonizer *Paraburkholderia* sp. OAS925, and cell-free medium control). Standardized consumables, seeds, cryopreserved SynCom inocula, environmental data loggers, and video-annotated protocols were centrally distributed, and harvested root/media samples were returned to the organizing facility for centralized 16S rRNA V4 amplicon sequencing and HILIC LC–MS/MS targeted (60 metabolites) and untargeted (833 features) metabolomics. In addition, the authors performed comparative genomics across the 17 isolate genomes, fluorescent microscopy of RFP-expressing *Paraburkholderia* sp. OAS925, crystal violet biofilm/growth assays, and pH-dependent soft-agar motility assays. The study is an exemplary model of open, standardized, and reproducible microbiome science, demonstrating 99% sterility retention (208/210 tests) and highly consistent *Paraburkholderia* dominance ($98 \pm 0.03\%$ root relative abundance) across laboratories. A few statistical modeling and reporting refinements are recommended.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unblocked Pooling in Cross-Laboratory Plant Biomass ANOVA (Fig 2b):** In Figure 2b, plant biomass data from all five laboratories are pooled and analyzed via a one-way ANOVA with Tukey's test, reporting "$n = 7$" in the caption (whereas the pooled plot contains $5 \times 7 = 35$ biological replicates per treatment across the 5 laboratories). Because S1 Fig and S2 Fig demonstrate substantial inter-laboratory differences in baseline plant growth driven by chamber light sources (LED vs. fluorescent), temperature profiles, and photoperiod (Lab C used a 16 h photoperiod vs. 14 h in Labs A, B, D, and E), analyzing the combined dataset with a simple one-way ANOVA ignores the hierarchical blocking structure of the ring trial. A two-way ANOVA (`Biomass ~ Treatment * Lab`) or linear mixed-effects model with Laboratory as a random intercept (`Biomass ~ Treatment + (1|Lab)`), along with reporting the exact pooled sample size ($N = 35$ per group across 5 labs of $n = 7$), should be used for Figure 2b.
2. **Compositional Closure in Two-Way ANOVA on 16S Relative Abundances (Section 2.6, S6 Fig):** To partition variance between laboratory and treatment effects ($\text{SS}_{\text{effect}} / \text{SS}_{\text{total}}$), the authors applied standard two-way ANOVA directly to untransformed 16S relative abundance proportions. Because *Paraburkholderia* sp. OAS925 is absent by design in SynCom16 (0%) and comprises ~98% of reads in SynCom17, the remaining 16 taxa are mathematically compressed into the remaining 2% of the simplex in SynCom17. Applying standard Gaussian ANOVA to raw proportions across SynCom16 vs. SynCom17 conflates true biological suppression with compositional closure and violates homoscedasticity. Performing centered log-ratio (CLR) transformation (on the 16 shared members) or complementing relative abundances with total bacterial load estimates would strengthen the variance partitioning analysis.
3. **Heterogeneous Root Segmentation Pipelines and Internal Text Discrepancy (Fig 2c vs. Section 4.3):** Due to condensation and reflection artifacts in Lab A's flatbed scans, Labs B–E were analyzed with automated RhizoNet segmentation while Lab A was analyzed with an alternative tool, and raw pixel counts were normalized to the within-lab maximum. Notably, there is a minor discrepancy between the Figure 2c caption (which states Lab A was analyzed using "ImageJ") and Materials and Methods Section 4.3 (which states "SmartRoot V4.21 was used instead"). This should be reconciled, and the limitation that within-lab maximum normalization precludes evaluating inter-laboratory variance in absolute root architecture should be noted.
4. **Centralized vs. Decentralized Analytical Variance:** All 16S amplicon library preparation/sequencing and LC–MS/MS metabolomics were performed at a single organizing laboratory (LBNL). While this is a sound design choice to isolate experimental/cultivation variation across the five laboratories, the reported inter-laboratory variance (<18% for 16S microbiome structure) therefore reflects biological execution and shipping variance rather than end-to-end analytical reproducibility (e.g., DNA extraction, PCR, sequencing, and MS platform differences across facilities). The Discussion notes this distinction well, though it is worth emphasizing in the abstract/summary metrics.
5. **Minor Typographical Error in Sequencing Recipe (Section 4.6):** Line 155 states that MiSeq V3 (600-cycle) sequencing followed a "2 × 00 indexed run recipe" (missing the "3" in $2 \times 300$).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: EcoFAB 2.0 devices and standardized protocols enable reproducibly sterile plant-microbiome experiments across geographically dispersed laboratories.** `[Well Supported]` Across 210 sterility tests at 0 and 22 DAI in 5 laboratories, only 2 plates showed colonies (one attributable to a cracked Petri dish lid and the other showing 0% unknown reads in 16S sequencing of the corresponding EcoFAB), providing strong empirical validation of gnotobiotic integrity.
- **Claim 2: *Paraburkholderia* sp. OAS925 reproducibly dominates the *B. distachyon* root microbiome in SynCom17, reduces plant biomass/root growth, and restructures the rhizosphere exometabolome across all five laboratories.** `[Well Supported]` The dominance of *Paraburkholderia* ($98 \pm 0.03\%$) and the resulting clustering of both targeted (60 metabolites) and untargeted (833 features) rhizosphere metabolomes by treatment rather than laboratory are robust across all five participating sites.
- **Claim 3: Dominance of *Paraburkholderia* sp. OAS925 is linked to fast growth, rapid swimming motility under acidic rhizosphere conditions (pH 5.5–6.0), and unique genomic repertoire for acid resistance and Type III secretion.** `[Moderately Supported]` The comparative genomics (unique T3SS and glutamate/arginine acid-resistance transporters/decarboxylases), in vitro pH motility assays (pH 4–9), RFP root colonization imaging, and observed depletion of rhizosphere amino acids in SynCom17 form a highly coherent mechanistic model. As the authors appropriately acknowledge in the Discussion, genetic knockout validation (e.g., $\Delta\text{T3SS}$ or acid-resistance mutants) or mono-association controls will be needed in future work to establish causal necessity.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
Reproducibility and FAIR data compliance are exemplary and set a benchmark for the field:
- **Protocols & Consumables:** Full step-by-step protocol with embedded video annotations and exact vendor part numbers is deposited on protocols.io (`10.17504/protocols.io.kxygxyydkl8j/v1`), and the 17 bacterial strains are publicly available via DSMZ (collection `DSM 200000SY`).
- **Sequencing & Genomes:** 16S rRNA amplicon data are deposited in NCBI SRA under BioProject `PRJNA1151037`. All 17 isolate genomes are available with explicit IMG/M Taxon IDs, GOLD Project IDs (`Gp0588949`–`Gp0589682`, `Gp0440934`), and a Hugging Face repository (`10.57967/hf/5885`).
- **Metabolomics & Phenomics:** Raw LC–MS/MS `.raw` and `.mzML` files are deposited in MassIVE (`MSV000095476`) and GNPS2 (`task=2ccbf82840724c99a2acc2c9e512a302`). All raw phenotype tables, HOBO logger traces, root scans, RhizoNet reports, agar sterility photos, and Python/R analysis scripts are archived on Figshare (`10.6084/m9.figshare.c.7373842` and `10.6084/m9.figshare.29700029`) and indexed via the NMDC portal (`nmdc:sty-11-ev70y104`).
