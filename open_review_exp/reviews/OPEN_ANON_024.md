### 1. Executive Summary
This study applies Maximum Path Likelihood (MPL)—a diffusion-limit Wright-Fisher inference framework that accounts for genetic linkage (via the time-integrated allele frequency covariance matrix $C_{\text{int}}$), asymmetric mutational flux, and pairwise epistasis—to longitudinal intra-host HIV-1 and SHIV envelope (Env) sequence datasets from 2 human donors who developed broadly neutralizing antibodies (bnAbs; CH505 and CH848) and 13 rhesus macaques (RMs; 7 infected with SHIV.CH505 and 6 with SHIV.CH848) from Roark et al. (2021). The authors demonstrate that: (1) strongly selected early mutations predominantly target autologous strain-specific antibody (ssAb) escape, N-linked glycosylation shifts, viral-load-enhancing sites, and consensus reversions, whereas subsequent bnAb escape mutations experience weaker positive selection; (2) Env fitness landscapes inferred independently from humans and RMs sharing the same transmitted/founder (TF) Env are remarkably concordant ($r = 0.96$ for CH505, $r = 0.95$ for CH848, and $r = 0.84 / 0.74$ on site-shuffled sequences); and (3) rapid early viral population fitness gains precede and distinguish RMs that develop broad neutralizing responses from those that do not. The mathematical formulation is rigorous and well-validated with epistatic and bootstrap extensions, though the small host sample size for the bnAb forecasting claim and minor typographical errors in the Methods equations warrant minor revision.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Small Host Sample Size ($N=13$ RMs, 4 with bnAbs) and Absence of Formal Hypothesis Testing for the Fitness-Gain Signature (Figure 6):** The central translational claim that "Evolutionary dynamics forecast antibody breadth" (Figure 6) is based on 7 RMs infected with SHIV.CH505 (2 with bnAbs, 1 intermediate outlier `RM6072`, 4 without bnAbs) and 6 RMs infected with SHIV.CH848 (2 with bnAbs, 4 without bnAbs). While the visual separation of mean fitness trajectories in Figure 6A–B is striking, no formal longitudinal statistical test (e.g., a permutation test on the area under the fitness gain curve or initial slope prior to week 14/16 across the 4 bnAb+ vs. 8–9 bnAb- macaques) is reported. Moreover, in Figure 6, the fitness of sequences within each RM is evaluated using the *joint* RM selection coefficients $\hat{s}$ inferred across the RMs in that cohort (Eq. 20). If RMs that develop bnAbs have higher viral loads, deeper sampling, or longer persistence of intermediate polymorphic sweeps, their covariance matrices $C_{\text{int}}^{(r)}$ and net frequency changes $\Delta x_{\text{int}}^{(r)}$ could disproportionately weight the joint estimator $\hat{s}$ in Eq. (20). Evaluating each RM's fitness trajectory using leave-one-host-out selection coefficients (or purely human-derived CH505/CH848 selection coefficients) would eliminate any in-sample circularity in Figure 6.
2. **Confounding of Cross-Species Landscape Correlation ($r = 0.96, 0.95$) by Shared Consensus Reversions and Invariant Sites:** In Figure 5, the Pearson correlation between sequence fitness $F(g)$ evaluated under human HIV-1 and macaque SHIV selection coefficients is $r = 0.96$ and $0.95$ ($r = 0.84$ and $0.74$ on column-shuffled sequences). However, sequence fitness $F(g) = 1 + \sum_i s_i(g_i)$ sums over all mutated sites in a sequence, and as shown in Eq. (21) ($r = \frac{s^\top C h}{\sqrt{s^\top C s \, h^\top C h}}$), this correlation is heavily dominated by the handful of high-frequency, high-effect consensus reversions and shared glycan-shift mutations (such as `N334S`, `K302N`, `T234N`) that sweep in both species, whereas the majority of mutations have $s_i(a) \approx 0$ due to $L_2$ ridge regularization ($\gamma = 10$). Reporting the direct mutation-level correlation between the vectors $s_{\text{human}}$ and $s_{\text{SHIV}}$ (both across all observed mutations and restricted to non-reversion mutations) alongside sequence-level $F(g)$ correlations would provide a more transparent picture of landscape similarity.
3. **Time-Invariant Selection Assumption ($\hat{s}$) in a Coevolutionary Arms Race:** MPL assumes a static selection coefficient $s_i(a)$ over the entire multi-year infection trajectory (Eq. 15–16). In reality, an immune escape mutation (e.g., against an autologous nAb or Cooperating lineage CH235/DH272) experiences zero or negative selection prior to antibody emergence, intense positive selection during antibody maturation, and neutral drift once fixed. Because $\hat{s} = (C_{\text{int}} + \gamma I)^{-1}[\Delta x_{\text{int}} - \Delta u_{\text{int}}]$ divides the net frequency change by the integrated variance $\int \Delta t \, x(1-x)$, a mutation that sweeps rapidly early and remains fixed has a small $C_{ii,\text{int}}$ and thus a large inferred $\hat{s}$, whereas a bnAb escape mutation that fluctuates or remains polymorphic over a longer late window accumulates a larger $C_{ii,\text{int}}$, potentially attenuating its time-averaged $\hat{s}$. While the authors acknowledge static $\hat{s}$ in the Discussion, a sliding-window or epoch-split MPL check (e.g., $<6$ months vs. $>6$ months) would strengthen the comparison between ssAb and bnAb selection strengths.
4. **Mathematical Typographical Errors in the Methods Section:**
   - **Equation (5):** The multinomial prefactor $N!$ from Eq. (2) is omitted in Eq. (5), and the product index is written as $\prod_{a=1}^M$ instead of $\prod_{\alpha=1}^M$.
   - **Equation (11):** The projection onto allele frequencies is written as $x_i(a) = \sum_a g_{i,a}^\alpha z_\alpha$, where the summation index is erroneously written as $a$ instead of genotype index $\alpha$ ($\sum_{\alpha=1}^M g_{i,a}^\alpha z_\alpha$).
   - **Equation (20):** The mutational flux term is written as $\Delta a \bar{\mu}_{\text{int}}$ instead of $\Delta \bar{u}_{\text{int}}$.
   - **Equation (23):** In the second line (for $d_{ij}(a,b)$), the inner summation upper limit over alleles $c$ is written as $\sum_{c=1}^L$ instead of $\sum_{c=1}^q$.
   - **Equation (25):** The pairwise mutational flux $u_{ij}(a,b)$ contains an unbound index $d$ inside a sum over $c$ ($\mu_{ac} x_{ij}(c,d) - [\mu_{cd} + \mu_{ca}]x_{ij}(a,b)$), which should be $\mu_{ac} x_{ij}(c,b)$ and $\mu_{cb} + \mu_{ca}$.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Beneficial HIV-1 and SHIV mutations are strongly enriched for nonsynonymous changes in variable loops, consensus reversions, and N-linked glycosylation motif shifts.** `[Well Supported]` — Backed by exact Fisher's tests showing 14- to 20-fold enrichment for reversions and >90- to 190-fold enrichment for simultaneous glycan shifts (`p < 10^{-4}` to `10^{-11}`), and validated against known experimental phenotypes (e.g., recovering 5 of 6 viral-load-enhancing SHIV.CH505 mutations from Bauer et al., 2023 in the top 6 inferred mutations).
- **Claim 2: Inferred Env fitness landscapes in SHIV-infected rhesus macaques closely mirror those of HIV-1 in human donors with the same founder Env.** `[Well Supported]` — Supported by cross-species sequence fitness evaluations ($r = 0.96, 0.95$), shuffled-sequence controls ($r = 0.84, 0.74$), and negative cross-strain controls (CH505 vs. CH848 showing near-zero correlation).
- **Claim 3: Rapid early viral fitness gains precede and distinguish hosts that develop broadly neutralizing antibodies.** `[Moderately Supported]` — The pattern is clear and biologically compelling in the 13 RMs examined, and the analysis of `RM6072` (which developed a CD4bs lineage similar to CH505 that stalled at the T234N glycan barrier) adds strong mechanistic nuance. However, given $n=2$ bnAb+ RMs per SHIV construct, generalizability to broader cohorts remains to be prospectively validated.
- **Claim 4: Inferred selection coefficients are robust to pairwise epistasis and finite sampling noise.** `[Well Supported]` — Directly demonstrated via factorized epistatic MPL inference ($\mathcal{O}((qL)^2)$) and bootstrap resampling across time points ($r \approx 0.85\text{–}0.95$).
