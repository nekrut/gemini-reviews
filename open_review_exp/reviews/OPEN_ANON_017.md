### 1. Executive Summary
This 30-day dairy cattle manure composting study investigates the effects of adding cellulase ($50\text{ g}$, Group B) or xylanase ($50\text{ g}$, Group C) alongside a compound microbial agent ($100\text{ g}$, Group A control) in $0.7\text{ m}^3$ plastic containers. Across five sampling time points (days 0, 3, 10, 20, and 30), the authors measure physicochemical properties (temperature, pH, OM, TN, AN, GI, HA, FA), four enzymatic activities (CA, UA, ALP, DHA), bacterial 16S rRNA profiles (V3–V4 region), and 285 antibiotic resistance genes (ARGs) plus 10 mobile genetic elements (MGEs) via SmartChip high-throughput qPCR. While combining multi-time-point physicochemical profiling, 16S sequencing, and HT-qPCR resistome tracking provides a broad view of composting dynamics, the manuscript suffers from unclear biological replication of the composting reactors, inappropriate use of ordinary (non-repeated-measures) two-way ANOVA on longitudinal data, arithmetic and unit errors in Table 1, contradictory statements within the Results and between Results and Discussion, and omission of the HT-qPCR normalization formula.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unclear Biological Replication of Composting Containers and Longitudinal Pseudoreplication:** The Materials and Methods (lines 56–70) describe setting up three groups (A, B, C) in $0.7\text{ m}^3$ plastic containers and collecting samples at days 0, 3, 10, 20, and 30 using a five-point sampling and quartering method, but never state how many independent replicate composting containers were constructed per treatment group ($n$ containers per group). If only one container per treatment was run and subsampled (or assayed in technical triplicates), treating subsamples as independent biological replicates in ANOVA constitutes pseudoreplication. Furthermore, applying "Ordinary two-way ANOVA" (Figures 1–3 captions) to longitudinal measurements collected repeatedly from the same composting containers over 30 days violates independence of error terms; a repeated-measures ANOVA or linear mixed-effects model is required.
2. **Arithmetic and Unit Errors in Table 1:**
   - In Table 1, the column header for the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio is labeled `C/N (g/kg)`; because both Carbon and Nitrogen are in $\text{g/kg}$, `C/N` is a dimensionless ratio.
   - For `sawdust`, Carbon is reported as $593.2\text{ g/kg}$ and Nitrogen as $2.6\text{ g/kg}$. The ratio $593.2 / 2.6$ is $228.15$, not `288.2` as reported in Table 1 (a digit-transposition error).
3. **Internal Contradictions in the Results and Between Results and Abstract/Discussion:**
   - *Dehydrogenase activity (DHA), Line 115:* Two consecutive sentences directly contradict each other regarding Group C: "As for DHA, there was little change in group A and C in the early 10 days, followed a significant decrease in 10–30 day (Fig 2F). The DHA of group C rose abruptly to peak value from day 3 to day 10, and then gradually decreased (Fig 2F)." (The first sentence appears to have mislabeled Group B as Group C).
   - *Organic matter (OM) degradation and temperature:* The Abstract (line 21) and Discussion (line 199) claim that "Cellulase notably accelerated the degradation of organic matter... by promoting a rapid temperature rise," whereas the Results (line 107) report that Group C (xylanase) reached the highest maximum temperature ($\sim 60^\circ\text{C}$ at day 7 vs. $\sim 50^\circ\text{C}$ in Group B) and that Group C (not Group B) had significantly lower OM than Group A at days 3 and 30 (Fig 1C).
   - *ARG abundance at day 30:* The Abstract and Conclusion claim that enzyme addition reduces ARG abundance and mitigates resistance gene spread, whereas Results line 143 and Fig 5B show that total ARG abundance in both Group B and Group C *increased* during the maturation stage (days 20–30).
4. **Missing HT-qPCR Quantification Formula and Normalization Units (Line 87 & Fig 5):** While the SmartChip HT-qPCR $C_T$ cutoff ($31$) and amplification efficiency criteria ($1.8\text{--}2.2$) are stated, the manuscript does not specify how "total abundances of ARGs" and "total abundances of MGEs" in Figure 5B and 5D were calculated (e.g., relative copy number normalized to 16S rRNA via $10^{(31 - C_T)/(10/3)}$ or $2^{-\Delta C_T}$, vs. absolute copies per gram of dry compost).
5. **Contradictory Taxonomic Resolution and Abundance Input in SparCC Network Analysis (Line 97 & Table 3):** Line 97 states: "Briefly, absolute abundance data were used to analyze correlation at genus level within group. Relative abundances of the 200 most abundant species were selected." First, these two sentences contradict each other on both input scale (absolute vs. relative abundance; note that SparCC is specifically designed for compositional count/relative abundance data) and taxonomic rank (genus vs. species; short-read V3–V4 16S amplicons cannot reliably resolve 200 species). Second, constructing a 200-taxon co-occurrence network per group by pooling only 5 longitudinal time points (days 0, 3, 10, 20, 30) confounds shared temporal trajectories across heating/cooling phases with ecological interactions. Third, in Table 3, $2E / V$ for Groups A, B, and C equals $2(875)/173 = 10.116$, $2(927)/179 = 10.358$, and $2(1077)/187 = 11.519$, which differ from the reported `Ave number of neighbors` ($10.222, 10.500, 11.802$), indicating isolated nodes or multi-edges across the 2–3 connected components that are not explained.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Enzyme supplementation (cellulase and xylanase) alters temperature profiles, lowers day-30 pH, increases total/available nitrogen (in xylanase treatment), and increases bacterial Shannon diversity and Pielou evenness at day 30.** `[Moderately Supported]` — Supported by the time-series measurements in Figures 1–3, subject to clarification of biological container replication.
- **Claim 2: Cellulase and xylanase reduce total ARG abundance and mitigate ARG dissemination by the end of the 30-day composting cycle.** `[Overstated]` — While ARG *diversity* (number of detected subtypes, Fig 5A) decreased by $7.2\%\text{--}39.7\%$ particularly at day 20 in Group B, total ARG *abundance* (Fig 5B) rebounded between days 10 and 30 in both enzyme-treated groups, as acknowledged in Discussion line 203 but omitted from the Abstract and Conclusion.
- **Claim 3: Variation partitioning analysis (VPA) shows bacterial community ($28.21\%$), physicochemical properties ($11.86\%$), and their joint effect ($40.24\%$) explain $80.32\%$ of ARG/MGE variation.** `[Moderately Supported]` — Standard descriptive multivariate ordination result, though high explained variance is expected when fitting multiple explanatory variables to a small number of time-point samples.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw 16S rRNA sequencing reads are deposited in NCBI BioProject under accession `PRJNA1246353`, and Supporting Information files contain the 296 HT-qPCR primer pairs and figure numerical data.
- **Protocol Completeness:** Raw material properties, container dimensions, turning schedule, assay kit catalog numbers (`BC0155`, `BC0125`, `BC0280`, `BC0395`), and 16S PCR cycling parameters are clearly stated. However, the exact number of biological replicate composting containers per group and the HT-qPCR relative/absolute copy-number calculation formula must be explicitly documented.
