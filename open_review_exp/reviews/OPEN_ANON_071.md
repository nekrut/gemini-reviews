### 1. Executive Summary
This cross-sectional observational study investigates the association between gut microbiota composition (assessed via V3–V4 16S rRNA gene sequencing processed with QIIME2/DADA2) and brain amyloid-$\beta$ status (determined by $^{18}\text{F}$-Flutemetamol PET visual read and validated against SUVR $\ge 0.58$, $\kappa = 0.792$) in 136 community-dwelling older adults in Tokyo, Japan (47 PET-positive [34.6%] and 89 PET-negative [65.4%]). While the clinical phenotyping via amyloid PET in a community cohort is valuable, the statistical and bioinformatic analysis is severely undermined by arbitrary median dichotomization of compositional microbiome data, refusal to apply multiple testing correction across the six tested bacterial phyla for a borderline nominal result ($p = 0.047$ by $\chi^2$; $p = 0.043$ by logistic regression), omission of critical measured and unmeasured confounders (education, *APOE* $\varepsilon 4$ genotype, BMI, diet), and completely null findings across all $\alpha$-diversity, $\beta$-diversity, and FDR-corrected genus-level analyses.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Arbitrary Median Dichotomization of Compositional Microbiome Data:** Rather than modeling relative abundances using modern compositional data analysis (CoDA) frameworks (e.g., centered log-ratio [CLR] transformation, ANCOM-BC, ALDEx2, or MaAsLin2), the authors dichotomized every phylum and genus into binary "above-median" vs. "below-median" categories prior to running $\chi^2$ tests and binomial logistic regression. Dichotomizing continuous/compositional proportions at the sample median discards substantial statistical information, fails to address the constant-sum simplex constraint of 16S sequencing data, and introduces extreme sensitivity to small shifts around the median (where the group mean relative abundance of Firmicutes differed only modestly: 52.6% in PET-positive vs. 56.8% in PET-negative).
2. **Unadjusted Multiple Testing at the Phylum Level and Fragile Borderline Significance:** The entire headline finding of the paper rests on a single borderline association for the phylum Firmicutes ($\chi^2$ $p = 0.047$; adjusted OR = 2.15, 95% CI [1.03, 4.52], $p = 0.043$ in Table 2). Six phyla were tested simultaneously in Table 2 (Actinobacteria, Bacteroidota, Firmicutes, Proteobacteria, Verrucomicrobiota, and Unclassified), yet the authors declined to apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR or Bonferroni correction at the phylum level on the post-hoc rationale that Firmicutes was their primary hypothesis. Under any standard correction across the six phyla (e.g., BH-adjusted $q = 0.258$), the Firmicutes association is non-significant.
3. **Complete Null Signal Across Finer Taxonomic Resolutions and Diversity Metrics:** At the genus level (Table 3), zero genera survived Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction. Similarly, $\alpha$-diversity (Shannon Index $p = 0.915$, Simpson Index $p = 0.557$, Observed OTUs $p = 0.391$) and $\beta$-diversity (unweighted UniFrac PERMANOVA $p = 0.661$) showed no differences between PET-positive and PET-negative groups.
4. **Omission of Measured and Unmeasured Confounders in Regression Models:** Table 1 shows statistically significant baseline differences between PET-positive and PET-negative participants in education ($12.2 \pm 2.3$ vs. $13.4 \pm 2.5$ years, $p = 0.006$), MMSE score ($27.7 \pm 2.1$ vs. $28.6 \pm 1.7$, $p = 0.012$), and CDR $\ge 0.5$ ($38.3\%$ vs. $20.2\%$, $p = 0.023$). However, the logistic regression models in Tables 2 and 3 adjusted only for age, sex, and antibiotic use within 2 weeks. Education (a strong proxy for socioeconomic status and dietary habits) was excluded from the multivariable adjustment. Furthermore, the prevalence of antibiotic use within 2 weeks is not reported in Table 1 (why were recent antibiotic users not excluded outright?), body mass index (BMI) and diet were not recorded or controlled for, and *APOE* $\varepsilon 4$ status—the single strongest determinant of amyloid PET positivity—was not measured.
5. **Internal Degrees-of-Freedom Inconsistency in $\alpha$-Diversity Reporting:** In the Results section ("Comparison of gut microbiota diversity"), the independent-samples $t$-test for the Shannon Index is reported as $t = -0.107, \text{df} = 125, p = 0.915$, despite Levene's test indicating equal variances ($F = 1.944, p = 0.166$). For $N = 136$ total participants (47 PET-positive, 89 PET-negative), an equal-variance $t$-test must have $\text{df} = 134$. A value of $\text{df} = 125$ implies either 9 unreported missing samples in the diversity analysis or an unstated Welch correction/data mismatch.
6. **Temporal Lag in Cognitive Testing:** Cognitive assessments (MMSE-J and CDR) were performed an average of $117 \pm 36.7$ days prior to the PET scan and stool collection, introducing potential misclassification of baseline cognitive status.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** "Lower Firmicutes abundance was significantly associated with an increased odds of PET positivity (OR: 2.15 [1.03, 4.52])." — `[Fragile]` / `[Overstated]`. This association is nominal ($p = 0.043$), depends on median dichotomization, does not survive correction for the 6 phyla tested, and does not control for *APOE* genotype, BMI, diet, or education.
- **Claim 2:** "The observed phylum-level association may not be driven by a single genus with a strong effect, but rather by broader compositional features within the Firmicutes phylum." — `[Overstated]`. Within Table 3, nominal genus-level effects inside Firmicutes point in opposite directions (e.g., *Clostridium* below-median has OR = 2.34, $p = 0.026$, whereas *Dorea* below-median has OR = 0.47, $p = 0.044$), and overall community structure (PERMANOVA $p = 0.661$) shows no compositional separation.
- **Claim 3:** Speculative mechanistic pathways linking Firmicutes reduction to systemic inflammation, insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and SCFA depletion driving cerebral amyloid-$\beta$ accumulation. — `[Overstated]`. The cohort excluded individuals with diabetes (only 3 PET-negative and 0 PET-positive participants had a history of diabetes in Table 1, $p = 0.551$), and neither fecal/serum SCFAs nor systemic inflammatory markers were measured.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Sequencing Data:** Raw 16S rRNA V3–V4 sequencing data are deposited in the DDBJ Sequence Read Archive (DRA) under BioProject accession number `PRJDB37280` (`DRA022037`), with primer sequences (`341F`/`806R`), extraction kits (NucleoSpin 96 Soil), and QIIME2 version (`2020.2` with `silva-138-99-nb-classifier.qza`) clearly documented.
- **Clinical & Analytic Reproducibility:** Individual-level clinical metadata (PET status, SUVR values, age, sex, antibiotic usage) are restricted due to ethics committee policy, and no analysis scripts (R/SPSS) or ASV count tables are provided in the supplementary materials. Furthermore, Table 3 is truncated in the main text without reporting the exact number of total genera tested after filtering out taxa with median zero abundance, although the Benjamini–Hochberg FDR threshold column (e.g., $p = 0.026$ corresponding to an FDR threshold of $0.001$, and $p = 0.943$ corresponding to $0.049$) allows back-calculating that approximately 50 genera were tested.
