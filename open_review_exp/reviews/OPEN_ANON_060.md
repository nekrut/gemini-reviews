### 1. Executive Summary
This study reports the isolation of *Streptomyces rochei* strain FE-3-1 from rice rhizosphere soil, evaluates its in vitro antagonism against the rice blast fungus *Pyricularia oryzae* in a dual-culture plate bioassay (reporting a 73.33% colony radius inhibition rate), and presents its complete genome sequence (`8,247,561 bp`, 72.51% GC, 7,158 predicted CDSs) generated via hybrid PacBio and Illumina sequencing. The authors perform functional annotation (`COG`, `GO`, `KEGG`, `Prokka`), predict 31 secondary metabolite biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) using `antiSMASH 7.0`, screen annotations for putative heavy metal resistance and plant growth-promoting (PGP) traits, and conduct comparative/pan-genome analyses against 8 reference *Streptomyces* genomes.

While the underlying genome assembly of *S. rochei* FE-3-1 and the dual-culture antagonism assay provide a useful genomic resource, the manuscript suffers from severe bioinformatic reporting contradictions, fundamental biochemical misinterpretations of universal housekeeping pathways, and unsupported causal claims: (1) the Methods claim that PacBio + Illumina hybrid reads were assembled into a complete genome using `SOAPdenovo v1.05` (an obsolete short-read-only assembler incapable of processing PacBio long reads or closing linear *Streptomyces* chromosomes); (2) universal bacterial housekeeping genes (`ppa`, `maf`, `trpC`, `ftsH`, `htpX`) and the assimilatory sulfate reduction pathway for L-cysteine biosynthesis (`cysNDCH`, `sirA`) are misclassified as specialized "plant growth-promoting", "heavy metal resistance", and "soil $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ heavy-metal precipitation" traits; (3) the Abstract and Discussion claim that FE-3-1 *metabolites* were tested against *P. oryzae*, whereas only live whole-cell dual-culture streaks were performed; and (4) the anti-*P. oryzae* activity is attributed to Cluster 23 (`arylpolyene` / lipopeptide 8D1-1) without experimental evidence while ignoring the 95%-identical **candicidin** polyene antifungal cluster (Cluster 4).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Incompatible Genome Assembly Pipeline Description (Lines 48, 98, Table 1):**
   - In Section 2.4 (Line 48), the authors state: *"The whole genome sequence of the strain FE-3-1 was obtained through Pacbio and Illumina Hiseq×10 platforms... Genomic DNA was randomly fragmented using Covaris or Bioruptor method, followed by sequencing adaptor ligation according to the manufacturer's instructions for Illumina Paired-End sequencing library preparation... The resulting reads were de novo assembled via SOAPdenovo v1.05."*
   - In Section 3.3 (Line 98) and Table 1, however, the sequencing dataset is described as *"341255 reads with an average length of 4546.05 bp"* (which are long PacBio reads totaling 1.55 Gb, ~188$\times$ coverage) assembled into a single contiguous `8,247,561 bp` genome map (Fig. 4). `SOAPdenovo v1.05` (released in 2010) is a short-read De Bruijn graph assembler that **cannot** take 4.5 kb PacBio reads as input and cannot assemble a high-GC (72.51%), BGC-rich *Streptomyces* genome into a single complete chromosome. The authors must report the actual long-read/hybrid assembler (e.g., `Canu`, `HGAP`, `Flye`, or `Unicycler` + `Pilon`) used by Shanghai Majorbio.
   - Note also that *Streptomyces* chromosomes are **linear** chromosomes with terminal inverted repeats, not circular chromosomes, although depicting them as circular plots (Fig. 4) is a common visualization convention that should be clarified.
2. **Misclassification of Universal Bacterial Housekeeping Genes as Specialized Plant-Beneficial and Metal-Resistance Traits (Tables 2, 3, and 5; Lines 150–207, 308–340):**
   - **Assimilatory Sulfate Reduction Misinterpreted as Extracellular $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ Metal Precipitation (Table 5, Figs. 12–13, Line 308):** In the Discussion (Line 308), the authors claim: *"The strain can produce hydrogen sulfide through sulfur metabolism pathway (Table 5, Fig 12), which can combine with free heavy metal ions in soil to form residual sulfide, increase plant stress resistance and reduce the absorption and transport of heavy metals by plants."* However, the genes listed in Table 5 and Fig. 12b (`cysN`, `cysD`, `cysC`, `cysH`, `sirA`) encode the canonical **assimilatory sulfate reduction pathway** present in nearly all aerobic bacteria to reduce sulfate into intracellular sulfide for incorporation into the amino acid L-cysteine (`cysK`/`cysM`). Assimilatory sulfate reduction is tightly feedback-regulated and does not excrete bulk $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ into the soil (which is performed by anaerobic dissimilatory sulfate-reducing bacteria via `sat`, `aprAB`, and `dsrAB`).
   - **Tryptophan Biosynthesis (`trpC`) Misinterpreted as Auxin Phytohormone Synthesis (Table 3, Line 195):** Table 3 lists `trpC` (*indole-3-glycerol phosphate synthase*) under the trait `"Phytohormone synthesis"`. `trpC` is an essential housekeeping enzyme in the universal L-tryptophan biosynthetic operon (`trpABCDEF`), not an indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) phytohormone biosynthesis gene (such as `iaaM`/`iaaH`, `ipdC`, or `nit`).
   - **Essential Housekeeping Enzymes Misclassified as Phosphate Solubilization and Biocatalysis (Tables 2 and 3):** Table 3 lists `ppa` (inorganic diphosphatase, essential for driving biosynthetic reactions in all living cells), `maf` (housekeeping dUTPase/septum-arrest nucleotide pyrophosphatase), `rnhA` (RNase H, which degrades RNA/DNA hybrids during replication), and `rsbU`/`prpC` (intracellular serine/threonine signaling protein phosphatases) as `"Phosphate solubilization"` genes! None of these cytoplasmic enzymes solubilize extracellular soil tricalcium/inorganic phosphate (unlike secreted `phoD` alkaline phosphatase or `gcd` gluconic acid dehydrogenase). Similarly, `ftsH` and `htpX` (membrane protein quality-control proteases conserved across bacteria) are listed both as heavy metal (Zinc) resistance proteins in Table 2 and as antifungal biocatalysts in Table 3.
3. **Contradiction Between Pan-Genome Methods (`Roary` vs. `OrthoMCL`) and Gene Family Counts (Lines 63, 284, 289):**
   - Section 2.7 (Line 63) states that pan-genome analysis of the 9 *Streptomyces* genomes was conducted using the `Roary Pan-genome Pipeline`, and Section 3.9 (Line 284) reports *"a total of 17,816 gene families were found in the 9 genomes, of which 2,920 genes constituted the core genome."*
   - Immediately afterward, Section 3.10 (Line 289) states that the amino acid sequences of the same 9 *Streptomyces* genomes were clustered using `OrthoMC` (OrthoMCL, $E\text{-value} = 10^{-5}$, Markov inflation $= 1.5$), reporting *"A total of 8962 orthologous gene clusters were identified, with 2920 gene clusters being core orthologous gene clusters."* The authors must clarify why two different pipelines (`Roary` and `OrthoMCL`) are described with different total pan-genome sizes (17,816 vs. 8,962) but the exact same core genome count (2,920).
4. **Inappropriate Statistical Test Reporting and Unsubstantiated Metabolite Claims (Lines 8, 38, 68, 242, 260–263):**
   - **Line 68:** Section 2.8 states: *"All data collected were statistically analyzed according to Duncan's multiple range test (P = 0.05)."* The only quantitative bioassay in the manuscript is a two-group comparison of *P. oryzae* colony radius with ($8.00 \pm 0.035\text{ mm}$) vs. without ($30.00 \pm 0.046\text{ mm}$) strain FE-3-1 ($n = 3$ replicates, Line 90). Duncan's multiple range test is a post-hoc test for $\ge 3$ treatment groups and is inapplicable to a 2-group comparison.
   - **Line 8 & Line 344:** The Abstract states *"its metabolites exhibited potent antagonistic activity against plant pathogen Pyricularia oryzae"*, but no cell-free culture filtrate or metabolite extract was ever tested—only live bacterial colonies streaked on PDA plates (Line 38).
   - **Lines 242 & 260–263:** In Table 4, Cluster 23 is classified by `antiSMASH 7.0` as an `arylpolyene` cluster (matching `BGC0001370` at 86%), yet in Section 3.7 (Line 262) the authors refer to it as *"lipopeptides 8D1-1 and 8D1-2 from the NRPS/PKS gene cluster"* and assert that it *"may play a major role in antagonizing Pyricularia oryzae"*, while overlooking Cluster 4, which shares 95% identity with the canonical antifungal polyene macrolide **candicidin** (`BGC0000034`), and Cluster 8, which shares 100% identity with the broad-spectrum antifungal/antibacterial **streptothricin** (`BGC0000432`).

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Strain FE-3-1 belongs to Streptomyces rochei and inhibits mycelial growth of Pyricularia oryzae in dual-culture plate assays.* `[Well Supported]` — Supported by 16S rRNA phylogeny, whole-genome ANI/AAI ($>95\text{--}96\%$) and Mauve synteny with *S. rochei* NRRL B-2410, and the dual-culture plate inhibition assay (Fig. 3; note that Line 172 erroneously cites Fig. 1 instead of Fig. 3 for antifungal activity).
- **Claim 2:** *The 8.25 Mb genome of S. rochei FE-3-1 encodes 31 secondary metabolite BGCs, including high-similarity clusters for candicidin (95%), streptothricin (100%), albaflavenone (100%), and desferrioxamine B/E (83%).* `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by `antiSMASH 7.0` and MIBiG homology mapping (Table 4, Fig. 8).
- **Claim 3:** *Lipopeptides 8D1-1 and 8D1-2 from Cluster 23 play a major role in antagonizing P. oryzae.* `[Unsupported]` — Speculative in silico attribution without transcriptomic, knockout, or metabolic/LC-MS evidence, especially given the presence of complete candicidin and streptothricin antifungal clusters in the same genome.
- **Claim 4:** *S. rochei FE-3-1 possesses specialized genes for phytohormone synthesis (`trpC`), extracellular phosphate solubilization (`ppa`, `maf`, `rnhA`), and heavy-metal precipitation via $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ production (`cysNDCH`, `sirA`).* `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Rests on misinterpreting universal bacterial housekeeping genes and assimilatory L-cysteine biosynthesis as specialized rhizosphere PGP/bioremediation pathways, with zero wet-lab assays for phosphate/potassium solubilization, IAA production, siderophore CAS activity, or heavy metal minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs).

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** The whole-genome sequencing data and assembly are publicly deposited in NCBI under BioProject `PRJNA1098797`, BioSample `SAMN40923439`, and SRA `SRR28714279`.
- **Methodological Corrections Needed:** The authors must replace the inaccurate `SOAPdenovo v1.05` assembly description with the actual PacBio/Illumina hybrid assembly pipeline and software versions used, reconcile the `Roary` vs. `OrthoMCL` pan-genome methods, and remove housekeeping genes (`ppa`, `maf`, `rnhA`, `trpC`, `ftsH`, `cysNDCH`) from the specialized PGP/bioremediation tables.
