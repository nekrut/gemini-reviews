### 1. Executive Summary
This study presents an eQTL and response-eQTL (reQTL) mapping effort in whole blood from the GEOCODE cohort, initially recruiting 406 healthy European individuals and analyzing 1,305 post-QC 3' QuantSeq RNA-seq samples across 359 subjects under four conditions: unstimulated control (n=354), LPS/TLR4 stimulation (n=327), R848/TLR7-8 stimulation (n=334), and anti-CD3/anti-CD28 TCR stimulation (n=290) after 24 hours of ex vivo culture. Using QTLtools (10,000 permutations, FDR $\le$ 0.05) and a custom $\theta$-metric colocalization/clustering pipeline, the authors identify 13,679 autosomal cis-eQTLs (6,496 eGenes), group them into 8,401 monogenic and 453 multigenic cis-regulatory modules (cRMs), and perform colocalization with GWAS summary statistics for IBD, RA, and COVID-19. While the cohort is well-standardized and the biological finding that `rs1801274` (*FCGR2A* R131H) drives a massive trans-regulatory and cytokine response continuum under ex vivo anti-CD3/CD28 stimulation via Fc$\gamma$RIIa engagement is compelling, the manuscript suffers from several methodological and statistical weaknesses: (1) defining reQTLs and mechanistic categories via binary FDR thresholding across unbalanced sample sizes (e.g., n=290 in TCR vs n=354 in CTRL) rather than formal genotype-by-environment ($G \times E$) interaction mixed models; (2) circular covariate optimization (selecting 29–38 expression PCs to maximize cis-eQTL discovery); (3) manual, subjective definition of genomic comparison windows for GWAS colocalization (`_a`, `_b` modified windows) coupled with unadjusted nominal p-value thresholds; and (4) pervasive terminology and internal reporting inconsistencies (e.g., repeatedly calling anti-CD3/anti-CD28 a "TCR antagonist" rather than an agonist, and conflicting percentages of reQTLs between the Results and Discussion).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Absence of Formal $G \times E$ Interaction Testing and Unbalanced Sample Size Confounding in cRM Classification:** The classification of eQTLs into 4-digit binary presence/absence modules (e.g., `0011`, `1011`) and the quantification of the three mechanistic scenarios (2.6% low expression, 72.8% loss of genotype effect, 16.5% module switching) rely on whether an eQTL crosses the FDR $\le$ 0.05 threshold within each condition separately, supplemented by a post-hoc check using $|\theta| \ge 0.6$ on an "extended catalogue." However, the final sample sizes differ substantially across conditions: Control has $n=354$, LPS has $n=327$, R848 has $n=334$, and TCR has only $n=290$ (an 18% reduction in sample size compared to Control). Because statistical power to detect an eQTL scales with sample size, the observation that "many eQTLs were specifically lost after TCR stimulation" (`1011` pattern) is directly confounded by lower statistical power in the TCR group ($n=290$) and by the massive variance introduced by `rs1801274` in TCR-stimulated samples. Rigorous reQTL identification requires a joint linear mixed model across all 1,305 samples with a donor random effect and an explicit Genotype $\times$ Condition interaction term.
2. **Subjective Manual Window Selection and Multiple Testing in $\theta$ Colocalization:** In the Methods ("Correlations EAP and DAP") and Table 1/2 footnotes, the authors state that for each GWAS locus, they "manually visualized the distribution of association $-\log(p)$ values to define the comparison window" and manually adapted windows for specific loci (denoted with suffixes `_a` and `_b`, such as `rs4845604-a`, `rs6738825-b`, `rs7282490_b`). Manually tuning the genomic window boundaries around a peak before computing the weighted correlation metric $\theta$ introduces researcher degrees of freedom that can artificially inflate $|\theta|$ by excluding flanking non-colocalizing background variants. Furthermore, DAP-EAP matches are declared significant using unadjusted empirical permutation p-values ($p \le 0.05$ for IBD; $p \le 0.01$ for RA and COVID-19) without false discovery rate (FDR) correction across the hundreds of locus-gene-condition pairs tested, nor benchmarking against standard Bayesian colocalization (`coloc`/`SuSiE`).
3. **Internal Numerical and Conceptual Contradictions:**
   - **Terminology Error ("TCR antagonist"):** Throughout the Abstract, Introduction, Discussion, and Methods, stimulation with anti-CD3 and anti-CD28 antibodies is repeatedly described as a "T-cell receptor (TCR) antagonist." Anti-CD3/anti-CD28 antibodies crosslink CD3 and provide co-stimulation to activate T cells (as evidenced by the induction of IFN$\gamma$ and IL-2 reported in S2 Fig); they are canonical TCR **agonists**, not antagonists.
   - **Conflicting Proportions of Response eQTLs:** In the Results (line 58), the authors report that 4,695 of 8,401 monogenic cRMs (55.8%) are not detected in resting conditions ("More than half of the cis-eQTLs..."), and on line 68 they state that stimulation-only modules account for "56% (4296/7723) of all cRMs" (leaving unexplained why the denominator changed from 8,401 to 7,723). Then, in the Discussion (lines 154 and 158), they state that "72.9% of eQTLs, referred to as response eQTLs, were revealed through in vitro stimulation" and "72.9% of eQTLs were not detected in resting conditions," apparently conflating the 72.8% estimate of "Mechanism 2" (line 66) with the overall percentage of response eQTLs.
   - **Mechanism Percentages Do Not Sum to 100%:** In the Results (line 66), the three mechanisms explaining why a cRM is active in condition $a$ but not $b$ are reported as 2.6% (Scenario 1: low expression), 72.8% (Scenario 2: loss of genotype effect), and 16.5% (Scenario 3: cRM switching). These sum to $2.6\% + 72.8\% + 16.5\% = 91.9\%$, leaving 8.1% of cases unaccounted for, even though the Methods (line 87) state that "The remaining activity patterns were attributed to the second mechanism."
   - **Table 1 Inconsistencies:** In Table 1, `PHETA1` (`Chr12_a`, `rs3184504`) is listed as a "Relevant reQTL-specific colocalisation" with $\theta = -0.76$ under TCR stimulation, but its p-value is listed as `(NS)` (non-significant), violating the table's inclusion criterion ($|\theta| > 0.6, p < 0.05$). Similarly, `CA8` (`Chr8`, `rs2875968`) lists `-0.49 (NA)` under TLR4. In the S9 Fig caption (line 120), the text erroneously states that association p-values were calculated for the expression of `AJ009632.2` when describing the `PLCL1` locus on Chromosome 2.
4. **DESeq2 Normalization Description vs. Implementation:** In the Methods (line 58), the authors state that "Differentially expressed genes were identified normalizing each pair of comparison (Stimulation vs Control condition) together," while PCA was run across all four conditions. It is unclear whether the expression matrices input to QTLtools were normalized per condition or jointly, and whether rank-based inverse normal transformation (quantile normalization) was applied to RNA-seq counts prior to linear regression in QTLtools, as was done for GWAS phenotypes.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: `rs1801274` (*FCGR2A*) drives inter-individual variation in whole-blood TCR stimulation response via Fc-receptor engagement.** `[Well Supported]` — The GWAS on PC1 of the TCR transcriptome ($p = 4 \times 10^{-45}$), backed by 313 trans-eGenes and strong associations with secreted IFN$\gamma$ ($p = 7.72 \times 10^{-40}$) and IL-2 ($p = 2.63 \times 10^{-15}$), is a robust and biologically insightful finding demonstrating how soluble murine anti-CD3/CD28 IgG antibodies engage Fc$\gamma$RIIa on myeloid/granulocytic cells in whole blood.
- **Claim 2: Immune cell proportions remain unchanged after 24 hours of in vitro LPS, R848, or anti-CD3/CD28 stimulation.** `[Fragile]` — The claim relies solely on in silico bulk RNA-seq deconvolution (`S1 Data`), which is notoriously unreliable when reference gene signatures themselves are massively perturbed by TLR/TCR cytokine storms over 24 hours, and when neutrophils undergo rapid ex vivo apoptosis/degranulation during 24h culture.
- **Claim 3: More than half of cis-eQTLs require immune stimulation to be detected, and these fall into three quantitative mechanistic classes.** `[Moderately Supported]` — While stimulation clearly uncovers inducible regulatory elements (e.g., *IL36G*, *CSF3*, *OAS1*), the exact proportions (55.8% vs 72.9%) and the dominance of "Mechanism 2" are inflated by binary thresholding across unequal sample sizes without formal interaction testing.
- **Claim 4: Opposite-sign colocalizations (e.g., *PLCL1* in CD vs. RA; *AJ009632.2* in Ctrl vs. TLR4) identify causal disease mechanisms.** `[Moderately Supported]` — These loci show compelling pattern correlations, but without fine-mapping (`SuSiE`/`coloc`) to rule out distinct causal variants in LD, opposite-direction effects must be interpreted cautiously.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Raw sequencing and genotype data are deposited under controlled access at EGA (`EGAD50000001320`), and a public web browser (`https://tools.giga.uliege.be/cedar/publiclw`) is provided for interactive visualization.
- **Code & Pipeline Completeness:** No repository containing the custom scripts for the $\theta$-metric colocalization, cRM graph clustering, or the permutation validation of Mechanism 2 is provided (the text only cites an external preprint DOI `10.1101/2024.10.14.24315443`).
- **Parameter & Metadata Gaps:** The exact concentrations ("IC50 dose") of LPS, R848, and anti-CD3/CD28 antibodies (as well as their clone IDs and isotypes, which are critical given the *FCGR2A* IgG subclass interaction) are omitted from the main Methods text and deferred to `S1 Data`. The exact number of expression PCs included per condition (stated only as a range of "29–38") should be explicitly tabulated alongside sample dropout reasons (from 406 recruited subjects / 1,574 RNA extractions / 1,407 sequenced libraries down to 1,305 post-QC samples across 359 subjects).
