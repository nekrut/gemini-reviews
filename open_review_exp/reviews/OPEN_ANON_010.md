### 1. Executive Summary
This study critically dissects a widely assumed paradigm in plant microbiome research: that the consistent drought-induced relative abundance enrichment (DE) of the genus *Streptomyces* in plant roots is a conserved lineage-level trait that predicts plant growth promotion (PGPR) under drought. Combining field sorghum (*Sorghum bicolor* RTx430) root endosphere profiling via both V3–V4 (Illumina) and full-length (PacBio CCS) 16S rRNA sequencing, untargeted LC–MS/MS exometabolomics and physiological phenotyping (sorbitol osmotic tolerance, CAS-LB siderophore assays) of 12 isolates sharing 100% V3–V4 identity with the dominant field ASV (`ASV650`), whole-genome sequencing and pangenomics of 48 field-derived *Streptomyces* endophytes (17 species, 21 strains; 17,557 orthogroups), and gnotobiotic microbox mono-association assays ($n = 4$ replicates $\times 48$ isolates), the authors demonstrate that: (1) `ASV650` masks at least five full-length 16S ASVs and dozens of genomically and metabolically divergent strains; (2) mono-association DE varies widely across the 48 isolates (from $-2.48$ to $+1.73$), lacks phylogenetic signal (Pagel's $\lambda = 7.3 \times 10^{-5}, P = 1$; Blomberg's $K = 2.6 \times 10^{-6}, P = 0.35$), and exhibits zero correlation with shoot biomass promotion or drought water retention ($\rho = -0.20\text{ to }0.16, P \ge 0.17$); and (3) orthogroup copy numbers of fatty acid biosynthesis reductase *FabG* (8 orthogroups) and osmolyte-proton symporter *ProP* (7 orthogroups) positively associate with DE. This is an exemplary, multi-modal investigation that pairs transparent reporting of null phylogenetic/PGPR correlations with deep genomic and metabolomic resolution. Only minor statistical clarifications regarding the pangenome association filtering criteria and microbox replication structure are needed.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Unstandardized Slope/SE Filtering and Multiple-Testing Correction in Pangenome PGLS Regressions (Results, Line 72; Methods, Line 41 of block 2):** To test associations between orthogroup copy number and DE across the 48 genomes, the authors ran phylogenetic generalized least squares (`nlme::gls` with `ape::corPagel`) on $n = 2,735$ orthogroups. In Methods Line 41 (block 2), the filtering criteria for the 342 retained clusters are described as: *"Finally, results were filtered for standard error <0.25, slope estimate >0.25, and normality of model residuals with Shapiro–Wilk test."* Three statistical issues should be clarified:
   - *Implied $t$-statistic and FDR:* A threshold of `slope > 0.25` and `SE < 0.25` without an explicit $p$-value or Benjamini–Hochberg FDR cutoff allows orthogroups with $t = \hat{\beta}/\text{SE}$ as low as $1.0$ ($p \approx 0.32$) to pass. The authors should state whether a nominal $p$-value or BH-FDR threshold was also applied to define the 342 clusters, and report the BH-adjusted $q$-values for the 8 *FabG* and 7 *ProP* orthogroups in Fig 5C–D.
   - *Scale dependence of `SE < 0.25`:* Because $\text{SE}(\hat{\beta}) = \hat{\sigma} / \sqrt{(N-1)\text{Var}(X)}$, thresholding on unstandardized $\text{SE} < 0.25$ and $\hat{\beta} > 0.25$ treats an orthogroup varying between 0 and 1 copy differently from an orthogroup varying between 1 and 10 copies. Standardizing predictors or filtering directly on the $t$-statistic / FDR avoids copy-number scale bias.
   - *One-sided positive slope filter:* Restricting to `slope > 0.25` excludes orthogroups whose copy number is significantly *negatively* associated with DE (e.g., traits causing drought depletion or plant defense activation).
2. **Minor Threshold Discrepancy Between Results and Methods (Results, Line 72 vs. Methods, Line 41 of block 2):** Results Line 72 states that orthogroups with copy number variance **`>0.2`** were tested ($n = 2,735$), whereas Methods Line 41 (block 2) states that only clusters with copy number variance **`>0.25`** and present in at least $1/4$ of genomes ($n = 2,735$) were tested. The text should be harmonized to specify which variance threshold was used.
3. **Experimental Unit Definition in Microbox Mono-Association Assays (Results, Line 64; Methods, Line 113 of block 1 & Line 1 of block 2):** Methods Line 113 states: *"Each 5 L microbox (SacO2, Belgium) housed four seedlings planted in 1 kg of calcined clay"*, and Line 1 of block 2 states: *"DE = log2(Drought/Water) where 'Drought' refers to the mean abundance of 4 replicates under drought and 'Water' refers to mean abundance of 4 irrigated replicates."* The authors should explicitly clarify whether the $n = 4$ replicates per isolate per condition represent four seedlings harvested from a *single* 5 L microbox per treatment (technical/sub-sampling replicates sharing the same microbox dry-down and rhizosphere environment) or four *independent* 5 L microboxes per treatment. Additionally, computing $\text{DE}$ as the difference of mean log-abundances ($\overline{\log_2(\text{Drought})} - \overline{\log_2(\text{Water})}$) rather than the log of the ratio of arithmetic means ($\log_2(\bar{D}/\bar{W})$) is less sensitive to single-replicate qPCR outliers, though the highlighted significant tiles in Fig 6 already use $t$-tests with BH-FDR $< 0.05$.
4. **Use of Legacy GreenGenes (August 2013) Database for 16S Taxonomic Classification (Methods, Line 11 of block 2):** While the 48 whole genomes were classified with the current GTDB-Tk v2.4.0 (r220), the field V3–V4 and full-length 16S rRNA amplicon datasets were classified against the August 2013 GreenGenes database. Although this does not affect the sequence-exact ASV matching (`ASV650` to `AC2–AC36`) or the in silico primer matching analysis, noting consistency with SILVA/GTDB taxonomy for the field phylum/genus summaries is recommended.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Standard V3–V4 and even full-length 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing obscure extensive genomic, metabolic, and physiological diversity among root-colonizing Streptomyces isolates.* `[Well Supported]` — Conclusively demonstrated by showing that a single dominant V3–V4 ASV (`ASV650`) splits into 5 full-length 16S ASVs and 28 isolates with markedly different sporulation pigmentation, LC–MS/MS exometabolomes on root tissue, siderophore production, osmotic stress tolerance, and accessory gene repertoires (up to 1,660 unshared orthogroups within the same >99% ANI strain group).
- **Claim 2:** *Drought enrichment (DE) in mono-association and plant growth promotion under watered or drought conditions are highly variable across Streptomyces isolates, lack phylogenetic signal, and are uncorrelated with one another.* `[Well Supported]` — Backed by quantitative phenotyping across 48 sequenced isolates, formal phylogenetic signal tests (Pagel's $\lambda$ and Blomberg's $K$), and striking sister-taxon contrasts (e.g., `DC01` vs. `SAI126`). The Discussion provides a nuanced, mechanistically insightful analysis of why field community relative-abundance DE differs from gnotobiotic mono-association DE (compositional total-sum constraints vs. inter-microbial interactions).
- **Claim 3:** *Copy number expansion of FabG (fatty acid biosynthesis) and ProP (osmolyte-proton symporter importing proline, betaine, pipecolic acid, and taurine) is associated with higher root DE in Streptomyces.* `[Moderately Supported]` — Biologically compelling and directly linked to prior metabolomic evidence showing accumulation of those exact osmolytes in drought-stressed RTx430 sorghum roots, subject only to reporting the formal multiple-testing-adjusted $q$-values from the PGLS screen.
