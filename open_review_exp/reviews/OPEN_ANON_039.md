### 1. Executive Summary
This study addresses the high histopathologic misclassification rate of thin invasive cutaneous melanoma (CM, Breslow $\le 1.0\text{ mm}$) versus benign nevi (BN) and intermediate melanocytic lesions. First, the authors generate a new targeted whole-transcriptome TempO-Seq dataset of FFPE specimens comprising 50 BN and 51 thin CM (archived 2017–2021 at a community pathology lab) alongside 66 clinically atypical nevi (AN: 61 dysplastic nevi [DN] and 5 BN, archived 2009–2010 at UA and Stanford). Forensically inspecting their own PCA and read depths, the authors discover a severe archival storage-duration batch effect completely confounding the older 2009–2010 AN/DN blocks with lower read counts and separate PCA clustering; rather than forcing a flawed batch correction on a completely confounded design, they transparently exclude the AN/DN arm from the TempO-Seq analysis. Using the 2017–2021 BN ($n=50$) and CM ($n=51$) TempO-Seq samples, they perform age-residualized, sex-stratified `limma-voom` (`voomWithQualityWeights` + `duplicateCorrelation` for within-patient clustering) differential expression, identifying 450 DEGs enriched in immune pathways and demonstrating via external comparison (`GSE112509` Schartl dataset) that sex differences in BN-vs-CM fold changes do not replicate. Second, to incorporate intermediate lesions, they integrate three public RNA-seq datasets (`GSE98394` Badal, `GSE112509` Schartl, and `phs001550` Bastian macrodissected progressing nevi [PN] paired with CM), apply `ComBat`, and use 1,000 bootstrap iterations of elastic-net ordinal regression (`BN=1, PN=2, CM=3`) to select a 23-gene signature (`PRAME`, `MMP11`, `PYGL`, `TRPM1`, `COL1A1`, `RGS1`, etc.). Finally, they evaluate coefficient directionality and magnitude across three independent multi-platform cohorts (their TempO-Seq dataset plus `GSE114445` Krueger and `GSE12391` Scatolini microarrays) and perform matched immunohistochemical (IHC) validation of MMP11 and PYGL in BN ($n=9$) and CM ($n=9\text{–}10$).

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Exemplary Scientific Integrity in Diagnosing and Handling the Archival Storage Batch Confounder:** A major strength of this manuscript is Figure 1 and its accompanying discussion: when the authors saw that the 2009–2010 UA/Stanford AN/DN samples clustered apart from the 2017–2021 community lab BN/CM samples with lower library sizes, they recognized that archive age/institution was 100% confounded with the DN phenotype and refused to apply batch correction or train a classifier on the confounded DN group. This level of self-critical statistical rigor is rare and commendable.
2. **Careful Demographic Adjustment and Sex-Chromosome Alignment:** In the TempO-Seq differential expression analysis, accounting for the significant age ($43.3$ vs. $63.7$ years, $p = 4.2 \times 10^{-7}$) and sex ($67\%$ vs. $33\%$ female, $p = 0.0024$) imbalances between BN and CM by residualizing age, stratifying by sex, using `duplicateCorrelation` for multi-sample patients (50 BN from 46 patients, 51 CM from 45 patients), and hard-masking Y-chromosome/PAR regions during sex-specific `Salmon` alignment in the public RNA-seq cohorts demonstrates strong bioinformatic attention to detail.
3. **Confounding Between Dataset Batch (`phs001550`) and the "Progressing Nevi" (PN) Class During `ComBat` Correction:**
   - In the second half of the study (Figure 4 & Figure 5), the authors combine three public RNA-seq datasets: Badal (`GSE98394`: 54 BN, 102 CM; fresh-frozen), Schartl (`GSE112509`: 23 BN, 57 CM; LCM fresh biopsies), and Bastian (`phs001550`: 17 PN, 20 CM; macrodissected FFPE).
   - Notice that **all 17 Progressing Nevi (PN) samples come exclusively from the Bastian FFPE dataset**, which also contains **zero BN samples** ($0\text{ BN}, 17\text{ PN}, 20\text{ CM}$), whereas Badal and Schartl contain **zero PN samples**!
   - In the Methods ("Creation of the transcriptomic biomarker", line 30 of chunk 2), the authors state they ran `ComBat` from `sva` across the three datasets without specifying whether lesion type was passed as a model covariate (`mod`), and even if `mod` was included, `ComBat` estimates batch location/scale parameters across all samples in each batch. Because Bastian is the *only* FFPE RNA-seq dataset and the *only* dataset containing PN (while having no BN), batch-centering the Bastian dataset to match the global mean of Badal and Schartl (which are mixtures of BN and CM) mathematically forces the Bastian samples (17 PN + 20 CM) to straddle the global mean—pushing the lower half of Bastian (the 17 PN samples) toward the middle of the BN–CM axis!
   - Thus, the observation in Fig 4B that "PN clustered in a component distinct and intermediate to BN and CM" after `ComBat` is partially an algebraic consequence of centering a `[PN + CM]` batch against `[BN + CM]` batches without a shared BN anchor in the Bastian cohort. While biologically plausible (since PN are nevi adjacent to melanoma), this partial batch-group confounding in the `ComBat` step should be explicitly acknowledged.
4. **In-Sample Training AUC Reporting and Cross-Platform Refitting:**
   - The AUCs reported in the Abstract ($\ge 0.90$) and Results (line 117: BN vs. PN $\text{AUC} = 0.930$, PN vs. CM $\text{AUC} = 0.998$, BN vs. CM $\text{AUC} = 0.9997$) are evaluated *in-sample on the training dataset* after selecting the 23 genes that appeared in 100% of 1,000 bootstrap elastic-net resamples of that same training dataset. Out-of-bag (OOB) or nested cross-validation AUCs should be reported alongside training AUCs to prevent optimism bias.
   - When validating in TempO-Seq, Krueger (`GSE114445`), and Scatolini (`GSE12391`), the authors did not apply the locked training weights (due to platform differences between RNA-seq, TempO-Seq, and 1-/2-channel microarrays), but instead refit a 23-gene multivariable linear regression independently in each test cohort and compared the resulting partial regression coefficients ($\beta_j \pm \text{SE}$). Because the 23 genes are correlated along PC1 (Fig 5C), refitting a 23-predictor multivariable linear model in small test cohorts ($N = 28$ in Krueger, $N = 52$ in Scatolini) suffers from multicollinearity and high variance in individual partial regression coefficients. Comparing *univariate* (marginal) standardized effect sizes per gene across platforms, or applying a rank-based/Z-score-standardized locked linear predictor to compute true out-of-sample test AUCs in TempO-Seq, Krueger, and Scatolini, would provide a much stronger validation of the 23-gene signature.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1: Transcriptomic differences between thin CM and BN are dominated by immune pathways and are highly consistent between males and females.** `[Well Supported]` Rigorously demonstrated via age-adjusted, sex-stratified analysis in TempO-Seq ($N=101$) and replicated in the Schartl dataset ($N=80$).
- **Claim 2: The 23-gene signature shows consistent directionality across multiple independent cohorts and platforms, and IHC confirms PYGL loss and dermal MMP11 gradient loss in thin CM vs. BN.** `[Well Supported]` The authors are commendably cautious in the Discussion (line 164), explicitly noting training sample size limitations, overfitting risk in training AUCs, and the small matched IHC cohort ($n = 9\text{–}10$ per group). The IHC demonstration that BN exhibits a superficial-to-deep dermal MMP11 maturation gradient (analogous to HMB45) and strong dermal PYGL expression that is lost in CM provides valuable histopathologic grounding.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Data Availability:** Exceptional transparency. The newly generated TempO-Seq raw count matrix and sample-level clinical/demographic metadata are provided directly in S1 and S2 Tables, and all five external dataset accessions (`GSE98394`, `GSE112509`, `GSE114445`, `GSE12391`, `phs001550.v2.p1`) are clearly tabulated in Table 2.
- **Methods & Protocols:** Antibody catalog numbers, dilutions, antigen retrieval solutions, and exact Trimmomatic/Salmon/limma/glmnet parameters are thoroughly documented.
