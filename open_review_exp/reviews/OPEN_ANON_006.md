### 1. Executive Summary
This study investigates sex-biased gene expression and protein evolutionary rates ($d_N/d_S$) in floral buds and flowers at anthesis of the dioecious cucurbit *Trichosanthes pilosa* ($n = 3$ male and $n = 3$ female plants per developmental stage; 12 RNA-seq libraries). Using a de novo assembled reference transcriptome (59,051 unigenes) and 1-to-1 orthologous groups (OGs) across four Cucurbitaceae species (`((T. anguina, T. pilosa), T. kirilowii), Luffa cylindrica`), the authors report that sex-biased expression is ~15-fold more prevalent in floral buds (5,096 female-biased and 4,214 male-biased genes) than in mature flowers (380 and 233 genes), and that male-biased genes in floral buds (343 OGs) exhibit significantly elevated $d_N/d_S$ driven by positive selection (`CodeML`, `aBSREL`, `BUSTED`) and relaxed purifying selection (`RELAX`). While the transcriptomic dataset in an understudied dioecious plant with heteromorphic XX/XY chromosomes is valuable, the molecular evolutionary conclusions are weakened by a critical technical asymmetry (comparing de novo RNA-seq assemblies in the two dioecious foreground species against whole-genome annotations in the two monoecious background species), an unexplained massive inflation of synonymous substitution rates ($d_S$, $p = 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$) in male-biased genes indicative of hidden paralog pairing, and the misinterpretation of `RELAX` intensification ($K > 1$) as a test for positive selection.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Technical Asymmetry Between Foreground De Novo Transcriptomes and Background Reference Genomes combined with Anomalous $d_S$ Inflation (Methods, Lines 11–15; Results, Line 106):** The two dioecious foreground species (*T. pilosa* and *T. kirilowii*) lack reference genomes and were represented by de novo Trinity + Corset transcriptome assemblies from floral RNA-seq, whereas the two monoecious background species (*T. anguina* and *L. cylindrica*) were represented by reference genome gene models. Crucially, in Line 106 and Figure 4—figure supplement 3, the authors report that **median synonymous substitution rates ($d_S$) in male-biased genes are dramatically higher** than in female-biased and unbiased genes in both the free-ratio ($p = 6.44 \times 10^{-12}$ and $p = 4.56 \times 10^{-13}$) and two-ratio ($p = 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ and $p = 9.42 \times 10^{-8}$) models, even though male-biased genes have *stronger* codon usage bias (lower ENC, $p = 0.0001$). In a 4-taxon comparison, true 1-to-1 orthologs diverging at the same speciation node (`T. pilosa` vs. `T. anguina`) should share the same baseline species-divergence $d_S$ up to minor mutation-rate/codon-bias variation. A massive spike in $d_S$ ($p < 10^{-15}$) specifically in male-biased genes—combined with the authors' observation that these genes belong to duplicated gene families—strongly suggests that when male-specific paralogs are expressed in male floral buds of *T. pilosa* (or *T. kirilowii*) or collapsed by Trinity/Corset into chimeric unigenes, `OrthoFinder` pairs **out-paralogs or allelic/isoform chimeras** as pseudo-"1-to-1 orthologs." Paralogous pairings and local Trinity assembly errors on the *T. pilosa* terminal branch inflate both $d_S$ and $d_N$ and are a well-known source of false-positive hits in branch-site codon models (`CodeML`, `aBSREL`, `BUSTED`).
2. **Conflation of HyPhy `RELAX` Intensification ($K > 1$) with Positive Selection (Results, Line 104; Discussion, Line 134; Fig 5):** In Line 104, the authors state that *"61 out of 343 OGs (17.73%) exhibited significant evidence of intensified positive selection ($K = 2.3363\text{–}50, \omega_2 \ge 1$)"* using `RELAX`, and in Figure 5 and Line 134 they take the union of `CodeML` (39 genes), `aBSREL` (84 genes), `BUSTED` (69 genes), and `RELAX` ($K > 1$, 61 genes) to claim that **28.57% (98/343) of male-biased genes underwent positive selection**. In the `RELAX` model (Wertheim et al., 2015), the selection intensity parameter $K$ scales all three $\omega$ rate classes ($\omega_{\text{test}} = \omega_{\text{ref}}^K$). A significant $K > 1$ LRT (testing $K = 1$ vs. $K \neq 1$) means that **overall selection—including purifying selection on $\omega_0, \omega_1 < 1$—has intensified** (pushing $\omega_0, \omega_1$ closer to 0 and $\omega_2$ away from 1); it does *not* test whether $\omega_2$ is significantly greater than 1! Treating `RELAX` $K > 1$ as a positive-selection detector inflates the reported positive-selection rate.
3. **Foreground Specification in the `two-ratio` Model vs. Phylogenetic Topology (Results, Line 66; Methods, Line 15 of block 2):** On the tree `(((T. anguina, T. pilosa), T. kirilowii), L. cylindrica)`, dioecious *T. pilosa* is sister to monoecious *T. anguina*, while dioecious *T. kirilowii* is an outgroup. In the `two-ratio` model, grouping the terminal branch of *T. pilosa* and the branch of *T. kirilowii* into a single shared foreground $\omega_{\text{foreground}}$ forces a gene that is male-biased in *T. pilosa* to share its $\omega$ parameter with *T. kirilowii* (where its sex-bias status was not even measured, as *T. kirilowii* reads from SRA were mixed/unsexed floral transcriptomes). Furthermore, the authors state they extracted foreground $d_S$ values from the `two-ratio` model (Line 106), whereas PAML's `model = 2` estimates branch-specific $\omega$ ratios on top of standard branch lengths rather than a separate foreground $d_S$ parameter.
4. **Degrees of Freedom in `CodeML` Branch-Site LRT (Methods, Line 22 of block 2):** The authors tested branch-site Model A against the null model (`fix_omega = 1, omega = 1`) using a $\chi^2$ distribution with $\text{df} = 2$. Because only a single parameter ($\omega_2$) is constrained to the boundary $\omega_2 = 1$ under the null, the asymptotic null distribution is a 50:50 mixture of point mass at 0 and $\chi^2_1$ (or conservatively $\chi^2_1$, $\text{df} = 1$; Zhang et al., 2005). Using $\chi^2_2$ is overly conservative and explains why `CodeML` identified far fewer genes (39) than `aBSREL` (84) or `BUSTED` (69) even before multiple-testing correction inconsistencies (Note: Line 90 says `CodeML` used `LRTs, p<0.05` whereas Methods Line 22 says FDR-adjusted; the authors should clarify whether the 39 genes passed raw $p < 0.05$ or BH-FDR $< 0.05$).
5. **Decimal Point Error in Sex-Linked Gene Percentage (Discussion, Line 140):** Line 140 states: *"computed the overall comparable proportions of sex-linked genes among male-biased (3/343=0.087%), female-biased (19/1145=1.66%) and unbiased genes (36/2378=1.51%)."* However, $3 / 343 = 0.008746 = 0.87\%$, not `0.087%`.
6. **Severe Attrition of Sex-Biased Genes in Orthogroup Filtering (Results, Lines 41 & 66):** Out of 4,214 male-biased unigenes and 5,096 female-biased unigenes in floral buds, only 343 male-biased (8.1%) and 1,145 female-biased (22.5%) survived 1-to-1 orthogroup assignment across the four species. Notice that male-biased unigenes were **2.8-fold more likely to drop out of 1-to-1 orthogroups** than female-biased unigenes ($8.1\%$ vs. $22.5\%$ retention, $\chi^2 p < 10^{-16}$), which is a classic signature of lineage-specific multi-copy/paralogous gene families or fragmented de novo transcripts in the male floral bud transcriptome.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim 1:** *Sex-biased gene expression in dioecious T. pilosa is ~15 times more pervasive in floral buds (9,310 genes) than in flowers at anthesis (613 genes).* `[Well Supported]` — Despite the modest sample size ($n = 3$ biological replicates per sex/stage), the massive contrast in differential expression between developing floral buds and mature flowers at anthesis is clear and biologically consistent with floral organogenesis and meiosis occurring in the bud stage.
- **Claim 2:** *Male-biased genes in floral buds evolve more rapidly ($d_N/d_S$) than female-biased and unbiased genes, representing the first demonstration of rapid male-biased gene evolution in dioecious angiosperms.* `[Fragile]` — While the median $\omega$ increase in the 343 retained male-biased OGs (0.257 vs. 0.227 and 0.230) is statistically significant ($p = 0.0021$ and $p = 0.0051$), 91.9% of male-biased unigenes were excluded from the 1-to-1 ortholog set, and the retained 343 OGs exhibit an anomalous $d_S$ inflation ($p = 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$) that points to paralogous pairing or de novo assembly divergence rather than purely elevated nonsynonymous substitution rates in true single-copy orthologs.
- **Claim 3:** *28.57% (98/343) of male-biased genes evolved under positive selection.* `[Overstated]` — This percentage relies on pooling `RELAX` ($K > 1$) hits with `aBSREL`/`BUSTED`/`CodeML` and comparing a 4-taxon tree where foreground sequences come from de novo transcriptomes and background sequences come from reference genomes.

The assembled reference transcriptome, orthology tables, and alignments are referenced as `Source data 1`, and gene lists/statistics are provided in `Supplementary files 1–14`.
