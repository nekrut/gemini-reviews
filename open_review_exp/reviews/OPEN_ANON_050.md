### 1. Executive Summary
This immunology study employs an in vitro primary human tonsil organoid model to dissect the innate and adaptive cellular mechanisms governing humoral and $\text{CD4}^+$ T cell responses to the adenoviral vector vaccine `ChAdOx1 nCoV-19`. Using pre-pandemic and post-pandemic tonsillectomy specimens ($n=6$ to $24$ donors across experiments), the authors demonstrate that `ChAdOx1 nCoV-19` induces robust SARS-CoV-2 Spike S1-specific IgG and $\text{IgD}^-\text{CD38}^{++}$ antibody-secreting cell (ASC) differentiation in post-pandemic donors in proportion to baseline S1-tetramer-binding switched memory B cell frequency ($R^2 = 0.6953, p = 0.0007$). By tracking vector transgene (`S1` or `GFP`) expression and innate activation at 24 hours, they show that plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) exhibit the highest transduction rate (~40–50%) and drive early IFN-$\alpha$ release. Through magnetic `BDCA-2` pDC depletion (98.6–99.9% efficiency), type I IFN blockade (`B18R`), recombinant IFN-$\alpha$ rescue (in both pDC-depleted `ChAdOx1` and weakly immunogenic `Ad5 nCoV-19` cultures), `CD19` B cell fractionation, and anti-IL-6R blockade, they establish that pDC-derived IFN-$\alpha$—rather than pDC-produced spike antigen—is essential for humoral and $\text{T}_\text{FH}$ effector responses, acting both directly and via downstream IL-6 induction. The experimental design is mechanistically rigorous and well-controlled, requiring only minor revisions to fix antibody clone errors in `Table 1`, clarify donor exclusion accounting, and apply multiple-testing adjustments across multi-cell/cytokine panels.

### 2. Methodological & Statistical Audit
1. **Swapped and Erroneous Antibody Clone Identifiers in `Table 1` (`Lines 76–112`)**:
   - Several monoclonal antibody clone designations in `Table 1` ("List of flow cytometry reagents") are mismatched:
     - **Line 78**: `AF488 | SARS-CoV-2 spike | Clone: 3G8 | Mouse | Biotechne`. Clone `3G8` is the well-known anti-human CD16 monoclonal antibody (which is also listed on Line 82 for `APC CD16`), not an anti-SARS-CoV-2 spike antibody! The correct Biotechne catalog/clone identifier for the AF488 anti-Spike antibody must be provided.
     - **Lines 81 and 86**: Line 81 lists `PE-Cy7 | CD19 | Clone: M5E2` and Line 86 lists `BV650 | CD14 | Clone: HIB19`. However, `M5E2` is an anti-CD14 clone and `HIB19` is an anti-CD19 clone (as correctly paired in Lines 95, 100, and 101 of the same table!). The clone names in Lines 81 and 86 were inadvertently transposed.
2. **Donor Screening, Exclusion Accounting, and Anonymized Covariates (`Lines 38, 139`, `S2C Fig`)**:
   - In Line 139, the authors report that 4 out of 22 post-pandemic donors spontaneously secreted anti-S1 IgG in unstimulated cultures (`S2C Fig`) and were excluded from downstream analyses, and that 13 of the remaining 16 (or 18?) donors mounted robust anti-S1 IgG responses (`Fig 1A`). Wait: $22 - 4 = 18$ unstimulated-negative donors, yet the next sentence states *"in the majority of post-pandemic donors ($n = 13/16$; Fig 1A)"*—leaving 2 donors unaccounted for between $18$ and $16$. Providing a clear summary flow or donor table specifying how many total post-pandemic and pre-pandemic tonsils were screened, how many were excluded for spontaneous baseline S1-IgG or non-response, and whether non-responding donors ($3/16$ in `Fig 1A`) were retained or excluded in the subsequent pDC-depletion and cytokine-blockade experiments (`Figs 3–7`, $n=6$ to $14$) is important for assessing selection bias.
   - Additionally, while Line 38 notes that tonsils came from "adult or pediatric patients" and were fully anonymized, clarifying whether age group (pediatric vs. adult) was recorded would be helpful given known differences in tonsillar memory B cell and pDC composition between young children and adults.
3. **Multiplicity Correction in Parallel Cell-Type and Cytokine Wilcoxon Tests (`Figs 2, 3, 4`)**:
   - While multi-treatment comparisons for a single readout appropriately use the non-parametric Friedman test with Dunn's post-hoc correction (`Figs 4F, 4H, 5A, 6D–E, 7B–F`), panels testing treatment effects across 6–7 cell subsets or 4–5 cytokines simultaneously (`Fig 2A, C–E`, `Fig 3E–G`, `Fig 4E`, `S3A, C, D`) apply separate unadjusted Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank tests (or multiple t-tests in `S3A Fig`). Adding Holm-Bonferroni or Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjustment across parallel cell types/cytokines is recommended.
4. **Minor Text Cross-Reference Typo (`Line 209`)**:
   - Line 209 cites `Fig 2G` for reduced IL-6 levels upon pDC depletion (*"Depletion of pDCs reduced IL-6 levels in the culture (Fig 2G)"*); `Fig 2` only has panels `A–E`, and this should reference `Fig 3G` (and `S4D Fig`).
5. **Exemplary Causal Perturbation Controls (`Figs 1, 3, 4, S5`)**:
   - The combination of loss-of-function (`BDCA-2` pDC depletion and `B18R` type I IFN decoy receptor blockade) and gain-of-function rescue (exogenous recombinant IFN-$\alpha$ restoring S1-specific IgG in both pDC-depleted `ChAdOx1 nCoV-19` cultures and naturally low-IFN `Ad5 nCoV-19` cultures, plus verifying that IFN-$\alpha$ alone without `ChAdOx1` induces polyclonal $\text{CD38}^{++}$ B cell activation without S1-specific IgG in `S5C Fig`) provides rigorous mechanistic proof separating cytokine licensing from cognate antigen availability.

### 3. Biological / Experimental Grounding & Claim Proportionality
- **Claim that the human tonsil organoid model recapitulates recall/booster humoral responses rather than primary naive priming (`Fig 1`, `Line 16` [Discussion])**: `[Well Supported]` — The authors appropriately recognize and highlight in the Discussion that S1-specific IgG production requires pre-existing S1-specific switched memory B cells ($R^2 = 0.6953$) and is absent in pre-pandemic tonsils even after 27 days, accurately framing their model as a recall/booster system.
- **Claim that pDC-derived type I IFN (and not pDC antigen presentation) is the primary driver of `ChAdOx1`-induced S1-specific IgG (`Figs 2–4`)**: `[Well Supported]` — Directly established by the near-complete rescue of S1-specific IgG in pDC-depleted organoids supplemented with exogenous IFN-$\alpha$ (`Fig 4F`) and the gain of S1-specific IgG in `Ad5 nCoV-19` organoids upon IFN-$\alpha$ addition (`S5F Fig`).
- **Claim that IL-6 acts downstream of IFN-$\alpha$ as well as through IFN-independent B-cell pathways to support humoral and $\text{T}_\text{FH}$ responses (`Figs 5–7`)**: `[Well Supported]` — Supported by anti-IL-6R blockade, intracellular IL-6 flow cytometry, and `CD19+` vs. `CD19-` fractionation experiments, with the authors candidly noting the technical limitations of isolating exact cDC vs. B cell IL-6 contributions in `Discussion` Line 28.

### 4. Reproducibility & Data Completeness
- **Experimental Protocols & Gating**: Flow cytometry gating hierarchies (`S1 Fig`), vector MOIs ($1,000\text{ vp/cell}$), bead depletion kits, blocker concentrations (`B18R` at $10\ \mu\text{g/mL}$, anti-IL-6R at $10\ \mu\text{g/mL}$), and ELISA kits are thoroughly documented.
- **Items to Correct**: Correct the swapped/erroneous antibody clone names in `Table 1` (Lines 78, 81, 86) and ensure all underlying individual-donor numerical source data for the plots are included in the Supporting Information files.
