# Experiment 4: Value-Added of Human Peer Review — Paired Version-Blinded Preprint vs. Published Audit & Direct Comparison to Human Peer Review Files

## 1. Executive Summary

A central question in meta-science is whether traditional human peer review acts primarily as a **selective filter** (sorting stronger manuscripts into higher-prestige journals) or as a **constructive treatment** (identifying and repairing methodological, statistical, and epistemic flaws prior to publication). Conversely, when flawed papers appear in the published literature, did human peer reviewers fail to notice the flaws, or did they notice them and accept cosmetic revisions?

To answer both questions without hindsight, survivorship, or prestige bias, we conducted a two-part experimental investigation:

1. **Part A — Paired Version-Blinded Preprint (`v1`) vs. Final Published Journal Audit ($N = 20$ Pairs = $40$ Full-Text Manuscripts):**
   We linked 20 full-text preprints (`PPR...` JATS XML from Europe PMC) to their final peer-reviewed journal counterparts (`PMC...` HTML from PubMed Central), stratified equally into **10 Tier 1/2 (Selective Flagship & Society Journals: *Nature Structural & Molecular Biology, Nature Cell Biology, Nature Communications, Science Advances, eLife, Nucleic Acids Research, PLoS Pathogens, PLoS Computational Biology, Genome Biology and Evolution*)** and **10 Tier 3 (Megajournal & Open-Threshold Venues: *iScience, BMC Biology, BMC Genomics, Scientific Reports, Frontiers in Neuroscience, mSystems, G3, RNA Biology, Parasites & Vectors*)**. Both versions of each paper were stripped of all identifying metadata, author names, journal titles, dates, DOIs, licensing strings (`CC-BY`), and version markers (`bioRxiv`, `medRxiv`, `preprint`, plus any appended open peer-review decision letters) and assigned across two disjoint blinded waves (`PR_W1_01`–`PR_W1_20` and `PR_W2_01`–`PR_W2_20`) so that no `anon-reviewer` subagent ever saw both versions of the same study.
2. **Part B — Direct Head-to-Head Comparison Against Verbatim Human Peer Review Files ($5$ Multi-Reviewer Dossiers, $210+$ Pages of Official Reports):**
   For five manuscripts with complete published Open Peer Review files (`PAIR_01` *PLoS Pathogens*, `PAIR_02` *eLife*, `PAIR_04` *Nature Communications*, `PAIR_05` *Nature Cell Biology*, and `C101 / ANON_201` *Nature* 2026 *Miao et al.*), we compared the blinded AI reviewer's independent preprint critique point-by-point against the verbatim reports of the human referees and editorial decision letters.

### Headline Findings

* **Human Peer Review Adds a Statistically Significant `+0.65` Points Overall ($p = 0.0004$), Driven `38.5%` by Data/Code Deposition and `61.5%` by Scientific Revisions:**
  Across all 20 matched pairs, composite scores increased from a Preprint Mean of **`6.53 / 10.0`** ($\text{SD} = 1.47$, Median = `6.60`) to a Published Mean of **`7.18 / 10.0`** ($\text{SD} = 1.38$, Median = `7.40`), yielding a mean treatment effect of **$\Delta S = +0.65 \pm 0.69$ points** (Median = **`+0.65`**, paired $t = 4.27, p = 0.0004$; Wilcoxon signed-rank $p = 0.0003$). Fourteen of 20 pairs (`70.0%`) improved by $> +0.2$ points, `4 / 20` (`20.0%`) were unchanged ($|\Delta S| \le 0.2$), and `2 / 20` (`10.0%`, both in Tier 3) slightly declined (`-0.3`).
* **Sub-Score Decomposition Reveals Where Peer Review Works—and Where It Does Not:**
  Decomposing $\Delta S = +0.65$ across the four rubric dimensions shows that **Data, Code & Reproducibility (`/2.0`) accounts for `+0.25` points (`38.5%` of the entire peer-review gain)**—reflecting editorial enforcement of GEO/SRA/PRIDE/Zenodo accession numbers at publication. **Methodological & Statistical Rigor (`/3.5`) gains `+0.21` points (`32.3%`)**, **Claim–Evidence Alignment (`/2.5`) gains `+0.14` points (`21.5%`)**, and **Biological & Evolutionary Models (`/2.0`) gains only `+0.04` points (`6.2%`)**—confirming that core model misspecification is almost never redesigned during peer review.
* **Tier 1/2 vs. Tier 3 Demonstrates Both a Strong Selection Effect (`+1.21` pts at Preprint) and a 2.7× Non-Reproducibility Treatment Gap (`+0.59` vs. `+0.22` pts):**
  * **Preprint Selection Gap:** Manuscripts that eventually publish in Tier 1/2 venues start out **`+1.21` points higher as preprints** (`7.13` vs. `5.92` in Tier 3).
  * **Treatment Gap:** Tier 1/2 peer review adds **`+0.85` points** ($p = 0.0049$; `80%` improved, `0%` declined), including **`+0.59` points of substantive methodological/claim improvement** ($\Delta\text{Rigor} = +0.30, \Delta\text{Claims} = +0.21, \Delta\text{Bio} = +0.08$) and **`+0.26` points of reproducibility gain**. By contrast, Tier 3 megajournal peer review adds only **`+0.46` points** ($p = 0.0427$), of which **more than half (`+0.24 / +0.46 = 52.2%`) comes purely from adding Data Availability statements**, leaving only **`+0.22` points** of substantive scientific improvement ($\Delta\text{Rigor} = +0.13, \Delta\text{Claims} = +0.08, \Delta\text{Bio} = +0.01$).
* **Zero Flaw Resolution in Tier 3 (`50%` Preprint `< 6.0` $\rightarrow$ `50%` Published `< 6.0`):**
  In Tier 1/2, the sole preprint scoring `< 6.0` (`PAIR_07`, *Nucleic Acids Research*, `5.2`) underwent major experimental and benchmarking expansion during peer review to reach **`7.3`** (`+2.1` pts). In Tier 3, **`5 / 10` (`50.0%`) preprints scored `< 6.0`**, and after human peer review **`5 / 10` (`50.0%`) published papers still scored `< 6.0`**—including all three severely flawed preprints (`PAIR_17` *BMC Genomics* `2.9 -> 4.5`, `PAIR_14` *BMC Genomics* `4.4 -> 4.3`, and `PAIR_20` *Parasites & Vectors* `4.9 -> 5.6`), whose fatal mathematical and evolutionary errors passed through peer review untouched.
* **Blinded AI Reviewer Matches `91.7%` (`22 / 24`) of Major Human Referee Critiques While Catching Fatal Mathematical/Statistical Errors Missed by Human Reviewers:**
  Across the 5 Open Peer Review dossiers (`210+` pages of referee reports), the single-pass blinded AI reviewer independently identified **`22 / 24` (`91.7%`)** of the consensus major methodological and overclaiming critiques raised by the human review panels (e.g., 2-fold toxin dilution confounding in `PAIR_04`, missing statistical tests and read-level pooling in `PAIR_01`, survivorship bias and unamortized build costs in `C101`), while additionally catching **14 concrete quantitative/algebraic errors, internal numerical contradictions (`51` vs. `58` interactions; `1 mM` stock diluted to `1 mM` final), and revision-induced pseudoreplication** that escaped all human referees and editors.

### 3.2 Three Structural Insights from Comparing Human vs. AI Peer Review

1. **High Diagnostic Concordance on Epistemic & Experimental Design Flaws (`91.7%` Recall):**
   Across 24 distinct major methodological and claim-alignment critiques raised by 14 human referees across the 5 dossiers, our single-pass blinded `anon-reviewer` agent independently raised **22 (`91.7%`)** of them—without ever seeing the journal name, author list, or human referee reports.
2. **Complementary Strengths (Wet-Lab Assay Nuance vs. Mathematical/Statistical Auditing):**
   * **Where Top-Tier Human Referees Excel:** In `PAIR_05` (*Nature Cell Biology*) and `PAIR_04` (*Nature Communications*), human domain specialists excelled at demanding **targeted wet-lab mechanistic extensions** (e.g., testing `ATG9A` and `Beclin-1` knockdowns, performing recombinant *E. coli* pull-downs + HDX-MS, or testing 3-species synthetic gut communities). When authors executed those wet-lab experiments (as in `PAIR_04`, `+1.5` pts, and `PAIR_07`, `+2.1` pts), manuscript quality improved substantially.
   * **Where Human Referees Consistently Fail (and Blinded AI Auditing Excels):** Across both Tier 1/2 and Tier 3 journals, human referees routinely failed to audit **equations, statistical model specifications, degrees of freedom, internal table-vs-text consistency, and clinical cohort confounding**. For example:
     * In `PAIR_01` (*PLoS Pathogens*), Human Reviewer #1 rightly complained that the preprint had no $p$-values on its violin plots—but when the authors came back in revision with Wilcoxon $p$-values computed across **tens of thousands of pooled single-molecule reads ($N = \text{reads}$)**, the human reviewer was satisfied and approved publication, whereas our blinded AI reviewer (`PR_W2_12`) immediately flagged that the newly added Wilcoxon test was pseudoreplicated and declared EBV significant ($p < 0.001$) even though the stochastic superiority effect size was in the **wrong direction** ($A = 0.46 < 0.50$)!
     * In `PAIR_05` (*Nature Cell Biology*), three human cell-biology referees scrutinized the autophagy western blots in detail, but **not one human referee noticed** that the `SCAN-B` breast cancer cohort used in Figures 6h–i (`2010–2018`) **never received PARP inhibitors**, nor that parametric ANOVAs were run on $n = 2$ biological replicates!
     * In `PAIR_14` (*BMC Genomics*), `PAIR_17` (*BMC Genomics*), and `PAIR_20` (*Parasites & Vectors*), human peer review completely missed an inverted `FUBAR` purifying/diversifying selection ratio ($\beta/\alpha = 0.736/4.657$), a 1977 reference-strain Hamming-distance artifact, an algebraic division by $\sqrt{\hat{h}_G^2}$ that mechanically fabricates GBLUP superiority, and an impossible Kruskal–Wallis test ($\chi^2 > 16.3, \text{df} = 9, p < 0.050$ called "non-significant").
3. **"The Rebuttal Concession Problem" Explains Why Flagship Papers Still Score `< 6.5`:**
   As `C101` (*Nature* 2026, *Paper2Agent*, `6.1 / 10.0`) proves, when a flawed or overclaimed paper appears in *Nature*, it is often **not** because the human peer reviewers failed to spot the flaws. Referee #1 at *Nature* wrote a devastatingly accurate critique of *Paper2Agent*'s survivorship bias, $n=15$ author-written benchmark, unamortized 3-hour build cost, and unvalidated ADHD GWAS claim—matching our blinded `ANON_201` review almost word-for-word. Yet in Round 2, the human review process allowed the manuscript to proceed to publication with those structural limitations largely intact in the main text, whereas an objective rubric-locked evaluator scores the final published text on what was actually fixed rather than what was argued in a rebuttal letter.
