# Experiment 4: Value-Added of Human Peer Review — Paired Version-Blinded Preprint vs. Published Audit & Direct Comparison to Human Peer Review Files

## 1. Executive Summary

A central question in meta-science is whether traditional human peer review acts primarily as a **selective filter** (sorting stronger manuscripts into higher-prestige journals) or as a **constructive treatment** (identifying and repairing methodological, statistical, and epistemic flaws prior to publication). Conversely, when flawed papers appear in the published literature, did human peer reviewers fail to notice the flaws, or did they notice them and accept cosmetic revisions?

To answer both questions without hindsight, survivorship, or prestige bias, we conducted a two-part experimental investigation:

1. **Part A — Paired Version-Blinded Preprint vs. Final Published Journal Audit ($N = 20$ Pairs = $40$ Full-Text Manuscripts):**
   We linked 20 full-text preprints (`PPR...` JATS XML from Europe PMC) to their final peer-reviewed journal counterparts (`PMC...` HTML from PubMed Central), stratified equally into **10 Tier 1/2 (Selective Flagship & Society Journals: *Nature Structural & Molecular Biology, Nature Cell Biology, Nature Communications, Science Advances, eLife, Nucleic Acids Research, PLoS Pathogens, PLoS Computational Biology, Genome Biology and Evolution*)** and **10 Tier 3 (Megajournal & Open-Threshold Venues: *iScience, BMC Biology, BMC Genomics, Scientific Reports, Frontiers in Neuroscience, mSystems, G3, RNA Biology, Parasites & Vectors*)**. Both versions of each paper were stripped of all identifying metadata, author names, journal titles, dates, DOIs, licensing strings (`CC-BY`), and version markers (`bioRxiv`, `medRxiv`, `preprint`, plus any appended open peer-review decision letters) and assigned across two disjoint blinded waves (`PR_W1_01`–`PR_W1_20` and `PR_W2_01`–`PR_W2_20`) so that no `anon-reviewer` subagent ever saw both versions of the same study.
2. **Part B — Direct Head-to-Head Comparison Against Verbatim Human Peer Review Files ($4$ Blinded Preprint Dossiers + $1$ Post-Publication *Nature* Dossier, $210+$ Pages of Official Reports):**
   For four matched pairs with published Open Peer Review files (`PAIR_01` *PLoS Pathogens*, `PAIR_02` *eLife*, `PAIR_04` *Nature Communications*, `PAIR_05` *Nature Cell Biology*), we compared the blinded AI reviewer's independent preprint critique item-by-item against the verbatim reports of the human referees and editorial decision letters. Separately, for `C101` (*Nature* 2026, *Miao et al. — Paper2Agent*), we audited the 96-page *Nature* peer-review file against our identified review of the final published article ([`review.md`](reviews/2026/pmid42749808-miao-reimagining-research-papers-as-interactive/review.md)) to examine what human peer review caught, what it conceded in rebuttal, and what revision-stage mathematical/tabular errors escaped all 5 human referees.

### Headline Findings

* **Substantive Scientific Core ($\Delta\text{Core}_8$, Excluding Reproducibility) Improves Significantly Overall (`+0.405 / 8.0` pts, $p = 0.0031$) and Is $2.68\times$ Larger in Tier 1/2 (`+0.590`) Than in Tier 3 (`+0.220`, $p = 0.118$):**
  Because the initial `parse_ppr_xml` pipeline extracted `<body>` and omitted `<back>` from Europe PMC preprint JATS XML—which stripped `<back>` Data Availability sections in 9 of 20 preprints (`build_peer_review_exp.py` has now been patched to extract `<back>` availability sections)—the cleanest, artifact-free measure of peer-review value-added is the **8.0-point Scientific Core ($\text{Core}_8 = \text{Rigor}_{/3.5} + \text{Claims}_{/2.5} + \text{Biology}_{/2.0}$)**, which completely excludes `Reproducibility & Data (/2.0)`:
  * **All 20 Pairs ($N = 20$):** Preprint $\text{Core}_8$ rose from **`5.210 ± 1.213 / 8.0`** (Median = `5.450`) to **`5.615 ± 1.196 / 8.0`** (Median = `5.800`), yielding **$\Delta\text{Core}_8 = +0.405 \pm 0.536$ points** ($\text{SE} = 0.120$, Median = `+0.250`; **scaled to `/10.0`: `+0.506` points**; **paired $t = 3.38, p = 0.0031$; Wilcoxon signed-rank $p = 0.0019$**; `16 / 20` [`80.0%`] strictly positive, `1 / 20` [`5.0%`] zero, `3 / 20` [`15.0%`, all in Tier 3] negative).
  * **Tier 1/2 Selective Flagship & Society ($n = 10$):** $\text{Core}_8$ increased from **`5.700 ± 1.077`** to **`6.290 ± 0.654`**, a substantive scientific gain of **$\Delta\text{Core}_8 = +0.590 \pm 0.606$ points** ($\text{SE} = 0.192$, Median = `+0.400`; **scaled to `/10.0`: `+0.738` points**; **paired $t = 3.08, p = 0.0132$; Wilcoxon $p = 0.0076$** [`p = 0.0039` exact on non-zeros]; `9 / 10` [`90%`] positive, `1 / 10` zero, `0 / 10` negative).
  * **Tier 3 Megajournal & Open-Threshold ($n = 10$):** $\text{Core}_8$ moved from **`4.720 ± 1.249`** to **`4.940 ± 1.332`**, a non-significant shift of **$\Delta\text{Core}_8 = +0.220 \pm 0.402$ points** ($\text{SE} = 0.127$, Median = `+0.200`; **scaled to `/10.0`: `+0.275` points**; **paired $t = 1.73, p = 0.1177$; Wilcoxon $p = 0.1309$**; `7 / 10` positive, `3 / 10` negative).
* **Composite 10-Point Score ($\Delta S = +0.655$, $p = 0.0004$) and Conservative `<back>`-Adjusted Total ($\Delta S_{\text{adj}} = +0.515$, $p = 0.0011$):**
  On the full 10-point scale, composite scores increased from **`6.53 ± 1.47`** to **`7.18 ± 1.38`** (**$\Delta S = +0.655 \pm 0.685$**, paired $t = 4.27, p = 0.0004$; Wilcoxon $p = 0.0008$). Even under the conservative adjustment holding $\Delta\text{Repro} := 0$ for all 9 pairs that had `<back>`-only accessions in the preprint XML, the composite treatment effect is **$\Delta S_{\text{adj}} = +0.515 \pm 0.599$ points** ($t = 3.84, p = 0.0011$; Wilcoxon $p = 0.0016$), and among the 11 pairs without `<back>`-only accessions, `Reproducibility & Data (/2.0)` still improves significantly (**$\Delta\text{Repro} = +0.200 \pm 0.257$**, $t = 2.58, p = 0.0273$) due to genuine publication-time depositions (e.g., `PRJNA1034001`/`PRJNA1037558` in `PAIR_02`, `Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.18862597` in `PAIR_16`, and public release of a previously private GitHub repository in `PAIR_19`).
* **Robustness to Preprint Posting Timing ($N = 18$ Excluding Late-Posted Preprints; $N = 12$ Strictly Confirmed Pre/Co-Submission):**
  Auditing exact preprint posting dates against journal `Received` and `Accepted` dates shows that two preprints were posted late in the review cycle: `PAIR_05` (*Nature Cell Biology*, posted 15 months after initial submission at the Round 1 revision stage, 4 months before acceptance) and `PAIR_20` (*Parasites & Vectors*, posted after formal acceptance). Excluding both (`N = 18`) **strengthens** the treatment effect (**$\Delta\text{Core}_8 = +0.417 \pm 0.561$, $p = 0.0058$; $\Delta S = +0.672 \pm 0.719$, $p = 0.0010$**) and **widens the Tier 1/2 vs. Tier 3 substantive scientific gain ratio from $2.68\times$ to $3.41\times$** (`+0.644` [$p = 0.0139$] vs. `+0.189` [$p = 0.2079$]). Restricting to the $N = 12$ strictly confirmed pre- or co-submission preprints yields **$\Delta\text{Core}_8 = +0.425 \pm 0.596$ ($p = 0.0310$; `11/12` positive)** and **$\Delta S = +0.675 \pm 0.644$ ($p = 0.0040$)**, with Tier 1/2 ($n=7$) gaining **`+0.671` on $\text{Core}_8$ (`100%` positive, $p = 0.0311$)** vs. Tier 3 ($n=5$) at **`+0.080` ($p = 0.629$)**.
* **Single-Run $\text{MDC}_{95}$ (`1.28` pts) vs. Group-Level Precision ($\text{SE}_{\text{mean}} = 0.153$ pts) and Tier 3 `< 6.0` Rate (`5/10 -> 5/10`):**
  From Experiment 2 (`CALIBRATION_AND_VARIANCE.md`), single-reviewer test–retest $\text{SD}_{\text{within}} = 0.464$ points implies a single-pair Minimum Detectable Change at 95% confidence of $\text{MDC}_{95} = 1.96 \times \sqrt{2} \times 0.464 = 1.28$ points; four individual pairs (`PAIR_07` `+2.1`, `PAIR_10` `+1.8`, `PAIR_17` `+1.6`, `PAIR_04` `+1.5`) exceed this single-run threshold on their own, whereas small individual shifts ($|\Delta S| \le 0.5$, such as `PAIR_13` `-0.3`, `PAIR_15` `-0.3`, `PAIR_14` `-0.1`) lie within single-run stochastic noise. At the cohort level ($N = 20$), random noise averages down by $\sqrt{N}$ ($\text{SE}_{\text{mean}} = 0.685/\sqrt{20} = 0.153$ pts on $\Delta S$; $0.120$ pts on $\Delta\text{Core}_8$), giving $>98\%$ power to detect the observed group-level treatment effect. In Tier 3, **`5 / 10` (`50.0%`) preprints scored `< 6.0`**, and after human peer review **`5 / 10` (`50.0%`) published papers scored `< 6.0`**—including all three severely flawed preprints (`PAIR_17` `2.9 -> 4.5`, `PAIR_14` `4.4 -> 4.3`, `PAIR_20` `4.9 -> 5.6`).
* **Blinded Preprint AI Reviewer Matches `83.3%` (`15 / 18` Overall: `12 / 18` [`66.7%`] Strict Direct Match + `3 / 18` [`16.7%`] Partial/Conceptual Match) of Human Referee Critiques Across the 4 Blinded Preprint Dossiers (`85.7%` [`12 / 14`] on the 3 Pre-Review Preprints):**
  Across the 4 strictly blinded preprint dossiers (`PAIR_01`, `PAIR_02`, `PAIR_04`, `PAIR_05`), our single-pass blinded AI reviewer independently matched **`12 / 18` (`66.7%`)** human referee critiques directly and **`3 / 18` (`16.7%`)** partially/conceptually (**`15 / 18 = 83.3%`** overall; on the 3 strictly pre-review preprints `PAIR_01`, `PAIR_02`, `PAIR_04`, **`10 / 14 = 71.4%` direct + `2 / 14 = 14.3%` partial = `12 / 14 = 85.7%`**), while additionally catching fatal algebraic, statistical, and clinical cohort flaws missed by all human referees.

#### 2.3.1 Audit of `<back>` JATS XML Extraction vs. `<body>` and Why $\text{Core}_8$ (`/8.0`) Is Invariant

In Europe PMC preprint JATS XML (`PPR...`), some preprint servers place Data and Code Availability statements inside `<back><sec sec-type="data-availability">` rather than `<body>`, whereas PMC HTML (`PMC...`) renders back-matter availability sections inside the main article body. In our initial `build_peer_review_exp.py` script, `parse_ppr_xml` parsed `soup.find("body")` and omitted `<back>` (`build_peer_review_exp.py` has now been patched so `parse_ppr_xml` also extracts `<back>` availability sections).

To quantify the exact scope of this input asymmetry without re-running LLM evaluations, we audited the raw Europe PMC JATS XML `<back>` and `<body>` elements across all 20 preprints (full metadata recorded in [`peer_review_exp/manifest.json`](peer_review_exp/manifest.json) and [`peer_review_exp/peer_review_results.json`](peer_review_exp/peer_review_results.json)):

#### 2.3.3 Single-Run $\text{MDC}_{95}$ (`1.28` pts) vs. Paired Cohort Inference ($\text{SE}_{\text{mean}} = 0.153$ pts)

A standard statistical distinction applies when interpreting per-pair deltas versus cohort-level paired tests:

1. **Single-Pair Resolution ($\text{MDC}_{95} \approx 1.28$ points):**
   In Experiment 2 (`CALIBRATION_AND_VARIANCE.md`), repeated independent blinded reviews of the same manuscript yielded a within-paper standard deviation of $\text{SD}_{\text{within}} = 0.464$ points (`/10.0`), implying a standard error for the difference of two single runs of $\text{SE}_{\Delta, 1} = \sqrt{2} \times 0.464 = 0.656$ points and a 95% Minimum Detectable Change of $\text{MDC}_{95} = 1.96 \times 0.656 = 1.28$ points. Accordingly:
   * **Four individual pairs exceed $\text{MDC}_{95}$ on their own:** `PAIR_07` (`+2.1`, *Nucleic Acids Research*), `PAIR_10` (`+1.8`, *Genome Biology and Evolution*), `PAIR_17` (`+1.6`, *BMC Genomics*), and `PAIR_04` (`+1.5`, *Nature Communications*).
   * **Individual pair shifts with $|\Delta S| \le 0.5$ (`PAIR_13` `-0.3`, `PAIR_15` `-0.3`, `PAIR_14` `-0.1`, `PAIR_03` `0.0`, `PAIR_06` `+0.1`, `PAIR_18` `+0.1`, `PAIR_05` `+0.3`, `PAIR_02` `+0.4`, `PAIR_12` `+0.4`) lie well within single-run stochastic noise** and should not be over-interpreted as statistically significant individual-paper changes (for example, in `PAIR_13`, while the published version did add a flawed one-tailed paired $t$-test paragraph, the `-0.3` score difference itself is smaller than $\text{SD}_{\text{within}} = 0.464$).
2. **Group-Level Paired Resolution ($\text{SE}_{\text{mean}} = 0.153$ points on $\Delta S$; $0.120$ points on $\Delta\text{Core}_8$):**
   Because Wave 1 and Wave 2 errors are independent and identically distributed with zero mean across the $N = 20$ pairs, the standard error of the **mean paired difference** scales as $\sigma_{\Delta} / \sqrt{N}$:
   $$\text{SE}(\overline{\Delta S}) = \frac{0.685}{\sqrt{20}} = 0.153\text{ pts} \quad (t = 4.27, p = 0.0004), \qquad \text{SE}(\overline{\Delta\text{Core}_8}) = \frac{0.536}{\sqrt{20}} = 0.120\text{ pts} \quad (t = 3.38, p = 0.0031).$$
   Under pure single-run measurement noise ($\sigma_{\Delta,\text{null}} = \sqrt{2}\times 0.464 = 0.656$ pts), the null standard error of the mean across $N = 20$ pairs is $0.656 / \sqrt{20} = 0.147$ points (95% two-sided noise band $\pm 0.288$ points)—which the observed mean gain (`+0.655` pts on $\Delta S$; `+0.506` pts on $\Delta\text{Core}_8$ scaled to `/10.0`) exceeds by $3.4\text{--}4.5\text{ SE}$.

### 3.3 Three Structural Insights from Comparing Human vs. AI Peer Review

1. **Strong Blinded Preprint Concordance on Experimental & Statistical Flaws (`83.3%` [`15/18`] Across All 4 Blinded Preprints; `85.7%` [`12/14`] Across the 3 Pre-Review Preprints):**
   Without seeing the journal name, author list, or human referee reports, our single-pass blinded `anon-reviewer` agent independently raised `12 / 18` (`66.7%`) of the human referee critiques directly and `3 / 18` (`16.7%`) partially across `PAIR_01`, `PAIR_02`, `PAIR_04`, and `PAIR_05` (`10 / 14 = 71.4%` direct and `12 / 14 = 85.7%` overall on the 3 strictly pre-review preprints `PAIR_01`, `PAIR_02`, `PAIR_04`).
2. **Complementary Strengths (Wet-Lab Assay Nuance vs. Mathematical, Tabular & Statistical Verification):**
   * **Where Top-Tier Human Referees Excel:** In `PAIR_05` (*Nature Cell Biology*) and `PAIR_04` (*Nature Communications*), human domain specialists excelled at demanding **targeted wet-lab mechanistic extensions** (e.g., testing `ATG9A` and `Beclin-1` knockdowns, performing recombinant *E. coli* pull-downs + HDX-MS, or testing 3-species synthetic gut communities). When authors executed those wet-lab experiments (as in `PAIR_04`, `+1.5` pts total / `+1.1` `Core_8`, and `PAIR_07`, `+2.1` pts total / `+1.9` `Core_8`), manuscript quality improved substantially.
   * **Where Human Referees Consistently Fail (and AI Auditing Excels):** Across both Tier 1/2 and Tier 3 journals, human referees routinely failed to verify **equations, statistical model degrees of freedom, internal table-vs-text consistency, clinical cohort treatment histories, and revision-added calculations**:
     * In `PAIR_01` (*PLoS Pathogens*), Human Reviewer #1 rightly complained that the preprint had no $p$-values on its violin plots—but when the authors came back in revision with Wilcoxon $p$-values computed across **tens of thousands of pooled single-molecule reads ($N = \text{reads}$)**, the human reviewer was satisfied and approved publication, whereas our blinded AI reviewer (`PR_W2_12`) immediately flagged that the newly added Wilcoxon test was pseudoreplicated and declared EBV significant ($p < 0.001$) even though the stochastic superiority effect size was in the **wrong direction** ($A = 0.46 < 0.50$).
     * In `C101` (*Nature* 2026), Human Referee #1 asked for statistical tests in Round 1 and praised the authors' new $p = 0.0042$ paired $t$-test in Round 2—missing both the run-level pseudoreplication ($\text{df}=4$ across 5 runs of the same 15 queries), the `$0.38 / $0.20 = 1.90× \neq 2.8×` arithmetic error in `Supplementary Note 7`, and $>20$ text-vs-table contradictions in `Supplementary Table 1`.
     * In `PAIR_05` (*Nature Cell Biology*), three human cell-biology referees scrutinized the autophagy western blots in detail, but **not one human referee noticed** that the `SCAN-B` breast cancer cohort used in Figures 6h–i (`2010–2018`) **never received PARP inhibitors**, nor that parametric ANOVAs were run on $n = 2$ biological replicates.
     * In `PAIR_14` (*BMC Genomics*), `PAIR_17` (*BMC Genomics*), and `PAIR_20` (*Parasites & Vectors*), human peer review completely missed an inverted `FUBAR` purifying/diversifying selection ratio ($\beta/\alpha = 0.736/4.657$), a 1977 reference-strain Hamming-distance artifact, an algebraic division by $\sqrt{\hat{h}_G^2}$ that mechanically fabricates GBLUP superiority, and an impossible Kruskal–Wallis test ($\chi^2 > 16.3, \text{df} = 9, p < 0.050$ called "non-significant").
3. **Revision Verification Gap:**
   Even when human referees diagnose major weaknesses in Round 1, second-round review rarely audits the mathematical or tabular validity of the newly added rebuttal material (`PAIR_01` read-pooled Wilcoxon tests, `PAIR_04` `51` vs. `58` positive interactions, `C101` `Supplementary Table 1` and `Supplementary Note 4/7`). Pairing human domain review with automated formal/statistical verification of revised manuscripts directly closes this gap.
