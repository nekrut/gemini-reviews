### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript adapts a single-cell tumor mutational ordering algorithm (SCITE / Mutational Order Approach, MOA) to reconstruct the early mutational history and ancestral progenitor genome (`proCoV2`) of SARS-CoV-2 across two GISAID snapshots: 29,681 genomes collected through July 7, 2020 (29KG, 49 SNVs at $>1\%$ frequency) and 68,057 genomes collected through October 12, 2020 (68KG, 84 SNVs), with spatiotemporal lineage tracking extended to 172,480 genomes through December 30, 2020 (172KG). By orienting variants using three animal sarbecovirus outgroups (RaTG13, RmYN02, and pangolin MT121216.1) coupled with Co-occurrence Index (COI) optimization, the authors infer a progenitor genome (`proCoV2`) that differs from Wuhan-Hu-1 by three mutations ($\alpha_1$: C18060U, $\alpha_2$: C8782U, and $\alpha_3$: U28144C), placing the ancestor of the North American WA1/Lineage A.1 clade at the root of the global pandemic and estimating that `proCoV2` originated 5.8–8.1 weeks prior to December 24, 2019 (late October 2019, with a speculative index case in September 2019). Sequence alignments, `proCoV2` FASTA files, and an interactive web classifier (`http://sars2evo.datamonkey.org/`) are publicly provided.

While the mutational fingerprinting taxonomy and client-side web dashboard provide a fast, practical way to track major pandemic clades (e.g., $\alpha\beta\varepsilon$ / B.1.1 and $\alpha\beta\gamma\delta$ / B.1.2) across 172,480 genomes, the core evolutionary inference regarding `proCoV2` and the pre-December 2019 pandemic timeline rests on violated model assumptions and circular mutation orientation rules. Specifically, SCITE assumes that variant frequency in the sample reflects mutational age (higher-frequency variants are placed upstream of lower-frequency variants) and treats genomes as independent samples with uniform false-positive/false-negative error rates. In a pandemic with extreme spatiotemporal sequencing skew (thousands of genomes sequenced in the US and Europe in spring 2020 vs. 18 genomes in Wuhan in December 2019), variant frequency in July 2020 is governed by founder effects and regional sequencing intensity, not ancestral order. Combined with outgroup rooting over a $\sim 1,100$-mutation branch (where C$\to$U hypermutation and homoplasy are rampant) and post-hoc reversal of allele encodings whenever COI $< 70\%$, MOA misidentifies the derived C18060U mutation (which defines the Washington State WA1 / Lineage A.1 outbreak) as ancestral (`proCoV2`). Addressing these limitations requires evaluating MOA on realistic epidemic coalescent simulations with biased sampling, removing the frequency-to-age assumption and ad hoc allele flipping, and accounting for Poisson mutational variance in molecular clock dating.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The progenitor of all human SARS-CoV-2 infections (`proCoV2`) differs from Wuhan-1 by three variants ($\alpha_1$: 18060, $\alpha_2$: 8782, $\alpha_3$: 28144), implying none of the earliest December 2019 China patients carry the ancestral genome: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The inference that `proCoV2` carries 18060U (alongside Lineage A alleles 8782U and 28144C) and that Wuhan-1 evolved via three sequential mutations $\alpha_1 (\text{U18060C}) \to \alpha_2 (\text{U8782C}) \to \alpha_3 (\text{C28144U})$ is an artifact of two methodological choices in Materials and Methods (lines 188–194): (1) initializing ancestral states (`0`) from distant bat/pangolin outgroups ($\sim 4\%$ divergent, $>1,100$ differences) that happen to share U at 18060 (a known C$\to$U hypermutable third-codon-position site in SARS-CoV-2), and (2) using SCITE, which explicitly assumes that higher-frequency variants arose earlier than lower-frequency variants (*"In MOA, higher frequency variants are expected to have arisen earlier than low-frequency variants in clonally reproducing populations. We used the highest frequency variants to anchor the analysis"*, line 188). Because 95.1% of the 29KG dataset carries 18060C while 4.9% (primarily the heavily sequenced Washington State / North American A.1 outbreak clade carrying C18060U) carries 18060U, coding 18060U as ancestral (`0`) forces SCITE to place the 95.1% allele ($\alpha_1$: U18060C) at the trunk of the tree above $\alpha_2$ (91.0%) and $\alpha_3$ (90.8%). Even more strikingly, in Table 1 (lines 55–57) and Figure 1c, the three mutations $\mu_1$ (2416 U$>$C, vf = 98.1%), $\mu_2$ (19524 U$>$C, vf = 98.6%), and $\mu_3$ (23929 U$>$C, vf = 98.4%) are treated as mutations from `proCoV2` present in $>98\%$ of global genomes, meaning `proCoV2` is defined as carrying the minority alleles at those positions as well!

* **Post-hoc reversal of ancestral/mutant allele orientation based on Co-occurrence Index ($\text{COI} < 70\%$) and likelihood: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Materials and Methods (lines 190–194 and chunk 2 lines 1–4), the authors state that whenever the outgroup-based ancestral coding yielded $\text{COI} < 70\%$ (e.g., position 3037, $\beta_1$, where outgroups have U, giving $\text{U}\to\text{C}$ with $\text{COI} = 60\%$), they manually reversed the coding ($\text{C}\to\text{U}$, $\text{COI} = 100\%$) to match the high-frequency D614G ($\beta_2$) background. However, they did *not* apply symmetric outgroup uncertainty to positions 18060 ($\alpha_1$), 8782 ($\alpha_2$), or 28144 ($\alpha_3$), because coding the rare WA1/Lineage A alleles as `0` and the $>90\%$ Wuhan-1 alleles as `1` automatically guarantees $\text{COI} > 95\%$ for any downstream mutation arising in the dominant B / B.1 clades! Thus, the COI rule asymmetrically flips outgroup homoplasies when they occur in derived clades (position 3037) while locking in outgroup homoplasies when they occur near the root (position 18060).

* **Divergence of `proCoV2` occurred 5.8–8.1 weeks before December 24, 2019 (late October 2019), with a putative index case in September 2019: [Overstated].**  
  In Materials and Methods ("Timing of the Progenitor Genome", chunk 2 line 10), the 5.8–8.1 week interval is calculated deterministically by dividing $d = 3$ point mutations (between `proCoV2` and Wuhan-1) by the substitution rate range ($6.64 \times 10^{-4}$ to $9.27 \times 10^{-4}$ substitutions/site/year $\approx 19.8\text{–}27.7$ substitutions/genome/year). First, as shown above, the 3-mutation distance rests on treating C18060U (and Lineage A) as the linear ancestor of Wuhan-1. Second, even if a 3-mutation path were true, nucleotide substitutions accumulate as a stochastic Poisson process: the 95% confidence interval for the waiting time to accumulate 3 mutations under a Poisson process spans more than a 5-fold range (from $\sim 1.3$ weeks to $>18$ weeks), which the authors omit by plugging $d = 3$ directly into $t = d / \mu$.

* **Major mutational fingerprints ($\alpha\beta\varepsilon$, $\alpha\beta\gamma\delta$, $\alpha\beta\zeta$) accurately classify 172,480 genomes and track continental strain replacements across 2020: [Well Supported].**  
  Once the tree moves past the root orientation of $\mu/\nu/\alpha$ into the high-frequency derived clades ($\beta$: D614G/C3037U/C14408U; $\gamma$: G25563U; $\delta$: C1059U; $\varepsilon$: G28881A/G28882A/G28883C; $\zeta$: A222V / 20A.EU1), the variant co-occurrence structure is unambiguous, shows $>90\%$ concordance with Pango lineages (Supplementary Fig. S3), and accurately captures the replacement of $\alpha$ by $\alpha\beta\varepsilon$ and $\alpha\beta\zeta$ in Europe and the persistence of $\alpha\beta\gamma\delta$ (B.1.2 / 20C) in North America (Figure 5).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Benchmark MOA Root Reconstruction on Epidemic Coalescent Simulations with Spatiotemporal Sampling Bias and Homoplasy
* **WHY:** SCITE was designed for single-cell tumor phylogenetics where a known normal diploid cell defines the ancestral state `0` and variant frequency correlates with clonal age. In viral epidemics with superspreading and heavily skewed temporal/geographic sequencing, a derived lineage sequenced at high volume (or sharing a convergent C$\to$U mutation with a distant outgroup) can have its complement coded as a $>95\%$ "mutation," placing the derived lineage at the root.
* **WHAT:** Simulate viral genealogies and genome alignments under a realistic branching/coalescent model starting from a single known root genome (e.g., Wuhan-Hu-1) with empirical SARS-CoV-2 C$\to$U substitution biases and GISAID's exact temporal sampling schedule (few sequences in Month 1, tens of thousands in Months 3–6), attach a 4%-divergent outgroup, and measure how often MOA + COI recoding recovers the true root vs. a derived clade.
* **HOW:** Use `iqtree`/`seq-gen` or `phastSim` on a known SARS-CoV-2 tree rooted at Wuhan-Hu-1 to simulate 100 datasets of 29,681 genomes + 3 outgroups at 4% divergence. Run the exact outgroup-consensus + COI $<70\%$ flipping + SCITE pipeline on each replicate and report the root reconstruction error rate.

#### 2. Test Alternative Root Orientations Symmetrically Using Likelihood and Temporal Sampling Information
* **WHY:** Reversing allele coding at position 3037 because $\text{COI} = 60\%$ while retaining outgroup coding at 18060 ($\alpha_1$), 8782 ($\alpha_2$), and 28144 ($\alpha_3$) is asymmetric: any leaf near the base of an unrooted tree will yield high COI for internal branches if chosen as the root.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate the SCITE log-likelihood and COI distribution across all candidate root nodes (including Wuhan-Hu-1, Lineage A [8782U/28144C with 18060C], and `proCoV2` [8782U/28144C/18060U]) without relying on the 4%-divergent bat outgroups at hypermutable C/U sites.
* **HOW:** Construct the binary genotype matrices anchored separately on (a) Wuhan-Hu-1, (b) Lineage A consensus (18060C, 8782U, 28144C), and (c) `proCoV2` (18060U, 8782U, 28144C, plus check 2416/19524/23929). Report the SCITE log-likelihood, number of homoplasies/reversions required, and temporal rank correlation with sampling dates for each candidate root.

#### 3. Propagate Poisson Mutational Variance into the $t_{\text{MRCA}}$ Confidence Interval
* **WHY:** Dividing exactly 3 nucleotide differences by the substitution rate HPD interval ($6.64 \times 10^{-4}$ to $9.27 \times 10^{-4}$ subs/site/year) ignores the stochastic variance of the Poisson mutation process when $d = 3$ is small, creating an artificially narrow 5.8–8.1 week window.
* **WHAT:** Incorporate Gamma/Poisson uncertainty for observing $k = 3$ substitutions along a lineage of length $t$ with genome-wide rate $\Lambda = L \cdot \mu$.
* **HOW:** Compute the posterior distribution of $t$ given $k = 3$ mutations via $p(t \mid k=3) \propto \int \text{Poisson}(k=3 \mid \Lambda t) \, p(\Lambda) \, d\Lambda$, where $\Lambda \in [19.3, 27.0]$ substitutions/year, and report the full 95% credible interval for $t$ (which spans from $<2$ weeks to $>16$ weeks) alongside the Lineage A (2-mutation) alternative.

#### 4. Temper Claims Regarding Pre-December 2019 Global Circulation of `proCoV2`
* **WHY:** Observing genomes with the `proCoV2` or $\mu$-only fingerprint in Washington State and China in January–March 2020 (Figure 1c, d) does not mean `proCoV2` was "spreading worldwide months before... the first reported cases of COVID-19 in China" (Abstract, line 8); travel-associated exports from China in January 2020 fully explain January–March 2020 North American and European detections.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Abstract and Results ("The Progenitor Genome and the Index Case" and "Estimated Timing of MRCA") to distinguish between the persistence of basal lineages in early 2020 and pre-December 2019 global spread.
* **HOW:** Change the Abstract statement *"suggesting that the progenitor was spreading worldwide months before and after the first reported cases of COVID-19 in China"* to state that basal lineages persisted and spread internationally for months *after* their initial emergence in late 2019, and frame the September–October 2019 origin dates as model-dependent estimates contingent on outgroup rooting at position 18060.
