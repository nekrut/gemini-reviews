### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study evaluates whether whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data (>30$\times$ coverage) can be used to predict physical and biometric traits—3D facial shape and color, voice pitch and identity vectors, biological age (from telomere repeat content and mosaic sex-chromosome loss), height, weight, BMI, eye color, and skin color—and match de-identified genomes to phenotypic profiles in a convenience sample of 1,061 ethnically diverse adults from San Diego, California. Using 10-fold cross-validation, the authors fit ridge regression and shrinkage models incorporating up to 1,000 genomic principal components (PCs), sex, predicted age/BMI, and literature SNPs, and then train a maximum-entropy weighted distance metric to rank (`select at N`, $s_N$) or perform one-to-one bipartite assignment (`match at N`, $m_N$) between genomes and phenotype profiles. They report re-identifying $>8$ of 10 individuals ($m_{10} = 83\%$, $s_{10} = 74\%$) in an ethnically mixed pool of 10, and $\sim 5$ of 10 within African American ($s_{10} = 44\%$, $m_{10} = 47\%$) or European ($s_{10} = 50\%$, $m_{10} = 57\%$) pools. Access to the WGS dataset requires a managed-access agreement, no source code repository is provided, and Table 2 in the manuscript text is entirely blank.

While genomic privacy is an important topic, the manuscript's central claim—that genomic prediction of complex biometric traits such as 3D facial structure, voice, and stature enables individual re-identification—is severely overstated and contradicted by the paper's own internal component analyses. Specifically, the authors' own results show that (i) known facial-morphology SNPs contribute *zero* predictive accuracy beyond sex and genomic ancestry PCs (Figure 3, line 41), and a facial landmark GWAS in the cohort yielded zero significant hits; (ii) 97 of the 100 voice identity-vector dimensions have $R^2_{\text{CV}} < 0.10$, while the $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.70$ for voice pitch simply reflects biological sex (XX vs. XY); and (iii) polygenic height SNPs alone explain only $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.06\text{--}0.08$ within sex (which the authors note in line 89 offers "marginal improvements in discriminative power over random"), jumping to $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.53$ only when males and females are pooled. Consequently, the matching algorithm is not identifying individuals via individualized polygenic facial or vocal reconstruction; rather, it is performing coarse demographic triangulation on **biological sex, continental and sub-continental genetic admixture (plus skin/eye pigmentation), and age** within tiny closed lineups of $N = 10$ individuals. Furthermore, including 1,000 genomic PCs in a 10-fold cross-validation training set of $\sim 955$ individuals exceeds the rank of the training sample covariance matrix, and the missing Table 2 and unshared code prevent independent verification.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Genomic prediction of 3D facial structure and voice contributes meaningful biometric identification power beyond sex, age, and ancestry: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Results line 41 and Figure 3, the authors show that adding previously reported facial SNPs produces no improvement over sex and genomic PCs alone, and that predicted faces simply reflect "the sex and ancestry proportions of the individual" (line 33) plus predicted age and BMI. Furthermore, in Materials and Methods line 147, the "3D face" embedding $\psi_P(p)$ explicitly includes regression models trained to predict **sex, age, and ancestry** from the 3D photograph (which Table 1 shows are visually recoverable from a face photo with $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.75\text{--}0.89$) and matches them against genome-derived **sex, ancestry, and age** in $\phi_P(g)$. Presenting "3D face alone achieves $s_{10} = 58\%$ ($m_{10} = 64\%$)" (line 101) as a demonstration of genomic facial reconstruction is misleading when the 3D face embedding is acting primarily as a visual carrier of sex, skin color/ancestry, and age. Similarly, voice prediction ($R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.70$ for pitch, $<0.10$ for 97/100 identity dimensions) is overwhelmingly a proxy for chromosomal sex.

* **The maximum-entropy algorithm re-identifies $>8$ of 10 individuals ($m_{10} = 83\%$) in an ethnically mixed cohort and $\sim 5$ of 10 within African Americans or Europeans: [Overstated].**  
  Three factors inflate these headline numbers:
  1. *Demographic and continuous admixture sorting in small pools ($N = 10$):* In a random pool of 10 individuals from a multi-ethnic cohort spanning ages 18–82 (Figure 1), chromosomal sex alone cuts the candidate pool in half, continental ancestry and continuous admixture proportions (Figure 1B, which vary widely even within self-reported African American and European residents of San Diego and strongly correlate with continuous RGB skin/eye color) narrow it further, and age narrows the remainder. The manuscript never compares the complex "face + voice + anthropometrics" model against a transparent baseline model containing *only* `{Chromosomal Sex + Admixture Proportions + Eye/Skin Pigmentation SNPs + Age}` within sex-and-ancestry-stratified pools.
  2. *Closed-world bipartite matching assumption ($m_N$):* The `match at 10` metric ($m_{10} = 83\%$) assumes the attacker is given exactly 10 genomes and the exact 10 matching people, solving a constrained $10 \times 10$ one-to-one assignment problem by elimination. In realistic database re-identification, a genomic sample must be matched against an open population or a database of thousands to millions of people without a closed $N = 10$ bijection.
  3. *Rank deficiency of 1,000 genomic PCs in $N = 1,061$:* Using 1,000 genomic PCs in a cohort of 1,061 individuals ($N_{\text{train}} \approx 955$ per 10-fold CV split, lines 33, 55, 89) exceeds the maximum number of non-zero orthogonal principal components ($N_{\text{train}} - 1 = 954$) and captures cryptic relatedness/family pairs within the convenience sample rather than generalizable population structure.

* **WGS-derived telomere length and mosaic sex chromosome loss predict chronological age with $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.44$ ($\text{MAE} = 8.0\text{ y}$): [Fragile].**  
  In line 78, the authors report that telomere repeat fraction alone predicts age with $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.29$ in their 1,061-person cohort, yet immediately note that the same method yields only $R^2 = 0.05$ in the literature and $R^2 = 0.05$ in 1,960 females from the *same cohort sequenced on the same pipeline*. A nearly 6-fold jump in variance explained ($0.29$ vs. $0.05$) between subsets of the same sequencing pipeline strongly suggests confounding between age, ethnicity/ancestry (Figure 1A–C), and DNA extraction or sequencing batch in the 1,061-person convenience sample.

* **Continuous RGB eye color and skin color can be predicted from genomic PCs and pigmentation SNPs with $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.77\text{--}0.82$: [Well Supported].**  
  Because human eye and skin pigmentation have oligogenic architectures with large-effect variants (e.g., *HERC2/OCA2*, *SLC24A5*, *SLC45A2*, *MC1R*) and strong correlation with continental ancestry in an admixed cohort, high cross-validated $R^2$ for continuous RGB color in a multi-ethnic sample is biologically plausible and consistent with forensic pigmentation models.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Benchmark All Re-Identification Models Against a Transparent Demographic + Pigmentation Baseline
* **WHY:** Because facial SNPs add zero predictive accuracy (Figure 3), height SNPs alone yield $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.06\text{--}0.08$, and 97/100 voice dimensions fail ($R^2_{\text{CV}} < 0.10$), the reader cannot tell how much—if any—of the reported $s_{10}$ and $m_{10}$ accuracy comes from complex trait prediction (3D face geometry, voice identity, height/BMI) versus simple matching on sex, genomic ancestry/admixture, eye/skin color, and age.
* **WHAT:** Add an explicit baseline predictor consisting strictly of `{Sex + Top 10 Ancestry PCs + 19 Eye/Skin Pigmentation SNPs + WGS Age}` and measure the exact incremental gain ($\Delta s_N$ and $\Delta m_N$, with bootstrap $95\%$ CIs) when adding 3D facial geometry residuals, voice identity vectors, and polygenic height/BMI.
* **HOW:** Residualize 3D facial shape PCs, voice embeddings, and height/weight/BMI on sex, age, and the top 10 ancestry PCs (and pigmentation SNPs) within each training fold. Re-run the maximum-entropy matcher to report (i) the baseline demographic/pigmentation accuracy, (ii) the accuracy of residualized biometric traits alone, and (iii) the combined accuracy, stratified simultaneously by both sex and ancestry (e.g., within EUR males and within AFR females).

#### 2. Audit and Eliminate Kinship Leakage and PC Over-Parameterization (1,000 PCs in $N = 1,061$)
* **WHY:** A convenience sample of $N = 1,061$ individuals cannot support 1,000 orthogonal principal components in a 90% training fold ($N_{\text{train}} \approx 955$), and any related individuals (siblings, parent-offspring, cousins) split across training and test folds will allow ridge regression on 954 PCs to memorize family-specific facial, vocal, and anthropometric traits.
* **WHAT:** Filter the cohort to strictly unrelated individuals (kinship coefficient $< 0.044$, third-degree relatives or closer excluded), restrict genomic ancestry covariates to the top 5–20 PCs computed strictly within each training fold (or projected from 1000 Genomes reference populations), and verify that nested cross-validation is used when learning both the ridge regression embeddings ($\phi_P, \psi_P$) and the maximum-entropy similarity weights ($w_d$).
* **HOW:** Compute pairwise KING kinship coefficients across all 1,061 WGS samples, remove one member of each related pair, project genotypes onto reference 1000 Genomes PCs, and ensure that the maximum-entropy weights $w_d$ (Equation 1) are trained exclusively on out-of-fold predictions within the 9 training folds rather than on the held-out test fold.

#### 3. Investigate Confounding in the Telomere Age Predictor ($R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.29$ vs. $0.05$)
* **WHY:** The 6-fold discrepancy between telomere-age $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.29$ in the 1,061 study participants and $R^2 = 0.05$ in 1,960 females from the same pipeline (line 78) points to batch or ancestry confounding in telomere read proportions.
* **WHAT:** Test whether the telomere-age $R^2_{\text{CV}}$ in the 1,061 cohort is driven by sequencing flowcell/library preparation batch, sex, or ancestry differences in age distribution (Figure 1C).
* **HOW:** Regress estimated telomere length on sequencing batch/date, mean coverage depth, sex, and ancestry PCs, and report the partial $R^2_{\text{CV}}$ of telomere length and X/Y mosaic loss for predicting age within single-ancestry, single-batch strata.

#### 4. Align Claims with Realistic Pool Sizes and Stratified Performance
* **WHY:** Highlighting $m_{10} = 83\%$ in the Abstract—which relies on closed-world bipartite elimination in an ethnically mixed pool of 10 people of different sexes and ages—overstates the risk of re-identifying an anonymous genome in a real-world biobank where sex and ancestry are already known metadata and candidate pools number in the thousands.
* **WHAT:** Reframe the Abstract and Discussion to report open-set identification accuracy ($s_N$ at $N = 100$ to $1,000$) within same-sex, same-ancestry strata, and explicitly state in the Abstract that complex trait models (3D face shape, voice, height/BMI) are driven primarily by sex, ancestry/admixture, and age rather than individual-specific polygenic variants.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract (line 8) to report the same-sex, same-ancestry select accuracy ($s_{10}$ and $s_{100}$) alongside the mixed-cohort numbers, and note that known facial SNPs did not improve facial prediction over sex and ancestry PCs.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** A missing main-text table and corrupted mathematical notation in Equation 1 impede evaluation of the results.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Table 2 (line 122):** Populate the missing Table 2 ("Top one accuracy in match and select"), which is currently rendered as an empty table cell (`| |`).
  2. **Equation 1 (line 156):** Replace the forward slashes `/` with absolute value bars `|` and add a negative sign so that $\delta_P$ is a similarity rather than a distance: $\delta_P(\psi_P(p), \phi_P(g)) = -\sum_{d=1}^{D} w_d \left|\psi_P(p)_d - \phi_P(g)_d\right|$.
  3. **Results (line 33, 55, 89):** Explain how 1,000 genomic PCs were defined in 10-fold CV when each training fold contains only $\sim 955$ individuals (or correct the number of PCs if 1,000 PCs were computed on a larger external dataset).
  4. **Code Availability:** Deposit the feature extraction, cross-validation, and maximum-entropy bipartite matching scripts in a public repository (e.g., GitHub/Zenodo).
