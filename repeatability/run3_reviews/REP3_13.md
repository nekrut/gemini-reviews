### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript combines suspension mass cytometry (CyTOF), 16S rRNA gene sequencing (performed at two independent facilities, SIgN and WIS), anaerobic/aerobic bacterial culture, scanning electron microscopy (SEM), 16S RNA in situ hybridization (RNA-ISH), and in vitro syngeneic dendritic cell (DC)–T cell co-culture assays to argue that human second-trimester fetal organs (12–22 weeks estimated gestational age, EGA) are colonized by live bacteria that prime antigen-specific memory T cells in utero. The authors show that fetal organs harbor $\text{CD45RO}^+$ effector memory T cells ($\text{T}_{\text{em}}$) and $\text{PD1}^+\text{Ki67}^+$ regulatory T cells ($\text{T}_{\text{reg}}$), detect a low-biomass 16S rRNA signal above PBS controls, isolate live bacterial colonies (predominantly *Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Enterococcus*, and *Prevotella*) from 6 of 8 fetal donors, visualize cocci-like structures and 16S rRNA in the fetal mid-gut lumen ($n=3$), and show that fetal mesenteric lymph node (mLN) T cells proliferate when co-cultured with autologous DCs pulsed with heat-killed fetal *Staphylococcus* or *Lactobacillus* ($n=4$). Raw sequencing data are deposited in GEO (GSE169306).

While the multi-modal experimental effort and inclusion of operator, environmental, reagent, and PBS negative controls are substantial strengths, the study's central conclusion—that live microbes colonize healthy human fetuses in utero and prime microbe-specific memory T cells—is severely compromised by an unaddressed clinical sampling confounder and overinterpreted immunological assays. Specifically, all second-trimester fetal specimens were obtained from medically induced prostaglandin terminations delivered vaginally through the birth canal (STAR Methods, line 130). Passage of a non-viable fetus through the maternal cervix and vagina during prostaglandin-induced labor directly exposes fetal skin, placenta, and mucosal orifices (via aspiration/swallowing into the lung and gut) to high-density maternal vaginal and skin microbiota (*Lactobacillus*, *Gardnerella*, *Prevotella*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Enterococcus*)—precisely matching both the taxonomic identity of the isolates and the observed "external-to-internal" organ gradient (highest diversity in placenta and skin, intermediate in lung and gut, lowest in spleen and thymus). Furthermore, the in vitro T cell proliferation assay lacks MHC class I/II blocking or TCR specificity controls to distinguish true in utero cognate antigen recall from staphylococcal superantigen or innate PAMP-driven polyclonal activation. Resolving these issues requires comparing vaginally delivered fetuses against hysterotomy/cesarean-delivered or intact-sac controls alongside paired maternal vaginal swabs, correcting the differential abundance mathematics, and demonstrating MHC-restricted antigen specificity.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Second-trimester human fetal organs harbor a diverse pool of tissue-resident $\text{CD45RO}^+$ memory T cells, $\text{TNF}^+/\text{IFN}\gamma^+$ effector memory T cells, and $\text{PD1}^+\text{Ki67}^+$ $\text{T}_{\text{reg}}$ cells: [Well Supported].**  
  The CyTOF profiling across fetal gut, lung, liver, skin, thymus, mLN, and blood (Figure 1 and Figure S1) is technically well executed and reproduces prior independent demonstrations of $\text{CD45RO}^+$ T cells and $\text{T}_{\text{reg}}$ enrichment in second-trimester human fetal tissues. However, the presence of $\text{CD45RO}^+$ T cells in the fetal thymus and peripheral organs is a well-known consequence of homeostatic proliferation in lymphopenic fetal environments and recognition of maternal microchimerism or self/non-inherited maternal antigens, and does not by itself imply live microbial colonization.

* **Live bacteria isolated and sequenced from second-trimester fetal tissues (*Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Enterococcus*, *Prevotella*) represent authentic in utero fetal colonization rather than intrapartum vaginal contamination: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In STAR Methods ("Fetal dissection and organ collection", line 130), the authors disclose that *"Mid-trimester terminations were medically induced by the use of prostaglandins, and the fetus was delivered through the birth canal."* Laboratory PBS, reagent, and operator hand swabs taken *after* delivery cannot control for contamination acquired *during* labor and vaginal passage through the birth canal. The dominant genera isolated by culture and 16S sequencing (*Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Prevotella*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Enterococcus*; Figures 2G and 3D) are the exact hallmark taxa of the human vagina and perineum, and the authors' own finding of a steep diversity and CFU gradient from surface-exposed tissues (placenta, skin) to mucosal tracts (lung, gut) to sterile internal organs (thymus, spleen) (Figures 2F, 3C, S3B) is the textbook signature of intrapartum surface exposure and mucosal aspiration/ingestion during delivery. No maternal vaginal swabs were collected to test strain identity, and no intact-amniotic-sac or surgical hysterotomy controls were analyzed.

* **Differential taxonomic enrichment in the fetal gut (Figures 2H and S2K) is statistically supported by the Relative Percentage Abundance (RPA) and $\log_2\text{FC}$ metrics: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In STAR Methods ("Estimation of Differentially Enriched Bacterial Taxa in Gut", lines 231–253), the authors define $\text{RPA}_{\text{OTU}} = (S_{\text{Gut}} \times 100) / (S_{\text{Gut}} + S_{\text{Spleen/Thymus}} + S_{\text{PBS}})$, where $S$ is the raw count of samples in which reads exist, without normalizing for the different total numbers of gut, spleen/thymus, and PBS samples! Furthermore, at SIgN, 16S libraries were generated using three sequential rounds of nested PCR totaling 54–72 cycles (12 cycles V2–V6 + 12 or 30 cycles V4–V5 + 30 cycles barcoding PCR; line 182), which severely distorts read abundance and amplifies trace DNA, and differential taxa in Figures 2H and S2K are called using an arbitrary $\log_2\text{FC} \ge 2$ cutoff on mean counts without formal zero-inflated/compositional statistical testing (`ALDEx2` or `ANCOM-BC`) or multiple-testing correction.

* **Proliferation of fetal mLN T cells in response to autologous DCs pulsed with heat-killed fetal *Staphylococcus* or *Lactobacillus* demonstrates prior in utero microbial priming and antigen-specific memory recall: [Overstated].**  
  In Figures 5 and 6 ($n = 4$), 55% to 70% of all fetal mLN T cells become $\text{CTV}^{\text{neg}}$ (undergo complete proliferative dilution) within 6 days of exposure to *Staphylococcus*- or *Lactobacillus*-pulsed DCs, even when DCs are fixed with 1% PFA or treated with protein transport inhibitors after 8 hours of bacterial pulsing. In a naive or memory T cell repertoire, cognate peptide-MHC recall to a single commensal bacterial species engages a small precursor fraction ($0.01\%\text{–}1\%$) of polyclonal T cells; having $70\%$ of the entire mLN T cell pool divide points strongly to polyclonal mitogenic or superantigen-driven activation (e.g., staphylococcal enterotoxins/superantigens cross-linking MHC class II on PFA-fixed DCs to TCR $V\beta$ chains) or innate TLR/cytokine co-stimulation. No MHC class I/II blocking antibodies, naive cord-blood/thymocyte negative responder controls, or non-fetal bacterial controls (e.g., soil/marine bacteria never encountered in utero) were included to prove cognate memory specificity.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Include MHC Class I/II Blocking Controls and Non-Encountered Bacterial Controls in the T Cell Expansion Assay
* **WHY:** A 55–70% proliferation rate of bulk mLN T cells after 6 days (Figures 5C–F and 6B–D) is orders of magnitude higher than expected for cognate antigen-specific memory T cell precursors and can be driven by bacterial superantigens (which remain active on PFA-fixed MHC-II+ DCs) or innate PAMP signaling.
* **WHAT:** Test whether T cell proliferation is abolished by pan-MHC class I and class II blocking antibodies and whether sorted $\text{CD45RA}^+$ naive fetal T cells (or thymocytes) vs. sorted $\text{CD45RO}^+$ memory fetal T cells respond differently, alongside a control bacterium that could not have been encountered in utero.
* **HOW:** Repeat the syngeneic mLN DC–T cell co-culture ($n = 4$) with (a) anti-HLA-DR/DP/DQ and anti-HLA-A/B/C blocking antibodies added during T cell co-culture, (b) pre-sorted $\text{CD45RO}^+$ vs. $\text{CD45RA}^+$ fetal mLN T cells plated separately, and (c) DCs pulsed with an environmental bacterium absent from humans (or purified staphylococcal enterotoxin B as a superantigen comparison).

#### 2. Control for Intrapartum Vaginal Delivery Contamination
* **WHY:** Because all fetuses were delivered through the vaginal canal following prostaglandin induction (line 130), maternal vaginal flora (*Lactobacillus*, *Gardnerella*, *Prevotella*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*) inevitably contact the placenta, fetal skin, and upper respiratory/digestive tracts.
* **WHAT:** Stratify microbiome and culture data by amniotic membrane rupture duration / delivery mode (if any intact-sac specimens exist in Table S1), and formally quantify the overlap between "fetal-enriched" taxa and canonical human vaginal microbiota.
* **HOW:** If intact-sac or surgical termination specimens are available, compare their BactQuant qPCR $C_t$ values and CFU counts directly against vaginally delivered specimens. Otherwise, explicitly reclassify *Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Prevotella*, *Staphylococcus*, and *Streptococcus* as candidate maternal vaginal/cervical transfers acquired either prenatally or intrapartum during prostaglandin delivery, and highlight this confounder prominently in the Abstract and Results rather than only briefly in the Limitations.

#### 3. Correct the Differential Taxa Mathematical Formulation and Apply Compositional Statistical Tests
* **WHY:** The RPA formula in STAR Methods (line 249), $\text{RPA}_{\text{OTU}} = (S_{\text{Gut}} \times 100) / (S_{\text{Gut}} + S_{\text{Spleen/Thymus}} + S_{\text{PBS}})$, uses unnormalized sample counts across groups of unequal sample size ($n = 42$ PBS controls vs. smaller numbers of individual fetal organs) and lacks $p$-values or FDR control.
* **WHAT:** Replace the ad hoc RPA/$\log_2\text{FC}$ quadrant filter with sample-size-normalized prevalence and a formal differential abundance test (`ANCOM-BC`, `ALDEx2`, or paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test across fetus-matched organs and PBS controls) with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction.
* **HOW:** Using the unrarefied OTU count table in Table S3, restrict to fetuses with matched Gut, Spleen/Thymus, and PBS samples, compute paired log-ratio differences per fetus, and report BH-adjusted $q$-values for the 22 candidate gut-enriched genera in Figures 2H and S2K.

#### 4. Correct Statistical Test Mismatches in Figure Legends (Paired vs. Unpaired Tests)
* **WHY:** In Figures 5 and 6 (and S6), the same $n=4$ donor co-culture experiments are analyzed with a paired $t$-test in panel D/C (total T cell counts) but an unpaired Student's $t$-test in panel H/E (frequency of expanded $\text{CD69}^+$ or $\text{CD45RO}^+$ cells), and multiple treatment conditions are compared without multiple-comparison adjustment (e.g., Dunnett's or Holm-Šídák test).
* **WHAT:** Apply a consistent repeated-measures / paired statistical framework with multiple-testing correction across all donor-matched co-culture conditions in Figures 5, 6, S5, and S6.
* **HOW:** Analyze the donor-matched conditions (DC alone, DC + *Staphylococcus*, DC + *Lactobacillus*, DC + PFA/PTI) using a one-way repeated-measures ANOVA (or Friedman test) followed by Holm-Šídák post-hoc tests against the unprimed DC control.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Contradictions between the main text and STAR Methods regarding which bacterial genera were used in experiments create serious confusion about experimental provenance.
* **WHAT & HOW:**

Correct `"Salmonella"` to `"Staphylococci"` and specify the exact isolate strain IDs from Table S5 used in the co-culture assays.
  * **Nested PCR cycle count clarification (STAR Methods, line 182):** Clarify the exact number of PCR cycles used in the second nested V4–V5 amplification step at SIgN (line 182 states *"similar to V2-V6 PCR cycling profile"* [12 cycles], followed by 30 cycles of communal barcoding PCR = 54 total cycles).
  * **Typo in Results (line 76):** Fix `"Neverthelss"` to `"Nevertheless"`.
