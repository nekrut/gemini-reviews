### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates the existence and clinical correlates of a human placental microbiome using a population-based case-cohort of 320 placental specimens collected via the PeriBank repository (assayed by 454 pyrosequencing of the 16S rRNA V1–V3 region) and a nested subset of 48 specimens assayed by Illumina HiSeq 2500 whole-genome shotgun (WGS) sequencing. Comparing placental taxonomic and metabolic profiles against nonpregnant Human Microbiome Project (HMP) reference datasets and gravidae vaginal datasets, the authors report that the placenta harbors a unique low-biomass microbial community dominated by *Escherichia coli* and phyla Firmicutes, Tenericutes, Proteobacteria, Bacteroidetes, and Fusobacteria that is taxonomically most similar to the human oral microbiome (Bray-Curtis dissimilarity $< 0.3$). Using PERMANOVA, LEfSe, and Boruta feature selection, they further report significant community differences associated with preterm birth ($<37$ weeks, $P = 0.001$) and remote first- or second-trimester antenatal infection ($P = 0.006$). The 16S data are deposited in SRA (PRJNA238913) and WGS data in dbGaP.

Despite the large clinical cohort ($n = 320$) and careful sterile tissue excision protocol, the manuscript's central conclusions are compromised by three major methodological flaws: (1) an asymmetric bioinformatic pipeline comparison in which placental WGS reads classified via loose BLASTX/MG-RAST thresholds ($e\text{-value} \le 1$, minimum alignment length 15 aa, identity $\ge 50\%$ in samples with $>99\%$ human DNA) are directly compared against HMP reference profiles generated by the stringent clade-specific marker tool MetaPhlAn; (2) failure to sequence and computationally subtract extraction blank ("kitome") reads, while highlighting classic reagent and environmental water contaminants (*Burkholderia*, *Acinetobacter*, *Paenibacillus*, and marine chemolithoautotrophs *Thioalkalivibrio* and *Pseudoalteromonas*) as biologically meaningful biomarkers of preterm birth and antenatal infection; and (3) complete confounding between remote antenatal infection and systemic maternal antibiotic administration. Re-evaluating these data requires processing placental and HMP WGS reads through an identical, stringent taxonomic pipeline after rigorous human read depletion, sequencing all negative extraction controls to apply quantitative decontamination (`decontam`), and stratifying clinical analyses by antibiotic exposure.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The human placenta harbors a unique resident microbiome dominated by *E. coli* and commensal species: [Fragile].**  
  As acknowledged in the Discussion (line 90) and Materials and Methods (line 143), the placental specimens have extremely low microbial biomass (~0.002 mg estimated bacterial DNA per gram of tissue) and $>99\%$ human reads in WGS data. Although one non-template extraction blank was included per 11 samples (line 131), the authors rely on the absence of a visible PCR band on an agarose gel (`fig. S4`) to assert that no 16S sequences were generated from controls, rather than deep-sequencing all blanks alongside tissue samples and reporting read counts in WGS and 16S runs. Furthermore, classifying remaining short reads after heuristic `BMTagger` filtering using MG-RAST with a permissive cutoff of maximum $e\text{-value} = 1$, minimum identity $50\%$, and alignment length $15$ (line 143) is highly prone to false-positive mapping of residual human, phiX, or cloning-vector sequences to reference genomes such as *E. coli* (which frequently contains human/vector contamination in draft databases).

* **Placental microbiome profiles are most akin (Bray-Curtis dissimilarity $< 0.3$) to the human oral microbiome: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To compare body sites in Figures 1 and 2, the authors did not process raw HMP WGS reads through their placental MG-RAST pipeline; instead, they downloaded pre-computed HMP WGS taxonomic tables generated by **MetaPhlAn** (`http://hmpdacc.org/HMSMCP/`, line 136) and merged them with placental profiles generated by **MG-RAST** ($e\text{-value} \le 1$, length $\ge 15$, line 143). MetaPhlAn uses stringent clade-specific unique marker genes, whereas MG-RAST M5NR best-hit matching at $e \le 1$ assigns short, low-complexity reads across broad phyla (inflating Proteobacteria and Firmicutes). Computing phylum-level Bray-Curtis dissimilarity and SourceTracker proportions across two incompatible taxonomic classifiers confounds biological niche similarity with bioinformatic pipeline bias.

* **Specific placental bacterial taxa (*Burkholderia*, *Paenibacillus*, *Acinetobacter*, *Thioalkalivibrio*, *Pseudoalteromonas*) distinguish preterm birth and remote antenatal infection: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Results (lines 72–77; Figs. 3B and 4B–D), Boruta feature selection and LEfSe identify *Burkholderia* and *Paenibacillus* as top discriminators of preterm vs. term gestation, and *Acinetobacter*, *Thioalkalivibrio*, and *Pseudoalteromonas* as top discriminators of remote antenatal infection. *Burkholderia*, *Paenibacillus*, and *Acinetobacter* are among the most ubiquitous DNA extraction kit and PCR reagent contaminants documented in MO-BIO PowerSoil kits and molecular biology water, while *Thioalkalivibrio* (an extremophilic haloalkaliphilic sulfur-oxidizing bacterium) and *Pseudoalteromonas* (a marine bacterium) have no plausible biological niche in the human placenta. Their differential abundance between clinical groups strongly points to batch effects in extraction kit lots, processing dates, or sample biomass rather than in utero colonization.

* **A remote history of first- or second-trimester antenatal infection alters the placental microbiome and increases lipopolysaccharide (LPS) biosynthesis pathways: [Overstated].**  
  In Results (line 77; Fig. 4A, E), the authors report a PERMANOVA $P = 0.006$ for remote antenatal infection. However, as noted in the Discussion (line 88), all women diagnosed with clinically symptomatic antenatal UTIs or STIs received clinically indicated antibiotic therapy (`table S3`). The study design cannot separate the effect of the infection from the effect of maternal antibiotic exposure (or differences in sample collection/extraction batches). Moreover, PERMDISP for antenatal infection is borderline significant ($P = 0.07$, line 77), indicating that group dispersion differences contribute to the unweighted UniFrac PERMANOVA signal.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Unify the Taxonomic and Functional Classification Pipeline Across Placental and HMP WGS Data
* **WHY:** Comparing MG-RAST ($e\text{-value} \le 1$, alignment length $\ge 15$, identity $\ge 50\%$) outputs on placental samples directly against MetaPhlAn outputs on HMP reference samples introduces severe algorithmic bias that invalidates Bray-Curtis and SourceTracker comparisons (Figs. 1 and 2).
* **WHAT:** Re-analyze the 48 placental WGS samples using MetaPhlAn (matching the HMP protocol) and stringent alignment thresholds, or conversely run raw HMP WGS subsets through the exact same MG-RAST pipeline.
* **HOW:** Apply `Bowtie2` alignment against the full human reference genome (GRCh38 + telomere-to-telomere CHM13) after `BMTagger` to remove all residual human reads, then run `MetaPhlAn` (with default clade-specific marker stringency) on the non-human reads from the 48 placental WGS samples. Recompute the Bray-Curtis dissimilarity matrix, PCoA (`fig. S2`), and SourceTracker estimates across body sites.

#### 2. Sequence All Blank Extraction Controls and Apply Quantitative Decontamination
* **WHY:** In low-biomass tissues (~0.002 mg bacterial DNA/g tissue), reagent contaminants (*Burkholderia*, *Acinetobacter*, *Paenibacillus*) and database/index-hopping artifacts (*Thioalkalivibrio*, *Pseudoalteromonas*) dominate sequencing libraries even when negative control gels appear blank by eye.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact read counts and taxonomic profiles recovered from all non-template extraction blanks (1 per 11 samples) in both 454 16S and Illumina HiSeq WGS runs, and filter out contaminant taxa before running PERMANOVA, Boruta, or LEfSe.
* **HOW:** Run the `decontam` R package (using both the frequency method based on PicoGreen DNA concentrations and the prevalence method comparing placental samples against the ~30 extraction blanks) on the 16S OTU table and WGS species table. Remove flagged reagent contaminants and re-evaluate whether the preterm birth ($P = 0.001$) and antenatal infection ($P = 0.006$) PERMANOVA signals persist.

#### 3. Test and Control for Extraction Batch, Kit Lot, and Overlap Between Case-Cohorts
* **WHY:** Because the two case-cohorts (preterm birth and remote antenatal infection) were constructed with overlapping subjects (lines 27, 112) and processed across multiple extraction batches/runs, batch effects or cross-cohort confounding can drive apparent clustering in PCoA and heatmaps (Figs. 3 and 4).
* **WHAT:** Include DNA extraction batch, sequencing run ID, and co-occurring case status (preterm birth $\times$ antenatal infection) as covariates in multifactorial PERMANOVA (`adonis2`) models.
* **HOW:** Run `vegan::adonis2(dist_matrix ~ Extraction_Batch + Sequencing_Run + Preterm_Birth + Antenatal_Infection + Delivery_Mode, data = metadata, by = "margin")` on both weighted and unweighted UniFrac matrices for the $n = 320$ cohort and report the marginal $R^2$ and $P$-values for each factor.

#### 4. Temper Claims of a Viable Resident Microbiome and Hematogenous Oral Seeding
* **WHY:** Metagenomic DNA fragments in $>99\%$ host-DNA tissue homogenates do not prove the presence of live, metabolically active bacteria in utero, nor does phylum-level Bray-Curtis similarity to nonpregnant HMP oral samples prove hematogenous seeding from the maternal mouth.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Abstract, Results, and Discussion to distinguish between low-level microbial DNA signatures and a metabolically active resident microbiome, and explicitly caveat the antibiotic confounding in the UTI cohort.
* **HOW:** Reframe claims of "metabolic activity of the placental microbiome" (Fig. 3C caption, line 59) as "predicted functional gene carriage inferred from DNA reads," remove *Burkholderia*, *Paenibacillus*, *Thioalkalivibrio*, and *Pseudoalteromonas* as putative placental commensals, and state that oral-placental seeding remains an untested hypothesis in the absence of paired maternal oral and vaginal swabs from the same 320 gravidae.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Typographical errors in $P$-values and incomplete methodological specifications hinder reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Typo in birth weight $P$-value (Results, line 27):** Fix `"P = 0 001"` to `"P = 0.001"`.
  * **Clarify MG-RAST alignment length units (Materials and Methods, line 143):** Specify that `"minimum alignment length cutoff of 15"` refers to 15 amino acids (in translated BLASTX against M5NR) and justify why such a short, high-$e$-value ($e \le 1$) threshold was used for 150-bp paired-end Illumina reads.
  * **Report exact sample sizes in PCoA and heatmaps (Figs. 3 and 4):** Specify the exact number of cases and controls plotted in Fig. 3A, Fig. 3C, and Fig. 4A after excluding samples that failed the 5% OTU prevalence filter or had overlapping preterm/infection diagnoses.
