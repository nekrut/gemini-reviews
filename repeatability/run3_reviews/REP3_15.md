### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript constructs a 13-gene "necroptosis-related lncRNA signature" (NRLS) for glioma prognosis using public transcriptomic datasets from TCGA ($n = 663$, pooled LGG and GBM) and GTEx normal brain as the discovery cohort and CGGA ($n = 1,018$, pooled `mRNAseq_693` and `mRNAseq_325`) as the validation cohort. The authors identify 20 differentially expressed necroptosis-related genes between merged TCGA-Glioma and GTEx-Brain data, filter for lncRNAs correlated at $|r| > 0.5$ ($P < 0.001$) and associated with overall survival in univariate Cox regression ($P < 0.001$) in both TCGA and CGGA, and apply LASSO Cox regression in TCGA to select 13 lncRNAs. They evaluate the resulting risk score via Kaplan-Meier curves, multivariate Cox regression, nomogram ROC curves (reporting 1-, 3-, and 5-year AUCs of 0.88, 0.92, and 0.86 in TCGA), GSEA, immune deconvolution (CIBERSORT, ESTIMATE, TIDE), `pRRophetic` drug sensitivity prediction, consensus clustering ($k = 2$), and qRT-PCR of 5 lncRNAs in 5 non-tumor epilepsy brain tissues and 10–11 glioma tissues. Public database links are provided, though custom analysis code is not deposited.

The study follows a formulaic bioinformatics template and suffers from severe methodological, statistical, and logical flaws: (1) target leakage between the training and validation cohorts during feature pre-selection, where candidate lncRNAs were required to pass univariate Cox survival screening ($P < 0.001$) and correlation filtering in *both* TCGA and CGGA before fitting the LASSO model in TCGA; (2) improper application of `DESeq2` to a count matrix pre-processed with `limma::removeBatchEffect()`; (3) pooling biologically distinct diseases—WHO Grade II/III lower-grade glioma (LGG, largely IDH-mutant) and Grade IV glioblastoma (GBM, largely IDH-wildtype)—without comparing the 13-lncRNA signature against a clinical baseline model (age, grade, IDH status, 1p/19q codeletion); and (4) a direct self-contradiction between Section 3.6 and Section 3.8 regarding whether high-risk / Cluster 1 patients are *less* or *more* susceptible to immunotherapy. Substantial re-analysis removing CGGA from feature selection, stratifying by WHO 2021 molecular subtypes, and correcting internal contradictions is required.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Identification of 20 differentially expressed necroptosis-related genes between TCGA glioma and GTEx normal brain and 43 prognostic NRLs: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Materials and Methods (Sections 2.1 and 2.2, lines 49–54), the authors state that RNA-seq count matrices of TCGA-Glioma and GTEx-Brain were merged using `limma::removeBatchEffect()` and then analyzed with `DESeq2` ($|\log_2\text{FC}| > 2$, adjusted $P < 0.05$). `limma::removeBatchEffect()` is a linear model subtraction designed for continuous log-transformed expression values and produces non-integer (and negative) numbers; feeding batch-subtracted continuous values into `DESeq2` violates `DESeq2`'s core requirement for raw negative-binomial integer counts. Furthermore, in Section 2.2 (line 54), the 43 candidate lncRNAs were selected by intersecting Pearson correlations ($|r| > 0.5$) and univariate Cox regressions ($P < 0.001$) across *both* TCGA and CGGA cohorts before running LASSO in TCGA. Using the CGGA validation cohort to pre-select survival-associated features ($P < 0.001$) constitutes direct data leakage and invalidates CGGA as an independent validation set.

* **The 13-lncRNA signature (NRLS) is a robust independent prognostic factor with high predictive accuracy (AUCs 0.88–0.92 in TCGA, 0.77–0.84 in CGGA): [Overstated].**  
  By pooling lower-grade gliomas (LGG, median survival 5–10+ years) and glioblastoma (GBM, median survival ~15 months) into a single cohort, any transcriptomic signature that tracks tumor grade and IDH mutation status automatically achieves large hazard ratios and high AUCs (~0.85–0.90) in the combined LGG+GBM cohort. In Section 3.3 (line 133 and Figure 4), the authors report ROC AUCs only for the combined model (or risk score across the pooled LGG+GBM cohort) without comparing the C-index or time-dependent AUC of `Clinical + NRLS` against a `Clinical-only` baseline model (Age + WHO Grade + IDH mutation + 1p/19q codeletion), nor do they report time-dependent ROC AUCs separately within IDH-wildtype GBM. Moreover, dichotomizing the continuous risk score using an "optimal cut-off value" via `survminer` (line 67) inflates Type I error and Kaplan-Meier separation.

* **The NRLS risk score and NRL-based consensus clusters consistently stratify glioma immunotherapy susceptibility: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The manuscript directly contradicts itself across two consecutive sections. In Section 3.6 (line 160; Figure 6e–g), the authors find that the high-risk group has higher immune checkpoint expression, higher immune dysfunction, and significantly higher TIDE scores, concluding: *"These results suggested that the low-risk group is more susceptible to immunotherapy."* Two paragraphs later, in Section 3.8 (line 182; Figure 8d–e), after showing that Cluster 1 corresponds to the high-risk group with worse survival and significantly higher risk scores (`Fig S5`), the authors write: *"We observed that Cluster 1 has stronger immune infiltration than Cluster 2. It might result in different immunotherapeutic responses. Therefore, we could consider Cluster 1 was more susceptible to immunotherapy."* Claiming that the low-risk group is more susceptible to immunotherapy in Section 3.6 and that the high-risk group (Cluster 1) is more susceptible in Section 3.8 is logically incoherent.

* **High-risk glioma patients are more sensitive to six chemotherapeutic agents (A-443654, Gefitinib, Temsirolimus, Trametinib, Bortezomib, Pazopanib): [Fragile].**  
  In Section 3.7 (line 171; Figure 7), drug sensitivity is imputed computationally from bulk tumor RNA-seq using `pRRophetic` ridge regression trained on GDSC cancer cell lines. Bulk glioma tissue RNA-seq is heavily confounded by non-neoplastic stromal and microglial/macrophage infiltration (which correlates positively with the NRLS risk score, Figure 5g–i), and none of the six highlighted agents (e.g., Pazopanib, Gefitinib, A-443654) are standard-of-care chemotherapies for glioma, while temozolomide (the actual standard-of-care alkylating agent mentioned in the Introduction, line 31) is omitted from the analysis.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Validation Leakage by Performing All Feature Selection Strictly Within TCGA
* **WHY:** Pre-filtering lncRNAs by requiring univariate Cox $P < 0.001$ and $|r| > 0.5$ in *both* TCGA and CGGA (Section 2.2, line 54) leaks survival outcomes from the validation cohort into the training pipeline.
* **WHAT:** Restrict all correlation screening, univariate Cox filtering, LASSO tuning, and cut-off selection strictly to the TCGA training cohort, lock the model coefficients and median threshold, and evaluate the locked model once in CGGA.
* **HOW:** Re-run the univariate Cox filter ($P < 0.001$) and `glmnet::cv.glmnet` LASSO strictly on the 663 TCGA patients. Use the TCGA median risk score (rather than an outcome-optimized `survminer` cutpoint) to stratify CGGA (`mRNAseq_693` and `mRNAseq_325`, batch-corrected or evaluated separately) and report the resulting unbiased log-rank $P$-values and AUCs.

#### 2. Fix the TCGA-GTEx Differential Expression Pipeline and Benchmark Against Clinical Baselines Within WHO 2021 Subtypes
* **WHY:** Applying `limma::removeBatchEffect()` before `DESeq2` violates count distribution assumptions, and evaluating survival AUCs on pooled LGG+GBM conflates the signature's predictive power with known differences between Grade II/III IDH-mutant gliomas and Grade IV IDH-wildtype glioblastomas.
* **WHAT:** Use raw integer counts with study batch included in the `DESeq2` design formula (or use `limma-voom`), and report the incremental C-index and 1/3/5-year AUC of the NRLS signature over standard clinical variables (Age + Grade + IDH + 1p/19q) both in the full cohort and separately within IDH-wildtype GBM and IDH-mutant LGG.
* **HOW:** In `timeROC` / `survival`, compute and plot ROC curves side-by-side for (a) `NRLS alone`, (b) `Clinical variables alone (Age + Grade + IDH + 1p/19q)`, and (c) `Clinical + NRLS` in TCGA and CGGA, and run a likelihood ratio test comparing the nested Cox models (b) vs. (c).

#### 3. Resolve the Internal Contradiction Regarding Immunotherapy Susceptibility
* **WHY:** Section 3.6 concludes that the *low-risk* group is more susceptible to immunotherapy based on lower TIDE scores, whereas Section 3.8 concludes that *Cluster 1 (the high-risk group)* is more susceptible to immunotherapy based on higher immune infiltration.
* **WHAT:** Reconcile Sections 3.6 and 3.8 by evaluating TIDE scores across Cluster 1 and Cluster 2 and acknowledging that high myeloid/M2-macrophage and Treg infiltration in high-risk gliomas reflects an immunosuppressed, T-cell-dysfunctional microenvironment rather than responsiveness to immune checkpoint blockade.
* **HOW:** Compute TIDE dysfunction/exclusion scores for Cluster 1 vs. Cluster 2, remove the contradictory claim at the end of Section 3.8 (line 182), and include temozolomide (`TMZ`) in the `pRRophetic` drug sensitivity analysis alongside *MGMT* promoter methylation status.

#### 4. Reconcile Sample Size Discrepancies and Add Statistical Tests to the qRT-PCR Validation
* **WHY:** Section 2.9 (line 97) lists *"five non-tumor brain tissues, three WHO grade 2, three WHO grade 2 [sic], and four GBM"* (which equals 5 normal + 10 glioma = 15 samples, with "WHO grade 2" duplicated instead of Grade 3), whereas Figure 9's caption (line 199) states *"5 non-tumor brain tissues and 11 glioma tissues"*, and no statistical test or $P$-value method for Figure 9 is described in Section 2.9.
* **WHAT:** Clarify the exact number of WHO Grade 2, Grade 3, and Grade 4 (GBM) tissue specimens assayed by qRT-PCR and report Kruskal-Wallis / Dunn's post-hoc $P$-values across grades.
* **HOW:** Correct Section 2.9 and Figure 9 to state the exact sample counts per WHO grade, plot individual sample data points overlaid on the bar charts in Figure 9a–e, and report two-sided non-parametric test $P$-values.
