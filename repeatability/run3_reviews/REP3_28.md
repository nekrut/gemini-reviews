### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates the adaptive recovery of *Methylobacterium extorquens* AM1 strains carrying six engineered synonymous alleles of the essential formaldehyde-activating enzyme gene (`fae`: AF, AR, RN, VA, CO, and AC, containing 46–150 synonymous substitutions across 186 codons) during experimental evolution on methylamine as a sole carbon source. Across 55 replicate populations evolved at two effective population sizes in two independent laboratories for 60–250 generations, 48 populations acquired a single point mutation either within the first 14 coding codons of `fae` (9 distinct mutations across AF, AC, and VA, 4 of which were synonymous) or in the immediate upstream noncoding region (6 mutations across AR, CO, and RN), showing high nucleotide-level parallelism (e.g., an identical synonymous SNP fixed in all 8 AF populations). Using scarless chromosomal allelic exchange (`snp` and `bkg` strains; Figure 3A, Supplementary Figure S4), qPCR, anti-FLAG Western blots, enzyme activity assays, a 13-residue N-terminal tag insertion (Figure 4), a 37-mutant plasmid library testing predicted 5' mRNA minimum folding energy (`RNAfold` MFE) and internal Shine–Dalgarno (anti-SD) affinity (Figure 5), and 12 intragenic cross-swaps (Figure 6), the authors show that single N-terminal point mutations—including synonymous ones—drive large fitness rescues (from zero growth to ~75% of WT in AF and AC) in an allele-specific, epistatic manner that is not predicted by 5' MFE or internal anti-SD binding models. Strain identifiers (`CM2563`, `CM2006`), vectors (`pHC115`, `pLC291`), and Supplementary Tables S1–S5 are provided, though raw microplate growth trajectories, qPCR data, and Western blot quantifications are not deposited in an external public repository (`[ACCESSION_REDACTED in scrubbed input]`).

This is a well-designed experimental evolution study that pairs cross-laboratory replication with clean chromosomal allelic replacements (`snp` and `bkg`) and systematic mutational perturbations to test competing mechanistic models of synonymous selection. The main methodological caveats are: (1) a shift in genetic context between the chromosomal evolution/reconstruction assays (Figures 1–3) and the plasmid-based mechanistic and cross-swap assays (`pHC115` in Figure 4 and `pLC291` in Figures 5–6), where plasmid-specific 5' UTR sequences (−50 to −1 bp) can alter the actual −50 to +50 bp 5' mRNA folding landscape relative to the native chromosomal locus; (2) physiological decoupling in the expression assays for non-growing ancestors (AF and AC), where mRNA and protein were measured after a 2-hour methylamine pulse in succinate-grown cultures (Figure 3B–D) rather than during steady-state growth on methylamine (Figure 3A); and (3) low statistical power when testing for differences between synonymous ($n=4$) and nonsynonymous ($n=4$) coding mutations, plus several Figure 5 panel cross-reference errors in the main text and legend. All of these issues can be resolved through computational recalculations of plasmid-context MFE, explicit statistical power reporting, and textual revisions.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Single point mutations in the 5' N-terminal coding region (including four synonymous mutations) and upstream noncoding region of `fae` repeatedly fix under strong positive selection and are sufficient to explain the evolved fitness recovery: [Well Supported].**  
  Independent evolution across two laboratories on different continents rules out cross-contamination, sequencing of founding colonies rules out standing variation, and scarless chromosomal allelic replacements (`snp` and `bkg` strains for 10 of 15 mutations, including 8 of 9 coding SNPs; Figure 3A and Supplementary Figure S4A) demonstrate that the focal `fae` point mutations account for nearly 100% of the evolved growth rate gains.

* **Standard computational models of 5' mRNA minimum folding energy (MFE) and internal anti-Shine–Dalgarno (anti-SD) binding affinity fail to predict the fitness effects of engineered or evolved point mutations in the 5' region of `fae`: [Well Supported].**  
  Testing 37 engineered point mutations across WT, AF, VA, and AC (Figure 5E–J, Supplementary Figure S9) and conducting sensitivity analyses across temperatures (Supplementary Figure S11), dangling-end parameters (Supplementary Figure S12), and full 16S rRNA tail sequences (Supplementary Figure S13) show no correlation between predicted ΔMFE or Δanti-SD affinity and growth rate. One caveat requiring verification is whether the −50 to +50 bp `RNAfold` window in Figure 5 used the native chromosomal 5' UTR or the `pLC291` plasmid 5' UTR present in the experimental assay.

* **The fitness benefit of evolved N-terminal coding mutations depends on intragenic epistatic interactions with the rest of the recoded `fae` sequence: [Well Supported].**  
  Because all strains share an identical `CM2563` genomic background and expression vector (`pLC291`), the finding that only 2 of 12 cross-swapped evolved mutations outperform the native evolved alleles—and that 0 of 9 tested mutations (including 4 beneficial SNPs from AF and VA) rescue the AC allele (Figure 6)—directly demonstrates intragenic epistasis within `fae`.

* **Phenotypic impacts on mRNA, protein production, and fitness do not differ between beneficial synonymous and nonsynonymous mutations ($P > 0.2$): [Fragile].**  
  While the four synonymous coding mutations clearly confer large fitness gains comparable in range to the nonsynonymous coding mutations, comparing $n=4$ synonymous versus $n=4$ nonsynonymous evolved mutations (or small subgroup sizes in Supplementary Figures S5 and S10) with an unpaired Student's $t$-test across heterogeneous ancestral backgrounds (AF, AC, VA) has insufficient statistical power to establish equivalence; failing to reject the null ($P > 0.2$) should not be framed as proof of equal effect distributions.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Verify and Recalculate −50 to +50 bp MFE Using the Exact Plasmid 5' UTR Sequence (`pLC291` and `pHC115`)
* **WHY:** In Figures 4 and 5, MFE is calculated over a 100 bp window (−50 to +50 bp relative to the `fae` start codon), which spans 50 nucleotides of the 5' UTR and 50 nucleotides of the coding sequence. However, the fitness assays in Figure 4 (`pHC115`, cumate-inducible) and Figures 5–6 (`pLC291`, anhydrotetracycline-inducible) were performed on inducible plasmids whose upstream 5' UTR sequences differ from the native chromosomal `fae` 5' UTR used in Figures 1–3. Base-pairing between the plasmid 5' UTR and the first 50 coding nucleotides can substantially change ΔMFE predictions.
* **WHAT:** Confirm whether the MFE values in Figures 4 and 5 were computed with the chromosomal 5' UTR or the plasmid (`pHC115`/`pLC291`) 5' UTR, and report both.
* **HOW:** Re-run `RNAfold` (ViennaRNA v2.0 at 30°C) using the exact 50 bp upstream transcribed 5' UTR sequence of `pLC291` (for Figure 5) and `pHC115` (for Figure 4) concatenated to the first 50 bp of each `fae` allele, and add a supplementary scatter plot showing growth rate versus plasmid-context MFE and ΔMFE.

#### 2. Quantify Detection Limits and Steady-State vs. Pulse-Induction Effects for AF and AC Protein Levels
* **WHY:** In Figure 3B–D, eAF and eAC alleles show tiny, statistically non-significant changes in mRNA and Western blot protein levels after a 2-hour methylamine pulse in succinate-grown cultures, yet confer a jump from zero growth to ~75% of WT growth rate on methylamine alone (Figure 3A). Because ancestral AF and AC cannot grow on methylamine, comparing 2-hour transition measurements in succinate against steady-state methylamine growth rates confounds induction kinetics with steady-state expression.
* **WHAT:** Clarify the lower limit of detection (LOD) of the anti-FLAG Western blot and NADPH enzyme assay, and compare evolved `snp` strains against WT during steady-state growth on methylamine.
* **HOW:** Report the quantitative LOD and signal-to-noise ratio on Figure 3C and 3D for ancestral AF and AC (which had undetectable FAE protein), and plot the steady-state methylamine protein levels of the viable evolved `snp` strains (which *can* grow on methylamine alone, already measured for Figure 3H) directly alongside their steady-state growth rates.

#### 3. Replace Underpowered Null-Equivalence Claims with Effect-Size Confidence Intervals
* **WHY:** With only 4 synonymous and 4–5 nonsynonymous evolved coding mutations distributed across three distinct ancestral alleles (AF, AC, VA), a Student's $t$-test ($P > 0.2$, line 39 and Supplementary Figure S5) has high Type II error and cannot rule out substantial differences between mutational classes.
* **WHAT:** Report 95% confidence intervals for the mean difference (and non-parametric Mann–Whitney $U$ / permutation statistics) between synonymous and nonsynonymous mutations in Supplementary Figures S5 and S10.
* **HOW:** Calculate the 95% CI on the difference in growth rate, mRNA, protein, and enzyme activity between synonymous and nonsynonymous SNPs (both raw and normalized to the respective ancestral allele) and rephrase line 39 to state that both classes span a similar empirical range of large beneficial effects rather than claiming statistical equivalence from $P > 0.2$.

#### 4. Account for the Five Unreconstructed Evolved Mutations in Discussion of Parallelism
* **WHY:** The authors successfully constructed chromosomal `snp` replacements for 10 of 15 `fae`-associated mutations (line 37), leaving 5 mutations (1 coding and 4 noncoding, including the high-frequency upstream mutation in RN) without direct chromosomal `snp` verification.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly list in the main text (lines 37–39) which 5 of the 15 evolved mutations could not be reconstructed chromosomally and why cloning/recombination failed for those constructs.
* **HOW:** Add a brief parenthetical in line 37 specifying the exact allele identifiers (from Figure 2A/B) of the 10 reconstructed and 5 unreconstructed `snp` strains, and note whether the `bkg` replacement (replacing the evolved `fae` with ancestral `fae` in the evolved clone, Supplementary Figure S4A) was completed for any of those 5 lineages.
