### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript combines forward epidemiological-evolutionary simulations (`FAVITES`) and Bayesian phylogeographic inference (incorporating air passenger traffic covariates via a generalized linear model and unsampled ghost lineages inferred from incidence data across 12 undersampled regions) to reconstruct the establishment of the earliest sustained SARS-CoV-2 transmission networks in North America (Washington state, `A.1` clade) and Europe (Northern Italy, `B.1` clade). By simulating 61-day viral epidemics seeded by `WA1` (sampled 15 January 2020) under realistic doubling times ($4.7\text{ days}$, 95% range $4.2\text{--}5.1\text{ days}$) and substitution rates ($0.8 \times 10^{-3}\text{ subs/site/year}$), the authors test whether the observed Washington outbreak phylogeny—which lacked basal siblings or intermediate genotypes between `WA1` and the `C17747T`/`A17858G`-defined outbreak clade—could have descended from `WA1`. Across 1,000 simulations sampling 294 genomes (and later 1,000 genomes accounting for the late-sampled `WA-S566` lineage), they reject direct descent from `WA1` ($p < 0.001$ to $p = 0.010$) and similarly reject the hypothesis that the German `BavPat1` outbreak seeded the Northern Italian `B.1` epidemic ($p = 0.004$). Furthermore, they forensically demonstrate that 16 British Columbia genomes ostensibly carrying the ancestral `C17747` allele alongside `A17858G` are amplicon-sequencing artifacts caused by misincorporation of the `ARTIC` `nCoV2019_58_RIGHT` primer rather than genuine evolutionary intermediates. Their Bayesian Markov jump reconstructions date the independent founding introduction of the Washington outbreak clade from Hubei/Zhejiang to ~1 February 2020 (95% HPD: 14 January – 15 February) and the independent introduction from Hubei to Northern Italy to ~28 January 2020 (95% HPD: 20 January – 6 February), which subsequently seeded the New York City outbreak around 12 February 2020.

This is an exemplary genomic epidemiology study that pairs hypothesis-driven null simulations with Bayesian phylodynamics and sequence-level forensic quality control. Rather than relying on naive visual inspection of a single maximum-likelihood tree of low-diversity viral sequences, the authors formally quantify topological probabilities under forward coalescent/transmission models, identify a pervasive primer-trimming sequencing artifact (`nCoV2019_58_RIGHT`) that would otherwise falsify the evolutionary trajectory, and test sensitivity across doubling times, substitution rates, and time-homogeneous vs. time-inhomogeneous phylogeographic priors with and without unsampled taxa. The minor limitations are already transparently acknowledged in the text: low sequence diversity limits certainty regarding exact source provinces within China (Hubei vs. Zhejiang) and produces occasional implausible MCC transitions (such as a U.S.-to-Malaysia branch in Figure 4), and the time-homogeneous covariate model does not explicitly parameterize the early February suspension of non-U.S. resident travel from China.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The sustained Washington state (`A.1`) and Northern Italy (`B.1`) outbreaks were initiated by independent viral introductions from China in late January / early February 2020 rather than descending from the earliest detected local cases (`WA1` and `BavPat1`): [Well Supported].**  
  Using `FAVITES` epidemic and sequence evolution simulations matched to empirical sample sizes ($N = 294$ and $N = 1,000$ in Washington; $N = 27$ in Italy), the authors show that an outbreak founded by `WA1` on 15 January produces basal polytomies in 99.7% of simulations ($p = 0.003$) and sibling lineages diverging prior to two mutations in 100% of simulations ($p < 0.001$), whereas 0 of 1,000 simulations matched the observed tree topology (Figure 2). Even after incorporating the single basal lineage `WA-S566` sampled on 29 March into a 1,000-genome sample, 99.0% of simulations produce three or more basal lineages ($p = 0.010$). Similarly, the absence of a basal polytomy in the Italian clade relative to `BavPat1` rejects a German origin ($p = 0.004$), corroborated by Bayesian phylogeographic support ($\text{pp} = 0.84$ for independent introductions from China vs. $\text{pp} = 0.16$ for Germany-to-Italy; Figure 5).

* **Apparent intermediate `A.1` genomes in British Columbia and U.S. states carrying the ancestral `C17747` nucleotide alongside `A17858G` are sequencing artifacts from `ARTIC` primer `nCoV2019_58_RIGHT` rather than genuine evolutionary intermediates: [Well Supported].**  
  This is a textbook forensic bioinformatics finding (Figure 3, Table S2). By showing that six `C17747` genomes from British Columbia (and additional genomes from California, Oregon, Wyoming, and Minnesota) share 1 to 4 derived downstream synapomorphies with locally co-circulating `17747T + 17858G` clade members—a pattern that would require multiple independent homoplasies or reversions at the exact binding site of `ARTIC` primer `nCoV2019_58_RIGHT`—the authors conclusively demonstrate that `C17747` in these sequences is a primer-trimming/reference-calling artifact.

* **Early public health containment successfully extinguished the initial `WA1` (U.S.) and `BavPat1` (Bavaria, Germany) introductions, while sustained transmission in Washington state began ~2 weeks later (~1 February 2020) than assumed under a `WA1`-origin model: [Well Supported].**  
  Bayesian Markov jump timing estimates place the founding introduction of the Washington outbreak clade at 1 February 2020 (95% HPD: 14 January – 15 February) with unsampled taxa, or 26 January 2020 (95% HPD: 15 January – 7 February) under a time-inhomogeneous model without unsampled taxa, and 7 February 2020 (95% HPD: 23 January – 18 February) when treating `WA-S566`/`NY` as a separate introduction (Figures 4, S5, S6). This timeline is independently corroborated by the Seattle Flu Study's retrospective testing of 6,908 archived respiratory swabs from January–February 2020, which detected 0 positives in January and early February and the first 10 positives only at the end of February.

* **Bayesian GLM phylogeography with unsampled taxa resolves the precise sub-national geographic source and downstream international dispersal paths of the `A.1` clade: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the Hubei vs. Europe/U.S. origin inferences are robust, the authors candidly note (lines 33–37) that low genetic diversity produces topological uncertainty in the MCC tree: Zhejiang clusters with the WA clade in the point-estimate MCC tree (Figure 4) even though Hubei has higher marginal posterior probability across the full posterior distribution, and the MCC tree places a Malaysian genome as a descendant of the U.S. `A.1` introduction rather than a direct export from China. The authors appropriately flag these nuances in the main text.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Incorporate Time-Varying Travel Restrictions Directly into the Unsampled-Taxa GLM Phylogeographic Model
* **WHY:** As noted on lines 35 and 39, the primary phylogeographic reconstruction incorporating unsampled lineages assumes time-homogeneous covariate effect sizes and does not explicitly model the sharp curtailment of air passenger traffic out of Wuhan/Hubei after 23 January 2020 or the U.S. restriction on non-resident travel from China starting 2 February 2020, whereas the time-inhomogeneous sensitivity model (Figures S4–S5) was run *without* unsampled taxa.
* **WHAT:** Combine the time-inhomogeneous epoch GLM (allowing air traffic matrices and Hubei outflow rates to change across pre-lockdown January vs. post-lockdown February epochs) with the unsampled-taxa dataset.
* **HOW:** Specify a two-epoch GLM diffusion matrix in `BEAST` (with epoch boundary at 23 January / 2 February 2020) on the full alignment including the 12 undersampled location ghost lineages, and report whether this reconciles the 6-day difference in mean tMRCA/introduction date between the homogeneous unsampled model (1 February) and the inhomogeneous sampled-only model (26 January).

#### 2. Quantify the Sensitivity of `FAVITES` Rejections to Preferential / Non-Uniform Diagnostic Sampling
* **WHY:** The `FAVITES` simulation analysis subsamples 294 (or 1,000) viral genomes uniformly at random (or temporally matched) from the simulated infected population, whereas real clinical sequencing in Washington state in late February and March 2020 was often clustered around localized outbreaks (e.g., long-term care facilities in King/Snohomish counties) and hospital admissions. Clustered sampling can reduce the number of captured basal lineages relative to uniform random sampling.
* **WHAT:** Test whether moderate spatial/transmission-chain clustering in sequence sampling alters the probability of failing to sample basal sibling lineages in the `FAVITES` simulations.
* **HOW:** Instead of drawing 294 tips uniformly at random across all simulated transmissions, subsample tips with overdispersed cluster weights (e.g., drawing 50% of sequences from random transmission subtrees and 50% uniformly) and report the updated $p$-values for patterns (i)–(iv) in Figure 2.
