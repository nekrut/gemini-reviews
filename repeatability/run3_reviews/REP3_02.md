### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study examines the variation in raw codon usage frequencies across 1,484 complete proteobacterial genomes from RefSeq (evaluating both whole-genome coding sequences and 13 highly expressed genes) and tests whether the presence of tRNA anticodon modification enzymes explains differences in codon usage variability and genomic GC content (GC%). Using `Roary` and `Scoary` on 25 high-GC ($>67\%$) and 25 low-GC ($<32\%$) genomes followed by `RPSBlast` domain profiling across all 1,484 species, the authors report that (i) certain codons exhibit narrow cross-species variance in raw usage frequency ("low variability codons" such as Asn `AAC`, Arg `CGA`/`AGG`, and Gly `GGA`) whose usage is largely flat across GC%, and (ii) the presence of genes involved in synthesizing threonylcarbamoyl-adenosine ($t^6\text{A}$: `tsaB`, `tsaE`), glutamyl-queuosine ($\text{GluQ}$: `gluQ`, `tgt`), and 2-thiouridine ($s^2\text{U}$: `tusE`) correlates with shifts in synonymous codon frequencies and higher genomic GC%. All datasets, supplementary tables, and custom Perl scripts are publicly available in an institutional repository (`doi:10.34691/UCHILE/AYDRZL`).

While the comparative genomic dataset is broad and the authors perform careful `tblastn` checks for false-negative motif hits, the manuscript's two central conclusions rest on unaddressed statistical and evolutionary confounders. First, all comparative tests across the 1,484 genomes (Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney and Kruskal–Wallis tests in Figures 3–4) treat bacterial species as statistically independent observations, ignoring phylogenetic non-independence across clades where small, AT-rich endosymbiont genomes co-inherit both low GC% and the loss of non-essential tRNA modification genes. Second, the identification of "low variability codons" (Figures 1–2 and 5) relies on raw, unnormalized genome-wide codon frequencies ($f_c$) rather than relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU). Because total proteome amino acid frequencies shift strongly with genomic GC% (AT-rich proteomes are enriched for Asn, Lys, Ile, Phe, and Tyr, whereas GC-rich proteomes are enriched for Gly, Ala, Arg, and Pro), raw codon frequency $f_c = f_{\text{AA}} \times w_{c|\text{AA}}$ conflates synonymous codon preference with proteome-wide amino acid composition and mean-variance scaling. Re-analyzing the data with phylogenetic comparative methods and separating amino acid abundance from synonymous choice are doable using the authors' existing dataset and would place the findings on solid ground.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **A specific subset of codons (such as Asn `AAC`, Arg `CGA`/`AGG`, and Gly `GGA`) is evolutionarily restricted to a narrow range of usage independent of genomic GC%: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The authors reject normalizing codon frequencies by amino acid usage (Section 2.1, line 31) and classify codons by the unnormalized standard deviation (SD) and 90th–10th percentile range of raw codon frequencies $f_c$, as well as the linear slope of $f_c$ versus GC% (Figures 1, 2, and 5). This metric introduces two mathematical artifacts:
  1. *Mean-variance scaling:* Because $f_c \ge 0$, rare codons with low mean usage across proteobacteria (such as Arg `CGA` and `AGG`, which average $<0.3\%$ of all codons) are mathematically bounded near zero and cannot exhibit raw SDs or linear slopes comparable to abundant codons (such as Leu `CTG` or Ala `GCC`, which reach $5\text{--}8\%$ of all codons).
  2. *Opposing gradients of amino acid composition and synonymous preference:* For Asn (`AAT` and `AAC`), AT-rich genomes have both a higher total proteome fraction of Asn ($f_{\text{Asn}}$ increases in low-GC genomes) and a higher synonymous preference for `AAT` ($w_{\text{AAT}|\text{Asn}}$ increases in low-GC genomes). Their product $f_{\text{AAT}} = f_{\text{Asn}} \cdot w_{\text{AAT}|\text{Asn}}$ therefore varies dramatically (from $0.2\%$ to $11.3\%$). Conversely, for `AAC`, as genomic GC% increases, total Asn abundance $f_{\text{Asn}}$ *decreases* while the synonymous share $w_{\text{AAC}|\text{Asn}}$ *increases*; these two opposing effects cancel in the product $f_{\text{AAC}} = f_{\text{Asn}} \cdot w_{\text{AAC}|\text{Asn}}$, producing an artificially narrow raw variance and a near-zero slope against GC% (Figure 5) even though synonymous preference for `AAC` over `AAT` increases strongly with GC%.

* **The presence of tRNA modification genes (`tsaB`, `tsaE`, `gluQ`, `tgt`, `tusE`) is significantly associated with altered codon usage frequencies across the 1,484 genomes: [Fragile].**  
  In Section 2.3 and Figure 3, the authors apply standard Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney tests ($p < 0.00005$) comparing genomes with and without each modification gene, treating all 1,484 genomes as independent draws. Because bacterial traits are shared through common descent—and the loss of `tsaB`, `tsaE`, and `gluQ` is concentrated in specific lineages of host-associated and streamlined bacteria with genomes $< 2\text{ Mbp}$ (Section 2.4, line 82)—ordinary rank-sum tests suffer from severe phylogenetic pseudoreplication. Without phylogenetic generalized least squares (PGLS) or Pagel's test of correlated evolution on the species tree, it is unclear whether these associations reflect independent evolutionary co-adaptation or clade-level confounding with genome reduction and AT mutational bias.

* **tRNA modification enzymes (`tsaB` and `gluQ`) shape the evolution of genomic GC content in proteobacteria: [Overstated].**  
  The Abstract (line 8) and Figure 4 caption (line 80: "Genes involved in $t^6\text{A}$ and GluQ formation impact the GC content in proteobacterial genomes") frame tRNA modifications as causal drivers of genome-wide GC%. Biologically, GluQRS (`gluQ`) modifies a single tRNA species ($\text{tRNA}^{\text{Asp}}$, which decodes only Asp `GAC`/`GAT`, representing $\sim 5\%$ of proteome codons); loss of a single tRNA modification cannot plausibly drive whole-genome GC content from $>60\%$ down to $<35\%$. Rather, as acknowledged as an alternative in the Discussion (line 111), obligate intracellular lifestyle and genome reduction (<2 Mbp) jointly cause loss of DNA repair enzymes (driving AT mutational bias and low GC%) and deletion of non-essential accessory tRNA modification genes (`tsaB`, `tsaE`, `gluQ`).

* **`Roary` pangenome clustering across 50 extreme-GC proteobacterial genomes identifies translation and tRNA modification genes associated with GC%: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While `Roary` at a $40\%$ BlastP identity threshold is not well suited for phylum-wide orthology inference (as the authors candidly note in Section 2.3, line 57), the authors appropriately treat it only as an initial heuristic screen and re-verify candidate pathways across all 1,484 genomes using CDD `RPSBlast` profiles and `tblastn` searches.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Decouple Synonymous Codon Preference from Proteome Amino Acid Composition and Mean Scaling
* **WHY:** Raw genome-wide codon frequency $f_c$ is the product of amino acid abundance $f_{\text{AA}}$ and relative synonymous codon usage $w_{c|\text{AA}}$. Because $f_{\text{Asn}}$, $f_{\text{Lys}}$, and $f_{\text{Ile}}$ decrease with GC% while $f_{\text{Gly}}$, $f_{\text{Ala}}$, and $f_{\text{Arg}}$ increase with GC%, raw SD and raw linear slopes $\text{d}f_c/\text{d}(\text{GC}\%)$ confound synonymous decoding constraints with proteome-level amino acid shifts and mean usage levels.
* **WHAT:** Recompute codon variability and GC% regression slopes using (i) Relative Synonymous Codon Usage ($\text{RSCU}_c$ or synonymous fraction $w_{c|\text{AA}} = f_c / f_{\text{AA}}$) and (ii) the coefficient of variation ($\text{CV}_c = \text{SD}(f_c) / \overline{f}_c$) to control for differences in mean usage.
* **HOW:** Using Supplementary Tables 7 and 8, calculate $w_{c|\text{AA}} = f_c / \sum_{c' \in \text{AA}} f_{c'}$ for each multi-codon amino acid in each of the 1,484 genomes. Generate side-by-side heatmaps (matching Figure 2) and GC%-slope plots (matching Figure 5) for raw $f_c$, $\text{CV}(f_c)$, and $w_{c|\text{AA}}$, and explicitly quantify how much of the apparent invariance of `AAC` is explained by the negative correlation between total Asn frequency $f_{\text{Asn}}$ and GC%.

#### 2. Control for Phylogenetic Non-Independence Across the 1,484 Genomes
* **WHY:** Treating 1,484 proteobacterial genomes as independent in Wilcoxon and Kruskal–Wallis tests (Figures 3 and 4) inflates Type I error rates when gene losses (`tsaB`, `gluQ`, `tusE`) and low GC% are phylogenetically clustered within streamlined clades.
* **WHAT:** Re-evaluate the associations between tRNA modification gene presence/absence, codon usage, and GC% using phylogenetic comparative methods on the 1,484-taxon phylogeny already constructed in Section 4.3.
* **HOW:** Load the species tree from Section 4.3 and Supplementary Tables 7–9 into R using the `ape` and `phylolm` packages. For each focal codon and for genomic GC%, fit a phylogenetic linear regression (`phylolm(codon_freq ~ gene_presence + log10(genome_size) + GC_content, phy = tree, model = "BM")` and `"OUfixedRoot"`). Report the phylogenetically corrected effect sizes and $P$-values alongside the unadjusted boxplots in Figures 3 and 4.

#### 3. Stratify by Genome Size and Control for Background GC% When Testing Codon Usage Shifts
* **WHY:** Because genomes lacking `tsaB` or `gluQ` have much lower genome-wide GC% ($<40\%$) and smaller genome sizes ($<2\text{ Mbp}$, Supplementary Figures 17–18), comparing AT-ending (`AAT`, `GAT`, `AAA`) and GC-ending (`AAC`, `GAC`, `AAG`) codon frequencies directly between gene-present and gene-absent groups (Figure 3) conflates background genomic GC% with specific tRNA decoding effects.
* **WHAT:** Test whether the presence of `tsaB`, `gluQ`, and `tusE` alters target codon usage *beyond* what is expected from background genomic GC% (or intergenic/4-fold degenerate GC3s) and genome size.
* **HOW:** Fit a multiple regression (and PGLS model) of target codon usage (or synonymous fraction $w_{\text{GAC}|\text{Asp}}$ for `gluQ`, $w_{\text{AAC}|\text{Asn}}$ for `tsaB`, and $w_{\text{AAA}|\text{Lys}}$ for `tusE`) on modification gene presence while controlling for genome-wide GC% (or GC% at non-target 4-fold degenerate sites) and genome size, and perform a matched-GC% subsample comparison within the $35\%\text{--}50\%$ GC range where gene-present and gene-absent distributions overlap.

#### 4. Temper Causal Claims Linking tRNA Modifications to Genome-Wide GC%
* **WHY:** Cross-sectional correlations cannot establish causal direction, and it is biochemically implausible that `gluQ` (which modifies only $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Asp}}$) drives whole-genome GC content.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Abstract, Figure 4 caption, and Discussion to present the co-occurrence of low GC%, small genome size, and loss of `tsaB`/`gluQ` as correlated evolutionary syndromes of genome reduction rather than directional causation from tRNA modification to genomic GC%.
* **HOW:** Replace "Genes involved in $t^6\text{A}$ and GluQ formation impact the GC content in proteobacterial genomes" in the Figure 4 caption (line 80) with "Presence of genes involved in $t^6\text{A}$ and GluQ formation is correlated with higher genomic GC content in proteobacteria," and revise the Abstract (line 8) to note that genome reduction and AT mutational bias may jointly drive both GC% reduction and the loss of accessory tRNA modification enzymes.
