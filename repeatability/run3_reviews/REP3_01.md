### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This prospective cohort study from the Tohoku Medical Megabank Community-Based Cohort (TMM CommCohort) investigates associations between genome-wide and pathway-specific polygenic risk scores (PRSs), 43 baseline nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) plasma metabolites, and incident type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) over a median 4.3-year follow-up in 12,461 Japanese adults (354 incident cases). Using external multi-ancestry GWAS summary statistics, the authors construct a genome-wide PRS and select eight pathway-specific PRSs in a discovery subcohort ($n = 9,801$, Type 2 survey) before evaluating incident T2DM in the validation subcohort ($n = 12,461$, Type 1 survey) via modified Poisson regression, logistic regression with restricted cubic splines, and causal mediation analysis. They report that the genome-wide PRS ($\text{RR} = 2.09$ per 1-SD) and the nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS ($\text{RR} = 1.32$ per 1-SD) are associated with incident T2DM, and that seven circulating metabolites—most prominently glucose—mediate 1–7% of the genome-wide PRS effect. Raw data and analytical code are not publicly deposited and are restricted to email requests.

While the study leverages a large non-European biobank and avoids discovery-target sample overlap in constructing the genome-wide PRS, its primary conclusions are undermined by severe methodological, biological, and reporting flaws. First, treating baseline plasma glucose as a causal "mediator" between genetic risk and incident T2DM—when T2DM is defined by blood glucose $\ge 200\text{ mg/dL}$ or $\text{HbA1c} \ge 6.5\%$ and baseline glycemic traits are already elevated in high-PRS individuals ($P < 0.001$)—conflates pre-existing baseline dysglycemia with a distinct downstream metabolic pathway. Second, all 43 metabolites were quantified from non-fasting blood samples without adjusting for fasting duration, heavily confounding amino acid, glycolytic, and ketone body concentrations with postprandial state. Third, the PRS–metabolite dose-response analysis reverses the biological direction of causality by modeling a dichotomized PRS as the binary outcome in a logistic regression with metabolites as predictors. Finally, the manuscript contains pervasive numerical contradictions across the Abstract, Article Highlights, Results, and Tables—including an impossible $P < 0.001$ reported for an estimate of $\text{RR} = 1.32$ ($95\%\text{ CI: } 1.03\text{--}1.70$, which corresponds to $P \approx 0.030$), point estimates lying outside their own $95\%$ confidence intervals in Tables 2 and 3, and three mutually inconsistent lists of mediating metabolites. Addressing these issues requires substantial statistical re-analysis and reframing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Genome-wide PRS is positively associated with incident T2DM ($\text{RR} = 2.09$, $95\%\text{ CI: } 1.68\text{--}2.60$ per 1-SD) independent of lifestyle covariates: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The genome-wide PRS is derived from external summary statistics without sample overlap and shows a strong association with incident T2DM in modified Poisson regression. However, out of 40,433 baseline Type 1 survey participants, 15,262 ($37.7\%$) were excluded due to loss to follow-up and 6,438 due to missing genotypes, creating substantial attrition bias that the Discussion (line 350) mischaracterizes as avoiding healthy volunteer bias.

* **The nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS is significantly associated with incident T2DM ($\text{RR} = 1.32$, $95\%\text{ CI: } 1.03\text{--}1.70$, $P < 0.001$): [Overstated].**  
  A relative risk of $1.32$ with a $95\%\text{ CI}$ of $1.03\text{--}1.70$ yields $\ln(\text{RR}) = 0.278$ and $\text{SE} = (\ln(1.70) - \ln(1.03))/3.92 = 0.128$, corresponding to a Wald statistic $Z = 2.17$ and a two-sided $P = 0.030$, not $P < 0.001$ as claimed in the Abstract (line 21). Because eight pathway-specific PRSs were tested in the validation cohort (six of which were null, line 194), an unadjusted $P = 0.030$ does not survive Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction ($q \approx 0.12 > 0.05$). Moreover, this pathway PRS showed no statistically significant association with any of the 43 circulating metabolites (line 210; Table 3).

* **Fourteen circulating metabolites are linearly and positively associated with the genome-wide PRS: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Statistical Analysis (line 120) and Figure 2 (line 216), the authors fit logistic regression models with restricted cubic splines using the PRS as the *outcome variable* and each circulating metabolite as the predictor. Because germline genetic variants precede circulating metabolites, modeling PRS as the dependent variable reverses the causal direction; furthermore, fitting a logistic regression required dichotomizing the continuous PRS at $+1\text{ SD}$ (Table 1: $n = 1,990$ vs. $n = 10,471$), discarding continuous genetic variation. In addition, the text contradicts itself on how many metabolites were associated with PRS: the Abstract (line 21) and Discussion (line 346) state 14 metabolites, but Results line 210 explicitly lists 19 metabolites as "14 metabolites including succinic acid, 3-hydroxyisobutyric acid, 2-aminobutyric acid, 3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid, methionine, 2-ketoisocaproic acid, creatine, 2-hydroxybutyric acid, cysteine, arginine, ornithine, tyrosine, histidine, leucine, lysine, valine, glutamine, glucose, and 3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid."

* **Seven metabolites (including glucose, alanine, valine, and branched-chain ketoacids) partially mediate 1–7% of the association between PRS and T2DM incidence: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This core claim fails on three grounds. First, identifying baseline NMR glucose ($7.0\%$ mediated, $95\%\text{ CI: } 3.0\text{--}15.0\%$) as a mediator of incident T2DM—which is diagnosed primarily by blood glucose $\ge 200\text{ mg/dL}$ or $\text{HbA1c} \ge 6.5\%$—is circular when baseline glucose and HbA1c are not adjusted for and are already elevated in the high-PRS group at baseline (Table 1, $P < 0.001$). Second, all metabolites were measured in non-fasting plasma without accounting for time since last meal. Third, the manuscript provides three contradictory lists of the seven mediating metabolites: Article Highlights (line 46) names "glucose, alanine, and valine" (yet Table 2 reports $0.0\%$ mediation for alanine $[\text{NIE} = 1.00, 95\%\text{ CI: } 0.99\text{--}1.01]$ and an NIE $95\%\text{ CI}$ including $1.00$ for valine $[0.99\text{--}1.01]$); the Abstract (line 21) includes 2-hydroxybutyric acid ($\text{NIE} = 1.01, 95\%\text{ CI: } 0.99\text{--}1.02$) and omits 3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid; and the Results (line 221) includes 3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid and omits 2-hydroxybutyric acid. Across Table 2, five of the claimed mediators have proportion-mediated $95\%$ CIs bounded at $0.0\%$, and no FDR correction is applied across the 43 mediation tests.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct the Direction and Functional Form of the PRS–Metabolite Regression Models
* **WHY:** Germline polygenic risk precedes circulating metabolite levels, and dichotomizing a standardized PRS to fit a logistic regression (Figure 2, line 120) reverses causality while sacrificing statistical power.
* **WHAT:** Re-estimate the associations between PRS and the 43 metabolites using linear regression (or generalized additive models with restricted cubic splines) where each continuous standardized metabolite is the dependent variable and the continuous PRS is the independent variable.
* **HOW:** For each metabolite $M_j$ ($j = 1, \dots, 43$), standardize $M_j$ (or log-transform skewed distributions prior to standardization) and fit $M_j = \beta_0 + f(\text{PRS}) + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top \mathbf{Z} + \varepsilon$ using `rms::ols` in R, where $f(\text{PRS})$ is a 3-knot restricted cubic spline of the continuous PRS and $\mathbf{Z}$ is the covariate vector. Report $\beta$ coefficients (SD change in metabolite per 1-SD increase in PRS), $95\%$ CIs, and Benjamini–Hochberg FDR $q$-values across all 43 metabolites.

#### 2. Remove Tautological Glycemic Mediation and Adjust for Baseline Glycemia and Fasting Time
* **WHY:** Including baseline plasma glucose as a mediator of a glucose- and HbA1c-defined outcome without controlling for baseline glycemic status conflates pre-existing subclinical hyperglycemia (prediabetes) with metabolic mediation, and non-fasting blood draws confound amino acid and ketone concentrations.
* **WHAT:** Exclude glucose from the set of non-glycemic metabolic mediators, adjust all metabolite–T2DM and mediation models for baseline HbA1c (and postprandial interval / hours since last meal), and conduct a sensitivity analysis excluding individuals with baseline prediabetes ($\text{HbA1c} \ge 5.7\%$).
* **HOW:** Add baseline HbA1c and hours since last meal (recorded at TMM blood draw) to the covariate vector in the modified Poisson and mediation regressions. Re-run the mediation models for the remaining 42 non-glucose metabolites, apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction to the natural indirect effect $P$-values across all 42 tests, and report both baseline-HbA1c-adjusted and prediabetes-excluded estimates.

#### 3. Propagate Imputation Uncertainty and Address Follow-Up Attrition Bias
* **WHY:** Single imputation via `missForest` (line 118) treats imputed values (such as physical activity, which has $17.4\%$ missingness in Table 1) as observed data, underestimating standard errors, while excluding 15,262 non-participants at follow-up introduces selection bias (Additional File 3).
* **WHAT:** Replace single imputation with multiple imputation or inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting (IPCW) to account for both covariate missingness and attrition between the baseline and follow-up surveys.
* **HOW:** Estimate the probability of follow-up participation among the eligible baseline Type 1 cohort ($n = 27,723$ with baseline genotype and metabolomics data) using a logistic regression on baseline age, sex, BMI, smoking, drinking, education, baseline glucose/HbA1c, and PRS. Multiply the modified Poisson regression weights by the stabilized inverse probability of follow-up participation, and compute bootstrap $95\%$ CIs (1,000 resamples) for the mediation direct, indirect, and proportion-mediated effects.

#### 4. Align Claims with Multiple-Testing-Corrected Statistical Support
* **WHY:** The nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS association ($\text{RR} = 1.32$, $95\%\text{ CI: } 1.03\text{--}1.70$, actual $P \approx 0.030$) does not pass FDR correction across the eight tested PRSs, and most claimed mediators have natural indirect effect CIs bordering or spanning $1.00$ ($0.0\%$ proportion mediated).
* **WHAT:** Temper the claims regarding the nitrogen compound transport PRS and the number of statistically supported mediating metabolites across the Abstract, Article Highlights, Results, and Discussion.
* **HOW:** Replace $P < 0.001$ with the exact $P$-value and FDR-adjusted $q$-value for the nitrogen compound transport PRS in the Abstract and Results, reframe it as a nominal/suggestive association that did not survive multiple-testing correction or show metabolite associations, and restrict mediation claims to non-glycemic metabolites whose natural indirect effects remain significant at $\text{FDR} < 0.05$ after baseline HbA1c adjustment.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Numerous numerical errors, impossible confidence intervals, and text-table contradictions undermine confidence in the data analysis pipeline.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Abstract (line 21):** Correct the $P$-value for the nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS ($\text{RR} = 1.32$, $95\%\text{ CI: } 1.03\text{--}1.70$) from $P < 0.001$ to $P = 0.030$, and reconcile the list of mediating metabolites with Results line 221 and Table 2.
  2. **Article Highlights (line 46):** Remove "alanine, and valine" as examples of mediating metabolites, since Table 2 shows alanine has $0.0\%$ mediation ($\text{NIE} = 1.00$) and valine has an NIE $95\%\text{ CI}$ of $0.99\text{--}1.01$.
  3. **Results (line 210):** Reconcile the statement "associated with 14 metabolites" with the 19 metabolites actually listed in that sentence, and fix the discrepancy between "glutamine" in the first sentence and "glutamic acid" in the second sentence.
  4. **Results (line 221):** Change "no metabolites showed a statistically significant natural direct effect (Table 3)" to "natural indirect effect," since the natural direct effects in Table 3 are $\sim 1.35$ (with CIs $> 1.00$) while all natural indirect effects span $1.00$.
  5. **Tables 2 and 3 (lines 232, 255, 299, 316, 324):** Audit the proportion-mediated calculation script to fix point estimates that fall outside their own $95\%$ CIs (Table 2 Succinic acid: `-1.0 (-0.4–1.0)`; Table 3 Acetic acid: `-1.0 (-0.9–3.0)`) and fix formatting typos (`-1.0–.0` for Glycerol in Table 2; `0.0 (-2.0–60)` for Serine and `-1.0 (-6.03.0)` for Lactic acid in Table 3).
  6. **Spelling and captions (lines 21, 199, 205, 210, 216, 257, 314):** Correct `3-methyl-2-oxiovaleric acid` to `3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid`, `3-Hyudroxybutyric acid` to `3-Hydroxybutyric acid`, and remove the duplicate `total energy intake` in the captions of Figures 1 and 2.
