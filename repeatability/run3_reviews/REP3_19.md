### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents version 7 of the MAFFT multiple sequence alignment (MSA) software package, summarizing new algorithmic options implemented since version 6 and demonstrating their utility and failure modes across three biological test cases. Key features include phylogeny-aware profile alignment (`--addprofile`), alignment extension with full-length (`--add`) or fragmentary (`--addfragments`) sequences via dynamic programming (`--multipair`) or shared 6-mer distance estimation (`--6merpair`), multi-threaded execution (`--thread`), automatic nucleotide strand adjustment (`--adjustdirection`), and structure-seeded alignment (`--seed` combined with ASH residue equivalence scores). Quantitative benchmarking is provided on the 16S.B.ALL bacterial SSU rRNA dataset (13,822 reference sequences and up to 138,210 artificially truncated fragments; Table 2), alongside qualitative demonstrations on fungal internal transcribed spacers (ITS1/ITS2; ~300 full-length and ~5,000 environmental fragments; Figure 2) and three divergent PIN-domain protein structures (PDB: 3v33, 2qip, 1taq; Figure 4). While the MAFFT software is open and command lines are clearly documented in Table 1 and the text, the fungal ITS dataset used in Test Case 1 is cited as a personal communication without a public accession, and equations in the manuscript text suffer from missing mathematical expressions during typesetting.

Overall, this is a practical and candid software update paper that clearly explains when standard progressive or iterative alignment algorithms fail—specifically when non-overlapping fragments violate distance matrix additivity—and provides scalable heuristics (`--addfragments`) to address these regimes. The primary methodological limitations are the lack of quantitative accuracy metrics or competing tool baselines (such as PAGAN, PaPaRa, SEPP, or 3DCoffee/PROMALS3D) for Test Cases 1 and 3, the reliance on a single highly conserved rRNA benchmark in Table 2 where `--multipair` fails to scale to the larger fragment sets (Cases 2 and 3), and the ad hoc thresholding of ASH structural equivalence scores without sensitivity analysis. These limitations can be addressed through additional benchmark reporting, inclusion of public accessions or supplementary alignments for the ITS and PIN-domain test cases, and minor editorial repairs to the missing equations and typographical errors.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Two-step alignment with `--addfragments` resolves misalignments of non-overlapping fragments (ITS1/ITS2) and achieves >99.4% accuracy on bacterial 16S rRNA fragments in minutes to hours: [Well Supported].**  
  The theoretical explanation for why standard all-at-once progressive (FFT-NS-2) and iterative (L-INS-i) methods fail on mixed ITS1/ITS2 reads—namely, that non-overlapping ITS1 and ITS2 fragments have near-zero distance to full-length sequences but maximal distance to each other, violating tree additivity and distorting the WSP/consistency objective—is mathematically sound and visually confirmed in Figure 2A–E. On the 16S.B.ALL benchmark (Table 2, Case 1: 13,822 backbone sequences × 13,821 fragments), `--multipair --addfragments` achieves 0.9969 accuracy (18.3 h wall-clock on 10 cores) and `--6merpair --addfragments` achieves 0.9949 accuracy (36.2 min wall-clock), outperforming `--localpair --add` (0.9707 accuracy in 2.43 days) and naive profile alignment (0.2779 accuracy).

* **Standard profile alignment (`mafft-profile`) is inappropriate for adding unaligned sequences unless the added alignment forms an isolated or monophyletic cluster (`--addprofile`): [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 1A–D clearly formalizes the implicit phylogenetic assumptions of profile-to-profile versus sequence-to-backbone alignment. Table 2 demonstrates that sequentially aligning fragments to a collapsed profile drops accuracy on 16S.B.ALL from >0.96 to 0.2779 because all new sequences are implicitly forced to attach at the root of the backbone tree (Figure 1C). The safeguard in `--addprofile` that checks monophyly of `alignment1` before proceeding is a sound design choice.

* **The fast `--6merpair --addfragments` heuristic scales to 138,210 fragments with negligible accuracy loss relative to dynamic programming (`--multipair`): [Moderately Supported].**  
  In Table 2 (Cases 2 and 3, with 1,000 and 13,822 backbone sequences and 138,210 fragments), `--6merpair --addfragments` completes in 33.8 min and 5.91 h (10 threads) with accuracies of 0.9969 and 0.9949, respectively. However, the default `--multipair --addfragments` option could not be run on Cases 2 and 3 due to scalability bottlenecks, and 16S rRNA is highly conserved. As the authors acknowledge via citation to prior simulation benchmarks, `--6merpair` degrades on more divergent sequences where shared 6-mers become sparse; thus, its near-parity with `--multipair` in Table 2 cannot be generalized to rapidly evolving protein or viral families without empirical stratification by sequence divergence.

* **Seeding MAFFT with ASH pairwise structural alignments (`--seed`) overcomes sequence-level misalignment in divergent proteins (Test Case 3, PIN domain): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Figure 4 shows that `mafft-linsi` misaligns the middle conserved catalytic aspartic acid across three divergent PIN-domain structures (3v33, 2qip, 1taq), whereas seeding with ASH pairwise alignments filtered at residue equivalence $e_{ij} \ge 0.5$ ($d_0 = 4\text{ \AA}$) restores the active-site alignment. While biologically plausible and visually convincing as a proof of concept, this evaluation is restricted to a single trio of proteins without quantitative comparison against established structure-informed aligners (e.g., 3DCoffee, PROMALS3D) or sensitivity analysis across equivalence thresholds.

* **Multi-threaded iterative refinement (`--thread`) yields alignment accuracy comparable to serial execution: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The authors note that while progressive methods are deterministic across thread counts, parallelized iterative refinement (`--thread`) can produce non-identical alignments compared to serial runs. Support for accuracy equivalence relies entirely on a citation to an earlier note ([AUTHOR] and Toh 2010), with no variance metrics or cross-thread concordance statistics reported for the version 7 implementations in the present manuscript.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Quantify Alignment Accuracy and Include External Baselines in Test Cases 1 and 3
* **WHY:** Test Case 1 (fungal ITS; Figure 2) relies purely on visual inspection in Jalview, and Test Case 3 (PIN domain; Figure 4) evaluates only three sequences without comparing the ASH+MAFFT `--seed` workflow against existing structure-guided aligners (3DCoffee, PROMALS3D) or competing fragment-insertion tools (PaPaRa, PAGAN, SEPP).
* **WHAT:** Report quantitative accuracy metrics for Test Cases 1 and 3, and add baseline comparisons on the same inputs.
* **HOW:** For Test Case 1, compute the conserved 5.8S/flanking column recovery or sum-of-pairs/total-column scores among the known ITS1 and ITS2 partitions, and verify that zero ITS1 fragments overlap ITS2 columns in the final alignment. For Test Case 3, report the fraction of structurally equivalent residue pairs recovered by ASH+MAFFT (`--seed`) versus standalone `mafft-linsi`, 3DCoffee, and PROMALS3D on the PIN-domain trio (and, ideally, a standard structural benchmark subset such as BAliBASE RV11).

#### 2. Deposit the Test Case 1 Fungal ITS Dataset and Test Case 3 Seed Files
* **WHY:** Test Case 1 uses ~300 full-length and ~5,000 environmental 454 ITS1/ITS2 sequences cited only as "Chen W, personal communication," preventing independent reproduction of Figure 2 and the associated CPU timing benchmarks (15 min + 1.5 min vs. 98 h).
* **WHAT:** Archive the exact unaligned input sequences, backbone alignment, and ASH seed files used in Test Cases 1 and 3 in a public repository or supplementary archive.
* **HOW:** Upload the anonymized/environmental FASTA files (`full_length_sequences`, `new_sequences`) for Test Case 1 and the three ASH seed files (`ash_3v33A-2qipA`, `ash_3v33A-1taqA`, `ash_2qipA-1taqA`) for Test Case 3 to Zenodo, Dryad, or the MAFFT benchmark web page, and cite the direct URL/DOI in the text.

#### 3. Quantify the Memory and Runtime Bottleneck of `--multipair --addfragments` in Table 2
* **WHY:** In Table 2, the authors state that "At present, the default option (`--multipair --addfragments`) cannot handle cases 2 and 3," without specifying whether it fails due to $O(N_{\text{backbone}} \times N_{\text{fragments}})$ pairwise dynamic programming runtime, RAM exhaustion, or an internal array limit.
* **WHAT:** Provide the exact computational complexity and empirical scaling limits (CPU hours and peak RAM) of `--multipair` versus `--6merpair` and `--lastmultipair`.
* **HOW:** Add a column or footnote to Table 2 reporting peak memory usage (GB) for each run, state the algorithmic time and space complexity of `--multipair --addfragments` in terms of backbone size $M$, fragment count $K$, and length $L$, and include `--lastmultipair` (listed in Table 1) in Table 2 to show how adaptive seed alignment bridges the speed/accuracy gap on Cases 1–3.
