### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript contrasts the selective regimes acting on SARS-CoV-2 during the first 11 months of human circulation (133,741 QC-filtered GISAID genomes collected through October 12, 2020) against the historical evolutionary forces that shaped the progenitor lineage ("nCoV" clade) across 69 bat and pangolin *Sarbecovirus* genomes. Partitioning the *Sarbecovirus* alignment into 19–20 non-recombinant regions (NRRs) and applying codon-based selection models (`SLAC`, `FEL`, `MEME`, `aBSREL`, and `BUSTED[S]` augmented with a 3-category Hidden Markov Model for autocorrelated synonymous rate variation and multi-nucleotide substitutions), the authors show that early human SARS-CoV-2 evolution was characterized by weak purifying selection ($\omega \approx 0.4\text{–}0.9$) and sparse episodic positive selection, whereas strong episodic diversifying selection acted on the deep internal branches of the bat `nCoV` clade (6 non-recombinant segments across *Orf1ab*, *Spike*, and *N*, encompassing 85 codons) with no detectable positive selection on the terminal branch leading to SARS-CoV-2. Using a base-composition-corrected Synonymous Dinucleotide Usage metric (`SDUc`), Ornstein-Uhlenbeck phylogenetic shift detection (`PhylogeneticEM`), and local-clock Bayesian dating (`BEAST 1.10`), they further demonstrate an adaptive shift toward strong CpG depletion at the base of the `nCoV` clade and show that the bat virus RmYN02 (diverging from SARS-CoV-2 around 1976) is a recombinant between low-CpG (`nCoV`) and high-CpG (`non-nCoV`) lineages, proving co-infection within bat reservoirs. BEAST XML files, accession tables, and a live selection tracking resource (`http://covid19.datamonkey.org`) are publicly provided.

This is a methodologically rigorous comparative genomics study that directly addresses the major confounding artifacts in viral molecular evolution: terminal-branch sequencing/lab-recombination errors in pandemic SARS-CoV-2 genomes, ubiquitous recombination across *Sarbecoviruses*, site-to-site synonymous substitution rate variation, multi-nucleotide mutations, and single-nucleotide base composition biases in dinucleotide metrics. The authors' conclusions are carefully bounded, explicitly acknowledging where competing models cannot be statistically distinguished (such as a branch-specific vs. clade-wide substitution rate shift in BEAST, `S3 Text`) and where multi-nucleotide substitutions explain apparent positive selection (`ORF1ab/regE`, `S3 Table`). Only minor refinements regarding multiple-testing calibration across longitudinal selection scans, ultrametric tree transformation in the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck analysis, and textual consistency in non-recombinant fragment counts are suggested.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Early human evolution of SARS-CoV-2 (December 2019 to October 2020) was dominated by weak purifying selection and exponential growth with limited adaptive evolution: [Well Supported].**  
  By diagnosing that terminal branches in pandemic GISAID trees are enriched for sequencing errors, intrahost dead-ends, and laboratory recombination artifacts (`S2 Text`, `S1 Table`) and restricting `FEL` and `MEME` scans to internal tree branches (Figure 1C–E), the authors provide a clean, well-calibrated view of early pandemic evolution. Frequency-stratified `SLAC` $d_N/d_S$ estimates declining from $\sim 0.9$ (rare variants surfing exponential expansion) to $\sim 0.4\text{–}0.6$ (higher-frequency variants) and stable internal-branch gene-wide $\omega$ in *Spike* and *RdRp* strongly support nearly neutral / weakly purifying dynamics prior to late-2020 antigenic variants.

* **Episodic diversifying selection acted on deep internal branches of the bat `nCoV` clade rather than the terminal branch leading to human SARS-CoV-2: [Well Supported].**  
  The authors avoid recombination-induced false positives by analyzing 19–20 non-recombinant fragments separately, incorporate a 3-rate HMM into `BUSTED[S]` to account for $>20$-fold autocorrelated synonymous rate variation (`S3 Fig`), and further test a multi-nucleotide substitution extension (`S3 Table`). Showing that 5 of 6 positively selected regions (and 85 `MEME` sites) remain robust while transparently reporting that `ORF1ab/regE` loses significance once multi-nucleotide mutations are modeled exemplifies symmetric, self-critical hypothesis testing.

* **An adaptive shift toward CpG depletion occurred at the base of the `nCoV` clade, and RmYN02 represents a recombinant between low-CpG (`nCoV`) and high-CpG (`non-nCoV`) bat lineages: [Well Supported].**  
  Developing `SDUc` (Equation 1) to normalize synonymous CpG usage by each genome's empirical nucleotide composition eliminates the confounding effect of C$\to$U mutational bias. Both the `PhylogeneticEM` Ornstein-Uhlenbeck shift detection on `NRR1` and `NRR2` (Figure 3A, B; `S6 Fig`) and the sharp step-change in CpG RDA/SDUc precisely across the RmYN02 *Spike* recombination breakpoint (positions 21248–24058; Figure 4A, B, $p = 0.0450$) provide compelling, orthogonal lines of evidence that low-CpG and high-CpG sarbecoviruses co-circulate and recombine within the same bat hosts.

* **Divergence time estimates placing the `nCoV` clade MRCA around 1467 and the RmYN02/SARS-CoV-2 split around 1976 under a branch-specific local clock: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In the Results (line 62) and Methods (chunk 2 line 1; `S3 Text`), the authors appropriately note that the `Sarbecovirus` alignment lacks a strong tip-calibration temporal signal (requiring an external normal prior of $5 \times 10^{-4} \pm 2 \times 10^{-4}$ subs/site/year) and that the BEAST analysis cannot formally distinguish between a transient rate acceleration on the internal branch leading to the `nCoV` clade versus a clade-wide rate elevation, which substantially affects deeper node age estimates (such as the 1467 MRCA date).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Evaluate the Sensitivity of `PhylogeneticEM` CpG Shift Detection to Non-Ultrametric Tree Handling
* **WHY:** In the Methods ("Identifying shifts in CpG content", chunk 2 line 6), the authors note that because the scalar Ornstein-Uhlenbeck model in `PhylogeneticEM` requires an ultrametric tree, *"The trees were forced to be ultrametric, as required for the scalar OU model, by extending all the external edges of the trees to match the most recently sampled tip."* While most tips were sampled within a 15-year window (2003–2020) that is small relative to the tree height (~550 years), artificially stretching terminal branches alters tip variances/covariances under an OU process.
* **WHAT:** Verify that the inferred CpG shift on the branch ancestral to the `nCoV` clade is invariant to using the native non-ultrametric tree (via a phylogenetic mixed model / diagonal OU implementation that accepts non-ultrametric trees) or to dropping the earliest-sampled tips (e.g., 2003 SARS-CoV-1) so almost no branch extension is needed.
* **HOW:** Run `PCMBase` / `ouch` or a phylogenetic generalized least squares (`nlme::gls` / `phylolm` with an indicator variable for the `nCoV` clade under an OU covariance structure on the un-extended MCC tree) on the log-transformed `SDUc` values for `NRR1` and `NRR2`, and confirm that the `nCoV` ancestral branch shift remains statistically supported ($\Delta\text{AICc}$ or $\Delta\text{BIC} > 10$).

#### 2. Apply Multiple-Testing Control to the `aBSREL`, `BUSTED[S]`, and Longitudinal `MEME` Scans
* **WHY:** In Figure 1E, 52 codons in *Spike* and *RdRp* are plotted because they reached nominal $p \le 0.05$ at least once across 7 longitudinal GISAID snapshots, and in the `nCoV` analysis (Figure 2), `BUSTED[S]` and `aBSREL` are run across 19–20 non-recombinant segments at nominal $p \le 0.05$.
* **WHAT:** Report Holm-Bonferroni or Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-adjusted $p$-values across the 20 non-recombinant segments for `BUSTED[S]` (`S3 Table`) and distinguish sites in Figure 1E that survive FDR correction at the final timepoint from transient nominal hits.
* **HOW:** Add a column of Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted $q$-values across the 20 segments to `S3 Table` for both `BUSTED[S]-HMM` and `BUSTED[S]-HMM-MNS`, and annotate in Figure 1E which of the 52 codons achieve $q \le 0.10$ (or $p \le 0.01$) at the October 12, 2020 snapshot.

#### 3. Replace the 3-Observation $t$-Test in Figure 4B with a codon-bootstrap or permutation test
* **WHY:** In Figure 4B (line 66), the authors compare the absolute difference in `SDUc` between Wuhan-Hu-1 and RmYN02 in the `non-nCoV` vs. `nCoV` regions of *Spike* using an unpaired one-tailed $t$-test across the $n = 3$ dinucleotide frame positions (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge`), yielding $t_{2.07} = 3.03, p = 0.0450$. Because `pos1`, `pos2`, and `bridge` are three frame partitions of the same sequence rather than independent biological replicates, a codon-level permutation or bootstrap test across the two *Spike* subregions provides a more direct and higher-powered test of the regional CpG difference.
* **WHAT:** Supplement the 3-frame-position $t$-test in Figure 4B with a bootstrap or permutation test across codons comparing the `non-nCoV` (positions 1–2810 of *Spike*) and `nCoV` (positions 2811–end) segments.
* **HOW:** Resample codons with replacement (10,000 replicates) within the `non-nCoV` and `nCoV` *Spike* regions of Wuhan-Hu-1 and RmYN02, compute the overall synonymous CpG `SDUc` difference $|\Delta\text{SDUc}_{\text{non-nCoV}}| - |\Delta\text{SDUc}_{\text{nCoV}}|$ for each replicate, and report the bootstrap 95% confidence interval and empirical $p$-value.
