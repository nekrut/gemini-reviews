### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates how the ratio of nonadaptive convergent to divergent amino acid substitutions ($C/D$) varies with evolutionary (patristic) distance between branches. Analyzing a concatenated alignment of 13 mitochondrial protein-coding genes across 629 tetrapod species (from an initial download of 641 species) alongside analytical and simulation models—including Jukes–Cantor (JC), WAG, codon-based Zihengian (Z), gamma rate-variation extensions, the site-heterogeneous CAT-60 mixture model, and a structure-based thermodynamic epistasis simulation (the Stokes–Fisher [SF] model folded on purple acid phosphatase, PDB `1QHW`)—the authors show that empirical $C/D$ ratios start near 0.40–0.45 at short patristic distances and decline below 0.20 at large distances. By conditioning on whether the ancestral amino acids at the two compared branches are identical (Figure 2A) or different (Figure 2B), they demonstrate that static site-heterogeneous models (CAT-60) predict an *increasing* $C/D$ ratio with divergence for identical ancestral states due to survivorship enrichment of low-entropy conserved sites (Figure 6A), whereas the empirical mitochondrial data exhibit a marked decrease that is reproduced by temporally fluctuating epistatic constraints in the SF model (Figure 4B, Figure 5). While standard software packages (ClustalX, PRANK, MrModeltest v2.2, MrBayes 3.0b4, PLEX) and the structural template (`1QHW`) are specified, the 641/629 GenBank accession IDs (`[ACCESSION_REDACTED in scrubbed input]`), concatenated alignment, reconstructed phylogeny, and custom SF simulation scripts are not deposited in an external public archive.

The study makes a strong theoretical and empirical contribution by showing that conditioned $C/D$ decay at sites sharing an identical ancestral residue cleanly separates static spatial rate/profile heterogeneity from temporally shifting substitution processes. The primary methodological limitations are: (1) unmodeled lineage-specific nucleotide mutational and compositional shifts across vertebrate mitochondrial genomes, which could generate distance-dependent $C/D$ decay even without amino acid epistasis when reconstructed under a single stationary amino acid matrix (`mtMam`); (2) reliance on a single fixed COI topology (described inconsistently as a Bayesian consensus tree and a maximum-likelihood tree) across 629 taxa, where local topological errors on short internal branches can map single synapomorphies onto two nearby branches as false convergence events; and (3) thresholding and circular site-partitioning choices in the empirical summaries ($D \ge 20$ truncation and $>90\%$ posterior probability cutoffs in Figures 1–4, and defining conservation classes from total substitution counts on the same tree). These issues can be addressed through computational sensitivity analyses and text corrections without generating new experimental data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Nonadaptive convergence ($C/D$) in vertebrate mitochondrial proteins is high (~0.40–0.45) between closely related branches and declines below 0.20 with increasing patristic distance, including when restricted to branch pairs sharing the same ancestral amino acid: [Well Supported].**  
  Bayesian ancestral reconstruction in PLEX integrating over state uncertainty (Supplementary Figure S2, Supplementary Table S1) and conditioning on identical ancestral states (Figure 2A) confirm a substantial decline in $C/D$ with patristic distance (99% credible interval for the linear slope: $[-0.109, -0.094]$, Supplementary Figure S3). However, the magnitude of the earliest part of the curve is sensitive to alignment algorithm choice (ClustalX vs. PRANK, Supplementary Figure S7), local phylogenetic error on short branches, and the $D \ge 20$ truncation threshold applied in Figure 1.

* **Time-homogeneous site-heterogeneous mixture models (CAT-60) cannot explain the empirical decline in $C/D$ among sites sharing the same ancestral amino acid, because survivorship bias toward conserved sites forces their identical-ancestor $C/D$ prediction upward over time: [Well Supported].**  
  The analytical calculation in Figure 6A and its inset provides a clean mathematical demonstration: under a time-homogeneous mixture of site profiles (CAT-60), fast-evolving high-entropy sites rapidly diverge in ancestral state, leaving the pool of unchanged-ancestor sites progressively enriched for low-entropy, highly constrained sites whose $C/D$ ratio rises rather than falls with patristic distance.

* **The empirical decay of $C/D$ in mitochondrial proteins is driven by epistatic or coevolutionary "evolutionary Stokes shift" dynamics: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Simulations of the thermodynamic SF model over the mitochondrial tree (Figure 4B) and star phylogeny (Figures 5 and 6B) closely match the empirical trajectory and variance of $C/D$ across conserved/buried and variable/exposed sites. Nevertheless, the inference is observational and does not rule out non-epistatic sources of temporal non-stationarity—specifically, shifting strand-specific nucleotide mutation biases (GC/AT skews) across tetrapod mitochondrial clades (acknowledged by the authors in Methods via Krishnan et al. 2004), which cause closely related lineages to share similar mutational spectrum biases in 2-fold and 4-fold degenerate codon families that decay with phylogenetic distance.

* **Once ancestral amino acids at a site have diverged between two branches, the site retains no detectable site-specific information about convergence probability ($C/D \approx 0.08$, equivalent to $m \approx 13.4$ effective residues): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The site-resampling permutation test conditioned on the pair of diverged ancestral amino acids (Supplementary Figure S4 vs. Figure 2B) reproduces the flat empirical $C/D \approx 0.08$ curve. However, pooling replacements across all sites and branches washes out site-class heterogeneity, and in finite branch lengths a single inferred difference between branch ancestors includes both recently diverged sites (which under the SF model still share correlated structural environments) and saturated fast-evolving sites.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Lineage-Specific Mitochondrial Mutational Bias and Codon Structure
* **WHY:** The authors note in Materials and Methods (line 72) that vertebrate mitochondrial genes exhibit strong, shifting strand-asymmetric mutation rates across lineages. Because PLEX reconstructs substitutions across all 13 concatenated proteins using a single time-homogeneous amino acid matrix (`mtMam`), clade-specific nucleotide composition and transition/transversion biases can induce correlated amino acid replacements on nearby branches that decay with patristic distance, mimicking an epistatic Stokes shift in Figure 2A.
* **WHAT:** Quantify how much of the identical-ancestor $C/D$ decay in Figure 2A is explained by nucleotide/codon mutational bias heterogeneity across the tetrapod tree versus amino acid-level epistasis.
* **HOW:** Using the existing 629-taxon nucleotide alignments of the 13 mitochondrial genes, estimate clade- or branch-specific nucleotide composition/substitution parameters (or simulate neutral/purifying codon evolution along the 629-taxon tree using clade-specific empirical codon frequencies and $\kappa$ values without epistasis), translate the simulated sequences, run the exact PLEX `mtMam + 5\Gamma` pipeline, and plot the resulting identical-ancestor $C/D$ curve alongside the empirical and SF curves in Figure 4B.

#### 2. Evaluate Sensitivity to Local Phylogenetic Topology Error at Short Patristic Distances
* **WHY:** All convergence counts across the 13 genes are mapped onto a single fixed topology inferred from COI alone. On a 629-taxon tree with many short internal branches, gene-tree estimation error or incomplete lineage sorting can misplace a sister clade, causing a single true substitution to be reconstructed as two independent convergent substitutions on closely separated branches (inflating $C/D$ specifically at small patristic distances).
* **WHAT:** Propagate phylogenetic topological uncertainty into the $C/D$ vs. patristic distance curve and resolve the text discrepancy regarding how the COI tree was constructed.
* **HOW:** Re-run the PLEX substitution mapping across a sample of 100 posterior trees from the MrBayes 3.0b4 COI run (and compare against a tree inferred from the full 13-gene concatenation), recomputing the identical-ancestor $C/D$ trajectory after excluding branch pairs separated by poorly supported internal nodes (e.g., posterior probability $<0.95$). Clarify in Materials and Methods (line 72) whether the PLEX guide tree was the MrBayes consensus tree or a maximum-likelihood COI tree.

#### 3. Eliminate Truncation and Circularity Biases in $C/D$ Visualizations and Site Partitioning
* **WHY:** Filtering branch pairs by $D \ge 20$ (Figures 1 and 4) or $D \ge 7, 10$ (Figure 3) while plotting $C/D$ truncates the distribution of $D$ on short branch pairs, and defining "conserved" ($\le 60$ substitutions) versus "fast-evolving" ($\ge 75$ substitutions) classes using realized substitution counts on the same tree (line 72) creates statistical coupling between the conditioning variable and the $C/D$ counts.
* **WHAT:** Replace truncated branch-pair ratios with pooled/hierarchical estimates and cross-validate the conservation-class analysis using independent rate estimates.
* **HOW:** Compute binned or kernel-smoothed ratios $\sum C_b / \sum D_b$ summed across all eligible branch pairs $b$ within each patristic distance bin without applying a minimum-$D$ cutoff (or plot without the $>90\%$ posterior probability filter, using full posterior expectations from the MCMC samples). For Figure 3, classify sites into rate categories using site rates estimated on an independent sub-tree (or split the tree into two non-overlapping subclades: one to define site rate classes and the other to compute $C/D$).

#### 4. Temper Mechanistic Attribution in the Abstract and Discussion
* **WHY:** While the SF model shows that thermodynamic epistasis is *sufficient* to produce the observed $C/D$ decay, other non-stationary processes (shifting mutational spectra, lineage-specific physiological adaptation across ectothermic and endothermic tetrapods, and local tree reconstruction errors) are not formally excluded.
* **WHAT:** Frame the SF model comparison as demonstrating consistency with fluctuating epistatic constraints rather than exclusive proof of the Stokes-shift mechanism.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract (line 5) and Discussion (lines 56–60) to state that temporally fluctuating site constraints—arising from intramolecular epistasis and potentially compounded by lineage-specific mutational or functional shifts—explain the decline in identical-ancestor $C/D$ where static spatial mixture models fail.
