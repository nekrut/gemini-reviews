### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates the statistical principles governing differential expression (DE) analysis across biological conditions in single-cell, single-nucleus, and spatial transcriptomics. Surveying 500 recent scRNA-seq publications (205 performing between-condition DE), the authors benchmark the 14 most common DE methods—seven cell-level tests (Wilcoxon rank-sum, *t*-test, logistic regression, Poisson/negative binomial GLMs, bimodal LRT, and `MAST`), six pseudobulk methods (`edgeR-LRT`, `edgeR-QLF`, `DESeq2-Wald`, `DESeq2-LRT`, `limma-trend`, and `limma-voom`), and mixed-effects models—against 18 curated ground-truth datasets with matched bulk RNA-seq (and bulk proteomics) from identical purified cell populations. They show that pseudobulk methods consistently outperform cell-level methods in gene-level concordance (AUCC), proteomics concordance, and GSEA functional recovery. Through mechanistic perturbation experiments (expression-tercile stratification, ERCC spike-in controls, disabling cell aggregation, and shuffling biological replicate labels into "pseudo-replicates" across 46 published scRNA-seq datasets), they prove that cell-level methods treat individual cells as independent observations, systematically underestimating between-replicate variance—particularly for highly expressed, inter-replicate-variable genes—and generating hundreds to thousands of false discoveries in unperturbed control splits across 14 scRNA-seq datasets and spatial transcriptomics. Finally, they generate a new snRNA-seq dataset ($19,237$ nuclei) from the mouse lumbar spinal cord after mid-thoracic contusion injury, showing via in vivo `RNAscope` validation ($n = 4$ mice per group) that `edgeR-LRT` predominantly identifies true hypoxic endothelial responses ($5/6$ validated) whereas the single-cell Wilcoxon test predominantly nominates false positives ($3/13$ validated). Raw sequencing data (`GSE165003`), the 18 ground-truth datasets (`doi:10.5281/zenodo.5048449`), the `Libra` R package (`github.com/neurorestore/Libra`), and full analysis code (`github.com/neurorestore/DE-analysis`) are publicly available.

This is an exemplary methodological and experimental study that pairs clear mathematical intuition (hierarchical variance decomposition across biological replicates versus cells) with multi-layered empirical validation and open software. Two minor methodological nuances can be clarified to make the presentation even stronger. First, in the 18 matched bulk/single-cell datasets, the reference bulk DE rankings were generated using the same six `edgeR`, `DESeq2`, and `limma` models evaluated as pseudobulk methods (Methods line 172); adding a distribution-free bulk reference ranking (or emphasizing the independent proteomics and `RNAscope` validations) ensures that pseudobulk superiority cannot be attributed to shared parametric shrinkage assumptions. Second, in the `RNAscope` validation comparison ($5/6$ vs. $3/13$, $N = 19$), Fisher's exact test should be reported in place of Pearson's $\chi^2$ test due to small expected cell counts, and the gene-count discrepancy between Results line 130 ("six genes") and Methods line 95 ("7 genes" stated, six listed) should be corrected.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Pseudobulk DE methods consistently outperform cell-level single-cell DE methods across 18 matched bulk/scRNA-seq ground-truth datasets, matched bulk proteomics, and GSEA functional enrichment: [Well Supported].**  
  The superiority of pseudobulk aggregation holds across all six pseudobulk variants (`edgeR`, `DESeq2`, `limma`), across alternative concordance metrics (AUCC and transcriptome-wide Spearman rank correlation, Supplementary Figure 1), and against independent label-free quantitative proteomics (Supplementary Figure 1e–f), ruling out metric-specific artifacts.

* **Cell-level DE methods fail because ignoring biological replication underestimates between-replicate variance, creating a systematic false-positive bias toward highly expressed genes: [Well Supported].**  
  The two causal ablation experiments in Figure 3—(i) running `edgeR`, `DESeq2`, and `limma` on unaggregated individual cells (Figure 3a–b) and (ii) aggregating equal numbers of cells across shuffled replicate labels ("pseudo-replicates," Figure 3c–d)—cleanly isolate biological replicate structure as the necessary and sufficient condition for variance calibration. The partial correlation analysis across 46 datasets (Methods line 15 of part 2) further shows that highly expressed genes are disproportionately affected because they exhibit the largest true inter-replicate variance ($\Delta$-variance).

* **Widely used cell-level DE methods generate hundreds to thousands of false discoveries in the complete absence of biological perturbation across simulated data, 14 unperturbed scRNA-seq control cohorts, and spatial transcriptomics: [Well Supported].**  
  By splitting unperturbed control biological replicates into random groups across 14 real scRNA-seq datasets (Figure 4d–e) and wild-type mouse spinal cord spatial transcriptomics (Figure 4f), the authors provide an airtight empirical null test demonstrating massive Type I error inflation under cell-level pseudoreplication and its elimination by pseudobulk aggregation.

* **In the injured mouse lumbar spinal cord, `edgeR-LRT` identifies true in vivo endothelial transcriptional changes ($5/6$ validated by `RNAscope`) whereas the cell-level Wilcoxon test predominantly yields false discoveries ($3/13$ validated): [Well Supported].**  
  The prospective `RNAscope` screen in `Pecam1`+ lumbar endothelial cells across $n = 4$ biological replicates per group (Figure 5f–h) provides direct independent experimental validation in tissue sections, corroborated histologically by vascular atrophy (Figure 5i).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Add a Model-Independent Bulk Reference Check for the 18 Ground-Truth Datasets
* **WHY:** Because the bulk RNA-seq reference rankings in 16 of the 18 ground-truth datasets were computed using `DESeq2`, `edgeR`, and `limma` (Methods line 172), a skeptic could argue that pseudobulk `DESeq2`/`edgeR`/`limma` achieves higher AUCC partly by sharing empirical Bayes dispersion priors and parametric families with the bulk analyzer.
* **WHAT:** Verify that the AUCC gap between pseudobulk and cell-level methods persists when the bulk RNA-seq ground truth is ranked using a simple, non-shrunk statistic (e.g., ordinary per-gene *t*-test on $\log_2(\text{CPM} + 1)$ or rank-product across bulk replicates) in datasets with $\ge 4$ bulk replicates.
* **HOW:** Re-compute bulk reference rankings in the ground-truth datasets using a standard two-sample *t*-test on TMM-normalized $\log_2\text{CPM}$ counts, recompute the AUCC matrix across the 14 single-cell DE methods, and report the correlation with Figure 1c in Supplementary Figure 1.

#### 2. Control for Total Endogenous RNA Shifts in the ERCC Spike-In Analysis
* **WHY:** In the Hagai et al. human dermal fibroblast dataset (`E-MTAB-7051`, comparing 2 h vs. 6 h of IFN-$\beta$ stimulation; Methods line 141 of part 2), if library size factors are computed across combined endogenous and ERCC reads, a global transcriptional burst between 2 h and 6 h of IFN-$\beta$ treatment can change the fraction of reads mapping to ERCC spike-ins, creating an apparent shift in ERCC relative counts.
* **WHAT:** Confirm that the ERCC false-positive correlation with mean expression (Figure 2d–e) holds when ERCC counts are normalized strictly by total ERCC spike-in library size (or within unperturbed biological replicates at a single timepoint).
* **HOW:** Re-run the ERCC spike-in DE analysis (i) normalizing the 80 ERCC transcripts using size factors computed solely from the ERCC count matrix (`DESeq2::estimateSizeFactorsForMatrix(ercc_counts)`), and (ii) comparing randomly split replicates *within* the 2 h timepoint alone.

#### 3. Use Fisher's Exact Test and Report Power Bounds for the `RNAscope` Validation Screen
* **WHY:** In Results line 130, comparing $5/6$ validated genes (`edgeR-LRT`) against $3/13$ validated genes (`Wilcoxon`) involves a $2 \times 2$ table with total sample size $N = 19$ and minimum expected cell count $6 \times 8 / 19 = 2.53 < 5$, where the asymptotic $\chi^2$ approximation is less accurate than Fisher's exact test.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact Fisher's test $P$-value ($P = 0.041$ two-sided; $P = 0.022$ one-sided) and odds ratio for the $2 \times 2$ validation contingency table, and reconcile the gene count between Results and Methods.
* **HOW:** Replace `(p < 0.05, \chi^2 test)` on line 130 with `(p = 0.041, two-sided Fisher's exact test; odds ratio = 14.1)`.

#### 4. Expand Practical Guidance on Pseudobulk Aggregation When Cell Counts per Replicate Are Very Small
* **WHY:** In Supplementary Figure 10, downsampled analyses show performance convergence above 500 cells, but many rare cell subpopulations in complex tissues (such as rare neuronal subtypes in the spinal cord) yield $<10\text{--}20$ cells per biological replicate, where summing UMI counts across variable cell numbers introduces heteroskedasticity across replicates.
* **WHAT:** Add a brief practical recommendation in the Discussion or Methods on how `Libra` users should handle cell-count imbalance across biological replicates in rare cell types (e.g., weighting replicates by cell count in `limma-voom` or filtering replicates with $<5\text{--}10$ cells).
* **HOW:** Stratify the 46-dataset compendium or the 18 ground-truth datasets by median cells per replicate ($<20$, $20\text{--}100$, $>100$) to report the minimum cell count per replicate required for stable pseudobulk estimation in `Libra`.
