### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This prospective cohort study evaluates the association between nine baseline peripheral blood systemic inflammatory biomarkers—C-reactive protein (CRP), lymphocyte, monocyte, neutrophil, and platelet counts, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII)—and incident abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) in the UK Biobank. After excluding 584 participants with prevalent aortic aneurysm/dissection, 46,719 without baseline blood biomarker data, and 183,996 with baseline malignancies, hematologic, autoimmune, chronic inflammatory, or connective tissue diseases, the authors analyze 271,068 participants (629 incident ICD-10 AAA cases over a median follow-up of 13.89 years). Using stepwise multivariable Cox proportional hazards models, restricted cubic splines, and Fine–Gray competing-risk models (accounting for death), and incorporating a 31-SNP AAA polygenic risk score (PRS), they find that a 1-SD increase in $\log_{10}$-transformed CRP ($\text{HR} = 1.46$, 95% CI $1.35\text{--}1.59$), monocytes ($\text{HR} = 1.27$), neutrophils ($\text{HR} = 1.27$), and lymphocytes ($\text{HR} = 1.17$) is independently associated with elevated AAA risk, whereas platelet count ($\text{HR} = 0.90$) and PLR ($\text{HR} = 0.82$) are inversely associated. No multiplicative interaction is observed between CRP and the 31-SNP PRS ($p_{\text{interaction}} = 0.85$), and combining high CRP (top quartile) with high PRS (top tertile) among male current smokers aged $\ge 65$ increases 14-year cumulative AAA incidence from 4.78% to 10.05%.

This is a well-powered prospective epidemiological analysis that leverages a large cohort, long follow-up, competing-risk sensitivity analyses, and genetic risk stratification to address a clinically relevant screening question. However, several methodological and technical weaknesses temper the conclusions: (1) incomplete cardiovascular and pharmacological confounder adjustment (diabetes mellitus, BMI, pack-years of smoking, statin/antiplatelet/antihypertensive medication use, and non-HDL vs. protective HDL cholesterol are omitted from Models 2–3, leaving clear residual confounding where hypercholesterolemia appears artifactually protective in Table 1); (2) absence of formal risk prediction and reclassification metrics ($\Delta\text{C-index}$, Net Reclassification Improvement [NRI], or calibration curves) to substantiate the claim that CRP meaningfully improves AAA screening beyond clinical risk factors and PRS; (3) potential sample overlap or overfitting if any of the 31 PRS SNPs or weights were derived from GWAS meta-analyses that included the UK Biobank; and (4) minor numerical discrepancies in sample size counts across Table 1, Table 2, and the main text, as well as the inappropriate use of "linear interpolation" to impute cross-sectional baseline missing data. All of these issues can be resolved through targeted re-analysis of the existing UK Biobank dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Elevated baseline CRP, neutrophil, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts are independently associated with higher risk of incident AAA, while platelet count is inversely associated: [Well Supported].**  
  Across 270,439 participants and 629 incident AAA events over 13.9 years, the associations remain robust across progressive covariate adjustments (Models 1–3, Table 2), outlier exclusion ($\pm 3\text{ SD}$, Table S10), and Fine–Gray competing risk regression accounting for mortality (Table S11). Restricted cubic splines (Figure 2) confirm a monotonic log-linear dose-response for CRP and threshold increases above the 75th percentile for leukocyte subsets.

* **Systemic inflammation (CRP) and polygenic predisposition (31-SNP PRS) act additively without multiplicative interaction ($p_{\text{interaction}} = 0.85$) to stratify AAA risk: [Well Supported].**  
  The hazard ratio per 1-SD increase in $\log_{10}(\text{CRP})$ is stable across low, intermediate, and high PRS tertiles ($\text{HR} = 1.44\text{--}1.49$, Figure 3), and individuals in both the highest CRP quartile and highest PRS tertile exhibit a 5.3-fold higher hazard than those in the lowest categories (Figure 4). However, the manuscript does not specify whether the two source GWASs [22, 23] used to select and weight the 31 SNPs included UK Biobank participants, which would inflate the marginal PRS effect size ($\text{HR} = 1.32$ per SD).

* **Incorporating CRP complements conventional AAA risk factors and PRS to improve clinical screening identification rates: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In the target screening demographic of male current smokers aged $\ge 65$, restricting to the top CRP quartile and top PRS tertile doubles cumulative incidence from 4.78% to 10.05% (line 185). However, restricting screening to the top 1/4 of CRP and top 1/3 of PRS ($\sim 1/12$ of the high-risk population) inevitably misses the majority of total AAA cases occurring in the remaining 11/12 of that demographic. Without reporting sensitivity, specificity, number needed to screen (NNS), or formal discrimination improvement ($\Delta\text{C-index}$ / NRI) over a baseline clinical model, the clinical utility for population screening remains incomplete.

* **Covariate adjustments in Models 2 and 3 fully account for baseline cardiometabolic and treatment confounders: [Fragile].**  
  In Table 1 (lines 102–103), participants who developed AAA had *lower* baseline total cholesterol ($5.38$ vs. $5.72\text{ mmol/L}$, $p < 0.001$) and a *lower* prevalence of hypercholesterolemia ($53.9\%$ vs. $67.8\%$, $p < 0.001$). In an older, predominantly male smoking population (mean age 63 vs. 55 years), lower total cholesterol is a classic signature of statin therapy (and/or lower HDL-C), which also lowers CRP and modifies AAA risk. Moreover, diabetes mellitus (a well-established negative risk factor for AAA that elevates CRP and leukocyte counts), BMI/waist-to-hip ratio, and smoking pack-years were not adjusted for in Model 2 or Model 3.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Expand Multivariable Adjustment to Include Statin Use, Diabetes, BMI, HDL/LDL, and Pack-Years
* **WHY:** Omitting statin/lipid-lowering medication use, diabetes status, BMI, and smoking intensity (pack-years) leaves confounding pathways open—as evidenced by the paradoxical inverse association between total cholesterol/hypercholesterolemia and AAA in Table 1.
* **WHAT:** Add baseline lipid-lowering medication (statin) use, antihypertensive use, diabetes mellitus, BMI, HDL-C, LDL-C (replacing binary total cholesterol $\ge 5.18\text{ mmol/L}$), and smoking pack-years to Model 3.
* **HOW:** Extract UK Biobank baseline fields for statin/medication use (Field 20003 / 6177 / 6153), diabetes diagnosis (Field 2443), BMI (Field 21001), pack-years (Field 20161), and LDL/HDL cholesterol (Fields 30780, 30760), refit Model 3 in Table 2, and report the updated HRs and 95% CIs.

#### 2. Quantify Incremental Predictive Discrimination ($\Delta\text{C-index}$, NRI, and Sensitivity/Specificity Trade-offs)
* **WHY:** Hazard ratios alone do not demonstrate that a biomarker improves clinical risk prediction or screening efficiency, and restricting screening to the top CRP quartile + top PRS tertile sacrifices sensitivity.
* **WHAT:** Compute Harrell's C-index (and time-dependent 10-year/14-year AUC), continuous Net Reclassification Improvement (NRI), and a screening trade-off table (Sensitivity, Specificity, Positive Predictive Value, and Number Needed to Screen) comparing: (a) Base clinical model, (b) Base + PRS, (c) Base + CRP, and (d) Base + PRS + CRP.
* **HOW:** Using the `survival` and `timeROC` / `nricens` packages in R, calculate the change in C-index with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals when adding log-CRP and PRS to the Model 2 covariates, both in the full cohort and within men aged $\ge 65$.

#### 3. Replace "Linear Interpolation" Imputation with Multiple Imputation and Verify PRS Independence
* **WHY:** In Sensitivity Analysis 1 (line 51), the authors state they imputed missing baseline values *"by linear interpolation."* Linear interpolation is a time-series method for ordered longitudinal sequences; applying it across rows of an cross-sectional participant table imputes a participant's missing value as the average of the two arbitrarily adjacent participant IDs in the spreadsheet. Additionally, the source GWASs for the 31 PRS SNPs must be checked for UK Biobank sample overlap.
* **WHAT:** Replace linear interpolation with Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (`mice`) or random forest imputation, and explicitly state in the Methods whether the 31 SNPs and their $\beta$-weights were derived from cohorts completely independent of the UK Biobank.
* **HOW:** Re-run Supplemental Table S9 using `mice` in R (5 imputed datasets combined via Rubin's rules) and document the exact discovery cohorts of references [22–24] in the "Genetic data and polygenic risk score calculation" section.

#### 4. Reconcile Participant Count Discrepancies Across Text and Tables
* **WHY:** The total number of included participants fluctuates by 1 to 629 across the manuscript without explanation:
  1. Line 61 states **$271,068$** participants were included.
  2. Table 1 (line 76) lists $270,440$ participants without AAA + $629$ with AAA = **$271,069$** participants.
  3. Within Table 1, the rows for Age ($233,144 + 37,295$), Sex ($124,102 + 146,337$), Ethnicity ($253,416 + 1,699 + 5,610 + 4,472 + 5,242$), and Education ($33,528 + 236,911$) in the "Participants without AAA" column all sum to **$270,439$**, not $270,440$ as printed in the column header.
  4. Table 2 footnote (line 145) states *"A total of 270,439 participants involved in this analysis"*—which equals the number of non-AAA controls alone rather than controls + 629 cases ($271,068$), or implies 629 participants had missing covariates in Models 1–3.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Audit the exact analytical sample sizes: change the Table 1 non-AAA header from `270440` to `270,439` (so that $270,439 + 629 = 271,068$, matching line 61), and clarify in the Table 2 footnote the exact number of total participants and AAA events included in complete-case Models 1, 2, and 3.
