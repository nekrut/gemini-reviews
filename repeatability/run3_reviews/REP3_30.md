### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates the association between the baseline oral and gut (fecal) microbiome and clinical response to anti-PD-1 immune checkpoint blockade in patients with metastatic melanoma ($N = 112$ enrolled, $n = 89$ clinically evaluable by RECIST 1.1 at 6 months: 54 responders [R] and 35 non-responders [NR]). The authors perform 16S rRNA gene sequencing on 109 oral and 53 fecal samples (with 43 fecal samples from evaluable anti-PD-1 patients: 30 R and 13 NR), whole-genome shotgun (WGS) metagenomic sequencing on a subset of 25 fecal samples (14 R, 11 NR), whole-exome sequencing (WES) on 10 tumors, multiparameter immunohistochemistry (IHC) on 21 tumors (15 R, 6 NR), peripheral blood flow cytometry/cytokine profiling, and fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) into germ-free (GF) mice implanted with syngeneic melanoma tumors treated with anti-PD-L1. They find that while the oral microbiome shows no association with clinical response ($p = 0.11$), the baseline gut microbiome of responders exhibits significantly higher $\alpha$-diversity (Inverse Simpson $p < 0.01$; high vs. intermediate/low diversity PFS $\text{HR} \approx 3.6$, $p = 0.02\text{--}0.04$), distinct weighted UniFrac $\beta$-diversity clustering, enrichment of *Ruminococcaceae* and *Faecalibacterium* (associated with prolonged PFS, $p = 0.03$, and correlated with intratumoral $\text{CD8}^+$ T cell density, $r^2 = 0.42$, $p < 0.01$), and enrichment of anabolic/amino acid biosynthetic pathways, whereas non-responders are enriched for *Bacteroidales* (associated with shorter PFS, $p = 0.05$, and peripheral $\text{T}_{\text{reg}}$/MDSC frequencies). In GF mice, R-FMT reduces day-14 tumor growth relative to NR-FMT ($p = 0.04$). Sequencing data are deposited in ENA (`PRJEB22894`, `PRJEB22874`, `PRJEB22895`, `PRJEB22893`) and EGA (`EGAS00001002698`).

This is an influential translational study that integrates human multi-omic profiling (16S, WGS metagenomics, WES, tumor IHC, peripheral immunophenotyping) with gnotobiotic mouse FMT experiments to link gut microbial composition to anti-PD-1 efficacy. At the same time, a rigorous statistical audit reveals several sample-size and analytical fragility points that temper the strength of specific claims: (1) although 112 patients are enrolled, the core fecal evaluable cohort is small ($n = 43$: 30 R and only 13 NR; further reduced to $n = 39$ in the PFS survival analyses of Figures 1D–E and 3C), and critical mechanistic sub-analyses rest on very small subsets ($n = 15$ matched fecal-IHC samples in Figure 4B–C; $n = 2$ high-*Faecalibacterium* vs. $n = 2$ high-*Bacteroidales* patients for multiplex myeloid IHC in Figure 4E–F; and $n = 2$ R-FMT vs. $n = 3$ NR-FMT vs. $n = 2$ Control mice in the anti-PD-L1 experiment in Figure 4I–K, where the caption explicitly reports non-significant Mann–Whitney $p$-values of $p = 0.20$, $p = 0.30$, and $p = 0.17$ despite the main text claiming significant effects); (2) use of forward stepwise selection on $n = 39$ patients to build the multivariate Cox model and failure to adjust for prior systemic antibiotic exposure or diet/BMI; and (3) a few figure cross-reference mismatches in the main text.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Higher baseline fecal (but not oral) $\alpha$-diversity and enrichment of *Ruminococcaceae* / *Faecalibacterium* vs. *Bacteroidales* are associated with clinical response and prolonged PFS on anti-PD-1 therapy: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Across the 43 evaluable fecal samples (30 R, 13 NR), Inverse Simpson diversity is significantly higher in responders ($p < 0.01$, Figure 1C), patients in the top diversity tertile ($n = 13$) have longer PFS than intermediate ($n = 13$, $p = 0.02$) or low ($n = 13$, $p = 0.04$) tertiles (Figure 1E), and *Ruminococcaceae*/*Faecalibacterium* enrichment in responders is corroborated across 16S Mann–Whitney FDR-adjusted tests (Figure 2E), phylogenetic `crOTU` clustering (Figure 3A–B, $p = 0.02$), and shotgun metagenomics ($n = 25$, Figure 2F). However, support is moderated by the small number of non-responders ($n = 13$), wide hazard ratio confidence intervals (e.g., $\text{HR} = 3.60$, 95% CI $1.02\text{--}12.74$ for diversity; $\text{HR} = 0.39$, 95% CI $0.15\text{--}1.03$, $p = 0.05$ for *Bacteroidales*), use of forward stepwise variable selection in a 39-patient cohort, and lack of an external validation cohort or explicit control for recent pre-treatment antibiotic use.

* **Fecal *Faecalibacterium* / *Ruminococcaceae* abundance positively correlates with baseline intratumoral $\text{CD8}^+$ T cell infiltration and peripheral effector T cell phenotypes: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In the subset of $n = 15$ patients with matched baseline fecal 16S and tumor IHC data (Figure 4B–C), linear regression and Spearman correlation show a positive relationship between fecal *Faecalibacterium* abundance and intratumoral $\text{CD8}^+$ T cell density ($r^2 = 0.42$, $p < 0.01$). However, the claim on line 198 that *"patients with a high abundance of Faecalibacterium in the gut microbiome had a higher density of immune cells and markers of antigen processing and presentation compared to those with a high abundance of Bacteroidales (Fig. 4, E and F)"* is based on only **$n = 2$ vs. $n = 2$ patients** (Figure 4F caption), which is too small for valid statistical inference.

* **Germ-free mice receiving R-FMT exhibit significantly improved responses to anti-PD-L1 therapy and significantly higher intratumoral and gut $\text{CD8}^+$ T cell densities compared to NR-FMT mice: [Overstated].**  
  While baseline day-14 tumor growth prior to anti-PD-L1 cutoff across two pooled experiments ($n = 5$ R-FMT vs. $n = 6$ NR-FMT mice) is significantly lower in R-FMT ($p = 0.04$, Figure 4H), the main text (line 206) claims: *"Importantly, mice transplanted with R-FMT also exhibited improved responses to anti-PD-L1 therapy (Fig. 4I) in contrast to mice that were transplanted with stool from NR (NR-FMT)... These studies demonstrated that tumors of mice receiving R-FMT had a higher density of CD8+ T cells than mice receiving NR-FMT, consistent with human data (Fig. 4J)... Moreover, FMT from R locally increased the number of CD45+ immune and CD8+ T cells in the gut compared to NR-FMT (Fig. 4K)."* Inspecting the Figure 4I–K caption (line 204) reveals that these experiments had only **$n = 2$ R-FMT, $n = 3$ NR-FMT, and $n = 2$ Control mice** (with 5–12 microscopic high-power fields [HPFs] pseudoreplicated across only 2 mice per group in Fig. 4J–K) and that the Mann–Whitney tests were **statistically non-significant**: $p = 0.20$ for R-FMT vs. NR-FMT in Figure 4I, $p = 0.30$ in Figure 4J, and $p = 0.17$ in Figure 4K.

* **Tumor mutational burden does not differ between anti-PD-1 responders and non-responders in this cohort: [Fragile].**  
  As the authors appropriately caveat on line 168, whole-exome sequencing was available for only $n = 10$ tumors ($7\text{ R}, 3\text{ NR}$), which provides almost zero statistical power to detect known mutational burden differences.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Pseudoreplication in Murine IHC (Figures 4J–K) and Align Text Claims with the Non-Significant $p$-Values in Figures 4I–K
* **WHY:** In Figure 4I–K, the biological sample size is $n = 2$ R-FMT mice, $n = 2\text{--}3$ NR-FMT mice, and $n = 2$ Control mice. The Figure 4I–K caption reports $p = 0.20$ (R-FMT vs. NR-FMT tumor volume on anti-PD-L1), $p = 0.30$ (tumor $\text{CD8}^+$ density), and $p = 0.17$ (gut $\text{CD8}^+$ density), while the stars (`** p < 0.01`, `**** p < 0.0001`) appear to come from treating 5 to 12 microscopic regions (HPFs) from just 2 mice as independent replicates. Treating multiple fields of view from the same animal as independent observations is classic pseudoreplication, and claiming an "improved response to anti-PD-L1 therapy" when $p = 0.20$ on $n = 2$ vs. $n = 3$ mice overstates the statistical evidence.
* **WHAT:** Compute all murine statistical comparisons strictly at the animal/donor level (or using a linear mixed-effects model with mouse ID and human donor ID as random effects), and revise the main text (line 206) to state that anti-PD-L1 growth and IHC differences showed numerical trends in a small pilot cohort ($n = 2\text{--}3$ mice/group) rather than statistically significant effects.
* **HOW:** Specify in the Figure 4G–K caption how many unique human R and NR stool donors were used across the FMT experiments, remove significance asterisks derived from pooling microscopic HPFs across $n = 2$ mice in Figures 4J–K, and qualify the text on line 206 to reflect $p = 0.20$.

#### 2. Account for Pre-Treatment Antibiotic Exposure, BMI, and Diet in Microbiome Survival Models
* **WHY:** Recent systemic antibiotic exposure prior to anti-PD-1 initiation is a strong confounder that simultaneously depletes gut $\alpha$-diversity / *Ruminococcaceae* and worsens ICI progression-free survival. Furthermore, selecting a multivariate Cox model via forward stepwise selection (line 194) in a sample of $n = 39$ patients (with $\sim 15\text{--}20$ progression events) is prone to overfitting and unstable hazard ratio estimates.
* **WHAT:** Report baseline antibiotic exposure (within 1–3 months prior to stool collection), BMI, and PPI/steroid use in Table S2, and fit a pre-specified multivariable Cox model adjusting for prior systemic therapy, LDH/M-stage, and recent antibiotic use rather than relying on stepwise selection.
* **HOW:** Add a sensitivity analysis to Table S6 showing the hazard ratios for Inverse Simpson diversity and *Faecalibacterium* abundance after excluding patients who received antibiotics within 30 days of baseline sampling (or adjusting for antibiotic status as a covariate).

#### 3. Clarify Patient Attrition Across Assay Modalities ($N = 112 \to 89 \to 53 \to 43 \to 39$)
* **WHY:** The sample sizes drop substantially across paragraphs: 112 total melanoma patients enrolled $\to$ 89 eligible/evaluable for response at 6 months (54 R, 35 NR) $\to$ 86 evaluable oral samples (54 R, 32 NR) $\to$ 53 total fecal samples $\to$ 43 evaluable fecal samples (30 R, 13 NR) $\to$ 39 fecal samples in the baseline diversity tertile and *Faecalibacterium*/*Bacteroidales* KM PFS analyses (Figures 1D–E and 3C).
* **WHAT:** Explain why 4 of the 43 evaluable fecal patients (30 R + 13 NR = 43) were excluded from the $n = 39$ Kaplan–Meier PFS survival analyses in Figure 1D–E ($13 + 13 + 13 = 39$) and Figure 3C ($19 + 20 = 39$).
* **HOW:** Explicitly state the reason for the 4 excluded samples (e.g., whether 4 fecal samples were collected post-baseline rather than at baseline, as hinted by "fecal samples (n=39)... at baseline" in the Figure 1D caption) and confirm that the Figure 1C Mann–Whitney test ($n = 43$) remains significant when restricted to the $n = 39$ baseline-only samples.
