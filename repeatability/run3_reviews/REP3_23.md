### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study integrates single-cell RNA-seq data from 7 bladder cancer (BC) patients (`GSE135337`, restricted to 1,517 non-epithelial tumor microenvironment cells out of 36,300 total cells) with bulk RNA-seq and overall survival (OS) data from 89 advanced BC patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) (`GSE176307`). Using the `Scissor` algorithm (Cox regression with $\alpha = 0.1$), the authors identify 115 `Scissor-` cells associated with favorable survival (primarily macrophages and plasma B cells) and 173 `Scissor+` cells associated with poor survival (monocytes and fibroblasts). From 15 upregulated genes in `Scissor-` cells, they select 10 genes (`SSR4`, `RGS1`, `HLA-DRB5`, `APOE`, `C1QB`, `C1QA`, `APOC1`, `JCHAIN`, `C1QC`, and `DERL3`) to construct a bladder cancer gene signature (`BC-GS`) defined as the mean z-score of their expression. They evaluate `BC-GS` alone and stratified with tumor mutation burden (TMB) in the discovery cohort (`GSE176307`, $n=89$) and two external cohorts (`IMvigor210` bladder subset, $n=195$; `UC-GENOME`, $n=101$), reporting significant survival stratification and enrichment of CD8+ T effector, antigen presentation, and M1 macrophage/CD4+ memory T cell signatures. Custom analysis code is provided on GitHub.

While the integration of scRNA-seq and bulk ICI cohorts addresses an important clinical problem and shows replication across two external datasets, the study suffers from three major methodological flaws that undermine its validity as a standalone predictive tool: (1) post-hoc feature selection and training-set circularity, where the 10-gene signature was selected because those genes were "stably overexpressed... across the datasets" (line 63) and `GSE176307` is simultaneously used as the discovery cohort to train `Scissor` and presented in Figures 2–4 as one of "three independent datasets" for survival validation; (2) severe statistical misapplication in differential expression, where the authors claim to use "pseudobulk methods" to mitigate single-cell bias (line 53) but actually run Seurat's `FindMarkers(..., test.use = "DESeq")` directly on individual cells (115 `Scissor-` cells vs. background cells across only 7 patients); and (3) univariate survival testing without multivariable adjustment for baseline clinical prognostic factors (e.g., ECOG performance status, visceral/liver metastases, prior chemotherapy, PD-L1 IHC) or formal interaction testing with treatment to distinguish prognostic from ICI-predictive value. These issues are salvageable through computational re-analysis of the existing datasets.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **High `BC-GS` is associated with longer overall survival in ICI-treated bladder cancer cohorts: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In the two external cohorts not used as input to `Scissor`, patients above the median `BC-GS` show improved OS (`IMvigor210` $n=195$: $\text{HR} = 0.70$, 95% CI $0.49\text{--}0.99$, $p = 0.043$; `UC-GENOME` $n=101$: $\text{HR} = 0.36$, 95% CI $0.20\text{--}0.67$, $p = 0.00081$). However, the effect in the largest cohort (`IMvigor210`) is borderline ($p = 0.043$, upper CI $0.99$), the Methods (line 63) indicate that the reduction from 15 DEGs to 10 genes was informed by expression stability "across the datasets" (introducing lookahead leakage into the validation sets), and `GSE176307` ($\text{HR} = 0.49$, $p = 0.008$) is the discovery cohort rather than an independent validation set.

* **Differential expression analysis using pseudobulk methods accurately identifies transcriptional markers of survival-associated `Scissor-` cells: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Methods (line 53), the authors state: *"To identify the DEGs in Scissor- cells compared to that in other cells without Scissor+ cells, the 'FindMarkers' function in Seurat with the DESeq test argument was used [18]. To mitigate bias toward highly expressed genes, pseudobulk methods were used instead of single-cell differential expression approaches [19]."* Calling `FindMarkers(test.use = "DESeq2")` on a cell-level Seurat object does **not** perform patient-level pseudobulk aggregation; it treats each of the 115 `Scissor-` cells as an independent biological replicate ($n = 115$) rather than aggregating counts per patient across the 7 tumors in `GSE135337`, severely inflating Type I error rates due to intra-patient cell-to-cell correlation. Furthermore, transforming already length-normalized TPM values with `limma::voom` (line 63), which is designed for raw count matrices to model the mean-variance relationship of sequencing depth, is a statistical misspecification.

* **Combining `BC-GS` and TMB significantly improves prognostic stratification over TMB alone: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Stratifying high-TMB patients by median `BC-GS` (`HH` vs. `LH`) yields significant log-rank separation across all three cohorts (Figure 4c). However, the authors only perform 4-group log-rank tests and pairwise subgroup log-rank comparisons (`HH` vs. `LH`); they do not fit a multivariable Cox model containing both continuous `BC-GS` and continuous `TMB` (and their interaction term) or compute the change in concordance index ($\Delta\text{C-index}$ / likelihood ratio test) to prove that `BC-GS` adds statistically significant predictive power beyond TMB and clinical covariates.

* **The 10-gene `BC-GS` signature reflects CD8+ T cell activation and antigen presentation pathways: [Overstated].**  
  The Abstract (line 8) and Introduction (line 24) state that *"Genes in the BC-GS were predominantly involved in CD8+ T cell activation, antigen presentation, and immune checkpoint pathways."* In reality, the 10 `BC-GS` genes themselves are complement (`C1QA`, `C1QB`, `C1QC`), apolipoprotein (`APOE`, `APOC1`), plasma B cell/ER (`JCHAIN`, `SSR4`, `DERL3`), `HLA-DRB5` (MHC class II), and `RGS1`—which Reactome enrichment correctly maps to plasma lipoprotein and complement cascades (Figure 2b), not CD8+ T cell activation or immune checkpoints. The CD8+ T effector and checkpoint differences shown in Figure 3a–b are simply downstream bulk tumor pathway correlations in `GSE176307` (reflecting general immune-infiltrated "hot" vs. "cold" tumors), not pathways encoded by the `BC-GS` genes.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Lookahead Leakage and Separate Discovery from Validation Cohorts
* **WHY:** Selecting the 10 signature genes based on whether they were "stably overexpressed... across the datasets" (line 63) leaks information from `IMvigor210` and `UC-GENOME` into signature definition, and labeling the training cohort `GSE176307` as one of "three independent datasets" (lines 103, 109) overstates validation performance.
* **WHAT:** Define the gene signature strictly within the discovery data (`GSE135337` + `GSE176307`) using a pre-specified, blinded rule, and lock all gene weights/thresholds before evaluating `IMvigor210` and `UC-GENOME`.
* **HOW:** Use all 15 upregulated DEGs (or filter strictly within `GSE176307` without inspecting `IMvigor210` or `UC-GENOME`), compute the signature score in the two external validation cohorts, and clearly label `GSE176307` as the "Discovery / Training Cohort" in all text and figures (Figures 2, 3, 4, and 5).

#### 2. Perform True Patient-Level Pseudobulk Differential Expression and Proper Normalization
* **WHY:** Running `Seurat::FindMarkers(test.use = "DESeq")` on individual cells treats cells from the same patient as independent replicates, and applying `limma::voom` to TPM values violates `voom`'s requirement for raw count data.
* **WHAT:** Aggregate single-cell raw UMI counts by patient and cell status (`Scissor-` vs. other cells across the 7 patients in `GSE135337`) before running `DESeq2`, and replace `voom` on TPMs with standard $\log_2(\text{TPM} + 1)$ transformation before z-scoring.
* **HOW:** Use `AggregateExpression()` or `muscat` in R to sum raw UMI counts per patient per group in `GSE135337`, run `DESeq2` with patient ID as a blocking factor, and compute `BC-GS` from $\log_2(\text{TPM} + 1)$ z-scores in `GSE176307` and `IMvigor210`.

#### 3. Fit Multivariable Cox Models Adjusting for Clinical Confounders and TMB
* **WHY:** Unadjusted Kaplan–Meier curves dichotomized at the median do not establish whether `BC-GS` provides independent prognostic information beyond TMB, PD-L1 IHC, and standard clinical variables (age, sex, ECOG performance status, visceral/liver metastasis).
* **WHAT:** Fit multivariable Cox proportional hazards models in `GSE176307`, `IMvigor210`, and `UC-GENOME` including continuous and dichotomized `BC-GS`, `TMB`, PD-L1 status (available in `IMvigor210`), and clinical covariates.
* **HOW:** Report a multivariable regression table with adjusted Hazard Ratios, 95% CIs, $p$-values, and Harrell's C-index comparing (a) clinical covariates + TMB alone vs. (b) clinical covariates + TMB + `BC-GS` (with a likelihood ratio test $p$-value for the added value of `BC-GS`).

#### 4. Clarify Cell Selection Criteria in `GSE135337` and Align Pathway Claims
* **WHY:** The authors state they selected 1,517 non-epithelial TME cells out of 36,300 total cells in `GSE135337` (line 92), but do not explain whether the remaining ~34,783 cells were all epithelial cells or if additional subsampling was performed. Moreover, the Abstract inaccurately claims the `BC-GS` genes themselves belong to CD8+ T cell activation and checkpoint pathways.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact cell-type breakdown of all 36,300 cells in `GSE135337` and revise the Abstract and Introduction to accurately state that the 10 `BC-GS` genes encode macrophage complement/apolipoprotein (`C1QA/B/C`, `APOE`, `APOC1`) and plasma B cell (`JCHAIN`, `SSR4`, `DERL3`) markers that correlate with bulk CD8+ T effector infiltration.
* **HOW:** Add the cell counts per cluster for the full 36,300-cell dataset to Supplementary Table 2/3, and edit line 8 of the Abstract and line 24 of the Introduction to distinguish the intrinsic biological function of the 10 genes from the correlated bulk tumor pathways.
