### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the emergence, reassortment, and spread of highly pathogenic (HP) and low pathogenic (LP) avian influenza A/H7N9 in Guangdong province during the fifth epidemic wave (2016–2017). Drawing on longitudinal surveillance of 34,106 environmental and poultry swabs across 21 prefecture-level cities in Guangdong from January 2016 to August 2017, the authors sequenced 297 full viral genomes (58 HP A/H7N9, 19 LP A/H7N9, and 220 A/H9N2) and combined them with over 15,000 publicly available A/H7N9 and A/H9N2 segment sequences from GISAID and NCBI. Using maximum-likelihood phylogenetics (`IQ-TREE`) and Bayesian molecular clock phylogeography (`BEAST 1.8`), they demonstrate that three H7N9 lineages co-circulated in Guangdong during Wave 5 (local LP Pearl River Delta [PRD] lineage at 13%, newly imported LP Yangtze River Delta [YRD] lineage at 23%, and HP YRD lineage at 64%), that HP A/H7N9 evolved from Wave 3/4 YRD viruses and underwent extensive internal gene reassortment with imported YRD LP and local H9N2 viruses (generating 15 HP genotypes in Guangdong), and that 93.7% (45/48) of human HP cases carried the V1 HA cleavage site motif (`PKRKRTAR↓G`) compared to 49.6% (57/115) of live poultry market (LPM) isolates. Newly generated sequences are deposited in GISAID (Supplementary Table S1).

This is a valuable, well-executed genomic surveillance study that pairs dense longitudinal LPM sampling with multi-segment phylodynamic analysis and poultry trade records. Its primary limitations are: (1) reliance on descriptive percentages rather than formal, spatially stratified statistical tests when claiming that the V1 HA cleavage site motif facilitates human infection (despite Figure 5 showing strong geographic confounding between V1 vs. V3 distribution across Guangdong regions); (2) unaddressed passaging and within-host selection biases when comparing amino acid substitutions between human clinical isolates and egg-passaged LPM isolates (Table 2); and (3) minor inconsistencies in geographic region definitions and denominator counts in Table 2. These issues can be resolved through straightforward statistical testing, textual clarification, and table corrections.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Co-circulation of three A/H7N9 lineages in Guangdong during Wave 5 and replacement of local PRD internal genes via reassortment with imported YRD LP and local A/H9N2 viruses: [Well Supported].**  
  The multi-segment phylogenetic analyses (Figures 3 and 4; Supplementary Figs. S1 and S2) across all eight viral segments clearly establish that Wave 5 HP A/H7N9 (Clade C-1) derived its HA from Wave 3/4 YRD-lineage viruses while acquiring internal segments from Clades 1, 2, and 3 YRD LP viruses and local H9N2 strains, consistent with the 5.5 billion live birds imported into Guangdong (Figure 2).

* **Genotype distributions indicate limited outward spread of HP A/H7N9 from Guangdong to other provinces compared to inward importation of LP A/H7N9: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Twelve of the 15 HP A/H7N9 genotypes identified in Guangdong were unique to Guangdong, while genotype G108 accounted for 66% (83/125) of HP sequences nationwide (Results, line 133). However, because Guangdong contributed 71% (89/125) of all sequenced HP A/H7N9 genomes in the dataset (line 117) due to the authors' intensive local LPM surveillance, rare transient reassortant genotypes are much more likely to be detected in Guangdong than in less intensively sampled provinces. This sampling asymmetry should be noted when interpreting genotype endemicity.

* **The difference in HA cleavage site motif distribution between LPMs (49.6% V1, 43.4% V3) and humans (93.7% V1, 2.1% V3) suggests the V1 motif facilitates human infection or poultry replication: [Fragile].**  
  In the Abstract (line 8), Results (line 143), and Discussion (line 217), the authors highlight that V1 accounts for 45/48 (93.7%) of human cases vs. 57/115 (49.6%) of LPM isolates. First, no formal statistical test ($p$-value or odds ratio) is reported. Second, and more importantly, the comparison is confounded by geography and phylogenetic non-independence: as shown in Figure 5 and stated in line 143, V1 was widely distributed across northern, western, central, and eastern Guangdong (where human cases occurred), whereas V3 was geographically restricted to specific cities in central and eastern Guangdong. Without stratifying by prefecture/city (e.g., via a Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test) or testing for phylogenetic association (`BaTS` / phylogenetic logistic regression), spatial founder effects cannot be separated from intrinsic phenotypic differences between V1 (`PKRKRTAR↓G`) and V3 (`PKGKRTA R↓G`, which differs by a single R$\to$G substitution).

* **Human-exclusive substitutions in PB2 (E627K, D701N), NA (R292K), and HA (Q226L/H) distinguish human from environmental HP A/H7N9 isolates: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Table 2 accurately catalogues these substitutions across 48 human and 58 environmental HP A/H7N9 genomes. However, two biological processes confound direct host comparisons: (1) PB2 E627K/D701N and NA R292K frequently emerge *de novo* within infected human patients during replication in the mammalian respiratory tract and under neuraminidase inhibitor (oseltamivir) treatment, rather than reflecting pre-existing variants transmitted from LPMs; and (2) all environmental LPM isolates were blindly passaged for 2–3 generations in 9- to 10-day-old embryonated chicken eggs prior to sequencing (Section 2.2, line 30), which strongly selects against mammalian-adapted variants (such as HA 226L or PB2 627K) and favors avian-like consensus sequences.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Geographic Confounding and Clonal Relatedness in the V1 vs. V3 Cleavage Site Comparison
* **WHY:** Comparing province-wide human V1 frequency (45/48) to province-wide LPM V1 frequency (57/115) ignores that V3 was spatially restricted to a subset of cities (Figure 5) and may represent a single local LPM amplification cluster.
* **WHAT:** Perform a city-stratified statistical comparison of V1 vs. non-V1 motifs in human vs. LPM samples and check whether the 50 V3 LPM isolates represent a single monophyletic outbreak cluster on the HA tree.
* **HOW:** Group the 48 human and 115 LPM HP A/H7N9 sequences by prefecture-level city (from Figure 5) and run a stratified Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test (as well as an unstratified Fisher's exact test restricted to the cities in central and eastern Guangdong where both V1 and V3 co-circulated). Report the adjusted odds ratio and $95\%$ confidence interval in Section 3.5.

#### 2. Account for Sampling Intensity in Genotype Endemicity and Phylogeographic Inferences
* **WHY:** With 89 of 125 (71%) HP A/H7N9 genomes originating from Guangdong (line 117), observing more unique HP genotypes in Guangdong (12 unique out of 15) than in Eastern (5) or Central (2) China is partly a consequence of deeper sampling in Guangdong.
* **WHAT:** Perform rarefaction / subsampling of the Guangdong HP A/H7N9 genomes to match the sample size of other regions when comparing genotype richness, and report posterior support (Bayes factors / posterior probabilities) for the BEAST discrete phylogeographic transition rates.
* **HOW:** Randomly subsample $n = 36$ HP A/H7N9 genomes from the 89 Guangdong genomes across 1,000 replicates to match the non-Guangdong sample size ($125 - 89 = 36$), compute the expected number of unique genotypes, and add a column or sentence in Section 3.3 reporting the BEAST discrete trait Bayes factors for inward vs. outward viral migration.

#### 3. Discuss Egg-Passaging Selection and Within-Host Evolution for Table 2 Markers
* **WHY:** Blindly passaging LPM swabs for 2–3 generations in embryonated chicken eggs (Section 2.2, line 30) can purge low-frequency mammalian-adaptive alleles (PB2 627K, HA 226L), while clinical antiviral treatment drives within-host selection of NA R292K in hospitalized humans.
* **WHAT:** Expand the Discussion (line 217) to explicitly distinguish pre-spillover LPM mutations (such as HA G186V and PB2 K526R, present in 77.5–100% of egg-passaged LPM isolates) from post-infection human within-host adaptations (PB2 E627K/D701N, NA R292K).
* **HOW:** Add a paragraph in Section 4 noting that 2–3 passages in embryonated chicken eggs may select against mammalian receptor-binding variants in LPM isolates and that NA R292K (27.1% in humans vs. 0% in LPMs) reflects oseltamivir selection in treated patients.

#### 4. Harmonize Geographic Region Definitions Between Sections 2.4 and 2.5
* **WHY:** Sections 2.4 (line 40) and 2.5 (line 47) use mutually inconsistent provincial groupings: Section 2.4 defines six regions (placing Henan in Central, Fujian in Eastern, Gansu in North-western, and Hainan in Southern), whereas Section 2.5 defines seven regions for BEAST phylogeography (moving Henan to Northern, Fujian to Southeastern, Gansu to Southwestern, and Guangdong to Southern).
* **WHAT:** Reconcile or explicitly explain the difference between the agricultural trade region groupings (Section 2.4) and the BEAST phylogeographic discrete trait groupings (Section 2.5).
* **HOW:** In Section 2.5 (line 47), note why Gansu and Henan were reassigned relative to Section 2.4 (or harmonize the region definitions across both sections if Gansu's placement in "Southwestern China" in line 47 was a typographical error for "Northwestern China").

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Several numerical and typographical errors appear in Table 2 and the text.
* **WHAT & HOW:**

Because the denominator for V1 human PB2 sequences is $n = 44$ ($46 / 44 = 104.5\%$) while total human PB2 sequences is $n = 46$, the total count (`46`) was mistakenly entered into the V1 column instead of `44 (100)`. Change `46 (104.5)` to `44 (100)`.

The sum of those six internal segments is $1,159 + 1,150 + 1,162 + 1,151 + 1,172 + 1,140 = 6,934$ (not $6,950$). Correct the total or segment counts.
