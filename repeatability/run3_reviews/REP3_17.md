### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study integrates three lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) single-cell RNA-seq datasets (GSE150938, GSE189357, and HRA001130) spanning atypical adenomatous hyperplasia (AAH), adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), and invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC). Using `inferCNV`, `GSVA`, `Monocle2`, `scMetabolism`, and `CytoTRACE`, the authors identify nine malignant epithelial clusters and highlight Clusters 0 and 6 as increasing in abundance from AAH to IAC and correlating with worse survival in TCGA. They then evaluate combinations of 10 machine learning algorithms on Cluster 0 and 6 marker genes across TCGA (training), six GEO validation datasets ($n = 720$ total across GSE13213, GSE26939, GSE29016, GSE30219, GSE31210, and GSE42127), and the OAK/POPLAR immunotherapy cohorts ($n = 296$), selecting a CoxBoost + plsRcox composite prognostic model (CPM). Finally, they perform in vitro siRNA knockdown of a top model gene, *S100A16*, in A549 and H1299 cell lines, demonstrating reduced colony formation, wound healing, and transwell migration/invasion. Public accessions are listed, but neither custom analysis scripts nor the exact CPM gene weights are provided in the main text.

Combining single-cell stage progression data (AAH $\to$ AIS $\to$ MIA $\to$ IAC) with multi-cohort bulk survival validation and in vitro functional assays is a constructive study design. However, three methodological issues weaken the manuscript's quantitative claims: (1) validation-set leakage during model selection, where the winning CoxBoost + plsRcox algorithm was chosen specifically because it achieved the highest average C-index across the six external validation cohorts, which simultaneously inflates its subsequent benchmark ranking against 144 published signatures on those same six cohorts; (2) an internal biological contradiction between the `Monocle2` pseudotime orientation (which places Clusters 0 and 6 at the *earliest* nascent stage declining over pseudotime) and the clinicopathological staging data (which show Clusters 0 and 6 are lowest in early pre-invasive AAH/AIS and peak in late invasive IAC); and (3) omission of the complete CPM gene list, regression coefficients, and replication/statistical details for the in vitro knockdown assays. These flaws can be remedied by selecting the machine learning model strictly via cross-validation within TCGA, rooting the pseudotime trajectory on AAH/normal-like cells, and reporting full model parameters.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Identification of nine malignant epithelial clusters with Clusters 0 and 6 increasing in proportion from pre-invasive (AAH/AIS/MIA) to invasive (IAC) LUAD: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The `inferCNV` separation of high-CNV malignant epithelial clusters from endothelial and low-CNV reference clusters (Figure 2D, E) and the progressive increase in sample-level proportions of Clusters 0 and 6 from AAH to IAC (Figure 5A, B) are plausible and supported by high MYC target, cell-cycle, DNA repair, and glycolytic activity in Cluster 6 (Figures 3A and 4A). However, Figure 5A and 5B pool cells across patients without reporting patient-level variance or a formal statistical test (e.g., Jonckheere-Terpstra trend test or Dirichlet/beta-binomial regression across patients) to confirm that the stage increase is not driven by 1–2 high-cell-count IAC donors.

* **Clusters 0 and 6 represent an "early developmental stage" that diminishes over pseudotime: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 3.2 (line 104; Figure 3B, C), the authors state: *"Intriguingly, Clusters 0 and 6 localize to the nascent stages of development, with a subsequent diminution in prevalence over time, as illustrated in Figure 3C."* Yet in Section 3.4 (line 126; Figure 5A, B), Clusters 0 and 6 are scarce in precancerous AAH and progressively expand to dominate late-stage invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC), and carry the highest `CytoTRACE` undifferentiation/stemness scores and proliferation signatures (Figure 4B). Because `Monocle2` (`DDRTree`) learns an unrooted manifold and assigns pseudotime $= 0$ to whichever branch end is chosen as the root, rooting the trajectory at the dedifferentiated, highly proliferative IAC-enriched state (Clusters 0 and 6) reverses the true chronological tumor progression (AAH $\to$ AIS $\to$ MIA $\to$ IAC), making late-stage dedifferentiation appear as "nascent early development."

* **The CoxBoost + plsRcox (CPM) signature achieves superior prognostic accuracy across six validation cohorts compared to 144 published LUAD signatures: [Overstated].**  
  In Section 3.5 (line 137), the authors explicitly state: *"The TCGA dataset was employed as the training cohort, with six GEO datasets utilized for validation purposes. The criterion for model selection was based on the average c-index across the six validation cohorts."* Selecting the best algorithm combination by maximizing the average C-index on the six validation cohorts constitutes direct test-set selection leakage. Furthermore, in Section 2.1 (line 29), applying `sva::ComBat` across datasets prior to evaluation leaks cross-cohort batch parameters. When the authors subsequently compare their validation-selected CPM model against 144 fixed literature signatures on those exact same six GEO datasets (Section 3.6, line 148; Figure 7), the comparison is biased in favor of CPM by post-hoc selection on the test sets.

* **Knockdown of *S100A16* inhibits proliferation, migration, and invasion of A549 and H1299 LUAD cells: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The colony formation, wound healing, and Matrigel transwell assays in A549 and H1299 cells (Figure 9) show consistent reductions upon `si-S100A16` transfection and align with prior literature on *S100A16* in LUAD. However, only a single siRNA sequence (without a second independent siRNA or rescue control to rule out off-target seed effects) is described in Section 2.7, the number of independent biological replicates ($n$) is omitted from Section 2.11 and the Figure 9 legend, and the wound-healing assay (48 h in 10% FBS medium without mitomycin C or serum starvation, Section 2.9) conflates cell proliferation with true migration.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Validation Leakage by Selecting the Machine Learning Model Strictly Within TCGA Cross-Validation
* **WHY:** Picking `CoxBoost + plsRcox` out of all candidate machine learning pipelines based on its average C-index across the six GEO validation cohorts (Section 3.5, line 137) invalidates those six cohorts as independent test sets and artificially inflates CPM's ranking against the 144 published signatures (Figure 7).
* **WHAT:** Select the optimal machine learning algorithm using 10-fold cross-validation C-index strictly within the TCGA training cohort, lock the model weights, and evaluate the locked model on the six GEO cohorts without cross-cohort `ComBat` pooling.
* **HOW:** Compute the mean 10-fold cross-validated C-index inside TCGA for each of the candidate algorithm combinations, pick the top-performing model on TCGA CV alone, standardize each GEO validation cohort independently (within-cohort $z$-score of log-TPM without pooling across cohorts in `ComBat`), and update Figures 6 and 7.

#### 2. Re-Root the `Monocle2` Pseudotime Trajectory Using Histopathological Stage (AAH) and Normal Epithelial Reference Cells
* **WHY:** Placing Clusters 0 and 6 (which peak in invasive IAC and drive poor survival) at the start of pseudotime (Figure 3C) contradicts the clinicopathological progression from precancerous AAH to invasive IAC (Figure 5A, B).
* **WHAT:** Specify the biological root state of the `Monocle2` trajectory as the branch enriched for pre-invasive AAH cells (or low-CNV Clusters 1 and 2 from Figure 2E) so that pseudotime increases along the AAH $\to$ AIS $\to$ MIA $\to$ IAC progression axis.
* **HOW:** In `Monocle2`, call `orderCells(cds, root_state = state_with_max_AAH_proportion)` to orient pseudotime from AAH toward IAC, re-plot Figure 3B and 3C, and interpret high `CytoTRACE` scores in Clusters 0 and 6 as malignant dedifferentiation / acquisition of stem-like plasticity during invasive progression.

#### 3. Test Patient-Level Proportions Across Histopathological Stages and Publish the Complete CPM Formula
* **WHY:** Aggregating single cells across donors can allow a single sample with many sequenced cells to dominate cluster proportions in Figure 5A, and readers cannot reproduce or apply the CPM model because its constituent genes and coefficients are never listed in the manuscript.
* **WHAT:** Add a table listing every gene in the `CoxBoost + plsRcox` model alongside its exact regression coefficient, and test cluster proportion trends at the patient/sample level across AAH, AIS, MIA, and IAC.
* **HOW:** Include a main-text table (or Supplementary Table) with the exact linear predictor formula $\text{CPM} = \sum_i \beta_i x_i$, and plot sample-level boxplots of Cluster 0 and Cluster 6 fraction across patients grouped by stage (AAH, AIS, MIA, IAC) with a Kruskal-Wallis / Jonckheere-Terpstra trend $p$-value.

#### 4. Report Biological Replicate Counts, siRNA Sequences, and Proliferation Controls for *S100A16* Assays
* **WHY:** Neither the siRNA target sequence(s), knockdown verification method (qRT-PCR primers vs. Western blot in Figure 9A), nor the number of biological replicates ($n$) are stated in Sections 2.7–2.11 or Figure 9.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact nucleotide sequences of `si-S100A16` and `si-NC`, specify the number of independent biological replicates per assay in Figure 9, and clarify whether serum-free medium was used during the 48 h wound-healing and transwell migration assays.
* **HOW:** Add the siRNA and primer sequences to Section 2.7, state $n$ and the exact statistical test (e.g., two-tailed unpaired $t$-test across $n = 3$ independent biological replicates) in the Figure 9 legend, and note that 48 h wound closure reflects combined proliferation and migration if performed in 10% FBS.
