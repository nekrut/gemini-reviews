### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces `BUSTED` (Branch-Site Unrestricted Statistical Test for Episodic Diversification), a random-effects codon substitution likelihood ratio test designed to detect gene-wide evidence of episodic positive selection ($\omega > 1$ at a subset of sites along a subset of all branches or a pre-specified foreground branch partition). Building on the unrestricted branch-site random effects (`BS-REL`) framework with three $\omega$ rate categories ($\omega_1 \le \omega_2 \le 1 \le \omega_3$, weighted by $p_1 + p_2 + p_3 = 1$) estimated independently for foreground and background branch partitions, `BUSTED` tests the null hypothesis that $\omega_3 = 1$ on the foreground branches while allowing unconstrained $\omega_3 \ge 1$ on background branches. The authors bound the intractable asymptotic mixture distribution ($\chi_0^2, \chi_1^2, \chi_2^2$) conservatively using $\chi_2^2$, and introduce a zero-cost exploratory site-level Evidence Ratio ($\text{ER}_s = 2 \log [P(D_s | M) / P(D_s | M_s)]$) by clamping $\omega_3 = 1$ at site $s$ without re-optimizing global parameters. Across simulated benchmarks from Lu and Guindon (2014) ($N = 10\text{--}64$ taxa, 300 codons, 500 replicates per scenario), strict neutral simulations, and parametric simulations based on sperm lysin, `BUSTED` controls empirical Type I error below nominal levels ($0.006\text{--}0.020$ at $\alpha = 0.05$, whereas the covarion package `fitmodel` exhibits severe false positive inflation up to $15\%\text{--}23\%$) and achieves superior statistical power when calibrated at matched 5% Type I error rates. On empirical benchmarks, `BUSTED` site-level ERs identify 13/15 known HIV-1 RT drug resistance mutations (matching specialized directional models `MEDS` [13/17] and `EDEPS` [13/16]), and a large-scale screen of 10,779 Euteleostomes alignments from `Selectome` v06 detects episodic selection in 24.87% of genes without saturation loss across realistic tree lengths. Open-source `HyPhy` batch code and a `Datamonkey` web application are publicly available.

This is a concise, mathematically transparent, and well-benchmarked methodological contribution to molecular evolution. The model formulation directly solves two major failure modes in prior selection tests: (1) loss of gene-wide power when applying multiple-testing corrections across site-level tests (`MEME`), and (2) confounding or power loss in Nielsen–Yang branch-site models caused by assuming homogeneous $\omega$ distributions across all foreground or background branches. The limitations are clearly stated by the authors: the $\chi_2^2$ reference distribution is conservative (reducing power at very small branch/site proportions such as `Tree 1` $\alpha\text{XV}$ when foreground is unspecified), the site-wise Evidence Ratios do not have calibrated frequentist $p$-values because nuisance parameters are not re-optimized per site, and the baseline model assumes constant synonymous substitution rates ($d_S$) across sites and only single-nucleotide instantaneous substitutions.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **`BUSTED` maintains conservative Type I error control across neutral and purifying/neutral branch-site variation scenarios, whereas the covarion test (`fitmodel`) can be strongly anticonservative: [Well Supported].**  
  In Table 1 (`Tree 1` $\alpha\text{XX}$, `16 XX`, `32 XX`, `64 XX`), `BUSTED`'s empirical false positive rate at nominal $\alpha = 0.05$ ranges from $0.006$ to $0.020$, whereas `fitmodel` (`LGc`) reaches $0.150$ on the 64-taxon null (`64 XX`) and $0.23$ at $P \le 0.05$ ($0.13$ at $P \le 0.01$) on null simulations parameterized from sperm lysin (Figure 2B). The conservative $\chi_2^2$ bound for the $\chi_0^2 + \chi_1^2 + \chi_2^2$ boundary mixture is theoretically sound (Self and Liang 1987).

* **At calibrated Type I error rates or with specified foreground branches, `BUSTED` achieves higher power than `fitmodel` and the Nielsen–Yang branch-site (`NYbs`) model: [Well Supported].**  
  When the foreground branches are specified (`BUSTED FG`), `BUSTED` outperforms `NYbs` on 4 of 5 power scenarios in Table 1 (e.g., $0.259$ vs. $0.102$ on `Tree 1` $\alpha\text{XV}$; $0.850$ vs. $0.785$ on `16 B XU`; $0.930$ vs. $0.754$ on `32 B XU`), losing only on the smallest 10-taxon tree (`Tree 2` $\delta\gamma\text{XV}$). When testing the entire phylogeny without a priori foreground specification (`BUSTED`), it outperforms `LGc` whenever a moderate fraction of sites ($p_s = 0.20$) experiences selection (`16 B XU`: $0.290$ vs. $0.122$; `32 B XU`: $0.540$ vs. $0.020$) and outperforms size-calibrated `fitmodel` across all $\omega_3 > 1$ values in Figure 2C.

* **Exploratory site-level Evidence Ratios ($\text{ER}_s > \chi_{1, 0.01}^2$) accurately localize positively selected codons in HIV-1 reverse transcriptase: [Well Supported].**  
  Applied to the HIV-1 RT post-treatment branch partition (Table 2), `BUSTED` flags 15 sites at $2\log(l_s) > 6.635$ ($\chi_{1, 0.01}^2$), of which 13 are confirmed Stanford HIVDB resistance mutations ($\text{TP/All} = 13/15$, compared to $3/6$ for the fixed-effects site test `FEEDS`, $13/17$ for `MEDS`, and $13/16$ for `EDEPS`). The authors appropriately caveat (line 41, line 114) that $\text{ER}_s$ is an exploratory diagnostic rather than a formal likelihood ratio test.

* **Across 10,779 vertebrate gene alignments (`Selectome` v06), detection of episodic selection scales with alignment length and $\hat{\omega}_3$ without degrading at longer tree lengths: [Well Supported].**  
  The large-scale empirical screen (Figure 3A–D) demonstrates expected statistical behavior: power increases monotonically with codon count (sample size of independent sites) and with the MLE of $\omega_3$ (saturating near the numerical optimization ceiling $\omega_3 \approx 40$), while remaining stable across realistic total tree lengths.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Derive or Empirically Estimate the Mixture Weights of $\chi_0^2 : \chi_1^2 : \chi_2^2$ to Reduce Over-Conservatism
* **WHY:** Using a pure $\chi_2^2$ distribution makes the test overly conservative (empirical Type I error of $0.006\text{--}0.016$ at nominal $\alpha = 0.05$ in Table 1), which noticeably reduces power in sparse regimes such as `Tree 2` $\delta\gamma\text{XV}$ ($N = 10$ taxa, where `NYbs` achieves $0.368$ vs. `BUSTED FG` $0.058$) and `Tree 1` $\alpha\text{XV}$ unconstrained ($0.008$).
* **WHAT:** Evaluate whether a standard constrained boundary mixture (e.g., $0.5\chi_0^2 + 0.5\chi_1^2$ when $\hat{\omega}_2 < 1$, or an empirical mixture such as $0.45\chi_0^2 + 0.45\chi_1^2 + 0.10\chi_2^2$) maintains nominal $\alpha \le 0.05$ across the null simulation suite while recovering power on small alignments.
* **HOW:** Tabulate the empirical 95th and 99th percentiles of the LRT statistic across the four null scenarios in Table 1 (`Tree 1` $\alpha\text{XX}$, `16 XX`, `32 XX`, `64 XX`) and the lysin null simulations, compare them against the critical values of $\chi_2^2$ ($5.99$) vs. $0.5\chi_1^2 + 0.5\chi_2^2$ ($5.14$), and report the resulting power gain on `Tree 2` $\delta\gamma\text{XV}$ in Supplementary Table S1.

#### 2. Quantify the Sensitivity of `BUSTED` to Synonymous Rate Variation ($d_S$) Across Sites
* **WHY:** In the Discussion (line 178), the authors note that `BUSTED` does not currently model site-to-site synonymous rate variation ($d_S$), which acts as a proxy for selection at the DNA/RNA level. When $d_S$ varies strongly across sites (e.g., due to RNA secondary structure in viral genomes or splicing enhancers), sites with depressed $d_S$ and normal $d_N$ can exhibit elevated $d_N/d_S$ ratios that mimic episodic positive selection under a constant-$d_S$ model.
* **WHAT:** Discuss how site-to-site synonymous rate variation affects the false positive rate of `BUSTED` and clarify whether the 3-rate random-effects $\omega$ distribution can be parameterized with separate synonymous ($\alpha_s$) and nonsynonymous ($\beta_{s,b}$) rates.
* **HOW:** Add a brief paragraph or supplementary simulation check running `BUSTED` on an alignment simulated with gamma-distributed site-to-site synonymous rates ($\alpha_s \sim \text{Gamma}$) and $\omega \le 1$ everywhere to document when synonymous rate variation should be modeled.

#### 3. Clarify the Interpretation of Site-Level Evidence Ratios When the Gene-Wide Test Is Non-Significant
* **WHY:** Because $\text{ER}_s = 2 \log [P(D_s | M) / P(D_s | M_s)]$ is computed from the unconstrained alternative model $M$, users might be tempted to scan $\text{ER}_s$ across sites and claim site-level selection even when the omnibus gene-wide LRT fails to reject the null hypothesis ($P > 0.05$).
* **WHAT:** Explicitly recommend a gated (hierarchical) testing procedure in the "Evidence of Selection at Individual Sites" section.
* **HOW:** Add an explicit statement on line 41 (and in the `Datamonkey` web interface) that site-level Evidence Ratios should only be interpreted when the omnibus gene-wide `BUSTED` test rejects the null hypothesis (e.g., at $P \le 0.05$), functioning analogously to post-hoc comparisons following a significant omnibus ANOVA.
