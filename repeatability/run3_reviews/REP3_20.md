### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript constructs a character-based median-joining phylogenetic network (`Network 5011CS`, with Steiner tree post-processing at $\epsilon = 0$ and $\epsilon = 10$) of 160 human SARS-CoV-2 complete genomes sampled from GISAID up to March 4, 2020 (yielding 100 distinct human sequence haplotypes across positions 56 to 29,797), rooted with the bat coronavirus `BatCoVRaTG13` (96.2% identity). From this network, the authors define three central clusters—named A, B, and C—designating cluster A as the ancestral human root type based on its connection to the bat outgroup. They conclude that while types A and C are common in Europeans and Americans, ancestral type B is confined to East Asia and appears not to have spread outside East Asia without first mutating into derived B types, attributing this pattern to *"founder effects or immunological or environmental resistance against this type outside Asia."* They further claim that the median-joining network faithfully traces documented COVID-19 infection routes and can be used to trace undocumented infection sources for quarantine.

The manuscript's core evolutionary and epidemiological conclusions are fundamentally invalid due to three compounding methodological failures: (1) applying an uncalibrated, distance/parsimony heuristic (median-joining networks, designed for intraspecific mitochondrial haplotypes without a distant outgroup) and rooting a 160-genome human tree (where sequences differ by $0\text{–}10$ nucleotides) using a bat outgroup (`BatCoVRaTG13`) separated by $\sim 1,200$ nucleotide substitutions (3.8% divergence), where rampant homoplasy and C$\to$U hypermutation render the parsimony attachment point statistically arbitrary; (2) ignoring collection dates and severe early-pandemic ascertainment bias (160 opportunistically sequenced genomes worldwide as of March 4, 2020, dominated by targeted testing of returning travelers), leading to the biologically baseless speculation that "immunological or environmental resistance" prevented ancestral type B from spreading outside East Asia; and (3) equating one-mutation links in an uncalibrated parsimony network of 160 global genomes with direct person-to-person transmission routes when thousands of unsampled infections separate the sequenced cases. Because median-joining parsimony rooting over a 1,200-mutation outgroup branch is statistically unidentifiable and the 160-genome sample cannot support claims of host immunological resistance or direct transmission tracing, the main conclusions cannot be salvaged without complete methodological replacement.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Character-based median-joining phylogenetic networks are the "method of choice" for reconstructing the evolutionary paths and ancestral human genome of SARS-CoV-2: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Abstract (line 7) and Materials and Methods (line 16), the authors assert that because SARS-CoV-2 genomes are closely related and experience parallel mutations, median-joining networks (`Network 5011CS`) are the method of choice. Unlike likelihood- or Bayesian-based phylogenetic methods (`IQ-TREE`, `RAxML`, `BEAST`), the median-joining algorithm (`Bandelt et al. 1999`) is a heuristic parsimony network constructor that treats all nucleotide substitutions and up-to-24-bp deletions as equally weighted unweighted steps, lacks an explicit nucleotide substitution model (failing to account for the $>5$-fold transition/transversion and C$\to$U hypermutation bias in SARS-CoV-2), does not incorporate collection dates, and provides no statistical measure of branch or root support (such as bootstrap proportions or posterior probabilities).

* **Cluster A is the ancestral human SARS-CoV-2 genome type based on outgroup rooting with `BatCoVRaTG13`: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To root the human SARS-CoV-2 network, the authors connect the 100 distinct human haplotypes directly to `BatCoVRaTG13` (lines 8, 16, 29). While human SARS-CoV-2 sequences from January–March 2020 differ from each other by only a handful of mutations (e.g., 2 mutations—positions 8782 and 28144—separating node A and node B) and the entire 160-genome human Steiner tree has a total length of less than ~200 mutations, `BatCoVRaTG13` is 3.8% divergent ($\sim 1,150\text{–}1,200$ nucleotide differences across the 29,742-bp alignment; notice that the total Steiner tree length reported in line 16 is only "229 mutations", which does not even include the $\sim 1,150$ mutations on the branch to `BatCoVRaTG13`). Over a branch of $\sim 1,200$ substitutions, the expected number of back-mutations and convergent C$\to$U transitions at third codon positions (such as T8782C) makes parsimony outgroup rooting statistically uninformative (random outgroup attachment / long-branch attraction), while completely ignoring the fact that the earliest December 2019 Wuhan patients all carried type B.

* **Ancestral type B did not spread outside East Asia without first mutating, pointing to "immunological or environmental resistance against this type outside Asia": [Overstated].**  
  In the Abstract (line 8), the authors claim that ancestral type B was restricted to East Asia and required additional mutations to spread into Europe and America, suggesting *"immunological or environmental resistance against this type outside Asia."* Basing a claim of continental human immunological or environmental resistance on 160 opportunistically sampled genomes collected prior to March 4, 2020 ignores extreme sampling and ascertainment skew: during January–February 2020, sequencing outside China consisted of a tiny number of travel-associated clusters (e.g., the Munich/Bavarian cluster carrying C241T/C3037T/A23403G and the Washington State cluster). With an average of 1 substitution occurring every ~2 weeks (~2–3 transmissions), any viral lineage exported via a single traveler after 4–6 weeks of circulation will naturally carry 1–3 derived passenger mutations via neutral genetic drift and founder effects. Furthermore, the two mutations separating A and B (T8782C synonymous in *ORF1ab* and C28144T L84S in *ORF8*) and the synonymous/passenger mutations defining derived B sub-lineages provide zero biological basis for host-population immunological resistance.

* **The 160-genome median-joining network faithfully traces routes of COVID-19 infection and can identify undocumented infection sources for quarantine: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Abstract (line 8), the authors claim that the network faithfully traces routes of infection and can be used to trace undocumented infection sources. By March 4, 2020, over 90,000 COVID-19 cases had been confirmed globally, meaning the 160 analyzed genomes represent $<0.2\%$ of known infections. Because the SARS-CoV-2 evolutionary rate (~24 substitutions/genome/year) is slower than the serial interval of transmission (~4–5 days), identical viral genomes are shared across hundreds of unlinked patients in an outbreak, and a 1-mutation edge in a tree of 160 sparsely sampled global sequences reflects shared ancestry across weeks of unsampled community transmission rather than a direct epidemiological transmission link between the two sequenced individuals.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Unweighted Median-Joining Parsimony with Probabilistic Likelihood and Time-Calibrated Bayesian Phylogenetics
* **WHY:** Median-joining parsimony (`Network 5011CS`) treats all nucleotide changes equally, ignores C$\to$U hypermutation bias and transition/transversion rates, ignores sampling dates, and provides no statistical uncertainty on tree topology or root placement.
* **WHAT:** Reconstruct the phylogeny of the 160 human SARS-CoV-2 genomes using maximum likelihood (`IQ-TREE` with `GTR+F+I` or codon models) and Bayesian time-calibrated phylodynamics (`BEAST` with tip collection dates).
* **HOW:** Run `IQ-TREE` with 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates on the 160 human genomes, and run `BEAST` incorporating the exact GISAID collection dates under an exponential growth coalescent prior to estimate the posterior probability of the root position (type B vs. type A) without relying on parsimony attachment of the 3.8%-divergent bat outgroup.

#### 2. Quantify Outgroup Rooting Uncertainty Across the ~1,200-Mutation Branch to `BatCoVRaTG13`
* **WHY:** Attaching `BatCoVRaTG13` (~1,200 differences from SARS-CoV-2) to a human haplotype network where central nodes A and B differ by only 2 nucleotides (positions 8782 and 28144) assumes zero homoplasy or back-mutation along the 1,200-mutation branch.
* **WHAT:** Calculate the probability of convergent or back-mutations at positions 8782 and 28144 along the branch separating `BatCoVRaTG13` and human SARS-CoV-2, and report the likelihood ratio test (or Shimodaira-Hasegawa / AU test) comparing root placement at node A vs. node B.
* **HOW:** Fit an asymmetric substitution model in `IQ-TREE` or `HyPhy` to the 161-taxon alignment (160 human + `BatCoVRaTG13`), compute the marginal ancestral state probabilities at the human MRCA node for positions 8782 and 28144, and show that the posterior/likelihood support for rooting at A vs. B is statistically indistinguishable when outgroup homoplasy and temporal tip dates are modeled.

#### 3. Test Geographic Clustering Against a Null Model of Travel Exportation and Founder Effects
* **WHY:** Observing that derived B haplotypes (rather than the exact ancestral B haplotype) appear in Europe and North America among 160 early genomes is the expected consequence of neutral mutation accumulation over 4–8 weeks prior to international travel seeding.
* **WHAT:** Remove all speculative claims regarding *"immunological or environmental resistance against this type outside Asia"* (Abstract, line 8) and test whether the number of mutations separating non-Asian B genomes from ancestral node B simply matches the molecular clock expectation given their later collection dates.
* **HOW:** Regress the root-to-tip (or node-B-to-tip) mutation count of all B-cluster genomes against their calendar collection date (December 2019 to March 2020) stratified by continent (East Asia vs. Europe/America). Demonstrate that non-Asian B genomes have more mutations than December 2019 Wuhan B genomes simply because they were sampled 4–8 weeks later in time.

#### 4. Reframe Network Links as Broad Phylogeographic Ancestry Rather Than Direct Person-to-Person Transmission Routes
* **WHY:** With 160 genomes sampled out of $>90,000$ global cases (and a mutation rate slower than the transmission serial interval), a parsimony link between two genomes from different countries cannot identify a direct infection source or rule out unsampled intermediate transmitters.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Abstract to remove claims that phylogenetic networks of sparse public GISAID sequences can directly trace undocumented infection sources for quarantine.
* **HOW:** Replace the concluding sentence of the Abstract (line 8) with a statement noting that while viral genome sequencing can corroborate broad geographic importations when combined with contact-tracing metadata, low sequence divergence and incomplete sampling preclude inferring direct person-to-person transmission chains from sequence networks alone.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** The manuscript text omits essential quantitative results and contains inconsistencies in reported mutation counts.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Clarify the Steiner tree length of 229 mutations (Materials and Methods, line 16; Figure 1 caption, line 29):** Line 16 and line 29 state that the 161-taxon network (160 human + 1 bat virus `BatCoVRaTG13`) yields most-parsimonious trees of length `229 mutations`. However, `BatCoVRaTG13` differs from human SARS-CoV-2 by $>1,100$ nucleotides (3.8% of 29,742 bp $\approx 1,130$ bp). Clarify that the 229 mutations count *only* the intra-human polymorphic sites (excluding the $>1,100$ fixed differences on the outgroup branch to `BatCoVRaTG13`) and state explicitly which specific nucleotide positions connect `BatCoVRaTG13` to Node A.
  * **Provide a quantitative table of cluster counts by geographic region and collection date:** The main text contains no results table summarizing how many of the 160 genomes belong to clusters A, B, and C across East Asia, Europe, North America, and Australia, or their date ranges. Add a summary table with exact counts and dates.
  * **Capitalization typo in Abstract (line 8):** Fix `"SARS-Cov-2"` to `"SARS-CoV-2"` and add a space after `"Significance"` in line 7 (`"Significance This is..."`).
