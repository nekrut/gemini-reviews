### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript performs a two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to evaluate the potential causal effect of COVID-19 susceptibility ($n = 1,683,768$; 38,984 cases), COVID-19 hospitalization ($n = 1,882,773$; 9,986 cases), and severe COVID-19 ($n = 1,388,342$; 5,101 cases) on the risk of Graves' disease (GD; OpenGWAS ID `bbj-a-123`: 2,176 hospitalized cases and 210,277 controls). Selecting instrumental variable SNPs at a relaxed threshold of $P < 5 \times 10^{-6}$ clumped at $r^2 < 0.001$ using the 1000 Genomes European reference panel (yielding 19, 21, and 27 SNPs, respectively), the authors apply inverse-variance weighted (IVW), weighted median, MR-Egger, simple mode, and MR-PRESSO estimators. They report null causal estimates across all three exposures—susceptibility IVW $\text{OR} = 0.989$ (95% CI $0.405\text{–}2.851$, $P = 0.93$), hospitalization IVW $\text{OR} = 0.974$ (95% CI $0.852\text{–}1.113$, $P = 0.70$), and severity IVW $\text{OR} = 0.979$ (95% CI $0.890\text{–}1.077$, $P = 0.66$)—and conclude that COVID-19 does not cause the onset or exacerbation of GD.

Despite following standard `TwoSampleMR` reporting conventions, the study suffers from a foundational population genetics flaw and severe numerical inconsistencies in its main results table: (1) an unacknowledged trans-ancestry mismatch between the **European-ancestry** COVID-19 exposure GWASs (and European 1000 Genomes LD clumping/proxy panel) and the **East Asian (Japanese)** Graves' disease outcome GWAS (`bbj-a-123`, from BioBank Japan), which the authors mistakenly describe as European in the Methods and Limitations; (2) mathematically impossible confidence intervals in Table 2, where the COVID-19 susceptibility MR-Egger confidence interval ($0.786\text{–}1.245$) is four times *narrower* than the IVW confidence interval ($0.405\text{–}2.851$) despite zero heterogeneity ($Q$ test $P = 0.39$) and is asymmetric around $\text{OR} = 1.047$ on the log scale; and (3) absence of any SNP-level instrument table, $F$-statistics, or minimum detectable effect size (power) calculations, accompanied by a broken date placeholder (`"presented in 3/7/2025 [ 16 ]"`) in the Methods. Re-running the analysis with ancestry-matched GWAS summary statistics (e.g., FinnGen or UK Biobank European GD outcomes to match European COVID-19 exposures, or East Asian COVID-19 exposures to match BioBank Japan GD), auditing the Table 2 IVW/MR-Egger calculations, and performing formal power calculations are essential.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Two-sample MR reveals no causal effect of COVID-19 susceptibility, hospitalization, or severity on Graves' disease (`bbj-a-123`): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  A core assumption of two-sample MR is that the exposure and outcome GWAS summary statistics represent the same underlying ancestral population with consistent allele frequencies and linkage disequilibrium (LD) structure. In the Methods ("Outcome Data", line 71; "Instrument Construction", line 76) and Limitations (line 181), the authors state that they used GWAS ID `bbj-a-123` (2,176 cases, 210,277 controls) as the GD outcome, clumped SNPs and selected proxy SNPs ($r^2 > 0.8$) using the **European** 1000 Genomes reference cohort, and assert that *"our analysis primarily relied on data from the European population."* In fact, `bbj-a-123` is **BioBank Japan**—a 100% **East Asian (Japanese)** cohort. Pairing European COVID-19 exposure instruments and European LD proxy SNPs ($r^2 > 0.8$) with an East Asian GD outcome GWAS violates two-sample MR validity, as European-selected lead SNPs and proxies have different LD tagging of causal variants, different minor allele frequencies, and potential population-specific effects in East Asians.

* **The IVW, Weighted Median, and MR-Egger estimates for COVID-19 susceptibility in Table 2 are internally consistent: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Inspection of Table 2 (lines 125–128) reveals severe mathematical anomalies in the COVID-19 susceptibility column ($K = 19$ SNPs):
  1. The IVW estimate is $\text{OR} = 0.989$ with 95% CI $(0.405, 2.851)$, which corresponds to a log-OR standard error of $\text{SE}_{\text{IVW}} \approx [\ln(2.851) - \ln(0.405)] / 3.92 = 0.498$ (and is also slightly asymmetric around $\ln(0.989) = -0.011$, since $\exp(-0.011 - 1.96 \times 0.498) = 0.373$ vs. $0.405$).
  2. Yet in the very same 19-SNP dataset with no heterogeneity (Cochran's $Q$ $P = 0.39$, Table 3), the Weighted Median 95% CI is $(0.685, 1.362)$ ($\text{SE} \approx 0.175$) and the MR-Egger 95% CI is $(0.786, 1.245)$ ($\text{SE} \approx 0.117$). Mathematically, under fixed or random effects with $I^2 = 0$ ($Q$ $P = 0.39$), the IVW estimator has the *smallest* variance of all linear MR estimators, whereas MR-Egger estimates an additional intercept parameter and has $\text{SE}_{\text{Egger}} \ge \text{SE}_{\text{IVW}} / \text{cor}( |\hat{\beta}_X| )$, which is strictly *larger* than $\text{SE}_{\text{IVW}}$! Having $\text{SE}_{\text{IVW}}$ be $4.2$-fold *larger* than $\text{SE}_{\text{Egger}}$ (and MR-Egger's CI $[0.786, 1.245]$ be markedly asymmetric on the log scale around $\text{OR} = 1.047$: $\ln(1.047) - \ln(0.786) = 0.287$ vs. $\ln(1.245) - \ln(1.047) = 0.173$) indicates a transcription error or corrupted table entry in Table 2.

* **The null MR findings rule out COVID-19 as a causal trigger for the onset or exacerbation of Graves' disease: [Overstated].**  
  First, even setting aside the European-to-East-Asian ancestry mismatch, the authors conflate absence of statistical significance ($P > 0.05$) with proof of the null hypothesis without reporting statistical power or minimum detectable odds ratios. Second, as noted in the Abstract (line 31), the authors claim their study evaluates *"the onset or exacerbation of GD"*, whereas the outcome GWAS (`bbj-a-123`) only measures prevalent/hospitalized GD diagnosis vs. controls and cannot assess *exacerbation* or relapse of pre-existing GD. Third, genetic liability to SARS-CoV-2 infection captured by host susceptibility SNPs (such as *ABO* or *ACE2*/*IFNAR2* loci) reflects lifelong germline predisposition rather than acute viral exposure during the pandemic.

* **Direction of causality in the Results text matches the specified exposure-outcome design: [Fragile].**  
  In the Results (line 105), the authors write: *"Regarding COVID-19 susceptibility, the IVW MR analysis indicated that GD did not significantly increase the risk (odds ratio [OR] 0.989, 95% CI 0.405–2.851; P = .93)"*. This sentence reverses the exposure and outcome: in their design, COVID-19 susceptibility is the exposure and GD is the outcome, not the effect of GD on COVID-19 susceptibility.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Resolve the European vs. East Asian Ancestry Mismatch Between Exposure and Outcome GWASs
* **WHY:** Combining European COVID-19 exposure summary statistics and a European 1000 Genomes LD reference panel with BioBank Japan (`bbj-a-123`, East Asian) GD outcome statistics violates the core single-population assumption of two-sample MR.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the primary two-sample MR analysis using ancestry-matched European Graves' disease summary statistics (e.g., from FinnGen or UK Biobank), and perform a separate ancestry-matched East Asian analysis using East Asian COVID-19 HGI summary statistics paired with `bbj-a-123` (clumped with the 1000 Genomes `EAS` reference panel).
* **HOW:** Query OpenGWAS / FinnGen for European-ancestry Graves' disease (e.g., `finn-b-E4_GRAVES_STRICT`), extract the European COVID-19 HGI instruments clumped with 1000G `EUR` ($r^2 < 0.001$), harmonize alleles, and compute IVW, Weighted Median, MR-Egger, and MR-PRESSO estimates. In parallel, clump East Asian COVID-19 HGI summary statistics with 1000G `EAS` and test against `bbj-a-123`, then meta-analyze the two ancestry-matched MR estimates.

#### 2. Audit and Correct the Confidence Intervals in Table 2
* **WHY:** In Table 2 (line 125–127), the COVID-19 susceptibility IVW 95% CI ($0.405\text{–}2.851$) and MR-Egger 95% CI ($0.786\text{–}1.245$ around $\text{OR} = 1.047$) are mathematically incompatible with each other and with log-normal symmetry.
* **WHAT:** Re-export the exact `TwoSampleMR::mr()` output table directly from R and verify all $\beta$, $\text{SE}$, $\text{OR} = \exp(\beta)$, and $95\%\text{ CI} = \exp(\beta \pm 1.96 \times \text{SE})$ values across Table 2 and the Abstract.
* **HOW:** Inspect the `res <- mr(dat)` data frame in R, check whether the IVW and MR-Egger CIs in the COVID-19 susceptibility column were swapped or miscalculated, and replace Table 2 with exact programmatic outputs verified to satisfy $\ln(\text{OR}) = [\ln(\text{UCI}) + \ln(\text{LCI})] / 2$.

#### 3. Provide the Complete SNP Instrument Table, $F$-Statistics, and Formal MR Power Calculations
* **WHY:** Although the Methods (line 76) state that $F = (R^2 / K) / [(1 - R^2) / (N - K - 1)]$ was computed (note: the formula in line 76 is also missing the division slash before $(N-K-1)$), no $F$-statistics, $R^2$ values, or SNP rsIDs are reported anywhere in the paper, and line 66 contains a broken placeholder (`"is presented in 3/7/2025 [ 16 ]"`).
* **WHAT:** Add a supplementary or main-text table listing all 19, 21, and 27 instrumental SNPs (rsID, chromosome, position, effect/other allele, EAF, $\beta_{\text{exp}}$, $\text{SE}_{\text{exp}}$, $P_{\text{exp}}$, $\beta_{\text{out}}$, $\text{SE}_{\text{out}}$, $P_{\text{out}}$, and per-SNP $F$-statistic), and compute the minimum detectable OR at 80% power ($\alpha = 0.05$).
* **HOW:** Correct the $F$-statistic formula in line 76 to $F = \frac{R^2 / K}{(1 - R^2) / (N - K - 1)}$, report the total phenotypic variance explained ($R^2$) and mean/range of SNP $F$-statistics for each exposure, and use the `mRnd` analytical power formula for binary outcomes (with 2,176 cases, 210,277 controls, and observed $R^2$) to state the minimum OR detectable with 80% power.

#### 4. Perform Bidirectional MR and Temper Clinical Claims About Disease Exacerbation
* **WHY:** The Discussion (lines 170–176) extensively discusses bidirectional effects (whether pre-existing GD increases COVID-19 risk or severity) and the Limitations (line 181) note that bidirectional MR was not performed, even though the exact same summary datasets allow immediate reverse MR testing.
* **WHAT:** Run the reverse MR analysis (GD genetic instruments $\to$ COVID-19 susceptibility, hospitalization, and severity) using ancestry-matched GWASs, and revise the Abstract and Conclusions to remove claims about GD "exacerbation."
* **HOW:** Extract genome-wide significant ($P < 5 \times 10^{-8}$) independent instruments for GD, harmonize them against the ancestry-matched COVID-19 outcome GWASs, report the reverse MR estimates in a new subsection/table, and revise line 105 (*"indicated that GD did not significantly increase the risk"*) to state the correct exposure-to-outcome direction.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Broken text placeholders, formula typos, and sample size discrepancies undermine technical credibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Broken citation/date placeholder in Methods (line 66):** Fix *"is presented in 3/7/2025 [ 16 ]"* to cite Table 1 / Supplementary Table S1 properly.
  * **Sample size arithmetic discrepancy in Table 1 vs. Methods (lines 62, 64, 99):** For COVID-19 hospitalization, line 62 gives total $n = 1,882,773$ and line 64 gives $9,986$ cases, whereas Table 1 (line 99) lists $9,986$ cases and $1,877,672$ controls ($9,986 + 1,877,672 = 1,887,658 \ne 1,882,773$). Reconcile the exact control and total sample sizes from the OpenGWAS metadata.
  * **Fix $F$-statistic equation missing division operator (line 76):** Change `F = (R 2 /K)/[(1−R 2 ) (N−K−1)]` to `F = (R^2 / K) / [(1 - R^2) / (N - K - 1)]`.
  * **Typo in Abbreviations (line 204):** Fix `"Mendelian randomizaion-pleiotropy"` to `"Mendelian randomization-pleiotropy"`.
