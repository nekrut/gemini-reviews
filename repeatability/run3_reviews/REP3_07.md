### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This comparative genomics study examines the phylogenetic, metabolic, and accessory-genome relationships between *Enterococcus faecium* and its closely related sister species *Enterococcus lactis*. Starting from 2,838 GenBank assemblies (2,727 labeled as *E. faecium* and 111 as *E. lactis*), the authors filter for CheckM completeness $\ge 90\%$ and contamination $\le 10\%$, dereplicate at $99.5\%$ nucleotide identity across 92 core housekeeping genes (UBCG2), and analyze 192 representative genomes (128 *E. faecium* and 64 *E. lactis*). Using average nucleotide identity (ANI), core-gene maximum-likelihood phylogenetics, KEGG metabolic reconstruction, PANINI accessory-genome clustering, and BLAST-based screening against CARD, VFDB, BAGEL4/BACTIBASE, and mobileOG-db, the authors show that (i) 16S rRNA phylogeny cannot resolve the two species, explaining why many *E. lactis* genomes in GenBank are misclassified as *E. faecium*; (ii) whole-genome ANI ($>97.8\%$) and core-gene phylogeny separate *E. lactis* from *E. faecium* and divide *E. faecium* into a multi-host subclade I and a human-restricted clinical subclade II; and (iii) *E. faecium* subclade II genomes are larger ($2.98\pm 0.20\text{ Mb}$), lower in GC content ($37.82\%$), and enriched for mobile genetic elements, antibiotic resistance genes (e.g., *vanA*, *ermB*, *tet(M)*, *dfrF/G*), and biofilm-associated surface proteins (*esp*, *fss3*, *sgrA*). The analyzed assemblies are publicly accessible via NCBI taxon queries, and accessions are listed in Table S1.

The core phylogenomic and ANI demonstration that GenBank contains numerous *E. lactis* assemblies misclassified as non-pathogenic *E. faecium*—and that human clinical *E. faecium* subclade II carries a distinct mobile resistome—is useful and well supported. However, the study is undermined by three major methodological and biological flaws. First, permissive BLASTX filtering against functional databases introduced severe false-positive gene annotations in Figure 6, where essential bacterial housekeeping genes (`tufA` [EF-Tu], `groEL`, `ddl`, `clsA`, `clpC/E/P`, `gndA`, `znuC`) and even eukaryotic homolog names (`fosB, transcription factor AP-1 subunit` and `vatE, vacuolar H+-ATPase E subunit`) are misclassified as antibiotic resistance, virulence, and bacteriocin genes. Second, the Discussion repeatedly claims that *E. faecium* "evolved from *E. lactis*" (lines 153 and 157), misinterpreting two reciprocally monophyletic sister clades on a rooted tree (Figure 1). Third, the statistical comparisons in Figures 2 and 5 apply unadjusted two-sample Student's *t*-tests between a composite parent group (`EF`, all 128 *E. faecium*) and its own constituent subsets (`EF-I` and `EF-II`), violating sample independence. Cleaning the functional annotations, correcting the phylogenetic interpretation, and replacing the non-independent *t*-tests can be accomplished directly with the existing dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Whole-genome ANI and 92-gene core phylogenetics cleanly separate *E. faecium* (subclades I and II) from *E. lactis*, whereas 16S rRNA sequences cannot resolve the two species: [Well Supported].**  
  The 92-gene UBCG2 phylogeny (Figure 1), ANI clustering ($>98.3\%$ and $>97.8\%$ within-species identity, Figure S1), and PANINI accessory-gene ordination (Figure 3) consistently resolve *E. lactis* and the two *E. faecium* subclades and explain the widespread misannotation of *E. lactis* as *E. faecium* clade B in public repositories.

* **Core-genome and accessory-genome profiles of antibiotic resistance, virulence, and bacteriocin genes across all strains (Figure 6 and lines 121–127): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Because the authors used loose BLASTX cutoffs without domain curation (Methods line 18 of part 2), Figure 6 and the accompanying text misclassify conserved housekeeping genes and database homolog collisions as resistance, virulence, and bacteriocin factors:
  1. *Eukaryotic name collisions in AMR annotation (Figure 6 caption, line 121):* Group 3 defines `fosB` as `transcription factor AP-1 subunit` (the mammalian proto-oncogene FosB, rather than the bacterial fosfomycin resistance thiol transferase FosB) and `vatE` as `vacuolar H+-ATPase E subunit` (the eukaryotic V-ATPase subunit, rather than the enterococcal streptogramin A acetyltransferase Vat(E)), and defines `aad(6)` as `putative aryl-alcohol dehydrogenase` rather than aminoglycoside 6-adenylyltransferase.
  2. *Essential housekeeping genes counted as AMR, virulence, and bacteriocins:* Group 4 and Group 7 count essential translation elongation factor Tu (`tufA`), chaperonin `groEL`, peptidoglycan D-Ala-D-Ala ligase (`ddl`), and cardiolipin synthase (`clsA`) as AMR/adherence genes; Groups 11 and 14 count central pentose-phosphate enzyme 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase (`gndA`) and housekeeping proteases (`clpC`, `clpE`, `clpP`) as virulence factors; and Group 15 counts the zinc uptake ABC transporter (`znuC`), gram-negative lipid A flippase homolog (`msbA`), phenolic acid regulator (`padR`), and two-component sensor (`regX3`) as "bacteriocins."
  3. *Text-figure contradiction on exotoxins:* Main text line 125 states that "no genes classified as exotoxins, indicating a direct virulence for hosts, were detected," directly contradicting the Figure 6 caption (line 121, Group 10), which explicitly lists `speB, pyrogenic exotoxin`. In addition, line 123 refers to the macrolide resistance gene `msrC` as `mcrC` (the colistin resistance gene).

* ***E. faecium* evolved from *E. lactis* alongside loss of *ebpA* and *hylB* (Discussion lines 153 and 157): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the rooted maximum-likelihood core-gene phylogeny (Figure 1, rooted with *E. hirae* ATCC 9790), *E. faecium* and *E. lactis* form reciprocally monophyletic sister clades that diverged from a common ancestor. Inferring that extant *E. faecium* evolved from extant *E. lactis* commits a fundamental tree-reading fallacy; trait differences between the two sister clades (such as the presence of `hylB` in *E. lactis* and its absence in *E. faecium* subclade II) could equally reflect gain along the *E. lactis* stem branch or loss along the *E. faecium* branch.

* **Statistical comparisons of genome size, gene count, GC%, and functional gene counts across `EF`, `EF-I`, `EF-II`, and `EL` (Figures 2 and 5): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Figures 2 and 5, the authors apply pairwise Student's *t*-tests comparing `EF` (all 128 *E. faecium* genomes) against `EF-I` and `EF-II` (the two subsets that make up `EF`). Comparing a pooled parent sample against its own subsamples violates the independence requirement of Student's *t*-test. Moreover, discrete skewed gene counts (Figure 5) are tested with parametric *t*-tests without multiple-testing correction, and CheckM quality filtering allowed up to $10\%$ contamination and $10\%$ incompleteness (Methods line 170), which can inflate apparent genome size and gene counts in draft assemblies.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Purge Housekeeping Genes and Database Homolog Collisions from the AMR, Virulence, and Bacteriocin Matrices
* **WHY:** Counting universal housekeeping proteins (`tufA`, `groEL`, `ddl`, `clpP`, `gndA`, `znuC`) and misannotated eukaryotic homolog descriptions (`transcription factor AP-1 subunit` for `fosB`; `vacuolar H+-ATPase E subunit` for `vatE`) as AMR, virulence, and bacteriocin genes distorts the per-genome gene counts in Figure 5, the presence/absence heatmaps in Figure 6, and the PCA/PERMANOVA in Figure 7.
* **WHAT:** Re-screen the 192 genomes using curated, mechanism-aware tools (`AMRFinderPlus` or `RGI --strict` for CARD, and structural bacteriocin cluster validation in `BAGEL4`) and remove core housekeeping genes and false-positive BLASTX hits prior to recalculating Figures 5–7.
* **HOW:** Run NCBI `AMRFinderPlus` (or `RGI` with curated bitscore cutoffs) on the 192 proteomes, restrict bacteriocin calls to structural bacteriocin precursor peptides (excluding ABC transporters `znuC`/`msbA` and regulators `padR`/`regX3`), and remove core housekeeping genes present in $>95\%$ of all *Enterococcus* genomes from the VFDB matrix unless specific resistance-conferring point mutations (e.g., *liaFSR* or *pbp5* alleles) are present. Re-compute the boxplots (Figure 5), heatmaps (Figure 6), and PCA/PERMANOVA (Figure 7).

#### 2. Replace Non-Independent Student's *t*-Tests with Disjoint Three-Group Nonparametric Tests and Tighten Assembly Quality Controls
* **WHY:** Testing `EF` ($n = 128$) against its own subsets `EF-I` and `EF-II` violates observation independence, and CheckM thresholds of $\le 10\%$ contamination / $\ge 90\%$ completeness allow up to $\sim 280\text{ kb}$ of contaminating contigs to bias genome size and gene count comparisons (Figure 2).
* **WHAT:** Compare only the three mutually exclusive groups (`EF-I`, `EF-II`, and `EL`) using Kruskal–Wallis tests followed by Dunn's post-hoc test with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR adjustment, and verify that genome size differences hold in a strict high-quality subset ($\ge 98\%$ completeness, $\le 2\%$ contamination, or complete closed genomes).
* **HOW:** In R, run `kruskal.test` and `FSA::dunnTest(..., method = "bh")` across the three disjoint groups (`EF-I`, `EF-II`, `EL`) for Figures 2 and 5, and add a supplementary sensitivity plot restricting Figure 2 to assemblies with CheckM completeness $\ge 98\%$ and contamination $\le 2\%$.

#### 3. Correct the Phylogenetic Interpretation of Sister Clades and Reconstruct Ancestral Gene Gain/Loss
* **WHY:** Because *E. faecium* and *E. lactis* are reciprocally monophyletic sister species rooted by *E. hirae* (Figure 1), *E. faecium* did not evolve from *E. lactis*.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Discussion to describe *E. faecium* and *E. lactis* as sister species diverging from a common ancestor, and formally test whether genes such as `hylB` and `ebpA` were ancestral and lost in *E. faecium* subclade II or horizontally acquired in *E. lactis*.
* **HOW:** Check for the presence of `hylB` and `ebpA` in the outgroup *E. hirae* ATCC 9790 and other closely related *Enterococcus* species (e.g., *E. durans*, *E. mundtii*), map their presence/absence onto the rooted 92-gene phylogeny using parsimony or maximum-likelihood ancestral state reconstruction (`ape::ace`), and revise lines 153 and 157 accordingly.

#### 4. Account for GenBank Sequencing Ascertainment When Interpreting Isolation Source Distributions
* **WHY:** Public GenBank genomes of *E. faecium* are heavily skewed toward clinical vancomycin-resistant surveillance isolates from human hospitals, so the $64.1\%$ human proportion among dereplicated *E. faecium* clusters reflects database deposition bias rather than natural habitat prevalence.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly note GenBank clinical sampling bias when discussing the ecological distribution of *E. faecium* subclades I and II and in the PERMANOVA interpretation (Figure 7).
* **HOW:** Add a caveat in the Results (line 60) and Discussion (line 151) stating that isolation source proportions in GenBank reflect clinical vs. food-microbiology sequencing priorities, and run a partial PERMANOVA (`vegan::adonis2(dist_matrix ~ Clade + Source, by = "margin")`) to report the variance in gene profiles uniquely explained by Clade after controlling for Isolation Source, and vice versa.
