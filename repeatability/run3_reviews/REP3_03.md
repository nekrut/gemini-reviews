### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study develops and validates genome-wide polygenic scores (GPS) for five common complex diseases—coronary artery disease (CAD), atrial fibrillation (AF), type 2 diabetes (T2D), inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and breast cancer—using summary statistics from large external genome-wide association studies (GWAS) that have no participant overlap with the UK Biobank. For each disease, 31 candidate predictors (24 pruning-and-thresholding scores and 7 Bayesian `LDpred` scores across causal fraction tuning parameters $\rho \in [0.001, 1.0]$) are tuned in a UK Biobank Phase 1 validation cohort ($N = 120,280$) and evaluated out-of-sample in an independent UK Biobank Phase 2 testing cohort ($N = 288,978$ White British individuals, with a sensitivity analysis in $N = 222,529$ unrelated individuals). The authors show that `LDpred` genome-wide scores (comprising 6.6–6.9 million variants) outperform pruning-and-thresholding and earlier published scores for four of the five traits, and that the upper tails of the GPS distributions have average odds ratios (ORs) $\ge 3.0$ relative to the remainder of the population in $8.0\%$ (CAD), $6.1\%$ (AF), $3.5\%$ (T2D), $3.2\%$ (IBD), and $1.5\%$ (breast cancer) of individuals ($19.8\%$ for at least one disease). The GPS variant weights are publicly deposited at `http://www.broadcvdi.org/informational/data`.

This is a methodologically rigorous, large-scale validation study with strict separation between external GWAS discovery, hyperparameter tuning, and out-of-sample testing, paired with a candid discussion of healthy-volunteer ascertainment and European-ancestry transferability limits. Two statistical framing issues warrant refinement. First, the primary claim that $8.0\%$ of the population is "at greater than three-fold increased risk" for CAD (and analogous percentages for the other four diseases in Table 2) is based on comparing the *average* odds ratio across the entire upper tail ($\text{GPS} > c$) against the remainder of the population ($\text{GPS} \le c$). Because the upper tail averages over extreme right-tail individuals (e.g., top $0.5\%$ with $\text{OR} = 5.17$) and uses a below-average-risk reference group, an individual near the $92\text{nd}$ percentile cutoff actually has a marginal risk substantially below $3.0$-fold relative to the population mean. Second, the AUCs reported in Table 1 ($0.81$ for CAD, $0.77$ for AF, $0.73$ for T2D) are derived from logistic regressions that include age and sex alongside the GPS; reporting only the covariate-adjusted AUC without the baseline age/sex AUC or GPS-only AUC obscures the incremental discrimination attributable to genetics alone. Both points can be addressed directly using the existing testing cohort data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Genome-wide `LDpred` polygenic scores incorporating millions of variants outperform restricted pruning-and-thresholding and previously published polygenic scores for CAD, AF, T2D, and IBD, with out-of-sample discrimination replicating between validation and testing cohorts: [Well Supported].**  
  The two-stage validation ($N = 120,280$) and testing ($N = 288,978$) split within UK Biobank, absence of discovery sample overlap, and kinship-filtered sensitivity check ($N = 222,529$, Supplementary Table 10) provide strong, unbiased empirical support that accounting for genome-wide linkage disequilibrium via `LDpred` improves polygenic risk stratification over genome-wide-significant SNP subsets (Supplementary Tables 1–7).

* **The polygenic scores identify $8.0\%, 6.1\%, 3.5\%, 3.2\%,$ and $1.5\%$ of the population at $\ge 3$-fold increased risk for CAD, AF, T2D, IBD, and breast cancer, respectively (and $20$-fold more individuals for CAD than familial hypercholesterolemia mutations): [Overstated].**  
  In Table 2 and Figure 2a, the threshold defining the top $q\%$ of the distribution as having "Odds ratio $\ge 3.0$" is found by fitting a binary indicator $\mathbb{I}(\text{GPS} > c_q)$ in a logistic regression comparing the entire upper tail against the remaining $(100 - q)\%$. This procedure inflates the apparent size of the $\ge 3$-fold risk group in two ways:
  1. *Tail averaging vs. marginal risk at the threshold:* The odds ratio of the upper tail $\mathbb{I}(\text{GPS} > c_q)$ is a weighted average of risks from percentile $100 - q$ all the way to percentile $100$. As shown in Table 3, the top $10\%$ vs. remaining $90\%$ for CAD has $\text{OR} = 2.89$ and the top $5\%$ vs. remaining $95\%$ has $\text{OR} = 3.34$, while the top $1\%$ has $\text{OR} = 4.83$. Consequently, an individual at the $92\text{nd}$ percentile boundary ($\text{GPS} \approx +1.41\text{ SD}$) has a marginal odds ratio of approximately $2.0\text{--}2.2$ relative to a median-GPS individual ($\text{GPS} = 0$), not $\ge 3.0$.
  2. *Shifting reference group:* Comparing the top $q\%$ against the remaining $(100 - q)\%$ (whose mean GPS is negative) yields a larger OR than comparing against the population mean or median percentile.

* **Out-of-sample discriminative capacity reaches $\text{AUC} = 0.81$ for CAD, $0.77$ for AF, $0.73$ for T2D, $0.63$ for IBD, and $0.69$ for breast cancer (Table 1): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the calculations are numerically accurate as defined in the Table 1 footnote, these AUCs reflect a combined model containing age, sex, genotyping array, four genetic PCs, and the GPS. In the UK Biobank (ages 40–69), age and sex alone provide substantial discrimination for CAD ($\text{AUC} \approx 0.75\text{--}0.76$) and AF, which also explains why all 31 candidate CAD scores had seemingly narrow validation AUCs between $0.79$ and $0.81$ (line 46). Presenting the joint model AUC in Table 1 without the covariate-only baseline AUC or GPS-only AUC can mislead readers into attributing an AUC of $0.81$ to the genetic score alone.

* **Traditional clinical risk factors and family history do not identify most individuals in the high-$\text{GPS}_{\text{CAD}}$ tail (Supplementary Table 8): [Well Supported].**  
  The empirical comparison in the testing cohort demonstrates only modest enrichment of hypercholesterolemia ($20\%$ vs. $13\%$), hypertension ($32\%$ vs. $28\%$), and family history ($44\%$ vs. $35\%$) in the top $8\%$ of $\text{GPS}_{\text{CAD}}$, supporting the claim that polygenic risk captures inherited susceptibility largely orthogonal to self-reported baseline clinical phenotypes.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Distinguish Marginal Risk at a GPS Percentile from the Average Risk of the Cumulative Upper Tail
* **WHY:** Defining "carriers at $\ge 3$-fold risk" by equating the cumulative upper-tail odds ratio $\text{OR}(\text{GPS} > c \text{ vs. } \text{GPS} \le c) = 3.0$ with individual risk at threshold $c$ overstates the fraction of the population whose individual polygenic risk is at least 3-fold higher than average.
* **WHAT:** Report both (i) the cumulative upper-tail odds ratios currently in Table 2 and (ii) the exact GPS percentile and population fraction where an individual's *marginal* predicted risk (relative to the population median, $\text{GPS} = 0$, or the middle quintile, $40\text{th}\text{--}60\text{th}$ percentile) reaches $3.0$-, $4.0$-, and $5.0$-fold.
* **HOW:** Using the logistic regression model $\text{logit}\,P(Y=1) = \alpha + \beta\,\text{GPS} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^\top\mathbf{Z}$ (where $\text{GPS}$ is standardized to mean 0 and SD 1), compute the continuous log-odds slope $\hat{\beta}$ per 1-SD of GPS for each disease, solve for the standardized threshold $z_k = \ln(k)/\hat{\beta}$ where the marginal odds ratio relative to the population mean ($\text{GPS}=0$) equals $k \in \{3, 4, 5\}$, and add a column to Table 2 reporting the tail proportion $1 - \Phi(z_k)$ alongside the cumulative tail-vs-remainder proportions.

#### 2. Report Baseline Covariate-Only AUC, GPS-Only AUC, and $\Delta\text{AUC}$ in Table 1
* **WHY:** Including age and sex in the logistic regression used to compute the Table 1 AUCs masks the standalone discriminative power of the GPS and compresses the apparent spread across the 31 candidate models.
* **WHAT:** Expand Table 1 to display the AUC of (i) covariates alone (age, sex, array, PC1–4), (ii) the GPS alone, and (iii) the combined model (with $\Delta\text{AUC}$ and $95\%$ CIs).
* **HOW:** In the Phase 1 validation and Phase 2 testing sets, run `pROC::roc` on the predicted probabilities from the covariate-only logistic regression, the unadjusted GPS vector alone, and the full model, and use `pROC::roc.test` (DeLong's test) to report $\Delta\text{AUC}$ and its $95\%\text{ CI}$ in Table 1.

#### 3. Separate Prevalent Cases at Enrollment from Incident Cases During Follow-Up using Survival Models
* **WHY:** Combining baseline prevalent diagnoses (self-report and prior hospitalizations) with incident diagnoses in a cross-sectional logistic regression is susceptible to survival/ascertainment bias (for example, fatal premature myocardial infarctions prior to UK Biobank enrollment at ages 40–69 are unobserved) and prevents estimation of absolute 10-year clinical risk.
* **WHAT:** Add a stratified analysis in the testing cohort comparing GPS performance on baseline prevalent cases versus prospective incident cases after enrollment using Cox proportional hazards models.
* **HOW:** Partition the testing cohort cases for each of the five diseases into prevalent cases (diagnosis date $\le$ enrollment date) and incident cases (diagnosis date $>$ enrollment date among baseline disease-free participants). Fit Cox proportional hazards models (`survival::coxph`) adjusted for age, sex, array, and PC1–4 to report hazard ratios (HRs) per 1-SD and for the top $5\%$ and $1\%$ GPS strata alongside Harrell's C-index.

#### 4. Temper Clinical Equivalence Framing Between Upper-Tail GPS and Monogenic Mutations
* **WHY:** Unlike rare monogenic mutations (such as *LDLR* familial hypercholesterolemia variants) that disrupt a single actionable biochemical pathway and confer high penetrance across environments, a polygenic tail threshold aggregates thousands of small-effect pathways and depends on the reference group definition.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Abstract and main text to clarify that $8.0\%, 6.1\%, 3.5\%, 3.2\%,$ and $1.5\%$ represent the sizes of the upper tails whose *group-average* risk is 3-fold higher than the remainder of the cohort.
* **HOW:** In the Abstract (line 8) and main text (line 56), change "identifies 8.0%, 6.1%, 3.5%, 3.2% and 1.5% of the population at greater than three-fold increased risk" to "identifies upper tails comprising 8.0%, 6.1%, 3.5%, 3.2% and 1.5% of the population with an average risk at least three-fold higher than the remainder of the population."
