### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This brief four-paragraph commentary (75 lines, 5 references) summarizes recent reports of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1, clade 2.3.4.4b) spillover into marine mammals, fur mammals, and U.S. livestock (dairy cattle in Texas, Kansas, New Mexico, Idaho, and Michigan, and a goat in Minnesota) in March 2024. The text provides a general overview of *Orthomyxoviridae* taxonomy (Influenza A, B, C, and D viruses) and concludes with a recommendation for vaccination and surveillance across susceptible animals. No original genomic sequences, epidemiological datasets, phylogenetic analyses, or statistical models are presented.

Despite listing "Data curation" and "Formal analysis" in the CRediT authorship statement (line 49), the manuscript contains no data or formal analysis, and its brief narrative is compromised by direct contradictions between the Highlights and the main text, internal contradictions between adjacent paragraphs, and factual errors in basic influenza virology. Specifically, Highlights 4 and 5 assert "Cross-species transmission of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus between ruminant animals and humans" and "Highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus RNA was present in milk," yet neither human-to-ruminant/ruminant-to-human transmission evidence nor viral RNA detection in milk is mentioned anywhere in the body of the article. Furthermore, the text incorrectly states that Influenza B virus "causes the greatest lesions to humans" compared to Influenza A virus (line 40), and contradicts itself within three sentences by stating first that "cows routinely contract influenza viruses" (line 42) and then that "cattle were largely unaffected by IAVs" (line 44) without distinguishing Influenza D virus from Influenza A virus. Because the piece lacks original analysis and misstates basic virological and epidemiological facts, it requires a complete rewrite and re-classification as an opinion/correspondence piece rather than an analytical study.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Highlight 5 — "Highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus RNA was present in milk": [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The presence of H5N1 viral RNA in milk is asserted as a primary finding in Highlight 5 (line 34), but the body of the manuscript never mentions milk testing, RT-qPCR detection, viral loads in milk, or mammary gland tropism (line 42 merely states that "Sick cows had a mild illness, and produced less milk"). A highlight claim that has zero corresponding text, data, or citation in the manuscript body is unsupported.

* **Highlight 4 — "Cross-species transmission of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus between ruminant animals and humans": [Overstated].**  
  Highlight 4 claims established cross-species transmission between ruminants and humans (line 32), whereas the main text provides no case reports, sequence links, or epidemiological evidence of cow-to-human transmission, and instead speculates in line 42 that the cattle/goat infections were "caused by infected wild birds or humans by HPAI virus on farms," citing a single general news report (Ref. 3).

* **Influenza B virus (IBV) "causes the greatest lesions to humans" among influenza genera (line 40): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In comparing the four genera of *Orthomyxoviridae* (IAV, IBV, ICV, IDV), line 40 asserts that "IBV mainly infects humans, pigs, and seals, and causes the greatest lesions to humans." Biologically and clinically, Influenza A virus (IAV)—which is responsible for all historical human influenza pandemics (1918 H1N1, 1957 H2N2, 1968 H3N2, 2009 H1N1) as well as severe zoonotic H5N1 and H7N9 pneumonia—causes substantially greater morbidity, pathology, and mortality in humans than IBV.

* **Bovine susceptibility to influenza viruses and the mandate for widespread cattle vaccination (lines 42–44): [Overstated].**  
  Line 42 states that "cows routinely contract influenza viruses," whereas line 44 states that "Generally speaking, cattle were largely unaffected by IAVs." This self-contradiction arises from conflating *Deltainfluenzavirus* (Influenza D virus, for which cattle are the primary reservoir) with *Alphainfluenzavirus* (IAV). In addition, the concluding call to implement "vaccine immunization... in susceptible or possible susceptible animals" (line 44) is presented without any quantitative risk assessment, antigenic matching data, or consideration of DIVA (differentiating infected from vaccinated animals) surveillance and trade constraints in dairy cattle.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reconcile the Highlights with the Main Text and Cite Primary Virological Evidence
* **WHY:** Highlights 4 and 5 make specific empirical claims (ruminant–human transmission and H5N1 viral RNA in milk) that are completely absent from the main text.
* **WHAT:** Either add a dedicated paragraph in the main text summarizing the peer-reviewed primary genomic and RT-qPCR data on H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b shedding in bovine milk and documented dairy-worker conjunctivitis/respiratory cases, or remove Highlights 4 and 5.
* **HOW:** Cite the primary USDA/CDC and genomic epidemiology reports documenting H5N1 genotype B3.13 in unpasteurized bovine milk and the PB2 E627K-carrying human dairy-worker isolate from Texas, and explicitly summarize these findings in paragraph 3 (line 42).

#### 2. Correct Factual Errors Regarding Influenza A, B, and D Host Tropism and Pathogenicity
* **WHY:** Claiming that IBV causes greater lesions in humans than IAV (line 40) and stating that cows both "routinely contract influenza viruses" (line 42) and are "largely unaffected by IAVs" (line 44) confuses readers and conflates IDV with IAV.
* **WHAT:** Revise paragraphs 2, 3, and 4 to accurately state the relative severity of IAV vs. IBV in humans and clearly distinguish bovine endemic Influenza D virus (IDV) infections from historically rare bovine Influenza A virus (IAV) infections.
* **HOW:** In line 40, revise the text to state that IAV causes the most severe disease and pandemics in humans, followed by IBV. In line 42, replace "Although cows routinely contract influenza viruses" with "Although cattle are natural reservoirs for Influenza D virus (IDV), they have historically been refractory to Influenza A virus (IAV) infection, and this represents the first documented outbreak of HPAI H5N1 in dairy cattle."

#### 3. Substantiate or Temper the Animal Vaccination Recommendation
* **WHY:** Recommending widespread vaccination across "susceptible or possible susceptible animals" (line 44) ignores the practical barriers to H5N1 vaccination in dairy cattle and wildlife, including silent shedding, bulk-tank milk surveillance interference, and lack of licensed bovine H5 vaccines.
* **WHAT:** Reframe the concluding sentence to emphasize biosecurity, bulk-milk and wildlife genomic surveillance, and targeted vaccine feasibility studies rather than an unqualified vaccination mandate.
* **HOW:** Revise the final sentence of line 44 to: "Under the ongoing global outbreaks of HPAI H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b, enhanced genomic surveillance of milk and livestock, strict farm biosecurity, and evaluation of candidate veterinary vaccines are urgently needed."

#### 4. Correct the CRediT Authorship Statement to Match the Article Type
* **WHY:** The CRediT statement (line 49) lists "Data curation" for two authors and "Formal analysis" for another, even though the article is a 4-paragraph literature commentary containing no curated dataset or formal statistical/bioinformatic analysis.
* **WHAT:** Update the CRediT roles to accurately reflect literature synthesis and commentary writing.
* **HOW:** Remove `Data curation` and `Formal analysis` from the CRediT authorship contribution statement (line 49), retaining `Conceptualization`, `Writing – original draft`, `Writing – review & editing`, `Supervision`, and `Funding acquisition`.

* **WHAT & HOW:**
