### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript systematically characterizes bacterial DNA contamination in commercial DNA extraction kits and laboratory reagents ("the kitome") and quantifies its impact on both 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing and whole-genome shotgun metagenomics in low-biomass samples. Using five 10-fold serial dilutions of a pure *Salmonella bongori* culture (spanning $\sim 10^8$ down to $\sim 10^3$ cells), the authors perform parallel 16S rRNA (V1–V2) profiling at three independent institutions (WTSI, ICL, UB) using distinct batches of the FastDNA SPIN Kit for Soil at 20 and 40 PCR cycles, alongside 16S qPCR absolute quantification and shotgun metagenomic sequencing across four commercial extraction kits (FastDNA SPIN, MoBio UltraClean, QIAamp DNA Stool Mini, and PSP Spin Stool DNA Plus). They demonstrate that reagent background DNA plateaus at $\sim 500$ 16S rRNA gene copies per $\mu\text{L}$ of eluate, dominating sequencing reads whenever input biomass falls below $10^3\text{--}10^4$ cells in both amplicon and PCR-free shotgun workflows. Finally, they present an empirical case study of 182 longitudinal infant nasopharyngeal swabs from a cohort in Maela, Thailand, showing that apparent age-driven microbiome clustering on PCoA was entirely an artifact of chronological extraction across four contaminated kit batches—a pattern that vanished upon bioinformatic removal of kit-associated OTUs and failed to replicate when re-extracted with a clean kit lot. Raw 16S and shotgun datasets are deposited in ENA (`ERP006737`, `ERP006808`).

This is a landmark forensic and methodological study with major practical importance for microbiome research. The experimental design—combining a known pure-culture dilution series across multiple laboratories, PCR cycle numbers, quantification modalities (qPCR), sequencing modalities (16S and shotgun metagenomics), kit manufacturers, and a real-world clinical cohort re-extraction control—is exceptionally well conceived and executed. The conclusions are strictly bounded by the evidence, and the actionable guidelines in Box 1 provide a lasting standard for low-biomass microbiome studies. The only minor limitations are the absence of technical extraction replicates per kit/dilution within each laboratory (`n = 1` aliquot extracted per dilution per institute/kit) and minor differences in extraction input/elution volumes at one site (ICL used $500\,\mu\text{L}$ input / $100\,\mu\text{L}$ elution vs. $200\,\mu\text{L}$ / $50\,\mu\text{L}$ at UB and WTSI).

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Contaminating DNA is ubiquitous in commercial DNA extraction kits and dominates both 16S rRNA and shotgun metagenomic profiles below $10^3\text{--}10^4$ input cells: [Well Supported].**  
  Across all three institutes for 16S sequencing (Figure 1) and all four commercial kits for shotgun metagenomics (Figure 3), *S. bongori* comprises $\sim 100\%$ of reads at $10^8$ cells but drops progressively with each 10-fold dilution until contaminant taxa (e.g., *Burkholderia*, *Bradyrhizobium*, *Chryseobacterium*, *Sphingomonas*, *Acidobacteria* Gp2) dominate by dilution 4 ($10^4$ cells) or dilution 5 ($10^3$ cells). Crucially, the qPCR standard curve (Figure 2) provides independent physical corroboration, showing linear 10-fold reductions down to dilution 3 followed by a flat plateau at $\sim 500$ 16S copies/$\mu\text{L}$ caused by extraction kit background DNA.

* **Contaminant taxonomic profiles vary systematically between kit manufacturers and between production batches of the same kit: [Well Supported].**  
  By processing aliquots concurrently in the same laboratory (UB) across four manufacturers' kits alongside an unextracted ultrapure water control (dominated by *Pseudomonas*), the authors show that the contaminant profiles are kit-specific rather than airborne laboratory contamination (e.g., FastDNA SPIN dominated by *Burkholderiaceae*/*Enterobacteriaceae*, PSP by *Bradyrhizobiaceae*/*Chitinophagaceae*, QIAamp by a diverse mix of *Aerococcaceae*/*Bacillaceae*/*Flavobacteriaceae*; Figure 3b). Similarly, processing the same dilution series with three different lot numbers of the FastDNA SPIN kit at three institutes (alongside template-free PCR water controls) identifies 63 extraction-stage contaminant taxa that vary by lot (Figure 1b, Figure S2).

* **Chronological processing across different extraction kit batches can generate spurious biological associations in longitudinal low-biomass clinical studies: [Well Supported].**  
  In the Maela infant nasopharyngeal cohort ($N = 182$ swabs from 20 infants), the authors demonstrate that PCoA clustering initially attributed to infant age (Figure 4a) maps one-to-one onto the four FastDNA SPIN kit lots used in chronological order (Figure 4b, d, e). Removing the kit-correlated OTUs identified by `metastats` abolishes the age clustering (Figure 4c), and re-extracting archived sample aliquots with a new kit lot confirms that the putative "founder microbiome" OTUs were absent from the biological specimens.

* **Increasing PCR cycle number from 20 to 40 increases sequencing yield in low-biomass samples but amplifies reagent background: [Well Supported].**  
  Comparing 20 vs. 40 PCR cycles on the same extractions (Figure 1, Figure S1, Table S1a) demonstrates the practical trade-off faced by low-biomass studies: 20 cycles fail to yield sufficient amplicon for equimolar multiplexing at dilutions 4–5, whereas 40 cycles yield $>14,000$ reads per sample that are overwhelmingly derived from kit contaminants.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Standardize Cell Input Numbers Across Laboratories and Report Exact Cell-Equivalent Inputs per Reaction
* **WHY:** In the Methods (line 157), UB and WTSI extracted DNA from $200\,\mu\text{L}$ of each culture dilution and eluted in $50\,\mu\text{L}$ (a $4\times$ concentration factor), whereas ICL extracted from $500\,\mu\text{L}$ and eluted in $100\,\mu\text{L}$ (a $5\times$ concentration factor). Furthermore, line 150 states the starter culture $\text{OD}_{600} = 1.62$ equated to $\sim 10^9\text{ CFU/mL}$, so $200\,\mu\text{L}$ of the undiluted starter culture equals $2 \times 10^8\text{ CFU}$ and $500\,\mu\text{L}$ equals $5 \times 10^8\text{ CFU}$.
* **WHAT:** Add a column to Additional file 1: Table S1a/b listing the exact input volume, elution volume, and estimated *S. bongori* CFU per $\mu\text{L}$ of eluate for each institute so readers can directly compare the signal-to-noise ratio between ICL and UB/WTSI.
* **HOW:** Compute $\text{CFU}/\mu\text{L eluate} = (\text{CFU/mL} \times V_{\text{input}}) / V_{\text{eluate}}$ for each dilution at each site and include these exact numbers in Table S1a and the Figure 1 caption.

#### 2. Include Sequence-Based Blank Extraction Controls Alongside PCR-Only Water Controls in All Figures
* **WHY:** In Figure 1, the negative controls (`con`) are described as "template-free PCRs" ("PCR-amplified ultrapure water with no template DNA added," line 81), and in Figure 3 (`water`), the control is "ultrapure water, without DNA extraction" (line 103). While Table 1 summarizes genera found in blank DNA extractions over four years, including a sequenced "mock extraction blank" (ultrapure water or sterile LB broth passed through the DNA extraction column) directly in Figure 1 and Figure 3 would allow direct visual comparison of the pure extraction blank against the $10^3$-cell dilution.
* **WHAT:** Clarify in the Results and captions of Figures 1–3 why PCR-only water blanks were plotted alongside the dilution series, and if sequenced extraction-blank profiles exist for lots `#38098`, `#15447`, and `#30252`, include them in Figure 1 and Figure 3.
* **HOW:** Add the sequenced extraction-blank columns to Figure 1 and Figure 3 (or explicitly note in the captions that Dilution 5 at $10^3$ cells effectively serves as the extraction-kit background profile compared against the unextracted `water`/`con` PCR blanks).

#### 3. Quantify Within-Lot vs. Between-Lot Variance Criteria in Box 1
* **WHY:** Each dilution was extracted once per kit/institute ($n = 1$ extraction per condition), whereas the qPCR was run in technical triplicate (Figure 2).
* **WHAT:** Explicitly note in the Discussion that because a single extraction was performed per dilution per kit lot, minor stochastic variation between spin columns within the same box could also contribute to within-lot variance, reinforcing the Box 1 recommendation to process biological samples in replicates across kit lots.
* **HOW:** Add a brief caveat in the Discussion noting that spin-column-to-spin-column variation within a single lot was captured across the 6 serial dilutions of each kit (which showed highly consistent contaminant profiles across dilutions 3–5 within each lot in Figures 1b and 3b).

#### 4. Technical and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** A few minor typographical errors appear in the text and figure captions.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Line 92 (Figure 2 Caption):** The caption states *"plateauing after four dilutions indicates consistent background levels"* whereas the main text (line 86) states *"at the third dilution copy number remained stable and did not reduce further."* Harmonize the numbering (specify whether it is dilution 3 [$10^5$ cells] or the 4th data point including the undiluted sample).
  * **Line 105:** Fix the spelling of `Comomonadaceae` to `Comamonadaceae`.
  * **Line 159:** Note that Illumina Nextera XT (line 173) uses PCR amplification during library indexing (typically 12 cycles after tagmentation), so modify the phrasing on line 97 ("which do not involve a targeted PCR step") and line 107 ("even in the absence of a PCR amplification step") to specify "in the absence of a targeted 16S PCR amplification step" so readers do not confuse Nextera XT with wholly PCR-free library prep.
