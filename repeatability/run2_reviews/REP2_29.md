### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents a comprehensive empirical and theoretical audit of differential expression (DE) methods for single-cell and single-nucleus transcriptomics (scRNA-seq/snRNA-seq). Avoiding circular synthetic simulations as the primary benchmark, the authors curate 18 gold-standard multi-replicate datasets featuring matched scRNA-seq and bulk RNA-seq (plus quantitative proteomics in a subset) on purified cell populations subjected to identical perturbations in the same laboratories, supplemented by a 46-study scRNA-seq compendium, ERCC spike-in controls, Splatter null simulations, 14 multi-replicate unstimulated control cohorts, a spatial transcriptomics dataset, and an original snRNA-seq + in vivo RNAscope validation experiment in the injured mouse lumbar spinal cord ($N = 19,237$ nuclei; 19 genes screened by RNAscope across $n = 4$ biological replicates per group). Benchmarking 14 DE methods (accounting for ~90% of recent literature usage across 500 surveyed papers) plus 8 generalized linear mixed models (GLMMs), the authors demonstrate that cell-level DE tests (e.g., Wilcoxon rank-sum, Seurat `FindMarkers` default tests, MAST without random effects) treat individual cells as independent replicates (pseudoreplication), systematically underestimating between-replicate variance, biasing detections toward highly expressed and highly variable genes, and generating hundreds to thousands of false discoveries under the null hypothesis. Aggregating counts within biological replicates ("pseudobulk" followed by `edgeR`, `DESeq2`, or `limma`) restores valid replicate-level variance estimation and outperforms cell-level methods on transcriptomic, proteomic, GO-enrichment, and in vivo RNAscope validation benchmarks (`5/6` edgeR-LRT vs. `3/13` Wilcoxon genes validated in spinal cord endothelial cells). All raw data (GEO: GSE165003; Zenodo: 10.5281/zenodo.5048449) and an open-source R package (`Libra`) plus analysis code are publicly available.

This is an exemplary methodological and empirical study that identifies a pervasive statistical flaw in single-cell genomics (pseudoreplication at the cell level), proves its mechanistic cause through clean ablation experiments (disabling aggregation vs. permuting replicate labels into "pseudo-replicates"), and validates its biological consequences in vivo. Only minor methodological nuances and editorial typos warrant attention: (1) in the in vivo RNAscope screen, Methods (offset 46080, line 95) lists 6 `edgeR-LRT` gene probes (`Slc7a11`, `Gjb2`, `Pi16`, `Rbp4`, `Col1a1`, `Igfbp6`) while stating "7 genes identified by edgeR-LRT" (and Results line 130 states "five of the six genes called as DE by edgeR-LRT" and "nineteen putatively DE genes" = 13 + 6); (2) when comparing pseudobulk sum-aggregation against single-cell methods, differences in library size or cell counts per biological replicate can weight replicates unevenly if not normalized or offset properly; and (3) a few minor text typos in figure captions and Methods should be corrected.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Pseudobulk DE methods (`edgeR`, `DESeq2`, `limma`) significantly outperform cell-level single-cell DE methods in recovering matched bulk RNA-seq, bulk proteomics, and functional GO enrichment ground truth across 18 datasets (lines 38–44, Figure 1c–f, Supplementary Fig. 1): [Well Supported].**  
  Across all 18 matched bulk/single-cell datasets, the six pseudobulk methods consistently achieve higher area under the concordance curve (AUCC) and transcriptome-wide Spearman rank correlations than all seven cell-level methods and the default linear mixed model, regardless of the cutoff $k$ or bulk reference method used, and recover biologically expected pathways (such as `response to exogenous dsRNA` in poly-I:C-stimulated phagocytes, Figure 1f) that cell-level methods miss.

* **Single-cell DE methods exhibit a systematic false-positive bias toward highly expressed genes due to underestimation of between-replicate variance when biological replication is ignored (lines 47–90, Figures 2–3): [Well Supported].**  
  The two mechanistic ablation experiments in Figure 3 are particularly decisive: (i) disabling cell aggregation and running `edgeR`, `DESeq2`, and `limma` directly on individual cells (Figure 3a–b) immediately degrades their AUCC to the level of single-cell methods and induces strong false-positive correlation with ERCC spike-in abundance; and (ii) keeping the exact same cell-count aggregation step but shuffling replicate labels across cells to create "pseudo-replicates" (Figure 3c–d) causes the exact same collapse in performance and variance underestimation across 98.2% of genes in poly-I:C phagocytes and 100% (46/46) of compendium datasets (Figure 3e–f), with partial correlation analysis confirming that $\Delta$-variance is driven by between-replicate expression variance (offset 46080, line 15).

* **Ignoring variation between biological replicates generates hundreds to thousands of false-positive DE genes under the null in both simulated and real unperturbed scRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics datasets, especially in human cohorts (lines 93–109, Figure 4): [Well Supported].**  
  Splitting real unstimulated control biological replicates into random artificial groups across 14 scRNA-seq studies (Figure 4d–e) and control mouse spinal cord spatial transcriptomics regions (Figure 4f) demonstrates that cell-level tests (and pseudobulk applied to shuffled pseudo-replicates) call hundreds to thousands of false-positive DE genes and hundreds of spurious GO terms under the empirical null, whereas true-replicate pseudobulk methods control false discoveries near zero. The comparison of 18 human vs. 20 mouse datasets (Figure 4g) further confirms substantially higher inter-replicate variance ($\Delta$-variance) in human studies.

* **In vivo RNAscope validation in the injured mouse lumbar spinal cord confirms that `edgeR-LRT` pseudobulk DE identifies genuine hypoxia-related endothelial responses (`5/6` validated) whereas cell-level Wilcoxon rank-sum yields mostly false positives (`3/13` validated) (lines 112–132, Figure 5): [Well Supported].**  
  Crucially, the authors perform the RNAscope validation statistical test at the level of biological replicates ($n = 4$ injured vs. $n = 4$ uninjured mice, averaging 10 $\text{Pecam1}^+$ endothelial cells per mouse; offset 46080, line 95), avoiding pseudoreplication in the validation assay itself and confirming a statistically significant difference in validation rate ($5/6$ [83%] vs. $3/13$ [23%], $p < 0.05$, $\chi^2$ test; or $p = 0.041$ by two-sided Fisher's exact test).

* **Negative binomial and Poisson generalized linear mixed models (GLMMs) converge to pseudobulk accuracy in datasets of $\ge 500$ cells while requiring orders of magnitude more compute time (lines 135–137, Supplementary Fig. 10): [Well Supported].**  
  Evaluating 8 GLMM variants (with/without library-size offsets, Wald vs. LRT) across downsampled cell counts shows that properly parameterized negative binomial GLMMs with library-size offsets match pseudobulk AUCC at $\ge 500$ cells per dataset but require ~13.5 hours per cell type vs. minutes for pseudobulk, justifying pseudobulk as the practical default.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Discuss Cell-Count Imbalance and Mean vs. Sum Aggregation in Pseudobulk Construction
* **WHY:** In `Libra`, pseudobulk matrices are constructed by summing UMI/read counts across all cells of a given type within each biological replicate via matrix multiplication (line 170). When the number of captured cells per replicate varies by an order of magnitude (e.g., 20 cells in Replicate 1 vs. 1,000 cells in Replicate 2), the precision (inverse variance) of the pseudobulk count differs across replicates, and zero-count replicates can occur in rare cell types.
* **WHAT:** Briefly discuss or benchmark in the Methods/Discussion how extreme cell-count imbalance across biological replicates affects pseudobulk inference, and state recommended minimum cells-per-replicate filtering thresholds (e.g., $\ge 10$ cells per replicate) or precision weighting (`limma-voom` with sample quality weights).
* **HOW:** Add a short note in the Discussion or Methods section detailing the behaviour of sum-aggregation vs. mean-aggregation and `limma-voom` observation weights when cell numbers per biological replicate are highly unbalanced.

#### 2. Reconcile the Count of `edgeR-LRT` RNAscope Probes Between Results and Methods
* **WHY:** Results line 130 states *"We obtained RNAscope probes for nineteen putatively DE genes... RNAscope validated five of the six genes called as DE by edgeR-LRT. In marked contrast, only three of thirteen genes called as DE by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test could be corroborated"* ($13 + 6 = 19$). However, Methods (offset 46080, line 95) states *"In total, probes were obtained for 13 genes identified as DE by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (...) and 7 genes identified by edgeR-LRT (Slc7a11, catalog no. 422511; Gjb2, catalog no. 518881; Pi16, catalog no. 451311; Rbp4, catalog no. 508501; Col1a1, catalog no. 319371; Igfbp6, catalog no. 425721)"*—where the parenthetical list actually contains **6** genes, matching the Results text.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Change `7 genes identified by edgeR-LRT` in Methods (offset 46080, line 95) to `6 genes identified by edgeR-LRT` to match the 6 listed catalog numbers and the $6 + 13 = 19$ total in Results line 130.
