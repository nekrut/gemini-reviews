### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript constructs a median-joining phylogenetic network (`Network 5011CS`, with parameter $\epsilon = 0$ and $\epsilon = 10$ followed by Steiner tree parsimony reduction) of 160 complete human SARS-CoV-2 genomes sampled up to March 4, 2020 from GISAID, rooting the network with the bat sarbecovirus `BatCoVRaTG13` (96.2% nucleotide identity). From this 229-step parsimony network, the authors define three central haplotype clusters—designated A, B, and C—and conclude that type A is the ancestral human viral genome because it connects directly to the `BatCoVRaTG13` outgroup node. They further claim that type B is indigenous to East Asia and could not spread outside East Asia without first mutating into derived B haplotypes due to "founder effects or immunological or environmental resistance against this type outside Asia," and assert that median-joining networks faithfully trace individual COVID-19 infection routes and can identify undocumented transmission sources for quarantine. The GISAID accession lists and shareware network files (`Network5011CS`) are referenced as available online.

The manuscript's core evolutionary, epidemiological, and biological conclusions are fundamentally invalid due to fatal methodological and statistical errors. First, rooting an intraspecific parsimony network of 160 nearly identical human viral genomes (diverged by 0–10 substitutions) using an outgroup (`BatCoVRaTG13`) separated by $\sim 1,150$ nucleotide substitutions ($\sim 3.8\%$ divergence, representing decades of evolution) violates basic phylogenetic principles: random homoplasy and directional C$\to$U hypermutation along the long outgroup branch render parsimony rooting at 1–2 polymorphic ingroup sites (`T29095C`, `T8782C`, `C28144T`) statistically unidentifiable and contradict the empirical collection dates of the earliest Wuhan genomes (which belong to cluster B). Second, median-joining networks are uncalibrated distance/parsimony visualizations that lack a probabilistic substitution model, ignore sampling dates, and provide zero statistical support (no bootstrap, posterior probabilities, or likelihood ratio tests). Third, inferring host immunological or environmental resistance and person-to-person transmission chains from 160 heavily convenience-sampled early GISAID genomes mistakes glaring travel-related ascertainment bias and low viral mutation rates for biological mechanisms. Because these flaws are intrinsic to using an uncalibrated parsimony network with a distant outgroup on sparsely sampled early surveillance data, the paper's central claims cannot be salvaged without replacing the entire analytical framework.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Cluster A is the ancestral human SARS-CoV-2 genome type based on outgroup rooting with `BatCoVRaTG13`: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The 160 human SARS-CoV-2 genomes span 29,742 aligned bases (positions 56–29,797) and differ from one another by only a handful of mutations, whereas `BatCoVRaTG13` differs from Wuhan-Hu-1 by $>1,100$ substitutions. In parsimony median-joining, the attachment point of an outgroup separated by $>1,100$ steps to an ingroup separated by 2 steps (positions 8782 and 28144 separating A and B) is determined entirely by whether those 2 sites happened to experience a parallel mutation or reversion along the $\sim 1,150$-step branch. Given strong transition bias and C$\to$U mutational asymmetry in coronaviruses, the probability of homoplasy at a single cytosine/thymine site over $>1,100$ substitutions is high. Worse, the authors ignore molecular clock rooting using sample collection dates, which shows that Cluster B genomes from Wuhan in December 2019 predate Cluster A genomes.

* **Ancestral Type B could not spread outside East Asia without mutating due to founder effects or immunological or environmental resistance outside Asia: [Overstated].**  
  This claim rests entirely on visual inspection of the geographic colors of 160 early GISAID sequences collected before March 4, 2020. Early sequencing was severely skewed toward Wuhan hospital cohorts and specific contact-traced traveler clusters in Europe and North America. No formal test of selection ($d_N/d_S$, branch-site models, or phylodynamic birth–death fitness models), no control for geographic sampling density, and no immunological or functional assays are performed. Invoking "immunological or environmental resistance" outside Asia from 160 convenience samples is an unsupported speculation that ignores extreme epidemiological ascertainment bias.

* **Character-based median-joining networks are the "method of choice" for reconstructing SARS-CoV-2 evolutionary paths in the presence of parallel evolution: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Median-joining (Bandelt et al. 1999) was designed for intraspecific mitochondrial markers without recombination and relies on unweighted (or manually weighted) Manhattan distances without an explicit continuous-time Markov model of nucleotide substitution, rate heterogeneity across sites, or temporal priors. In the caption to Figure 1, the authors note that network reticulations are "mainly caused by recurrent mutations at np11083." Position 11083 resides in a poly-U homopolymer tract (`GTTTTTTA`) that is notorious for sequencing/consensus-calling errors and recurrent homoplasy across diverse sequencing platforms. Treating unmasked sequencing artifacts and hypermutable sites as unweighted parsimony characters generates artificial reticulations rather than true evolutionary paths.

* **The phylogenetic network faithfully traces documented infection routes and can be used to trace and quarantine undocumented COVID-19 infection sources worldwide: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  SARS-CoV-2 evolves at $\sim 1\text{--}2$ substitutions per month ($\sim 8 \times 10^{-4}$ substitutions/site/year), which is roughly 2–3 times slower than its serial interval ($\sim 4\text{--}5$ days). Consequently, dozens of infected individuals across multiple transmission generations and countries carry 100% identical consensus genomes (as shown by 160 genomes collapsing into 100 haplotypes in the authors' own dataset). When only a tiny fraction of global infections is sequenced (160 genomes out of tens of thousands of cases by early March 2020), observing matching or 1-step-adjacent genomes between two travelers cannot establish direct transmission or identify an unsequenced intermediary source without joint epidemiological contact data.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Outgroup Parsimony Rooting with Time-Calibrated Probabilistic Phylodynamics
* **WHY:** Rooting a near-identical intraspecific viral alignment with a 96.2%-similar bat virus via unweighted parsimony suffers from severe long-branch homoplasy and ignores exact sample collection dates.
* **WHAT:** Remove `BatCoVRaTG13` from the ingroup parsimony network and infer root placement and uncertainty using tip-dated maximum likelihood or Bayesian coalescent phylodynamics under a realistic nucleotide substitution model.
* **HOW:** Run IQ-TREE + TreeTime or BEAST on the 160 human genomes using an HKY+$\Gamma$ or GTR+$\Gamma$ substitution model with empirical collection dates (`2019-12-24` to `2020-03-04`), and report the posterior root probability of clade B versus clade A alongside root-to-tip regression statistics.

#### 2. Mask Known Homoplastic and Sequencing-Error Sites (`np11083`) and Quantify Topology Uncertainty
* **WHY:** Unmasked sequencing errors at homopolymer tracts (`np11083`) and terminal amplicon primers dominate parsimony step counts in low-diversity SARS-CoV-2 alignments, creating spurious loops and clusters.
* **WHAT:** Mask known problematic sites and compute statistical support for internal nodes.
* **HOW:** Mask position 11083 (and any lab-specific recurrent variants present in only a single submitting laboratory in Dataset S1), re-estimate the phylogeny using maximum likelihood with 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates, and report bootstrap support values (which will be near 0–50% for single-mutation internal branches).

#### 3. Retract Claims of Immunological/Environmental Resistance and Direct Transmission Tracing
* **WHY:** A descriptive haplotype plot of 160 unequally sampled genomes provides zero statistical power to distinguish biological adaptation or host resistance from stochastic importations and testing disparities.
* **WHAT:** Remove all claims regarding "immunological or environmental resistance against this type outside Asia" and claims that the network can identify undocumented infection sources for quarantine.
* **HOW:** Rewrite the Abstract and Main Text to state explicitly that geographic cluster frequencies in GISAID as of March 4, 2020 reflect early travel introductions and heterogeneous national sequencing capacity rather than viral fitness differences or complete transmission chains.

#### 4. Account for Geographic and Temporal Sampling Skew via Subsampling Sensitivity Analysis
* **WHY:** Over-representation of specific travel clusters (e.g., early Italian, German, Washington State, and Wuhan sequences) distorts node sizes and apparent geographic fixation in Figure 1.
* **WHAT:** Quantify how sensitive haplotype proportions (A, B, C) are to geographic and contact-cluster subsampling.
* **HOW:** Stratify the 160 sequences by submitting laboratory and epidemiological cluster (Dataset S1), collapse known household/travel clusters to a single index sequence, and report the resulting geographic distribution with exact binomial confidence intervals.
