### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This short commentary/correspondence piece summarizes the taxonomy and host range of influenza viruses (Types A, B, C, and D) and highlights the March 2024 spillover of clade 2.3.4.4b highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) into U.S. dairy cattle and a goat in Minnesota. The text consists of four paragraphs (lines 38–44) and five bullet-point highlights (lines 26–34), citing five references, without any original primary data, phylogenetic analysis, epidemiological modeling, or quantitative tables.

While the commentary notes an important emerging zoonotic event, its scientific utility is severely compromised by claims in the Highlights that are never substantiated or even mentioned in the main text, internal contradictions regarding historical influenza infections in cattle, and CRediT authorship declarations claiming "Formal analysis" and "Data curation" despite the complete absence of data or analysis. Resolving these issues requires rewriting the Highlights to match the text, correcting the contradictory statements on bovine influenza susceptibility, and revising the CRediT statement to reflect a commentary rather than an empirical study.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Highlights 4 and 5 claiming cross-species transmission of H5N1 between ruminants and humans and the presence of H5N1 viral RNA in milk (lines 32–34): [Overstated].**  
  Highlight 4 asserts "Cross-species transmission of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus between ruminant animals and humans," and Highlight 5 states "Highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus RNA was present in milk." Neither human cases linked to ruminants nor viral RNA detection in milk (only that sick cows "produced less milk," line 42) is discussed, cited, or supported anywhere in the body of the manuscript (lines 38–44).

* **Claims regarding historical susceptibility of cattle to Influenza A viruses (lines 42 vs. 44): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The manuscript contradicts itself across consecutive paragraphs. Line 42 states that "Although cows routinely contract influenza viruses, this is the first time that a HPAI H5N1 virus has been found in cattle," whereas line 44 states that "Generally speaking, cattle were largely unaffected by IAVs, even though most of the domesticated and wild animals at the human-animal interface succumbed to infection over the past few decades." The text fails to distinguish between Influenza D virus (where cattle are the primary reservoir, noted in line 40) and Influenza A virus.

* **Claim that the U.S. cattle and goat outbreaks were caused by infected wild birds or humans on farms (line 42): [Fragile].**  
  Line 42 attributes the source of the livestock infections to "infected wild birds or humans by HPAI virus on farms or the farm surroundings [3]" without providing any phylogenetic, genomic, or epidemiological evidence for human-to-cattle transmission, nor discussing cattle-to-cattle transmission via milking equipment or animal movement.

* **Proposal for universal vaccine immunization and viral surveillance across susceptible or possibly susceptible animals (line 44): [Overstated].**  
  The concluding recommendation that "it is very necessary to undergo vaccine immunization and viral surveillance in susceptible or possible susceptible animals" is an unsubstantiated policy prescription that ignores antigenic matching, DIVA (differentiating infected from vaccinated animals) trade barriers, and economic/logistical feasibility in livestock and wildlife.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Align Highlights with Main Text Content and Citations
* **WHY:** Highlights 4 and 5 make specific empirical claims (ruminant-to-human transmission and H5N1 RNA in milk) that do not appear anywhere in the body text.
* **WHAT:** Either expand the main text with primary literature citations documenting H5N1 viral loads in unpasteurized milk and confirmed dairy-worker infections, or remove Highlights 4 and 5.
* **HOW:** Add a paragraph to line 43 citing the primary virological and epidemiological reports of H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b shedding in bovine mammary tissue/milk and occupational conjunctivitis/respiratory infection in dairy workers, or restrict the Highlights strictly to the wild mammal and livestock spillover events described in lines 42–44.

#### 2. Resolve Internal Contradictions on Bovine Influenza Susceptibility
* **WHY:** Stating that "cows routinely contract influenza viruses" (line 42) and then "cattle were largely unaffected by IAVs" (line 44) confuses readers by conflating Influenza D virus with Influenza A virus.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly distinguish bovine susceptibility to Influenza D virus (IDV) versus Influenza A virus (IAV).
* **HOW:** Revise line 42 to read: *"Although cattle serve as a primary reservoir for Influenza D virus (IDV), they were historically considered refractory to Influenza A viruses (IAVs), making this the first documented outbreak of HPAI H5N1 in dairy cattle."*

#### 3. Correct the CRediT Authorship Contribution Statement
* **WHY:** Line 49 lists "Data curation" for two authors and "Formal analysis" for another, yet the manuscript contains no dataset, table, figure, or statistical/phylogenetic analysis.
* **WHAT:** Update the CRediT roles to accurately reflect a literature commentary.
* **HOW:** Remove `Data curation` and `Formal analysis` from line 49, retaining `Conceptualization`, `Literature search`, `Writing – original draft`, `Writing – review & editing`, `Supervision`, and `Funding acquisition`.
