### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This multi-modal study investigates whether live bacteria colonize second-trimester human fetal organs (12–22 weeks estimated gestational age [EGA]) and prime tissue-resident fetal T cells in utero. The authors combine suspension mass cytometry (CyTOF) of T cells across fetal organs ($n = 3$–$9$ per organ), BactQuant 16S qPCR, nested V2–V6 / V4–V5 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing at Singapore Immunology Network (SIgN) and 5-region multiplexed 16S sequencing at the Weizmann Institute of Science (WIS) ($n = 169$ fetal tissue samples and $n = 107$ negative controls), anaerobic/aerobic bacterial culture ($n = 8$ fetuses), scanning electron microscopy (SEM, $n = 4$) and 16S RNA-ISH ($n = 3$) of fetal intestine, and in vitro syngeneic T cell expansion assays ($n = 4$) coculturing fetal mesenteric lymph node (mLN) T cells with autologous dendritic cells (DCs) pulsed with heat-killed fetal bacterial isolates. Raw sequencing data are deposited at GEO (GSE169306).

While the CyTOF characterization of second-trimester fetal T cell heterogeneity (including $\text{CD45RO}^+$ memory, $\text{TNF}^+\text{IFN}\gamma^+$ effector memory, and $\text{PD1}^+\text{Ki67}^+$ regulatory T cells) is technically solid, the central conclusion that healthy human fetuses harbor a live multi-organ microbiome that primes fetal T cells in utero is undermined by severe sampling, microbiological, and immunological confounders: (1) all fetal specimens were obtained from prostaglandin-induced medical terminations delivered vaginally through the birth canal (STAR Methods, line 130 of offset 46080), and the dominant "fetal" signal follows a stark external-to-internal gradient (highest in placenta and skin, lower in gut/lung, lowest in spleen/thymus; Figures 2F and 3C) dominated by classic cervico-vaginal and skin taxa (*Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Prevotella*, *Finegoldia*); (2) the SIgN 16S sequencing protocol uses 72 total cycles of nested PCR (12 + 30 + 30 cycles; STAR Methods line 182 of offset 46080) and explicitly classifies well-known kit/water contaminants (*Flavobacterium*, *Afipia*, *Bradyrhizobium*, *Brevundimonas*, *Pseudomonas*) as "fetal-enriched taxa" (Figure 2G); (3) the syngeneic T cell proliferation assay tests *Staphylococcus* (and a taxon inconsistently described as *Lactobacillus* and *Staphylococcus* in Results vs. *Lactobacilli* and *Salmonella* in STAR Methods line 10 of offset 92160) without TCR/MHC Class I/II blocking controls or superantigen exclusion, where staphylococcal superantigens (enterotoxins/TSST-1) and innate TLR ligands induce polyclonal T cell proliferation independent of prior in utero antigenic priming; and (4) the differential enrichment formulas in STAR Methods (lines 239–253 of offset 46080) contain mathematical definitions that do not compute abundance fold changes. Re-framing the manuscript around the confounder of vaginal delivery, controlling for superantigen/innate activation, and correcting the mathematical and methods discrepancies are essential.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Second-trimester human fetal organs harbor a diverse, tissue-specific T cell repertoire including $\text{CD45RO}^+$ effector memory T cells and $\text{PD1}^+\text{Ki67}^+$ Tregs (lines 16–36, Figure 1): [Well Supported].**  
  The CyTOF profiling across fetal gut, lung, liver, skin, thymus, mLN, and blood (Figure 1A–I, Figure S1) clearly demonstrates distinct tissue-specific clustering between fetal and adult organs, enrichment of $\text{CD25}^+\text{FOXP3}^+\text{CD152}^+$ Tregs in fetal tissues, and the presence of $\text{CD45RO}^+$ T cells capable of producing TNF and IFN$\gamma$ upon PMA/ionomycin stimulation in peripheral fetal organs but not thymus.

* **16S rRNA sequencing and anaerobic culturing demonstrate genuine in utero bacterial colonization across fetal organs rather than intrapartum vaginal/skin contamination or kit background (lines 39–76, Figures 2–3): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Three independent lines of evidence within the manuscript point to intrapartum cervico-vaginal contamination and low-biomass amplification artifacts rather than in utero colonization:
  1. **Vaginal delivery and anatomical gradient:** As disclosed in STAR Methods (offset 46080, line 130), *"Mid-trimester terminations were medically induced by the use of prostaglandins, and the fetus was delivered through the birth canal."* During prostaglandin-induced labor and vaginal passage after membrane rupture, maternal vaginal fluid coats the placenta and fetal skin and enters the fetal oropharynx, respiratory tract, and gastrointestinal tract. Both the 16S $\beta$-diversity distance to PBS (Figure 2F) and the number of cultured species (Figure 3C) are highest on surface-exposed tissues (placenta and skin), intermediate in mucosally connected organs (lung and gut), and lowest in internal parenchymal organs (spleen and thymus), matching the exact physical gradient expected from intrapartum surface exposure. Furthermore, the dominant cultured genera across organs are canonical vaginal/skin flora: *Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Prevotella*, and *Finegoldia* (Figure 3D).
  2. **Kit/reagent contaminants misclassified as "fetal-enriched taxa":** At SIgN, DNA was amplified using 72 cycles of three-step nested PCR (12 cycles V2–V6 + 30 cycles V4–V5 + 30 cycles barcoding; offset 46080, line 182). In Figure 2G and line 57, five of the seven genera highlighted as "fetal-enriched taxa" (*Flavobacterium*, *Afipia*, *Pseudomonas*, *Bradyrhizobium*, and *Brevundimonas*) are notorious reagent/ultrapure-water contaminants documented in the low-biomass microbiome literature (Salter et al., 2014; Eisenhofer et al., 2019), and none of those five environmental water rods were recovered in the anaerobic/aerobic culture experiments from the same tissues (Figure 3D).
  3. **Flawed differential abundance equations:** In STAR Methods (offset 46080, lines 245–253), "Relative Percentage Abundance" ($\text{RPA}_{\text{OTU}}$) used to filter differentially enriched gut taxa in Figures 2H and S2K is defined not by sequence read abundance, but by sample presence counts: $\text{RPA}_{\text{OTU}} = (S_{\text{Gut}} \times 100) / (S_{\text{Gut}} + S_{\text{Spleen/Thymus}} + S_{\text{PBS}})$, where $S$ is the *"Counts of samples where reads exist"*, which is an unnormalized prevalence ratio that is distorted whenever unequal numbers of Gut, Spleen/Thymus, and PBS samples are sequenced.

* **SEM and 16S RNA-ISH confirm live bacteria colonizing the 14-week EGA fetal mid-gut lumen (lines 79–95, Figure 4): [Fragile].**  
  The SEM images of three 14-week EGA fetal intestines show 1–2 $\mu\text{m}$ spherical structures embedded in mucin-like threads (Figure 4A), which cannot be definitively distinguished by morphology alone from mucin granules, cellular debris, or extracellular vesicles without immunogold labeling. While 16S RNA-ISH (Figure 4C–E, $n = 3$) shows luminal green fluorescence in the gut compared to liver and mLN, the STAR Methods (offset 92160, lines 3–5) reveal that image color intensity was manually boosted to 150% for EB-16S-rRNA and that CellProfiler threshold correction factors were varied across a 4-fold range (`1–4 for EB-16 s-rRNA and 1.7–2.5 for EPCAM`), and no scrambled/sense negative-control RNAscope probe on serial fetal gut sections is shown to exclude non-specific mucin/meconium probe trapping.

* **Proliferation of fetal mLN T cells in response to autologous DCs pulsed with heat-killed fetal *Staphylococcus* or *Lactobacillus* proves antigen-specific in utero microbial memory priming (lines 98–100 and offset 46080 lines 1–29, Figures 5–6): [Overstated].**  
  First, the manuscript contains a major discrepancy regarding which bacteria were used in the T cell assays: the Results text and Figures 5–6 state that DCs were primed with fetal *Lactobacilli* and fetal *Staphylococci*, whereas STAR Methods (offset 92160, line 10) states: *"Fetal bacteria (Lactobacilli and Salmonella) were isolated and cultured as indicated above."* Second, observing 70% ($\pm 15\%$) and 55% ($\pm 13\%$) proliferation of total fetal mLN T cells after 6 days with heat-killed *Staphylococcus* or *Lactobacillus* (Figure 5C) is far too high to represent recall of rare microbe-specific memory clones (especially given that $<12\%$ of input mLN T cells were $\text{CD45RO}^+$ at Day 0, line 1 of offset 46080, and naive T cells converted to $\text{CD45RO}^+\text{CD69}^+$ during the 6-day culture). *Staphylococcus* species produce potent heat-stable superantigens (staphylococcal enterotoxins, TSST-1) that cross-link MHC Class II on PFA-fixed or PTI-treated DCs to TCR $V\beta$ chains, driving massive polyclonal expansion of both naive and memory T cells without prior antigen encounter. Without MHC Class I/II blocking antibodies, TCR sequencing, or testing an exogenous non-fetal microbe (e.g., *E. coli* or a marine bacterium never encountered by humans) as a specificity control, this assay cannot distinguish innate/superantigen-driven polyclonal activation from genuine in utero memory recall.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Include Non-Fetal Microbial Specificity Controls and MHC/Superantigen Controls in T Cell Assays
* **WHY:** A 55%–70% expansion of total mLN T cells from an input pool containing $>88\%$ $\text{CD45RO}^-$ naive T cells upon exposure to heat-killed *Staphylococcus* or *Lactobacillus* (even with PFA-fixed DCs) is consistent with staphylococcal superantigen cross-linking or innate PAMP costimulation rather than clonal recall of in utero-primed memory T cells.
* **WHAT:** Perform specificity and mechanistic controls to test whether the T cell proliferation requires cognate peptide-MHC presentation and whether it is specific to fetal-isolated strains.
* **HOW:** (1) Sort pure pre-existing $\text{CD45RO}^+$ memory vs. $\text{CD45RA}^+$ naive fetal mLN T cells prior to CTV labeling and test each fraction separately; (2) include pan-HLA-DR/MHC-I/II blocking antibodies during DC-T cell coculture; (3) test heat-killed non-human/environmental bacteria that could not have been encountered in utero alongside the fetal isolates; and (4) screen the *Staphylococcus* isolate genome/supernatant for enterotoxin/superantigen genes.

#### 2. Control for Intrapartum Vaginal Exposure and Correct the Differential Taxa Equations
* **WHY:** Because all fetuses were delivered vaginally after prostaglandin induction, maternal cervico-vaginal microbes inevitably contact the placenta, skin, and mucosal orifices, explaining why *Gardnerella* and *Lactobacillus* dominate and why diversity decreases from skin/placenta to internal organs. Additionally, the $\text{RPA}_{\text{OTU}}$ formula in STAR Methods (offset 46080, line 249) is an unnormalized sample-count ratio rather than relative read abundance.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly stratify or compare against hysterotomy/cesarean-delivered fetal tissues (or matched maternal vaginal swabs), remove known kit/water contaminants (*Bradyrhizobium*, *Afipia*, *Brevundimonas*, *Flavobacterium*, *Pseudomonas*) from the "fetal-enriched" claim, and replace the flawed $\text{LOG}_2\text{FC}$ / $\text{RPA}_{\text{OTU}}$ heuristic with a formal statistical contamination-removal and differential abundance framework.
* **HOW:** Apply `decontam` (using both the prevalence method across the 42 PBS + 10 reagent + 55 environmental controls and the frequency method using the BactQuant qPCR Ct values from Figure 2B) to identify and filter reagent contaminants, and use `ANCOM-BC` or `ALDEx2` with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjustment for Figure 2H and S2K.

#### 3. Add Negative-Probe Controls and Uniform Thresholding for 16S RNA-ISH
* **WHY:** In STAR Methods (offset 92160, lines 3–5), the RNAscope threshold correction factor was varied between 1 and 4 across images, and liver/mLN parenchyma were used as negative tissue controls rather than a negative-control probe (e.g., *dapB*) on the mucin-rich fetal gut lumen itself.
* **WHAT:** Re-quantify the RNA-ISH images using a single fixed intensity threshold across all tissues and include a negative-control probe on serial fetal gut sections.
* **HOW:** Re-run the CellProfiler pipeline with a constant threshold correction factor across all gut, liver, and mLN sections (Figure 4D–E), and report the signal-to-background ratio relative to a non-targeting control probe in the gut lumen.

#### 4. Resolve Text and Methods Discrepancies
* **WHY:** Conflicting bacterial species names and statistical test descriptions between the main text, figure legends, and STAR Methods create confusion about what was actually performed.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Salmonella vs. Staphylococci discrepancy (offset 92160, line 10):** STAR Methods states *"Fetal bacteria (Lactobacilli and Salmonella) were isolated and cultured as indicated above"*, whereas Figures 5–6 and Results state *Lactobacilli* and *Staphylococci* were used (and *Salmonella* does not appear anywhere in the cultured isolates of Figure 3D). Correct *Salmonella* to *Staphylococci* (or clarify if *Salmonella* was used).
  * **Paired vs. unpaired $t$-tests in Figures 5 and 6 (offset 46080, lines 7 and 21):** The captions for Figures 5D/6C state significance was determined by *"paired t test"*, whereas Figures 5H/6E on the exact same $n = 4$ donor experiments state *"two-tailed, unpaired Student's t test"*, and no multiple-comparison correction across the 4 experimental conditions is applied. Use a consistent paired/repeated-measures test (e.g., repeated-measures ANOVA with Dunnett's or Holm-Bonferroni post-hoc test) across all panels of Figures 5 and 6.
