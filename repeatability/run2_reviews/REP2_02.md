### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces BUSTED (Branch-Site Unrestricted Statistical Test for Episodic Diversification), a random-effects codon substitution model designed to test for gene-wide evidence of episodic positive selection ($\omega = d_N / d_S > 1$) across either an entire phylogeny or an *a priori* specified subset of foreground branches. Building on the BS-REL framework, BUSTED models branch-site substitution rates using three discrete $\omega$ categories ($\omega_1 \le \omega_2 \le 1 \le \omega_3$) whose weights and values are estimated independently for foreground and background branch partitions, and tests the null hypothesis $\omega_3^{\text{FG}} = 1$ using a conservative $\chi^2_2$ reference distribution. The authors also define a zero-extra-cost site-level Exploratory Evidence Ratio ($ER_s = 2 \log [P(D_s \mid M) / P(D_s \mid M_s)]$) by clamping $\omega_3^{\text{FG}} = 1$ at site $s$ without re-optimizing global parameters. They benchmark BUSTED on 11 simulation scenarios from Lu & Guindon (2014), parametric simulations based on sperm lysin, an empirical HIV-1 reverse transcriptase (RT) drug-resistance dataset, and 10,779 Euteleostome alignments from Selectome v06. Both the HyPhy batch code and an interactive Datamonkey web application are publicly available.

This is a rigorously formulated and well-validated methodological contribution. By allowing $\omega$ to vary stochastically across both foreground and background branches, BUSTED resolves two well-known vulnerabilities of classical branch-site (`NYbs`) and covarion (`fitmodel`) tests: loss of power when positive selection is restricted to a subset of foreground branches, and false positives caused by unmodeled positive or relaxed selection on background branches. The simulation benchmarks are symmetric and informative, particularly the size-adjusted power analysis (Figure 2B–C) demonstrating that `fitmodel`'s apparent power advantage stems from an inflated Type I error rate (up to 15–23% at $\alpha = 0.05$). Only minor improvements—such as deriving the exact asymptotic mixture weights for the likelihood ratio test (LRT) statistic, evaluating sensitivity to site-to-site synonymous rate variation, and formalizing false-discovery thresholds for the exploratory site-level evidence ratios—are needed.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **BUSTED maintains conservative Type I error control under both strict neutral nulls and complex branch-site variation: [Well Supported].**  
  In Table 1 (`Tree 1 alpha XX`, `16 XX`, `32 XX`, `64 XX`), BUSTED's empirical false-positive rate at nominal $\alpha = 0.05$ ranges from $0.006$ to $0.020$, whereas the covarion test (`LGc` / `fitmodel`) reaches $0.150$ on the 64-taxon null (`64 XX`) and $0.23$ on the sperm lysin null simulations (Figure 2B). The conservative behavior is mathematically justified by bounding the intractable boundary mixture of $\chi^2_0, \chi^2_1$, and $\chi^2_2$ (which arises when $\omega_2 = \omega_3 = 1$ or $p_3 = 0$ under the null) with a $\chi^2_2$ distribution.

* **When foreground branches are specified *a priori*, BUSTED achieves higher power than the Nielsen–Yang branch-site (`NYbs`) and covarion (`LGc`) models across most simulation regimes: [Well Supported].**  
  Across the Lu & Guindon (2014) benchmarks with known foreground partitions (`BUSTED FG`, Table 1), BUSTED outperforms `NYbs` in 4 of 5 scenarios (e.g., $0.850$ vs. $0.785$ in `16 B XU`; $0.930$ vs. $0.754$ in `32 B XU`; $0.259$ vs. $0.102$ in `Tree 1 alpha XV`), only trailing on the smallest 10-taxon alignment (`Tree 2 delta gamma XV`, $0.058$ vs. $0.368$) where estimating a separate 3-bin random-effects distribution on very few foreground branches incurs a variance penalty.

* **On size-calibrated tests (controlling empirical Type I error at 0.05), BUSTED has higher power than the covarion model (`fitmodel`) across the whole phylogeny: [Well Supported].**  
  In Figure 2C, calibrating the LRT cutoff of `fitmodel` to match the nominal $\alpha = 0.05$ error rate on null simulations eliminates its apparent power advantage and confirms that BUSTED provides superior detection power across simulated $\omega_3 > 1$ values.

* **Site-wise Evidence Ratios ($ER_s$) in HIV-1 RT identify known drug-resistance mutations comparably to specialized directional selection models (`MEDS`, `EDEPS`): [Moderately Supported].**  
  In Table 2, using a heuristic cutoff of $ER_s > 6.635$ (nominal $\chi^2_1$ $P < 0.01$), BUSTED flags 15 sites, of which 13 are Stanford HIVDB resistance mutations (13/15 true positives, vs. 13/17 for `MEDS` and 13/16 for `EDEPS`). While the authors appropriately caution that $ER_s$ does not constitute a formal LRT because global parameters are not re-optimized under $M_s$, the sensitivity of this 13/15 concordance to the ad hoc $P < 0.01$ cutoff and the lack of multiple-testing calibration across all RT codons should be noted.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Characterize a Less Conservative Asymptotic Mixture Distribution for the Gene-Wide LRT
* **WHY:** Using $\chi^2_2$ as the reference distribution when the non-degenerate boundary test ($\omega_2 < 1$, testing $\omega_3 = 1$ vs. $\omega_3 > 1$ with $p_3 > 0$) follows a $0.5 \chi^2_0 + 0.5 \chi^2_1$ mixture makes BUSTED overly conservative (Type I error $0.006$–$0.016$ at $\alpha = 0.05$), sacrificing power on small alignments such as `Tree 2 delta gamma XV` ($N = 10$).
* **WHAT:** Report how much power is recovered by using a $50{:}50$ mixture of $\chi^2_1$ and $\chi^2_2$ (or $0.25\chi^2_0 + 0.5\chi^2_1 + 0.25\chi^2_2$) when $\hat{\omega}_2 < 1$ under the null.
* **HOW:** Re-tabulate the detection rates in Table 1 and Figure 2A–B using the standard boundary mixture distribution when $\hat{\omega}_2$ is strictly bounded away from $1$, reserving the $\chi^2_2$ bound for degenerate boundary fits where $\hat{\omega}_2 \approx 1$.

#### 2. Evaluate Robustness of BUSTED to Synonymous Rate Variation ($\alpha_s$)
* **WHY:** As noted in the Discussion, BUSTED assumes a constant synonymous substitution rate across sites and branches ($\alpha = 1$). Strong site-to-site synonymous rate variation (e.g., RNA secondary structure or overlapping reading frames) can inflate $\omega_3$ estimates in random-effects codon models.
* **WHAT:** Test BUSTED's Type I error rate under neutral simulations with site-to-site synonymous rate variation ($\alpha_s \sim \text{Gamma}$), or note how site-level synonymous rate variation affects the Selectome screen (Figure 3).
* **HOW:** Simulate 500 neutral replicates ($\omega \le 1$) on the sperm lysin tree with gamma-distributed synonymous rates ($\text{CV} = 0.5$) and report the empirical false-positive rate of BUSTED at $\alpha = 0.05$.

#### 3. Provide Precision–Recall or ROC Curves Across Thresholds for the HIV-1 RT Site-Level Benchmark
* **WHY:** Table 2 compares BUSTED ($ER_s$ at $\chi^2_1$ $P < 0.01$), `MEDS` ($P < 0.005$), `FEEDS` ($P < 0.01$), and `EDEPS` ($\text{BF} > 100$) at single, non-equivalent thresholds, making it difficult to assess whether BUSTED's 13/15 precision holds across the full ranking of codons.
* **WHAT:** Add a supplementary precision–recall or ROC curve comparing site rankings across all codons in HIV-1 RT against the Stanford HIVDB ground truth.
* **HOW:** Using the already-computed $ER_s$, `MEDS` $p$-values, `FEEDS` $p$-values, and `EDEPS` Bayes factors for all RT sites, plot True Positives vs. False Positives as the threshold varies continuously, and recommend checking the gene-wide BUSTED $p$-value ($p \le 0.05$) as a gatekeeper before interpreting individual $ER_s$ values.
