### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This translational study investigates the relationship between the oral and gut microbiomes and clinical response to anti-PD-1 immunotherapy in metastatic melanoma patients ($N = 112$ enrolled; $n = 89$ evaluable by RECIST 1.1 at 6 months; $n = 109$ oral 16S samples; $n = 43$ baseline fecal 16S samples comprising 30 responders [R] and 13 non-responders [NR]; $n = 25$ fecal metagenomic WGS samples comprising 14 R and 11 NR). The authors combine 16S rRNA and shotgun metagenomic profiling with tumor whole-exome sequencing ($n = 10$), tumor multiplex immunohistochemistry ($n = 21$ for CD8+ IHC; $n = 4$ for myeloid multiplex IHC), peripheral blood flow cytometry, and fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) into germ-free (GF) mice ($n = 5$ R-FMT vs. $n = 6$ NR-FMT at day 14; $n = 2$–$3$ per group for anti-PD-L1 therapy and immune profiling). They report that gut (but not oral) alpha diversity and *Ruminococcaceae*/*Faecalibacterium* abundance are higher in responders and associated with longer progression-free survival (PFS), whereas *Bacteroidales* is enriched in non-responders. Raw 16S, WGS, and WES datasets are publicly deposited in ENA/EGA (PRJEB22894, PRJEB22874, PRJEB22895, PRJEB22893, EGAS00001002698).

This work provides an important multi-modal dataset linking patient gut microbiome composition to checkpoint blockade outcomes, with commendable open data deposition. However, several statistical and experimental design limitations weaken the strength of its causal and predictive claims: (1) substantial attrition from $N = 112$ enrolled patients to $n = 43$ with evaluable fecal 16S (and only $n = 39$ in the PFS survival analysis, $n = 25$ in WGS, $n = 15$ in tumor IHC correlation, and $n = 4$ in multiplex IHC); (2) post-hoc feature selection ("top hits") evaluated on the same 39–43 patients using forward stepwise Cox regression without cross-validation or an independent validation cohort; (3) severely underpowered murine FMT experiments ($n = 2$ R-FMT vs. $n = 3$ NR-FMT mice in Figure 4I–K, where the anti-PD-L1 tumor growth difference is non-significant at $p = 0.20$, and appears derived from single human R and NR donors rather than biological replication across multiple patient donors); and (4) internal inconsistencies in hazard ratio directionality and figure panel citations in the main text. These issues can be addressed through rigorous statistical re-analysis (penalized regression with cross-validation, formal treatment of donor-level random effects in FMT) and tempering the causal claims for the underpowered murine and multiplex IHC sub-analyses.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Higher baseline fecal (but not oral) microbiome alpha diversity and *Ruminococcaceae* / *Faecalibacterium* abundance in anti-PD-1 responders vs. non-responders (lines 164, 176–178, Figures 1C, 2C–F): [Well Supported].**  
  Within the $n = 43$ fecal subcohort (30 R, 13 NR), higher alpha diversity ($p < 0.01$ across multiple indices) and enrichment of *Ruminococcaceae*/*Faecalibacterium* in R vs. *Bacteroidales* in NR are consistently supported across 16S OTU enrichment, LEfSe, FDR-adjusted Mann-Whitney tests (Figure 2E), crOTU phylogenetic clustering (Figure 3A–B), and shotgun metagenomic species profiling ($n = 25$, Figure 2F). The specificity to the gut microbiome is reinforced by the negative control provided by the larger matched oral microbiome cohort ($n = 86$ evaluable, $p = 0.11$).

* **Fecal alpha diversity and *Faecalibacterium* / *Bacteroidales* abundances are independent clinical predictors of progression-free survival (lines 176, 194, Figures 1E, 3C): [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 1E ($n = 39$, 13 per tertile), univariate Cox hazard ratios for High vs. Intermediate diversity (HR = 3.60, 95% CI: 1.02–12.74, $p = 0.02$) and High vs. Low diversity (HR = 3.57, 95% CI: 1.02–12.52, $p = 0.04$) barely exclude 1.0, and the HR direction is inverted in the text (reporting HR > 1 for High vs. Low despite High diversity having longer PFS; line 194 repeats this inversion by reporting Intermediate HR = 3.60 and Low HR = 3.57 alongside High *Faecalibacterium* HR = 2.92 and *Bacteroidales* HR = 0.39). Furthermore, selecting "top hits" from differential abundance testing on the $n = 43$ cohort and entering them into forward stepwise Cox selection on the same $n = 39$ patients (line 194) introduces circularity (winner's curse) and risks overfitting given the small number of progression events.

* **Enhanced intratumoral CD8+ T cell infiltration and antigen presentation machinery in patients with high gut *Faecalibacterium* (lines 198, Figure 4A–F): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The positive correlation between gut *Faecalibacterium*/*Ruminococcaceae* abundance and pre-treatment intratumoral CD8+ T cell density ($r^2 = 0.42, p < 0.01$ in $n = 15$ matched patients, Figure 4B–C) and peripheral effector T cells (Figure 4D) provides plausible immunological corroboration. However, the multiplex IHC analysis of myeloid antigen-processing markers (Figure 4E–F) includes only $n = 2$ high-*Faecalibacterium* patients vs. $n = 2$ high-*Bacteroidales* patients, which is too small for statistical inference.

* **Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) in germ-free mice establishes a causal link to anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy response and intratumoral CD8+ T cell infiltration (lines 164, 206, Figure 4G–K): [Overstated].**  
  While baseline tumor growth at day 14 (prior to or without checkpoint blockade) was lower in R-FMT ($n = 5$) vs. NR-FMT ($n = 6$) mice ($p = 0.04$, Figure 4H), the key experiment testing response to $\alpha$-PD-L1 therapy (Figure 4I) and tissue CD8+ quantification (Figure 4J–K) used only $n = 2$ R-FMT mice, $n = 3$ NR-FMT mice, and $n = 2$ Control mice, yielding non-significant Mann-Whitney $p$-values across all reported comparisons ($p = 0.20$ for R-FMT vs. NR-FMT in Figure 4I; $p = 0.30$ in Figure 4J; $p = 0.17$ in Figure 4K). Despite these non-significant statistics in the figure caption, line 206 claims that "mice transplanted with R-FMT also exhibited improved responses to anti-PD-L1 therapy (Fig. 4I)" and "a higher density of CD8+ T cells than mice receiving NR-FMT (Fig. 4J)" with `**** p < 0.0001` asterisks apparently derived by treating 12 microscope high-power fields (HPFs) from $n = 2$ mice as independent biological replicates (pseudoreplication).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Pseudoreplication and Report Donor-Level Replication in Murine FMT Analyses
* **WHY:** In Figure 4I–K, sample sizes are $n = 2$–$3$ mice per group, and the statistical significance symbols in Figure 4J–K are computed across 5–12 microscopic regions (HPFs) pooled from only 2 mice per group, violating independence and inflating Type I error, while the mouse-level Mann-Whitney tests reported in the caption are non-significant ($p = 0.17$ to $p = 0.33$).
* **WHAT:** Re-analyze all murine histology and flow cytometry endpoints with the individual mouse (and human stool donor) as the unit of replication, and temper the main-text claims where $p > 0.05$.
* **HOW:** Fit a linear mixed-effects model with random intercepts for mouse ID (and human donor ID if multiple donors were used) when analyzing multi-region HPF counts, explicitly state how many distinct R and NR human patients provided stool for the FMT experiments, and revise line 206 to acknowledge that the $\alpha$-PD-L1 treatment comparison ($n = 2$ vs. $n = 3$, $p = 0.20$) is a preliminary trend rather than statistically significant proof.

#### 2. Correct Cox Hazard Ratio Directionality and Account for Selection Bias in PFS Modeling
* **WHY:** In Figure 1E and line 194, the reported hazard ratios mix reference categories inconsistently: line 194 states that the strongest predictors of response were Intermediate diversity (HR = 3.60), Low diversity (HR = 3.57), *Faecalibacterium* (HR = 2.92), and *Bacteroidales* (HR = 0.39), which reverses the direction of hazard between diversity (where HR > 1 is reported as higher risk of progression in Low/Intermediate) and taxa (where HR > 1 for *Faecalibacterium* and HR < 1 for *Bacteroidales* actually represent *reduced* vs. *increased* risk of progression, or vice versa). Additionally, forward stepwise selection on $n = 39$ patients using taxa pre-screened on the same cohort overfits the data.
* **WHAT:** Standardize all Cox models so that HR < 1 consistently denotes prolonged PFS (reduced hazard of progression) and HR > 1 denotes shortened PFS, and evaluate out-of-sample predictive accuracy using cross-validation.
* **HOW:** Re-tabulate all univariate and multivariable Cox models with explicit reference groups (e.g., High vs. Low), report optimism-corrected concordance indices ($C$-statistics) via 10-fold cross-validation or bootstrap resampling for the ROC curves in Figure S16, and avoid forward stepwise selection in small samples.

#### 3. Assess Potential Selection Bias from Fecal Sample Attrition ($N = 112 \to n = 43 \to n = 39$)
* **WHY:** Although 112 patients were enrolled and 89 were clinically evaluable (54 R [60.7%], 35 NR [39.3%]), only 43 provided baseline fecal samples (30 R [69.8%], 13 NR [30.2%]), and only 39 are included in Figure 1D–E and Figure 3C.
* **WHAT:** Test whether the 43 patients with fecal samples (and the 39 in the PFS analysis) differ systematically from the remaining evaluable patients in clinical characteristics or response rate, and explain why 4 of the 43 fecal samples were excluded from Figure 1D–E and Figure 3C.
* **HOW:** Add a column to Table S2 comparing baseline covariates (age, sex, stage, LDH, prior therapy, response rate) between patients with ($n = 43$) and without ($n = 46$) baseline fecal samples, and document the exclusion criteria for the 4 patients omitted from the $n = 39$ survival analyses.

#### 4. Correct Figure Panel Cross-References in the Main Text
* **WHY:** In Section 1 (lines 194–196), figure panel citations are shifted by one letter relative to the Figure 3 caption.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * Line 194 cites `(p=0.05, Fig. 3D)` for the *Faecalibacterium* and *Bacteroidales* Kaplan-Meier PFS curves, whereas the Figure 3 caption (line 192) labels the KM PFS curves as `(C)` and the MetaCyc pathway clustering heatmap as `(D)`. Change `Fig. 3D` in line 194 to `Fig. 3C`.
  * Line 196 cites `(Fig. 3E)` three times for the unsupervised hierarchical clustering of MetaCyc pathways, which corresponds to `Fig. 3D` in the Figure 3 caption (line 192). Change `Fig. 3E` in line 196 to `Fig. 3D`.
