### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This multicenter cross-sectional study profiles the fecal microbiota of 374 pulmonary and critical care medicine (PCCM) inpatients across three Chinese hospitals (comprising 81 airway disease, 104 lung infection, 37 respiratory failure, 128 non-small cell lung cancer [NSCLC], and 24 other cases) alongside 105 community controls using V3–V4 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing. Using OTU clustering at 97% identity (QIIME v1.9.1 / GreenGenes 13.8), PAM enterotyping, Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, distance-based redundancy analysis (db-RDA), and Random Forest classification, the authors report reduced alpha diversity in inpatients, a small but statistically significant shift in community composition (ANOSIM $R = 0.0371, P = 0.001$), an inpatient-specific *Enterococcaceae* enterotype (7.3%–15.4% across hospitals), 15 differentially abundant families, and an AUC of 0.75 (95% CI: 0.69–0.80) for classifying inpatients vs. controls. Raw sequencing reads are deposited in GSA (CRA012416).

Despite a substantial sample size and public sequence deposition, the study's conclusions face major methodological and analytical limitations. First, the text contains direct internal contradictions regarding the number of identified enterotypes ("Four enterotypes" in the Abstract and Discussion vs. "three distinct enterotypes" in Results and Figure 3C) and the direction of the Simpson index (Abstract and Discussion claim lower diversity across all four indices, whereas Results line 124 and Figure 1G–H show the Simpson index is significantly *higher* in inpatients because QIIME 1 calculates $\sum p_i^2$, a dominance metric). Second, healthy controls were recruited exclusively from a single community cohort rather than matched across the three hospitals, and severe baseline confounders—smoking prevalence (53.5% vs. 28.6%, $P < 0.01$), pack-years (42.8 vs. 19.2, $P < 0.01$), and BMI (21.37 vs. 23.26, $P < 0.01$)—are not adjusted for in the univariate Wilcoxon, ANOSIM, enterotype, or Random Forest analyses. Third, the Random Forest classifier (AUC = 0.75) is evaluated without an independent hold-out test set or cross-hospital validation, and relies on an outdated bioinformatic pipeline (97% OTUs in QIIME 1.9.1 with GreenGenes 13.8, last updated in 2013, while assigning modern 2021 GTDB/NCBI taxonomy names such as *Erysipelatoclostridiaceae* and *Verrucomicrobiota* that do not exist in GreenGenes 13.8). Addressing these flaws requires reprocessing reads into ASVs against a current reference database, fitting multivariable compositional models adjusted for BMI, smoking, age, and hospital center, and validating the classifier across independent hospital sites.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **PCCM inpatients exhibit lower gut microbial alpha diversity and altered beta diversity compared to healthy controls (lines 21, 124–132): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Reduced richness (ACE, Chao1) and Shannon diversity in inpatients are consistent across the three hospitals (Figure 1A–F). However, the authors misinterpret the Simpson index: Results line 124 states "the Simpson index was significantly higher than that of healthy control," whereas Discussion line 199 contradicts this by stating "The lower alpha diversity indices (ACE, Shannon, Chao, and Simpson) observed in inpatients," overlooking that QIIME's Simpson index measures concentration/dominance ($\sum p_i^2$) rather than diversity ($1 - \sum p_i^2$). Furthermore, the beta-diversity effect size is tiny (ANOSIM $R = 0.0371$, PERMANOVA $R^2 = 0.035$), and neither alpha nor beta diversity is adjusted for the large baseline imbalances in BMI and smoking (Table 1) or unmeasured pre-admission non-antibiotic medications (e.g., proton pump inhibitors, chemotherapy).

* **Identification of four vs. three distinct enterotypes, with an exclusive *Enterococcaceae* enterotype in PCCM inpatients (lines 21, 146, 201): [Fragile].**  
  The manuscript is internally inconsistent on the number of enterotypes: Abstract line 21 and Discussion line 201 claim "Four enterotypes... were identified," whereas Results line 146 and Figure 3C caption state "The microbial profiling revealed the presence of three distinct enterotypes at the family level... *Lachnospiraceae* (Enterotype 1), *Enterobacteriaceae* (Enterotype 2) and *Enterococcaceae* (Enterotype 3)." Moreover, PAM clustering with Calinski-Harabasz index optimization was applied to family-level relative abundances without reporting silhouette widths or cluster stability, and the *Enterococcaceae*-dominated cluster (7.3%–15.4% of inpatients) likely reflects nosocomial/prior healthcare exposure or proton-pump inhibitor use rather than a respiratory-specific "gut-lung axis" signature, especially since its prevalence is highest in the heterogeneous "Others" group (17.4%, line 180).

* **Differential abundance of 15 bacterial families between PCCM inpatients and healthy controls (lines 21, 159, 180): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Differential abundance was tested using standard non-parametric Wilcoxon rank-sum tests and one-way ANOVA on relative proportions (lines 76, 159, 192) without accounting for the compositional nature of 16S amplicon data (e.g., ALDEx2, ANCOM-BC, MaAsLin2). Although line 76 states that analyses were "adjusted for age and smoking history," Wilcoxon rank-sum tests and one-way ANOVA cannot adjust for covariates, and BMI (which differed significantly at $P < 0.01$, Table 1) was omitted even from the claimed adjustment list.

* **Random Forest classifier distinguishes PCCM inpatients from healthy controls with AUC = 0.75 based on 15 genera (lines 21, 175): [Overstated].**  
  The Random Forest feature selection (15 genera) and ROC evaluation (AUC = 0.75, 95% CI: 0.69–0.80) were performed on the full dataset without describing any train/test split, nested cross-validation, or leave-one-hospital-out validation, introducing feature-selection leakage. In addition, the class distribution is heavily imbalanced (374 inpatients vs. 105 controls, or 78.1% cases), where ROC-AUC can be overly optimistic compared to Precision-Recall AUC, and no baseline clinical model (using BMI and smoking pack-years alone) is compared.

* **Correlations between gut microbiota and clinical factors (age, medical costs, length of stay, WBC, hemoglobin) via db-RDA (lines 16, 170): [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 5B and line 170, the authors correlate baseline admission fecal microbiota (collected on Day 1 before examinations or treatments, line 64) with cumulative hospital stay duration ($R^2 = 0.0378, P = 0.002$) and total medical costs ($R^2 = 0.0518, P = 0.001$) using univariate marginal tests in db-RDA without adjusting for primary diagnosis (e.g., NSCLC vs. acute pneumonia vs. respiratory failure) or age ($R^2 = 0.0735$), which strongly confound both length of stay and hospital billing.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reconcile the Bioinformatic Pipeline Description and Reprocess Reads with ASV Methods
* **WHY:** Line 71 states that taxonomy was assigned using RDP Classifier v2.11 and GreenGenes 13.8 (released in 2013), yet the text and figures report taxonomy names introduced only in SILVA 138 / GTDB / 2021 NCBI taxonomy (e.g., *Verrucomicrobiota*, *Erysipelatoclostridiaceae*, *Tannerellaceae*). Either the reference database was misreported (likely SILVA 138 on the Majorbio Cloud Platform) or the methods text was copied from an older template.
* **WHAT:** Clarify the exact taxonomic database used and re-run the pipeline using modern denoising (DADA2 or Deblur in QIIME 2) to generate amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) classified against SILVA 138.1 or Greengenes2.
* **HOW:** Process the raw FASTQ files (GSA: CRA012416) through DADA2 in QIIME 2, rarefy to a documented sequencing depth (reporting the exact read depth threshold and rarefaction curves), and update Figures 1–7.

#### 2. Control for Confounders (BMI, Smoking, Age, Hospital Center) Using Multivariable Compositional Models
* **WHY:** Inpatients and controls differ markedly in BMI ($21.37$ vs. $23.26\text{ kg/m}^2$) and smoking (53.5% vs. 28.6%, 42.8 vs. 19.2 pack-years), and all 105 controls come from a single geographic pool while inpatients come from three hospitals in two cities (Guangzhou and Hengyang/Nanhua). Unadjusted Wilcoxon tests and ANOVA conflate these confounders with disease status.
* **WHAT:** Replace unadjusted Wilcoxon/ANOVA tests with multivariable compositional models (ANCOM-BC2 or MaAsLin2) and multivariable PERMANOVA.
* **HOW:** Fit `Abundance ~ Group + Age + Sex + BMI + Smoking_PackYears + (1|Hospital)` in MaAsLin2/ANCOM-BC2, and run marginal PERMANOVA (`adonis2(..., by="margin")`) including `BMI`, `Smoking`, `Age`, and `Sex` before `Group` to quantify the variance uniquely explained by respiratory hospitalization.

#### 3. Implement Leave-One-Hospital-Out Validation and Clinical Baselines for the Random Forest Classifier
* **WHY:** Selecting 15 genera and evaluating ROC-AUC on the same dataset overestimates out-of-sample generalization and ignores whether microbiome features add predictive value beyond basic clinical variables.
* **WHAT:** Perform nested cross-validation and leave-one-hospital-out external validation, comparing the microbiome classifier against a clinical-only baseline (Age + Sex + BMI + Smoking).
* **HOW:** Train the Random Forest with internal feature selection on Hospitals 1 and 2 (Guangzhou) and evaluate both ROC-AUC and PR-AUC on Hospital 3 (University of South China), reporting DeLong's test comparing `Clinical + Microbiome` vs. `Clinical Only`.

#### 4. Fix Internal Contradictions and Numerical Errors Across Text and Tables
* **WHY:** Direct contradictions between the Abstract, Results, Discussion, and Table 1 undermine the reliability of the manuscript.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Enterotype count (lines 21, 146, 201):** Correct Abstract line 21 and Discussion line 201 from *"Four enterotypes"* to *"Three enterotypes"* to match Results line 146 and Figure 3C.
  * **Simpson index direction (lines 21, 124, 199):** Clarify in Methods line 71 and Discussion line 199 that QIIME 1 computes Simpson's dominance index ($D = \sum p_i^2$), where higher values indicate lower diversity/evenness, reconciling line 124 (*"Simpson index was significantly higher"*) with line 199.
  * **Disease group count (lines 59, 180):** Line 59 states *"classified into four major groups"* and then lists five numbered groups (1. Airway diseases, 2. Lung infections, 3. Respiratory failure, 4. NSCLC, 5. Other conditions); line 180 repeats this discrepancy (*"four distinct disease groups: airway diseases, lung infections, respiratory failure, NSCLC, and others"*). Change *"four"* to *"five"*.
  * **Table 1 gender percentage (line 98):** In Table 1, 252 males out of 374 patients is $252 / 374 = 67.4\%$, not $69.6\%$ (which corresponds to $252 / 362$, indicating 12 patients had missing gender data). State the exact denominator or correct the percentage and $\chi^2$ $P$-value.
  * **Method citation mismatch (line 78):** Line 78 cites reference `[18]` (Majorbio Cloud Platform, Ren et al.) for *"Enterotyping was conducted as described by Costea et al. [18],"* whereas Costea et al. is reference `[19]` (cited in line 146). Update line 78 to cite `[19]`.
