### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study develops and validates genome-wide polygenic scores (GPS) for five major common diseases—coronary artery disease (CAD), atrial fibrillation (AF), type 2 diabetes (T2D), inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and breast cancer (BC)—using summary statistics from large external European-ancestry discovery GWASs (which excluded UK Biobank participants) and an LD reference panel of 503 Europeans from 1000 Genomes Phase 3. For each disease, 31 candidate scores (24 pruning-and-thresholding scores across $p$-value and $r^2$ grids, plus 7 Bayesian `LDPred` scores across causal fraction parameters $\rho \in [0.001, 1.0]$) are tuned in the UK Biobank Phase 1 validation cohort ($N = 120,280$) and evaluated in the independent UK Biobank Phase 2 testing cohort ($N = 288,978$). In the testing cohort, the optimal GPSs identify 8.0%, 6.1%, 3.5%, 3.2%, and 1.5% of the population at $\ge 3$-fold increased odds (compared to the remainder of the population) for CAD, AF, T2D, IBD, and BC, respectively—identifying 20-fold more individuals at $\ge 3$-fold CAD risk than rare monogenic familial hypercholesterolemia mutations (~0.4% carrier prevalence). Score weights are publicly shared via the Broad CVDI repository.

The study is methodologically rigorous: it enforces strict separation between discovery GWAS, hyperparameter validation ($N = 120,280$), and out-of-sample testing ($N = 288,978$); demonstrates near-identical discriminative accuracy between validation and testing cohorts; verifies robustness after removing third-degree or closer relatives ($N = 222,529$); and candidly discusses the limitation of European-ancestry restriction. A few analytical and reporting refinements would further strengthen the manuscript: (1) Table 1 reports total model AUCs (e.g., 0.81 for CAD, 0.77 for AF) from logistic regressions that combine GPS with age and sex, which can mislead readers into attributing the high AUC primarily to the GPS unless the covariate-only baseline AUC and GPS-only AUC are reported directly alongside in Table 1; (2) the primary analysis pools prevalent (retrospective/baseline) and incident cases via logistic regression (reporting odds ratios against the "remainder of the population" rather than relative risks against the population median/average), which slightly inflates tail odds ratios for common outcomes and is susceptible to survival/ascertainment effects; and (3) minor numerical typos in Table 1 and Methods should be cleaned up.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Genome-wide polygenic scores (`LDPred` and pruning-and-thresholding) generalize with virtually zero overfitting from the UK Biobank Phase 1 validation set ($N = 120,280$) to the Phase 2 testing set ($N = 288,978$) (lines 29–34, 44–46, Table 1): [Well Supported].**  
  Because discovery GWAS summary statistics contained no UK Biobank overlap and only 31 candidate hyperparameter combinations were tuned on $N = 120,280$, the out-of-sample testing AUCs on $N = 288,978$ match the validation AUCs to within 0.01 across all five diseases (CAD: 0.81 vs. 0.81; AF: 0.77 vs. 0.77; T2D: 0.72 vs. 0.73; IBD: 0.63 vs. 0.63; BC: 0.68 vs. 0.69), and empirical risk across percentile bins closely tracks logistic-predicted risk (Figures 2–3, Supplementary Figs. 2–3).

* **The right tail of the GPS distribution identifies 8.0%, 6.1%, 3.5%, 3.2%, and 1.5% of the European-ancestry UK Biobank population at $\ge 3$-fold increased odds of CAD, AF, T2D, IBD, and breast cancer (lines 8, 56–151, Tables 2–3): [Well Supported].**  
  The tail odds ratios in Tables 2 and 3 are precisely estimated with narrow 95% confidence intervals (e.g., top 5% vs. remaining 95% for CAD: OR = 3.34, 95% CI: 3.12–3.58, $P = 6.5 \times 10^{-264}$) and remain unchanged after excluding cryptically related individuals down to third-degree relatives (Supplementary Table 10). One interpretive nuance worth emphasizing in the main text is that these odds ratios compare the top $X\%$ tail against the *remainder* of the population ($(100 - X)\%$, which includes the protective left tail) rather than against the middle quintile/median of the population, and that odds ratios slightly exceed relative risks as disease prevalence increases in the upper percentiles (e.g., 19.0% vs. 4.2% prevalence for breast cancer in the top 0.1%, line 151, where the prevalence ratio is $19.0 / 4.2 = 4.52$ vs. OR $\ge 5.0$).

* **High $\text{GPS}_{\text{CAD}}$ identifies 20-fold more individuals at $\ge 3$-fold risk than familial hypercholesterolemia monogenic mutations and is largely orthogonal to conventional clinical risk factors (lines 8, 56, 92): [Well Supported].**  
  Comparing the 8.0% prevalence of $\text{OR} \ge 3.0$ $\text{GPS}_{\text{CAD}}$ carriers to the ~0.4% population prevalence of familial hypercholesterolemia mutations (conferring ~3-fold CAD risk) directly supports the 20-fold ratio. Furthermore, Supplementary Table 8 (summarized in line 92) shows only modest enrichment of traditional clinical risk factors in the top 8% $\text{GPS}_{\text{CAD}}$ group (hypercholesterolemia 20% vs. 13%; hypertension 32% vs. 28%; family history 44% vs. 35%), confirming that standard clinical history alone does not capture most high-GPS individuals.

* **Reporting of overall covariate-adjusted AUCs (0.63–0.81) in Table 1 as the discriminative capacity of the GPS (lines 29–36, 46): [Moderately Supported].**  
  In Table 1 and line 46 ("The predictors had AUC ranging from 0.79 – 0.81 in the validation set... with AUC of 0.81"), the reported AUC is from a logistic regression model containing `GPS + age + sex + array + 4 PCs` (Table 1 footnote). For age- and sex-dependent diseases like CAD and AF, age and sex alone account for the majority of the discriminative AUC (typically ~0.75–0.76 for CAD in UK Biobank), whereas the GPS alone has a substantially lower marginal AUC (and incremental $\Delta\text{AUC}$ of ~0.04–0.05). While this is detailed in the Supplementary Tables (and Nagelkerke's partial pseudo-$R^2$ is computed in Supplementary Table 9, line 193), presenting only the full-model AUC in Table 1 without the covariate-only baseline AUC or GPS-only AUC can overstate the standalone discrimination of the genetic score to clinical readers.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report Covariate-Only Baseline AUCs and GPS-Only AUCs Alongside Full-Model AUCs in Table 1
* **WHY:** In Table 1, reporting `AUC = 0.81` for CAD and `0.77` for AF from the combined model (`GPS + age + sex + array + 4 PCs`) conflates demographic discrimination (age and sex) with genetic discrimination, particularly when comparing across breast cancer (restricted to females, so sex contributes 0 discrimination; AUC = 0.69) and IBD (weak age/sex gradient; AUC = 0.63).
* **WHAT:** Add columns or parenthetical values to Table 1 showing (i) the covariate-only AUC (`age + sex + array + 4 PCs`), (ii) the standalone `GPS-only` AUC, and (iii) the full-model AUC (`Covariates + GPS`).
* **HOW:** Extract the validation and testing AUCs for the covariate-only and GPS-only logistic models already computed for Supplementary Tables 1–6 and 9, and include them directly in Table 1 alongside the incremental $\Delta\text{AUC}$.

#### 2. Compare Tail Risk Against the Population Median Decile/Quintile and Separate Incident vs. Prevalent Cases
* **WHY:** Defining "carriers" as the top $X\%$ tail and comparing them to the remaining $(100 - X)\%$ includes the low-risk left tail in the reference group, which yields a slightly higher odds ratio than comparing against an average-risk individual (e.g., the 40th–60th percentile). Additionally, combining prevalent (pre-enrollment) and incident cases in cross-sectional logistic regression can be influenced by survivorship bias for fatal events (e.g., fatal MI prior to UK Biobank enrollment at age 40–69).
* **WHAT:** Provide a supplementary sensitivity table reporting (i) odds ratios using the middle quintile (40th–60th percentile of GPS) as the reference group, and (ii) Cox proportional hazards ratios restricted strictly to incident disease diagnoses occurring after UK Biobank baseline assessment.
* **HOW:** Fit Cox proportional hazards models (`Surv(time_to_event, incident_status) ~ GPS_tail + age + sex + array + PC1:PC4`) excluding participants with prevalent disease at baseline, and report hazard ratios alongside the cross-sectional odds ratios for both "vs. remainder" and "vs. middle quintile" reference definitions.
