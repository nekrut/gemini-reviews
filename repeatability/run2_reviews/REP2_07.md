### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript contrasts the selective regimes acting on 133,741 human SARS-CoV-2 genomes sampled during the first 11 months of the COVID-19 pandemic (December 2019 to October 2020) against historical selection across 69 bat and pangolin *Sarbecovirus* genomes partitioned into 20 recombination-free alignment segments. Using codon substitution models implemented in HyPhy (`SLAC`, `FEL`, `MEME`, `aBSREL`, and an extended `BUSTED[S]` model incorporating a 3-category hidden Markov model [HMM] for spatially autocorrelated synonymous rates and multinucleotide substitutions), alongside a base-composition-corrected Synonymous Dinucleotide Usage metric (`SDUc`), Ornstein–Uhlenbeck trait-shift detection (`PhylogeneticEM`), and local molecular clock dating (`BEAST` v1.10), the authors demonstrate that early human SARS-CoV-2 evolution was characterized by weak purifying selection ($d_N/d_S \approx 0.4$–$0.9$) and sparse episodic positive selection ($\sim 2\%$ of sites), whereas strong episodic diversifying selection and a lineage-specific adaptive shift toward CpG depletion occurred along the deep ancestral branches of the bat `nCoV` clade (diverging from `RmYN02` around 1976). Furthermore, recombination breakpoint analysis of `RmYN02` Spike confirms coinfection between low-CpG (`nCoV`) and high-CpG (`non-nCoV`) lineages within bat hosts. Analysis pipelines (`covid19.datamonkey.org`) and `BEAST` XML specifications are publicly provided.

This is a methodologically thorough and well-controlled evolutionary study. The authors systematically anticipate and neutralize the major artifacts that plague viral selection scans: they exclude terminal branches in human SARS-CoV-2 alignments to eliminate sequencing errors and intrahost dead-end mutations (S2 Text), partition *Sarbecovirus* alignments across recombination breakpoints, model spatially autocorrelated synonymous rate variation ($\text{CV} > 0.5$) and multinucleotide substitutions (`BUSTED[S]+MH`, showing that the apparent positive selection signal in `ORF1ab/regE` is explained by multi-nucleotide hits; S3 Table), and correct CpG usage (`SDUc`) for mononucleotide base composition bias. Only minor methodological refinements—specifically regarding the ultrametric tree transformation in `PhylogeneticEM` and the 3-frame $t$-test in Figure 4B—are needed.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Human SARS-CoV-2 genomes from December 2019 to October 2020 exhibit weak purifying selection ($d_N/d_S \approx 0.4$–$0.9$) and limited episodic diversifying selection ($\sim 2\%$ of sites in Spike and RdRp): [Well Supported].**  
  By stratifying `SLAC` $d_N/d_S$ estimates by variant frequency (Figure 1A) and restricting `FEL`/`MEME` tests to internal phylogeny branches (Figure 1C–E), the authors cleanly separate transient low-frequency deleterious mutations surfing the exponential expansion wave (79% of variants present in $\le 10$ genomes) from transmitted variants on internal branches. They also explicitly note that shallow tree depth ($\sim 0.6$ and $0.4$ substitutions/site in Spike and RdRp) limits statistical power during early pandemic spread.

* **Episodic positive selection in the `nCoV` *Sarbecovirus* clade acted primarily on deep ancestral branches rather than the terminal branch leading to SARS-CoV-2: [Well Supported].**  
  Across the 20 recombination-free segments, `aBSREL` detects positive selection exclusively on deep internal branches of the `nCoV` clade (and terminal pangolin branches), with zero significant tests on the terminal branch leading to human SARS-CoV-2 (Figure 2B). At the gene-segment level, `BUSTED[S]` with HMM-autocorrelated synonymous rates and multinucleotide substitutions confirms robust positive selection in `N` and `Spike`/`Orf1ab` segments while demonstrating that `ORF1ab/regE` loses significance when multiple-nucleotide substitutions are accommodated (S3 Table).

* **An adaptive shift toward CpG dinucleotide depletion (`SDUc`) and an elevated substitution rate occurred at the base of the `nCoV` clade in bats: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The `SDUc` metric properly controls for whole-genome single-nucleotide composition via codon simulation (Equation 1), and both `NRR1` and `NRR2` consistently place an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck shift toward stronger CpG suppression on the ancestral branch leading to the `nCoV` clade (Figure 3A–B, S6 Fig), accompanied by a higher branch substitution rate under a fixed local clock in `BEAST`. Two minor technical caveats apply: first, `PhylogeneticEM` required forcing the heterochronous MCC tree to be ultrametric by extending external tip branches; second, as the authors candidly report in S3 Text, likelihood/Bayes factor comparisons cannot distinguish a transient rate burst on the ancestral `nCoV` branch from a clade-wide clock acceleration.

* **Differential CpG content across the recombinant Spike gene of bat virus `RmYN02` proves historical coinfection between `nCoV` and `non-nCoV` lineages in bats: [Well Supported].**  
  Six of seven methods in `RDP5` identify the recombination breakpoints flanking the `non-nCoV` Spike segment of `RmYN02` (positions 21248–24058), and the 3-kb sliding window of relative dinucleotide abundance (Figure 4A) shows that `RmYN02` tracks Wuhan-Hu-1 (`nCoV` low CpG) across the entire genome except within the recombinant `non-nCoV` Spike segment, where CpG rises sharply to match `non-nCoV` bat viruses. (However, the parametric unpaired $t$-test in Figure 4B across $n = 3$ codon frame positions [$t_{2.07} = 3.03, p = 0.0450$] has only 2 degrees of freedom and treats frame positions within one gene segment as independent observations.)

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace the 3-Frame Unpaired $t$-Test in Figure 4B with a Codon-Bootstrap or Permutation Test
* **WHY:** In Figure 4B (Line 66), testing whether the absolute difference in `SDUc` between Wuhan-Hu-1 and `RmYN02` is larger in the `non-nCoV` Spike segment than in the `nCoV` Spike segment using an unpaired two-sample $t$-test across the $n = 3$ frame positions (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge`; $t_{2.07} = 3.03, p = 0.0450$) relies on $n = 3$ summary points per region that are not independent trials.
* **WHAT:** Compute a block-bootstrap or codon-permutation $p$-value directly from the underlying codon alignments of the `non-nCoV` (positions 21248–24058) and `nCoV` Spike segments.
* **HOW:** Resample codons (and adjacent codon pairs for the `bridge` position) 10,000 times with replacement within the `non-nCoV` and `nCoV` Spike regions of Wuhan-Hu-1 and `RmYN02` to obtain the empirical 95% confidence interval and two-sided permutation $p$-value for $\Delta |\text{SDUc}_{\text{Wuhan}} - \text{SDUc}_{\text{RmYN02}}|$ between the two regions.

#### 2. Verify the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck CpG Shift Without Artificial Terminal Branch Extension
* **WHY:** In the Methods ("Identifying shifts in CpG content"), the heterochronous time-scaled `BEAST` MCC trees for `NRR1` and `NRR2` were forced to be ultrametric by extending external tip branches to match the most recently sampled tip so that `PhylogeneticEM`'s scalar OU algorithm could be applied. Extending terminal branches distorts the expected OU variance–covariance matrix for tips sampled decades apart or across branches with different local clock rates.
* **WHAT:** Confirm that the ancestral `nCoV` CpG shift holds on the un-stretched, non-ultrametric genetic distance (or time-calibrated) tree using a non-ultrametric OU/Brownian implementation.
* **HOW:** Run `l1ou` or `phylolm(..., model = "OUfixedRoot")` (or compare AICc of a 2-optimum OU model `OUwie` with a regime shift on the `nCoV` stem branch vs. single-optimum OU/BM directly on the un-modified `NRR1` and `NRR2` phylogenies) to confirm that the shift placement does not depend on terminal branch extension.

#### 3. Report Multiple-Testing Correction (FDR) Across the 20 Nonrecombinant Segments for `BUSTED[S]` and `aBSREL`
* **WHY:** In the *Sarbecovirus* selection analysis (Figure 2, S3 Table), `BUSTED[S]` and `aBSREL` are applied across 20 nonrecombinant segments using an unadjusted threshold of $p \le 0.05$.
* **WHAT:** Report Holm–Bonferroni or Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted $q$-values alongside the raw `BUSTED[S]` and `BUSTED[S]+MH` $p$-values across the 20 segments.
* **HOW:** Add a column of BH-adjusted $q$-values to `S3 Table` and indicate in Figure 2A–B which segments and branches remain significant at $\text{FDR} \le 0.05$.
