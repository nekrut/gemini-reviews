### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study performs a comparative pangenome and phylogenomic analysis of *Enterococcus faecium* and its closely related sister species *Enterococcus lactis* to resolve their taxonomic boundaries, metabolic capabilities, and pathogenic differentiation. Starting from 2,838 GenBank assemblies (2,727 *E. faecium* and 111 *E. lactis*), the authors filter for CheckM completeness $\ge 90\%$ and contamination $\le 10\%$ (2,825 genomes) and dereplicate them at 99.5% nucleotide identity across 92 core housekeeping genes (`UBCG2`), yielding 192 representative genomes (128 *E. faecium* and 64 *E. lactis*). Using average nucleotide identity (ANI), core-gene maximum-likelihood phylogenetics, Roary/PANINI accessory-gene clustering, KEGG metabolic reconstruction, and BLAST comparisons against CARD, VFDB, BACTIBASE/BAGEL4, and mobileOG-db, they demonstrate that: (1) 16S rRNA sequences and core carbon metabolic pathways cannot distinguish *E. faecium* from *E. lactis*, explaining widespread GenBank misclassification of *E. lactis* isolates as *E. faecium*; and (2) *E. faecium* splits into multi-host subclade I and human-restricted subclade II, with subclade II exhibiting significantly larger genomes ($2.98 \pm 0.20$ Mb vs. $2.67 \pm 0.18$ Mb), lower GC content ($37.82\%$ vs. $38.03\%$), and marked enrichment of mobile genetic elements (`tra`, `mob`), bacteriocins, biofilm adhesins (`esp`, `fss3`, `sgrA`), and horizontally transferred antibiotic resistance genes (`vanA`, `tet(M)`, `ermB`, `aacA-aphD`, `dfrF/G`). All GenBank accessions are listed in Table S1.

This is a useful and well-structured comparative genomics study that clarifies how taxonomic conflation of *E. lactis* (historically "Clade B" non-pathogenic *E. faecium*) with genuine *E. faecium* distorted earlier clinical vs. commensal comparisons. However, several evolutionary interpretations, statistical choices, and gene-annotation errors require correction: (1) in the Discussion, the authors repeatedly assert that *E. faecium* "evolved from *E. lactis*" and lost *E. lactis* genes during that transition, which misinterprets sister-clade monophyly on a rooted tree; (2) group comparisons of skewed gene counts across clades (Figs. 2 and 5) rely on unadjusted parametric Student's $t$-tests without accounting for phylogenetic non-independence or multiple testing; and (3) several bacterial resistance genes in Figure 6 (`fosB`, `vatE`, `fexB`) are misannotated with eukaryotic human/yeast gene descriptions (e.g., labeling bacterial fosfomycin resistance protein FosB as "transcription factor AP-1 subunit" and streptogramin acetyltransferase VatE as "vacuolar H+-ATPase E subunit"). Addressing these issues requires only computational re-analysis and textual revision.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Whole-genome ANI, 92 core housekeeping genes, and accessory pangenome profiles cleanly separate *E. faecium* and *E. lactis*, whereas 16S rRNA phylogeny and core carbohydrate metabolism do not: [Well Supported].**  
  Across the 192 dereplicated genomes, within-clade ANI values exceed 98.3% (*E. faecium* vs. NCTC 7171$^T$) and 97.8% (*E. lactis* vs. KCTC 21015$^T$), well above the 95%–96% species demarcation threshold, while 16S rRNA phylogeny fails to resolve the two species into monophyletic clades (Fig. S2). This convincingly explains why many GenBank genomes deposited as commensal/food *E. faecium* are actually misidentified *E. lactis* strains.

* ***E. faecium* subclade II is restricted to human clinical/hospital sources and is enriched for mobile elements, antibiotic resistance genes, biofilm adhesins, and larger, lower-GC genomes compared to subclade I and *E. lactis*: [Well Supported].**  
  Both core-gene phylogeny (Fig. 1) and PANINI accessory-gene ordination (Fig. 3) separate human-restricted subclade II from multi-habitat subclade I. Subclade II genomes carry ~300 more genes on average ($2,817 \pm 200$ vs. $2,518 \pm 170$), significantly lower GC content ($37.82\%$ vs. $38.03\%$), and high prevalence of conjugative transfer genes (`tra`, `mob`), glycopeptide/macrolide/tetracycline/aminoglycoside resistance operons (`vanA`, `ermB`, `tet(M)`, `aacA-aphD`), and surface adhesins (`esp`, `fss3`), supported by PERMANOVA ($R^2 = 0.5525, P = 0.001$; Fig. 7) and incongruent single-gene phylogenies (Fig. 8).

* ***E. faecium* evolved from *E. lactis* and lost *E. lactis*-associated genes (`hylB`, `ebpA`) during divergence: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Discussion (line 153), the authors state that *"Our phylogenetic analyses suggest E. faecium, primarily from humans and animals, may have evolved from E. lactis, found in various habitats, including fermented foods (Fig. 1)"* and in line 157 refer to *"potential gene loss during the evolutionary divergence from E. lactis to E. faecium."* In Figure 1 (rooted with *E. hirae* ATCC 9790$^T$), *E. faecium* and *E. lactis* form two reciprocally monophyletic sister clades sharing a common ancestor node. An extant monophyletic sister clade (*E. faecium*) cannot evolve from its extant sister clade (*E. lactis*); both lineages diverged symmetrically from their common ancestor, and the presence of `hylB` in *E. lactis* vs. absence in *E. faecium* is equally parsimonious with gain on the *E. lactis* stem branch or loss on the *E. faecium* stem branch unless polarized against outgroups (*E. hirae*, *E. durans*, *E. mundtii*).

* **Metabolic capabilities for D-sorbitol, glycerol, and D-xylose differentiate *E. faecium* from *E. lactis*: [Moderately Supported].**  
  KEGG pathway reconstruction supplemented by manual BLASTP verification (Fig. S4, lines 75–76) indicates that most *E. faecium* genomes encode D-sorbitol and glycerol utilization pathways whereas most *E. lactis* genomes encode D-xylose utilization. However, exact counts/proportions of genomes carrying intact operons (vs. pseudogenes/frameshifts) in subclade I, subclade II, and *E. lactis* are not tabulated with statistical tests.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct the Evolutionary Directionality Claims Using Outgroup Ancestral State Reconstruction
* **WHY:** Claiming that *E. faecium* evolved from *E. lactis* misreads a rooted sister-group phylogeny, and claiming that `hylB` and `ebpA` were lost in *E. faecium* requires outgroup polarization.
* **WHAT:** Reframe the Discussion to state that *E. faecium* and *E. lactis* diverged from a common ancestor, and check the presence/absence of `hylB`, `ebpA`, and carbohydrate utilization operons in the outgroup *E. hirae* ATCC 9790$^T$ (and related species *E. durans* / *E. mundtii*) to polarize gene gain versus loss.
* **HOW:** Run the same VFDB and KEGG BLAST searches against the outgroup genome *E. hirae* ATCC 9790 (`CP003504`), report which states are ancestral vs. derived at the *E. faecium*–*E. lactis* split, and revise lines 153–157 accordingly.

#### 2. Replace Unadjusted Student's $t$-Tests on Skewed Gene Counts with Nonparametric and Phylogenetically Informed Tests
* **WHY:** Counts of mobile elements, bacteriocins, and antibiotic resistance genes per genome (Fig. 5) are discrete, bounded at zero, and strongly right-skewed (especially in *E. lactis* where many strains have near-zero acquired AMR genes), violating normality and homoscedasticity assumptions of Student's $t$-test. In addition, pairwise comparisons across 4 groups (`EF`, `EF-I`, `EF-II`, `EL`) require multiple-testing correction, and comparing `EF` (which is the union of `EF-I` and `EF-II`) against `EF-I` and `EF-II` in the same plot (Figs. 2 and 5) tests overlapping non-independent sets.
* **WHAT:** Remove the redundant pooled `EF` comparisons against its own subsets (`EF-I`, `EF-II`), and test differences across the three mutually exclusive clades (`EF-I`, `EF-II`, `EL`) using Kruskal–Wallis followed by Dunn's post-hoc test (with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment) as well as phylogenetic generalized least squares (`phylolm` / `PGLS`) along the 92-gene tree.
* **HOW:** In R, run `kruskal.test()` and `dunnTest()` across `EF-I`, `EF-II`, and `EL` for genome size, gene count, GC%, AMR count, VF count, bacteriocin count, and MGE count, and update the significance asterisks in Figures 2 and 5.

#### 3. Fix Eukaryotic Gene Symbol Misannotations in Figure 6 and Technical Inconsistencies
* **WHY:** Automated gene-symbol lookup caused multiple bacterial antibiotic resistance genes in Figure 6's caption (line 121) to be defined as unrelated mammalian/eukaryotic proteins, which is biologically incorrect and confusing to microbiology readers.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Figure 6 caption (line 121) — Incorrect gene product descriptions:**
    * Change `fosB, transcription factor AP-1 subunit` to `fosB, fosfomycin resistance bacillithiol transferase`.
    * Change `vatE, vacuolar H+-ATPase E subunit` to `vatE, streptogramin A O-acetyltransferase`.
    * Change `fexB, aerobic respiration control sensor protein` to `fexB, florfenicol/chloramphenicol efflux MFS transporter`.
    * Change `aad(6), putative aryl-alcohol dehydrogenase` to `aad(6), aminoglycoside 6-adenylyltransferase`.
    * Change `mel, α-galactosidase` to `mel (msrD), macrolide efflux genetic assembly protein`.
  * **Date discrepancy between Results and Methods (line 47 vs. line 170):** Line 47 states genomes were retrieved from GenBank *"as of January 2023"*, whereas line 170 states *"As of February 2023"*. Reconcile to the exact retrieval date.
  * **Typo (line 132):** Fix `PREMANOVA` to `PERMANOVA`.
