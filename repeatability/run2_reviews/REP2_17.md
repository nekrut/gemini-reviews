### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study integrates three single-cell RNA-seq datasets (`GSE150938`, `GSE189357`, and `HRA001130`) spanning lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) progression stages from atypical adenomatous hyperplasia (AAH), adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), and minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA) to invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC). Using `inferCNV`, `Monocle2`, `CytoTRACE`, `scMetabolism`, and `GSVA`, the authors identify nine malignant epithelial cell clusters and highlight Clusters 0 and 6 as increasing from AAH to IAC and correlating with worse survival in TCGA-LUAD. They then train 10 machine learning survival algorithms on marker genes from Clusters 0 and 6 in TCGA-LUAD, select a `CoxBoost + plsRcox` composite prognostic model (CPM) based on its average C-index across six GEO validation datasets (`GSE13213`, `GSE26939`, `GSE29016`, `GSE30219`, `GSE31210`, `GSE42127`), evaluate survival stratification in the OAK/POPLAR trial cohorts, and perform in vitro siRNA knockdown of `S100A16` in A549 and H1299 cells using colony formation, wound-healing, and transwell assays. Public database accessions are listed, but no analysis scripts, processed single-cell metadata, or model gene weights are provided.

Despite combining multi-cohort transcriptomics with basic in vitro assays, the manuscript suffers from severe methodological flaws, validation data leakage, and missing model specifications that invalidate its central biomarker claims. First, the authors explicitly select their "optimal" machine learning model (`CoxBoost + plsRcox`) by maximizing the average C-index across the six GEO validation cohorts (Section 3.5, line 137) and then benchmark that same model against 144 published signatures on those exact same six cohorts (Section 3.6, Fig. 7)—a textbook test-set selection leakage that guarantees upward-biased validation performance. Second, the manuscript never reports the actual constituent genes or regression weights of the CPM model anywhere in the text, tables, or figures, rendering the biomarker non-reproducible and unverifiable. Third, the preprocessing pipeline claims to convert six GEO microarray datasets (`GSE13213`, `GSE31210`, etc.) into "transcripts per million (TPM)" and apply `ComBat` across datasets prior to modeling, which is mathematically inappropriate for microarray intensities and leaks cross-cohort moments. Fourth, stratifying overall survival in the OAK/POPLAR cohorts without testing treatment-arm interaction does not establish prediction of "immunotherapy responsiveness." Re-analysis with strict training/test isolation, full disclosure of model equations, and proper patient-level mixed modeling of single-cell stage proportions is required.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The `CoxBoost + plsRcox` Composite Prognostic Model (CPM) is an optimal prognostic biomarker that outperforms 144 published LUAD signatures across six independent validation cohorts: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 3.5 (line 137), the authors state: *"The TCGA dataset was employed as the training cohort, with six GEO datasets utilized for validation purposes. The criterion for model selection was based on the average c-index across the six validation cohorts."* Using the six external validation cohorts to select the winning algorithm combination among ~100 machine learning models violates the fundamental rule of out-of-sample validation. Once the six GEO cohorts are used to pick the winning model (`CoxBoost + plsRcox`), they are part of the model-selection loop; subsequently claiming in Section 3.6 (Fig. 7) that CPM outperforms 144 fixed published signatures across those same six cohorts is circular because the competing 144 models were not selected to maximize C-index on those six datasets. Worse, the manuscript never discloses the list of genes or coefficient weights in the CPM formula.

* **The CPM model predicts immunotherapy responsiveness and chemotherapy drug sensitivity in LUAD patients: [Overstated].**  
  The Abstract claims that CPM "predicted genomic alterations, chemotherapy drug sensitivity, and immunotherapy responsiveness." However, the Results section contains no chemotherapy drug sensitivity results at all, and the immunotherapy analysis (Section 3.5, Fig. 6I) consists solely of a Kaplan–Meier survival curve in pooled OAK/POPLAR patients stratified by high vs. low CPM score. Because Cluster 6 is heavily enriched for general proliferation, cell cycle, DNA repair, and MYC target genes (Fig. 3A), high proliferation scores predict shorter overall survival in almost any cancer cohort regardless of treatment. Demonstrating prediction of *immunotherapy responsiveness* requires showing a significant biomarker-by-treatment interaction (atezolizumab vs. docetaxel hazard ratio interaction) or differential objective response rate (CR/PR vs. PD), neither of which is tested.

* **Tumour cell Clusters 0 and 6 represent early developmental, high-stemness states whose abundance progressively increases from pre-invasive AAH/AIS/MIA to invasive IAC: [Fragile].**  
  First, there is an internal contradiction between Section 3.2 and Section 3.4: in Section 3.2 (line 104, Fig. 3C), the authors state that *"Clusters 0 and 6 localize to the nascent stages of development, with a subsequent diminution in prevalence over time,"* whereas in Section 3.4 (line 126, Fig. 5A–B) they state that Clusters 0 and 6 *increase* in abundance from early pre-invasive stages (AAH) to advanced invasive stages (IAC). Second, `CytoTRACE` measures total expressed gene counts/transcriptional diversity, which is strongly confounded by cell-cycle phase (proliferating S/G2/M cells in Cluster 6 express more genes and automatically receive high CytoTRACE scores without being developmental stem cells). Third, pooling cells across patients in Fig. 5A–B without reporting patient-level distributions or testing stage differences with patient-level mixed-effects models treats thousands of cells from the same tumour specimen as independent observations (pseudoreplication).

* **Knockdown of `S100A16` inhibits proliferation, migration, invasion, and "DNA replication capabilities" of A549 and H1299 cells: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While colony formation, scratch wound-healing, and Matrigel transwell assays show reduced colony counts and migration/invasion upon `si-S100A16` transfection (Fig. 9), the text on line 170 and line 187 claims that `S100A16` knockdown inhibited *"DNA replication capabilities"*—yet no DNA replication assay (such as EdU, BrdU, or PI cell-cycle flow cytometry) was performed anywhere in the study. Moreover, the knockdown experiments use only a single siRNA sequence against `S100A16` without a second independent siRNA duplex to exclude off-target seed effects, and neither the number of biological replicates ($n$) nor exact $p$-values are reported in Figure 9.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Disclose the Complete CPM Model Formula and Eliminate Validation-Set Selection Leakage
* **WHY:** A prognostic model whose genes and coefficients are omitted from the paper cannot be evaluated or applied by anyone, and selecting the best model via average C-index on the validation cohorts invalidates out-of-sample benchmarking.
* **WHAT:** Publish the complete table of CPM genes, univariate Cox hazard ratios, and `CoxBoost + plsRcox` coefficients, and select the optimal model strictly via 10-fold cross-validation within the TCGA training cohort before evaluating on the six held-out GEO datasets.
* **HOW:** Re-run the 10-algorithm pipeline where the winning algorithm is chosen by highest mean 10-fold CV C-index inside `TCGA-LUAD` alone, lock its coefficients, and then compute out-of-sample C-indices and 1/3/5-year time-dependent ROC AUCs across the six GEO cohorts.

#### 2. Correct the Cross-Platform Microarray Preprocessing Pipeline
* **WHY:** Section 2.1 (line 29) states that all TCGA and GEO datasets (`GSE13213`, `GSE26939`, `GSE29016`, `GSE30219`, `GSE31210`, `GSE42127`) were converted into "transcripts per million (TPM)" and batch-corrected with `ComBat`. All six GEO validation cohorts are microarray datasets (Agilent, Affymetrix, and Illumina BeadChip), for which TPM (an RNA-seq length-normalized count metric) is undefined. Furthermore, applying `ComBat` jointly across training and validation sets causes data leakage.
* **WHAT:** Process each microarray platform using its standard within-cohort log2-RMA/loess normalization and apply within-cohort Z-score standardization per gene rather than converting microarrays to TPM or running joint `ComBat`.
* **HOW:** Normalize each GEO dataset independently on its native log2 intensity scale, standardize predictor genes to mean 0 and variance 1 within each cohort, and verify that all reported C-indices in Figures 6 and 7 remain valid without cross-cohort `ComBat`.

#### 3. Perform Patient-Level Statistical Testing for Single-Cell Stage Proportions
* **WHY:** Aggregating all cells per histopathological stage (AAH, AIS, MIA, IAC) in Figure 5A–B ignores inter-patient heterogeneity and sample-size imbalance across lesions.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact number of patients and cells per stage (AAH, AIS, MIA, IAC) across `GSE150938`, `GSE189357`, and `HRA001130`, and test whether Cluster 0 and Cluster 6 fractions increase across stages at the *patient* level.
* **HOW:** Compute the proportion of Cluster 0 and Cluster 6 among malignant epithelial cells for each individual patient sample, plot patient-level boxplots across AAH, AIS, MIA, and IAC, and test for trend using a Jonckheere–Terpstra or Kruskal–Wallis test with patient $N$ as the unit of analysis.

#### 4. Test Treatment-Arm Interaction in OAK/POPLAR and Remove Unperformed Assay Claims
* **WHY:** Showing that CPM stratifies overall survival in OAK/POPLAR does not show that it predicts immunotherapy response over chemotherapy, and claiming inhibition of "DNA replication" and prediction of "chemotherapy drug sensitivity" without corresponding data misrepresents the experiments performed.
* **WHAT:** Compare CPM survival stratification separately in the atezolizumab arm vs. docetaxel arm of OAK/POPLAR (and compare objective response rates CR/PR vs. SD/PD), and remove unsubstantiated claims regarding DNA replication assays and chemotherapy drug sensitivity from the Abstract and Results.
* **HOW:** Fit a Cox proportional hazards model `Surv(OS_time, OS_event) ~ CPM_score * Treatment_Arm` in the OAK/POPLAR dataset and report the interaction $p$-value and responder/non-responder distributions; in Section 3.8 and Discussion, replace "DNA replication capabilities" with "clonogenic survival / colony formation."
