### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the emergence, reassortment, and spread of fifth-wave highly pathogenic (HP) and low pathogenic (LP) avian influenza A/H7N9 in Guangdong province, China, between January 2016 and August 2017. Drawing on 34,106 environmental and poultry swabs from sentinel live poultry markets (LPMs) across 21 prefecture-level cities (yielding 7,008 influenza A-positive and 1,253 H7N9-positive swabs, including 251 HP H7N9 and 1,002 LP H7N9), the authors isolated and sequenced 58 HP A/H7N9, 19 LP A/H7N9, and 220 A/H9N2 complete genomes on the Ion PGM platform after passage in embryonated chicken eggs. Combining these sequences with 1,371 publicly available Chinese H7N9 genomes (and 6,950 H9N2 segment sequences) plus Ministry of Agriculture live poultry import records, they classify 180 eight-segment genotypes (`G1`–`G180`), trace the origin of fifth-wave HP A/H7N9 (clade C-1) to third/fourth-wave Yangtze River Delta (YRD) lineage viruses, document extensive internal-gene reassortment with imported YRD-LP and local H9N2 viruses, and compare HA cleavage-site variants (`V1`–`V7`) and mammalian adaptation markers between LPM and human isolates. Sequence accession numbers are provided in GISAID (`Supplementary Table S1`).

The longitudinal LPM surveillance dataset (34,106 swabs across 21 cities) and the 297 newly sequenced avian influenza genomes represent a valuable empirical resource for tracking the fifth-wave LP-to-HP H7N9 transition. However, several methodological and technical issues weaken the quantitative and biological conclusions: (1) preferential inoculation and sequencing of HP over LP swabs distorts the reported lineage prevalence in Guangdong (claiming 64% HP YRD vs. 36% LP in the Abstract, when rRT-PCR surveillance in Table 1 shows LP H7N9 outnumbered HP H7N9 by 4-to-1); (2) blind passage of all environmental/poultry isolates for 2–3 generations in embryonated chicken eggs prior to sequencing introduces host-adaptation selection bias when comparing amino acid polymorphisms (`PB2 E627K/D701N`, `HA Q226L`, `NA R292K`) against human clinical sequences (Table 2); (3) the discrete BEAST phylogeographic model described in Methods (Section 2.5) is never quantitatively reported in the Results and uses a different geographic regionalization than the poultry-trade data (Section 2.4); and (4) Table 2 contains impossible percentages exceeding 100% (`PB2 Q591K: 46 (104.5%)`). All of these issues can be addressed through statistical re-analysis and table corrections.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **HP A/H7N9 (clade C-1) evolved from third/fourth-wave YRD-lineage LP A/H7N9 viruses and diversified into 19 HP genotypes (`G108` dominant) via reassortment of internal segments with imported YRD-LP, local PRD-LP, and H9N2 viruses: [Well Supported].**  
  Maximum-likelihood (`IQ-TREE`, GTR+F+I+G4) and time-scaled Bayesian (`BEAST` v1.8) phylogenies across all eight segments (Figures 3 and 4, Supplementary Figures S1–S3) clearly place fifth-wave HP A/H7N9 HA within a monophyletic clade (`C-1`) rooted among third- and fourth-wave eastern China and Guangdong YRD-lineage viruses, while incongruent topologies across `PB2`, `PB1`, `PA`, `NP`, `M`, and `NS` confirm ongoing reassortment with co-circulating Clade 1/2/3 internal gene pools.

* **During the fifth wave in Guangdong, co-circulating A/H7N9 lineages comprised 64% HP YRD, 23% newly imported LP YRD, and 13% local LP PRD: [Overstated].**  
  These percentages ($64\%$, $23\%$, $13\%$; Abstract and Section 3.4) are calculated solely from the small subset of **sequenced isolates** ($58$ HP and $19$ LP from this study plus public Guangdong sequences), which suffered from severe sampling and inoculation ascertainment bias. As shown in Table 1 and Section 2.2, during the fifth wave (November 2016–August 2017), rRT-PCR surveillance detected **251 HP H7N9 (24.4%)** and **777 LP H7N9 (75.6%)** positive swabs ($1,028$ total H7N9+ swabs). However, the authors inoculated all $251$ HP swabs ($100\%$, sequencing 58) but only $70$ of $1,002$ LP swabs ($7.0\%$, sequencing 19). Presenting the sequenced-isolate ratio ($64\%$ HP) as the true epidemiological lineage distribution in Guangdong reverses the actual 3:1 predominance of LP over HP H7N9 in LPMs.

* **The `V1` (`PKRKRTAR↓G`) HA cleavage-site motif facilitates human infection compared to `V3` (`PKGKRTAR↓G`) (`93.7%` [45/48] in humans vs. `49.6%` [57/115] in LPMs): [Fragile].**  
  While the raw proportion of `V1` vs. `V3` differs between human cases ($45/48$ `V1` vs. $1/48$ `V3`) and LPM isolates ($57/115$ `V1` vs. $50/115$ `V3`), Figure 5 shows strong geographic confounding: `V3` was spatially restricted to central and eastern Guangdong, whereas `V1` circulated broadly across northern, western, central, and eastern Guangdong where human cases were sampled. No spatially stratified Fisher's exact test or mixed-effects logistic regression controlling for prefecture-level city and sampling month is performed to rule out geographic exposure confounding.

* **Human-adaptive and drug-resistance substitutions (`HA Q226L/H`, `NA R292K`, `PB2 E627K/D701N`) are absent in environmental LPM HP A/H7N9 isolates (`0%` vs. `4.2–34.8%` in humans, Table 2): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While `PB2 E627K`/`D701N` (mammalian polymerase adaptation) and `NA R292K` (oseltamivir resistance) are expected to arise *de novo* within infected and treated human patients, all 58 environmental HP A/H7N9 viruses in this study were passaged for **2 to 3 generations in 9- to 10-day-old embryonated chicken eggs** prior to sequencing (Section 2.2). Multi-generation passage in avian eggs exerts strong purifying/reversion selection against mammalian-adapted variants (`PB2 627K`, `HA 226L`) present at low or moderate frequencies in original LPM swabs.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct Lineage Prevalence Estimates for Differential Inoculation/Sequencing Ascertainment
* **WHY:** Inoculating $100\%$ ($251/251$) of HP H7N9 PCR-positive swabs but only $7\%$ ($70/1,002$) of LP H7N9 PCR-positive swabs makes the unweighted sequenced sample ($58$ HP vs. $19$ LP) unrepresentative of the true LPM viral population.
* **WHAT:** Report inverse-sampling-probability weighted estimates of lineage prevalence alongside the raw sequenced counts in the Abstract and Section 3.4.
* **HOW:** Multiply the PRD-LP ($13\% / 36\% = 36.1\%$ of LP sequences) and YRD-LP ($23\% / 36\% = 63.9\%$ of LP sequences) fractions by the fifth-wave rRT-PCR LP prevalence ($777 / 1,028 = 75.6\%$) and the HP fraction by the rRT-PCR HP prevalence ($251 / 1,028 = 24.4\%$) to report both the sequenced-isolate proportions and the surveillance-adjusted LPM proportions ($\sim 24.4\%$ HP-YRD, $\sim 48.3\%$ LP-YRD, $\sim 27.3\%$ LP-PRD).

#### 2. Test the `V1` vs. `V3` Cleavage-Site Association Controlling for City and Month
* **WHY:** The enrichment of `V1` in human cases ($45/48$) relative to LPMs ($57/115$) could be driven by geographic heterogeneity if human cases were disproportionately sampled in cities where `V1` predominated (Figure 5).
* **WHAT:** Perform a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test stratified by prefecture-level city (and collection month) to test whether `V1` remains significantly enriched in human cases over co-circulating local LPM isolates.
* **HOW:** Using the metadata for the 48 human and 115 environmental HP A/H7N9 sequences in Guangdong, construct $2 \times 2$ tables (`Human` vs. `LPM` $\times$ `V1` vs. `non-V1`) within each prefecture-level city shown in Figure 5 and report the city-stratified Mantel–Haenszel odds ratio and $p$-value.

#### 3. Harmonize Regional Definitions and Report Quantitative Phylogeographic Support
* **WHY:** Section 2.4 divides China into 6 regions (putting Henan in Central and Fujian in Eastern), whereas Section 2.5 divides China into 7 regions (moving Henan to Northern, Fujian to a separate Southeastern region, and Gansu from Northwestern to Southwestern), and no quantitative BEAST discrete-trait results (Bayes factors, Markov jump counts, or posterior root state probabilities) are reported in the Results.
* **WHAT:** Harmonize the geographic partitioning between the poultry-trade data and the BEAST discrete phylogeographic model, and report the posterior transition support values.
* **HOW:** Re-aggregate the Ministry of Agriculture poultry import table (`Supplementary Table S2`) to match the exact 7 phylogeographic regions used in BEAST (or vice versa), and report the posterior probability of the clade C-1 ancestral location state and Bayes factors for viral migration into and out of Guangdong.

#### 4. Fix Arithmetic and Percentage Errors in Table 1, Table 2, and Main Text
* **WHY:** Impossible percentages exceeding $100\%$ in Table 2 and mismatched ratios in the text detract from data credibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Table 2 (`PB2 Q591K`, Line 192):** Fix `46 (104.5)` under Human HPAI H7N9 `V1` (the total number of human `V1` PB2 sequences is $n = 44$ per Footnote `b`, whereas $n = 46$ is the `Total` human PB2 count; `46` was mistakenly copied into the `V1` column and divided by $44$ to give $104.5\%$).
  2. **Table 2 (`NA E119V`, Lines 172–173):** Under Human HPAI H7N9 `V1` ($n = 45$), `E` is listed as `45 (100)` and `V` is listed as `1 (2.2)`, which sums to $46/45$ ($102.2\%$). Change `E` in `V1` from `45 (100)` to `44 (97.8)`.
  3. **Section 3.1 (Line 60):** Clarify "Approximately 25 per cent of all A/H7N9-positive swabs were positive for HP A/H7 (Table 1)": across the entire Jan 2016–Aug 2017 table, $251 / 1,253 = 20.03\%$, whereas during the fifth wave (Nov 2016–Aug 2017), $251 / 1,028 = 24.42\%$. State explicitly that the ~25% figure refers to the Nov 2016–Aug 2017 period.
  4. **Section 4 Discussion (Line 217):** Correct the statement "Mutations associated with host transmission (avian to human) were also found in the PB2 gene (12%)" or specify which PB2 mutation in Table 2 corresponds to $12\%$ (in Table 2, environmental PB2 K702R is $7/58 = 12.1\%$).
  5. **Editorial fixes:** Correct "fifty-eight and nineteenwere" (Line 30), "twennty-nine" (Line 40), "ffifty-nine" (Line 133), and "Data availiablility / Authors contributors" (Lines 227, 232).
