### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This prospective cohort study utilizes data from 271,068 UK Biobank participants (aged 38–73 years at recruitment, after excluding 584 with prior aortic aneurysm/dissection, 46,719 missing baseline blood markers, and 183,996 with baseline neoplasms, hematologic, autoimmune, or chronic inflammatory diseases) followed for a median of 13.89 years (629 incident abdominal aortic aneurysm [AAA] events). Using stepwise multivariable Cox proportional hazards models, Fine–Gray competing-risk models (accounting for death), restricted cubic splines, and subgroup analyses stratified by a 31-SNP AAA polygenic risk score (PRS) and clinical risk factors, the authors evaluate nine baseline peripheral blood inflammatory markers: C-reactive protein (CRP), lymphocyte, monocyte, neutrophil, and platelet counts, and four derived ratios (`NLR`, `PLR`, `LMR`, `SII`). They report that higher baseline log-transformed CRP ($\text{HR} = 1.46$ per 1-SD), neutrophils ($\text{HR} = 1.27$), monocytes ($\text{HR} = 1.27$), and lymphocytes ($\text{HR} = 1.17$) and lower platelets ($\text{HR} = 0.90$) and `PLR` ($\text{HR} = 0.82$) independently predict incident AAA, with CRP acting additively across PRS tertiles ($\text{HR} = 5.3$ in highest CRP quartile + highest PRS tertile) and doubling the 14-year cumulative incidence (from $4.78\%$ to $10.05\%$) when combined with PRS among male current smokers aged $\ge 65$ years.

The study leverages a large prospective cohort, a well-motivated exclusion of baseline inflammatory comorbidities, and appropriate Fine–Gray competing-risk sensitivity analyses. However, several statistical, confounding, and numerical flaws require correction: (1) **unadjusted confounding by statin/lipid-lowering therapy, BMI, and diabetes**, evidenced by the paradoxical finding in Table 1 that incident AAA cases had significantly *lower* baseline total cholesterol ($5.38$ vs. $5.72$ mmol/L, $P < 0.001$)—a classic signature of baseline statin use in older male smokers with cardiovascular risk, where statins directly lower both cholesterol and serum CRP; (2) **mathematically impossible $P$-values in Table 1** (`PLR` $140.62 \pm 55.10$ vs. $125.64 \pm 51.59$ is reported as $P = 0.559$ when the two-sample $t$-test yields $t = 6.82, P \approx 9 \times 10^{-12}$; `LMR` $4.65 \pm 3.10$ vs. $4.02 \pm 2.49$ is reported as $P = 0.096$ when $t = 5.09, P \approx 3.6 \times 10^{-7}$); (3) **internal sample-size contradictions** between the text ($271,068$), Table 1 column headers ($270,440 + 629 = 271,069$), Table 1 rows ($270,439$), and Table 2 ($270,439$, which omits the 629 AAA events from the stated total); and (4) unadjusted multiple testing across 45 subgroup interaction tests and inappropriate description of "linear interpolation" for missing cross-sectional covariate imputation.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Higher baseline serum CRP ($\text{HR} = 1.46$ per SD) and leukocyte counts (neutrophils $\text{HR} = 1.27$, monocytes $\text{HR} = 1.27$, lymphocytes $\text{HR} = 1.17$) are independently associated with incident AAA risk: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The associations in Model 3 (Table 2) are statistically strong ($P < 0.001$) and persist in Fine–Gray competing-risk models accounting for mortality (`Supplemental Table S11`). However, Model 3 omits three critical confounders available in the UK Biobank: **statin / lipid-lowering medication use**, **body mass index (BMI) / waist circumference** (adiposity is a primary determinant of baseline serum CRP), and **diabetes mellitus / metformin use** (which is inversely associated with AAA growth and alters systemic inflammation). The impact of omitting statin therapy is visible in Table 1: AAA cases had lower baseline total cholesterol ($5.38 \pm 1.29$ vs. $5.72 \pm 1.12$ mmol/L, $P < 0.001$) and lower "hypercholesterolemia" defined purely by unmedicated total cholesterol $\ge 5.18$ mmol/L ($53.9\%$ vs. $67.8\%$), because high-risk older men were already prescribed statins at baseline. Because statins reduce both LDL/total cholesterol and CRP, defining hypercholesterolemia solely by raw cholesterol $\ge 5.18$ mmol/L without adjusting for statin prescriptions misclassifies treated dyslipidemia and confounds the CRP–AAA estimate.

* **Baseline platelet count ($\text{HR} = 0.90, P = 0.005$) and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (`PLR`, $\text{HR} = 0.82, P < 0.001$) are inversely associated with incident AAA, whereas baseline `PLR` and `LMR` did not differ between AAA and non-AAA participants in Table 1: [Fragile].**  
  There is a direct contradiction between Table 1/Results text (Line 61: "PLR and LMR did not differ between individuals with and without AAA", listing $P = 0.559$ for `PLR` and $P = 0.096$ for `LMR`) and the reported means and SDs in Table 1 (`PLR`: $140.62 \pm 55.10$ in $N = 270,440$ vs. $125.64 \pm 51.59$ in $N = 629$; `LMR`: $4.65 \pm 3.10$ vs. $4.02 \pm 2.49$). Computing the two-tailed Student's $t$-statistic directly from the Table 1 summary values gives $t = (140.62 - 125.64) / \sqrt{55.10^2 / 270440 + 51.59^2 / 629} = 14.98 / 2.06 = 7.27$ ($P = 3.6 \times 10^{-13}$) for `PLR`, and $t = (4.65 - 4.02) / \sqrt{3.10^2 / 270440 + 2.49^2 / 629} = 0.63 / 0.0995 = 6.33$ ($P = 2.5 \times 10^{-10}$) for `LMR`! Furthermore, platelet counts decline with age and are higher in women than men; in unadjusted Table 1, AAA cases are 8 years older ($63.0$ vs. $55.0$ years) and $87.1\%$ male (vs. $45.9\%$ male), and baseline antiplatelet medication (aspirin/clopidogrel) is not adjusted for in Table 2.

* **Sex and smoking status significantly modify the associations of CRP, lymphocytes, neutrophils, platelets, `LMR`, and `SII` with AAA risk (Figure 3): [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 3, the authors perform 45 subgroup interaction tests (9 inflammatory biomarkers $\times$ 5 stratifying variables: PRS, age, sex, hypertension, smoking) without any multiple-testing correction (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg or Bonferroni threshold $0.05 / 45 = 0.0011$), and despite testing multiplicative hazards in Cox models where baseline hazard is ~6-fold higher in men and smokers.

* **Combining high CRP (top quartile) and high 31-SNP PRS (top tertile) identifies a 2.1-fold higher cumulative AAA incidence ($10.05\%$ vs. $4.78\%$) among male current smokers aged $\ge 65$ years: [Well Supported].**  
  The additive risk stratification of CRP and PRS ($P_{\text{interaction}} = 0.85$) within the guideline-recommended screening demographic (men $\ge 65$ who smoke) is clinically informative and supported by both Cox and Fine–Gray cumulative incidence estimates (Figure 4, Results Line 185), provided the 31-SNP PRS weights were derived from external GWAS cohorts independent of the UK Biobank.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Adjust Multivariable Cox Models for BMI, Diabetes/Metformin, Statin Therapy, and Antiplatelet Use
* **WHY:** Defining hypercholesterolemia solely as total cholesterol $\ge 5.18$ mmol/L (Table 1 footnote) misclassifies statin-treated high-risk patients as normocholesterolemic (explaining why AAA cases had lower cholesterol in Table 1), and omitting BMI, diabetes, and lipid/antiplatelet medications leaves major determinants of CRP and leukocyte/platelet counts unadjusted.
* **WHAT:** Add baseline BMI, history of diabetes mellitus, lipid-lowering medication (statin) use, and antiplatelet medication use to Model 3 (or as a Model 4) in Table 2.
* **HOW:** Extract UK Biobank baseline fields for BMI (`Field 21001`), diabetes diagnosed by doctor (`Field 2443`), and regular cholesterol-lowering/aspirin medication (`Fields 6177` and `6153`), redefine hypercholesterolemia to include participants taking lipid-lowering medications, and report the updated HRs and 95% CIs for all 9 biomarkers.

#### 2. Recompute All Table 1 $P$-Values and Reconcile Sample Size Counts Across the Manuscript
* **WHY:** The $P$-values for `PLR` ($0.559$), `LMR` ($0.096$), and `SBP` ($0.014$) in Table 1 are mathematically inconsistent with the reported means, SDs, and sample sizes ($N = 270,440$ vs. $629$), and the total sample size fluctuates across $271,068$, $271,069$, and $270,439$.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Table 1 $P$-values (Lines 99, 110, 112):** Re-run the two-sample Student's $t$-tests (and Wilcoxon rank-sum tests for skewed markers) in R for `SBP` ($138.59 \pm 19.51$ vs. $147.22 \pm 21.16$, true $P < 0.001$), `PLR` ($140.62 \pm 55.10$ vs. $125.64 \pm 51.59$, true $P < 0.001$), and `LMR` ($4.65 \pm 3.10$ vs. $4.02 \pm 2.49$, true $P < 0.001$), and update Line 61 of the Results text accordingly.
  2. **Sample size arithmetic (Lines 61, 76–98, 145):** Reconcile the total sample size: Line 61 states `271,068`; Table 1 header lists `270,440` without AAA + `629` with AAA (`= 271,069`); within Table 1, the rows for `Age` ($233,144 + 37,295 = 270,439$), `Sex` ($124,102 + 146,337 = 270,439$), `Ethnicity` ($270,439$), `Education` ($270,439$), and `Smoking` ($270,439$) sum to `270,439` instead of `270,440`; and the Table 2 footnote (Line 145) states "A total of 270,439 participants involved in this analysis" (which equals the number of non-AAA controls alone!).

#### 3. Clarify Missing Data Imputation Method and Apply Multiple-Testing Correction to Subgroup Interactions
* **WHY:** Methods (Line 51) states that missing values in sensitivity analysis 1 were imputed "by linear interpolation," which is only defined for ordered time-series data, not unordered cross-sectional participants; and Figure 3 tests 45 subgroup interactions without false-discovery control.
* **WHAT:** Replace "linear interpolation" with the actual cross-sectional imputation method used (e.g., multiple imputation by chained equations [`mice`] or multivariable linear regression imputation), and report Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted $P_{\text{interaction}}$ values across the 45 interaction tests in Figure 3.
* **HOW:** Apply `p.adjust(p_interaction_vector, method = "BH")` to the 45 interaction $p$-values in Figure 3, and document in the `Supplemental Method` whether the 31 AAA GWAS SNPs were discovered in cohortscompletely independent of the UK Biobank.
