### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates gut microbiota composition and peripheral blood mRNA expression of fatty acid metabolism enzymes (`ACC1`, `FASN`, `CPT1A`, normalized to `GAPDH`) in patients with rifampicin-resistant tuberculosis (RR-TB, $n = 23$), multi-drug resistant tuberculosis (MDR-TB, $n = 28$), and pre-extensively drug-resistant or extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis (pre-XDR/XDR-TB, $n = 11$; total TB $n = 62$), compared against a healthy control group (CK, $n = 5$) at Wenzhou Central Hospital. Fecal samples from individuals within the same group were physically pooled (300 mg mixed per group) prior to DNA extraction and sequencing, and blood mRNA expression was assayed by qRT-PCR.

This manuscript exhibits pervasive methodological, statistical, and reporting failures that invalidate its empirical conclusions. First, the study design physically pools fecal samples within each clinical group (`300 mg of faeces collected from the same group of individuals was mixed and analyzed`, lines 21 and 117), destroying biological replication ($N = 1$ composite pool per group) while claiming to compute individual-level $\alpha$-diversity (`The α-diversity of each subject... was different`, line 169) and reporting $p < 0.05$ across "three independent experiments" (Figure 2 caption). Second, the healthy control group consists of only $n = 5$ individuals (Table 2), and the demographic data in the main text directly contradict the tables: Results line 161 states that the 62 TB patients had a mean BMI of `24.12 kg/m²` and were aged `between 36 and 59 years`, which are the exact numbers listed for the 5 *healthy controls* in Table 2 (`Average age: 36–59`, `BMI: 24.12 ± 3.14`), whereas Table 1 lists the 62 TB patients as having an average age of `51.1` and BMI of `22.42 ± 3.99`. Third, the study design and exclusion criteria are self-contradictory: patients are described as receiving WHO anti-TB regimens (lines 107, 188) and changes are attributed to "gut microbiome dysbiosis induced by the antimicrobials" (lines 26, 186, 217), yet the exclusion criteria in line 112 state that subjects were excluded for `current (in the past six months) use of metabolic agents, probiotics, or antibiotics`. Fourth, no raw sequencing data accession number is provided, and the sequencing methodology description (line 122) is scientifically incoherent. Because physical pooling of stool samples eliminates patient-level variance and confounds any statistical inference, the microbiome portion of the study cannot be salvaged without re-sequencing individual patient specimens.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Significant differences in $\alpha$-diversity (Chao1, ACE, Shannon, Simpson) and taxonomic composition across RR-TB, MDR-TB, pre-XDR-TB, and healthy controls (lines 26, 169–196, Figures 1–5): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Lines 21 and 117 state that *"300 mg of faeces collected from the same group of individuals was mixed and analyzed via DNA extraction, high-throughput sequencing, and bioinformatics. ... The extracted DNA was divided into four test tubes."* Physically mixing stool across patients within a group reduces the biological sample size to $N = 1$ pooled specimen per group; splitting the extracted DNA into technical aliquots ("three independent experiments" in Figure 2 and Tables 1–2) measures only technical pipetting/sequencing variance, not inter-individual biological variance. Consequently, all reported one-way ANOVA $p$-values ($p < 0.05$) for diversity and taxon abundance are statistically invalid pseudoreplication, and the claim in line 169 that *"The α-diversity of each subject... was different"* is impossible under a pooled-stool protocol.

* **Taxonomic shifts are induced by multi-drug anti-TB antibiotic exposure (lines 26, 186–196, 217, 228): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The manuscript contains irreconcilable contradictions regarding sample collection timing and antibiotic exposure:
  1. Line 112 states that the exclusion criteria included *"current (in the past six months) use of metabolic agents, probiotics, or antibiotics,"* yet line 112 simultaneously states the patients were *"11 former XDR-TB patients, 23 drug-resistant TB patients, and 28 MDR-TB patients who were being treated at Wenzhou Central Hospital."*
  2. Line 107 states samples were taken *"at the start of anti-TB treatment and three months after the start of treatment"*; line 117 states caregivers collected stool *"at two and eight weeks"*; and Figures 1–6 present only a single cross-sectional bar/column per group (Con, RR-TB, pre-XDR-TB, MDR-TB) with no longitudinal time points shown.
  3. Line 26 claims *Eubacterium, Faecalibacterium, Roseburia,* and *Ruminococcus* were significantly increased in RR-TB in the Abstract, yet these four genera are never shown in the Results (only mentioned in the Discussion, line 224, as general members of *Clostridium* clusters IV and XIV). Conversely, line 188 claims *Bacteroidales* accounted for 51% (RR-TB), 26% (pre-XDR-TB), and 37% (MDR-TB) of bacteria, and two sentences later states *"the disappearance of Bacteroidales was seen in the patients with RR-TB, pre-XDR-TB, and MDR-TB,"* while Discussion line 228 states *Bacteroidales* was at *"1–2%"* and line 224 states *"Bacteroidetes... were only found in the healthy participants."*

* **Enhanced de novo fatty acid synthesis (`ACC1/GAPDH`, `FASN/GAPDH`, and `CPT1A/GAPDH` mRNA in whole blood) is driven by antimicrobial-induced gut dysbiosis and correlates with *Bacteroidetes/Bacteroidales/Bacteroidaceae* (lines 26, 207–217, Figure 6): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, no correlation analysis (Pearson $r$, Spearman $\rho$, regression slope, or $p$-value) between any bacterial taxon and blood `ACC1`, `FASN`, or `CPT1A` mRNA expression is ever computed or presented in the manuscript; the claimed correlation with *Bacteroidetes*, *Bacteroidales*, and *Bacteroidaceae* (lines 26, 217, 228) is pure assertion. Second, `CPT1A` (carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A) is the rate-limiting enzyme for mitochondrial **fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation**, not *de novo* fatty acid synthesis (which the authors themselves note in line 217 before immediately contradicting it by calling it *"the CPT1A to GAPDH axis... for common augmentation of the de novo fatty acids synthesis"* and labeling Figure 6 *"mRNA expression of CPT1, GAPDH, FASN and ACC1 in de novo synthesis of fatty acids"*). Third, measuring mRNA ratios in whole peripheral blood by $\Delta\text{Ct}$ does not measure hepatic or adipose *de novo* lipogenesis or serum FASN/free fatty acid levels (which Discussion line 226 conflates by referring to *"The profiles and compositions of FASN in serum"*).

* **Demographic and baseline clinical comparisons between TB patients ($n = 62$) and healthy controls ($n = 5$) (lines 59–105, 161, Tables 1–2): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, Table 1 ($n = 62$ patients) and Table 2 ($n = 5$ controls) carry the footnote: *"Note: Data are represented as means ± SD of three independent experiments,"* which is nonsensical for human demographic and clinical laboratory values (Gender, Age, BMI, Hemoglobin, Albumin, Total Protein). Second, Results line 161 swaps the patient and control demographics, stating that the 62 TB patients were *"aged between 36 and 59 years"* with a *"mean BMI of... 24.12 kg/m²,"* which are the exact values from Table 2 (healthy controls: age `36–59`, BMI `24.12 ± 3.14`), whereas Table 1 reports the 62 TB patients' average age as `51.1` and BMI as `22.42 ± 3.99`. Third, a control group of $n = 5$ subjects is severely underpowered and unmatched for the male-skewed TB cohort (49 males, 13 females).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Sequence Individual Patient Stool Samples Rather Than Pooled Group Composites
* **WHY:** Mixing 300 mg of stool across patients within each group (lines 21, 117) eliminates all information on inter-individual variation, making valid statistical testing between groups or correlation with individual patient blood markers impossible.
* **WHAT:** Extract DNA and perform 16S rRNA (or shotgun metagenomic) sequencing independently on each individual subject's unpooled stored stool specimen ($n = 62$ patients and an adequately powered, age- and sex-matched control cohort of at least $n = 30$ healthy controls).
* **HOW:** Retrieve the archived $-80^\circ\text{C}$ individual stool specimens (line 117), extract DNA per patient, sequence the V3–V4 or V4 16S rRNA region using a standard Illumina protocol, deposit the raw FASTQ files in a public repository (e.g., NCBI SRA or GSA), and compute patient-level $\alpha$- and $\beta$-diversity and compositional differential abundance (ANCOM-BC2 / ALDEx2).

#### 2. Clarify the Longitudinal Sampling Schedule and Antibiotic Exposure Status
* **WHY:** The manuscript gives three conflicting descriptions of when samples were collected (at start and 3 months in line 107; before and after the experiment in line 112; at 2 and 8 weeks in line 117) and contradicts itself on whether patients were excluded for antibiotic use (line 112) or actively taking second-line MDR-TB regimens.
* **WHAT:** Provide a clear CONSORT-style flow diagram and patient metadata table specifying exact drug regimens, treatment durations prior to sampling, and which timepoint(s) are plotted in each figure.
* **HOW:** Tabulate the exact anti-TB drug combinations (e.g., bedaquiline, linezolid, fluoroquinolones, cycloserine, clofazimine) for the RR-TB, MDR-TB, and pre-XDR-TB groups, remove the erroneous antibiotic exclusion statement from line 112, and analyze paired baseline vs. follow-up samples using linear mixed-effects models.

#### 3. Compute Formal Statistical Correlations and Correct Biochemical Misclassifications
* **WHY:** The Abstract and Results claim that *Bacteroidetes/Bacteroidales/Bacteroidaceae* correlate with *de novo* fatty acid synthesis without computing any correlation statistics, and misclassify `CPT1A` as a *de novo* fatty acid synthesis enzyme.
* **WHAT:** Once individual-level microbiome and blood qRT-PCR data are paired per patient, compute Spearman rank correlations with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction, and separate fatty acid synthesis (`ACC1`, `FASN`) from mitochondrial $\beta$-oxidation (`CPT1A`).
* **HOW:** Calculate patient-level $2^{-\Delta\Delta\text{Ct}}$ expression values (checking that `GAPDH` Ct values do not vary systematically across TB severity groups, as implied by the "GAPDH" bar in Figure 6), compute Spearman $\rho$ and FDR-adjusted $q$-values against CLR-transformed taxon abundances, and revise all text in lines 207–217 to accurately describe `CPT1A` as governing fatty acid oxidation rather than synthesis.

#### 4. Correct Text-Table Contradictions and Incoherent Sequencing Descriptions
* **WHY:** Swapped patient/control numbers and garbled methods descriptions undermine basic technical credibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Demographics swap (line 161 vs. Tables 1–2):** Correct line 161 so that the age and BMI of the 62 TB patients match Table 1 (`average age 51.1 years`, `mean BMI 22.42 ± 3.99 kg/m²`), and remove the erroneous footnote *"Data are represented as means ± SD of three independent experiments"* from Tables 1 and 2.
  * **Sequencing methods description (line 122):** Replace the garbled text (*"The high-throughput intestinal genomic DNA that was extracted was divided into appropriate sizes. DNA fragments were connected into complete DNA molecules. After the primer was hydrolyzed, the primer on the lead chain became a completely new chain to realize bridge amplification"*) with the exact PCR primers, 16S hypervariable region amplified (or shotgun library kit), sequencing instrument, read length, and quality-filtering/OTU/ASV pipeline used.
  * **Taxonomic abundance contradictions (lines 26, 188, 224, 228):** Reconcile the conflicting percentages reported for *Bacteroidales* (51%/26%/37% in line 188 vs. "disappearance" in line 188 vs. 1–2% in line 228 vs. "only found in the healthy participants" in line 224).
