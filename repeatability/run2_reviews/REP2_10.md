### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates when, where, and how the earliest sustained SARS-CoV-2 community transmission networks became established in North America (`A.1` Washington state outbreak clade) and Europe (`B.1` Northern Italy / European clade) during January–February 2020. Combining individual-based contact-network epidemiological and sequence-evolution forward simulations (`FAVITES`; 1,000 replicates per scenario parameterized with empirical doubling times and substitution rates) with Bayesian generalized linear model (GLM) discrete phylogeographic inference in `BEAST` that explicitly incorporates incidence-informed unsampled taxa from 12 undersampled regions and air-passenger flow covariates, the authors formally test whether the earliest detected introductions—`WA1` (sampled 15 January 2020 in Washington state) and `BavPat1` (sampled late January 2020 in Bavaria, Germany)—founded the subsequent `A.1` Washington and `B.1` Italian epidemics. They reject both direct-descent hypotheses ($P < 0.001$ for `WA1` in the initial $n = 294$ sample; $P = 0.010$ when including the later basal lineage `WA-S566` in $n = 1,000$ genomes; and $P = 0.004$ for `BavPat1` initiating the Italian clade), demonstrate that 16 early British Columbia genomes carrying the ancestral `C17747` allele are amplicon primer-incorporation artifacts (`nCov2019_58_RIGHT`), and date the independent introductions that founded sustained transmission to ~28 January 2020 (95% HPD: 20 January–6 February) for Hubei-to-Italy, ~1 February 2020 (95% HPD: 14 January–15 February) for China-to-Washington state, and ~12 February 2020 (95% HPD: 3–22 February) for Europe-to-New York City.

This is an exemplary work of forensic genomic epidemiology. Rather than over-interpreting a single point-estimate phylogeny in a low-diversity regime where substitution rates lag transmission rates, the authors construct formal, falsifiable coalescent/transmission null distributions via `FAVITES`, audit sequencing primers to resolve an apparent phylogeographic contradiction in British Columbia (`C17747T`), and mitigate spatial ascertainment bias by integrating unsampled taxa and mobility covariates in both time-homogeneous and time-inhomogeneous Bayesian reconstructions. Moreover, the text displays exceptional scientific candor by pointing out where its own maximum clade credibility (MCC) tree topology is biologically implausible (e.g., noting that a US-to-Malaysia branch in Figure 4 is an artifact of limited sequence resolution) and presenting competing hypotheses symmetrically.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The `WA1` case (15 January 2020) did not directly initiate the predominant Washington state `A.1` outbreak clade ($P < 0.001$ in $n = 294$; $P = 0.010$ including `WA-S566` in $n = 1,000$): [Well Supported].**  
  Across 1,000 forward `FAVITES` simulations (doubling time $4.7$ days, substitution rate $0.8 \times 10^{-3}$ subst/site/yr over 61 days), $95.5\%$ of simulated epidemics seeded by `WA1` produced sampled intermediates separated by 1 mutation, $99.7\%$ produced basal polytomies, and $100\%$ produced sibling lineages diverging prior to the two clade-defining substitutions (`C17747T` and `A17858G`), matching the observed tree in $0 / 1,000$ runs (Figure 2). When the later-sampled basal lineage `WA-S566` (29 March 2020) is included ($n = 1,000$ sampled genomes), $99.0\%$ of simulated epidemics still produce $\ge 3$ divergent basal lineages ($P = 0.010$), and the result remains robust under slower epidemic doubling times ($5.6$ days) and doubled evolutionary rates ($1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ subst/site/yr).

* **Apparent ancestral `C17747` / derived `A17858G` intermediate genomes in British Columbia (16/27 early genomes) and other US states are ARTIC primer-trimming sequencing artifacts rather than evolutionary intermediates: [Well Supported].**  
  In Figure 3 and Table S2, every single informative `C17747` + `A17858G` genome from British Columbia and multiple US states shares 1 to 4 downstream, location-specific derived substitutions with locally co-circulating `T17747` + `G17858` genomes, and traces to amplicon sequencing where primer `nCov2019_58_RIGHT` overlaps position 17747 and carries the ancestral `C17747` reference base. Given a $\sim 29.9$-kb genome and low divergence, multiple independent homoplasies or reversions across local transmission pairs are mathematically untenable, making the bioinformatic artifact proof definitive.

* **The Bavarian outbreak (`BavPat1`) was contained and did not seed the Northern Italy (`B.1`) epidemic ($P = 0.004$ in `FAVITES`; posterior probability $0.84$ for independent China-to-Italy introduction): [Well Supported].**  
  Forward `FAVITES` simulations of an epidemic seeded by `BavPat1` (doubling time $3.4$ days over 36 days) show that the complete absence of basal polytomy lineages in the observed tree is incompatible with `BavPat1` founding the Italian outbreak ($P = 0.004$, Fig. S7), and Bayesian GLM phylogeography independently supports separate introductions from China into Germany and Italy ($\text{pp} = 0.84$ vs. $0.16$ for Germany-to-Italy, Figure 5 and Fig. S8).

* **Sustained transmission networks were established around 28 January 2020 in Italy, 1 February (or 26 January) 2020 in Washington state, and 12 February 2020 in New York City: [Well Supported].**  
  The Markov jump timing estimates account for phylogenetic uncertainty, incorporate incidence-weighted unsampled taxa, are cross-checked against time-inhomogeneous models without unsampled taxa (yielding 26 January [95% HPD: 15 January–7 February] for Washington state, Fig. S5) and location-masked models for `WA-S566`/`NY` (yielding 7 February [95% HPD: 23 January–18 February], Fig. S6), and align closely with independent retrospective surveillance from the Seattle Flu Study (0 positives among 6,908 samples before late February) and NYC serosurveys.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Incorporate Time-Varying Travel Restrictions into the Phylogeographic Model with Unsampled Taxa
* **WHY:** As noted on Line 35, the time-homogeneous GLM diffusion model with unsampled taxa allows unsampled Hubei lineages to branch near the `WA` outbreak clade after the 23 January Wuhan lockdown and 2 February US travel suspension, shifting the mean introduction date slightly later (1 February vs. 26 January in the time-inhomogeneous model without unsampled taxa).
* **WHAT:** Combine the time-inhomogeneous GLM diffusion epochs (pre- and post-Hubei lockdown / travel ban) with the unsampled-taxa dataset in a single unified `BEAST` run.
* **HOW:** Apply the epoch-stratified air-traffic covariate matrices from Fig. S4/S5 directly to the dataset containing the incidence-derived unsampled taxa (Figure 4) to obtain a single reconciled 95% HPD interval for the Washington and Italian introduction dates.

#### 2. Quantify Sensitivity of `FAVITES` Topological Rejection to Overdispersed Superspreading ($k$) and Temporal Sampling Heterogeneity
* **WHY:** Early community surveillance in Washington state between 15 January and 24 February 2020 (`WA2`) had near-zero sequencing density, followed by intensive retrospective and prospective sampling in early March. If early transmission was highly overdispersed (negative binomial offspring distribution dispersion $k \approx 0.1$) and all 294 sequences were sampled strictly during the last 20 days of the 61-day window, early basal lineages that went stochastically extinct prior to late February would have zero probability of being sampled.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state the sampling-time schedule used in the `FAVITES` subsampling step (e.g., whether the 294 and 1,000 simulated tips were matched to the exact empirical collection dates between late February and mid-March) and report sensitivity across offspring dispersion values $k \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.2\}$.
* **HOW:** In the `FAVITES` simulation summary table (`Table S1`), report the rejection $P$-values for patterns `2B`–`2E` when simulated sequences are subsampled strictly according to the empirical histogram of collection dates in `WA` (zero samples during days 1–39, dense sampling during days 40–61).
