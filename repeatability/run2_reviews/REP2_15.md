### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates whether individual whole-genome sequencing (WGS, $>30\times$ coverage) data can be linked to physical and biometric traits to re-identify de-identified participants in a convenience cohort of $N = 1,061$ individuals from San Diego, CA (569 African, 273 European, 63 Latino, 63 East Asian, 18 South Asian, and 75 Other; ages 18–82). Using 10-fold cross-validation, the authors fit ridge regression and shrinkage models to predict 3D facial scans (shape and RGB color PCs), 100-dimensional voice embeddings and pitch, continuous RGB eye and skin color, height, weight, BMI, and chronological age (from telomere repeat read fractions and mosaic X/Y chromosome copy-number loss). They then train a maximum-entropy weighted L1 similarity metric and solve both a 1-of-$N$ candidate selection task (`select at N`, $s_N$) and a closed-set 1-to-1 bipartite graph matching task (`match at N`, $m_N$), reporting $m_{10} = 83\%$ and $s_{10} = 74\%$ in the ethnically mixed cohort and $m_{10} = 47\%\text{--}57\%$ ($s_{10} = 44\%\text{--}50\%$) within African American or European strata. Access to the genomic and phenotypic data is restricted behind a managed data access agreement.

While the ethical question of genomic privacy is important, the manuscript's headline claims—that genomic prediction of biometric traits such as 3D facial structure, voice, and stature enables individual re-identification—are severely overstated and rest on demographic confounding rather than individualized polygenic trait prediction. Specifically: (1) the authors' own ablation analyses (Fig. 3, Fig. 5B) reveal that known craniofacial SNPs add **zero** predictive power beyond sex and 1,000 genomic ancestry principal components (PCs), their facial GWAS yields zero significant loci, known height SNPs explain only $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.06\text{--}0.08$ before adding sex and ancestry PCs, and 97 of 100 voice identity dimensions have $R^2_{\text{CV}} \le 0.10$; (2) thus, the "genomic face and voice matcher" is overwhelmingly an estimator of sex, continental/sub-continental admixture proportions, and WGS coverage/telomere artifacts matched against sex, skin/face pigmentation, and age inferred from a 3D photograph; (3) the telomere age predictor ($R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.29$ in the 1,061 cohort vs. $R^2 = 0.05$ in the literature and in 1,960 females sequenced on the same pipeline) exhibits a 6-fold performance inflation characteristic of sequencing batch/library-prep confounding by age; and (4) the headline $m_{10} = 83\%$ metric relies on closed-set bipartite matching, which unrealistically assumes the adversary already possesses a 1-to-1 bijection of 10 genomes and 10 laboratory 3D laser facial scans. Substantial reframing and rigorous covariate-residualized baselines are required to separate genuine polygenic trait matching from basic demographic triangulation.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Genomic prediction of 3D facial morphology, voice, and stature enables accurate re-identification of $>8$ of 10 individuals ($m_{10} = 83\%$): [Overstated].**  
  The abstract and introduction frame the re-identification results around predicting biometric traits (3D face, voice, height, weight, eye/skin color) from WGS. However, Figure 3 demonstrates that for 3D facial shape, adding previously reported craniofacial SNPs to sex + 1,000 genomic PCs yields zero improvement in per-pixel $R^2_{\text{CV}}$, and a genome-wide association study of 36 facial landmark distances in this cohort identified no significant variants after multiple-testing correction. Similarly, for height (Fig. 5B), polygenic SNP scores alone achieve $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.06$ (males) and $0.08$ (females)—which the authors acknowledge provides "marginal improvements in discriminative power over random"—and jump to $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.53$ only when sex and genomic ancestry PCs are included. For voice, pitch ($R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.70$) simply reflects male vs. female vocal frequency, while 97 of 100 speaker-identity dimensions fail to exceed $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.10$. Furthermore, reporting closed-set bipartite matching ($m_{10} = 83\%$) in an ethnically mixed cohort where self-reported ethnicity, admixture, sex, and age vary widely inflates real-world privacy risk by assuming a closed 10-person permutation where eliminating 9 demographic mismatches automatically assigns the 10th person.

* **Within-ethnicity re-identification reaches $\sim 5$ of 10 African Americans or Europeans ($s_{10} = 44\%\text{--}50\%$, $m_{10} = 47\%\text{--}57\%$): [Fragile].**  
  Even when stratified by self-reported ethnicity (`AFR` or `EUR`) and sex, the San Diego cohort exhibits substantial continuous admixture (Fig. 1B: self-reported African Americans vary widely in European/African ancestry proportions, and Europeans vary in Southern/Northern European and AMR components) and spans a 64-year age range (18–82 years, Fig. 1C). Because continuous skin/eye RGB color, facial width/protrusion PCs, and WGS-estimated age strongly track continuous admixture fraction and chronological age within self-reported ethnic groups, the stratified test still conflates continuous admixture and age matching with individualized craniofacial/biometric gene prediction. No baseline showing re-identification accuracy after regressing out sex, age, and continuous admixture proportions from both phenotypes and genotypes is provided in the main text.

* **Chronological age can be predicted from WGS data alone with $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.44$ ($\text{MAE} = 8.0$ years) using telomere repeat read fraction ($R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.29$) and mosaic X/Y loss: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In line 78, the authors note that prior WGS telomere studies achieved only $R^2 = 0.05$ for age prediction, and that running their own pipeline on an independent set of 1,960 females sequenced on the exact same platform also yielded $R^2 = 0.05$. Yet in the 1,061-person study cohort, telomere length alone suddenly explains $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.29$ (nearly $6\times$ higher variance explained). In short-read Illumina WGS, the fraction of reads aligning to telomeric repeats `(TTAGGG)_n` and normalized X/Y read depth are highly sensitive to DNA source (whole blood vs. buffy coat vs. saliva), extraction kit batch, fragment size distribution, GC bias, and sequencing run/flow-cell batch. If participant age correlated with recruitment wave or sequencing batch in the 1,061 convenience sample, a machine learning model trained on telomeric and sex-chromosome coverage ratios will learn batch artifacts as a proxy for age.

* **Continuous RGB eye color and skin color are predicted with per-channel $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.77\text{--}0.82$: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Unlike complex craniofacial traits, pigmentation is controlled by a small number of large-effect loci (*HERC2/OCA2*, *SLC24A5*, *SLC45A2*, *MC1R*) and correlates strongly with continental ancestry across an multi-ethnic cohort. However, because $R^2_{\text{CV}}$ is computed across the pooled multi-ethnic cohort (where African vs. European ancestry alone accounts for most skin and eye RGB variance, as shown in Table 1 where face color predicts `AFR` with $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.89$), the pooled $R^2_{\text{CV}} = 0.77\text{--}0.82$ largely reflects between-population pigmentation differences rather than within-population SNP discrimination.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Add a Demographic-Only Baseline and a Covariate-Residualized Polygenic Benchmark
* **WHY:** The central scientific question is whether polygenic trait prediction from a genome adds re-identification power beyond the 4 basic demographic variables (sex, chronological age, continental admixture proportions, and pigmentation). Currently, `3D face` in the matching pipeline includes image-predicted sex, age, and ancestry (`ψ_P` in Materials and Methods, line 147), so reporting that "3D face alone achieves $s_{10} = 58\%$" actually credits the 3D face model with sex, age, and ancestry matching.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly separate (a) matching using only genomic sex + 5 ADMIXTURE proportions + WGS age against image-derived sex + ancestry + age + RGB pigmentation, from (b) matching using residualized 3D facial shape landmarks, voice identity vectors, and SNP-based height/BMI after projecting out sex, age, and the top 20 genomic PCs.
* **HOW:** Residualize all continuous phenotypes (3D facial PCs, voice i-vectors, height, weight, BMI, RGB eye/skin color) on `Sex + Age + AFR + EUR + EAS + AMR + CSA` within each training fold, repeat the `s_10` and `m_10` evaluations on the residuals, and report the exact incremental $\Delta s_{10}$ and $\Delta m_{10}$ over the demographic baseline in Table 2.

#### 2. Audit and Control for Sequencing Batch and DNA Source Confounding in WGS Age Prediction
* **WHY:** A jump in telomere-age $R^2_{\text{CV}}$ from $0.05$ (in both prior literature and 1,960 females on the same sequencing platform) to $0.29$ in the 1,061 cohort is biologically implausible and strongly points to technical batch confounding between sequencing/extraction runs and participant age.
* **WHAT:** Test whether sequencing flow-cell batch, library preparation date, mean insert size, GC dropout, or DNA tissue source correlate with participant age and telomere/X/Y read depth in the 1,061 cohort.
* **HOW:** Fit a linear model of estimated telomere length and X/Y copy ratio on `Age + Sequencing_Batch + Mean_Coverage + Insert_Size + GC_Bias`, report the partial $R^2$ of `Age` after controlling for technical sequencing metrics, and evaluate age prediction across held-out sequencing batches rather than random 10-fold CV.

#### 3. Eliminate Leakage Between Embedding Regression Training and Maximum-Entropy Weight Learning
* **WHY:** In the two-step matching framework (lines 142–160), ridge regressions ($\psi_P, \phi_P$) map phenotypes and genotypes into a shared $D$-dimensional embedding space, and a maximum-entropy model subsequently learns weights $w_d$ on the absolute residuals $|\psi_P(p)_d - \phi_P(g)_d|$. If $\psi_P, \phi_P$ and $w_d$ are fit on the same training folds without nested cross-validation, features that overfit in $\phi_P$ (such as 1,000 genomic PCs in $N \approx 955$ training samples) will have artificially small training residuals and receive distorted weights $w_d$.
* **WHAT:** Verify and document that maximum-entropy weights $w_d$ were trained strictly on out-of-fold predictions from an inner cross-validation loop (nested CV), never on in-sample ridge regression residuals.
* **HOW:** Detail the exact nested CV split structure in Materials and Methods and provide executable scripts demonstrating out-of-fold weight estimation.

#### 4. Reframe Title, Abstract, and Forensic Claims to Reflect Demographic Triangulation
* **WHY:** Claiming that WGS predicts 3D faces and voices well enough to identify crime-scene DNA or de-identified research genomes misleads policymakers and forensic practitioners when the underlying signal is sex, ancestry admixture, and pigmentation.
* **WHAT:** Temper the Abstract and Discussion to state clearly that current polygenic prediction of complex craniofacial shape, voice identity, and stature explains very little variance beyond sex and genetic ancestry, and that re-identification risk in heterogeneous cohorts is driven primarily by demographic and pigmentation triangulation.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract to report the SNP-only $R^2_{\text{CV}}$ values alongside the covariate-assisted $R^2_{\text{CV}}$ values, and emphasize open-set candidate selection ($s_N$ in large homogeneous pools $N \ge 100$) rather than closed-set 10-person bipartite matching ($m_{10}$).
