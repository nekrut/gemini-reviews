### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This prospective cohort study from the Tohoku Medical Megabank Community-Based Cohort Study (TMM CommCohort, Japan) investigates whether 43 NMR-quantified circulating plasma metabolites mediate the association between polygenic risk scores (PRSs; genome-wide and pathway-specific via `PRSice-2`/`PRSet`) and incident type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). PRSs were tuned in 9,801 participants from the Type 2 assessment-center survey and evaluated in 12,461 participants from the Type 1 municipal health check-up survey over a median follow-up of 4.3 years (354 incident T2DM cases). Using modified Poisson regression, restricted cubic splines, and causal mediation analysis adjusted for demographic and lifestyle covariates, the authors report that the genome-wide PRS (RR = $2.09$ per 1-SD) and the nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS (RR = $1.32$) predict incident T2DM, that 14 metabolites are positively and 1 (glycine) is inversely associated with T2DM incidence, and that seven metabolites—most prominently plasma glucose (7.0% mediated)—partially mediate the PRS–T2DM association.

While the large sample size ($N = 12,461$) and separation of PRS tuning (Type 2 survey) from target evaluation (Type 1 survey) are positive design features, the study is compromised by major epidemiological, statistical, and internal consistency flaws: (1) **tautological mediation by baseline glucose** (incident T2DM is defined by blood glucose $\ge 200$ mg/dL or HbA1c $\ge 6.5\%$, yet baseline plasma glucose is treated as a novel "mediating metabolite" without adjusting any of the other 42 metabolite models for baseline glycemic status/prediabetes); (2) **uncontrolled non-fasting blood collection**, where amino acids, ketone bodies, and glucose fluctuate acutely with postprandial interval; (3) **severe unadjusted follow-up attrition** (15,262 of 27,723 eligible participants [55.0%] were lost to follow-up); (4) **misspecified regression models** (Methods and Figure 2 describe fitting logistic regression with continuous standardized PRS as the dependent outcome variable); and (5) pervasive contradictions between the Abstract, Article Highlights, Results text, and Table 2 regarding which metabolites are significant and which mediate risk.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Genome-wide PRS (RR = 2.09, 95% CI 1.68–2.60) and the nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS (RR = 1.32, 95% CI 1.03–1.70) are associated with incident T2DM: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The separation of hyperparameter tuning ($N = 9,801$ Type 2 survey) and validation ($N = 12,461$ Type 1 survey) using external multi-ancestry GWAS summary statistics avoids discovery-target overlap. However, two caveats weaken this result: first, $55.0\%$ ($15,262 / 27,723$) of otherwise eligible Type 1 participants were excluded due to missing follow-up survey data (Section "TMM CommCohort study participants"), introducing substantial selection bias (acknowledged in Additional File 3) without inverse probability of attrition weighting. Second, selecting 8 pathway PRSs out of 9,270 candidate pathways in the cross-sectional Type 2 survey and validating 1 of 8 (`nitrogen compound transport`, RR = $1.32$, 95% CI $1.03$–$1.70$, which barely excludes $1.0$ before multiple-testing correction across the 8 validated pathways; meanwhile the Abstract claims $P < 0.001$ for an interval whose lower 95% bound is $1.03$, which mathematically corresponds to $P \approx 0.028$, not $P < 0.001$) is fragile.

* **Fourteen circulating metabolites are positively associated and glycine is inversely associated with incident T2DM: [Fragile].**  
  Blood samples in the Type 1 municipal check-up survey were collected under **non-fasting** conditions ("non-fasting glucose level of $\ge 200$ mg/dL"), and the models do not adjust for fasting duration (hours since last meal) or baseline HbA1c/glucose. Circulating glucose, branched-chain amino acids (leucine, valine, 2-ketoisocaproic acid), alanine, and ketone bodies (3-hydroxybutyric acid) change markedly within 1–4 hours after meals. Moreover, Table 1 shows that high-PRS individuals already had higher baseline glucose ($89.8$ vs. $88.2$ mg/dL, $P < 0.001$) and baseline HbA1c ($5.6\%$ vs. $5.5\%$, $P < 0.001$)—meaning many were already prediabetic at baseline. Without adjusting for baseline HbA1c and fasting time, metabolite associations with 4.3-year T2DM diagnosis largely reflect unadjusted baseline dysglycemia and postprandial state.

* **Seven metabolites (including plasma glucose at 7.0%, plus alanine/valine or 2-hydroxybutyric acid/3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid) mediate the association between PRS and incident T2DM: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, treating baseline **plasma glucose** as a metabolic mediator of an outcome defined by **plasma glucose $\ge 200$ mg/dL or HbA1c $\ge 6.5\%$** is circular: individuals with higher T2DM genetic risk are closer to the diagnostic glycemic threshold at baseline and therefore cross that threshold sooner during follow-up. Second, the manuscript contradicts itself across four places on the identity of the seven mediators:
  1. **Abstract (Line 21):** Lists `3-hydroxyisobutyric-acid, 2-Aminobutyric acid, 2-ketoisocaproic acid, 2-hydroxybutyric acid, leucine, glycine, and glucose`.
  2. **Article Highlights (Line 46):** Claims `metabolites, such as glucose, alanine, and valine, partially mediating this relationship, accounting for 1–7% of the effect`—yet in Table 2, **Alanine** has a Natural Indirect Effect (NIE) of $1.00$ ($0.99$–$1.01$) and Proportion Mediated of **$0.0\%$ ($-1.0$ to $2.0$)**, and **Valine** has an NIE of $1.01$ ($0.99$–$1.01$, non-significant)!
  3. **Results text (Line 221) & Discussion (Line 340):** Lists `3-hydroxyisobutyric-acid, 2-aminobutyric acid, 2-ketoisocaproic acid, leucine, glycine, glucose, and 3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid` (dropping `2-hydroxybutyric acid` and adding `3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid`).
  4. **Table 2 (Lines 232–270):** Reports mathematically inconsistent mediation quantities where both Natural Direct Effect (NDE $> 1$) and Natural Indirect Effect (NIE $> 1$) are positive, yet the "Proportion mediated" is reported as **negative** (e.g., `Succinic acid`: NDE $= 2.07$, NIE $= 1.01$, Proportion mediated $= -1.0\% [-0.4\text{ to }1.0]$; `Cysteine`: NDE $= 2.05$, NIE $= 1.01$, Proportion mediated $= -2.0\%$; `Lysine`: NDE $= 2.07$, NIE $= 1.01$, Proportion mediated $= -1.0\%$).

* **PRS is linearly and positively associated with 14 circulating metabolites in restricted cubic spline logistic regression models: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, in Methods (Line 120) and the Figure 2 caption (Line 216), the authors state they fitted "adjusted **logistic regression** models to examine the association between circulating metabolites and the standardized PRS (per 1-SD increase), which was used as the outcome variable" and report "odds ratio (OR)." A standardized PRS (mean 0, SD 1) is a continuous variable (and germline DNA precedes circulating metabolites, so PRS must be the exposure and metabolite concentration the continuous outcome in a linear regression, unless PRS was arbitrarily dichotomized at $+1$ SD as in Table 1). Second, the Results text (Line 210) states "PRS was linearly and positively associated with **14** metabolites including..." and then lists **19** distinct metabolites in the same sentence (`succinic acid, 3-hydroxyisobutyric acid, 2-aminobutyric acid, 3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid, methionine, 2-ketoisocaproic acid, creatine, 2-hydroxybutyric acid, cysteine, arginine, ornithine, tyrosine, histidine, leucine, lysine, valine, glutamine, glucose, and 3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid`).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Remove Baseline Glucose as a Mediator and Adjust All Metabolite Models for Baseline HbA1c and Fasting Time
* **WHY:** Including baseline plasma glucose as a mediator of a glucose-defined endpoint (T2DM) is tautological, and failing to adjust the remaining 42 NMR metabolites for baseline HbA1c/glucose and postprandial fasting interval confounds metabolite effects with baseline prediabetes and recent food intake.
* **WHAT:** Exclude baseline glucose from the candidate mediators, and re-run the modified Poisson regression (Figure 1) and mediation models (Table 2) with additional adjustment for baseline HbA1c (%), baseline glucose, and hours since last meal.
* **HOW:** Add `Baseline_HbA1c` and `Fasting_Hours` (recorded at blood draw in the TMM CommCohort protocol) to the covariate set in all regression and causal mediation models, and perform a sensitivity analysis excluding participants with baseline prediabetes (HbA1c $5.7$–$6.4\%$ or non-fasting glucose $140$–$199$ mg/dL).

#### 2. Correct the Exposure–Outcome Specification in Figure 2 and Audit the Mediation Formulas in Table 2
* **WHY:** Germline genetic risk (PRS) cannot be the downstream outcome of circulating metabolites in a logistic regression model, and a positive NDE ($>1.0$) combined with a positive NIE ($>1.0$) cannot yield a negative proportion mediated.
* **WHAT:** Re-fit the PRS-to-metabolite associations using linear regression with continuous standardized PRS as the independent variable and standardized metabolite level as the dependent variable, and recompute Table 2 mediation proportions using a validated causal mediation package (`mediation` or `CMAverse` in R).
* **HOW:** Fit `lm(scale(Metabolite) ~ rcs(PRS, 3) + covariates)` to report $\beta$ coefficients (SD change in metabolite per 1-SD increase in PRS). In Table 2, verify that the proportion mediated is computed on the log-RR scale as $\log(\text{NIE}) / [\log(\text{NDE}) + \log(\text{NIE})]$ (or counterfactual risk-difference scale $\text{NDE} \times (\text{NIE} - 1) / [\text{NDE} \times \text{NIE} - 1]$) so that signs and confidence intervals are mathematically consistent.

#### 3. Address the 55% Follow-Up Attrition Using Inverse Probability of Attrition Weighting (IPAW)
* **WHY:** Excluding 15,262 of 27,723 participants who did not return for the second-period assessment center survey (leaving 12,461 participants who were older, leaner, and less likely to smoke; Additional File 3) can bias risk and mediation estimates.
* **WHAT:** Apply inverse probability of attrition weights to the primary Poisson and mediation models.
* **HOW:** Among all 27,723 baseline-eligible Type 1 participants with genetic and NMR data, fit a logistic regression model predicting follow-up attendance ($R = 1$ vs. $0$) using baseline age, sex, BMI, smoking, drinking, education, physical activity, PRS, HbA1c, and the 43 metabolites, and weight the 12,461 complete cases by $w_i = P(R_i = 1) / P(R_i = 1 \mid X_i)$ with robust sandwich variance estimators.

#### 4. Reconcile Numerical and Textual Contradictions Across Abstract, Highlights, Text, and Tables
* **WHY:** Multiple mismatches between the text and tables prevent readers from knowing which results were actually found.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Abstract (Line 21) vs. Results (Line 194):** Check the $p$-value for the nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS (`RR 1.32, 95% CI 1.03–1.70`). A 95% CI of $1.03$–$1.70$ corresponds to $Z = \ln(1.32) / [(\ln(1.70) - \ln(1.03)) / 3.92] = 0.2776 / 0.1278 = 2.17$, which gives two-sided $P = 0.030$, not $P < 0.001$.
  2. **Results (Line 210):** Reconcile the claim of "14 metabolites" with the list of 19 metabolites that immediately follows in the same sentence.
  3. **Abstract (Line 21), Article Highlights (Line 46), Results (Line 221), and Table 2:** Reconcile the conflicting lists of mediating metabolites (specifically removing `alanine` and `valine` from Article Highlights, and clarifying whether `2-hydroxybutyric acid` [NIE 95% CI $0.99$–$1.02$] or `3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid` [NIE 95% CI $1.00$–$1.02$] is the seventh mediator).
  4. **Table 2 & Table 3:** Fix confidence interval typos (`-1.0 to -0.4–1.0` in Line 232; `-1.0–.0` in Line 255; `-1.0–-0.9–3.0` in Line 299; `0.0 (-2.0–60)` in Line 316; `-1.0 (-6.03.0)` in Line 324; `3-Hyudroxybutyric acid` in Lines 257 and 314) and duplicated `total energy intake` in the captions of Figures 1 and 2 (Lines 205, 216).
