### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript characterizes bacterial DNA sequences recovered from 320 human placental parenchymal specimens (collected at delivery via a population-based case-cohort design nested in PeriBank) using 16S rRNA (V1–V3) 454 pyrosequencing ($n = 320$) and Illumina HiSeq whole-genome shotgun (WGS) metagenomics ($n = 48$). Comparing the resulting taxonomic and functional profiles to nonpregnant reference body sites from the Human Microbiome Project (HMP) and published gravid vaginal cohorts, the authors report a unique "placental microbiome" dominated by *Escherichia coli* and *Tenericutes*, claim that its phylum-level profile is most similar to the human oral microbiome (Bray–Curtis dissimilarity $< 0.3$; `SourceTracker` 90% oral attribution), and report significant beta-diversity associations with preterm birth $< 37$ weeks (PERMANOVA $P = 0.001$) and remote first/second-trimester antenatal infections ($P = 0.006$). Sequencing data are deposited in SRA (`PRJNA238913`) and dbGaP.

Despite the large clinical cohort and careful physical excision of placental cuboidal sections, the study's central conclusions are invalidated by three severe methodological flaws: (1) failure to sequence and computationally subtract DNA extraction kit and reagent blank controls in an ultra-low-biomass tissue ($>99\%$ human reads, estimated $< 1\text{ pg}/\mu\text{L}$ bacterial DNA), where the putative signature taxa (*Escherichia coli*, *Burkholderia*, *Acinetobacter*, *Paenibacillus*, and marine extremophiles *Thioalkalivibrio* and *Pseudoalteromonas*) are hallmark reagent contaminants ("kitome") and short-read alignment artifacts; (2) an asymmetric bioinformatic pipeline comparison in which placental WGS reads were classified using `MG-RAST` with an ultra-permissive BLASTX $E$-value cutoff of $1.0$ (minimum alignment length 15 amino acids, 50% identity), whereas HMP reference body sites were classified using `MetaPhlAn` clade-specific marker genes; and (3) unaddressed confounding by DNA extraction kit lots and antenatal antibiotic administration. Re-analyzing the raw reads with a uniform taxonomic classifier alongside sequenced extraction blanks is required before any biological claim of an *in utero* placental microbiome can be supported.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The human placenta harbors a unique, metabolically active resident microbiome dominated by *Escherichia coli*: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The authors acknowledge that placental tissue has ultra-low microbial biomass ($>99\%$ human reads in WGS; $\sim 0.002$ mg bacterial DNA per gram of tissue) and state that DNA extraction was repeated with MO-BIO PowerSoil kits until sufficient DNA was obtained, with one nontemplate blank extraction per 11 samples. However, nontemplate controls were only checked by 16S PCR gel electrophoresis ("no 16S sequences were generated from nontemplate controls (fig. S4)") and were **never sequenced, quantified by taxonomic profile, or subtracted from the 16S or WGS datasets**. Commercial silica-column extraction kits (including MO-BIO PowerSoil), PCR master mixes, and sequencing enzymes ubiquitously contain background DNA from *Escherichia coli* (used to manufacture recombinant polymerases), *Burkholderia*, *Paenibacillus*, *Acinetobacter*, and *Streptococcus*. Furthermore, the authors' LEfSe and Boruta analyses (Figure 4B–D) identify *Thioalkalivibrio* (an obligate halophilic/haloalkaliphilic sulfur-oxidizing chemolithoautotroph from soda lakes) and *Pseudoalteromonas* (a marine bacterium) as discriminative placental genera—unmistakable signatures of permissive `MG-RAST` short-read misassignment ($E \le 1$, 15 aa alignment) and reagent background rather than a physiological human tissue community.

* **The placental microbiome taxonomic profile is most akin (Bray–Curtis dissimilarity $< 0.3$) to the human oral microbiome: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To compare placental WGS data against HMP reference niches (oral, stool, skin, nasal, vaginal; Figures 1 and 2), the authors processed their 48 placental WGS samples through `MG-RAST` (BLASTX against M5NR with $E \le 1$, $\ge 50\%$ identity, $\ge 15$ aa length; Methods, "WGS sequencing and analysis"), but downloaded the HMP reference WGS taxonomic profiles directly from `http://hmpdacc.org/HMSMCP/`, which were generated using `MetaPhlAn` (Methods, "HMP data retrieval"). Comparing `MG-RAST` ($E \le 1$ best-hit protein homology across all M5NR entries) against `MetaPhlAn` (strict nucleotide alignments to unique clade-specific marker genes) completely confounds body site with bioinformatic classifier. In addition, computing Bray–Curtis dissimilarity at the **phylum level** across only five major phyla forces any sample enriched in `MG-RAST` Proteobacteria (*E. coli* hits) and Firmicutes to cluster near oral sites (which have higher Proteobacteria than stool, vagina, or skin) purely as an artifact of coarse taxonomic aggregation.

* **The placental microbiome community structure is significantly altered in preterm birth ($< 37$ weeks; PERMANOVA $P = 0.001$, Boruta enrichment of *Burkholderia* and *Paenibacillus*): [Fragile].**  
  In ultra-low-biomass amplicon and shotgun sequencing, sample clustering on PCoA (Figure 3A) and differential abundance of known kit contaminants (*Burkholderia* in preterm vs. *Paenibacillus* in term placentas, Figure 3B) frequently reflect batch effects across MO-BIO PowerSoil kit lots, extraction dates, or 454/HiSeq sequencing runs when cases and controls are not randomized across extraction batches. Moreover, preterm placentas (<37 weeks) differ systematically in tissue cellularity, maternal blood content, and clinical handling at delivery compared to term placentas, which alters the host-to-reagent DNA ratio during amplification.

* **A remote history of first- or second-trimester maternal antenatal infection (such as UTI) shapes the placental microbiome ($P = 0.006$, enrichment of *Streptococcus*, *Acinetobacter*, *Thioalkalivibrio*, and *Pseudoalteromonas*): [Overstated].**  
  As the authors acknowledge in the Discussion (paragraph 4), every subject with a documented remote antenatal infection (UTI, chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis) received clinically indicated antibiotic therapy (Table S3), making infection history inseparable from systemic antibiotic exposure. Furthermore, the taxa claimed to be enriched in these placentas include marine/extremophile genera (*Thioalkalivibrio*, *Pseudoalteromonas*) that cannot colonize human tissues, demonstrating that the `MG-RAST`/LEfSe pipeline is fitting noise and database misannotations.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Sequence All Blank Extraction Controls and Apply Quantitative Decontamination
* **WHY:** Absence of visible bands on an agarose gel (Fig. S4) does not mean blank extractions are free of amplifiable or sequenceable DNA; when subjected to 454 pyrosequencing or deep HiSeq WGS (26.5–100 million reads/sample), reagent blanks routinely yield thousands of reads mapping to *E. coli*, *Burkholderia*, *Acinetobacter*, and *Paenibacillus*.
* **WHAT:** Sequence the stored nontemplate blank extractions (`1 per 11 samples`) alongside placental specimens using both 16S V1–V3 and HiSeq WGS, and statistically filter contaminant taxa.
* **HOW:** Run the sequenced extraction blanks through the exact same Qiime and WGS pipelines, compare total bacterial read counts (and qPCR 16S copy numbers per mg tissue vs. buffer blank), and apply `decontam` (frequency- and prevalence-based contaminant identification using PicoGreen DNA concentrations) to remove kit-derived OTUs and WGS species before any ecological or clinical analysis.

#### 2. Re-Process All Placental and HMP WGS Reads Through an Identical, Stringent Taxonomic Pipeline
* **WHY:** Comparing `MG-RAST` ($E$-value $\le 1$, length $\ge 15$ aa) on placental samples against `MetaPhlAn` on HMP samples creates severe algorithmic batch bias and generates false-positive hits to environmental extremophiles (*Thioalkalivibrio*, *Pseudoalteromonas*) and residual human/cloning-vector sequences.
* **WHAT:** Replace `MG-RAST` ($E \le 1$) with `MetaPhlAn` (the exact tool used for HMP `HMSMCP`) and re-compute species- and genus-level (rather than phylum-level) beta diversity.
* **HOW:** Run `MetaPhlAn` (with default stringent marker-gene alignment parameters) on the `BMTagger`-filtered non-human reads from the 48 placental WGS samples. Verify putative *E. coli* and *Prevotella tannerae* hits by mapping reads across their full reference genomes to confirm uniform horizontal genome coverage (ruling out localized rRNA/plasmid vector or cloning artifacts), and recompute Figures 1–2 at the species and genus levels.

#### 3. Audit and Control for DNA Extraction Kit Lot Numbers and Sequencing Run Batches
* **WHY:** In low-biomass studies, reagent microbial DNA varies substantially across manufacturer kit lots (`MO-BIO PowerSoil`) and sequencing plates, which can drive spurious PERMANOVA $P$-values if preterm or antenatal-infection samples were extracted or sequenced in partially segregated batches.
* **WHAT:** Include extraction batch, kit lot number, and sequencing run ID as covariates in PERMANOVA (`adonis`) and differential abundance models.
* **HOW:** Extract the DNA isolation dates, kit lot numbers, and 454/HiSeq run barcodes for all 320 samples; test whether batch IDs correlate with preterm status or antenatal infection (`chisq.test`), and run conditioned PERMANOVA (`adonis(dist ~ Batch + Preterm_Birth)`) to verify whether any biological signal survives batch adjustment.
