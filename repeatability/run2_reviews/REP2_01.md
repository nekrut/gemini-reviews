### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript re-analyzes a publicly available Affymetrix Human Genome U133A microarray dataset (`GSE20291`) comprising post-mortem putamen tissue from 15 Parkinson's disease (PD) patients and 15 pathologically normal controls across 12,688 Entrez genes. Using unadjusted two-sample Student's $t$-tests at $p < 0.05$ without a fold-change threshold, the authors report 715 differentially expressed genes (DEGs; 268 up-regulated and 447 down-regulated). They subject these 715 genes to Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment in DAVID (retaining terms at Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted $p < 0.10$), subset 40 genes belonging to nine neuron-related GO terms, map them onto the Human Protein Reference Database (HPRD) protein–protein interaction (PPI) network, and run Clique Percolation Method (`CFinder`, $k = 3$) module detection to highlight `PRKCA`, `BCL2`, and `CDC42` as putative regulators of intrinsic striatal tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) interneuron growth in PD.

The study's conclusions are invalidated by a fundamental statistical failure at the initial differential expression step. Performing 12,688 independent Student's $t$-tests at an unadjusted threshold of $p < 0.05$ yields an expected $12,688 \times 0.05 \approx 634$ false-positive genes under the complete null hypothesis—meaning that roughly $88.7\%$ ($634 / 715$) of the claimed DEGs are expected by chance alone. Because every downstream GO, KEGG, HPRD degree-centrality, and `CFinder` module analysis operates on this noise-dominated gene list, the biological conclusions regarding compensatory striatal TH interneuron growth and gap junction/calcium signaling cannot be distinguished from random microarray variation and generic PPI hub bias. Resolving these flaws requires re-running differential expression with moderated $t$-statistics (`limma`) and false discovery rate (FDR) control, normalizing PPI node degree against the background interactome, and removing unsupported cell-type-specific causal claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **715 genes (268 up-regulated, 447 down-regulated) are differentially expressed in the putamen of PD patients compared to controls: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The authors tested 12,688 genes using unadjusted Student's $t$-tests at $p < 0.05$ with no multiple-testing correction and no minimum log$_2$ fold-change filter (Methods, "Identification of differentially expressed genes"). Under a pure null distribution where no true expression differences exist, $12,688 \times 0.05 = 634.4$ genes are expected to achieve $p < 0.05$, yielding an estimated false discovery rate of $634.4 / 715 = 88.7\%$.

* **PD putamen expression alterations are significantly enriched in neuron morphogenesis GO terms and six KEGG pathways (Gap junction, Calcium signaling, Phosphatidylinositol signaling, Long-term potentiation, Alzheimer's disease, and GnRH signaling): [Fragile].**  
  Because nearly 90% of the 715 input genes are expected false positives, downstream over-representation analysis in DAVID is heavily contaminated by noise. In Table 2, none of the six KEGG pathways passes a standard Benjamini–Hochberg threshold of $0.05$ except Gap junction ($0.038$) and Calcium signaling ($0.045$), and the BH-adjusted $p$-values in Table 2 are non-monotonic with respect to the raw EASE $p$-values (`hsa04070` has raw $p = 0.002342$ and adjusted $p = 0.059518$, whereas `hsa04720` has a smaller raw $p = 0.001217$ but a larger adjusted $p = 0.061717$). Moreover, the authors did not specify the 7,670/12,688 U133A array gene universe as the custom background in DAVID, which artificially inflates neuron and signaling terms on older Affymetrix arrays.

* **Hub genes (`PRKCA`, `RXRA`, `CDC42`, `BCL2`) and a $k=3$ clique module containing `PRKCA`, `BCL2`, and `CDC42` play critical roles in PD striatal pathology: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The authors select 40 genes from nine overlapping neuron-related GO categories and count their total first-neighbor degree in the global HPRD network (reporting degrees of 166 for `PRKCA`, 81 for `RXRA`, 70 for `CDC42`, and 69 for `BCL2`; Results, "PPI network"). Well-studied signaling kinases and apoptosis regulators (`PRKCA`, `BCL2`, `CDC42`) have hundreds of literature-curated interactions in HPRD regardless of whether they are differentially expressed in PD. Without permutation testing against degree-matched random gene sets from the U133A platform, high total HPRD degree reflects ascertainment bias in the literature rather than PD-specific regulatory centrality.

* **Up-regulation of `OPHN1`, `PAX6`, `CaMKII`, `PRKCA`, and `BCL2` promotes intrinsic striatal TH interneuron growth and reduces functional excitatory synapses in PD: [Overstated].**  
  The Abstract Conclusion and Discussion assert a specific cellular mechanism—that expression changes in these genes promote intrinsic striatal TH+ interneuron growth and alter dendritic spine synapses. However, `GSE20291` measures bulk post-mortem putamen homogenates, which suffer from substantial neuronal loss and reactive gliosis in end-stage PD. Bulk microarray shifts cannot resolve rare intrinsic striatal TH+ interneurons from medium spiny neurons, astrocytes, or microglia, nor is any quantitative expression data (fold-changes, $t$-statistics, or adjusted $p$-values) ever reported for `OPHN1`, `PAX6`, `CaMKII`, `PRKCA`, `CDC42`, or `BCL2`.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Unadjusted $t$-Tests with Empirical Bayes Moderated $t$-Statistics and FDR Control
* **WHY:** Calling 715 DEGs out of 12,688 genes at unadjusted $p < 0.05$ yields ~634 expected false positives (FDR $\approx 89\%$), invalidating all downstream functional and network analyses.
* **WHAT:** Re-compute differential expression using `limma` moderated $t$-statistics with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control, and report a complete table of top genes with log$_2$ fold-changes, raw $p$-values, and BH-adjusted $q$-values.
* **HOW:** In R/Bioconductor, fit a linear model to the RMA-normalized expression matrix ($12,688 \times 30$) using `lmFit()` and `eBayes()`, controlling for age, sex, post-mortem interval, and brain pH (available in the `GSE20291` series matrix metadata). If few single genes pass BH $q < 0.05$ due to sample size ($n=15$ per group), use rank-based Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (`fgsea` on the full ranked $t$-statistic vector of 12,688 genes) rather than thresholding on unadjusted $p < 0.05$.

#### 2. Specify the Array Background and Normalize PPI Hub Centrality via Permutation
* **WHY:** Running DAVID against the whole human genome background inflates enrichment for brain-expressed genes well-represented on the U133A chip, and ranking proteins by raw degree in HPRD always selects generic literature hubs like `PRKCA` and `BCL2`.
* **WHAT:** Re-run GO/KEGG enrichment using the exact 12,688 Entrez genes present on the U133A array as the background universe, and test PPI degree/module significance against random gene sets.
* **HOW:** Upload the 12,688 Entrez IDs as the custom background in DAVID (or `clusterProfiler::enrichGO` / `enrichKEGG`). For the HPRD network (7,670 array genes, 31,041 edges), compute the ratio of DEG-to-DEG internal degree vs. total HPRD degree for each candidate gene, and generate 10,000 random samples of 40 neuron-annotated U133A genes to compute empirical permutation $p$-values for `PRKCA`, `BCL2`, and `CDC42` connectivity.

#### 3. Account for Bulk Tissue Cellular Composition Confounding
* **WHY:** Post-mortem PD putamen exhibits altered proportions of medium spiny neurons, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, and microglia compared to healthy controls; attributing bulk mRNA shifts to rare intrinsic TH+ interneurons is unsupported.
* **WHAT:** Estimate cell-type marker expression profiles across the 30 samples and reframe the discussion to acknowledge bulk tissue heterogeneity.
* **HOW:** Use marker gene sets for striatal cell types (e.g., `DRD1`/`DRD2`/`PPP1R1B` for medium spiny neurons, `GFAP`/`AQP4` for astrocytes, `MOG`/`MBP` for oligodendrocytes, `AIF1`/`CX3CR1` for microglia, and `TH` for dopaminergic axons/interneurons) to test whether cell-type proportions differ between the 15 PD and 15 control samples, and remove causal claims about TH+ interneuron growth from the Abstract and Conclusion.

#### 4. Fix Data Provenance Contradictions, Sample Size Discrepancies, and Table Errors
* **WHY:** Internal inconsistencies in sample counts, ethics attribution, and BH-adjusted $p$-value ordering undermine the reliability of the reported pipeline.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Methods ("Source of data", Line 45):** Clarify why public GEO dataset `GSE20291` (originally generated from US brain bank samples by Zhang et al., 2005) is described as "including 15 of PD patients samples approved by the ethic committee of the Fourth Affiliated Hospital of China Medical University."
  2. **Methods ("Source of data", Line 47):** Reconcile the contradiction between "A total of 30 samples of putamen tissue were available (15 PD and 15 controls)" (Line 45) and "we obtained 12688 genes of the expression profile for the 35 samples" (Line 47).
  3. **Table 2 (Lines 124–127):** Verify and correct the Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted $p$-values: `hsa04070` has a larger raw $p$-value ($0.002342$) than `hsa04720` ($0.001217$), `hsa05010` ($0.001671$), and `hsa04912` ($0.002101$), yet is listed with a smaller BH-adjusted $p$-value ($0.059518$ vs. $0.061717$–$0.063907$).
  4. **Editorial fixes:** Correct "Total715" (Line 21), "interneuronsas" (Line 35), and "inhibitIP3-mediated" (Line 166).
