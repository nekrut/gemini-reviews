### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript applies a single-cell tumor mutational ordering algorithm (`SCITE`, termed the Mutational Order Approach or MOA) to reconstruct the early evolutionary history and most recent common ancestor (`proCoV2`) of SARS-CoV-2 across two GISAID snapshots: 29,681 genomes (`29KG`, July 7, 2020; 49 SNVs with global frequency $>1\%$) and 68,057 genomes (`68KG`, October 12, 2020; 84 SNVs), with spatiotemporal lineage tracking extended to 172,480 genomes (`172KG`, December 30, 2020). By polarizing the 49 common SNVs against three animal sarbecovirus outgroups (`RaTG13`, `RmYN02`, and pangolin `MT121216.1`) and ordering mutations via `SCITE`, the authors infer a progenitor genome (`proCoV2`) that differs from Wuhan-Hu-1 (`NC_045512`) by three substitutions ($\alpha_1$: `U18060C`, $\alpha_2$: `U8782C`, $\alpha_3$: `C28144U`) and gives rise to an even earlier-branching North American $\nu$ lineage defined by the absence of three near-universal variants ($\mu_1$: `2416C`, $\mu_2$: `19524C`, $\mu_3$: `23929C`). From this graph, they conclude that Wuhan-Hu-1 and all December 2019 Wuhan genomes were derived strains three to six mutational steps removed from `proCoV2`, that `proCoV2` circulated globally from late October 2019 (with a putative index case in September–November 2019), and that genomes matching `proCoV2` persisted in China and the United States into January–April 2020. Sequence alignments, the `proCoV2` FASTA, and an interactive web classifier (`sars2evo.datamonkey.org`) are publicly available.

While the browser-based haplotype tracking dashboard is a useful descriptive visualization of major 2020 clades, the paper's central evolutionary conclusion—that `proCoV2` (lacking $\alpha_1\text{--}\alpha_3$ and giving rise to the North American $\nu$ lineage at the root) is the true progenitor of SARS-CoV-2 and that December 2019 Wuhan genomes are derived offshoots—is a methodological artifact of three compounding errors: (1) `SCITE` assumes that variant frequencies in a sample monotonically reflect mutational age in a single clonal tumor, an assumption catastrophically violated by global SARS-CoV-2 sequencing where $>85\%$ of `29KG` genomes come from spring 2020 European and North American outbreaks rather than early winter 2019/2020 infections; (2) polarizing `18060` ($\alpha_1$), `8782` ($\alpha_2$), and `28144` ($\alpha_3$) using distant bat/pangolin outgroups ($\sim 1,150$ substitutions away) mistakes a derived `C18060U` transition in the February 2020 Washington State (`A.1` / WA1) outbreak for the ancestral human state, while ad-hoc post-hoc flipping of mutation orientations whenever the Co-Occurrence Index ($\text{COI}$) fell below $70\%$ introduces circularity; and (3) restricting the character matrix solely to SNVs with global frequency $>1\%$ strips out all private and clade-specific mutations ($<1\%$ global frequency) present in January–March 2020 sequences, artificially collapsing derived tip genomes onto the internal `proCoV2` root node and creating the false impression that unmutated `proCoV2` genomes were sampled in Washington State and China in 2020.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The progenitor of all human SARS-CoV-2 infections (`proCoV2`) differs from Wuhan-Hu-1 by three substitutions ($\alpha_1$: `18060`, $\alpha_2$: `8782`, $\alpha_3$: `28144`) and gave rise to an early-diverging North American $\nu$ lineage (`2416U, 19524U, 23929U`) before Wuhan-Hu-1 evolved: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Look closely at Table 1 and Figure 2: in the `29KG` dataset, $\mu_1$ (`2416C`), $\mu_2$ (`19524C`), and $\mu_3$ (`23929C`) have global frequencies of $98.1\%$, $98.6\%$, and $98.4\%$, and $\alpha_1$ (`18060C`) has a frequency of $95.1\%$. In reality, all December 2019 Wuhan genomes—and $>98\%$ of all human SARS-CoV-2 genomes—carry `2416C, 19524C, 23929C`, and `18060C`. The $\sim 4.9\%$ of genomes carrying `18060U` belong to the Washington State (`WA1` / Pango lineage `A.1`) outbreak that expanded in North America in February–March 2020, and the $\sim 1.5\%$ of genomes carrying `2416U, 19524U, 23929U` ($\nu$) were sampled in North America **59 days after Wuhan-1** (line 119). Because the bat/pangolin outgroups happen to carry `U` at site 18060 (a site subject to rapid C$\to$U hypermutation in humans), the authors coded `18060U` as the ancestral state `0` and `18060C` as the derived mutation $\alpha_1 = 1$. Fed into `SCITE`, this single outgroup homoplasy forces the February 2020 Washington State `A.1` haplotype (`18060U, 8782U, 28144C`) to be ancestral to `18060C` ($\alpha_1$), which in turn forces Wuhan-Hu-1 (`18060C, 8782C, 28144U`) to sit 6 sequential branches downstream of `proCoV2` ($\text{proCoV2} \to \mu_1 \to \mu_2 \to \mu_3 \to \alpha_1 \to \alpha_2 \to \alpha_3$). This topology requires that `proCoV2`, $\nu$, and $\alpha_1$-ancestors circulated invisibly for months without being sampled once in December 2019, only to appear in North America 1–2 months later.

* **Multiple genomes sampled in China and Washington State in January–March 2020 had "zero base differences" from `proCoV2` (Fig. 1c): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Figure 1c and line 119 claim that six genomes sampled in January 2020 in China and Washington State (plus one in New York in March 2020) show "no base differences" from `proCoV2`, whereas the 18 December 2019 Wuhan genomes have 3 differences. However, as stated on line 119, this comparison was evaluated **only across the 49 positions with global frequency $>1\%$ in the July 2020 GISAID database** ("A comparison of the proCoV2 genetic fingerprint (49 positions) in the 29KG collection..."). Any mutation present in $<297$ genomes worldwide as of July 7, 2020 (which includes nearly all mutations in early Asian lineages that did not seed the massive European/US spring waves) was excluded from the 49-site matrix. Consequently, derived January–March 2020 genomes that carry 1–5 private or low-frequency substitutions outside those 49 sites have their actual mutations erased by the $>1\%$ filter and are falsely plotted in Figure 1c as having 0 differences from `proCoV2`.

* **Selective post-hoc reversal of outgroup mutation orientations when $\text{COI} < 70\%$ validates the mutational graph: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Materials and Methods (lines 190–194 and part 2 lines 1–4), the authors state that they initially polarized all 49 sites using the bat/pangolin outgroup consensus, but whenever a variant had a Co-Occurrence Index ($\text{COI}$) below $70\%$ in the resulting graph (such as position 3037, where the outgroup has `U` but Wuhan-1 has `C`), they manually reversed the ancestral/derived coding (`0 <-> 1`) to maximize $\text{COI}$ and `SCITE` log-likelihood. Notice the severe asymmetry of this rule: at position 3037 (a `C->U` mutation in the European B.1 clade that reached 77% global frequency), the bat outgroup has `U`, which gave $\text{COI} = 60\%$, so the authors flipped `3037` from `U->C` to `C->U`. Yet at position 18060 ($\alpha_1$, where the bat outgroup also has `U` due to `C->U` homoplasy in the WA1 clade at 4.9% frequency), `U->C` happened to exceed the arbitrary $70\%$ $\text{COI}$ threshold because `18060C` is present in $95.1\%$ of genomes, so it was not flipped! Applying an arbitrary threshold rule that flips outgroup homoplasies at 77% frequency (`3037`) while retaining outgroup homoplasies at 95% frequency (`18060`, `2416`, `19524`, `23929`) guarantees that rare derived C$\to$U revertants are placed at the root.

* **Major mutational fingerprints ($\alpha, \alpha\beta, \alpha\beta\epsilon, \alpha\beta\gamma\delta, \alpha\beta\zeta$) capture the broad continental replacement dynamics of high-frequency SARS-CoV-2 clades during 2020: [Well Supported].**  
  Once past the mis-oriented root nodes ($\mu_{1\text{--}3}, \nu, \alpha_{1\text{--}3}$), the downstream branches of the graph ($\beta_{1\text{--}3}$ corresponding to Pango lineage B.1 / D614G; $\epsilon_{1\text{--}3}$ corresponding to B.1.1; $\gamma_1, \delta_1$ corresponding to B.1.GH / North American clade 20C; and $\zeta_{1\text{--}3}$ corresponding to 20A.EU1 / B.1.177) accurately track the major high-frequency clades of 2020 and show $>90\%$ concordance with Pango designations across 74 of 80 groups.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Re-Evaluate `proCoV2` Rooting Using Full-Genome Substitutions Rather Than the Truncated 49-SNV Matrix
* **WHY:** Filtering out all genomic positions with $<1\%$ global frequency in July 2020 erases the actual substitutions carried by early 2020 genomes, creating the illusion that January–March 2020 lineage `A` and `A.1` genomes are identical to the root while December 2019 lineage `B` genomes are 3 steps derived.
* **WHAT:** Compute and plot in Figure 1c the true **whole-genome** nucleotide distance (across all 29,115 aligned coding sites, not just the 49 high-frequency sites) between `proCoV2` and every genome sampled in December 2019 and January 2020.
* **HOW:** Count all single-nucleotide differences across the full 29,115-bp alignment for the 18 December 2019 genomes and the January 2020 China/Washington genomes against `proCoV2` and `Wuhan-Hu-1`, and report how many additional private mutations ($<1\%$ frequency) are present in the "0-difference" January/March 2020 genomes and in the $\nu$ lineage.

#### 2. Test Sensitivity of the Root to C$\to$U Hypermutation Homoplasy at Sites `18060`, `2416`, `19524`, and `23929`
* **WHY:** Host APOBEC-mediated C$\to$U hypermutation causes recurrent `C->U` transitions in SARS-CoV-2. When a human lineage acquires a derived `C->U` mutation at a site where the distant bat outgroup also has `U` (`18060`, `2416`, `19524`, `23929`), naive outgroup polarization misidentifies the derived human `U` allele as ancestral (`0`).
* **WHAT:** Test the likelihood and temporal concordance of the mutation graph when `18060`, `2416`, `19524`, and `23929` are polarized as `C->U` (matching the earliest December 2019 genomes and matching the authors' recoding of `3037 C->U`) rather than `U->C`.
* **HOW:** Recode $\mu_1$ (`2416`), $\mu_2$ (`19524`), $\mu_3$ (`23929`), and $\alpha_1$ (`18060`) with `C = 0` (ancestral) and `U = 1` (derived), re-run `SCITE`, and compare the resulting temporal concordance (specifically, whether the 59-day temporal inversion where $\nu$ and `A.1` appear months after December 2019 Wuhan-1 is resolved).

#### 3. Correct for Extreme Spatiotemporal Sampling Skew in `29KG` and `68KG` via Temporally Stratified Subsampling
* **WHY:** `SCITE` uses global variant frequencies in the input matrix to anchor mutation order, assuming a single panmictic clonal population. Because $>80\%$ of `29KG` sequences come from Europe and the US in March–July 2020 (Fig. 1b), a mutation (`C18060U`) that happened to seed the heavily sequenced Washington State outbreak reaches $4.9\%$ global frequency, whereas genuine December 2019/January 2020 Wuhan diversity falls below the $1\%$ cutoff and is discarded.
* **WHAT:** Repeat the variant selection ($>1\%$ threshold) and `SCITE` inference on a temporally and geographically balanced subsample of genomes (e.g., equal numbers of genomes per month or restricted to December 2019–February 2020).
* **HOW:** Subsample 500 genomes per fortnight (or analyze all genomes collected up to February 15, 2020), re-identify variants with $>1\%$ frequency in that early window, run `SCITE`, and compare the inferred root sequence against `proCoV2`.

#### 4. Temper Claims Regarding the Timing of the Index Case (`tIndex` in September–October 2019)
* **WHY:** The estimate that `proCoV2` existed 5.8–8.1 weeks before December 24, 2019 (late October 2019, with speculative `tIndex` in September 2019) depends entirely on counting $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$ as three sequential pre-Wuhan-1 evolutionary steps along a single lineage rather than side-branches (lineage A and A.1).
* **WHAT:** Reframe the `tMRCA` and `tIndex` calculations as conditional on the outgroup-polarized `SCITE` root, and explicitly acknowledge that if `C18060U` (`A.1`) and $\mu_{1\text{--}3}$ ($\nu$) are derived C$\to$U branches rather than pre-Wuhan ancestors, the 3-to-6-step pre-December-2019 branch length vanishes.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract and the section "Estimated Timing of MRCA and the Index Case" (lines 132–136) to present the molecular clock timing with and without the $\alpha_1$ (`18060`) step.
