### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript conducts a two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to evaluate whether genetic predisposition to COVID-19 susceptibility ($38,984$ cases; $n = 1,683,768$), hospitalization ($9,986$ cases; $n = 1,882,773$), or severe respiratory disease ($5,101$ cases; $n = 1,388,342$) has a causal effect on Graves disease (GD; $2,176$ hospitalized cases and $210,277$ controls from OpenGWAS dataset `bbj-a-123`). Selecting instrumental variables at a relaxed significance threshold ($P < 5 \times 10^{-6}$, clumped at $r^2 < 0.001$ using the 1000 Genomes European reference panel), the authors apply inverse-variance weighted (IVW), weighted median, MR-Egger, simple mode, and MR-PRESSO estimators across 19, 21, and 27 SNPs, respectively. Observing null IVW odds ratios for susceptibility ($\text{OR} = 0.989$, 95% CI $0.405\text{--}2.851$, $P = 0.93$), hospitalization ($\text{OR} = 0.974$, 95% CI $0.852\text{--}1.113$, $P = 0.70$), and severe COVID-19 ($\text{OR} = 0.979$, 95% CI $0.890\text{--}1.077$, $P = 0.66$), they conclude that COVID-19 does not cause the onset or exacerbation of GD, "alleviating concerns about a surge in autoimmune thyroid diseases due to the pandemic." Public OpenGWAS dataset identifiers are cited, though no analysis scripts or harmonized SNP instrument tables are included in the main manuscript.

This study is undermined by critical methodological and numerical flaws that invalidate its conclusions: (1) there is an unacknowledged continental ancestry mismatch between the exposure GWAS and LD clumping panel (European ancestry) and the GD outcome GWAS (`bbj-a-123`, which is BioBank Japan—an East Asian cohort), violating the fundamental two-sample MR assumption that exposure and outcome samples share the same underlying population LD structure; (2) the authors appear unaware that `bbj-a-123` is Japanese, mistakenly stating in the Limitations (line 181) that their analysis "primarily relied on data from the European population"; (3) Table 2 contains mathematically impossible confidence intervals for COVID-19 susceptibility—where the IVW 95% CI (`0.405–2.851`) is both asymmetric on the log-OR scale and $>4$-fold wider than the MR-Egger (`0.786–1.245`) and weighted median (`0.685–1.362`) 95% CIs on the exact same 19 SNPs; (4) the $F$-statistic formula in the Methods is algebraically corrupted and no $F$-statistics or statistical power calculations are reported; and (5) an underpowered, wide-CI null result for lifelong genetic liability to testing positive for SARS-CoV-2 cannot rule out acute post-viral thyroid autoimmunity. Correcting this work requires re-running the analysis with ancestry-matched GWAS summary statistics, fixing the corrupted Table 2 interval estimates, and performing formal power calculations.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Two-sample MR shows no causal effect of COVID-19 susceptibility on Graves disease ($\text{OR} = 0.989$, 95% CI $0.405\text{--}2.851$, $P = 0.93$), alleviating concerns about pandemic-associated GD: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the exposure GWAS (`COVID-19 Host Genetics Initiative` European release) and LD clumping panel (1000 Genomes EUR) are European, whereas the outcome GWAS (`bbj-a-123`, 2,176 cases and 210,277 controls) is from BioBank Japan (East Asian). Two-sample MR requires that instrumental variables tag causal variants with identical LD and minor allele frequencies in both samples; across European and East Asian populations, lead variants at COVID-19 loci (such as 3p21.31 *LZTFL1/SLC6A20*, *ABO*, *OAS1/2/3*, and *MHC*) exhibit major differences in allele frequency and LD structure. Second, even taking the reported IVW estimate ($\text{OR} = 0.989$, 95% CI $0.405\text{--}2.851$) at face value, a 95% confidence interval spanning a 2.5-fold protective effect (`0.405`) to a nearly 3-fold harmful effect (`2.851`) has virtually zero statistical power to rule out clinically meaningful risk (e.g., $\text{OR} = 1.5\text{--}2.0$). Conflating an uninformative confidence interval with proof of no effect ("alleviating concerns about a surge in autoimmune thyroid diseases") is a classic failure to distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.

* **The Table 2 MR estimates and 95% confidence intervals across IVW, weighted median, and MR-Egger are internally consistent: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Table 2 (lines 125–128) for COVID-19 susceptibility ($K = 19$ SNPs):
  1. For IVW, $\text{OR} = 0.989$ ($\ln \text{OR} = -0.011$), yet the 95% CI is reported as `0.405–2.851` ($\ln \text{CI} = [-0.904, +1.048]$). Because standard IVW computes a symmetric normal-theory interval on the log-odds scale ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{IVW}} \pm 1.96 \times \text{SE}$), the lower and upper log-bounds must be equidistant from $-0.011$, which `[-0.904, +1.048]` violates ($\exp(-0.011 + 0.893) = 2.416 \neq 2.851$).
  2. For MR-Egger on the exact same 19 SNPs, $\text{OR} = 1.047$ ($\ln \text{OR} = +0.046$) with 95% CI `0.786–1.245` ($\ln \text{CI} = [-0.241, +0.219]$). Not only is `[-0.241, +0.219]` also asymmetric around $+0.046$, its log-width ($0.460$) is **4.2 times narrower** than the IVW log-width ($1.952$) on the same 19 SNPs without heterogeneity (Cochran's $Q$ $P = 0.39$)! Mathematically, because MR-Egger estimates an additional intercept parameter and weights by $\sigma_{Yj}^{-2}$ across the variance of $\hat{\beta}_{Xj}$ rather than $\sum \hat{\beta}_{Xj}^2$, $\text{SE}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{Egger}})$ is strictly *larger* than $\text{SE}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{IVW}})$ when Cochran's $Q \le K - 1$ (as actually seen in columns 2 and 3 of Table 2, where MR-Egger CIs are wider than IVW CIs). This indicates a transcription or calculation error in Table 2 column 1.

* **COVID-19 hospitalization ($\text{OR} = 0.974$, $P = 0.70$) and severity ($\text{OR} = 0.979$, $P = 0.66$) have no causal effect on Graves disease onset or exacerbation: [Fragile].**  
  Beyond the European-to-Japanese ancestry mismatch, two-sample MR using genetic instruments for *hospitalized or critical COVID-19* tests whether lifelong carriage of host alleles associated with severe pulmonary/systemic COVID-19 alters baseline population incidence of hospitalized GD in a pre-pandemic biobank cohort (`bbj-a-123` was recruited in BioBank Japan between 2003 and 2018, **before SARS-CoV-2 existed in humans**!). Because zero individuals in the `bbj-a-123` GD outcome cohort had ever been exposed to SARS-CoV-2 at the time their GD was ascertained, genetic variants that only influence GD *conditional on contracting SARS-CoV-2 infection* (gene-by-environment interaction) cannot produce an association in a pre-pandemic outcome cohort! Thus, the study design is biologically incapable of testing whether actual SARS-CoV-2 infection triggers GD onset or exacerbation.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Resolve the Pre-Pandemic Outcome Ascertainment Fallacy and Ancestry Mismatch
* **WHY:** Testing whether SARS-CoV-2 infection causes Graves disease by looking up COVID-19 host susceptibility SNPs in a pre-pandemic Japanese biobank (`bbj-a-123`, collected prior to 2019) is both ancestrally mismatched (EUR vs. EAS) and epidemiologically invalid (in a pre-2019 cohort, the environmental exposure—SARS-CoV-2 infection—was zero, so instruments for viral susceptibility cannot act *through* SARS-CoV-2 infection to cause GD; at most, they test shared baseline host immune pleiotropy).
* **WHAT:** Match exposure and outcome ancestry (using either European GD GWAS data from FinnGen / UK Biobank alongside European COVID-19 HGI data, or East Asian COVID-19 HGI data alongside `bbj-a-123`), and reframe the research question to state accurately that MR in pre-pandemic cohorts tests *shared genetic liability / pleiotropy* between COVID-19 host susceptibility and GD rather than post-infection viral triggering.
* **HOW:** Extract European GD summary statistics (e.g., FinnGen `E4_GRAVES_STRICT`) to match the European COVID-19 GWAS and 1000G EUR clumping panel, re-run `TwoSampleMR`, and explicitly state in the Abstract, Introduction, and Discussion that pre-pandemic GD cohorts test shared genetic architecture rather than acute post-COVID-19 sequelae.

#### 2. Audit and Correct the Mathematically Inconsistent Confidence Intervals in Table 2
* **WHY:** Reporting an IVW 95% CI of `0.405–2.851` alongside a much narrower MR-Egger 95% CI of `0.786–1.245` (both asymmetric on the log scale) for COVID-19 susceptibility in Table 2 indicates corrupted numbers.
* **WHAT:** Re-compute $\hat{\beta}$, $\text{SE}(\hat{\beta})$, $\text{OR} = \exp(\hat{\beta})$, and $\text{95\% CI} = \exp(\hat{\beta} \pm 1.96\,\text{SE})$ directly from `TwoSampleMR::mr()` output for all cells in Table 2.
* **HOW:** Verify that $\sqrt{\text{OR}_{\text{lower}} \times \text{OR}_{\text{upper}}} = \text{OR}_{\text{point}}$ holds to three decimal places for every row and column of Table 2, and publish the complete 19-, 21-, and 27-SNP harmonized instrument tables as Supplementary Tables.

#### 3. Fix the $F$-Statistic Equation and Report Instrument $R^2$, $F$-Statistics, and Minimum Detectable Odds Ratios
* **WHY:** In line 76, the formula $F = (R^2 / K) / [(1 - R^2)(N - K - 1)]$ places $(N - K - 1)$ in the denominator instead of the numerator (or misses a division slash), and no $F$-statistics or power calculations are reported anywhere in Results.
* **WHAT:** Correct the formula and report the total phenotypic variance explained ($R^2$), mean/minimum SNP $F$-statistic, and the minimum detectable odds ratio at 80% power ($\alpha = 0.05$) for each of the three exposures.
* **HOW:** Correct the equation on line 76 to $F = \frac{R^2 (N - K - 1)}{(1 - R^2) K}$, add $R^2$ and $F$ columns to Table 2, and compute power in R using `mRnd` or non-central $\chi^2$ (`pchisq(qchisq(0.95, 1), df=1, ncp = N_outcome * prop_cases * (1 - prop_cases) * R2_exposure * (log(OR))^2)`).
