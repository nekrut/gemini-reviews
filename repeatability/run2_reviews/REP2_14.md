### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study systematically characterizes bacterial DNA contamination originating from commercial DNA extraction kits and laboratory reagents, quantifying its impact on both PCR-based 16S rRNA gene amplicon surveys and PCR-free shotgun metagenomics across high- to low-biomass regimes. Using five serial ten-fold dilutions ($10^8$ down to $10^3$ cells) of a pure *Salmonella bongori* culture processed in parallel across three independent institutes (WTSI, ICL, UB) with distinct batches of the FastDNA SPIN Kit for Soil (at 20 and 40 PCR cycles), alongside 16S qPCR and shotgun metagenomics across four commercial extraction kits (FastDNA, MoBio UltraClean, QIAamp Stool, and PSP Spin Stool), the authors demonstrate that reagent-derived bacterial DNA plateaus at $\sim 500$ 16S rRNA gene copies per $\mu$L of eluate and dominates sequencing readouts whenever input biomass falls below $\sim 10^3\text{--}10^4$ cells. They then present a longitudinal clinical case study of 182 infant nasopharyngeal swabs from a Thailand–Burma cohort, showing that an apparent age-dependent PCoA succession of the infant microbiota was an artifact of extracting chronologically ordered samples with four sequential kit lots; removing kit-associated OTUs or re-extracting archived aliquots with a clean lot completely abolished the clustering. Raw sequencing datasets are publicly deposited in the European Nucleotide Archive (`ERP006737` and `ERP006808`) with sample-level accession mappings in Additional file 1.

This is an exemplary experimental benchmark that establishes a foundational standard for microbiome quality control. By combining a known monoclonal spike-in dilution series with multi-institute batch replication, absolute qPCR quantification, PCR-free shotgun metagenomics, and re-extraction of clinical specimens, the study rules out alternative explanations (such as PCR-only contamination or skin/airborne laboratory fallout alone) and proves that extraction kit reagents harbor lot-specific background microbiota. The conclusions are strictly proportional to the data, and the practical guidelines (Box 1) are directly actionable. Minor technical limitations include: (1) single extraction replicates per dilution step within each institute/kit combination for the sequencing assays (though qPCR was performed in triplicate); (2) very low shotgun read recovery (<210 reads beyond dilution 2) for the MoBio UltraClean kit due to unnormalized Nextera XT library pooling; and (3) shallow rarefaction depth (200 reads per sample) in the 454 nasopharyngeal dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Contaminating bacterial DNA in commercial extraction kits dominates both 16S rRNA amplicon and shotgun metagenomic profiles when input biomass falls below $10^3\text{--}10^4$ cells: [Well Supported].**  
  Across all three institutes in the 16S MiSeq dilution series (Fig. 1 and Fig. S1) and all four commercial kits in the PCR-free shotgun MiSeq series (Fig. 3), *S. bongori* comprises nearly 100% of reads at $10^8\text{--}10^7$ cells, progressively declines at intermediate dilutions, and is overtaken (>50%–90% non-*Salmonella* reads) at $10^4\text{--}10^3$ input cells. Orthogonal 16S qPCR (Fig. 2) independently confirms why: total 16S copy number decreases log-linearly over the first three dilutions before hitting a floor of $\sim 500$ copies/$\mu$L of extraction eluate, whereas template-free qPCR water controls remain below the detection limit (45 copies), localizing the background DNA to the extraction columns/buffers.

* **Contaminant taxonomic profiles vary systematically between kit manufacturers and between production lots of the same kit: [Well Supported].**  
  When identical *S. bongori* dilutions were extracted concurrently in the same laboratory (UB) using four different manufacturers' kits and sequenced by shotgun metagenomics, each kit yielded a distinct taxonomic signature (Fig. 3b): FastDNA was dominated by *Burkholderiaceae* and *Enterobacteriaceae*, PSP by *Bradyrhizobiaceae* and *Chitinophagaceae*, and QIAamp by a diverse mixture including *Bacillaceae*, *Microbacteriaceae*, and *Flavobacteriaceae*. Similarly, extracting the same dilutions across three production lots of the FastDNA kit (#38098, #15447, #30252) revealed 63 extraction-specific taxa (>0.1% abundance, absent from PCR water blanks) that differed reproducibly across lots (Fig. 1b, Fig. S2).

* **Chronological processing of clinical low-biomass samples across different extraction kit lots creates spurious biological associations that mimic longitudinal microbiota development: [Well Supported].**  
  In the Maela infant nasopharyngeal cohort (182 swabs across 20 infants), initial Jaccard PCoA ordination separated early-life swabs from later time points (Fig. 4a). Re-coloring the exact same ordination by the four FastDNA kit lots used in chronological sequence (Fig. 4b) and testing lot associations via `metastats` ($P < 0.01$, Table S2) showed that lots 1 and 2 contributed the majority of total reads via soil/water genera (*Achromobacter*, *Brevundimonas*, *Herbaspirillum*, *Ochrobactrum*, *Sphingomonas*, *Stenotrophomonas*). Crucially, the authors went beyond computational filtering (Fig. 4c) and re-extracted new aliquots from the original archived swabs using an independent kit lot, confirming that the putative "founder" taxa were absent from the biological specimens.

* **The MoBio UltraClean kit contains lower background contamination than the other three commercial kits tested in shotgun metagenomics: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In the shotgun experiment, the MoBio (MB) kit yielded only 210 reads at dilution 2, 79 reads at dilution 3, and <20 reads at dilutions 4–5 (Table S1b). While low read recovery in equimolar-pooled or volume-pooled Nextera XT libraries is consistent with lower total background DNA concentration in the eluate, Nextera XT tagmentation efficiency is also sensitive to buffer salt/EDTA carryover and high fragment-to-transposome ratios at picogram DNA inputs. Without parallel qPCR quantification of the MB, QIA, and PSP eluates, lower background DNA in MB is plausible but not quantitatively proven.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Add 16S qPCR Quantification Across All Four Extraction Kits Used in the Shotgun Experiment
* **WHY:** Currently, absolute background copy-number quantification by 16S qPCR (Fig. 2) is shown only for FastDNA lot #15447, whereas the comparison across the four commercial kits (FP, MB, QIA, PSP) relies on semi-quantitative shotgun read counts.
* **WHAT:** Report the qPCR 16S copy numbers (or Qubit/PicoGreen fluorescence values referenced in Methods line 173) for the dilution series and blank extractions across all four kits (FP, MB, QIA, PSP).
* **HOW:** Tabulate the measured Qubit concentrations and/or 16S qPCR $C_t$ values for each kit's dilution series alongside the read counts in Additional file 1: Table S1b to confirm whether MB's low read count reflects lower background DNA copy number rather than tagmentation inhibition.

#### 2. Quantify Variance Across Technical Extraction Replicates Within a Single Kit Lot
* **WHY:** Each dilution step of *S. bongori* was extracted once per institute/kit lot (`n = 1` per dilution per lot), so the consistency across the 6 dilution tubes within each lot serves as the primary measure of within-lot reproducibility.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly quantify the within-lot taxonomic consistency (e.g., Bray–Curtis or Jaccard similarity of non-*Salmonella* contaminant reads across dilutions 1–5 within each lot) versus between-lot dissimilarity.
* **HOW:** Compute pairwise Bray–Curtis distances on the non-*Salmonella* genus table (Fig. 1b and Fig. 3b) and run a PERMANOVA (`adonis` in R `vegan`) partitioning variance between `Kit_Lot / Manufacturer` and `Dilution_Factor`, reporting $R^2$ and permutation $P$-values in the Results text.

#### 3. Check for Index Hopping / Barcode Cross-Talk in Multiplexed MiSeq Runs
* **WHY:** When high-biomass pure *S. bongori* libraries ($10^8$ cells) are multiplexed on the same Illumina MiSeq flow cell as negative blank controls (`con`), trace index misassignment or adapter cross-contamination (~0.1%–0.5% of reads) can deposit *S. bongori* reads into blank controls or vice versa.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact count and percentage of *S. bongori* reads observed in the sequenced negative controls (`con` in Fig. 1a and `water` in Fig. 3a).
* **HOW:** Add a column to Table S1a and Table S1b listing the exact number of *S. bongori* reads vs. non-*Salmonella* reads in every sample and blank control to allow readers to distinguish reagent background from on-sequencer index bleed-through.
