### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript models the past (Last Interglacial [LIG] and Middle Holocene [MH]), current, and future (2050s and 2070s under RCP2.6, RCP4.5, and RCP8.5) potential distribution of the endangered Chinese endemic tree *Sinojackia xylocarpa* (Styracaceae) using the `biomod2` ensemble modeling package in R, and overlays the predicted current suitable range with China's national and provincial nature reserves. Combining 2022–2024 field surveys with herbarium and literature records, the authors compile 21 spatially rarefied ($1\text{ km} \times 1\text{ km}$) wild occurrence points across southeastern China. Selecting six algorithms with $\text{AUC} > 0.8$ and $\text{TSS} > 0.7$ (ANN, CTA, FDA, GBM, MaxEnt, RF), they report an ensemble $\text{AUC} = 0.9960$ and $\text{TSS} = 0.9500$, identify precipitation of the driest quarter (Bio17; $61.0\%$ contribution) as the primary driver of current distribution, estimate a current suitable habitat of $697,200\text{ km}^2$ ($7.26\%$ of China's land area, of which only $3.91\%$ lies within national or provincial nature reserves), and project an average $10.97\%$ habitat reduction under future climate scenarios. The 21 occurrence coordinates are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

The field survey verification of 21 extant wild localities of *S. xylocarpa* across Jiangsu, Anhui, Zhejiang, and Hunan and the reserve gap overlay address a practical conservation question. However, the species distribution modeling pipeline suffers from critical methodological and statistical flaws: (1) **severe overparameterization ($p > n$)**, where 22 environmental predictors are fitted to only $15\text{--}16$ training presence points ($75\%$ of $N=21$) across complex machine learning algorithms (ANN, RF, GBM, MaxEnt); (2) **invalid paleoclimate refitting and variable mismatch**, where instead of projecting a contemporary climate-calibrated model onto paleoclimate layers, the authors separately refit the 21 modern occurrence coordinates directly to LIG (10 bioclimatic variables) and MH (11 different bioclimatic variables) rasters while using 22 variables (including topography and 11 soil variables) for the current/future periods; (3) **mathematically impossible summary statistics** in Table 2 (an ensemble AUC of $0.9960 \pm 0.0641$ across 10 replicates on $[0,1]$, where the theoretical maximum possible SD for mean $0.9960$ is $0.0126$) and inflated discrimination from sampling 1,000 pseudo-absences across all of China; and (4) **direct self-contradictions** between Results and Discussion regarding whether habitat contracted or expanded from LIG to MH and the direction of future centroid shifts, alongside misidentifying CMIP5 models/RCPs as CMIP6 and dating the Last Interglacial to $12,000\text{--}14,000$ years ago. These issues can be resolved by re-running the `biomod2` pipeline with a parsimonious, time-invariant predictor set, buffered pseudo-absence sampling, and corrected text/tables.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Only $3.91\%$ of the predicted current suitable region of *S. xylocarpa* ($697,200\text{ km}^2$ in southeastern China) falls within existing national ($1.84\%$) or provincial ($2.15\%$) nature reserves: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The bioclimatic envelope generally captures the subtropical hilly region of southeastern China where the 21 wild populations occur, and overlaying this region with the World Database on Protected Areas / Ministry of Ecology and Environment reserve polygons provides a useful first-order approximation of low formal reserve coverage in human-impacted low-elevation (<392 m) hills. However, the exact area ($697,200\text{ km}^2$) is sensitive to model overparameterization and the nationwide background extent used to calibrate the `maxSSS` threshold ($0.1916$).

* **The six-model `biomod2` ensemble achieves near-perfect predictive accuracy ($\text{AUC} = 0.9960 \pm 0.0641$, $\text{TSS} = 0.9500 \pm 0.0610$; Table 2): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, training six flexible classifiers (ANN, CTA, FDA, GBM, MaxEnt, RF) on 22 predictor variables using only $15\text{--}16$ training presence points ($75\%$ of $N=21$) violates minimum sample-to-predictor ratios ($\ge 10$ presences per predictor) by more than an order of magnitude, resulting in severe overfitting to the training points. Second, generating 1,000 random pseudo-absences across the entire territory of China ($9.6\text{ million km}^2$, spanning arid northwest deserts, the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau, and boreal northeast China) trivially inflates AUC and TSS because any coarse macroclimatic split separates subtropical southeastern China from continental/alpine China. Third, the reported standard deviation in Table 2 ($\text{AUC} = 0.9960 \pm 0.0641$ across 10 runs) is mathematically impossible: for 10 numbers bounded in $[0, 1]$ with a mean of $0.9960$, the maximum mathematically possible sample standard deviation (achieved when nine runs equal $1.0000$ and one run equals $0.9600$) is $0.01265$, five times smaller than $0.0641$.

* **Climatic drivers of *S. xylocarpa* shifted over time (from Bio10/Bio17/Bio4 in the Last Interglacial and Bio15/Bio17/Bio11 in the Middle Holocene to Bio17 [61.0%] in the current period; Table 1), accompanied by habitat contraction/expansion across paleoclimatic epochs: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In classical hindcasting, a single SDM is calibrated on present-day occurrences and present-day climate variables (using an identical set of available bioclimatic predictors) and then *projected* onto paleoclimate layers. Instead, the authors took the 21 present-day GPS coordinates, extracted paleoclimate values at those coordinates for the LIG and MH epochs, performed separate correlation filtering yielding three mutually inconsistent predictor sets—10 bioclimatic variables for LIG, 11 different bioclimatic variables for MH, and 22 bioclimatic + topographic + soil variables for Current/Future (Table 1)—and trained separate models for each epoch. Refitting modern occurrence coordinates directly to paleoclimate layers assumes the species occupied the exact same 21 grid cells 6,000 and 120,000 years ago, and changing the predictor set across epochs (e.g., dropping Bio10 in MH and dropping Bio11 and Bio13 in Current while adding 13 soil/topography variables) makes variable contribution percentages and predicted areas across LIG, MH, and Current statistically non-comparable.

* **Suitable habitat contracted continuously from the Last Interglacial to the Middle Holocene, will decline by an average of $10.97\%$ in the 2050s–2070s, and its centroid will shift northeastward under all RCP scenarios: [Overstated].**  
  The manuscript directly contradicts itself on both paleoclimatic and future trajectories:
  1. In Results Section 3.4 (p. 9), the authors state: "the results show a **continuous contraction** and fragmentation of suitable habitats from the Last Interglacial to the Middle Holocene." Yet Table 3 and Discussion Section 4.4 (p. 11) report that suitable habitat **expanded** from $63.84 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$ (LIG) to $64.50 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$ (MH) and $69.72 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$ (Current).
  2. In Results Section 3.6 (p. 10) and the Abstract, the authors state: "In future projections, the centroid generally moves **northeast under all RCP scenarios**." Yet four sentences earlier in Section 3.6, they report that under RCP2.6 (2050s) the centroid shifts $147.91\text{ km}$ **northwest**, under RCP2.6 (2070s) it shifts $134.57\text{ km}$ **north**, and under RCP8.5 (2070s) it shifts $413.29\text{ km}$ **northwest** to $111.412^\circ\text{E}, 30.402^\circ\text{N}$.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reduce Predictor Dimensionality and Restrict Pseudo-Absence Sampling to an Accessible Biogeographic Buffer
* **WHY:** Fitting 22 variables to 15–16 training points causes severe overfitting, and sampling pseudo-absences across all of China artificially inflates AUC/TSS and distorts the `maxSSS` threshold.
* **WHAT:** Reduce the predictor set to at most 3–4 ecologically interpretable, non-collinear variables ($|r| < 0.7$, $\text{VIF} < 5$) and sample pseudo-absences within a $200\text{--}500\text{ km}$ buffer (or subtropical eastern China ecoregions) around known occurrences.
* **HOW:** Using the 21 occurrence points, perform variance inflation factor (`usdm::vifstep` in R) and jackknife importance screening to retain the top 3–4 predictors (e.g., Bio17, Bio10, Bio18, and Elevation), achieving at least $\sim 5$ presences per predictor. Generate 3 sets of 500–1,000 pseudo-absences restricted to the subtropical monsoon zone of eastern/central China (south of the Qinling–Huaihe line), and evaluate models using spatially blocked cross-validation (`blockCV` or `ENMeval` leave-one-out / 5-fold spatial blocks) rather than random 75/25 splits.

#### 2. Calibrate a Single Present-Day Climatic Model for Both Hindcasting (LIG/MH) and Forecasting (2050s/2070s)
* **WHY:** Training separate models with different variable sets (10 variables in LIG, 11 in MH, 22 in Current/Future) by matching present-day GPS coordinates to paleoclimate rasters is methodologically invalid.
* **WHAT:** Calibrate a single present-day model using a harmonized set of bioclimatic (+ static topographic) predictors available across all epochs (LIG, MH, Current, 2050s, 2070s), and project that single calibrated model onto the LIG, MH, and future climate layers.
* **HOW:** In `biomod2`, fit `BIOMOD_Modeling` and `BIOMOD_EnsembleModeling` *only* on the Current occurrence and environmental data (using the harmonized 3–4 bioclimatic/elevation variables). Then call `BIOMOD_Projection` and `BIOMOD_EnsembleForecasting` by passing the LIG, MH, 2050s, and 2070s raster stacks as `new.env`. Remove the separate LIG and MH variable contribution columns from Table 1.

#### 3. Audit and Correct the Ensemble AUC/TSS Standard Deviations in Table 2
* **WHY:** An ensemble AUC of $0.9960 \pm 0.0641$ is mathematically impossible for values bounded in $[0, 1]$.
* **WHAT:** Recompute the mean and standard deviation of out-of-fold test AUC and TSS across the 10 cross-validation runs for both the individual algorithms and the ensemble model.
* **HOW:** Extract the testing evaluation scores (`get_evaluations(myBiomodEM, metric.eval = c("ROC", "TSS"))`) across the 10 cross-validation replicates in R, verify that all replicate values lie in $[0, 1]$, and update Table 2 with the exact mean $\pm$ SD.

#### 4. Reconcile Textual Contradictions and Correct Technical, Chronological, and Cross-Reference Errors
* **WHY:** Multiple factual errors and broken figure/table citations undermine the reliability of the manuscript.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Last Interglacial Chronology Error (Section 2.2, p. 4):** Change `the Last Interglacial period, approximately 12,000–14,000 years ago` to `approximately 120,000–140,000 years ago`.
  2. **CMIP5 vs. CMIP6 Misidentification (Section 2.2, p. 4):** The models (`BCC-CSM1-1`, `CCSM4`, `MIROC-ESM`) and emission pathways (`RCP2.6`, `RCP4.5`, `RCP8.5`) are from **CMIP5** (WorldClim v1.4), not `Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6 (CMIP6)` (which uses SSP scenarios and updated GCM versions). Correct `CMIP6` to `CMIP5` (or re-run projections using actual CMIP6 SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5 layers from WorldClim v2.1).
  3. **Contradiction on LIG-to-MH Habitat Change (Section 3.4, p. 9 vs. Table 3 & Section 4.4, p. 11):** In Section 3.4, change `continuous contraction` to `slight expansion from 638,400 km^2 in the Last Interglacial to 645,000 km^2 in the Middle Holocene and 697,200 km^2 at present, accompanied by increasing spatial fragmentation` to match Table 3 and Section 4.4.
  4. **Contradiction on Future Centroid Shift Direction (Abstract & Section 3.6, p. 10):** Revise the claim that the centroid moves `northeast under all RCP scenarios` to accurately state that it shifts northeast under RCP4.5 and 2050s RCP8.5, north/northwest under RCP2.6, and northwest under 2070s RCP8.5.
  5. **Broken Figure and Table Citations:**
     - **Section 1 (p. 2) and Section 2.1 (p. 3):** In Figure 1, panels `(a)` and `(b)` show trees in Nanjing and Wuwei, while `(c)` and `(d)` show flowers and fruits. Fix Section 1 (`Figure 1 c,d` $\to$ `Figure 1 a,b`) and Section 2.1 (`Figure 1 a` and `Figure 1 b` $\to$ `Figure 1 c` and `Figure 1 d`).
     - **Section 4.4 (p. 11):** Fix `(Figure 4)` $\to$ `(Figure 5)` for LIG/MH maps; fix `(Figure 5)` $\to$ `(Figure 6)` for future maps; fix `(Figure 6)` $\to$ `(Figure 7)` for centroid shifts; and fix `(Table 2)` $\to$ `(Table 1)` for Bio17/Bio10 variable contributions.
