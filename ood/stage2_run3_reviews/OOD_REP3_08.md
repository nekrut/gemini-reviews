### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript adapts the Linear Predictive Coding EEG Algorithm for Parkinson's Disease (LEAPD) to generate a continuous neurophysiological index of cognitive function from approximately 2.5–3 minutes of eyes-open resting-state 60-channel scalp EEG. Using a discovery cohort of 100 Parkinson's disease (PD) patients (recorded ON dopaminergic medication) and 49 community controls, the authors fit autoregressive linear predictive coding (LPC) models to bandpass-filtered EEG signals, construct principal-component affine subspaces for cognitively impaired ($\text{MoCA} < 26$, $n=64$) and cognitively normal ($\text{MoCA} \ge 26$, $n=85$) groups, and combine single-electrode distance ratios across eight selected electrodes via a geometric mean. In cross-validation on the discovery cohort ($N=149$), the combined 8-electrode LEAPD index correlates with MoCA ($\rho = 0.68\text{--}0.70$, $p < 0.001$) and five NIH Toolbox domain scores ($\rho = 0.57\text{--}0.70$), and classifies MoCA-defined cognitive impairment with $\text{AUC} = 0.90\text{--}0.91$ and $79.9\%$ accuracy. In an external validation cohort of 32 PD patients (NCT03808675), the fixed 8-electrode model achieves $\rho = 0.68$ ($p < 0.001$) with MoCA and $\text{AUC} = 0.92$, though classification accuracy drops to $71.9\%$ ($50\%$ sensitivity, $100\%$ specificity) at the predefined $0.5$ threshold. Raw EEG data (OpenNeuro `ds004584`) and analysis code (GitHub) are publicly available.

While the external validation on 32 independent PD patients provides encouraging evidence that LPC-derived spectral profiles capture genuine variance associated with MoCA scores, the internal cross-validation and NIH Toolbox analyses suffer from a major methodological flaw: exhaustive hyperparameter optimization (bandpass frequency range across $2\text{--}34\text{ Hz}$, LPC order $K \in [2, 10]$, PCA subspace dimension $n$, and selection of the top 8 out of 60 electrodes) was performed on the entire 149-subject discovery dataset before holding those parameters fixed during subsequent leave-one-out (LOOCV), 5-fold, and 10-fold cross-validation. Additional issues include the complete absence of ocular and myogenic artifact removal in eyes-open EEG recordings, a significant age difference between cognitively impaired and unimpaired groups ($71.68 \pm 7.80$ vs. $67.53 \pm 7.66\text{ years}$, $p < 0.001$) that is not controlled for during subspace construction, ordinary least squares regression applied to a bounded non-Gaussian variable, and matrix dimension errors in the formal mathematical derivation. These limitations can be resolved through computational re-analysis (nested cross-validation, artifact-cleaned sensitivity checks, and age-matched subspace training) using the existing dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The 8-electrode LEAPD index correlates strongly with MoCA scores in out-of-sample PD patients ($\rho = 0.68$, $p < 0.001$, $\text{AUC} = 0.92$ in $N = 32$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Evaluating the locked 8-electrode model on the independent 32-patient exercise trial cohort avoids the hyperparameter selection leakage present in the discovery set and confirms substantial rank correlation with MoCA. However, at the prespecified classification threshold of $\rho_{\text{combined}} = 0.5$, sensitivity in the external cohort falls to $50.0\%$ ($9/18$ impaired patients detected, Table 2), and the external cohort has a restricted MoCA range ($22\text{--}30$) compared with the discovery PD cohort ($9\text{--}30$).

* **Internal cross-validation ($\rho = 0.68\text{--}0.70$, $80.4\%$ accuracy across LOOCV, 5-fold, and 10-fold CV) and randomization tests prove the absence of overfitting in the discovery cohort ($N = 149$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  As stated in the Methods ("LEAPD: EEG-based feature for cognition" and "LEAPD: theoretical framework for indexing cognition"), the authors used all 149 participants in an initial 10-fold search to exhaustively optimize four sets of hyperparameters per electrode—bandpass filter cutoffs within $2\text{--}34\text{ Hz}$, LPC order $K \in [2, 10]$, affine subspace dimensionality $n$, and the subset of 8 best-performing electrodes (Fig. 2c, d)—and then "kept them unchanged for all analyses and performance evaluations across all participants." Re-evaluating fixed hyperparameters and electrodes selected on the full 149-participant dataset inside LOOCV, 5-fold, and 10-fold CV constitutes in-sample feature/hyperparameter leakage. Similarly, shuffling MoCA labels while holding the pre-selected hyperparameters fixed does not measure the null distribution of the full exhaustive search procedure.

* **LEAPD generalizes across specific cognitive domains measured by the NIH Toolbox ($\rho = 0.56\text{--}0.70$ for PVT, PCPST, DCCST, FICAT, and PSMT): [Fragile].**  
  Unlike MoCA, the NIH Toolbox measures were not collected in the 32-patient external validation cohort (Table 1 and Table 2). Because the NIH Toolbox evaluations rely exclusively on internal cross-validation within the discovery cohort ($n = 139\text{--}140$ with available data) using median-split training groups and in-sample parameter optimization, their reported effect sizes are vulnerable to optimistic selection bias. Moreover, in control participants, LEAPD shows no significant correlation with processing speed (PCPST $\rho = 0.15$) or episodic memory (PSMT $\rho = 0.02$; Table 2).

* **LEAPD captures neurophysiological cortical oscillatory shifts independent of artifacts and demographic confounders: [Overstated].**  
  First, the EEG preprocessing pipeline only applies amplitude normalization and FFT notch removal at 60, 180, and 200 Hz, explicitly noting that "no further manual inspection, cleaning, or artifact removal was conducted." In eyes-open resting-state recordings, blink rate, horizontal eye movements, frontalis/temporalis muscle tension, and PD tremor strongly contaminate delta, theta, and beta power—including frontal electrode F4 and parieto-occipital electrodes PO7/O2. Second, the cognitively impaired group in the discovery set is significantly older than the cognitively normal group ($71.68 \pm 7.80$ vs. $67.53 \pm 7.66\text{ years}$, $p < 0.001$; Table 1). Because normal aging slows the posterior alpha peak and alters broadband $1/f$ spectral slopes, training the impaired vs. unimpaired affine subspaces on unadjusted groups bakes age-related spectral slowing directly into the LEAPD index, even if age is later included as a covariate in post-hoc partial correlations.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Implement Strict Nested Cross-Validation for All Discovery-Set Evaluations
* **WHY:** Selecting the optimal bandpass frequency bounds, LPC order $K$, PCA subspace dimension $n$, and top 8 electrodes across all 149 subjects prior to running LOOCV/k-fold CV leaks test-fold label information into the model specification and inflates internal performance metrics.
* **WHAT:** Re-compute all discovery-set correlations, regression $R^2$, and classification metrics (Table 2, Fig. 3e, Supplementary Tables 1–2) using fully nested cross-validation, and run the randomization test across the entire optimization pipeline.
* **HOW:** Within each outer fold of a 10-fold CV (holding out $10\%$ of subjects strictly as test data), run an inner 10-fold CV on the remaining $90\%$ of subjects to perform the grid search over bandpass frequencies ($2\text{--}34\text{ Hz}$), LPC order ($2\text{--}10$), and subspace dimension, rank the 60 electrodes solely on the inner-fold Spearman's $\rho$, select the top 8 electrodes, fit the affine subspaces $P_1, P_2$ on the outer training fold, and evaluate on the held-out outer test fold. For the permutation test, shuffle MoCA and NIH Toolbox scores *before* running the inner-fold grid search and electrode selection.

#### 2. Control for Ocular/Myogenic Artifacts and Age Confounding in Subspace Construction
* **WHY:** Uncleaned eye blinks, oculomotor drift, and muscle/tremor artifacts in eyes-open EEG can drive low-frequency ($2\text{--}10\text{ Hz}$) and beta-band LPC coefficients, and the $4.15$-year age gap between impaired and normal groups ($p < 0.001$) confounds the learned affine subspaces.
* **WHAT:** Demonstrate that LEAPD performance persists after automated independent component analysis (ICA) artifact rejection and when training subspaces on age-matched subgroups.
* **HOW:** Run an automated artifact rejection pipeline (e.g., `ICLabel` in EEGLAB/MNE-Python to remove blink, saccade, and muscle components with probability $> 0.80$) on the 149 discovery and 32 validation recordings and report the resulting nested-CV and out-of-sample metrics. Additionally, residualize the LPC coefficient vectors $a_{c,i}$ against age (using coefficients estimated on the training fold only) or propensity-match the impaired and unimpaired training subgroups on age before computing SVD in Equations (2)–(4).

#### 3. Replace Ordinary Least Squares Regression on Bounded LEAPD Scores with Proper Calibration
* **WHY:** In Table 2 and Fig. 3a, the regression models report $\text{RMSE} = 0.09\text{--}0.12$ and negative AIC values ($-226.40$ to $-55.32$) because the bounded non-Gaussian LEAPD index ($\rho \in [0, 1]$) is used as the dependent variable in ordinary least squares regression, violating Gaussian homoscedastic error assumptions. Furthermore, in clinically predicting cognition from EEG, MoCA should be the response variable predicted from the EEG biomarker, not the predictor.
* **WHAT:** Model MoCA (in native $0\text{--}30$ units) as the outcome predicted by the LEAPD index, and calibrate the classification threshold on the training set rather than hardcoding $0.5$.
* **HOW:** Fit models predicting $\text{MoCA}$ from $\rho_{\text{combined}}$ (reporting RMSE in MoCA points, which should be $\approx 2\text{--}3$ points) or use beta regression (`betareg` in R) if modeling $\rho_{\text{combined}} \in (0, 1)$. For binary classification on the 32-patient external cohort, determine the optimal Youden's $J$ threshold on the 149-patient discovery set and report external sensitivity and specificity alongside the uncalibrated $0.5$ threshold.

#### 4. Temper Claims Regarding Domain Specificity and Out-of-Sample Classification Sensitivity
* **WHY:** The Abstract and Discussion claim that LEAPD is "equally effective" in out-of-sample testing and indexes cross-domain cognition across all groups, overlooking the $50\%$ external sensitivity and the null correlations for PCPST ($\rho = 0.15$) and PSMT ($\rho = 0.02$) in controls.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Abstract, Results, and Discussion to reflect the exact external classification performance and control-group limitations.
* **HOW:** In the Abstract, specify that while out-of-sample rank correlation with MoCA was $\rho = 0.68$ ($\text{AUC} = 0.92$), external classification accuracy was $71.9\%$ ($50\%$ sensitivity, $100\%$ specificity). In the Discussion, explicitly note that NIH Toolbox models were not externally validated and did not correlate with processing speed or episodic memory in controls.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Corrections
* **WHY:** Matrix dimension mismatches in the mathematical methods and numerical inconsistencies in reported statistics reduce technical clarity.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Equations (2) and (3) (Methods, "LEAPD: theoretical framework for indexing cognition"):** $V_c$ has dimension $S \times K$, $D_c$ is $K \times K$, and $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times S}$, so $D_c Q^T$ has dimensions $(K \times K)(S \times K)$, which is non-conformable whenever $S \ne K$. Correct Eq. (2) to $W_c = (V_c - Q^T D_c) S^{-1/2}$ (or define $\mathbf{1}_S \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times 1}$ and write $W_c = V_c - \mathbf{1}_S m_c$), and use distinct sample sizes $S_1 = 64$ and $S_2 = 85$ for the two classes rather than assuming equal group size $S$.
  2. **Equation (1) (Methods):** The text states "$x(n)$ is approximated by $n$ preceding data samples", whereas the summation uses $K$ preceding samples ($i = 1, \dots, K$). Change "$n$ preceding data samples" to "$K$ preceding data samples" so $n$ (time index / subspace dimension) is not conflated with $K$ (LPC order).
  3. **Partial $\eta^2$ Discrepancy (Results, p. 4):** The text reports `PD: F(1,91) = 63.9, p value < 4 × 10^-12, partial η^2 = 0.28` and `Control: F(1,44) = 25.3, p value < 9 × 10^-6, partial η^2 = 0.34`. Mathematically, $\eta_p^2 = \frac{F \cdot \text{df}_1}{F \cdot \text{df}_1 + \text{df}_2}$, which equals $\frac{63.9}{63.9 + 91} = 0.413$ for PD and $\frac{25.3}{25.3 + 44} = 0.365$ for Controls. Clarify whether ordinary $\eta^2$ ($\text{SS}_{\text{effect}} / \text{SS}_{\text{total}}$) was reported instead of partial $\eta^2$, and correct the values.
  4. **Table 2 Row Label:** Change `AUC %` to `AUC` (since the reported entries `0.91`, `0.91`, `0.88`, and `0.92` are proportions on $[0, 1]$, whereas `Accuracy %`, `Sensitivity %`, and `Specificity %` are reported on $[0, 100]$).
