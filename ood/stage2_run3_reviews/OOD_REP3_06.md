### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This prospective, preregistered clinical neuroimaging study (ChiCTROOC-17012566) investigates whether baseline resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) of the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sgACC) predicts 12-week clinical remission in patients with major depressive disorder (MDD) treated with escitalopram monotherapy. Out of 110 enrolled medication-free MDD outpatients, 97 completed 12 weeks of escitalopram treatment ($10\text{–}20\text{ mg/day}$) and 87 passed head-motion quality control ($\text{mean FD}$ and $\ge 100$ volumes with $\text{FD} \le 0.2\text{ mm}$), yielding 42 remitters ($\text{RD}$, week-12 $\text{HAMD-17} \le 7$) and 45 non-remitters ($\text{NRD}$). Using a 4 mm spherical seed in the right sgACC ($\text{MNI: } 5, 25, -10$), whole-brain two-sample $t$-tests controlling for age, sex, education, and mean FD (voxel $p < 0.001$, cluster $\text{FWE } p < 0.05$) revealed significantly higher baseline rsFC in the RD group between right sgACC and four clusters in the fronto-parietal network (FPN): bilateral dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) and bilateral inferior parietal lobule (IPL). A 10-fold cross-validated pipeline combining training-fold Principal Component Analysis (PCA) with L2-penalized logistic regression classified RD vs. NRD with 72.64% mean accuracy ($p < 0.001$ by 1,000 permutations) and $0.74 \pm 0.21$ mean AUC, and Haufe forward-model transformation showed that 82.78% (832/1,005) of the top 1% predictive voxels in K-means cluster 1 overlapped with the four FPN clusters. De-identified imaging data and scripts are available only upon request.

The study is well-executed in several respects: it uses a preregistered prospective cohort of unmedicated MDD patients receiving a standardized single SSRI over a full 12-week acute phase, applies stringent motion scrubbing/exclusion criteria, fits PCA strictly inside the training folds during cross-validation, and appropriately uses the Haufe transformation to interpret linear classifier weights. Nevertheless, three methodological issues temper the strength of the conclusions. First, the RD group had lower baseline depression severity than the NRD group ($\text{HAMD-17} = 19.86 \pm 3.71$ vs. $21.53 \pm 4.13$, $t = -1.99, p = 0.050$) and shorter illness duration ($27.45 \pm 33.72$ vs. $45.67 \pm 67.78\text{ months}$); because remission is defined by an absolute threshold ($\text{week-12 HAMD-17} \le 7$), milder baseline severity directly increases the probability of crossing $\le 7$, yet baseline HAMD-17 was not included as a covariate in the voxel-wise $t$-test or evaluated as a baseline clinical benchmark in the prediction model. Second, the correlation analysis in Figure 2 ($r = 0.321\text{–}0.453$) extracts rsFC values from the four clusters identified by the RD vs. NRD contrast and correlates them with HAMD-17 percent improvement in the same 87 patients—a circular post-hoc analysis because RD/NRD status and HAMD-17 improvement measure the same 12-week outcome. Third, selecting optimal PCA/L2 hyperparameters by maximizing average 10-fold cross-validation accuracy without nested cross-validation can modestly inflate out-of-fold accuracy. All three issues can be resolved directly through computational re-analysis of the existing dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **MDD patients who achieve remission after 12 weeks of escitalopram exhibit higher baseline rsFC between the right sgACC and bilateral DLPFC and IPL (FPN) than non-remitters: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The whole-brain two-sample $t$-test is properly thresholded (voxel-wise $p < 0.001$, cluster-level $\text{FWE } p < 0.05$) and controls for age, sex, education, and head motion (Table 2). However, in Table 1, baseline HAMD-17 differs between RD ($19.86 \pm 3.71$) and NRD ($21.53 \pm 4.13$) right at the significance boundary ($t = -1.99, p = 0.050$), and illness duration is nearly 1.7-fold longer in NRD ($45.67$ vs. $27.45\text{ months}$; right-skewed distribution where a parametric $t$-test lacks power). Because achieving an absolute score $\le 7$ at week 12 is easier when starting from a lower baseline HAMD-17, omitting baseline HAMD-17 and illness duration from the voxel-wise ANCOVA leaves open the possibility that right sgACC–FPN connectivity partially reflects baseline clinical severity/chronicity rather than treatment-specific responsiveness.

* **Baseline right sgACC whole-brain rsFC independently predicts 12-week escitalopram remission with 72.64% accuracy and 0.74 AUC in 10-fold cross-validation: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Performing PCA dimensionality reduction inside each training fold before projecting the test fold (Methods, line 61) avoids the most common form of feature-extraction leakage, and permutation testing ($1,000$ shuffles) confirms above-chance discrimination. However, two caveats apply: (a) line 61 states that "the optimal model parameters were determined based on the average prediction accuracy" across the 10-fold CV rather than via an inner nested CV loop, which introduces hyperparameter-tuning optimism across the small test folds ($n = 8\text{–}9$ per fold, reflected in the wide AUC standard deviation of $\pm 0.21$); and (b) the classifier is not benchmarked against a clinical-only baseline model (containing baseline HAMD-17, illness duration, age, and sex) to demonstrate incremental predictive value of fMRI over free clinical variables.

* **Baseline sgACC–DLPFC and sgACC–IPL connectivity strengths correlate positively ($r = 0.321\text{ to }0.453, p \le 0.002$) with percentage HAMD-17 symptom improvement: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In "Results" (lines 111 and 139; Fig. 2), the four ROIs (bilateral DLPFC and IPL) were defined on the full sample of $N = 87$ patients by contrasting RD ($\text{week-12 HAMD-17} \le 7$) against NRD ($\text{week-12 HAMD-17} > 7$). Because week-12 remission status and percentage HAMD-17 improvement ($77.09\% \pm 11.49\%$ in RD vs. $35.97\% \pm 24.19\%$ in NRD, $t = 10.23, p < 0.001$) are strongly colinear functions of the exact same week-12 HAMD-17 score in the same 87 patients, correlating ROI values selected via the group contrast with continuous symptom improvement is circular ("double-dipping") and inflates the correlation coefficients and $p$-values.

* **Haufe-transformed predictive features cluster primarily within the fronto-parietal network (82.78% overlap with between-group ROIs) and may guide differential selection between SSRI pharmacotherapy and rTMS: [Fragile].**  
  Use of the Haufe forward transformation (covariance between voxel features and classifier predictions) is methodologically sound for linear models. However, the Methods (line 66) do not specify whether the Haufe scores were averaged across the 10 cross-validation folds or computed from a model refit to all 87 subjects, and applying K-means clustering to thresholded top-1% voxels (setting the remaining 99% to zero) trivially forces a large zero cluster (cluster 0: 144,705 voxels) and partitions the non-zero tail. Finally, because this is a single-arm open-label study without a placebo or rTMS comparator arm, attributing the prediction specifically to escitalopram pharmacodynamics versus non-specific placebo/remission propensity overreaches the single-arm design.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Baseline HAMD-17 Severity and Illness Duration in Both Voxel-Wise and Predictive Analyses
* **WHY:** Baseline HAMD-17 is lower in RD than NRD ($t = -1.99, p = 0.050$) and illness duration has a large numerical imbalance ($27.45$ vs. $45.67\text{ months}$) with extreme variance ($\text{SD} = 67.78$). Because remission is defined as an absolute week-12 score $\le 7$, baseline severity confounds remission status.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the whole-brain right sgACC two-sample $t$-test including baseline HAMD-17 and log-transformed illness duration as additional covariates, and compare the rsFC classifier against a baseline clinical logistic regression model.
* **HOW:**
  1. In Table 1, report a Mann–Whitney $U$ test (and median / IQR) for `Illness duration` and `Number of episodes` to account for skewness.
  2. Add `baseline HAMD-17` and $\log(\text{illness duration} + 1)$ to the SPM12/DPARSFA covariate matrix (alongside sex, age, education, and mean FD) and verify that the bilateral DLPFC and IPL clusters survive at voxel $p < 0.001$, cluster $\text{FWE } p < 0.05$.
  3. Repeat the group comparison and classification using **treatment response** ($\ge 50\%$ reduction in HAMD-17, which is less sensitive to baseline score than $\text{HAMD-17} \le 7$) as a sensitivity analysis.
  4. Report the 10-fold CV AUC of (i) clinical variables alone (age, sex, education, baseline HAMD-17, illness duration), (ii) sgACC rsFC alone, and (iii) combined clinical + sgACC rsFC, using DeLong's test or permutation testing to quantify incremental predictive value.

#### 2. Implement Nested Cross-Validation and Out-of-Fold Continuous Improvement Prediction
* **WHY:** Tuning PCA component counts and the L2 penalty parameter $C$ on the 10-fold average accuracy leaks test-fold information into hyperparameter selection, and correlating RD/NRD-selected clusters with HAMD-17 percent improvement (Fig. 2) is circular.
* **WHAT:** Use nested cross-validation (e.g., 10 outer folds $\times$ 5 inner folds) for the logistic regression classifier, and replace the circular ROI correlations in Figure 2 with out-of-fold continuous prediction (or independent anatomical atlas ROIs).
* **HOW:** In `scikit-learn`, wrap the `Pipeline([('pca', PCA()), ('clf', LogisticRegression(penalty='l2'))])` inside `GridSearchCV(inner_cv=StratifiedKFold(5))` and evaluate on `outer_cv=StratifiedKFold(10)` repeated 10 times to stabilize variance. To test continuous HAMD-17 percent improvement without circularity, either (a) extract right sgACC connectivity using a priori anatomical/functional DLPFC and IPL masks from the Yeo 7-network FPN parcellation (independent of the RD vs. NRD contrast), or (b) use nested cross-validated Ridge regression on the whole-brain sgACC rsFC map to predict out-of-fold HAMD-17 percent improvement ($r_{\text{oob}}$ and $R^2_{\text{oob}}$).

#### 3. Clarify Haufe Score Aggregation and Account for Subgenual Susceptibility Dropout
* **WHY:** The sgACC ($\text{MNI: } 5, 25, -10$) lies in an orbitofrontal region prone to EPI signal dropout and geometric distortion (especially with $3.5\text{ mm}$ slices and $90^\circ$ flip angle), and the manuscript does not state how Haufe maps were combined across the 10 CV folds.
* **WHAT:** Report the temporal signal-to-noise ratio (tSNR) and coverage of the 4 mm right and left sgACC spheres across all 87 subjects, and specify how fold-level Haufe weights were aggregated.
* **HOW:** Compute the mean and minimum tSNR within the right and left sgACC 4 mm spheres across all 87 participants to confirm that the null result for the left sgACC ($-5, 25, -10$) is not due to asymmetric susceptibility dropout. In "Model interpretation" (line 66), explicitly state that Haufe covariance weights were computed within each of the 10 training folds, standardized ($z$-scored), and averaged across folds prior to identifying the top 1% predictive voxels.
