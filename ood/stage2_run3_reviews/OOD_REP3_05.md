### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This computational medicinal chemistry study seeks to identify novel dual type-II kinase inhibitors targeting both vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VEGFR2) and focal adhesion kinase (FAK) to counter tumor angiogenesis and metastasis. After verifying sequence (34% identity, 54% positives) and 3D binding-pocket similarity between VEGFR2 (`4ASD`) and FAK (`4K9Y`) across the hinge region (Cys919/Cys502), $\alpha$C-helix gate area (Glu885/Glu471), and DFG motif (Asp1046/Asp564), the authors align 8 VEGFR2 and 2 FAK DFG-out crystal structures in MOE to manually construct 109 VEGFR2 and 14 FAK receptor-based pharmacophore models with excluded volume spheres. Evaluating these models against compiled test sets (reported as 39 actives + 1,200 DEKOIS 2.0 decoys for VEGFR2; 17 actives + 28 experimental inactives + 955 DUD-E decoys for FAK), they select `VEGFR2_Ph4_109` ($\text{Se} = 0.95$, $\text{Sp} = 0.93$, $\text{F1} = 0.48$) and `FAK_Ph4_12` ($\text{Se} = 0.88$, $\text{Sp} = 0.98$, $\text{F1} = 0.61$) to screen the ZINC purchasable library via ZINCPharmer, yielding 42,616 and 28,475 initial hits. Filtering by Oprea lead-likeness, Lipinski/Veber rules, mutagenicity, solubility, and PAINS leaves 13,023 VEGFR2 and 6,832 FAK hits with 124 overlapping molecules; rigid-receptor docking in MOE identifies 13 compounds reproducing key interactions in both kinases, and SwissADME profiling narrows the selection to `ZINC09875266`. All training/test lists and docking scores are provided in Additional file 1.

The structural rationale for targeting the conserved DFG-out conformation of VEGFR2 and FAK is well-grounded, and the authors appropriately frame their final hit (`ZINC09875266`) as a computational prediction requiring in vitro and in vivo testing. However, several methodological weaknesses in the virtual screening and docking pipeline limit the reliability of the hit nomination. First, generating 109 VEGFR2 and 14 FAK pharmacophores by manually varying feature radii and excluded volume counts (e.g., 359 vs. 378 excluded volumes) and selecting the winning models directly on the single test set introduces test-set selection bias without an independent external holdout set, and the selected models (`VEGFR2_Ph4_109` and `FAK_Ph4_12`) do not actually maximize the stated F1 selection metric. Second, the FAK pharmacophore training set relies on only two crystal structures, one of which (`4KAO`) is bound to a weak micromolar ligand ($\text{IC}_{50} = 7,000\text{ nM}$). Third, docking relies exclusively on a rigid-receptor protocol with single-point `London dG` scoring, without induced-fit side-chain refinement, MM/GBSA rescoring, or molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to confirm simultaneous stability in both kinases. Finally, Table 3 contains systematic arithmetic discrepancies between total hit counts ($N$) and $\text{TP} + \text{FP}$, as well as a mismatch in total VEGFR2 test actives ($39$ in text vs. $40$ in Table 3). These issues can be addressed computationally through cross-validated pharmacophore benchmarking, flexible-receptor/MD refinement, and tabular corrections.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **VEGFR2 and FAK share conserved DFG-out active-site topology across the hinge (Cys919/Cys502), $\alpha$C-helix (Glu885/Glu471), and DFG loop (Asp1046/Asp564), supporting the structural feasibility of dual type-II inhibition: [Well Supported].**  
  The sequence alignment (Fig. 3) and 3D active-site superposition of `4ASD` and `4K9Y` (Fig. 5) clearly demonstrate spatial and chemical conservation of the primary hydrogen-bonding and hydrophobic back-pocket sub-regions required for type-II DFG-out kinase binding.

* **Pharmacophore models `VEGFR2_Ph4_109` and `FAK_Ph4_12` provide unbiased, optimal discrimination between active inhibitors and decoys: [Fragile].**  
  First, the compiled test sets were used iteratively to tune and select among 109 manually edited VEGFR2 models and 14 FAK models (including fine-tuning 359 vs. 378 excluded volumes in `VEGFR2_Ph4_108` vs. `109`), turning the "test set" into a validation/tuning set with no independent holdout benchmark. Second, although the text states that F1 score "was used as the metric of choice for best model selection" (line 142), the authors bypassed higher-F1 models (`VEGFR2_Ph4_104` with $\text{F1} = 0.52$ and `103` with $\text{F1} = 0.50$; `FAK_Ph4_9` and `10` with $\text{F1} = 0.65$) in favor of third-ranked models based on subjective visual SAR agreement (line 209). Third, for FAK, one of the two training crystal structures (`4KAO`, Table 2) has a ligand $\text{IC}_{50}$ of $7,000\text{ nM}$ ($7\,\mu\text{M}$), which barely meets the $10\,\mu\text{M}$ activity cutoff and is 26-fold weaker than `4K9Y` ($266\text{ nM}$).

* **Thirteen common ZINC compounds, led by `ZINC09875266`, stably bind both VEGFR2 and FAK in the DFG-out conformation with favorable dual inhibitory potential: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Self-docking of the native ligands into rigid `4ASD` and `4K9Y` reproduced the crystallographic poses with low RMSD ($0.355\text{ \AA}$ and $0.151\text{ \AA}$). However, screening 124 prospective compounds with a structurally distinct scaffold into a strictly rigid receptor (`Rigid receptor protocol method` with `Triangle matcher` and `London dG` scoring, line 382) ignores DFG-loop and $\alpha$C-helix conformational plasticity. Moreover, the main text never compares the `London dG` binding scores of the 13 hits (relegated to Supplement S4.2) against the co-crystallized reference inhibitors ($-15.24$ and $-16.02\text{ kcal/mol}$) or against known FDA-approved multi-kinase inhibitors, nor is complex stability tested via explicit-solvent MD simulations.

* **Table 3 and text accurately report the VEGFR2 test-set sample sizes and confusion-matrix metrics: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In "Test set compilation" (lines 137 and 352), the authors state that the VEGFR2 test set contains **39 active inhibitors** and 1,200 decoys (which sums to 1,239, though stated as 1,240). However, in every row of Table 3 (lines 153–175) and in the text on line 216 ("chose 38 active compounds out of 40"), $\text{TP} + \text{FN} = \mathbf{40}$ actives. Even more concerning, the column $N$ in Table 3 (total retrieved hits, which by definition must equal $\text{TP} + \text{FP}$, as it correctly does in Table 4 for FAK) does not equal $\text{TP} + \text{FP}$ in any row of Table 3: for example, in `VEGFR2_Ph4-21`, $\text{TP} = 0$ and $\text{FP} = 1$, yet $N$ is listed as $41$; in `VEGFR2_Ph4-17`, $\text{TP} = 3$ and $\text{FP} = 4$, yet $N = 44$; in `VEGFR2_Ph4-109`, $\text{TP} = 38$ and $\text{FP} = 82$ ($\text{sum} = 120$), yet $N = 122$. If Yield of Actives ($Y_a = \text{TP}/N$), Enrichment ($E$), and F1 in Table 3 were computed using the corrupted $N$ column instead of $\text{TP} + \text{FP}$, all corresponding metrics in Table 3 are distorted (e.g., for `VEGFR2_Ph4-17`, $\text{TP}/(\text{TP}+\text{FP}) = 3/7 = 0.43$, whereas Table 3 reports $Y_a = 3/44 = 0.07$!).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct the Confusion-Matrix Arithmetic in Table 3 and Recompute VEGFR2 Validation Metrics
* **WHY:** In Table 3, the total hit count $N$ appears to have been corrupted (off by $+2$ to $+40$ from $\text{TP} + \text{FP}$ across all rows, likely due to adding $\text{FN}$ instead of $\text{TP}$ in a spreadsheet formula for rows 1–8 and off by $+2$ in rows 9–20), which directly corrupts $Y_a = \text{TP}/N$, Enrichment $E$, and $F_1$. Additionally, the text states 39 VEGFR2 test actives while Table 3 sums to 40.
* **WHAT:** Audit and recalculate $N = \text{TP} + \text{FP}$, $Y_a$, $E$, and $F_1$ across all 109 VEGFR2 models in Table 3 and Additional file 1 (Table S4), and reconcile whether 39 or 40 active compounds were screened.
* **HOW:** Replace the formula for $N$ in Table 3 and Table S4 with $\text{TP} + \text{FP}$ (matching the convention in Table 4 for FAK), recompute $Y_a = \text{TP} / (\text{TP} + \text{FP})$, $E = Y_a / (A / D_{\text{total}})$, and $F_1 = 2 \cdot \text{TP} / (2 \cdot \text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN})$, and verify whether the ranking of `VEGFR2_Ph4_109` changes after correcting $N$.

#### 2. Perform Induced-Fit Docking, MM/GBSA Rescoring, and Molecular Dynamics Validation for `ZINC09875266`
* **WHY:** Rigid-receptor docking with a fast placement scoring function (`London dG`) is prone to false positives when docking non-native scaffolds into the sterically tight DFG-out back pocket of two different kinases.
* **WHAT:** Refine the docking poses of the top 13 common compounds (and specifically `ZINC09875266`) using induced-fit (flexible side-chain) refinement with `GBVI/WSA dG` or MM/GBSA rescoring in MOE, and run explicit-solvent molecular dynamics (MD) simulations on the `ZINC09875266`–VEGFR2 and `ZINC09875266`–FAK complexes.
* **HOW:** In MOE Dock, change the refinement step from `Rigid Receptor` to `Induced Fit` with `GBVI/WSA dG` rescoring for the 13 hits alongside the co-crystallized controls (`Sorafenib` in `4ASD` and the pyrrolopyrimidine ligand in `4K9Y`), and include a summary table in the main text comparing their docking scores in $\text{kcal/mol}$. For `ZINC09875266`, perform a 100 ns all-atom MD simulation (e.g., GROMACS or Amber) in both `4ASD` and `4K9Y` to report ligand RMSD and hydrogen-bond occupancy with Cys919/Cys502, Glu885/Glu471, and Asp1046/Asp564.

#### 3. Verify Chemical Novelty and Scaffold Independence Between Training and Test Sets
* **WHY:** If any of the 39 VEGFR2 or 17 FAK test-set active compounds share identical scaffolds with the 8 VEGFR2 and 2 FAK crystallographic training ligands—or if `ZINC09875266` is a close analog of a known kinase inhibitor—performance metrics and novelty claims will be inflated.
* **WHAT:** Report the Tanimoto similarity (ECFP4 / Morgan fingerprints) between (a) the training and test active sets, and (b) `ZINC09875266` (and the 13 hits) against known VEGFR2 and FAK inhibitors in ChEMBL/BindingDB.
* **HOW:** Using RDKit or MOE fingerprint similarity, compute the maximum ECFP4 Tanimoto similarity of each test active to the training crystal ligands (confirming that the 10 training ligands were excluded from the test sets), and report the nearest known bioactive neighbor of `ZINC09875266` in ChEMBL.

#### 4. Clarify Excluded Volume Handling During ZINCPharmer Screening
* **WHY:** Lines 204–205 demonstrate that removing or reducing excluded volume spheres (`378` in VEGFR2, `68` in FAK) causes false positives to surge (`F1` drops from 0.61 to 0.14 in FAK), yet it is unclear whether all 378 and 68 excluded volume spheres were preserved when uploading the pharmacophores to the ZINCPharmer web server.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state in "Virtual screening" (line 366) whether the ZINCPharmer queries retained all 378 and 68 receptor excluded-volume spheres and the exact RMSD cutoff applied during the ZINCPharmer search.
* **HOW:** Add the ZINCPharmer query parameters (maximum RMSD cutoff, excluded volume enforcement, and receptor structure overlay settings) to the Methodology section and provide the `.ph4` / `.json` pharmacophore query files in the Supplementary Information.
