### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study reports a hierarchical virtual screening and experimental validation campaign to identify small-molecule inhibitors of c-Jun N-terminal kinase 3 (JNK3; PDB ID: `7S1N`). Starting from an in-house combined library of $\sim 1.6\text{ million}$ commercial compounds (MedChemExpress and ChemDiv), the authors apply sequential Schrödinger Glide docking filters—HTVS ($\le -6.532\text{ kcal/mol}$, $500,000$ retained), SP ($\le -8.689\text{ kcal/mol}$, $200,000$ retained), and XP ($\le -8.101\text{ kcal/mol}$, $9,000$ retained)—followed by MM/GBSA rescoring, DeepDock scoring, and a custom Graph Attention Network + Multilayer Perceptron (GAT-MLP) regression model trained on 1,072 ChEMBL JNK3 inhibitors ($8:1:1$ split; test $R^2 = 0.7228$). Ten candidate compounds selected by combining scoring metrics with medicinal chemistry visual inspection were tested in an HTRF kinase assay at $25\ \mu\text{M}$. One hit, Compound 6 (*N*-(4-(2-ethyl-4-(*m*-tolyl)thiazol-5-yl)pyridin-2-yl)benzamide), showed $99.73\%$ inhibition at $25\ \mu\text{M}$ and a biochemical $\text{IC}_{50}$ of $130.1\text{ nM}$, and reduced TNF-$\alpha$ secretion in LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 macrophages at $5$ and $10\ \mu\text{M}$. Binding pose metadynamics (BPMD) and AMBER molecular dynamics simulations were used to characterize hydrogen bonding with Met149 and Lys93. Custom GNN code, trained model weights, and screening structures are not publicly deposited.

The experimental identification of Compound 6 as a $130.1\text{ nM}$ biochemical inhibitor of JNK3 is a concrete empirical result. However, the manuscript's methodological and biological conclusions have major weaknesses: (1) Table 1 shows that the custom GNN rescoring model failed to rank the experimental hits—assigning its highest predicted potency ($\text{pIC}_{50} = 6.84$) to Compound 3 (which was completely inactive at $5.70\%$ inhibition at $25\ \mu\text{M}$) while ranking Compound 6 sixth ($\text{pIC}_{50} = 5.82$, an order-of-magnitude underprediction relative to its experimental $\text{pIC}_{50} = 6.89$); (2) the cellular assay uses RAW264.7 peritoneal macrophages, which express ubiquitous JNK1/JNK2 rather than brain-specific JNK3, without testing isoform selectivity against JNK1/JNK2 or measuring macrophage cytotoxicity (MTT/CCK-8) after $48\text{ h}$ exposure to $5\text{--}10\ \mu\text{M}$ drug; and (3) the text contains direct contradictions regarding MD simulation length ($100\text{ ns}$ vs. $40 + 30\text{ ns}$), IL-6 significance, and assay dilution volumes.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Compound 6 inhibits recombinant JNK3 enzymatic activity with an $\text{IC}_{50}$ of $130.1\text{ nM}$ (Fig. 4A, B; Table 1): [Well Supported].**  
  The biochemical HTRF screen of the 10 purchased candidates at $25\ \mu\text{M}$ and the follow-up 6-point dose–response curve ($0.1\text{ nM}$ to $10,000\text{ nM}$) alongside positive control staurosporine support the *in vitro* enzymatic potency of Compound 6 against JNK3.

* **The DeepDock + GAT-MLP Graph Neural Network rescoring model accurately predicts JNK3 $\text{pIC}_{50}$ and drives the successful identification of potent hits (Fig. 3, Table 1): [Overstated].**  
  First, the ChEMBL dataset ($N = 1,072$) was split randomly ($8:1:1$) rather than by molecular scaffold, inflating test $R^2$ ($0.7228$ vs. training $R^2 = 0.9617$) via congeneric analog overlap, and no ablation benchmark is presented comparing the GAT-MLP model against DeepDock alone, MM/GBSA, or Glide XP on the test set. Second, on the 10 experimentally tested compounds in Table 1, the GNN `pIC50 Predicted` values show no positive correlation with actual kinase inhibition ($r = -0.07$): the top GNN prediction, Compound 3 ($\text{pIC}_{50\text{ pred}} = 6.84$, corresponding to $\text{IC}_{50} \approx 145\text{ nM}$), achieved only $5.70 \pm 1.02\%$ inhibition at $25,000\text{ nM}$, and 9 of the 10 candidates inhibited JNK3 by $< 44\%$ at $25\ \mu\text{M}$ ($\text{true pIC}_{50} < 4.6$). Meanwhile, Compound 6 ($\text{true pIC}_{50} = 6.89$) was predicted at $\text{pIC}_{50} = 5.82$ (ranked 6th of 10), whereas traditional Glide XP ranked Compound 6 third ($-13.065\text{ kcal/mol}$). Third, reducing the 9,000 XP hits to 10 tested molecules relied on unquantified manual curation by medicinal chemists rather than an automated ranking threshold.

* **Inhibition of TNF-$\alpha$ and IL-6 release in LPS-stimulated RAW264.7 macrophages validates cellular JNK3 target engagement by Compound 6 (Fig. 4C, D): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  As the authors state in the Introduction (p. 2), "JNK1 and JNK2 are ubiquitously expressed, while JNK3 is predominantly expressed in the brain." Murine RAW264.7 macrophages are peripheral myeloid cells in which LPS-induced AP-1/cytokine signaling is mediated by JNK1 and JNK2, with negligible JNK3 expression. Because Compound 6 is an ATP-competitive hinge binder (forming hydrogen bonds with the conserved Met149 hinge residue; Fig. 6D) and was never profiled against JNK1 or JNK2, its suppression of macrophage TNF-$\alpha$ at $5\text{--}10\ \mu\text{M}$ ($38\text{--}77\times$ its JNK3 $\text{IC}_{50}$) likely reflects pan-JNK (JNK1/2) or off-target kinase inhibition. Furthermore, cells were incubated with $5\text{--}10\ \mu\text{M}$ Compound 6 for $48\text{ h}$ total ($24\text{ h}$ pretreatment $+ 24\text{ h}$ LPS) without any reported cell viability/cytotoxicity assay (e.g., CCK-8 or MTT), leaving open the possibility that reduced cytokine release partially reflects macrophage toxicity.

* **Binding pose metadynamics and 100 ns molecular dynamics simulations establish the stable binding mode of Compound 6 with Met149 and Lys93 in JNK3 (Figs. 5–6): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Combining multi-engine docking (AutoDock-GPU, Vina, LeDock, Glide) with 10-replicate binding pose metadynamics (PoseScore and PersScore) and triplicate AMBER MD simulations with per-residue MM/GBSA decomposition is a solid computational protocol for characterizing the hinge-binding pose in PDB `7S1N`, although the manuscript contains contradictory descriptions of the MD simulation length between Sections 2.7 and 4.8.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Benchmark the GAT-MLP Rescoring Model Using Scaffold Splitting and Ablation Controls
* **WHY:** On the 10 experimentally tested hits, GNN-predicted $\text{pIC}_{50}$ failed to correlate with experimental inhibition, and random $8:1:1$ splitting on ChEMBL overestimates generalization across novel scaffolds.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate the GAT-MLP model under Bemis–Murcko scaffold splitting on the 1,072 ChEMBL molecules and compare its test-set $R^2$, RMSE, and Spearman rank correlation against ablation baselines: (a) Glide XP alone, (b) MM/GBSA alone, (c) DeepDock alone, and (d) GAT without the DeepDock node feature.
* **HOW:** Partition the 1,072 ChEMBL ligands by Murcko scaffold into $80\%$ train / $10\%$ validation / $10\%$ test sets. Train the GAT-MLP with and without the DeepDock feature and report comparative metrics in a new table. Explicitly state in Section 2.3 the exact quantitative filtering rules used to narrow the 9,000 XP compounds down to the final 10 candidates.

#### 2. Measure Isoform Selectivity (JNK1/JNK2 vs. JNK3) and Macrophage Cell Viability
* **WHY:** Because Compound 6 targets the conserved ATP pocket (Met149 hinge) and RAW264.7 macrophages express JNK1/2 rather than brain-specific JNK3, cellular anti-inflammatory claims require knowing whether Compound 6 inhibits JNK1/2 or induces cytotoxicity at $5\text{--}10\ \mu\text{M}$.
* **WHAT:** Measure the biochemical $\text{IC}_{50}$ of Compound 6 against JNK1 and JNK2, and perform a CCK-8/MTT viability assay in RAW264.7 cells at $5$ and $10\ \mu\text{M}$ over $48\text{ h}$ (or reframe the cellular discussion if wet-lab re-testing is not performed).
* **HOW:** Run the same 6-point HTRF kinase protocol (Section 4.5) using recombinant human JNK1 and JNK2 to report isoform selectivity ratios. In RAW264.7 cells treated with $0, 5,$ and $10\ \mu\text{M}$ Compound 6 $\pm 10\text{ ng/mL}$ LPS for $48\text{ h}$, measure CCK-8 absorbance at $450\text{ nm}$. If no new experiments are conducted, revise the Abstract and Discussion to state that RAW264.7 cytokine suppression likely reflects cross-inhibition of macrophage JNK1/2 and that cell viability and CNS neuronal models remain to be tested.

#### 3. Deposit Custom GNN Code, Trained Model Weights, and Candidate SMILES
* **WHY:** The Data Availability Statement only links to the top-level homepages of ChEMBL, TargetMol, and MedChemExpress, making it impossible to reproduce the Masif/DeepDock/GAT-MLP rescoring pipeline or verify the 10 tested structures without manual transcription from Figure 3C.
* **WHAT:** Deposit the DGL/Optuna GNN training and inference code, the preprocessed 1,072 ChEMBL dataset split, and a table with SMILES, catalog IDs, and scores for the 10 tested compounds.
* **HOW:** Upload the Python scripts, trained GAT-MLP model weights, and a machine-readable CSV of the 10 tested compounds (including vendor catalog numbers from ChemDiv/MCE) to GitHub or Zenodo and add the persistent link to the Data Availability Statement.

#### 4. Resolve Contradictions and Technical Errors Across Sections
* **WHY:** Direct numerical and textual contradictions between Results, Discussion, and Materials and Methods reduce confidence in the technical execution.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **MD Simulation Duration Contradiction (Section 2.7 vs. Section 4.8):** Section 2.7 states `We performed three parallel sets of 100 ns molecular dynamics simulations... the RMSD of both the protein and ligand reached a steady state during the final 20 ns` (and Figure 6A, B plots $0\text{--}100\text{ ns}$), whereas Section 4.8 states `approximately 40 ns of constant pressure and temperature simulation (NPT) was carried out to equilibrate the system, which was followed by 30 ns of production simulation`. Update Section 4.8 to match the actual $100\text{ ns}$ trajectory length shown in Figure 6.
  2. **IL-6 Inhibition Contradiction (Section 2.5 vs. Section 3):** Section 2.5 states Compound 6 showed `slight inhibition of IL-6 secretion` (and Fig. 4D shows weak effect relative to TNF-$\alpha$), whereas Discussion Section 3 claims it `significantly curtailed the release of both IL-6 and TNFα`. Align Section 3 with Section 2.5 and Fig. 4D.
  3. **Kinase Assay Volume & Dilution Error (Section 4.5):** Section 4.5 states that a `50× test concentrations` drug solution was prepared, and then `25 μL of the drug solution was taken and then transferred to a 384-well plate... Then, 2.5 μL of 2× kinase and metal solution was mixed... An amount of 5 μL of Kinase Detection Reagent was added`, while omitting the volume of the `2× ATP and substrate solution`. Adding $25\ \mu\text{L}$ of $50\times$ drug to $2.5\ \mu\text{L} + 5\ \mu\text{L}$ reagents does not yield a $1\times$ final concentration and exceeds standard $10\text{--}20\ \mu\text{L}$ HTRF 384-well assay volumes. Correct the pipetting volumes (e.g., $0.2\text{--}2.5\ \mu\text{L}$ drug + $2.5\ \mu\text{L}$ kinase + $2.5\ \mu\text{L}$ ATP/substrate + $5\ \mu\text{L}$ detection reagent).
  4. **Target Name Typo (Section 3, final paragraph):** Correct `establishing a valuable foundation for the design and development of JAK3 inhibitors` to `JNK3 inhibitors`.
  5. **Docking Tool Description (Section 4.4):** Revise `all docking was performed using the Glide_SP tool, with the maximum output conformations set to 5 for HTVS precision and 20 for both SP and XP` to state `the Glide docking module at HTVS, SP, and XP precision levels`.
