### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study models the current and future potential geographic distribution in China of the medicinal wood-inhabiting macrofungal genus *Sanghuangporus* using Maximum Entropy (MaxEnt) species distribution modeling. The authors compiled 260 ITS/morphologically verified fungal occurrence records from three Chinese fungaria (HMAS, BJFC, IFP) and taxonomic literature, spatially thinned them at a 10 km radius to retain 72 records across 10 species, and paired them with WorldClim v2.1 bioclimatic layers, elevation, and a Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF)-derived host-plant layer at 30 arc-second ($\sim 1\text{ km}^2$) resolution. Comparing models with and without the host-plant variable, and projecting future distributions across four 20-year periods (2030s–2090s) and four CMIP6 Shared Socio-economic Pathways (SSPs) under a single general circulation model (BCC-CSM2-MR), the authors report an AUC of 0.903 with host plants versus 0.899 without host plants, a 155.468% expansion of "highly suitable habitat" when excluding host plants, and a 67.179%–118.255% increase in highly suitable habitat across all future climate scenarios. Occurrence metadata are provided in Supplementary Table S1, while environmental rasters and modeling scripts are not deposited.

While compiling DNA-barcoded herbarium records for *Sanghuangporus* is a useful curation effort, the modeling design suffers from three foundational methodological flaws that invalidate its main quantitative conclusions. First, despite criticizing prior work for taxonomic lumping and emphasizing that the 10 Chinese *Sanghuangporus* species exhibit strong host specificity and distinct ecological niches, the authors pool all 10 species into a single genus-level model, producing a composite environmental envelope spanning $-6.7$ to $21.4\,^\circ\text{C}$ in annual mean temperature and $42$ to $6,852\text{ m}$ in elevation. Second, for the current distribution model, the "host plant" variable is constructed by converting raw GBIF occurrence point counts of 18 tree genera into a $1\text{ km}^2$ raster ($\text{tree/km}^2$), which measures herbarium sampling density rather than true forest host availability and is zero across $>98\%$ of China's land area. Third, when projecting to future climate scenarios, the authors replace this sparse raw-count variable ($\text{tree/km}^2$, range $0\text{–}1,126$) with the maximum continuous MaxEnt suitability percentage ($0\text{–}100\%$) across 18 separately modeled host genera—a direct covariate scale and unit mismatch between training and projection that artificially inflates predicted future habitat suitability across all SSPs. Re-analysis at the species level (for sufficiently sampled taxa) using harmonized host-suitability covariates and multi-GCM ensembles is required to salvage the ecological inferences.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The genus-level MaxEnt model accurately predicts the ecological niche of *Sanghuangporus* in China ($\text{AUC} = 0.903$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The 72 retained records pool 10 distinct species (*S. alpinus*, *S. baumii*, *S. lonicericola*, *S. quercicola*, *S. sanghuang*, *S. subbaumii*, *S. vaninii*, *S. vitexicola*, *S. weigelae*, and *S. zonatus*) that inhabit incompatible thermal and elevational zones (from subalpine *S. alpinus* in southwestern mountains to temperate *S. baumii* / *S. vaninii* in northeastern forests and subtropical *S. zonatus*). Fitting a single MaxEnt model to pooled multi-species records violates the niche-modeling assumption of a unified species response, yielding an uninterpretable composite "highly suitable" range spanning $\text{Bio1} = -6.7\text{ to }21.4\,^\circ\text{C}$, $\text{Bio7} = 11.5\text{ to }63.7\,^\circ\text{C}$, and $\text{Elevation} = 42\text{ to }6,852\text{ m}$ ("Results", line 100). Moreover, evaluating 72 forest points against random background points drawn across all of China (including vast arid deserts and the Tibetan Plateau) trivially inflates AUC near 0.90 without demonstrating fine-scale predictive discrimination.

* **Excluding the host-plant variable increases highly suitable habitat area by 155.468% (from $20.238 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$ to $51.701 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$), proving the decisive biological role of host plants: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  For the current period, the host-plant layer was created by counting raw GBIF point occurrences per $30''$ ($\sim 1\text{ km}^2$) grid cell ("Materials and methods", line 82; Table 1 unit: $\text{tree/km}^2$). Because raw GBIF records at $1\text{ km}^2$ resolution are absent in almost all grid cells except heavily surveyed nature reserves and botanical collection sites—which are the same accessible mountains where mycologists collect *Sanghuangporus*—this raster acts as a spatial sampling-effort filter rather than a biological map of host-tree density. In addition, because correlated variables ($|r| > 0.8$) were filtered based on full-model percent contribution ("Model evaluation", line 89), removing the host-plant variable caused the model to swap climatic predictors from Annual Precipitation ($\text{Bio12}$, $48.0\%$) to Precipitation of the Warmest Quarter ($\text{Bio18}$, $57.1\%$; Table 1), confounding the host-plant comparison with a change in climatic covariates.

* **Highly suitable habitat for *Sanghuangporus* will increase dramatically by 67.179% to 118.255% across all four future time periods and all four SSP climate change scenarios: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In "Materials and methods" (line 82), the current MaxEnt model was trained using raw GBIF host-plant counts per $1\text{ km}^2$ pixel ($\text{tree/km}^2$, ranging from $0$ to $1,126$), whereas the future climate projections substituted the maximum predicted MaxEnt niche suitability percentage ($0\%\text{–}100\%$) across 18 tree genera for each pixel. Feeding a continuous $0\text{–}100\%$ suitability surface into a MaxEnt feature trained on sparse point counts ($0\text{–}1,126\text{ tree/km}^2$) creates a severe covariate domain shift: millions of unsampled grid cells that had $0\text{ tree/km}^2$ in the current GBIF count raster receive values of $50\text{–}100$ in the future suitability raster, mechanically driving the uniform $67\%\text{–}118\%$ surge in predicted "highly suitable" area across every future scenario.

* **The model with host plants ($\text{AUC} = 0.903$) is "excellent," whereas the model without host plants ($\text{AUC} = 0.899$) merely "performs well": [Overstated].**  
  With 72 records split 75%/25% (54 training, 18 test points per replicate), a difference of $\Delta \text{AUC} = 0.004$ across an arbitrary threshold ($0.900$) is well within the sampling variance of 18 test points and provides no statistical evidence of superior predictive accuracy.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Harmonize the Host-Plant Covariate Between Current and Future Models
* **WHY:** Training the current *Sanghuangporus* MaxEnt model on raw GBIF point counts per $1\text{ km}^2$ pixel ($\text{tree/km}^2$) and projecting the future model onto modeled host niche suitability percentages ($0\text{–}100\%$) introduces both severe herbarium sampling bias in the current map and a unit/scale mismatch in all future projections.
* **WHAT:** Use the exact same metric—modeled host-plant niche suitability ($0\text{–}100\%$, spatially thinned and bias-corrected)—for **both** the current baseline (1970–2000) and all future CMIP6 projections.
* **HOW:** Since the authors already built MaxEnt niche suitability models for the 18 host-plant genera to project future host distributions ("Materials and methods", line 82), generate the current (1970–2000) host-plant layer using the exact same maximum predicted host suitability index ($0\text{–}100\%$) across the 18 genera rather than raw GBIF point counts per $\text{km}^2$. Re-run the current and future *Sanghuangporus* models on this harmonized scale and recompute the percentage habitat changes in Supplementary Table S2.

#### 2. Stratify Models by Species or Functional Host Group Rather Than Pooling All 10 Species
* **WHY:** Pooling subalpine, temperate, and subtropical species with distinct host specificities into a single genus-level model violates niche identity and produces biologically implausible response ranges (e.g., elevation up to $6,852\text{ m}$ and $\text{Bio1}$ down to $-6.7\,^\circ\text{C}$).
* **WHAT:** Fit individual species distribution models for the dominant well-sampled species (or ecologically coherent species complexes) paired with their specific host-tree genera.
* **HOW:** Partition the 260 records in Supplementary Table S1 by species. For species with $\ge 15\text{–}20$ spatially independent occurrences (e.g., *S. baumii*, *S. vaninii*, *S. sanghuang*, *S. lonicericola*), fit species-specific MaxEnt models linked exclusively to each fungus's verified host genus (e.g., *Syringa*, *Populus*, *Morus*, *Lonicera*), and compare the sum/union of species-specific suitability maps against the pooled genus model.

#### 3. Fix the Climatic Predictor Set Across Model Comparisons and Control for Sampling Bias
* **WHY:** Selecting collinearity survivors based on preliminary model contribution caused the with-host model to use $\text{Bio12}$ and the without-host model to use $\text{Bio18}$ (Table 1), confounding the ablation test. Moreover, raw fungarium collections are clustered in accessible mountain reserves.
* **WHAT:** Lock the climatic predictor set prior to testing the host-plant effect, and incorporate a target-group background bias file.
* **HOW:** Fix the six abiotic predictors ($\text{Bio1}, \text{Bio3}, \text{Bio7}, \text{Bio12}, \text{Bio15}, \text{Elev}$) identically in both the with-host and without-host models so that the only difference between the two models is the inclusion of the host-plant layer. To account for collection bias across China, sample MaxEnt background points using a kernel density surface of all macrofungal (or polypore) collection localities in HMAS/BJFC/IFP rather than uniform random background sampling across deserts and high plateaus.

#### 4. Report Uncertainty Intervals and Use a Multi-GCM Ensemble
* **WHY:** Relying on a single climate model (BCC-CSM2-MR) and reporting single-point AUC values and area changes to three decimal places (e.g., $155.468\%$, $261.312\text{ km}$) overstates precision.
* **WHAT:** Report mean $\pm$ SD across the 10 cross-validation replicates for all AUC, area, and centroid metrics, and average future projections across at least 3–5 CMIP6 GCMs.
* **HOW:** Extract the test AUC standard deviation from the 10 MaxEnt runs, perform a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the 10 replicate test AUCs between the with-host and without-host models, download WorldClim CMIP6 rasters for at least two additional GCMs (e.g., MIROC6,ACCESS-CM2, or IPSL-CM6A-LR) to compute ensemble mean and spread, and round percentage and distance estimates to two significant digits.
