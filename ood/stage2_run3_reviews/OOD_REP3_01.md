### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates whether resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) network architecture varies across four frequency bands (F1: 0.007–0.052 Hz; F2: 0.052–0.106 Hz; F3: 0.106–0.206 Hz; F4: 0.206–0.438 Hz) using multiband EPI scans ($N = 140$, $\text{TR} = 645\text{ ms}$, multiband acceleration factor = 4) from the publicly available NKI-Rockland Sample. The authors parcellate the Yeo cortical gray matter mask into 2,143 sub-regions, define four frequency passbands via maximal overlap discrete wavelet transform (MODWT) followed by bandpass filtering, and cluster group-averaged binarized correlation matrices (top $1\%$ to $10\%$ densities) across $k = 3\text{–}20$ clusters. Selecting the optimal cluster number $k$ by maximizing a spatial overlap score ($RC_{tk}$, Eq. 1) against 24 Neurosynth task-activation maps, the authors report that the optimal number of cortical networks drops from 16 in F1 and 13 in F2 to 4 in F3 and 3 in F4. They conclude that finely subdivided low-frequency subnetworks functionally integrate into fewer networks at higher frequencies rather than reconfiguring, and they re-analyze a prior opposite-sex compatibility dataset in the F3 band to report lateralized cerebellar and cerebellar–limbic predictive contributions. While the NKI-Rockland dataset is publicly accessible, custom analysis code, derived parcellations, and the secondary compatibility dataset are not provided.

Despite addressing a relevant question regarding frequency-dependent organization in fast-TR rs-fMRI, the study's primary conclusions rest on methodological artifacts, internal procedural contradictions, and physiological mischaracterizations. First, the cluster-selection metric ($RC_{tk}$, Eq. 1) is mathematically biased toward small $k$ whenever spatial signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) declines, and its maximization across 10 cumulative thresholds of the same correlation matrix includes no variance estimation or null calibration. Second, attributing the collapse from 16 to 3–4 clusters above 0.1 Hz to neurobiological "integration" ignores the steep low-pass filtering of the canonical hemodynamic response function (HRF) above 0.15 Hz and the authors' misidentification of fundamental physiological frequencies (stating cardiac fluctuations occur at ~0.2 Hz rather than ~1.0–1.3 Hz, while true respiratory frequencies at 0.2–0.35 Hz fall directly within F3 and F4). Third, the F3 re-analysis of opposite-sex compatibility reports statistically significant cerebellar and cerebellar–limbic contributions even though the F3 clustering was restricted to the cerebral cortex (2,143 sub-regions of Yeo's cortical template) and absorbed the cortical limbic regions (OFC and ATL) into $\text{DMN}_{\text{F3}}$. Resolving these issues requires replacing the biased $RC_{tk}$ criterion with out-of-sample cluster stability and HRF-convolved noise simulations, correcting the physiological frequency accounting, and clarifying the anatomical provenance of the cerebellar and limbic ROIs in the compatibility re-analysis.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Optimal resting-state network resolution decreases from 16 clusters in F1 and 13 in F2 to 4 in F3 and 3 in F4, reflecting functional integration rather than SNR loss: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Eq. (1), $RC_{tk} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \max_j \left(\frac{|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|}{|Ma_i|} \times \frac{|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|}{|Mc_{tj}|}\right)$ evaluates the maximum Fowlkes–Mallows squared overlap between each of 24 broad Neurosynth task masks $Ma_i$ and a single cluster $Mc_{tj}$. Because the sensitivity factor $|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}| / |Ma_i|$ scales inversely with the number of clusters $k$ when a task mask spans distributed cortical systems, $RC_{tk}$ inherently favors coarse partitions ($k = 3\text{–}4$) unless high-SNR long-range correlations sharply delineate focal subnetworks. Furthermore, selection of the "best" $k$ by counting modes across 10 nested density thresholds ($x = 1\%,\dots,10\%$) of the same group matrix treats dependent thresholds as independent replicates without confidence intervals or permutation nulls.

* **The default mode network (DMN) and perceptual networks possess sufficiently strong functional architecture to survive in F4 (0.206–0.438 Hz): [Overstated].**  
  Above 0.2 Hz, sluggish neurovascular coupling attenuates neural BOLD oscillations by more than an order of magnitude, leaving variance dominated by 6 mm Gaussian spatial smoothing, DARTEL interpolation, large-vessel drainage (e.g., sagittal and transverse sinuses adjacent to posterior cingulate and visual cortices), and unmodeled respiratory harmonics (0.2–0.4 Hz) and aliased cardiac pulsatility. In the Methods ("fMRI data preprocessing"), the authors incorrectly state that the cardiac fluctuation spectrum lies "around 0.2 Hz" and the respiratory spectrum at "0.1–0.15 Hz." Because clustering a spatially smoothed correlation matrix under low neural SNR naturally recovers contiguous anatomical lobes (occipital visual cortex, pericentral somatomotor strip, and medial/lateral default regions), persistence of these coarse parcels in F4 cannot be interpreted as preservation of high-frequency neural communication without vascular and spatial-autocorrelation null controls.

* **Wavelet decomposition via MODWT defines participant-independent physiological frequency bands prior to bandpass filtering: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Methods ("Wavelet decomposition and multiband frequency definition"), the authors decompose regional time series into 7 MODWT levels (Daubechies Least Asymmetric 14), apply a fast Fourier transform (FFT) to extract the minimum and maximum frequency across regions for each participant, average those bounds across participants, and then discard the MODWT coefficients to apply standard bandpass filters to the un-decomposed BOLD signals. Mathematically, the nominal passbands of a dyadic MODWT depend strictly on the fixed sampling rate ($f_s = 1 / 0.645\text{ s} = 1.550\text{ Hz}$, yielding nominal bands $f_s / 2^{j+1}$ to $f_s / 2^j$), not on participant-specific data; measuring min/max frequencies of finite-length wavelet outputs via FFT merely captures filter transition-band leakage before making the entire wavelet step redundant by reverting to bandpass filtering.

* **Re-analysis of opposite-sex compatibility data using the F3 network architecture reveals right-dominant cerebellar ($p = 0.050$) and positive cerebellar–limbic ($p = 0.018$) contributions: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In "Parcellation of the cerebral cortex," the parcellation and clustering are restricted exclusively to Yeo's cerebral cortex gray matter template (2,143 cortical sub-regions), and in "RSN architecture for each frequency band," the four F3 clusters consist solely of $\text{VN}_{\text{F3}}$, $\text{SMN}_{\text{F3}}$, $\text{CEN}_{\text{F3}}$, and $\text{DMN}_{\text{F3}}$ (with OFC and ATL absorbed into $\text{DMN}_{\text{F3}}$). The F3 clustering defines neither a cerebellar network nor a distinct limbic network. Consequently, any cerebellar or cerebellar–limbic statistics in the re-analysis must have retained pre-existing anatomical definitions from the original study while altering only the cortical labels and the multiple-comparison denominator for false discovery rate (FDR) correction.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace or Calibrate the $RC_{tk}$ Cluster-Number Selection Criterion Against Spatial Null Models
* **WHY:** Equation (1) penalizes fine parcellations ($k > 10$) when evaluated against broad Neurosynth activation masks, and counting how often a given $k$ ranks first across 10 cumulative density thresholds ($1\%\text{–}10\%$) of the same correlation matrix provides no statistical test of whether $k=4$ in F3 is significantly better than $k=13\text{–}17$.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate cluster stability out-of-sample and benchmark $RC_{tk}$ against spatially autocorrelated null surrogates matched for frequency-specific SNR.
* **HOW:** Perform 100 split-half resamplings of the $N = 140$ NKI-Rockland participants (70 discovery, 70 replication), compute the adjusted Rand index (ARI) and von Mises–Fisher log-likelihood across $k = 3\text{–}20$ in each band, and generate 1,000 phase-randomized (or spatially smoothed white-noise) surrogate time series per band to compute a null distribution $\Delta RC_{tk} = RC_{tk}^{\text{empirical}} - RC_{tk}^{\text{null}}$. Additionally, resolve the contradiction between lines 53 and 66 by clarifying whether the final F1–F4 parcellations were generated by the generative von Mises–Fisher mixture model or by $k$-means clustering on the thresholded adjacency matrices.

#### 2. Control for Hemodynamic Low-Pass Attenuation and Correct Physiological Frequency Ranges
* **WHY:** Resting human cardiac rates are $1.0\text{–}1.3\text{ Hz}$ (not $0.2\text{ Hz}$) and adult respiratory rates are $0.2\text{–}0.35\text{ Hz}$ (not $0.1\text{–}0.15\text{ Hz}$), placing fundamental respiratory variance and aliased cardiac harmonics directly inside F3 ($0.106\text{–}0.206\text{ Hz}$) and F4 ($0.206\text{–}0.438\text{ Hz}$) where canonical HRF power is severely attenuated.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the residual physiological variance in F1–F4 after RETROICOR and test whether F1 network architecture collapses to the F3/F4 4-cluster solution simply by degrading F1's SNR to match F3/F4.
* **HOW:** Compute the power spectral density of the peripheral photoplethysmography and respiratory belt recordings alongside the BOLD signals pre- and post-RETROICOR across F1–F4. Then, add spatially matched Gaussian and physiological residual noise to the F1 time series to match the empirical temporal SNR of F3 and F4, and re-run the clustering pipeline to test whether $k=16$ in F1 collapses to $k=3\text{–}4$ purely from SNR degradation. Also note that on a multiband-4 acquisition ($\text{TR} = 645\text{ ms}$), slice-timing correction (if applied) should use multiband slice-acquisition timings prior to motion realignment or be omitted in favor of sub-second derivative nuisance regression.

#### 3. Eliminate Redundant Wavelet-to-Bandpass Filtering and Fix Methodological Descriptions
* **WHY:** Decomposing signals with MODWT only to measure FFT min/max bounds and then bandpass-filtering the raw BOLD time series discards the wavelet decomposition while introducing arbitrary passband boundaries.
* **WHAT:** Conduct the connectivity and clustering analysis directly on the scale-specific MODWT wavelet detail coefficients $d_1, \dots, d_6$ (or wavelet correlation matrices) rather than re-filtering the time series.
* **HOW:** Compute pairwise wavelet correlation matrices directly from the MODWT detail coefficients at scales corresponding to F1–F4 for each of the 2,143 sub-regions, and verify that the resulting cluster architectures match those obtained from Rectangular/Butterworth bandpass filtering.

#### 4. Clarify and Re-Validate the Opposite-Sex Compatibility Re-Analysis
* **WHY:** The F3 cortical clustering does not include the cerebellum and merges cortical limbic regions (OFC, ATL) into $\text{DMN}_{\text{F3}}$, making the origin of the reported cerebellar laterality ($p = 0.050$) and cerebellar–limbic inter-network ($p = 0.018$) results opaque.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly specify the dataset size ($N$), ROI parcellation, and how cerebellar and limbic networks were defined in F3, and correct the permutation and FDR procedures.
* **HOW:** Add a table in the Methods or Results listing all networks included in the compatibility re-analysis, the exact number of ROIs per network (including how cerebellar and limbic ROIs were parcellated if outside the 2,143 cortical nodes), the unmodified $p$-values Alongside the FDR-corrected $p$-values across all $\binom{k}{2}$ inter-network pairs, and replace simple ROI-label randomization with spatially constrained spin permutations (or Moran spectral randomization) that preserve hemispheric symmetry and spatial autocorrelation.

#### 5. Technical and Editorial Corrections
* **WHY:** Multiple textual errors reverse the physical direction of SNR across frequency bands and misstate physiological constants.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
