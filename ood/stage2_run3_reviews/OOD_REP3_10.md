### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents a purely computational virtual screening pipeline aimed at identifying small-molecule inhibitors of vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-2 (VEGFR2/KDR) from the eMolecules library ($\sim 10.5\text{ million}$ compounds). Using 5,789 Lipinski Rule-of-Five (RO5) compliant VEGFR2 bioactivity records from ChEMBL (binarized at $\text{pIC}_{50} = 6.8$), the authors train random forest (RF), support vector machine (SVM), and artificial neural network (ANN) classifiers on 2048-bit Morgan3 fingerprints via the TeachOpenCADD workflow. Selecting 61 PAINS-filtered compounds with RF predicted probability $> 85\%$, they rank candidates by 2D fingerprint similarity (Morgan2 and MACCS) against seven known VEGFR2 inhibitors and select two hit compounds—`molecule1` (PubChem CID 17379777) and `molecule2` (PubChem CID 4682044)—for downstream *in silico* evaluation using a WEKA RF regression model ($\text{predicted pIC}_{50} = 7.23$ and $7.31$), SwissADME/ProTox-II/pkCSM pharmacokinetic and toxicity predictions, AutoDock Vina docking (PDB: `4ASE`), and triplicate GROMACS molecular dynamics (MD) simulations with MM-PBSA binding free energy estimation ($-89.1$ and $-95.3\text{ kJ/mol}$ vs. $-87.4\text{ kJ/mol}$ for regorafenib). No custom code, trained model files, or list of the 61 intermediate hits are deposited in a public repository.

Although the multi-step *in silico* funnel follows a recognizable tutorial workflow (TeachOpenCADD), the study lacks any experimental biochemical or cellular validation and contains multiple methodological, numerical, and reporting flaws. Most notably, Table 3 contains identical copy-pasted similarity values (to 6 decimal places) for two structurally distinct reference drugs (pazopanib and axitinib), counts each reference drug (`Compound 0`) as one of the "Top 4" hits, and fails to map the internal table IDs (`38`, `23`, `40`, `16`, etc.) to `molecule1` and `molecule2`. Furthermore, the QSAR models rely on random 80/20 splits of congeneric ChEMBL series without scaffold splitting or verification that the 11 "external" approved drugs were excluded from the ChEMBL training set; docking scores for the 7 reference drugs and 2 candidates are never reported; MD simulation length is omitted from the Methods; MM-PBSA energy differences of $1.7\text{--}7.9\text{ kJ/mol}$ ($0.4\text{--}1.9\text{ kcal/mol}$) are interpreted without error bars across the triplicate runs; and the mathematical equations for MAE, RMSE, and variable definitions ($\bar{y}$ vs. $\hat{y}$) are written incorrectly.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Random forest classification ($\text{AUC} = 0.91$) and regression ($R^2 = 0.788$ on test set, $R^2 = 0.817$ on 11 approved inhibitors) accurately predict VEGFR2 inhibitory activity ($\text{pIC}_{50} = 7.23$ and $7.31$ for `molecule1` and `molecule2`): [Fragile].**  
  First, the 5,789 ChEMBL compounds were partitioned using a simple random 80/20 split rather than a Bemis–Murcko scaffold split, which places close structural analogs from the same medicinal chemistry series into both training and test sets and inflates apparent generalization. Second, hyperparameters ("number of trees, SVM kernel, gamma, and hidden layers") were tuned to maximize test performance without a separate validation split or nested cross-validation. Third, the 5,789 training/testing compounds were extracted directly from ChEMBL's VEGFR2 target data, where approved inhibitors (sorafenib, pazopanib, regorafenib, axitinib, lenvatinib, etc.) have multiple curated entries; the manuscript never states that the "11 approved inhibitors" used as an external test set in Table 4 were deduplicated and removed from the ChEMBL training set prior to model fitting. Fourth, the RF regression training $R^2 = 0.9832$ vs. 5-fold CV $R^2 = 0.7779$ ($\text{RMSE} = 0.739\text{ log units}$, i.e., a $5.5$-fold error in $\text{IC}_{50}$) indicates substantial variance that is not propagated to the point predictions of $7.23$ and $7.31$.

* **2D fingerprint similarity screening against seven known VEGFR2 inhibitors identifies `molecule1` and `molecule2` as the most frequent top-ranked analogues (Table 3): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Table 3 exhibits severe data integrity errors. Specifically, the entries under **Pazopanib** (compounds `38`, `23`, and `20` with `Tanimoto_morgan = 0.232877, 0.226667, 0.225352` and `Tanimoto_maccs = 0.509804, 0.750000, 0.461538`) and the entries under **Axitinib** (compounds `38`, `23`, and `20` with the exact same 12 numbers to 6 decimal places) are identical duplicates, which is chemically impossible given the distinct molecular graphs of pazopanib and axitinib. Moreover, Table 3 labels the self-comparison of each reference drug (`Compound 0`, similarity $= 1$) as one of the "Top 4 molecules", and never discloses which internal compound indices (`16`, `20`, `23`, `37`, `38`, `40`, `42`, `43`, `46`, `55`, `56`) correspond to `molecule1` (CID 17379777) and `molecule2` (CID 4682044).

* **Molecular docking and MD/MM-PBSA simulations demonstrate that `molecule1` ($-89.1\text{ kJ/mol}$) and `molecule2` ($-95.3\text{ kJ/mol}$) bind VEGFR2 as strongly as or more strongly than regorafenib ($-87.4\text{ kJ/mol}$): [Overstated].**  
  Although the Methods state that AutoDock Vina docking was performed on seven reference drugs and the two candidate hits, no docking scores (kcal/mol) or RMSD redocking validations of the co-crystallized ligand in PDB `4ASE` (tivozanib) are reported anywhere in the text or tables. For the GROMACS MM-PBSA calculations, although simulations were run in triplicate, the main text and Abstract report single point values ($-89.1$, $-95.3$, and $-87.4\text{ kJ/mol}$) without standard deviations or standard errors across replicates, and without conformational entropy ($-T\Delta S$) corrections. Because single-trajectory MM-PBSA typically carries replicate standard deviations of $5\text{--}15\text{ kJ/mol}$, differences of $1.7$ to $7.9\text{ kJ/mol}$ cannot be claimed as statistically distinguishable or superior binding.

* **`molecule1` and `molecule2` possess favorable ADMET and safety profiles suitable for anti-angiogenesis drug development: [Fragile].**  
  The *in silico* ADMET predictions themselves highlight major liabilities that contradict a favorable profile: `molecule2` has a predicted oral $\text{LD}_{50}$ of $393\text{ mg/kg}$ in ProTox-II (more than twice as acutely toxic as regorafenib at $800\text{ mg/kg}$, with only $68.1\%$ prediction accuracy; Table 5) and falls in the gray zone of the BOILED-Egg plot indicating poor passive gastrointestinal absorption (Fig. 7). Conversely, `molecule1` is predicted by ESOL to be poorly water-soluble and violates the lipophilicity/unsaturation bounds of the SwissADME bioavailability radar. Without *in vitro* kinase inhibition or cytotoxicity assays, both compounds remain unvalidated computational hits.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct the Duplicated Similarity Table and Map Internal Hit IDs to Chemical Structures
* **WHY:** Having identical 6-decimal-place similarity scores for pazopanib and axitinib in Table 3 invalidates the reported similarity ranking, and omitting the mapping between internal IDs (`16`, `38`, `40`, etc.) and PubChem CIDs prevents verification of how `molecule1` and `molecule2` were chosen.
* **WHAT:** Re-compute the Morgan2 and MACCS Tanimoto/Dice similarities for axitinib and all other reference inhibitors across the 61 filtered candidates, and provide a complete lookup table of all 61 compounds.
* **HOW:** Run the RDKit `DataStructs.TanimotoSimilarity` and `DiceSimilarity` script across all 61 SMILES strings against each of the 7 reference drugs, replace the corrupted Axitinib rows in Table 3, list the top 4 *actual* candidate compounds per drug (excluding `Compound 0`), explicitly label which compound numbers correspond to CID 17379777 (`molecule1`) and CID 4682044 (`molecule2`), and include a supplementary CSV/table containing the SMILES, PubChem CIDs, RF classification probabilities, and predicted $\text{pIC}_{50}$ values for all 61 hits.

#### 2. Eliminate Data Leakage in QSAR Model Evaluation Using Scaffold Splitting and Training-Set Deduplication
* **WHY:** Random 80/20 splitting of ChEMBL congeneric series and evaluating an "external" set of 11 approved VEGFR2 inhibitors that are already present in ChEMBL overestimates QSAR accuracy.
* **WHAT:** Re-evaluate the classification and regression models under Bemis–Murcko scaffold splitting and verify that all 11 approved reference inhibitors (and their stereoisomers/salts) are strictly excluded from the training set.
* **HOW:** Using RDKit (`MurckoScaffold.GetScaffoldForMol`), group the 5,789 ChEMBL compounds by scaffold and partition them into 80% training, 10% validation (for hyperparameter tuning), and 10% test sets so no scaffold is shared across splits. Before training the WEKA RF regression model, strip all InChIKeys matching the 11 approved VEGFR2 inhibitors from the ChEMBL dataset, and report 95% prediction intervals (from the variance across the 1,000 RF trees) for the predicted $\text{pIC}_{50}$ of `molecule1` and `molecule2`.

#### 3. Report Docking Validation, Full MD Protocol Details, and Replicate Uncertainty for MM-PBSA
* **WHY:** Claiming strong binding affinity without reporting docking scores, redocking RMSD, total MD simulation time in Methods, or replicate standard deviations for MM-PBSA leaves the structural claims unsupported.
* **WHAT:** Add a table of AutoDock Vina docking scores for all 7 reference inhibitors and 2 candidates, validate the docking protocol by redocking the native `4ASE` ligand, specify total MD simulation length, and report mean $\pm$ SD across the 3 MD replicates for all MM-PBSA energy terms.
* **HOW:** Redock the co-crystallized ligand (tivozanib) into PDB `4ASE` and report the heavy-atom RMSD relative to the crystal pose. Tabulate the Vina binding affinities ($\text{kcal/mol}$) for all 9 docked ligands. In the Methods ("Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation"), state the exact total production simulation time per replicate (e.g., $100\text{ ns}$) and the sampling interval used for `g_mmpbsa`. In the Abstract, Results, and Supplementary Table S3, report MM-PBSA $\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ as $\text{mean} \pm \text{SD}$ across the three independent trajectories.

#### 4. Fix Mathematical Equations and Software Misattributions
* **WHY:** Incorrect equations for standard statistical metrics and false software attributions in figure captions undermine the technical credibility of the manuscript.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Equations for Classification Metrics (Materials and methods, p. 3):** Add parentheses around numerators and denominators: $\text{Accuracy} = \frac{\text{TP} + \text{TN}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN} + \text{TN}}$, $\text{Sensitivity} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$, and $\text{Specificity} = \frac{\text{TN}}{\text{TN} + \text{FP}}$.
  2. **Equations for Regression Metrics (Materials and methods, p. 3):** Correct the MAE formula by adding absolute value bars and subscript $i$: $\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |y_i - \hat{y}_i|$. Correct the RMSE formula by adding the square root, opening parenthesis, and subscript $i$: $\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$. In the line immediately below, swap the definitions of $\bar{y}$ and $\hat{y}$ so that $\hat{y}_i$ denotes the predicted value and $\bar{y}$ denotes the mean of the observed values.
  3. **Table 3 Typo:** In the Ki8751 block (row `56`), `Tanimoto_maccs` is printed as `0.7660000` (7 decimal places) and lies out of descending order below row `40` (`0.760000`). Check and correct the value.
  4. **Figure Captions 3 and 5:** Figure 3 (physicochemical radar plots) and Figure 5 (2D molecular structure similarity maps) state `The figure is created by using scikit-learn v 0.23.1`. `scikit-learn` does not render chemical structure similarity maps or matplotlib radar plots; credit `RDKit` (`rdkit.Chem.Draw.SimilarityMaps`) and `Matplotlib` as used in the TeachOpenCADD notebooks.
