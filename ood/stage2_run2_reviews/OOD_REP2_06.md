### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study introduces `iNGNN-DTI`, a deep learning architecture for binary drug–target interaction (DTI) prediction that integrates 2D drug molecular graphs (RDKit) and 3D AlphaFold2 protein contact graphs ($8\text{ \AA}$ $C_\alpha$ cutoff) encoded via a $k$-subgraph Nested Graph Neural Network (NGNN) with virtual nodes, a bidirectional cross-Attention-Free Transformer (cross-AFT) interaction module, and sequence embeddings from pre-trained chemical (Chemformer) and protein (ESM) foundation models. Evaluated across five random 7:1:2 splits of the Davis, KIBA, and BIOSNAP benchmarks, as well as cold-drug and cold-target splits on Davis, `iNGNN-DTI` reports higher mean AUROC and AUPRC than DeepDTA, MolTrans, ML-DTI, and DGraphDTA. The authors also visualize implicit cross-AFT attention weights against PDB crystal complexes and perform virtual docking of top-scoring BIOSNAP compounds against human ACE2. Source code is publicly available on GitHub.

Combining subgraph-pooled GNN representations of AlphaFold2 contact maps with pre-trained chemical and protein language models is a sensible engineering design, and providing public code is commendable. However, several methodological, mathematical, and biophysical weaknesses affect the manuscript's claims: (1) the ablation study on Davis (Table 6) contradicts the claim that pre-trained embeddings consistently boost performance, as adding Chemformer + ESM embeddings *reduces* AUPRC both without cross-AFT ($0.464 \to 0.431$) and with cross-AFT ($0.478 \to 0.473$); (2) the mathematical formulation of cross-AFT (Eqs. 9, 11–12, 14–15) defines a fixed position-bias matrix $w \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{drug}} \times N_{\text{prot}}}$ that violates graph permutation invariance and variable molecular sizes, omits the graph pooling operation before feature concatenation, and contains index errors in Eq. (15); (3) cold-split gains on unseen drugs fall well within 1 SD of run-to-run variance ($\pm 0.049$–$0.070$), and Table 5 contains a duplicated entry; and (4) the COVID-19 ACE2 case study confuses binding free energy with binding affinity and conflates catalytic zinc-cleft binding of ACE2 with blocking SARS-CoV-2 spike attachment. These issues can be addressed through additional ablations, mathematical corrections, and reframing of the docking section.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **iNGNN-DTI outperforms sequence and GNN baselines on random splits of Davis, KIBA, and BIOSNAP: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Across random splits (Table 4), `iNGNN-DTI` achieves the highest mean AUROC and AUPRC on Davis ($0.931$ / $0.473$), KIBA ($0.915$ / $0.753$), and BIOSNAP ($0.934$ / $0.939$). However, two caveats apply. First, on KIBA, the gain over DeepDTA ($0.912 \pm 0.0037$ vs. $0.915 \pm 0.0016$ AUROC; $0.743 \pm 0.0127$ vs. $0.753 \pm 0.0071$ AUPRC) is marginal and within overlapping error bars. Second, on Davis, Table 4 reports $\text{AUPRC} = 0.316 \pm 0.0447$ for `DGraphDTA (Alphafold2)`, whereas the authors' own baseline `GNN` in Table 6 (Test 1, which uses the exact same DGraphDTA 78-D drug features, 54-D protein PPM/physicochemical features, and $8\text{ \AA}$ AlphaFold2 contact graphs) achieves $\text{AUPRC} = 0.447 \pm 0.0181$. Thus, over $80\%$ of the claimed "+23.8% AUPRC improvement" on Davis ($0.381 \to 0.473$) is already achieved by the base GNN ($0.447$) and vanilla NGNN ($0.464$, Test 2) before adding either cross-AFT or pre-trained language models.

* **Integrating both the cross-AFT module and pre-trained Chemformer/ESM features improves DTI prediction over vanilla NGNN: [Overstated].**  
  Table 6 (Davis ablation study) shows that while `NGNN + cross AFT` (Test 3) achieves the highest AUPRC of all models ($0.478 \pm 0.0222$), adding the pre-trained Chemformer and ESM embeddings on top of `NGNN` without cross-AFT (Test 4) causes AUPRC to drop sharply from $0.464 \pm 0.0357$ to $0.431 \pm 0.0103$ (worse than the plain GNN in Test 1 at $0.447$). Similarly, adding the pre-trained embeddings to `NGNN + cross AFT` (Test 5, full `iNGNN-DTI`) reduces mean AUPRC from $0.478$ to $0.473$. Furthermore, the ablation study was not run on the cold-drug or cold-target splits (Table 5), which is the specific regime where pre-trained foundation models were hypothesized to help (Introduction, line 31).

* **Implicit attention weights from the cross-AFT module accurately localize protein binding pockets and interacting drug atoms: [Fragile].**  
  As the authors candidly acknowledge in the Conclusion (line 422), "the attention weights assigned to the protein target do not always exhibit a strong correlation with the actual positions of the residues." In Section 3.3.1 (line 337), plotting "the residues with the highest weight scores in each dimension" selects up to $d$ residues ($d = 128$ feature dimensions) per protein, which inevitably scatters pink markers across surface loops and non-binding domains (Fig. 3). No quantitative metric (such as ROC-AUC or enrichment factor of attention weights within $5\text{ \AA}$ of the co-crystallized ligand across PDB complexes in Davis/KIBA) is computed to verify that cross-AFT localizes pockets better than random surface residues. Additionally, the mathematical definition of cross-AFT in Eq. (9) includes a pairwise position bias $w \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{drug}} \times N_{\text{prot}}}$, which is ill-defined for molecular graphs of varying atom/residue counts and arbitrary node indexing.

* **The ACE2 case study identifies promising COVID-19 therapeutics (e.g., marimastat, erythromycin) via DTI prediction and virtual docking: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, on line 399, the authors state that "erythromycin, exhibiting the lowest binding affinity with a score of $-14.5\text{ kcal/mol}$, has been the subject of other studies where it is recognized as a high-potential drug for COVID-19." In thermodynamics, a more negative docking score ($\Delta G = -14.5\text{ kcal/mol}$) indicates *stronger* (higher) binding affinity, not "lowest binding affinity"; moreover, $-14.5\text{ kcal/mol}$ for a large macrolide antibiotic ($734\text{ Da}$) in empirical docking is a well-known molecular-weight scoring artifact. Second, the biological premise of screening BIOSNAP for generalized "ACE2 binders" conflates two distinct mechanisms: small molecules that bind ACE2's catalytic zinc metalloprotease cleft (such as the hydroxamate metalloprotease inhibitor marimastat, DB00786) inhibit endogenous ACE2 physiological carboxypeptidase function—which is protective against acute lung injury—without necessarily blocking the distant extracellular Spike RBD–ACE2 protein-protein interaction interface.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Run the Ablation Study on the Unseen-Drug and Unseen-Protein Splits (Table 5)
* **WHY:** On the random split of Davis (Table 6), adding Chemformer and ESM embeddings degrades AUPRC ($0.464 \to 0.431$ and $0.478 \to 0.473$) because the 68 Davis drugs and 442 kinases are already seen during training, making pre-trained embeddings redundant or prone to overfitting on the majority negative class ($95\%$ negatives). Conversely, the paper's core motivation for using Chemformer and ESM is generalization to unseen drugs and proteins (Section 3.2).
* **WHAT:** Report the 5-row ablation table (Tests 1–5 from Table 6) on the `unseen drugs` and `unseen proteins` splits of Davis.
* **HOW:** Execute the existing ablation configurations (`GNN`, `NGNN`, `NGNN + cross-AFT`, `NGNN + Chemformer/ESM`, and full `iNGNN-DTI`) on the 5 cold-drug and 5 cold-protein splits from Section 3.2 and add these columns to Table 6 to demonstrate whether pre-trained sequence models drive the cold-start AUPRC gains ($0.169 \to 0.224$ and $0.259 \to 0.296$).

#### 2. Correct the Cross-AFT Mathematical Formulation and Graph Pooling Equations
* **WHY:** Equations (9)–(15) contain mathematical omissions and index errors that make the architecture impossible to reconstruct from the text alone:
  1. In Eq. (9), $w \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{drug}} \times N_{\text{prot}}}$ assumes fixed node counts $N_{\text{drug}}, N_{\text{prot}}$ and canonical node ordering across arbitrary molecular graphs.
  2. In Eqs. (11)–(12), node-level matrices $Y_{\text{drug}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{drug}} \times d}$ and $Y_{\text{prot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{prot}} \times d}$ are concatenated directly with 1D global vectors $P_{\text{drug}}, P_{\text{prot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ without specifying how the variable-length node dimension ($N_{\text{drug}}, N_{\text{prot}}$) is pooled into a fixed-length graph vector.
  3. In Eq. (15), the numerator writes $K_{\text{prot}}^{n,i} + w_n$ instead of $K_{\text{prot}}^{n',i} + w_{n,n'}$, and the index range writes $n = 1,\dots,N_{\text{prot}}$ instead of $n = 1,\dots,N_{\text{drug}}$ and $n' = 1,\dots,N_{\text{prot}}$.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Update Section 2.3.2, Section 2.3.3, and Section 3.3 to: (a) state explicitly how zero-padding/truncation to maximum node counts ($N_{\text{drug}}^{\max}, N_{\text{prot}}^{\max}$) is handled or whether $w_{n,n'}$ is omitted/zeroed; (b) write the explicit graph-level readout function (e.g., global mean or max pooling $\bar{y}_{\text{drug}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{drug}}} \sum_{n=1}^{N_{\text{drug}}} Y_{\text{drug}}^n \in \mathbb{R}^d$) before concatenating with $P_{\text{drug}}$ in Eqs. (11)–(12); and (c) fix the indices $n \in \{1,\dots,N_{\text{drug}}\}$ and $n' \in \{1,\dots,N_{\text{prot}}\}$ in Eq. (15).

#### 3. Quantify Pocket Localization Accuracy Against Ground-Truth PDB Binding Sites
* **WHY:** Visual inspection of 4 cherry-picked complexes (Fig. 3) where the top residue per feature dimension ($d = 128$) is plotted does not establish whether the cross-AFT weights enrich for true binding-site residues above chance.
* **WHAT:** Compute a quantitative binding-site localization metric across all ground-truth PDB crystal structures corresponding to the test pairs.
* **HOW:** For each protein-ligand PDB complex, aggregate the cross-AFT attention matrix $A_{\text{prot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{prot}} \times N_{\text{drug}} \times d}$ into a scalar importance score per residue $s_m = \frac{1}{N_{\text{drug}} d} \sum_{n,i} A_{\text{prot}}^{m,n,i}$, define ground-truth binding pocket residues as those within $5.0\text{ \AA}$ of any heavy atom of the co-crystallized ligand, and report the mean AUROC or top-$k$ precision of $s_m$ compared to a random-residue baseline.

#### 4. Fix Numerical Typos and Reframe the COVID-19 ACE2 Docking Discussion
* **WHY:** Inaccurate terminology and copy-paste errors detract from the technical quality of the paper.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Table 5 (line 306):** In the `DeepDTA` row, `unseen drugs` AUPRC (`0.145 ± 0.0300`) and `unseen proteins` AUPRC (`0.145 ± 0.0300`) are identical to four decimal places; check the raw evaluation logs and insert the correct `unseen proteins` AUPRC for DeepDTA.
  2. **Section 3.1 (line 256):** Correct `(from 0.382 to 0.473)` to `(from 0.381 to 0.473)` to match ML-DTI's AUPRC (`0.381`) in Table 4.
  3. **Section 3.5 (line 399):** Change "exhibiting the lowest binding affinity with a score of $-14.5\text{ kcal/mol}$" to "exhibiting the lowest binding free energy (strongest predicted affinity) with a score of $-14.5\text{ kcal/mol}$," and add a caveat noting that binding the ACE2 catalytic cleft does not automatically block SARS-CoV-2 Spike RBD attachment unless the binding pocket overlaps the RBD-binding interface (residues 19–83 and 353–357).
