### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript models the past, current, and future potential geographic distribution of the endangered Chinese endemic tree *Sinojackia xylocarpa* using the `biomod2` ensemble species distribution modeling (SDM) package in R. Combining field surveys (2022–2024), herbarium databases (CVH, NSII, PPBC), and literature records, the authors compile 104 records and spatially rarefy them at a $1\text{ km} \times 1\text{ km}$ resolution to $N = 21$ occurrence points across eastern and central China (Table S1). After Pearson correlation filtering ($|r| < 0.8$), they retain 10 bioclimatic variables for the Last Interglacial (LIG), 11 bioclimatic variables for the Middle Holocene (MH), and 22 variables (9 bioclimatic, 2 topographic, and 11 soil variables) for the current and future periods (2050s and 2070s under RCP2.6, RCP4.5, and RCP8.5). Selecting six algorithms with $\text{AUC} > 0.8$ and $\text{TSS} > 0.7$ (ANN, CTA, FDA, GBM, MaxEnt, RF) trained against 1,000 random pseudo-absences across China, they report an ensemble $\text{AUC} = 0.9960 \pm 0.0641$ and $\text{TSS} = 0.9500 \pm 0.0610$, identify precipitation of the driest quarter (Bio17, $61.0\%$ contribution) as the dominant predictor, estimate a current suitable area of $697,200\text{ km}^2$ with only $3.91\%$ covered by national or provincial nature reserves, and project an average $10.97\%$ contraction and northeastward centroid shift under future climate scenarios.

The field survey effort documenting extant wild populations of *S. xylocarpa* in Anhui and Jiangsu provides useful botanical records for a rare species. However, the statistical modeling and paleoclimate comparisons suffer from severe methodological flaws: (1) fitting complex machine learning algorithms with $p = 22$ predictors to only $N = 21$ presence records ($\sim 15\text{–}16$ training presences per 75/25 split) against 1,000 country-wide pseudo-absences induces severe overfitting and artificially inflates AUC/TSS; (2) the LIG, MH, and current/future models are parameterized with non-overlapping sets of bioclimatic and edaphic predictors (10 vs. 11 vs. 22 variables; Table 1), confounding temporal habitat comparisons with changes in model specification; and (3) the manuscript contains factual errors regarding CMIP6 vs. CMIP5 climate models, the geological age of the Last Interglacial, and contradictory statements between the Results and Discussion regarding past habitat expansion versus contraction. Re-running the SDM with parsimonious predictor sets ($p \le 4\text{–}5$) matched across all time periods, restricting pseudo-absence background sampling to an ecologically accessible buffer, and correcting the chronological and textual contradictions are necessary to salvage the projections.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The six-model `biomod2` ensemble achieves near-perfect predictive accuracy ($\text{AUC} = 0.9960 \pm 0.0641$, $\text{TSS} = 0.9500 \pm 0.0610$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Training flexible non-parametric algorithms (RF, GBM, ANN, MaxEnt, CTA, FDA) with $p = 22$ environmental predictors on only $N = 21$ presence points (15–16 training points and 5–6 testing points in each 75/25 split) violates basic sample-size-to-parameter ratios ($p > n$, less than 1 presence per predictor). Furthermore, sampling 1,000 pseudo-absences across the entire $9.6\text{ million km}^2$ expanse of China (including Tibetan Plateau, desert, and boreal regions) trivially separates subtropical eastern China from the rest of the country, inflating AUC and TSS. Note also that $\text{AUC} = 0.9960 \pm 0.0641$ implies an upper standard deviation bound of $1.0601$, exceeding the theoretical maximum of $1.0$.

* **Habitat suitability drivers shifted across epochs—from Bio10 ($21.7\%$), Bio17 ($19.6\%$), and Bio4 ($19.1\%$) in the Last Interglacial, to Bio15 ($27.5\%$) and Bio17 ($23.0\%$) in the Middle Holocene, to Bio17 ($61.0\%$) in the current period—accompanied by historical habitat changes: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  As shown in Table 1, the authors did not use the same predictor variables across epochs, nor did they fit a single model and project it onto paleoclimate layers. Instead, they refitted separate models with different variable subsets for each epoch: e.g., Bio10 (the #1 predictor in LIG at $21.7\%$) was completely excluded from the MH model; Bio11 ($16.6\%$ in LIG, $15.5\%$ in MH) and Bio13 ($3.7\%$ in LIG, $8.7\%$ in MH) were excluded from the Current model; and Bio1, Bio6, Bio12, Elevation, Slope, and 11 soil variables were included only in the Current model. Consequently, differences in variable importance (Table 1) and predicted suitable area between LIG ($63.84 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$), MH ($64.50 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$), and Current ($69.72 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$; Table 3) are artifacts of changing the predictor variables rather than climatic shifts. Moreover, Results Section 3.4 (line 223) claims *"a continuous contraction and fragmentation of suitable habitats from the Last Interglacial to the Middle Holocene,"* directly contradicting Table 3 and Discussion Section 4.4 (line 280), which state that suitable habitat *expanded* from $638,400\text{ km}^2$ in the LIG to $645,000\text{ km}^2$ in the MH and $697,200\text{ km}^2$ at present.

* **Under future climate scenarios (2050s and 2070s), suitable habitat for *S. xylocarpa* will decrease by an average of $10.97\%$ and shift its centroid northeastward: [Fragile].**  
  Because the current/future model is overparameterized ($p = 22$ on $N = 21$ presences), its projections across six future scenarios are noisy and non-monotonic: total suitable area decreases in the 2050s under RCP8.5 ($-16.32\%$) but *increases* in the 2070s under RCP8.5 ($+4.37\%$), while highly suitable area increases in four of the six future scenarios (mean $+6.18\%$; Table 3) and the RCP8.5 centroid jumps northeast in the 2050s ($115.722^\circ\text{E}, 29.221^\circ\text{N}$) before swinging $413\text{ km}$ northwest into Hubei/Chongqing in the 2070s ($111.412^\circ\text{E}, 30.402^\circ\text{N}$; line 243).

* **Only $3.91\%$ of the predicted current suitable habitat ($697,200\text{ km}^2$) overlaps with national ($1.84\%$) or provincial ($2.15\%$) nature reserves: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the GIS overlay of the predicted suitability raster with protected area polygons is straightforward, the $697,200\text{ km}^2$ baseline area is heavily inflated by commission errors (extending into Taiwan, Guangxi, Guizhou, and Guangdong where the species has never been recorded, as the authors acknowledge for Taiwan in Section 4.3) caused by the low `maxSSS` threshold ($0.1916$) and country-wide pseudo-absence sampling.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reduce Predictor Dimensionality to Respect Sample Size ($N = 21$) and Restrict Background Extent
* **WHY:** With $N = 21$ total occurrences (15–16 training points), fitting 22 predictors violates the rule of thumb of at least 5–10 presences per predictor, causing severe overfitting. Simultaneously, drawing pseudo-absences from all of China artificially inflates discrimination metrics (AUC/TSS) and distorts response curves.
* **WHAT:** Restrict the predictor set to at most 4–5 ecologically relevant, non-collinear variables ($|r| < 0.7$) and sample pseudo-absences within a biogeographically accessible buffer (e.g., $200\text{–}500\text{ km}$ around known occurrences or the subtropical evergreen/deciduous broad-leaved forest biome of southeastern China).
* **HOW:** Select the top 4 uncorrelated variables (e.g., Bio17, Bio10, Bio18, and Elevation, which already account for $84.9\%$ of importance), generate 500–1,000 pseudo-absences within a $300\text{ km}$ buffer of the 21 occurrence points using `biomod2::BIOMOD_FormatingData`, and use spatially blocked cross-validation (`blockCV` or `ENMeval` jackknife/checkerboard) to obtain realistic AUC and TSS estimates.

#### 2. Enforce Identical Environmental Predictors Across Past, Current, and Future Projections
* **WHY:** Training separate models with 10 variables for LIG, a different 11 variables for MH, and 22 variables for Current/Future makes it impossible to attribute differences in predicted distribution or variable contributions to climate change rather than variable inclusion/exclusion.
* **WHAT:** Calibrate the SDM on current occurrences and current climate layers using a single, fixed set of bioclimatic predictors (and static elevation if justified), and project that single calibrated ensemble model onto the LIG, MH, 2050s, and 2070s climate layers.
* **HOW:** In `biomod2`, train the model once on current climate predictors (`BIOMOD_Modeling` + `BIOMOD_EnsembleModeling`) and call `BIOMOD_Projection` and `BIOMOD_EnsembleForecasting` on the standardized LIG, MH, 2050s, and 2070s raster stacks containing the exact same bioclimatic variables. If soil variables are unavailable for LIG/MH, run a sensitivity comparison showing Current vs. Future with and without soil variables (noting that all 11 soil variables combined contributed only $2.6\%$ in Table 1).

#### 3. Reconcile Contradictions in Paleoclimate Trends and Future Scenario Descriptions
* **WHY:** Results Section 3.4 (line 223) states that suitable habitat showed *"a continuous contraction... from the Last Interglacial to the Middle Holocene,"* whereas Table 3 ($63.84 \to 64.50 \to 69.72 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$) and Discussion Section 4.4 (line 280) state that suitable habitat *expanded* from LIG to MH to Current. Similarly, Results Section 3.5 (line 236) claims RCP4.5 in the 2050s showed the smallest change ($-11.20\%$), overlooking that RCP8.5 in the 2070s changed by $+4.37\%$.
* **WHAT:** Harmonize Sections 3.4, 3.5, and 4.4 so that all textual descriptions match the numerical values in Table 3.
* **HOW:** Revise line 223 to state that total suitable habitat increased slightly from the LIG ($638,400\text{ km}^2$) to the MH ($645,000\text{ km}^2$) and Current ($697,200\text{ km}^2$), while clarifying that *highly* suitable habitat decreased ($43.46 \to 38.20 \to 34.15 \times 10^4\text{ km}^2$). In line 236, specify *"among the five scenarios showing a decrease"* when referring to the $-11.20\%$ value under 2050s RCP4.5.

#### 4. Technical, Factual, and Editorial Corrections
* **WHY:** Major factual errors in paleoclimate chronology and climate model nomenclature undermine the credibility of the Methods section.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Line 52 (Last Interglacial age):** Correct *"the Last Interglacial period, approximately 12,000–14,000 years ago"* to **approximately 120,000–140,000 years ago** (120–140 ka BP); 12,000–14,000 years ago corresponds to the Late Glacial period, not the LIG.
  2. **Line 52 (CMIP5 vs. CMIP6):** Correct *"Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6 (CMIP6)"* to **CMIP5**. The three GCMs listed (`BCC-CSM1-1`, `CCSM4`, `MIROC-ESM`), the `RCP2.6`/`RCP4.5`/`RCP8.5` pathways, and the WorldClim v1.4 2050s/2070s downscaled layers are all CMIP5 products (CMIP6 uses SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, SSP5-8.5 and 2041–2060 / 2061–2080 from newer GCMs).
  3. **Table 2 (line 153):** Check how the standard deviation of the Ensemble AUC ($0.9960 \pm 0.0641$) was computed across the 10 runs, as an SD of $0.0641$ with a mean of $0.9960$ is arithmetically impossible unless one of the 10 runs had an AUC $\approx 0.80$ while the remaining nine were $\approx 1.00$; report median and interquartile range or exact 10-run range.
  4. **Line 208 & Line 293 (Conservation gap percentages):** Clarify that $12,800\text{ km}^2$ ($1.84\%$) in national reserves plus $15,000\text{ km}^2$ ($2.15\%$) in provincial reserves equals $3.99\%$ ($27,800\text{ km}^2$) before accounting for reserve boundary overlap ($3.91\%$), and state the exact unprotected percentage ($96.09\%$) rather than *"over 90%."*
