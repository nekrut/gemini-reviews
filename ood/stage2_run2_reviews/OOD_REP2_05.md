### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates whether resting-state fMRI functional network architecture varies across frequency bands from $0.007$ to $0.438\text{ Hz}$. Using multiband fMRI ($TR = 645\text{ ms}$, $900$ volumes) from $N = 140$ participants in the NKI-Rockland Sample with peripheral cardiac/respiratory RETROICOR denoising, the authors parcellate the cerebral cortex into $2,143$ sub-regions, filter the BOLD time series into four bands (F1: $0.007$–$0.052\text{ Hz}$, F2: $0.052$–$0.106\text{ Hz}$, F3: $0.106$–$0.206\text{ Hz}$, F4: $0.206$–$0.438\text{ Hz}$), and apply von Mises-Fisher clustering across $k \in \{3,\dots,20\}$ clusters. By maximizing an overlap score ($RC_{tk}$, Eq. 1) against 24 Neurosynth task-activation maps across 10 edge-density thresholds, they select $k = 16, 13, 4,$ and $3$ clusters for F1–F4, concluding that finely subdivided low-frequency networks integrate into fewer macro-networks at higher frequencies, and re-analyze a prior opposite-sex compatibility fMRI dataset using the F3 architecture. The NKI-Rockland dataset is publicly available, but neither the analysis code nor the secondary compatibility dataset is provided.

Applying RETROICOR physiological noise modeling alongside a full comparison with and without global signal regression (GSR) on fast-TR multiband fMRI is a solid preprocessing foundation. However, the manuscript's core conclusions are undermined by four methodological inconsistencies: (1) the cluster-selection metric $RC_{tk}$ (Eq. 1) is mathematically biased toward small $k$ under lower signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and broad Neurosynth masks, and selecting $k$ by the mode across just 10 correlated density thresholds is unstable; (2) the claim that F3 truly integrates networks into $k = 4$ clusters is directly contradicted by Figure 3, which shows that forcing $k = 17$ in F3 successfully recovers almost all 17 subdivided networks from F1/F2; (3) the wavelet decomposition section describes decomposing signals with MODWT only to discard the wavelet coefficients and bandpass-filter the raw BOLD signal; and (4) the re-analysis of the compatibility dataset using the F3 architecture reports significant inter-network contributions between the "cerebellum and limbic areas" (line 116), even though the F3 cortical clustering ($k = 4$: $\text{VN}_{F3}, \text{SMN}_{F3}, \text{CEN}_{F3}, \text{DMN}_{F3}$) contains neither a cerebellum nor a limbic network. These issues can be resolved by using established clustering stability criteria, reconciling the $k = 4$ versus $k = 17$ interpretation, and clarifying the ROI definitions in the secondary re-analysis.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Resting-state networks subdivided in lower frequency bands ($k = 16$ in F1, $k = 13$ in F2) are functionally integrated into fewer networks in higher frequency bands ($k = 4$ in F3, $k = 3$ in F4): [Fragile].**  
  Two methodological artifacts drive the sharp drop from $k = 13$–$16$ (F1/F2) to $k = 4$ and $3$ (F3/F4). First, Equation (1) evaluates each of the 24 broad Neurosynth task masks $Ma_i$ against the *single* best-matching cluster $Mc_{tj}$ via $\max_{j} (|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|^2 / (|Ma_i| \, |Mc_{tj}|))$. Because Neurosynth meta-analytic maps for broad terms (e.g., "attention," "working memory," "cognitive control") span large distributed cortical territories, and because high-frequency BOLD ($>0.1\text{ Hz}$) suffers from canonical hemodynamic attenuation and reduced SNR, splitting F3/F4 into 15–20 small clusters fragments the noisy clusters (reducing $|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}| / |Ma_i|$), whereas partitioning the cortex into 3–4 coarse lobes preserves large overlaps. Second, in Figure 1b, the mean overlap score $RC_{tk}$ across thresholds varies very little across $k$ in F1 and F2, and picking the single $k$ that ranks first most often across only 10 highly correlated binarization thresholds ($x = 1\%,\dots,10\%$) amplifies small ties (in Fig. 1a, the "winning" $k$ in F1 and F2 wins in only 2–3 of the 10 thresholds).

* **The smaller number of networks in F3 ($k = 4$) reflects genuine functional architecture rather than lower signal-to-noise ratio, as proven by the $k = 17$ comparison: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section "Comparison of the architecture of high-resolution RSNs across frequency bands" (line 105, Fig. 3), the authors set $k = 17$ across all four frequency bands and report that "most of the networks detected in F1 and F2 were also detected in F3, which indicates that at least the smaller number of RSNs in F3 was not due to the lower signal-to-noise ratio." This observation refutes rather than supports the claim that F3 has merged into only 4 networks: if the 17 subdivided networks of Yeo et al. are still distinctly recoverable in F3 when $k = 17$ is specified (Fig. 3), then those subnetworks have *not* merged into 4 undifferentiated networks in F3; rather, the $RC_{tk}$ heuristic simply failed to select the finer parcellation in F3. Conversely, in F4, where $k = 17$ yields fragmented executive/attention clusters due to admitted SNR degradation (line 105), concluding that $k = 3$ reflects true functional integration conflates noise-induced loss of resolution with biological integration.

* **The default mode network (DMN) and primary perceptual/motor networks remain detectable up to $0.438\text{ Hz}$ (F4): [Well Supported].**  
  Both the $k$-varying analysis (Fig. 2) and the fixed $k = 17$ analysis (Fig. 3), with and without GSR (Figs. S2–S3), consistently recover the core DMN, visual, and somatomotor networks across F1–F4 after RETROICOR physiological regression. This replicates and extends prior multiband fMRI work showing that high-amplitude intrinsic connectivity in primary sensory-motor and DMN hubs persists above $0.1\text{ Hz}$.

* **Re-analyzing opposite-sex compatibility data with the F3 network architecture reveals a significant positive inter-network contribution between the cerebellum and limbic areas ($p = 0.018$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  According to Methods (line 41) and Results (line 92–100, Fig. 2), the study parcellated only the cerebral cortex ($2,143$ sub-regions within Yeo's cortical mask), and in F1 the limbic network (OFC and ATL) was identified, but in F3 ($k = 4$) the entire cortex was partitioned into only four networks: $\text{VN}_{F3}, \text{SMN}_{F3}, \text{CEN}_{F3},$ and $\text{DMN}_{F3}$, with OFC and ATL absorbed into $\text{DMN}_{F3}$ (line 100). Neither a "cerebellum" network nor a "limbic" network exists in the F3 architecture shown in Figure 2. It is therefore unclear how re-analyzing the compatibility dataset "using the newly defined RSN architecture of F3" (line 73) could test or detect an inter-network contribution between the cerebellum and limbic areas (line 116).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Evaluate Clustering Stability Directly Alongside Task-Map Overlap
* **WHY:** Relying on the mode of $RC_{tk}$ across 10 correlated graph-density thresholds biases higher-noise frequency bands toward coarse $k = 3$–$4$ partitions because single small clusters cannot cover broad Neurosynth meta-analytic maps.
* **WHAT:** Quantify intrinsic clustering stability across bootstrap/split-half resamples of the $N = 140$ NKI-Rockland participants for $k \in \{3,\dots,20\}$ (following the instability criterion of Yeo et al., 2011) and modify Eq. (1) to evaluate how well the *union* of subnetworks reconstructs each task mask or use normalized mutual information (NMI) against multi-network task parcellations.
* **HOW:** Split the $N = 140$ subjects into two random halves ($n = 70$ each) 50 times, run the von Mises-Fisher clustering on each half for $k = 3,\dots,20$ across F1–F4, plot the Hamming/variation-of-information clustering instability curve as a function of $k$ for each band, and compare the intrinsic stability minima against the $RC_{tk}$ maxima.

#### 2. Clarify the Wavelet Decomposition vs. Bandpass Filtering Pipeline and Clustering Algorithm
* **WHY:** In Methods (line 46), the text states that BOLD signals were decomposed into 7 levels using MODWT, FFT was used to find "minimum and maximum frequency across regions," and then "the BOLD signals without MODWT decomposition were bandpass filtered for each frequency band." In addition, line 53 states that von Mises-Fisher clustering was used, whereas line 66 states that the final network architecture was defined by "$k$-means cluster analysis of the average adjacency matrices."
* **WHAT:** Clarify whether connectivity matrices were computed from the MODWT wavelet detail coefficients $d_j(t)$ or from Butterworth/FIR bandpass-filtered raw time series, explain how minimum/maximum frequencies were defined from continuous FFT spectra, and resolve the contradiction between von Mises-Fisher mixture modeling and $k$-means clustering.
* **HOW:** Rewrite the "Wavelet decomposition" and "Clustering" subsections to state the exact mathematical filter applied to generate F1–F4 time series (note that for $TR = 0.645\text{ s}$, dyadic MODWT scales have fixed passbands $f_s / 2^{j+1}$ to $f_s / 2^j$ that do not vary across regions or subjects) and specify how the thresholded adjacency matrices were combined into the final cluster labels.

#### 3. Reconcile the $k = 4$ Integration Claim with the $k = 17$ Recovery in F3
* **WHY:** Claiming that F3 has only 4 integrated networks while simultaneously showing in Figure 3 that $k = 17$ in F3 cleanly recovers the subdivided networks of F1 and F2 is internally inconsistent.
* **WHAT:** Reframe the interpretation of F3 and F4 in the Abstract, Results, and Discussion as a *hierarchical, multi-scale* organization where coarse 3–4 network divisions (DMN, Visual, Somatomotor, Central Executive) are most robust to high-frequency attenuation, while finer 17-network subdivisions remain present in F3 ($0.106$–$0.206\text{ Hz}$) before degrading in F4 ($>0.2\text{ Hz}$).
* **HOW:** Compute the quantitative Dice/overlap coefficient between F1 ($k = 17$) and F3 ($k = 17$) and between F1 ($k = 4$) and F3 ($k = 4$), and report both values in the Results to show that F3 preserves both coarse and fine network scales.

#### 4. Fully Specify the Compatibility Dataset Re-Analysis and Explain Cerebellar/Limbic ROIs in F3
* **WHY:** Readers cannot evaluate the re-analysis in lines 71–73 and 115–116 because the F3 parcellation in Figure 2 has only 4 cortical networks ($\text{VN}_{F3}, \text{SMN}_{F3}, \text{CEN}_{F3}, \text{DMN}_{F3}$) and no cerebellar or limbic network.
* **WHAT:** State the exact ROI atlas, total number of ROIs, sample size, and network assignment table used in the opposite-sex compatibility re-analysis, and explain how "limbic areas" and "cerebellum" were defined when applying the F3 architecture.
* **HOW:** Add a supplementary table or paragraph in Methods listing how the ROIs from the original compatibility study [30, 31] were mapped onto the F3 clusters and why limbic and cerebellar regions were treated as separate modules despite OFC/ATL being absorbed into $\text{DMN}_{F3}$ in Figure 2.
