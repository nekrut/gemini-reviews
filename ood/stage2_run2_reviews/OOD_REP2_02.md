### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study adapts the Linear Predictive Coding EEG Algorithm for Parkinson's Disease (LEAPD) to generate a continuous electrophysiological index of cognitive function from brief (~2.6 min) eyes-open resting-state scalp EEG. Using a primary cohort of 100 patients with Parkinson's disease (PD) and 49 community controls, the authors encode bandpass-filtered EEG time series into autoregressive Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) coefficient vectors, project them onto principal-component affine subspaces for cognitively impaired ($\text{MoCA} < 26$) and cognitively normal ($\text{MoCA} \ge 26$) groups, and combine the geometric mean of relative subspace distances across 8 selected electrodes. The resulting index correlates with Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) scores in cross-validation ($\rho = 0.68$–$0.70, p < 0.001$) and in an independent out-of-sample cohort of 32 PD patients ($\rho = 0.68, p < 0.001, \text{AUC} = 0.92$), while also correlating with five NIH Toolbox cognitive tests in the primary cohort ($\rho = 0.57$–$0.70$). Raw EEG datasets (OpenNeuro `ds004584`) and analysis code (GitHub) are publicly available.

The manuscript presents a computationally fast, well-validated approach for compressing broadband spectral shifts (specifically posterior alpha/theta peak frequency and beta amplitude shifts) into a scalar biomarker, and the prospective external validation on 32 independent PD patients provides solid support for its association with MoCA. However, several methodological and technical issues temper the secondary claims: (1) hyperparameter optimization (bandpass frequency cutoffs, LPC order, subspace dimensionality, and top-8 electrode selection) was performed across the entire $n = 149$ dataset prior to cross-validation, which causes optimistic leakage in the internal CV metrics and in the NIH Toolbox analyses (which were not externally validated); (2) in healthy controls, the index fails to correlate with two of the five NIH Toolbox domains (processing speed $\rho = 0.15$, episodic memory $\rho = 0.02$); (3) at the pre-specified $0.5$ classification cutoff, external validation sensitivity drops to $50\%$ ($9/18$ impaired patients); and (4) the matrix algebra in Equations (2)–(3) contains dimension mismatches. These limitations can be addressed through nested cross-validation, threshold calibration, and mathematical corrections.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The 8-electrode combined LEAPD index strongly correlates with MoCA scores in PD patients ($\rho = 0.68, p < 0.001$) and generalizes to an out-of-sample PD cohort: [Well Supported].**  
  While the internal cross-validation estimate on the 149 discovery participants is subject to hyperparameter selection optimism, locking the 8 electrodes, bandpass filters, LPC orders, and subspace parameters and evaluating them on 32 independent PD participants from a separate trial (NCT03808675) yielded $\rho = 0.68$ ($p < 0.001$, $R^2 = 0.46$) and an ROC $\text{AUC}$ of $0.92$. Partial correlation analyses in the PD cohort also confirm that the association holds after adjusting for age, sex, UPDRS-III motor severity, disease duration, levodopa equivalent daily dose (LEDD), and geriatric depression score (GDS).

* **LEAPD accurately indexes cross-domain cognition on the NIH Toolbox across both PD and control participants ($\rho \ge 0.56$): [Overstated].**  
  First, the NIH Toolbox models were never evaluated on an external test set; their hyperparameters (filter passbands, LPC order, PCA dimensions, and electrodes) were optimized via exhaustive search on the same $n = 139$–$140$ discovery subjects using a single 10-fold split and then evaluated on the same subjects with fixed parameters, introducing outer-loop selection bias. Second, Table 2 shows that in the 49 control participants, LEAPD shows near-zero, non-significant correlations with Pattern Comparison Processing Speed (PCPST: $\rho = 0.15$) and Picture Sequence Memory (PSMT: $\rho = 0.02$). The high pooled correlations ($\rho = 0.57$–$0.59$) are partly driven by between-group differences between PD and control participants (Table 1 shows large PD vs. control differences on all five NIH Toolbox tests, $p < 0.01$ to $p < 0.001$).

* **LEAPD classifies MoCA-defined cognitive impairment with ~80% accuracy and balanced sensitivity/specificity: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Within the discovery cohort, LOOCV accuracy is $79.87\%$ ($\text{Sensitivity} = 79.69\%, \text{Specificity} = 80.00\%$). However, in the independent out-of-sample test ($n = 32$ PD: 18 cognitively impaired with MoCA 22–25, 14 cognitively normal with MoCA 26–30), using the theoretical threshold $\rho_{\text{combined}} < 0.5$ yields a sensitivity of only $50.0\%$ ($9/18$) and specificity of $100.0\%$ ($14/14$), for an overall accuracy of $71.88\%$ ($23/32$) against a majority-class base rate of $56.25\%$ ($18/32$). Because taking the geometric mean of 8 ratios $\rho_i \in (0,1)$ skews $\rho_{\text{combined}}$ downward or upward depending on subspace dimensionality and mild vs. severe impairment distributions, the uncalibrated $0.5$ cutoff is not invariant across cohorts with milder MoCA impairment ($23.44 \pm 1.15$ in out-of-sample vs. $22.0 \pm 3.49$ in discovery).

* **LPC spectral profiling captures cognition-related shifts in posterior theta-alpha peak frequency (7–13 Hz) and beta amplitude (13–25 Hz) without manual artifact removal: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The autoregressive spectral reconstructions at central-parietal channels (e.g., P8, P4, CP1, CP2) align well with established neurophysiological slowing of the posterior dominant rhythm in PD cognitive decline. However, because resting-state EEG was recorded eyes-open with no ocular (blink/saccade) or electromyographic (tremor/frontalis EMG) artifact rejection, low-frequency power at frontal/occipital channels (F4, O2, PO7; Fig. 2d) remains susceptible to differential blink rates (a hallmark of hypodopaminergic states in PD).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Implement Nested Cross-Validation for Hyperparameter and Electrode Selection
* **WHY:** Selecting the optimal bandpass filter cutoffs (from all combinations in 2–34 Hz), LPC order ($K \in \{2,\dots,10\}$), affine subspace dimension ($n < K$), and the top 8 electrodes on the full $n = 149$ dataset before running LOOCV, 5-fold CV, and 10-fold CV leaks test-fold information into model configuration. Furthermore, if the randomization tests kept the pre-selected hyperparameters fixed while only re-fitting the PCA subspaces on shuffled labels, they do not measure the null distribution of the full exhaustive search.
* **WHAT:** Perform a fully nested cross-validation (e.g., 10-fold outer loop with an inner 10-fold grid search) where filter bounds, $K$, $n$, and the top 8 electrodes are selected strictly within each outer training split, especially for the NIH Toolbox outcomes that lack an external test cohort.
* **HOW:** Within each outer training fold ($90\%$ of subjects), run the inner 10-fold grid search to select $(f_{\text{low}}, f_{\text{high}}, K, n)$ per channel and rank the top 8 channels; compute the affine subspaces $P_1, P_2$ on the outer training fold; and evaluate $\rho_{\text{combined}}$ on the held-out $10\%$ outer test fold. Repeat the exact same full grid-search pipeline on 100 label-permuted datasets to establish an unbiased permutation null.

#### 2. Decouple PD Diagnosis from Cognitive Subspace Construction
* **WHY:** In the $n = 149$ discovery set, the cognitively impaired group ($n = 64$) comprises $82.8\%$ PD patients ($53$ PD, $11$ controls), whereas the cognitively normal group ($n = 85$) comprises $55.3\%$ PD patients ($47$ PD, $38$ controls). Training the "cognitively impaired" vs. "cognitively normal" affine subspaces on the pooled cohort conflates PD disease status with cognitive status, which likely contributes to why LEAPD fails to predict NIH PCPST ($\rho = 0.15$) and PSMT ($\rho = 0.02$) within controls.
* **WHAT:** Train and evaluate the PCA affine subspaces strictly within the PD cohort ($n = 100$) when predicting NIH Toolbox and MoCA scores in PD, and report within-group partial correlations adjusting for PD status when pooling cohorts.
* **HOW:** Re-run the LEAPD subspace construction using only the $n = 100$ PD discovery participants (53 impaired, 47 normal) and report the resulting cross-validated and out-of-sample ($n = 32$ PD) correlations and classification metrics alongside the pooled-training results.

#### 3. Calibrate the Classification Decision Threshold on Training Data
* **WHY:** Although Eq. (6) defines single-channel $\rho_i = D_2 / (D_1 + D_2)$ such that $\rho_i = 0.5$ represents equal distance to both subspaces, Eq. (7) defines the multi-channel index as the geometric mean $\rho_{\text{combined}} = (\prod_{i=1}^L \rho_i)^{1/L}$. By AM-GM inequality and because subspace residual distances depend on the dispersion of each training group, the Bayes-optimal decision boundary for $\rho_{\text{combined}}$ is not guaranteed to sit at $0.5$, causing sensitivity to fall to $50\%$ in the external test set (Fig. 3b).
* **WHAT:** Learn the optimal classification threshold (e.g., maximizing Youden's $J = \text{Sensitivity} + \text{Specificity} - 1$) on the discovery dataset ($n = 149$) and apply that learned threshold to the $n = 32$ out-of-sample test set.
* **HOW:** Determine the cutoff $\tau^*$ that maximizes Youden's $J$ across the discovery out-of-fold predictions (which appears near $\tau^* \approx 0.53$–$0.55$ in Fig. 3b) and report the sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy on the 32 external PD subjects at both $\tau = 0.50$ and $\tau = \tau^*$.

#### 4. Correct Matrix Algebra and Statistical Reporting in Methods and Table 2
* **WHY:** Equations (2)–(5) contain matrix dimension incompatibilities and imprecise terminology, and Table 2 reports regression RMSE on the LEAPD scale rather than the clinical MoCA scale.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Make the following specific technical fixes:
  1. **Equations (2) and (3) (Methods, lines 30–44):** $V_c$ is defined as an $S_c \times K$ matrix (note also that the two classes have unequal sample sizes $S_1 = 64$ and $S_2 = 85$, not a common $S$), while $D_c$ is $K \times K$ and $Q$ is $K \times S_c$, making $D_c Q^T$ a $K \times S_c$ matrix that cannot be subtracted from $V_c \in \mathbb{R}^{S_c \times K}$. Rewrite Eq. (2) clearly as $W_c = S_c^{-1/2} (V_c - \mathbf{1}_{S_c} m_c)$, where $\mathbf{1}_{S_c} \in \mathbb{R}^{S_c \times 1}$ is a column vector of ones and $m_c \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times K}$ is the row vector of column means, and remove the word "scaled" unless columns are divided by their standard deviations.
  2. **Equation (5) (Methods, line 56):** Since the right singular vectors $p_{c,i}$ from SVD in Eq. (4) are orthonormal ($p_{c,i}^T p_{c,i} = 1$), simplify the projection term to $\sum_{i=1}^n (p_{c,i}^T (a - m_c^T)) p_{c,i}$ and refer to $D_c$ as the orthogonal Euclidean distance to subspace $c$ (reserving "normalized" for the ratio $\rho = D_2 / (D_1 + D_2)$ in Eq. 6).
  3. **Table 2 Regression Metrics (lines 101–108):** Clarify whether MoCA was normalized to $[0,1]$ or whether $\rho_{\text{combined}}$ was treated as the dependent variable (since $\text{RMSE} = 0.09$–$0.12$), and report the RMSE in raw MoCA points (out of 30) so clinicians can interpret the prediction error directly.
