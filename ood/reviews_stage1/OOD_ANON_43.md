### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates resting-state electroencephalography (EEG) oscillatory power in 31 children with developmental coordination disorder (DCD) and 52 age-matched typically developing (TD) controls (mean age 9.5 years, range 5.1–14.8) across 90-second eyes-open and eyes-closed recordings at 17 scalp electrodes. Using Welch's method (2 s Hann window) and spectral parameterization (`FOOOF`) to subtract aperiodic $1/f$ offset and exponent components, the authors compare group differences across delta (1–3 Hz), theta (4–7 Hz), alpha (8–12 Hz), beta (13–30 Hz), gamma 1 (31–40 Hz), and gamma 2 (50–80 Hz) bands via electrode-wise Mann–Whitney $U$ tests with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction. They report lower alpha power and higher delta power in the DCD group, and find that within the DCD group (but not the TD group), motor proficiency (average of BOT-2 SF and DCD-Q $T$-scores) correlates positively with alpha power ($\rho = 0.565, p < 0.001$) and negatively with delta power ($\rho = -0.416, p = 0.020$). Raw data are available only upon request and no analysis code is provided.

Although separating periodic oscillations from aperiodic $1/f$ background activity via `FOOOF` is a positive methodological step, the study's statistical pipeline and mechanistic conclusions suffer from several critical flaws: (1) direct self-contradictions in the Results section regarding whether delta and alpha power were higher or lower in DCD; (2) circular post-hoc electrode selection ("double dipping") when averaging only over electrodes showing significant group differences to compute within-group motor correlations; (3) severe artificial truncation of variance (range restriction) on the BOT-2 SF motor score in the DCD group ($\text{SD} = 3.5$ vs. $6.6$ in TD) induced by the $\le 16\text{th}$ percentile inclusion cutoff, coupled with unmodeled confounding from a highly significant group deficit in nonverbal IQ (WASI-2 Matrix Reasoning $p < 0.001$) and a promised ADHD covariate adjustment (Section 2.1, line 75) that was omitted from the Spearman correlation analyses; and (4) an artifactual `FOOOF` subtraction procedure that induces a strong negative correlation ($\rho = -0.752$) between 1–3 Hz delta residuals and 8–12 Hz alpha peaks, which the authors overinterpret as evidence of sleep-related synaptic downscaling failure. Re-analysis using a priori electrode clusters, linear mixed models controlling for WASI-2 Matrix Reasoning, ADHD-RS-IV, and age, and validation of `FOOOF` fit quality at 1–3 Hz are needed to salvage the empirical findings.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Children with DCD exhibit significantly lower resting-state alpha power and higher delta power than TD controls across eyes-open and eyes-closed conditions: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the abstract, discussion, and swarm plots (Figs. 2–3) indicate higher delta and lower alpha power in DCD, the main Results text (Section 3, line 136) directly contradicts itself twice: first stating *"In the eyes-open condition, DCD was associated with significantly lower power in the delta and alpha bands"* before saying two sentences later *"In this frequency band, delta power was higher in the DCD group"*, and then stating for the eyes-closed condition *"In the delta band, resting-state power was lower in the DCD group and higher in the alpha band."* Furthermore, extracting "absolute power" in $\mu\text{V}^2$ by subtracting a linear aperiodic fit from a power spectrum (Section 2.4.3, line 112; Fig. 1B) is sensitive to `FOOOF` boundary fitting at 1–3 Hz (where there is usually no true oscillatory peak and only 5 frequency bins at 0.5 Hz resolution: 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 Hz) and does not control for the large group difference in WASI-2 Matrix Reasoning ($46.7 \pm 11.4$ vs. $56.0 \pm 10.3$, $p < 0.001$).

* **Individual differences in motor functioning correlate significantly with resting-state alpha ($\rho = 0.565$) and delta ($\rho = -0.416$) power in the DCD group, and these correlations are significantly stronger than in the TD group ($z = 2.204$ and $z = 1.968$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the authors selected the subset of electrodes that passed FDR significance for the DCD vs. TD group contrast and averaged power across those post-hoc selected electrodes (Section 3, line 138) before correlating with the motor scores used to define the groups—a classic circular selection bias (Kriegeskorte et al., 2009). Second, in Section 2.1 (line 75), the authors explicitly state that data from the ADHD-RS-IV *"were used as a covariate in the correlation analyses"*, yet Section 3 (lines 138–140) computes unadjusted zero-order Spearman $\rho$ correlations without covarying for ADHD symptoms, age (which spans 5.1 to 14.8 years, across massive developmental shifts in alpha and delta power), or WASI-2 Matrix Reasoning. Third, the Fisher $r$-to-$z$ comparison between DCD ($n=31$) and TD ($n=52$) is miscalculated: for delta power ($\rho_{\text{DCD}} = -0.416$, $z' = -0.4428$; $\rho_{\text{TD}} = -0.023$, $z' = -0.0230$), the standard error of the difference is $\sqrt{\frac{1}{31-3} + \frac{1}{52-3}} = 0.2369$, which yields $z = -1.772$ ($p = 0.076$, two-tailed), not the reported $z = 1.968$ ($p = 0.049$)—which becomes even wider when accounting for the larger standard error of Spearman's rank correlation ($\text{SE}_z = \sqrt{\frac{1.06}{n-3}}$, giving $z = -1.72, p = 0.085$).

* **The strong negative correlation between resting-state delta and alpha power ($\rho = -0.752, p < 0.001$) supports a mechanism of incomplete sleep synaptic downscaling driving wakeful cortical hyperexcitability in DCD: [Overstated].**  
  The study collected no sleep EEG, actigraphy, or sleep questionnaire data. More importantly, in `FOOOF` spectral parameterization (1–45 Hz with `min_peak_height = 0.0` and `max_n_peaks = inf`), the aperiodic line is anchored at low frequencies by the 1–3 Hz delta band and at medium frequencies around the dominant 8–12 Hz alpha peak. Any subject with a prominent alpha peak or slight spectral knee pulls the linear $1/f$ fit upward in the 4–15 Hz region and tilts the slope/offset, creating a mechanical negative coupling between the 1–3 Hz residual ("periodic delta") and the 8–12 Hz residual ("periodic alpha") across subjects. Interpreting this mathematical coupling in a 90-second daytime recording as proof of impaired slow-wave sleep synaptic homeostasis is speculative and unsupported by the data.

* **Group-specific criteria for DCD-Q functional impairment (Criterion B) were validly established using the 95% confidence interval of the control group mean: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 2.1 (line 73), the authors define the cutoff for clinical functional impairment (DSM-5 Criterion B) as scoring below the lower bound of the *95% confidence interval of the mean* of the control group (e.g., $\text{CI}_{95\%}: 59.75 \pm 3.48$, i.e., a cutoff of $56.27$ when the control mean is $59.8$ and $\text{SD} = 12.6$). The 95% CI of the *sample mean* ($\bar{x} \pm 1.96 \cdot \text{SE}$) estimates the precision of the population mean, NOT the normal reference range of individual scores ($\bar{x} - 1.645 \cdot \text{SD}$ or 5th/16th percentile). Nearly half of typically developing children score below the lower bound of the 95% CI of the control mean; fortunately, Table 1 shows the DCD group had a mean DCD-Q of $35.7 \pm 9.4$, so most met genuine clinical cutoffs despite the flawed statistical definition of the threshold.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Circular Post-Hoc Electrode Selection with A Priori Anatomical ROIs and Partial Correlations
* **WHY:** Averaging EEG power exclusively over electrodes that were statistically significant in the between-group comparison introduces selection bias, and zero-order Spearman correlations omit the promised ADHD covariate as well as age (5.1–14.8 years) and nonverbal IQ (WASI-2 Matrix Reasoning, which differs between groups at $p < 0.001$).
* **WHAT:** Recompute the motor-proficiency correlations using a priori anatomical electrode clusters (frontal: F3, Fz, F4, F7, F8; central: C3, Cz, C4; parietal/occipital: P3, Pz, P4, P7, P8, O1, O2) and partial Spearman correlations controlling for age, ADHD-RS-IV total score, and WASI-2 Matrix Reasoning.
* **HOW:** Run partial rank correlations (`partialcorr` in MATLAB) within DCD ($n=31$) and TD ($n=52$) across the whole scalp and canonical regions, and correct the Fisher $r$-to-$z$ test statistic using the exact sample sizes ($n_1 = 31, n_2 = 52$) and degrees of freedom adjusted for covariates ($n - 3 - k$).

#### 2. Audit `FOOOF` Goodness-of-Fit ($R^2$, Error) and Validate Delta Residuals Against Unparameterized Relative Power
* **WHY:** Subtracting a linear `FOOOF` aperiodic fit in linear power space ($\mu\text{V}^2$) over 1–45 Hz with `max_n_peaks = no limit` and `min_peak_height = 0.0` can overfit noise peaks and distort the 1–3 Hz edge of the spectrum, artificially inducing the negative delta–alpha correlation ($\rho = -0.752$, Fig. 5) and producing negative residual power values (Fig. 1B, Figs. 2–3).
* **WHAT:** Report group means and SDs for `FOOOF` model fit quality ($R^2$ and MAE), verify whether genuine oscillatory peaks were detected in the 1–3 Hz delta band vs. broadband spectral knee residuals, and check whether the delta and alpha group differences and correlations hold when `FOOOF` is parameterized in standard log-power space with `max_n_peaks = 6` and when using standard relative band power.
* **HOW:** Extract the `r_squared` and `error` fields from the existing Python `FOOOF` objects for each participant/electrode, compare them between DCD and TD in Supporting Information S1, and re-run the group Mann–Whitney $U$ tests on the fitted Gaussian peak parameters (`peak_params`) rather than raw subtracted bin averages.

#### 3. Correct Contradictory Text in Section 3 and Statistical Definition of the DCD-Q Cutoff in Section 2.1
* **WHY:** Section 3 (line 136) states both that delta power was *lower* and *higher* in DCD in the eyes-open condition, and states that in the eyes-closed condition *"In the delta band, resting-state power was lower in the DCD group and higher in the alpha band"*—the exact opposite of the Abstract, Discussion, and Figures 2–3. In addition, Section 2.1 (line 73) confuses the 95% confidence interval of the mean with a normative percentile cutoff, and line 77 states that the DCD group obtained *"significantly lower scores on the Vocabulary and Matrix Reasoning subtests"* immediately after stating in the preceding sentence that the group difference on Vocabulary was *"non-significant"* ($p = 0.055$ in Table 1).
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. In Section 3 (line 136), correct the two reversed sentences so they accurately state that delta power was higher and alpha power was lower in the DCD group in both conditions.
  2. In Section 2.1 (line 77), remove *"Vocabulary and"* from the sentence claiming significant differences on both WASI-II subtests, consistent with Table 1 ($p = 0.055$).
  3. In Section 2.1 (line 73), report how many children in the DCD group met the standard published clinical DCD-Q cutoffs ($\le 46$ for ages 5–7, $\le 55$ for ages 8–9, $\le 57$ for ages 10–15; Wilson et al., 2009) rather than the lower bound of the 95% CI of the control mean.

#### 4. Temper Mechanistic Claims Regarding Sleep Synaptic Downscaling
* **WHY:** No sleep measurements were collected, and daytime eyes-closed delta residuals after `FOOOF` subtraction do not directly index slow-wave sleep synaptic homeostasis.
* **WHAT:** Reframe the sleep synaptic downscaling narrative in the Abstract, Discussion (line 157), and Conclusion (line 176) as a speculative hypothesis for future polysomnography studies, while explicitly acknowledging thalamocortical maturation delay, attentional/drowsiness fluctuations during eyes-closed rest, and spectral parameterization coupling as parsimonious explanations for elevated daytime delta and reduced alpha power.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract and Conclusion to state that reduced alpha and elevated delta power reflect altered resting-state cortical oscillatory dynamics in DCD, removing the unverified claim of incomplete sleep synaptic downscaling from the Abstract.
