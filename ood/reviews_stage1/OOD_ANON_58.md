### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This neuroimaging methods and cognitive neuroscience study investigates whether the cortical resting-state network (RSN) architecture of blood oxygen level-dependent (BOLD) fMRI signals varies across frequency bands. Using fast multiband ($\text{TR} = 645\text{ ms}$, 900 volumes, 10 min) resting-state fMRI and physiological (cardiac/respiratory) recordings from $N = 140$ participants in the publicly available NKI-Rockland Sample, the authors parcellate the cerebral cortex into 2,143 subregions, apply maximal overlap discrete wavelet transform (MODWT) to define four frequency bands—F1 ($0.007\text{--}0.052\text{ Hz}$), F2 ($0.052\text{--}0.106\text{ Hz}$), F3 ($0.106\text{--}0.206\text{ Hz}$), and F4 ($0.206\text{--}0.438\text{ Hz}$)—and cluster the corticocortical connectivity profiles in each band using a von Mises-Fisher mixture model across $k = 3\text{--}20$ clusters and 10 binarization thresholds ($1\%\text{--}10\%$). Selecting the optimal $k$ per frequency band by maximizing spatial overlap ($RC_{tk}$, Equation 1) with 24 Neurosynth task-activation maps, they find that the optimal cluster number drops from $k = 16$ (F1) and $k = 13$ (F2) to $k = 4$ (F3) and $k = 3$ (F4), while the default mode network (DMN) and visual network remain distinct across all bands. Finally, they re-analyze an earlier opposite-sex compatibility fMRI dataset using the F3 network architecture. The primary NKI-Rockland dataset is publicly accessible, although the authors' custom clustering and permutation scripts and the secondary compatibility dataset are not deposited.

The manuscript addresses an important methodological question in multiband rs-fMRI, uses physiological noise modeling (RETROICOR via PhysIO) alongside a global signal regression (GSR) vs. non-GSR sensitivity comparison, and applies a principled spherical clustering algorithm (Yeo et al. von Mises-Fisher model). However, three methodological limitations undermine the central claim that the reduction from 16–17 networks in F1/F2 to 3–4 coarse networks in F3/F4 reflects true biological "integration" rather than lower signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and sluggish hemodynamic filtering: (1) the cluster-selection criterion (Equation 1) evaluates each candidate parcellation $k$ by taking $\max_j$ over clusters $j = 1, \dots, k$ of the Dice-like product $\frac{|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|}{|Ma_i|} \times \frac{|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|}{|Mc_{tj}|}$ against 24 large-scale Neurosynth task maps; when higher-frequency connectivity matrices become noisier, subdividing into $k = 16$ fragments clusters into small, noisy patches that reduce $|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}| / |Ma_i|$, systematically penalizing larger $k$ in low-SNR bands; (2) the canonical hemodynamic response function (HRF) acts as a biological low-pass filter with a strong roll-off above $0.15\text{ Hz}$, meaning that thermal noise and unmodeled aliased cardiorespiratory harmonics dominate F4 ($0.206\text{--}0.438\text{ Hz}$), where only high-amplitude local/geometric autocorrelations (visual cortex and core DMN hubs) survive; and (3) the secondary re-analysis of the opposite-sex compatibility dataset adds cerebellum and limbic ROIs that were not part of the 2,143 cortical parcellation used to derive the F3 architecture. These issues can be addressed through explicit SNR-matched simulation controls and clearer separation of cortical versus subcortical/cerebellar parcellations.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The optimal number of cortical clusters maximizing overlap with Neurosynth task activation maps decreases from 16 (F1: 0.007–0.052 Hz) and 13 (F2: 0.052–0.106 Hz) to 4 (F3: 0.106–0.206 Hz) and 3 (F4: 0.206–0.438 Hz), both with and without GSR: [Well Supported].**  
  Within the authors' mathematical framework (MODWT/bandpass decomposition, top 1–10% correlation binarization, von Mises-Fisher clustering, and Equation 1 task-overlap scoring), the empirical decrease in the $RC_{tk}$-maximizing cluster number from $16\text{--}17$ at low frequencies to $3\text{--}4$ above $0.1\text{ Hz}$ is consistently demonstrated across binarization thresholds and replicated with and without GSR (Figure 1 and Figure S2).

* **The reduction to 4 networks in F3 and 3 networks in F4 reflects true functional network integration in higher frequency bands rather than lower SNR: [Overstated].**  
  To argue against an SNR artifact, the authors force $k = 17$ across all four frequency bands (Figure 3) and observe that because many F1/F2 subnetworks can still be visually matched in F3 at $k = 17$, the selection of $k = 4$ in F3 by Equation 1 "was not due to the lower signal-to-noise ratio but rather reflected the functional architecture" (Line 105). In fact, Figure 3 proves the exact opposite: when $k = 17$ is imposed on F3, the fine-grained networks *are* still present, meaning the underlying connectivity structure in F3 is *not* biologically merged into 4 giant networks; rather, the Equation 1 overlap metric ($RC_{tk}$) favors $k = 4$ in F3 because slightly higher noise blurs cluster boundaries enough that 4 broad clusters achieve a higher product of sensitivity ($\frac{|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|}{|Ma_i|}$) and precision ($\frac{|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|}{|Mc_{tj}|}$) against broad Neurosynth activation masks than 17 slightly noisier clusters. Furthermore, the authors do not run a synthetic SNR-degradation control (adding white/pink noise to F1 time series to match the empirical temporal SNR of F3 and F4) to test whether Equation 1 automatically selects $k = 3\text{--}4$ when F1 is degraded to F3/F4 SNR levels.

* **The DMN and visual/perceptual networks survive even in the highest frequency band (F4: 0.206–0.438 Hz) due to strong functional architecture and geometric distance from primary networks: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The persistence of visual and core DMN clusters in F3 and F4 (Figures 2 and 3) is empirically clear and consistent with prior multiband rs-fMRI studies (e.g., Gohel & Biswal). However, the interpretation should account for the fact that visual cortex and precuneus/PCC have the highest baseline microvascular density, cerebral blood flow, and local spatial autocorrelation (smoothed with a 6-mm FWHM kernel), which allows short-range and high-SNR homologous connectivity to survive above $0.2\text{ Hz}$ even when long-range frontoparietal (CEN/DAN) correlations are attenuated by hemodynamic dispersion and thermal noise.

* **Re-analyzing the opposite-sex compatibility dataset using the F3 network architecture reveals right-hemisphere-dominant negative contributions of the cerebellum ($p = 0.050$) and positive cerebellum–limbic inter-network contributions ($p = 0.018$): [Fragile].**  
  In the cortical parcellation pipeline (Methods, Line 41), the authors divided "Yeo's gray matter template into sub-regions... and obtained 2143 cortical sub-regions." Yeo's 2011 template is strictly cerebral cortex and does not include the cerebellum. In the re-analysis of the compatibility dataset (Line 71–73, 116), however, all reported significant findings involve the *cerebellum* and *limbic* regions. If the F3 clustering only merged cortical networks into 4 networks ($\text{VN}_{\text{F3}}, \text{SMN}_{\text{F3}}, \text{CEN}_{\text{F3}}, \text{DMN}_{\text{F3}}$, Figure 2) while leaving the cerebellum as a pre-defined anatomical ROI, then the cerebellum's internal definition did not change between the original study and the F3 re-analysis; its newly significant laterality ($p = 0.050$, borderline after FDR) and inter-network permutation $p$-values simply reflect reducing the number of multiple comparison tests (fewer cortical networks) and changing the limbic boundary rather than discovering a new cerebellar architecture in F3.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Perform a Synthetic SNR-Degradation Null Simulation to Test Whether $k = 3\text{--}4$ in F3/F4 Is Driven by Noise
* **WHY:** Because Equation 1 ($RC_{tk}$) multiplies task-mask coverage ($\frac{|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|}{|Ma_i|}$) by cluster purity ($\frac{|Ma_i \cap Mc_{tj}|}{|Mc_{tj}|}$), adding noise to a 16-network ground truth fragments small clusters and lowers task-mask coverage, which can cause $RC_{tk}$ to peak at $k = 3\text{--}4$ purely as a mathematical consequence of lower SNR.
* **WHAT:** Conduct a simulation where Gaussian or phase-randomized $1/f$ noise is added to the F1 ($0.007\text{--}0.052\text{ Hz}$) time series to match the empirical spectral power / tSNR of F3 and F4, and re-run the exact clustering and Equation 1 selection pipeline.
* **HOW:** Measure the temporal SNR (or mean inter-subject correlation matrix reliability) in F1, F2, F3, and F4. Add independent Gaussian noise to each participant's F1 time series so that the degraded F1 correlation matrices match the mean and variance (or split-half reliability) of F3 and F4. Run the von Mises-Fisher clustering for $k = 3\text{--}20$ and compute $RC_{tk}$. If the noise-degraded F1 data still select $k \approx 16$, the claim of genuine biological integration in F3/F4 is strongly validated; if noise-degraded F1 drops to $k = 3\text{--}4$, reframe the F3/F4 convergence as a resolution limit imposed by high-frequency SNR attenuation.

#### 2. Evaluate Clustering Stability (Split-Half Reproducibility) Alongside Neurosynth Task Overlap
* **WHY:** Neurosynth task activation maps are derived from standard task-fMRI studies that are themselves low-pass filtered by the canonical HRF ($< 0.1\text{ Hz}$). Using low-frequency task activation maps as the ground-truth benchmark to select $k$ in high-frequency resting-state bands ($0.1\text{--}0.44\text{ Hz}$) biases the benchmark toward coarse spatial overlap.
* **WHAT:** Compute intrinsic split-half clustering stability (Following Yeo et al. 2011) across the $N = 140$ participants for $k = 3\text{--}20$ in each frequency band, independent of Neurosynth masks.
* **HOW:** Randomly split the 140 participants into two groups of $n = 70$ (100 random splits), compute the group-averaged binarized connectivity matrices for each half in F1–F4, run the von Mises-Fisher clustering for $k = 3\text{--}20$, and calculate the Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) or Hungarian-matched Dice overlap between the two halves at each $k$. Plot stability vs. $k$ alongside Figure 1.

#### 3. Clarify the Wavelet Decomposition vs. Bandpass Filtering Pipeline in the Methods
* **WHY:** In the Section "Wavelet decomposition and multiband frequency definition" (Line 46), the authors state that BOLD signals were decomposed by 7-level MODWT to find average minimum and maximum frequencies for F1–F4, and then end the paragraph with: `"Finally, the BOLD signals without MODWT decomposition were bandpass filtered for each frequency band."` This makes it unclear whether the correlation matrices were computed on MODWT wavelet coefficients or on conventional Fourier/Butterworth bandpass-filtered time series.
* **WHAT:** Clarify whether MODWT was used only to determine frequency cutoffs ($0.007, 0.052, 0.106, 0.206, 0.438\text{ Hz}$) followed by bandpass filtering, or whether MODWT wavelet coefficients were used directly.
* **HOW:** Revise the final sentence of Section "Wavelet decomposition and multiband frequency definition" to state the exact filter type, order, and implementation used to generate the F1–F4 time series analyzed in the "Clustering" section.

#### 4. Clarify How the Cerebellum and Limbic Regions Were Defined in the F3 Compatibility Re-Analysis
* **WHY:** The main clustering analysis parcellated 2,143 cerebral cortical subregions (Yeo cortical mask), resulting in 4 cortical networks in F3 ($\text{VN}_{\text{F3}}, \text{SMN}_{\text{F3}}, \text{CEN}_{\text{F3}}, \text{DMN}_{\text{F3}}$, Figure 2), which absorbed the cortical limbic regions (OFC and ATL) into $\text{DMN}_{\text{F3}}$. Yet the re-analysis section (Line 116) reports significant results for "the cerebellum" and "the inter-network contribution of the cerebellum and limbic areas."
* **WHAT:** Explain how the cerebellum and limbic networks were defined in the F3 re-analysis when OFC and ATL were absorbed into $\text{DMN}_{\text{F3}}$ in Figure 2.
* **HOW:** Add a table or paragraph in the Methods listing the exact ROIs/networks used in the compatibility re-analysis, specifying whether subcortical limbic structures (amygdala, hippocampus) and cerebellum were retained as separate anatomical masks alongside the 4 F3 cortical clusters, and report both raw and FDR-adjusted $p$-values for all tested pairs.
