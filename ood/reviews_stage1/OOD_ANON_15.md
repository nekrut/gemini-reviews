### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This cross-sectional resting-state fMRI study investigates intra- and inter-network functional connectivity (FC) differences across three groups: major depressive disorder patients with a history of childhood maltreatment (MDD + CM, $n = 18$), depressed patients without maltreatment (MDD, $n = 19$), and healthy controls without maltreatment (HC, $n = 20$). Using 13-minute 3T rs-fMRI scans, the authors perform (1) FSL MELODIC group ICA followed by dual regression across 27 independent components (10,000 permutations with TFCE) to assess intra-network connectivity, and (2) CONN toolbox multivoxel pattern analysis (MVPA) to identify 8 seed regions showing between-group differences, which are then used as seeds for post-hoc ROI-to-voxel and ROI-to-ROI (MIST64 atlas) inter-network comparisons. They report altered intra-network FC in visual, default mode (DMN), sensorimotor, auditory, and cerebellar components, and extensive inter-network differences involving sensorimotor, putamen, frontal pole, and cerebellar seeds between MDD + CM and MDD patients, concluding that childhood maltreatment disrupts sensory-motor and cerebellar architecture and predisposes individuals to functional neurological symptom (conversion) disorder. Raw data are not publicly deposited ("available on request").

While the clinical phenotyping is thorough—matching groups on age, sex, IQ, education, and current BDI/BAI severity, and cross-checking retrospective Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ) scores with blinded structured interviews—the neuroimaging statistical pipeline and clinical claims suffer from severe methodological flaws. Most critically, the inter-network connectivity analysis uses whole-brain MVPA across the three groups to define 8 seed ROIs that maximize between-group variance, and then uses those exact same data-driven regions as seeds for pairwise between-group ROI-to-voxel and ROI-to-ROI tests in the same 57 participants without independent validation or omnibus multi-seed correction. Furthermore, the intra-network dual regression tests 27 ICA components without correcting for the 27 parallel component comparisons, no continuous correlation is found between CTQ severity and FC in the depressed patients, the 2$\times$2 factorial design lacks a healthy-maltreated (HC + CM) control group, and the abstract/discussion claims regarding perceptual deficits and conversion disorder are speculative extrapolations unsupported by any sensory or neurological assessments in this cohort.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Widespread inter-network FC alterations between MDD + CM and MDD patients across sensorimotor, basal ganglia, executive control, salience, DMN, and cerebellar networks (Table 4, Fig. 2): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 2.3.4 (line 104) and Section 3.3.1 (line 232), the authors state that CONN's multivoxel pattern analysis (MVPA) was first run on the 57 participants to identify 8 brain areas showing significant between-group differences in whole-brain connectivity patterns, and *"As a next step, between-group differences were evaluated by using these ROIs as seeds, to evaluate differences in FC strengths of a specific seed and other brain voxels, and FC strengths between the seed and other ROIs."* In CONN, seed-to-voxel and ROI-to-ROI tests initiated from omnibus MVPA clusters on the same dataset are post-hoc descriptive characterizations of the omnibus MVPA effect; treating their uncorrected seed-level $p$-values (across 8 seeds $\times$ 6 pairwise directional contrasts $= 48$ whole-brain searches) as independent confirmatory discoveries inflates false positives via circular seed selection ("double-dipping") and unadjusted multiplicity across seeds.

* **Intra-network FC differences between MDD + CM and MDD in DMN, visual, auditory, sensorimotor, and cerebellar networks (Table 2, Fig. 1): [Fragile].**  
  In Section 2.3.2 (line 90), FSL dual regression was run across 27 independent components using TFCE, where an omnibus $F$-test was followed by pairwise $t$-tests. However, standard FSL `dual_regression` applies TFCE family-wise error correction *within* each individual spatial component map, not across the 27 components and 6 pairwise contrasts ($27 \times 6 = 162$ maps). As shown in Table 2, nearly all reported local maxima in the MDD + CM $>$ MDD contrast have marginal within-component TFCE $p$-values between $0.016$ and $0.049$ (e.g., right cerebellum vermis VI $p = 0.049$, left cerebellum crus I $p = 0.035$, left planum polare $p = 0.035$, left precentral gyrus $p = 0.038$), none of which survive Bonferroni or FDR correction across the 27 tested ICA components ($\alpha = 0.05 / 27 = 0.00185$). In addition, the anatomical labels in Table 2 assign the left hippocampus ($-24, -18, -20$) and left amygdala ($-22, -12, -12$) to the "default mode network" component without showing which of the 27 ICA components each cluster belongs to.

* **Specificity of observed FC alterations to severe childhood maltreatment (CTQ $> 60$) as a dose-dependent developmental trauma effect: [Overstated].**  
  First, in Section 2.1 (lines 58 vs. 62), the inclusion criteria state that patients were enrolled in the MDD + CM group if they scored above the "low" cut-off on *any* CTQ subscale (e.g., $\text{EA} > 12, \text{PA} > 9, \text{SA} > 7, \text{EN} > 14, \text{PN} > 9$), which defines *moderate*—not strictly severe—maltreatment, and the boundary values ($\text{EA} = 12, \text{PA} = 9$, etc.) are omitted from both groups (`>12` vs. `<12`). Second, as acknowledged in the Discussion (line 301), there was *no* significant correlation between CTQ severity and FC strengths within the MDD + CM or combined MDD groups; the only reported CTQ $\times$ group interaction (Section 3.2.1, line 222) was driven by a tiny 12-voxel cluster in the right angular gyrus within the HC group ($n=20$), whose CTQ scores had almost zero variance (median $29$, IQR $27\text{--}33$, Table 1). Third, without an unmedicated sample or a healthy maltreated (HC + CM) group, the design cannot separate main effects of childhood maltreatment from maltreatment $\times$ depression interactions or heterogeneous psychotropic polypharmacy (97% medicated across SSRIs, SNRIs, NaSSAs, agomelatine, trazodone, mood stabilizers, and atypical antipsychotics).

* **Functional connectivity alterations in sensorimotor, visual, auditory, and cerebellar networks explain altered perception and predisposition to conversion disorder (functional neurological symptom disorder) in maltreated individuals: [Overstated].**  
  Both the Abstract ("Conclusions", line 31) and Discussion (line 6) claim that these resting-state FC differences *"may explain that maltreated individuals typically display altered perception and are prone to develop functional neurological symptom disorder (conversion disorder) in adulthood."* No perceptual tasks, somatosensory testing, or assessments of conversion/somatoform symptoms were conducted in this study, and somatic/neurological disorders were explicitly excluded (Section 2.1). Extrapolating resting-state BOLD correlations in $n = 18$ patients to conversion disorder pathophysiology overreaches the empirical data.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct for Multiplicity Across the 27 ICA Components in Dual Regression
* **WHY:** Running FSL `dual_regression` separately on 27 independent components without adjusting for the 27 parallel tests inflates the family-wise error rate to $1 - (1 - 0.05)^{27} \approx 75\%$.
* **WHAT:** Apply multiplicity correction across all 27 components and 3 group pairs (e.g., using FSL `palm` with `-corrmod` and `-corrcon`, or applying Benjamini–Hochberg FDR / Bonferroni adjustment across the 27 component TFCE $p$-value maps) and explicitly list the ICA component number (IC#) for every cluster in Tables 2 and 3.
* **HOW:** Run PALM (`palm -i dr_stage2_ic[#].nii.gz ... -d design.mat -t design.con -f design.fts -T -corrTemp -corrmod -corrcon -n 10000`) on the 27 stage-2 dual regression maps, and update Tables 2 and 3 to distinguish clusters that survive multi-component correction from exploratory uncorrected trends.

#### 2. Separate Omnibus MVPA Post-Hoc Characterization from Independent Seed-Based Inference
* **WHY:** Selecting 8 seeds via whole-brain MVPA because they show between-group differences in the 57 subjects and then reporting uncorrected-across-seeds ROI-to-voxel and ROI-to-ROI $p$-values from those same 8 seeds in the same 57 subjects is circular and fails to control false discovery across the 8 seeds $\times$ 6 contrasts.
* **WHAT:** (1) Explicitly frame the MVPA-seeded ROI-to-voxel analysis (Table 4) as a post-hoc directional characterization of the omnibus MVPA clusters rather than an independent seed-based discovery, applying a Bonferroni threshold of $p_{\text{FDR}} < 0.05 / 8 = 0.00625$ across the 8 seeds; and (2) perform a non-circular inter-network ROI-to-ROI analysis (e.g., functional network connectivity [FNC] across all 64 MIST atlas parcels or the 27 ICA timecourses) with network-based statistic (NBS) or whole-matrix FDR correction.
* **HOW:** In CONN's ROI-to-ROI tab, select all MIST64 atlas ROIs (rather than only the 8 MVPA-derived seeds), run the 3-group ANCOVA controlling for age, sex, and mean framewise displacement (FD) with whole-matrix parametric multivariate/NBS FDR correction ($p_{\text{FDR}} < 0.05$), and report the resulting unbiased connectogram in Figure 2.

#### 3. Include Mean Head Motion (Framewise Displacement) as a Group-Level Covariate and Report Motion Metrics
* **WHY:** Although ICA-FIX and scrubbing were applied during preprocessing, residual micro-head motion systematically alters short- vs. long-range resting-state FC (particularly in sensorimotor and cerebellar networks), and group-level GLMs in Sections 2.3.2 and 2.3.4 controlled only for age and gender.
* **WHAT:** Report the mean framewise displacement (FD) and number of scrubbed volumes for each of the three groups (MDD + CM, MDD, HC) in Table 1, and include mean FD as a nuisance covariate in the group-level dual regression and CONN models.
* **HOW:** Extract subject-level mean FD from CONN's `QC_MeanMotion` covariate, test for group differences via Kruskal–Wallis test in Table 1, and add `QC_MeanMotion` to the second-level design matrices.

#### 4. Temper Speculative Claims on Conversion Disorder and Clarify CTQ Cut-Off Boundaries
* **WHY:** Neither perceptual deficits nor conversion disorder symptoms were measured in this cohort, and the CTQ cut-off inequalities in Section 2.1 (`>12` vs. `<12`, etc.) leave exact boundary scores undefined.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Revise the Abstract Conclusions (line 31) and Discussion (Section 4.1, line 6; Section 4.5, line 48) to remove the claim that these findings explain conversion disorder or altered sensory perception, framing them strictly as resting-state connectivity differences between depressed patients with and without self-reported childhood maltreatment.
  2. In Section 2.1 (lines 58 and 62), correct the inequality signs (e.g., $\ge 13$ vs. $\le 12$ for EA) so that boundary integer scores are unambiguously assigned.
  3. Deposit the unthresholded group statistical maps on NeuroVault and share the de-identified clinical/ROI summary table on OSF or Zenodo.
