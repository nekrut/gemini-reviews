### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study uses the Maximum Entropy (`MaxEnt`) species distribution model to project current (1970–2000) and future (2050s and 2090s under RCP2.6 and RCP8.5, CCSM4 GCM) global, regional (China), and local (Liaoning Province) habitat suitability for the invasive annual vine *Sicyos angulatus* L. (burcucumber). The authors compile 4,886 global occurrence records—50.8% from GBIF, 28.1% from China's NSII, and 21.1% (~1,031 points) from an intensive 2023–2024 field survey across 14 cities in Liaoning Province—and spatially thin them at a 5 km radius to 2,083 presence records. After Pearson correlation filtering ($|r| > 0.8$) of the 19 WorldClim bioclimatic layers, 11 predictors are retained and modeled with tuned regularization ($\text{RM} = 0.5$, feature classes `LQHPT`, 10 replicates). The model yields a test AUC of 0.921, identifies precipitation in the driest month (`Bio14`, 37.4% contribution), annual precipitation (`Bio12`, 26.8%), mean annual temperature (`Bio1`, 18.1%), and temperature seasonality (`Bio4`, 14.9%) as primary drivers, and projects a 1.0- to 2.2-fold global expansion of highly suitable habitat under RCP2.6 and RCP8.5.

While the field survey dataset in Liaoning Province is a useful empirical resource and the authors apply spatial thinning (5 km buffer) and `ENMeval`-style parameter tuning ($\Delta\text{AICc}$), the study suffers from severe geographic ascertainment bias, an aggressive low-regularization complex feature parameterization ($\text{RM} = 0.5$ with `LQHPT`, including Product and Threshold features) that overfits regional sampling density, and a direct contradiction between the authors' own field data and their baseline model projections. Most notably, 21.1% of the global dataset (~1,031 occurrence records) was collected by the authors in Liaoning Province during 2023–2024 (Section 4.1.1, line 241; Fig. S2), yet the Results and Discussion repeatedly claim that Liaoning Province currently has *no* highly suitable habitat (0.1%) and *"no occurrence was found in the contemporary era (1970–2000)"* (Section 2.4, line 167; Section 2.3, line 154)—treating a mismatch between 1970–2000 baseline climate layers and 2023–2024 field presences (or local overfitting) as a future climate-change expansion. Furthermore, multiple internal contradictions between Table 1, Figure 3, and the text undermine technical precision. Re-running the model with a spatial sampling-effort bias file, removing `Threshold` features, using multi-GCM ensembles, and reconciling the Liaoning presence contradiction are required to make the projections reliable.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Precipitation in the driest month (`Bio14`), annual precipitation (`Bio12`), mean annual temperature (`Bio1`), and temperature seasonality (`Bio4`) are the primary climatic determinants of *S. angulatus* distribution: [Moderately Supported].**  
  These four variables account for 97.2% of percent contribution in Table 1 and align with the known physiological sensitivity of *S. angulatus* to drought and frost. However, there is a direct discrepancy between Table 1 and the text regarding permutation importance: Section 2.2 (line 88) states that by permutation importance, `Bio1` (57.5%) is followed by `Bio12` (16.0%), `Bio10` (mean temperature in the warmest quarter), `Bio14` (4.7%), and `Bio16`, whereas Table 1 (line 67) shows that `Bio3` (isothermality) has the second-highest permutation importance at **$18.7 \pm 3.2\%$** (higher than `Bio12` at $16.0\%$, while `Bio10` is only $0.4\%$ and `Bio16` is $0.2\%$).

* **Liaoning Province currently contains no highly suitable habitat ($0.1\%$) and had no contemporary occurrence, but will shift to $27.0\text{--}38.8\%$ highly suitable habitat in the 2050s–2090s under RCP2.6 and RCP8.5: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 4.1.1 (line 241), the authors state that 21.1% of their 4,886 global occurrence points (~1,031 sites across 14 cities in Liaoning Province) were recorded during their 2023–2024 field surveys in Liaoning Province and fed directly into the `MaxEnt` training set to fit the 1970–2000 WorldClim baseline. Yet in Section 2.3 (line 154) and Section 2.4 (line 167), the model classifies Liaoning as having virtually 0% highly suitable habitat in the contemporary period, and claims *"whereas no occurrence was found in the contemporary era (1970–2000)"*. If hundreds of training presence points in Liaoning were paired with 1970–2000 WorldClim variables during training, and the resulting model assigns Liaoning a cloglog suitability of $0.3\text{--}0.5$ ("moderately suitable", $52.2\%$) rather than $>0.5$ ("highly suitable", $0.1\%$), that reflects the arbitrary 0.5 cloglog threshold cutting right through the Liaoning mode—which then jumps from 0.1% to 27.3% under even mild warming (RCP2.6 2050s) as pixels cross from $0.48$ to $0.51$. Moreover, under RCP8.5 in Liaoning (Table 2, lines 140–141), highly suitable habitat actually *decreases* from $38.8 \pm 13.3\%$ in the 2050s to $27.0 \pm 10.2\%$ in the 2090s, which goes unmentioned in the text.

* **Global highly suitable habitat will expand by factors of 1.0 and 2.2 (or 2.2-fold) under RCP2.6 and RCP8.5: [Fragile].**  
  First, the projections rely on a single outdated CMIP5 general circulation model (`CCSM4` under RCP2.6 and RCP8.5, Section 4.2.3, line 272) rather than a multi-GCM CMIP6 ensemble, making continental precipitation projections (particularly `Bio14` and `Bio12`, which drive 64.2% of the model) highly vulnerable to single-GCM structural bias. Second, the model uses 10,000 random global background points without correcting for the massive spatial sampling bias in the occurrence dataset (where ~49% of all global records come from China/NSII and the authors' intensive Liaoning survey, while South America, Africa, and Russia are heavily undersampled) and uses a low regularization multiplier ($\text{RM} = 0.5$) with all five feature classes (`LQHPT`), which is known to produce jagged step-function response curves (visible in Fig. 4b, d) and overfit to sampled regions. Third, the text confuses "increase by $X$-fold" with "increase by $X\times 100\%$": in Table 2 (lines 127–131), global highly suitable area increases from $1.2\%$ (contemporary) to $2.4\%$ (2050s RCP2.6, a $+100\%$ increase or $2.0\times$ the original area) and to $3.9\%$ (2090s RCP8.5, a $+225\%$ increase or $3.25\times$ the original area).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Incorporate a Target-Group or Kernel Density Bias File for Background Point Sampling
* **WHY:** Over 21% of the global records come from a targeted 2023–2024 survey of a single Chinese province (Liaoning), and another 28% come from China's NSII, while uniform random background sampling across global land areas causes `MaxEnt` to confound regional survey intensity with climatic suitability.
* **WHAT:** Re-run `MaxEnt` using a spatial sampling bias grid (e.g., a Gaussian kernel density raster of sampling effort or Köppen–Geiger ecoregions buffered around known occurrences) to weight the 10,000 background points, and restrict feature classes to `LQH` (removing `Threshold` features, which have been deprecated in default `MaxEnt` since v3.4 due to step-function overfitting) with $\text{RM} \ge 1.0$.
* **HOW:** Generate a 2D kernel density raster from the 2,083 thinned occurrence points in ArcGIS/R, pass it to `MaxEnt` via the `biasfile` argument, and compare the resulting response curves and suitability maps against Fig. 4 and Fig. 5.

#### 2. Reconcile the Liaoning Field Survey Presences with the Contemporary Baseline Suitability Threshold
* **WHY:** Claiming that Liaoning Province has no contemporary occurrence or high suitability when 21.1% of the model's training presences were collected in Liaoning is internally contradictory and arises from using an arbitrary fixed threshold ($>0.5$) rather than an ecologically calibrated threshold (such as Maximum Training Sensitivity plus Specificity, MTSS, or 10th percentile training presence, which Section 4.2.1 line 262 claims was used).
* **WHAT:** Use the 10th percentile training presence cloglog value or MTSS threshold calculated by `MaxEnt` to define suitable habitat, and explicitly report the distribution of predicted contemporary cloglog values at the Liaoning field survey points.
* **HOW:** Extract the `10 percentile training presence Cloglog threshold` from `maxentResults.csv`, reclassify the contemporary and future rasters using that empirically derived threshold in ArcGIS, and revise Sections 2.3 and 2.4 to clarify that *S. angulatus* is already established in Liaoning.

#### 3. Reconcile Numerical Contradictions Between Text, Tables, and Figures
* **WHY:** Several numerical values and descriptions in the text conflict with the tables and figures.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Table 1 vs. Section 2.2 (lines 67 and 88):** Update Section 2.2 to acknowledge that `Bio3` (isothermality) has the second-highest permutation importance ($18.7 \pm 3.2\%$) in Table 1, rather than claiming `Bio10` ($0.4\%$) and `Bio16` ($0.2\%$) rank in the top five.
  2. **Table 1 zero-contribution variables (lines 69–84 vs. line 252):** Section 4.1.2 (line 252) explains that 8 collinear variables (`Bio5`, `Bio6`, `Bio7`, `Bio11`, `Bio13`, `Bio17`, `Bio18`, `Bio19`) were *excluded prior to running the final model*, yet Table 1 lists all 19 variables and reports their contributions as `0 | 0` as if they were included in the jackknife test and had zero weight. Clearly mark those 8 rows in Table 1 as `Excluded (collinear, |r| > 0.8)` rather than `0%`.
  3. **Table 3 units (lines 203–226):** The Table 3 title states *"Area changes with habitat suitability greater than 0.1"*, yet the footnote (line 226) states *"Values preceding the brackets denote the number of S. angulatus occurrence records."* Since the numbers in the World column sum to $5680 + 11101 + 906 = 17,687$ (under 2050s RCP2.6) and $15577 + 8993 + 3014 = 27,584$ (under 2090s RCP8.5)—far exceeding the 4,886 total occurrence records—clarify whether the values in Table 3 are in units of $10^3\text{ km}^2$, $10^4\text{ km}^2$, or raster grid cells.
  4. **Spatial resolution typo (line 250):** Section 4.1.2 states *"a spatial resolution of 30 arc-minutes (~1 km 2 )"*. A resolution of $\sim 1\text{ km}^2$ at the equator is **30 arc-seconds** ($0.5\text{ arc-minutes}$), whereas 30 arc-minutes is $0.5^\circ \approx 55\text{ km}$. Fix *"30 arc-minutes"* to *"30 arc-seconds"*.
  5. **Validation split contradiction (line 260 vs. line 262):** Line 260 states that a 75% training / 25% random test split was used, whereas line 262 states that a 10-fold cross-validation method (which uses 90% training / 10% test per fold) was used. Specify which validation scheme produced Figure 2 and Table 1.
