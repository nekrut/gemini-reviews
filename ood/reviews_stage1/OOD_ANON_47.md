### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study models current and future (2030s, 2050s, 2070s, 2090s under CMIP6 SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5) overwintering habitat suitability for the black-necked crane (*Grus nigricollis*) across Nyingchi City, southeastern Tibet ($114,400\text{ km}^2$), using Maximum Entropy (`MaxEnt`) and Random Forest (`RF`) models. Combining field transect surveys conducted along main roads between December 2023 and January 2024 with GBIF and literature records, the authors retain 52 spatially thinned (1 km grid) presence points and 520 background/pseudo-absence points across 14 environmental predictors (screened from 28 initial topographic, foraging, anthropogenic disturbance, and WorldClim variables via $|R| \ge 0.8$ and Jackknife filtering). They report AUC values of 0.942 (`MaxEnt`) and 0.945 (`RF`), identify distance to buildings (`d_b`, 15.1% cross-model average), distance to roads (`d_r`, 15.05%), and isothermality (`Bio3`, 5.85%) as key determinants, map current suitable habitat along the Yarlung Tsangpo and Nyang river confluence, and project an expansion of suitable habitat under the high-emission SSP5-8.5 scenario. No occurrence coordinates, raster outputs, or analysis scripts are publicly deposited despite the statement that data are contained within the article.

While monitoring wintering black-necked cranes in southeastern Tibet is valuable for regional conservation, the modeling pipeline has fundamental methodological and conceptual flaws that invalidate its main environmental attribution and future projection claims: (1) field surveys were explicitly conducted along main roads (*"The sample lines were set up along the main roads"*, Section 2.2.1, line 53), and the authors themselves admit in Section 3.2.2 (line 173) that *"Due to the region's rugged terrain, data sampling was limited to accessible areas. Consequently, most black-necked crane presence points were clustered near roads. This led to a significant correlation between distance from roads and crane habitat suitability"*—meaning the model's top predictors (`d_b`, `d_r`, `lut`, which make up 75.4% of the `MaxEnt` model in Table 2) are direct artifacts of road-side observer sampling bias rather than crane ecology; (2) Section 2.2.1 (line 57) gives contradictory descriptions of how pseudo-points were generated, stating simultaneously that *"Pseudo-presence points were generated using a random function within the study area, producing a total of 520 points"*, followed immediately by *"In this paper, the pseudo-existence points are selected, which are generated by spatial perturbation of known existence points"*, and then *"Pseudo-negative samples are randomly distributed"*; (3) the authors average incompatible variable-importance metrics across `MaxEnt` and `RF` when the two models actually disagree completely on the primary drivers (`lut`, `d_b`, `d_r` = 75.4% in `MaxEnt` vs. 18.8% in `RF`); and (4) future climate projections to the 2090s hold static 2020 land-use, building, and road distance layers constant while claiming in the Discussion that projected habitat expansion reflects *"the strong emphasis China places on ecological protection efforts"* (line 213) and *"heat resistance evolution"* (line 196). Re-modeling with road-bias correction and removing static human-access confounders is required.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Distance to buildings (`d_b`, 15.1%), distance to roads (`d_r`, 15.05%), and isothermality (`Bio3`, 5.85%) are the primary environmental determinants of black-necked crane habitat suitability: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the field survey transects were laid out along main roads in accessible low-elevation river valleys (Section 2.2.1, line 53; Section 3.2.2, line 173). Putting unadjusted `distance to roads` (`d_r`) and `distance to buildings` (`d_b`) into the model alongside random background points across $114,400\text{ km}^2$ of roadless high-mountain terrain guarantees that the SDM learns the spatial footprint of the road network where observers drove, rather than the crane's ecological niche. Second, `MaxEnt` and `RF` do not agree on variable importance (Table 2): in `MaxEnt`, anthropogenic/access variables (`lut` 30.7%, `d_b` 23.0%, `d_r` 21.7%) account for **75.4%** of the model, whereas in `RF`, those three variables account for only **18.8%**, and `RF` is instead driven by `d_wb` (10.6%), `Bio5` (10.4%), `Bio2` (8.8%), and `Bio7` (8.5%). Taking the simple arithmetic mean of `MaxEnt` percent contribution and `RF` Gini/permutation importance (e.g., $\frac{23.0 + 7.2}{2} = 15.1\%$ for `d_b`, $\frac{21.7 + 8.4}{2} = 15.05\%$ for `d_r`, and arbitrarily highlighting `Bio3` at $\frac{5.1 + 6.6}{2} = 5.85\%$ while ignoring `lut` at $\frac{30.7 + 3.2}{2} = 16.95\%$, `Bio5` at $7.35\%$, and `d_wb` at $7.2\%$) is statistically invalid and misrepresents Table 2.

* **`MaxEnt` (AUC = 0.942) and `RF` (AUC = 0.945) achieve high predictive accuracy and their comparison significantly reduces spatial uncertainty: [Overstated].**  
  With only 52 presence points concentrated in a narrow river valley corridor within a massive $114,400\text{ km}^2$ mountain prefecture, any classifier given distance to roads/buildings (`d_r`, `d_b`) and 520 random background points across uninhabited alpine peaks will trivially achieve $\text{AUC} > 0.94$ simply by separating road-accessible valleys from mountain ridges. Furthermore, no quantitative metric of inter-model spatial agreement (e.g., Schoener's $D$, correlation between predicted probability rasters, or ensemble variance map) is computed to support the claim that comparing the two models reduced spatial uncertainty, and the text contains contradictory statements on parameter tuning (Section 2.2 line 44 states *"all other settings remained at their default configurations"*, whereas Section 4 line 213 claims `MaxEnt` *"was optimized by adjusting two parameters, RM (regularization multiplier) and FC (feature classes)"* without ever reporting the chosen RM or FC values).

* **Suitable habitat for wintering black-necked cranes in Nyingchi will expand peak-wide by the 2090s under the high-emission SSP5-8.5 scenario due to plateau warming, heat-resistance evolution, and ecological protection policies: [Overstated].**  
  First, the manuscript provides no quantitative table of suitable area ($\text{km}^2$ or percentage of Nyingchi City) for any current or future scenario—relying entirely on visual inspection of small maps in Figures 5, 6, and 7. Second, in `MaxEnt` (Table 2), climate variables (`Bio2, Bio3, Bio5, Bio7, Bio14, Bio15`) account for only **17.2%** of the model, while static land-use, road, building, water, and topographic layers account for **82.8%**. Projecting a model dominated (82.8%) by static 2020 human infrastructure layers to the 2090s while varying only the 17.2% climatic variables cannot accurately capture future habitat shifts. Third, attributing SDM correlative projections under SSP5-8.5 to *"heat resistance evolution"* (line 196) or *"the strong emphasis China places on ecological protection efforts"* (line 213)—variables that are completely absent from the model—is unfounded.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct for Road-Side Survey Bias and Separate Anthropogenic Access from Ecological Suitability
* **WHY:** Because field transects followed main roads (line 53) and presence records clustered near roads due to terrain inaccessibility (line 173), including `distance to roads` (`d_r`) and `distance to buildings` (`d_b`) as habitat suitability predictors turns the SDM into a model of observer accessibility.
* **WHAT:** Remove `d_r` and `d_b` from the ecological niche predictors (or use `d_r` strictly as a spatial sampling-bias layer to weight background/pseudo-absence point generation within accessible distances of roads and valleys), and refit `MaxEnt` and `RF` using the biophysical, hydrological (`d_wb`), topographic (`alt`, `slp`, `asp`), and climatic predictors.
* **HOW:** Generate the 520 background/pseudo-absence points using a target-group or road-buffer sampling weight in R/ArcGIS so that background points share the same road-accessibility bias as the 52 presence points, refit both models, and report the updated variable importances in Table 2.

#### 2. Clarify Pseudo-Absence / Pseudo-Presence Generation and Model Tuning Parameters
* **WHY:** Section 2.2.1 (line 57) contains three mutually contradictory sentences stating that 520 *"pseudo-presence points"* were generated randomly across the study area, that *"pseudo-existence points"* were generated by spatial perturbation of known presences, and that *"pseudo-negative samples"* are randomly distributed. In addition, Section 2.2 (line 44) says default parameters were used while Section 4 (line 213) claims `MaxEnt` RM and FC parameters were tuned.
* **WHAT:** Clearly state whether the 520 points were random pseudo-absences (used as negative class `0` in `RF` and background points in `MaxEnt`) or spatially perturbed synthetic presences, and state the exact `MaxEnt` RM, FC, and `RF` (`ntree`, `mtry`) settings used.
* **HOW:** Rewrite Section 2.2.1 (line 57) and Section 2.2 (line 44) to unambiguously document the 52 presence (`1`) and 520 pseudo-absence (`0`) records, and provide the 52 coordinate pairs (or masked 1 km grid centroids if sensitive) in a supplementary table to fulfill the Data Availability Statement (line 246).

#### 3. Quantify Current and Future Suitable Area ($\text{km}^2$) in a Summary Table and Correct Table 2 Rankings
* **WHY:** Sections 3.3.1 and 3.3.2 make quantitative claims about when low-suitable, high-suitable, and total suitable areas peak across 2030s–2090s under SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5 (Figures 5–7), but no area numbers ($\text{km}^2$ or $\%$) are reported anywhere in the manuscript. Moreover, the Abstract and Section 3.2 claim `d_b` (15.1%), `d_r` (15.05%), and `Bio3` (5.85%) have the highest average contributions, ignoring that in Table 2 `lut` averages $16.95\%$, `Bio5` averages $7.35\%$, and `d_wb` averages $7.20\%$—all higher than `Bio3` ($5.85\%$).
* **WHAT:** Add a table reporting the exact area ($\text{km}^2$ and $\%$ of Nyingchi City) classified as unsuitable, low-suitable, and high-suitable for both `MaxEnt` and `RF` under current and all 8 future scenario-period combinations (using the stated TSS = 0.1657 and TPT = 0.0228 thresholds), and correct the variable ranking discussion in the Abstract, Section 3.2, and Section 4 to match the numbers in Table 2.
* **HOW:** Count the pixels in each suitability class in ArcGIS (`500 m × 500 m = 0.25 km²` per pixel) for the rasters plotted in Figs. 5–7, tabulate the areas, and report `MaxEnt` and `RF` variable rankings separately rather than averaging incompatible importance metrics.

#### 4. Justify or Replace GEBCO Bathymetric Elevation Data and Fix Editorial Errors
* **WHY:** Section 2.2.2 (line 107) states that `GEBCO` (General Bathymetric Chart of the Oceans, a 15 arc-second ~450 m global ocean/land grid optimized for seafloor bathymetry using satellite gravity) was chosen over SRTM (30 m radar interferometry) for terrestrial alpine terrain in Tibet because GEBCO *"integrates multibeam bathymetric data and depth predictions based on satellite gravity data"*, which is irrelevant for a landlocked plateau at 3,100 m elevation. Furthermore, `alt` (altitude) was dropped during variable screening despite elevation being a primary determinant of alpine wetland distribution.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Remove the erroneous claim in Section 2.2.2 (line 107) that multibeam ocean bathymetry and marine gravity data improve terrestrial terrain accuracy in Tibet, and explain why `alt` was excluded in Table 2 (e.g., collinearity with temperature `Bio1`/`Bio5`).
  2. Fix typos in the Abstract (line 8: *"Based on the had data"* $\rightarrow$ *"Based on the field distribution data"*), Section 2.2.2 (line 65: missing period/subject before *"demonstrated that crane foraging sites"*), and Section 4 (line 209: *"Panthera unica"* $\rightarrow$ *"Panthera uncia"*).
