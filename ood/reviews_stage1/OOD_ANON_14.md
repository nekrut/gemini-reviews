### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study adapts the Linear Predictive Coding EEG Algorithm for Parkinson's Disease (LEAPD) to index continuous cognitive performance and classify cognitive impairment ($\text{MoCA} < 26$) from $\sim 2.6$ minutes of 60-channel eyes-open resting-state EEG recorded during ON-medication states. In a primary discovery cohort of 100 Parkinson's disease (PD) patients and 49 community controls, the authors fit autoregressive linear predictive coding (LPC) models to bandpass-filtered EEG signals, project the resulting LPC coefficient vectors onto principal-component affine subspaces representing cognitively impaired ($n=64$) and cognitively normal ($n=85$) groups, and combine single-electrode distance ratios across the 8 top-performing electrodes via a geometric mean. They report age-adjusted Spearman partial correlations of $\rho = 0.68\text{--}0.70$ ($p < 0.001$) with MoCA and $\rho = 0.56\text{--}0.70$ with five NIH Toolbox cognitive tests, alongside leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) classification accuracy of 79.87% ($\text{AUC} = 0.91$). Crucially, they evaluate the frozen 8-electrode MoCA model on an independent out-of-sample cohort of 32 PD patients from a separate clinical trial (NCT03808675), replicating the MoCA correlation ($\rho = 0.68, p < 0.001$) and discrimination ($\text{AUC} = 0.92$, accuracy $= 71.88\%$, sensitivity $= 50\%$, specificity $= 100\%$). Raw EEG datasets (OpenNeuro `ds004584`) and MATLAB/R code (GitHub) are publicly available.

The public deposition of raw EEG data and code and the inclusion of a genuinely independent 32-patient external validation cohort are major strengths that set this work apart from many neuroimaging biomarker studies. However, the internal cross-validation and NIH Toolbox analyses on the 149-subject discovery cohort suffer from hyperparameter and feature-selection leakage: the bandpass filter ranges, LPC orders, affine subspace dimensions, and top 8 electrodes were optimized via an exhaustive search across all 149 participants and then held fixed during subsequent LOOCV, 5-fold, and 10-fold evaluations on the same 149 participants. Because the NIH Toolbox models were *not* tested on the external 32-PD cohort (which lacked NIH Toolbox data), their reported correlations ($\rho = 0.56\text{--}0.70$) reflect in-sample hyperparameter over-optimization rather than unbiased generalization. Furthermore, the external validation classification shows severe threshold miscalibration (sensitivity drops to 50% at the fixed 0.5 cutoff), and the absence of ocular/muscular artifact removal leaves open the possibility that differential blink or tremor artifacts contribute to the low-frequency ($2\text{--}10\text{ Hz}$) frontal/occipital LPC features. These issues can be resolved computationally by implementing fully nested cross-validation for the discovery cohort and performing EOG/EMG artifact sensitivity checks.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Out-of-sample generalization of the 8-electrode LEAPD index for predicting MoCA scores in PD ($\rho = 0.68, p < 0.001$; $\text{AUC} = 0.92$): [Well Supported].**  
  All hyperparameters (frequency bands, LPC orders, subspace dimensions, and the 8 selected electrodes) were locked on the 149-subject cohort before evaluating the model on the independent 32-subject PD cohort (NCT03808675). Achieving $\rho = 0.68$ ($R^2 = 0.46$) and $\text{AUC} = 0.92$ on an untouched external dataset provides solid empirical evidence that the MoCA-trained LEAPD index captures reproducible electrophysiological variance associated with global cognitive status in PD.

* **Internal cross-validation performance on the 149-participant discovery cohort ($\rho = 0.70$ for MoCA; $\rho = 0.56\text{--}0.70$ for NIH Toolbox tests; $\le 2\%$ variance across CV schemes): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Methods ("LEAPD: theoretical framework for indexing cognition", line 76), the authors state: *"The first three parameters [bandpass frequency range between 2 and 34 Hz, LPC order between 2 and 10, affine subspace dimensions] were optimized for each EEG electrode by conducting an exhaustive search and maximizing Spearman's rho correlation of the single-electrode LEAPD indices with cognitive scores using a single round of 10-fold cross-validation... We utilized EEG data from 149 participants (Table 1) for these optimizations. Once these parameters were determined, we kept them unchanged for all analyses and performance evaluations across all participants."* Exhaustively searching hundreds of bandpass ranges, LPC orders, and subspace dimensions across 60 electrodes on all 149 subjects to maximize CV correlation, selecting the top 8 electrodes, and then running LOOCV, 5-fold, and 10-fold CV on the exact same 149 subjects with those pre-selected parameters leaks information from the full dataset into the cross-validation loop. This explains why the internal CV correlations have tiny standard deviations ($\pm 0.004\text{--}0.02$) and why the unvalidated NIH Toolbox correlations ($\rho = 0.56\text{--}0.70$) are likely optimistically biased. Similarly, if the randomization test (MoCA shuffling) kept the hyperparameters fixed rather than re-running the exhaustive search on each shuffled permutation, it fails to measure the null distribution of the exhaustive hyperparameter search itself.

* **80% accuracy for detecting MoCA-defined cognitive impairment and robustness in external validation: [Fragile].**  
  While the external 32-PD cohort achieves a high rank-based $\text{AUC} = 0.92$, applying the theoretical decision threshold ($\rho_{\text{combined}} < 0.5$) yields only 50.0% sensitivity (9 of 18 cognitively impaired PD patients missed) alongside 100% specificity (Table 2, Fig. 3b,d). Moreover, Table 1 reveals that in the external cohort, EEG recording duration was significantly shorter in the cognitively impaired group ($2.15 \pm 0.21\text{ min}$) than in the cognitively normal group ($2.54 \pm 0.45\text{ min}$, $p < 0.05$), and the impaired external PD group had a much narrower and milder MoCA range ($22\text{--}25$, mean $23.44 \pm 1.15$) than the discovery impaired group ($9\text{--}25$, mean $22.00 \pm 3.49$).

* **Neurophysiological interpretation of LEAPD features as purely cortical theta–alpha and beta oscillations unconfounded by artifacts: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The authors demonstrate at electrode P8 that LPC order 7 captures a downward shift in theta–alpha peak frequency ($7\text{--}13\text{ Hz}$) and reduced beta amplitude ($13\text{--}25\text{ Hz}$) in cognitively impaired subjects (Fig. 2e,f), consistent with established posterior slowing in PD dementia. However, the Methods (line 8) note that *"No further manual inspection, cleaning, or artifact removal was conducted"* beyond FFT line-noise removal, and four of the eight selected electrodes (F4, O2, PO7, P4; Fig. 2d) use narrow low-frequency passbands extending down to $2\text{--}4\text{ Hz}$, where vertical/horizontal eye blinks (EOG) and PD rest tremor ($4\text{--}6\text{ Hz}$) have high power.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Implement Fully Nested Cross-Validation for the 149-Participant Discovery Cohort and NIH Toolbox Models
* **WHY:** Optimizing bandpass frequencies, LPC orders, subspace dimensions, and the top 8 electrodes across all 149 participants before running LOOCV/5-fold/10-fold CV leaks test-fold information into hyperparameter selection. While the MoCA model is rescued by the external 32-PD validation cohort, the five NIH Toolbox models have no external validation and rely entirely on leaky CV.
* **WHAT:** Re-evaluate the discovery cohort (for both MoCA and all five NIH Toolbox batteries) using strict nested cross-validation, where the exhaustive parameter search and top-8 electrode selection are performed *exclusively* inside the training folds (e.g., 10-fold outer loop, 10-fold inner loop) without ever seeing the held-out outer test fold.
* **HOW:** Modify the MATLAB/R cross-validation script so that for each outer fold $k \in \{1,\dots,10\}$, the grid search over filter cutoffs ($2\text{--}34\text{ Hz}$), LPC order ($K \in \{2,\dots,10\}$), PCA dimension ($n < K$), and top-8 channel ranking is executed using only the $9/10$ training subjects, and report the resulting unbiased out-of-fold Spearman $\rho$, $R^2$, and AUC in Table 2. Likewise, run theexhaustive parameter search inside the label-shuffled permutation test (at least 100–500 permutations) to establish the true null distribution of the optimization pipeline.

#### 2. Calibrate the Classification Threshold Using Training-Set Youden's Index and Account for Subspace Sample-Size Imbalance
* **WHY:** In Equations 2–5, the theoretical derivation assumes equal numbers of cognitively impaired ($S$) and cognitively normal ($S$) training subjects, whereas the full discovery cohort is imbalanced ($n=64$ impaired vs. $n=85$ normal). Because singular value decomposition (SVD) subspace approximation accuracy depends on sample size and variance, the uncalibrated $\rho_{\text{combined}} = 0.5$ threshold is shifted, causing external test sensitivity to drop to 50% despite an AUC of 0.92.
* **WHAT:** Determine the optimal classification cutoff empirically on the discovery training data (e.g., maximizing Youden's $J = \text{Sensitivity} + \text{Specificity} - 1$ on the discovery ROC curve) and evaluate that locked empirical threshold on the 32-PD external test set.
* **HOW:** Compute the ROC curve on the 149-participant LOOCV predictions, identify the threshold $\tau^*$ maximizing Youden's $J$, and report the sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, and accuracy on the 32 external PD patients at $\tau^*$ alongside the default $\tau = 0.5$ cutoff.

#### 3. Perform an Artifact-Rejection and Recording-Length Sensitivity Analysis
* **WHY:** Because no eye-blink (EOG), muscle (EMG), or tremor artifact removal (such as ICA) was applied prior to energy normalization and LPC fitting, and because recording duration differed significantly between impaired ($2.15\text{ min}$) and normal ($2.54\text{ min}$) patients in the external test set (Table 1), non-neural artifacts or recording duration differences could influence the index.
* **WHAT:** (1) Standardize all recordings in the 32-PD external cohort to a fixed duration (e.g., truncating all recordings to the minimum length of $1.8\text{ min}$) to verify that the recording-length difference does not drive the external classification; and (2) compare LEAPD performance on the 149-subject dataset with and without automated ICA ocular/muscle artifact removal (e.g., EEGLAB `ICLabel`).
* **HOW:** Truncate all 32 external EEG recordings to exactly $108\text{ s}$ (54,000 samples at 500 Hz) and recompute the external MoCA correlation and AUC; run `ICLabel` on the 149-subject dataset to reject eye/muscle components ($>80\%$ probability) and report the resulting Spearman $\rho$ with MoCA.
