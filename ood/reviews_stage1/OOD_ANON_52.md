### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study combines fecal DNA metabarcoding (12S and COX1 amplicons from 360 snow leopard scats collected across four sites in China) with Maximum Entropy (MaxEnt) species distribution modeling to project habitat suitability and predator-prey spatial overlap for snow leopards (*Panthera uncia*, 342 thinned occurrence points) and their primary prey, blue sheep (*Pseudois nayaur*, 154 thinned occurrence points), across the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau (QTP) under current and 2070s climate scenarios (RCP 2.6, RCP 4.5, and RCP 8.5). Dietary metabarcoding identified prey in 341 scats, showing that blue sheep account for 54.3% of total prey occurrences, followed by Himalayan marmot (14.6%) and plateau pika (9.9%). Using MaxEnt binary suitability maps thresholded by Maximum Training Sensitivity Plus Specificity (MTSPS), the authors project that snow leopard habitat will decline by 13.0% (RCP 4.5) and 23.4% (RCP 8.5), while blue sheep habitat will decline by 38.3% and 49.7%, respectively. They further partition the landscape into three spatial scenarios: S1 ("Ideal distribution range," where both predator and prey habitats overlap), S2 ("Stepping stone," prey habitat without predator habitat), and S3 ("Potential conflict area," predator habitat without prey habitat). Raw sequencing reads, GPS coordinates, and modeling scripts are not deposited in any public repository.

While the 360-scat dietary dataset is a valuable empirical contribution confirming blue sheep dominance in snow leopard diets in Qinghai and Qilian, the spatial modeling and scenario projections suffer from severe arithmetic contradictions between tables, geographic sampling bias, and biologically inverted scenario definitions. Most critically, the areas reported for the three overlap scenarios (S1, S2, S3) in Table 3 are mathematically impossible to reconcile with the total species habitat areas reported in Table 2 (where $\text{S1} + \text{S3}$ must equal total snow leopard habitat and $\text{S1} + \text{S2}$ must equal total blue sheep habitat, yet they differ by $240,000\text{--}300,000\text{ km}^2$ in every scenario). Furthermore, the Discussion claims that the potential conflict area (S3) "significantly expanded" while S1 "continued to decrease under RCP 4.5 and RCP 8.5," which is directly contradicted by Table 3 showing that S3 actually shrinks under RCP 8.5 ($409,792.80\text{ km}^2$ vs. $462,501.56\text{ km}^2$ current) and that S1 collapses by 94.5% under RCP 8.5 (to $10,794.82\text{ km}^2$, likely a tenfold typographical or calculation error). Resolving these issues requires auditing the GIS raster intersection calculations, correcting for severe spatial sampling bias in occurrence records outside Qinghai province, and publicly archiving the metabarcoding reads and occurrence data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Blue sheep (*Pseudois nayaur*) is the primary prey of snow leopards in the sampled regions (54.3% of prey occurrences), followed by Himalayan marmot (14.6%) and plateau pika (9.9%): [Well Supported].**  
  The amplicon metabarcoding of 341 successfully amplified scats provides solid empirical support for diet composition in the Qilian Mountains ($n = 266$) and Dulan ($n = 77$), although spatial representation across the broader QTP is highly skewed ($73.9\%$ of scats come from Qilian Mountains alone, with only $n = 2$ from Hoh Xil and $n = 15$ from Yushu) and Table 1 contains a major calculation error for Wild Yak ("11 (30%)" in the "All" column, where $11 / 363$ total occurrences is actually $3.0\%$, not $30\%$).

* **Snow leopard suitable habitat will decrease by 13.0% (RCP 4.5) and 23.4% (RCP 8.5), and blue sheep suitable habitat will decrease by 38.3% (RCP 4.5) and 49.7% (RCP 8.5), with Qinghai province holding 62–81% of all QTP habitat: [Fragile].**  
  Although the QTP spans $2.5\text{ million km}^2$ across Xizang (Tibet), Qinghai, Xinjiang, Sichuan, Gansu, Yunnan, and neighboring countries, the authors' occurrence data (Figure 2) are heavily concentrated in Qinghai and Gansu, with virtually no sampling across vast portions of Xizang and Sichuan. Without background target-group bias correction or spatial block cross-validation, MaxEnt overfits to the sampling density in Qinghai, resulting in the biologically implausible projection in Table 2 that Sichuan province—a major core range for blue sheep—contains only $168.79\text{ km}^2$ ($0.04\%$) of current blue sheep habitat and $0.34\text{ km}^2$ ($0.00\%$) under RCP 8.5. Furthermore, the models use outdated WorldClim 1.4 (1950–2000) and CMIP5 RCP scenarios with default MaxEnt regularization ($\text{RM} = 1$) without hyperparameter tuning.

* **The three overlap scenarios (S1: Ideal overlap, S2: Stepping stone, S3: Potential conflict area) accurately partition snow leopard and blue sheep distributions, and S3 significantly expands under climate change: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  By definition (Section 2.5), S1 is the intersection of snow leopard ($SL$) and blue sheep ($BS$) binary habitats ($SL \cap BS$), S2 is blue sheep habitat without snow leopard habitat ($BS \setminus SL$), and S3 is snow leopard habitat without blue sheep habitat ($SL \setminus BS$). Consequently, $\text{S1} + \text{S3}$ must exactly equal the total snow leopard habitat ($SL$) in Table 2, and $\text{S1} + \text{S2}$ must exactly equal the total blue sheep habitat ($BS$) in Table 2. However, in Table 3 under "Current," $\text{S1} + \text{S3} = 196,236.47 + 462,501.56 = 658,738.03\text{ km}^2$ (missing $328,940.85\text{ km}^2$ of the $987,678.88\text{ km}^2$ snow leopard habitat in Table 2), and $\text{S1} + \text{S2} = 196,236.47 + 126,244.77 = 322,481.24\text{ km}^2$ (missing $153,497.23\text{ km}^2$ of the $475,978.47\text{ km}^2$ blue sheep habitat in Table 2). Under RCP 8.5, Table 3 reports $\text{S1} = 10,794.82\text{ km}^2$ (a 94.5% drop from RCP 4.5's $139,251.44\text{ km}^2$, whereas Section 5 claims "S1 decreasing slightly"), and $\text{S3} = 409,792.80\text{ km}^2$, which is *smaller* than the current S3 ($462,501.56\text{ km}^2$), directly contradicting the Discussion claim (Section 4, Line 258) that "with climate change, the area of S3 (potential conflict area) significantly expanded."

* **Scenario S2 (presence of blue sheep prey without snow leopard abiotic suitability) represents ecological "stepping stones" for snow leopard migration, while S3 represents human-wildlife conflict zones: [Overstated].**  
  Defining all regions where blue sheep occur outside modeled snow leopard abiotic habitat (S2) as "stepping stones" for snow leopard dispersal (and recommending corridors for "Tibetan antelope (*Pantholops hodgsonii*)" in Section 5, a species not studied here) conflates lower-elevation ungulate habitat with carnivore movement corridors. Similarly, defining S3 (climatically suitable snow leopard habitat lacking modeled blue sheep presence) as a "human-wildlife conflict area" ignores alternative wild prey (such as marmots, pikas, and ibex, which comprise 36.1% of the diet in Table 1) and does not incorporate livestock density or human settlement layers in defining S3.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reconcile the Mathematical Contradictions Between Table 2 and Table 3
* **WHY:** Because S1 ($SL \cap BS$), S2 ($BS \setminus SL$), and S3 ($SL \setminus BS$) are mutually exclusive partitions of the binary suitability rasters from Figure 7, it is mathematically impossible for $\text{S1} + \text{S3} \neq \text{Total } SL$ and $\text{S1} + \text{S2} \neq \text{Total } BS$. In addition, the RCP 8.5 S1 value ($10,794.82\text{ km}^2$) appears to be missing a digit (likely $\sim 107,948\text{ km}^2$).
* **WHAT:** Re-compute the raster overlay areas for Table 2 and Table 3 in an equal-area projection and ensure exact additive consistency.
* **HOW:** Project all binary suitability rasters (Current, RCP 2.6, RCP 4.5, RCP 8.5) to an Albers Equal-Area Conic projection before calculating pixel areas. Compute $S1 = \text{sum}(SL == 1 \ \& \ BS == 1)$, $S2 = \text{sum}(SL == 0 \ \& \ BS == 1)$, and $S3 = \text{sum}(SL == 1 \ \& \ BS == 0)$, multiplied by the exact pixel area in $\text{km}^2$. Verify that $S1 + S3$ equals the sum of provincial snow leopard areas in Table 2 and $S1 + S2$ equals the sum of provincial blue sheep areas in Table 2.

#### 2. Control for Severe Geographic Sampling Bias in Occurrence Records
* **WHY:** Random $10\text{ km}^2$ grid thinning removes local clustering within surveyed sites (Qinghai/Qilian) but does not correct for regional non-sampling across Xizang and Sichuan. Consequently, MaxEnt treats unsampled provinces as unsuitable, predicting near-zero blue sheep habitat in Sichuan ($0.04\%$).
* **WHAT:** Implement background sampling bias correction, tune MaxEnt hyperparameters, and evaluate models via spatial block cross-validation.
* **HOW:** Create a Gaussian kernel density bias file from all mammal survey/camera-trap effort points across the QTP to weight background pseudo-absence selection in MaxEnt. Additionally, supplement blue sheep occurrences in Xizang, Sichuan, and Yunnan using GBIF and published QTP ungulate databases. Use `ENMeval` to tune regularization multipliers ($0.5\text{--}4.0$) and feature classes via spatial block partitioning (`blockCV`) rather than random 25% test splits.

#### 3. Propagate GCM Uncertainty Instead of Using Only the Strict Intersection Map
* **WHY:** The caption of Figure 7 states that "Future distributions are the intersection area of the three GCMs (HadGEM2-AO, CCSM4, and BCC-CSM1-1)." Taking the strict binary intersection ($\text{GCM}_1 \cap \text{GCM}_2 \cap \text{GCM}_3$) for future projections while comparing it against a single baseline current map systematically biases future habitat estimates downward because any pixel where even one GCM falls slightly below the MTSPS threshold is classified as lost habitat.
* **WHAT:** Average the continuous habitat suitability probabilities across the three GCMs *before* applying the MTSPS threshold, and report the mean and standard deviation (or range) across the three GCMs.
* **HOW:** For each RCP scenario, compute the ensemble mean probability raster $\bar{P}(x) = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{m=1}^{3} P_m(x)$, apply the MTSPS threshold to $\bar{P}(x)$, and also report the individual area estimates for HadGEM2-AO, CCSM4, and BCC-CSM1-1 in Table 2 and Table 3 to quantify inter-model variance.

#### 4. Align Discussion Claims with Table 3 Results and Incorporate Human/Livestock Data into S3
* **WHY:** The Discussion claims S3 expands under RCP 4.5 and 8.5, whereas Table 3 shows S3 decreases under RCP 8.5 ($409,792.80\text{ km}^2$) compared to Current ($462,501.56\text{ km}^2$) and RCP 2.6 ($619,604.43\text{ km}^2$). Moreover, absence of blue sheep does not automatically equate to human-wildlife conflict if snow leopards prey on marmots or pikas or if no humans/livestock are present.
* **WHAT:** Revise the text in Sections 3.4, 4, and 5 to match the corrected Table 3 numbers, and refine the definition of S3.
* **HOW:** Define "Potential Conflict Areas" by intersecting S3 ($SL \setminus BS$) with areas where Human Influence Index (HII) or grazing/livestock density exceeds a threshold, rather than assuming all blue-sheep-free snow leopard habitat represents human conflict.
