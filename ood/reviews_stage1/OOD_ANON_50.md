### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces VirtuDockDL, a Python/Flask web pipeline combining RDKit descriptor/fingerprint extraction, a PyTorch Geometric Graph Neural Network (GNN) binary classifier, Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) clustering on predicted probabilities, OpenMM protein energy minimization, and AutoDock Vina docking. The authors apply VirtuDockDL to screen for non-covalent inhibitors of Marburg virus VP35 (PDB: `4GH9`), reporting test accuracy of 0.9779 ($\text{AUC} = 0.9972$) and docking Clomipramine and Marizomib into VP35, and claim benchmark superiority on HER2 (`3PP0`, 99% accuracy vs. 89% for DeepChem and 82% for AutoDock Vina), *E. coli* TEM-1 beta-lactamase (`1XPB`, 93% accuracy), and *Candida* CYP51 (95% accuracy). Code is hosted on GitHub.

A forensic audit reveals that this manuscript is fundamentally invalid due to pervasive methodological circularity, fabricated or impossible benchmark comparisons, missing equations and corrupted text throughout the Methods and Results, and severe biochemical errors:
1. **Trivial synthetic decoy construction and circular feature leakage (Section "Case study and benchmarking", line 152):** First, the authors state that *"To date, there are no validated small molecule VP35 inhibitors"* (line 152), yet immediately claim to compile a positive dataset of active VP35 molecules from BindingDB, PubChem, and ChEMBL, paired with negative "decoys" constructed by *"randomly assembling chemical elements into SMILES strings, which are then validated using RDKit's `Chem.MolFromSmiles`"* alongside DUD-E decoys. Randomly concatenating element symbols into SMILES strings produces nonsensical chemical fragments whose simple 1D descriptors (Molecular Weight, LogP, TPSA, H-bond donors/acceptors, explicitly fed into the dense layer alongside the GNN; line 84 and Fig. 3A, B, D) trivially separate them from real bioassay compounds—explaining the 0.9972 AUC without learning any target-specific pharmacology.
2. **Impossible and undefined benchmarks against AutoDock Vina and DeepChem (Abstract line 8; Section "Benchmarking", lines 217–219):** The authors claim that on the HER2 dataset (`3PP0`), VirtuDockDL's ligand-only GNN classifier (which takes **only ligand SMILES/descriptors** as input and does not take protein structure as input at all!) outperformed structure-based **AutoDock Vina**, claiming AutoDock Vina *"predicted binding affinities with an accuracy of 82% and an F1 score of 0.84, compared to VirtuDockDL's 99% accuracy"* (line 219). AutoDock Vina outputs continuous physical free energy estimates in $\text{kcal/mol}$, not binary classification accuracies or F1 scores, and no energy threshold, decoy set, or active/inactive count is defined. Furthermore, `DeepChem` is a software library containing dozens of models (including `GCNConv`), not a single algorithm, and `MolDQN` (line 217) is a reinforcement-learning molecule generator, not a virtual screening classifier.
3. **Biochemically false drug characterizations (Section "Protein structure refinement and molecular docking analysis", lines 190–204):** The authors screen a library of *"non-covalent inhibitors"* and highlight **Marizomib** (Salinosporamide A) as their top **non-covalent** hit against Marburg VP35 (line 204). In reality, Marizomib is a classic **irreversible covalent** $\beta$-lactone-$\gamma$-lactam proteasome inhibitor that forms a covalent ester bond with the catalytic N-terminal threonine (`Thr1`) of the 20S proteasome and causes severe systemic cytotoxicity, making its selection as a "reversible, low-toxicity non-covalent viral VP35 inhibitor" (line 190) biochemically nonsensical. Similarly, in the HER2 benchmark (line 219), the authors highlight **Dasatinib** and **Nilotinib** as *"key inhibitors"* of HER2 (`3PP0`) with weak Vina scores of $-7.12$ and $-6.89\text{ kcal/mol}$, when Dasatinib (a BCR-ABL/Src inhibitor) and Nilotinib (a selective BCR-ABL inhibitor) are **not** HER2 inhibitors.
4. **Completely blank mathematical equation blocks, 64-layer GNN oversmoothing claim, and garbled results text (lines 39–136, 221, 229):** Every single mathematical equation block in the manuscript (lines 50, 58, 68, 78, 107, 117, 125) is **completely empty (`| |`)**, inline mathematical symbols are missing across lines 39–134, line 91 claims the GNN uses *"one input layer, 64 hidden layers, and two output layers"* (a 64-layer GCN would suffer total oversmoothing unless "64 hidden channels/units" was meant), and line 229 reads: *"with a test accuracy of 0. The model also obtained an accuracy of 0. 95 and a recall of 0. As shown in score 97"*.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **VirtuDockDL's GNN accurately identifies non-covalent inhibitors against Marburg virus VP35 ($\text{Accuracy} = 0.9779, \text{AUC} = 0.9972$), identifying Clomipramine and Marizomib as top non-covalent VP35 candidates: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the GNN model takes **only a single ligand's SMILES graph and RDKit physicochemical descriptors** (MW, LogP, TPSA, HBD, HBA, rotatable bonds, Morgan/MACCS fingerprints) as input—it contains no protein sequence or pocket representation whatsoever. Second, the negative class was populated by randomly assembling chemical symbols into SMILES strings (`de-novo generation function`, line 152), creating a trivial covariate shift in molecular weight and TPSA (visible in Fig. 3B, D) that any classifier can separate at $>97\%$ accuracy without learning VP35 binding. Third, the authors contradict themselves on whether active VP35 training ligands even exist: line 152 states *"To date, there are no validated small molecule VP35 inhibitors"*, followed three sentences later by *"the positive dataset (active molecules) was initially prepared by selecting only active molecules of protein of interest from a literature review and databases like BindingDB, PubChem and ChEMBL"* without reporting how many active molecules were found or what bioassay threshold defined activity. Fourth, Marizomib is a well-known **covalent** $\beta$-lactone proteasome inhibitor, not a reversible non-covalent viral inhibitor.

* **VirtuDockDL achieves 99% accuracy ($\text{F1} = 0.992, \text{AUC} = 0.99$) on the HER2 dataset (`3PP0`), surpassing DeepChem (89%), AutoDock Vina (82%), Glide, Chemprop, MolDQN, RosettaVS, PyRMD, and MzDOCK: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  No dataset sizes (number of actives and decoys), splitting protocols (random vs. scaffold split), hyperparameter settings, or evaluation metrics are provided for any of the benchmarked tools; `PyRMD`, `RosettaVS`, `MzDOCK`, `Chemprop`, `MolDQN`, and `Glide` are discussed purely via qualitative text assertions without a single quantitative metric table; `AutoDock Vina` is a physics-based pose/affinity docker ($\text{kcal/mol}$) rather than a binary classifier with an "accuracy of 82% and F1 score of 0.84"; and the authors validate their HER2 docking by citing predicted affinities for Dasatinib ($-7.12\text{ kcal/mol}$) and Nilotinib ($-6.89\text{ kcal/mol}$), which are BCR-ABL inhibitors rather than HER2 inhibitors.

* **VirtuDockDL accurately classifies and docks inhibitors for *E. coli* TEM-1 beta-lactamase (`1XPB`) and *Candida* CYP51: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section "Benchmarking" (lines 221 and 229), neither the source nor the size of the training/testing active and decoy sets for TEM-1 or CYP51 is disclosed. In addition, the reported metrics for TEM-1 (`Precision = 0.94`, `Recall = 0.95`, `F1 = 0.962` on line 221) are **mathematically impossible**: the F1 score is the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall ($\text{F1} = \frac{2 \times P \times R}{P + R}$), which is strictly bounded between $\min(P, R)$ and $\max(P, R)$. For $P = 0.94$ and $R = 0.95$, the harmonic mean is **0.9449**, so an F1 score of **0.962** (strictly greater than both Precision and Recall!) cannot exist. Similarly, the CYP51 paragraph (line 229) is severely corrupted (*"showcased a high accuracy in segmenting active inhibitors with a test accuracy of 0. The model also obtained an accuracy of 0. 95 and a recall of 0. As shown in score 97"*).

* **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) clustering on 1D predicted active probabilities ($P(y=1|x)$) groups compounds with similar drug-like structural properties ($\text{Silhouette} = 0.9355, \text{Davies-Bouldin} = 0.0201$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Fitting a GMM on a 1D scalar output probability $p_i \in [0, 1]$ from a binary classifier with AUC = 0.9972 (where predictions are bimodally concentrated near $0.0$ and $1.0$) simply partitions the scalar interval $[0, 1]$ into two tight numerical bins (near $0$ and near $1$), which trivially yields a Silhouette score near $1.0$ and Davies–Bouldin score near $0.0$. It does **not** cluster compounds by chemical scaffold or structural similarity.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Synthetic Random-SMILES Decoys and Evaluate on Property-Matched, Scaffold-Split Benchmarks
* **WHY:** Generating negative training examples by randomly concatenating element symbols into SMILES strings (`Chem.MolFromSmiles`, line 152) creates trivial physicochemical differences in MW, LogP, and TPSA between positives and negatives, inflating test accuracy to 97.8%–99.0% via 1D descriptor leakage.
* **WHAT:** Remove all randomly generated SMILES strings from the negative datasets for VP35, HER2, TEM-1, and CYP51. Use experimentally confirmed inactive compounds from PubChem/ChEMBL bioassays or property-matched DUD-E / LIT-PCBA decoys, split training and test sets by Bemis–Murcko chemical scaffolds rather than random stratified shuffle splits, and report exact dataset counts ($N_{\text{active}}, N_{\text{inactive}}$) for every target.
* **HOW:** Partition molecules using `rdkit.Chem.Scaffolds.MurckoScaffold` (80% train / 20% test scaffold split), train the GNN, and report test-set ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, and $\text{EF}_{1\%}$ alongside a random-forest baseline trained on the RDKit descriptors alone to quantify the actual contribution of the GNN layers.

#### 2. Correct the Mathematically Impossible Metrics, Missing Equations, and Corrupted Text
* **WHY:** All 7 displayed equations in the Methods section are blank (`| |`), inline variable symbols are missing, the TEM-1 F1 score ($0.962$) violates the harmonic mean identity for $P = 0.94, R = 0.95$ ($\text{F1} = 0.945$), and the CYP51 results paragraph (line 229) has truncated numbers (`0.`).
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Restore all missing mathematical expressions in the 7 equation blocks (lines 50, 58, 68, 78, 107, 117, 125) and inline variables (lines 39, 46, 54, 62, 64, 74, 82, 84, 86, 91, 103, 111, 121, 129, 134, 136).
  2. In line 91, change *"64 hidden layers"* to *"64 hidden channels"* (or specify the exact number of `GCNConv` message-passing layers and hidden dimension size).
  3. Recompute Precision, Recall, and F1 directly from the test-set confusion matrices for VP35, HER2, TEM-1, and CYP51 so that $\text{F1} = \frac{2PR}{P+R}$ holds exactly, and fix the corrupted sentences on lines 152, 204, 221, and 229.

#### 3. Replace Invalid AutoDock Vina "Accuracy/F1" Claims with Standard Enrichment Factor and ROC-AUC Benchmarks
* **WHY:** AutoDock Vina is an uncalibrated docking scoring function ($\Delta G$ in $\text{kcal/mol}$), not a binary classifier, and comparing a supervised ligand QSAR model trained on HER2 actives against unsupervised docking scores without specifying a threshold or shared test set is invalid.
* **WHAT:** On a shared held-out test set of known HER2 actives (e.g., Lapatinib, Neratinib, Tucatinib—not BCR-ABL inhibitors Dasatinib and Nilotinib) and property-matched decoys from DUD-E (`erbB2`), rank all compounds by (a) AutoDock Vina $\Delta G$, (b) DeepChem `GCNModel` trained on the exact same scaffold split, and (c) VirtuDockDL, and compare threshold-independent ROC-AUC, BedROC, and $\text{EF}_{1\%}$ in a comprehensive table.
* **HOW:** Dock the DUD-E `erbB2` test set into PDB `3PP0` using AutoDock Vina, compute ROC-AUC from the continuous $-\Delta G$ scores, and replace the undefined "82% accuracy" claim in the Abstract and Section "Benchmarking".

#### 4. Remove Covalent Proteasome Inhibitor Marizomib from the Non-Covalent Antiviral Claims
* **WHY:** Marizomib is a warhead-bearing irreversible covalent inhibitor of the human 20S proteasome, contradicting the section title and premise (*"Rescreening with library of non-covalent inhibitors"*, lines 190–204).
* **WHAT:** Filter the screening library for pan-assay interference compounds (PAINS) and reactive covalent warheads ($\beta$-lactones, epoxides, Michael acceptors) using RDKit `FilterCatalog` prior to docking against Marburg VP35, and benchmark docked hits against co-crystallized or experimentally validated filovirus VP35 IID binders (e.g., compound crystallographic fragments in PDB `4GH9`).
* **HOW:** Apply `rdkit.Chem.FilterCatalog` (PAINS + Brenk reactive filters) to the ZINC/PubChem screening library, re-dock the clean non-covalent candidates into the `4GH9` binding pocket, and report their binding poses and energies relative to the native `4GH9` crystallographic ligand.
