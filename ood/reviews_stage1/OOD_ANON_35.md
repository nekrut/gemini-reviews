### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces `DDGR-DTI`, a multimodal binary drug–target interaction (DTI) classifier that integrates sequence representations (character-tokenized SMILES and protein sequences processed through a Grouping Update Module [GUM] and BiLSTM), graph representations (RDKit molecular graphs and ESMFold residue contact maps processed through a 3-layer GAT), and pretrained language model embeddings (ChemBERTa and ESM-1b) via a Dual-Stream Attention Module (DAM) and a multi-stage MLP cascade termed a "Rebalanced Pyramid Network" (RPN). The model is evaluated on binarized Davis ($7,320$ positives / $18,452$ negatives), KIBA ($22,154$ positives / $94,196$ negatives), and balanced DrugBank ($17,511$ positives / $17,511$ negatives) benchmarks under random (8:1:1) and cold-start splits. Source code and processed datasets are provided on GitHub.

Although combining ESMFold contact maps with protein and chemical language models yields competitive binary classification performance in cold-start settings (Table 4), the manuscript has serious mathematical inconsistencies in its benchmark tables, unsubstantiated claims of binding-site localization, and asymmetric baseline comparisons. Specifically, multiple baseline $F_1$ scores in Tables 1 and 2 are mathematically impossible—most notably in Table 2, where MCANet's reported $F_1$ scores under Target cold start ($0.841$) and All unseen ($0.796$) exceed *both* their corresponding Precision ($0.823$ and $0.777$) and Recall ($0.627$ and $0.609$) values, violating the definition of the harmonic mean. Furthermore, the Abstract, Introduction, and Key Points claim that DAM "enables the accurate identification of binding sites," yet no quantitative binding-site benchmark is performed in the Results, and the Conclusion (line 453) explicitly admits that DAM attention weights "do not always correlate strongly with actual interaction sites." Addressing these issues requires recomputing all evaluation metrics from raw prediction logs, adding a symmetric PLM-augmented baseline, and removing unsupported binding-site and dynamic modality-rebalancing claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **DDGR-DTI outperforms six state-of-the-art baselines across Davis, KIBA, and DrugBank in warm-start and cold-start settings (Tables 1 and 2): [Fragile].**  
  First, Tables 1 and 2 contain multiple mathematically impossible $F_1$ values ($F_1 = \frac{2PR}{P+R}$ must lie strictly between Precision $P$ and Recall $R$). In Table 2 (Target cold start, line 341), MCANet is reported with $P = 0.823, R = 0.627$, and $F_1 = 0.841$ (true harmonic mean $= 0.712$); in Table 2 (All unseen, line 348), MCANet has $P = 0.777, R = 0.609$, and $F_1 = 0.796$ (true harmonic mean $= 0.683$, and $0.796$ is duplicated from the AUC column). Similarly, in Table 1, DO-GMA on Davis ($P = 0.629, R = 0.874$) is reported with $F_1 = 0.839$ (true $= 0.732$), DO-GMA on KIBA ($P = 0.571, R = 0.882$) is reported with $F_1 = 0.865$ (true $= 0.693$), and MolTrans on Davis ($P = 0.539, R = 0.892$) is reported with $F_1 = 0.748$ (duplicated from Accuracy $0.748$; true $= 0.672$). Second, the comparison is asymmetric: DDGR-DTI uses ESMFold 3D contact maps, ESM-1b, and ChemBERTa embeddings, whereas sequence/2D-graph baselines (MolTrans, HyperAttentionDTI, DrugBAN, MCANet) are trained without PLM or 3D structural inputs.

* **The Dual-Stream Attention Module (DAM) captures substructure-level interactions and enables accurate identification of binding sites: [Overstated].**  
  While the Abstract (line 8), Introduction (lines 29, 41), Methods (line 145), and Key Points (line 462) state that DAM accurately identifies binding sites, the Results section contains zero quantitative or qualitative evaluations of binding-site localization (no PDB pocket evaluations, no docking pose comparisons, and no residue-level ROC-AUC). Moreover, the Conclusion (line 453) contradicts the Abstract by stating that "the attention weights generated by the DAM do not always correlate strongly with actual interaction sites." Methodologically, GUM segments protein sequences into fixed 200-residue blocks (`L = 1000` divided into 5 coarse chunks; lines 259, 434), which cannot resolve individual binding-pocket residues.

* **The Rebalanced Pyramid Network (RPN) dynamically adjusts modality weights to alleviate modality learning bias: [Overstated].**  
  As described in lines 180–241, RPN consists of six fixed 3-layer MLPs ($\text{MLP}_{\text{seq}}, \text{MLP}_{\text{graph}}, \text{MLP}_{\text{plm}}, \text{MLP}_{\text{text}}, \text{MLP}_{\text{struc}}, \text{MLP}_{\text{mix}}$) trained end-to-end with a single binary classification loss. It includes no sample-adaptive gating weights, no gradient-norm balancing, and no modality-specific auxiliary losses. The t-SNE plots in Fig. 4 show progressive class separation across deeper MLP layers closer to the binary supervision head—an expected property of any deep supervised classifier rather than evidence of modality rebalancing.

* **Pretrained language model (PLM) features provide the largest performance gain in the all-unseen cold-start setting (Table 4): [Well Supported].**  
  Comparing Table 3 (warm-start Davis, where removing PLM drops AUC by only $0.003$, from $0.943$ to $0.940$) with Table 4 (all-unseen DrugBank, where removing PLM drops AUC by $0.053$, from $0.874$ to $0.821$) clearly demonstrates that transfer learning from ESM-1b and ChemBERTa is the primary driver of cold-start generalization.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Recompute All Precision, Recall, Accuracy, and $F_1$ Metrics in Tables 1 and 2 from Raw Test Predictions
* **WHY:** Multiple entries in Tables 1 and 2 violate the mathematical identity $F_1 = \frac{2PR}{P+R}$ or duplicate values from the AUC/Accuracy columns, undermining confidence in the benchmark tables.
* **WHAT:** Audit the evaluation script for all baselines and DDGR-DTI across Davis, KIBA, and DrugBank, and replace all corrupted $F_1$ entries in Tables 1 and 2.
* **HOW:** Re-run `sklearn.metrics.precision_recall_fscore_support` on the saved fold-level test set probability predictions (at threshold 0.5) for all 7 models, verify that every fold satisfies $\min(P, R) \le F_1 \le \max(P, R)$, and reconcile the Davis DDGR-DTI Accuracy standard deviation discrepancy between Table 1 (`0.896 (0.021)`) and Tables 3 and 5 (`0.896 (0.014)`).

#### 2. Add AUPR for Imbalanced Datasets (Davis and KIBA) and Symmetric PLM-Augmented Baselines
* **WHY:** Davis (1:2.52 positive-to-negative ratio) and KIBA (1:4.25 ratio) are class-imbalanced, where ROC-AUC and Accuracy can mask false-positive inflation, and comparing a PLM+ESMFold model against raw-sequence baselines confounds architecture gains with pretrained feature gains.
* **WHAT:** Report Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (AUPR) on Davis and KIBA, and evaluate at least two top baselines (e.g., DrugBAN and MCANet) initialized with the exact same ESM-1b and ChemBERTa embeddings used by DDGR-DTI.
* **HOW:** Replace the initial random token embedding layers in DrugBAN and MCANet with frozen/fine-tuned ESM-1b and ChemBERTa representations, retrain under the identical 8:1:1 warm and cold splits, and report both ROC-AUC and AUPR.

#### 3. Quantitatively Benchmark Binding-Site Localization or Reframe "Binding Site Identification" Claims
* **WHY:** Claiming "accurate identification of binding sites" throughout the paper without any experimental evaluation—while admitting in the Conclusion that attention weights do not correlate with actual binding sites—is internally contradictory.
* **WHAT:** Either evaluate residue-level and atom-level DAM attention weights against ground-truth PDB ligand-contacting residues (e.g., on the DrugBAN / PDBbind co-crystal subset) or remove claims of "accurate binding site identification" from the Abstract, Introduction, Methods, and Key Points.
* **HOW:** If retaining the claim, compute the top-$k$ recall / ROC-AUC of DAM graph-branch attention weights ($\text{DAM}_{\text{graph}}$) against heavy-atom contacts ($< 5\text{ \AA}$) on PDB complexes shared with DrugBank; otherwise, reframe DAM strictly as a coarse-grained cross-representation interaction module.

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Missing baseline descriptions, inconsistent notation, and discrepancy between listed and tabulated models hinder reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Lines 264–277 vs. Tables 1–2:** Reconcile the "Baseline models" section, which lists `DeepConv-DTI` [8] and omits `HyperAttentionDTI` [11], whereas Tables 1 and 2 tabulate `HyperAttentionDTI` and omit `DeepConv-DTI`.
  2. **Line 145:** Clarify how max-pooling of protein and drug graph features prior to DAM preserves residue-to-atom dimensionality for the interaction matrix in Equations 8–11.
  3. **Lines 178–241:** Replace claims that RPN "dynamically adjusts the relative weights of features" with an accurate description of static learned MLP fusion layers, or add an explicit softmax gating mechanism over the modality branches.
