### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study surveys vascular plants, insect pollinators (wild bees and hoverflies), and breeding birds across seven 1-hectare sites in the Subotica Sands protected area of northern Serbia during five field visits in 2021. The seven sites comprise three mowed sites (M1–M3), three sheep/goat-grazed sites (G1–G3), and one unmanaged control site (C). Using one-way ANOVA, Jaccard similarity, Gaussian generalized linear models (GLMs), linear discriminant analysis (LDA), and a scikit-learn Decision Tree classifier (with `max_depth = 30` and SMOTE oversampling), the authors conclude that active grassland management increases biodiversity across all three taxonomic groups, recommend prioritizing generalist/pioneer species in restoration, and advocate decision tree machine learning models for conservation management.

While multi-taxon biodiversity monitoring in Pannonian sand steppes is ecologically worthwhile, the study's statistical design, machine learning pipeline, and biological conclusions suffer from severe methodological flaws and internal contradictions. First, the experimental design has only $N = 7$ sites—with an unreplicated $N = 1$ control site—yet the analyses treat 224 spatiotemporal subplot observations ($8\text{ plots} \times 7\text{ sites} \times 4\text{ visits}$) as independent replicates in ANOVA, GLMs, LDA, and decision tree classification. Randomly splitting 224 correlated plot-visits 80/20 into training and validation sets after applying SMOTE oversampling leaks site-specific species composition between training and test sets, while `max_depth = 30` severely overfits the small sample. Second, the manuscript contradicts its own tables and methods: the text claims management significantly increased diversity across taxa, whereas Table 3 shows no significant difference in Shannon diversity for pollinators or birds across any sites or management regimes ($P > 0.05$); total species counts differ between sections (31 vs. 32 pollinators; 61 vs. 46 birds); Table 2 percentage cover columns sum to only 65.9%–73.0%; and the Conclusions claim that modern **cattle** are an adequate substitute for *Bos primigenius*, despite the Methods stating that the study sites were grazed exclusively by **sheep and goats**. Resolving these issues requires aggregating or clustering all statistical models at the true site level ($N = 7$), removing the leaked machine learning pipeline, and rewriting the conclusions to match the actual data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Grazing and mowing management strategies significantly increase species richness and diversity across plants, insect pollinators, and birds compared to unmanaged habitats: [Overstated].**  
  First, total species richness comparisons (e.g., 85 and 115 plant species in G and M vs. 59 in C; line 93) pool three 1-ha sites ($3\text{ ha}$ total effort) for G and M against a single 1-ha site ($1\text{ ha}$ effort) for C without rarefaction or per-site averaging. Second, when evaluated per site using the Shannon-Weaver index (Table 3), there are no statistically significant differences across sites or management treatments for pollinators (`0.44`–`1.36`, all group `a`/`A`) or birds (`0.85`–`2.00`, all group `a`/`A`), and the unmanaged control site C actually has the highest point estimate of bird diversity (`2.00`). Even for plants, line 125 contradicts itself in two consecutive sentences, first stating that Shannon diversity was "significantly higher at mowed sites than at grazed and unmanaged sites" and immediately stating that "no significant differences in the Shannon-Weaver index for plants were noted between mowed and grazed sites."

* **Plant diversity and external tree cover significantly drive bird diversity in Gaussian GLMs ($p = 0.00035$ and $p = 0.03$; Table 5): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Bird territory mapping and tree cover within a 150 m buffer were measured at the site level ($N = 7$ sites). A Gaussian GLM with 3 predictors (`Trees outside sites`, `Vegetation height`, `Plant diversity`) fitted to $N = 7$ independent sites has only $7 - 4 = 3$ residual degrees of freedom, for which $t = 4.15$ yields $p = 0.025$, not $p = 0.00035$ (and $t = 2.28$ yields $p = 0.107$, not $p = 0.03$). Obtaining $p = 0.00035$ at $t = 4.15$ requires $\sim 28\text{–}35$ degrees of freedom, indicating that site-level bird counts and buffer tree percentages were pseudoreplicated across repeated seasonal visits or vegetation subplots without site random effects.

* **Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) and a `max_depth = 30` Decision Tree classifier with SMOTE accurately classify management regimes (71% accuracy) and identify causal habitat-building plant species: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  All "Control" observations originate from a single 1-ha parcel (Site C). Splitting the 224 subplot-visit records randomly into 75/25 (LDA) or 80/20 (Decision Tree) train/test sets places subplots and repeated seasonal visits from the exact same 1-ha site into both training and validation sets. Applying SMOTE oversampling to the minority class (Site C) before or during cross-validation further fabricates synthetic clones of that single site's flora. Moreover, a decision tree with `max_depth = 30` on 224 observations has far more depth than needed to memorize individual plots ($2^{30} > 10^9$ possible leaves) and recovers correlative indicator presence (e.g., *Galium verum*, *Dactylis glomerata*), not "causal relationships" (line 86).

* **Cattle grazing in Subotica Sands is an adequate modern substitute for Pleistocene/Holocene wild bovids (*Bos primigenius*, *Bos taurus*): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The Conclusions (line 236) state that "we also conclude that the cattle used for habitat management today are an adequate substitute for the former cattle species (*Bos primigenius*, *Bos taurus*)." However, the Methods (lines 39, 65) and Discussion (line 221) explicitly state that the study area was grazed by **sheep and goats** ("270 sheep per 230 ha", "0.6 ewe/lamb pairs per hectare"), and cattle were never studied.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct Pseudoreplication by Analyzing Data at the Site Level or Using Mixed-Effects Models
* **WHY:** Treating 224 subplot-visit measurements ($8\text{ plots} \times 7\text{ sites} \times 4\text{ visits}$) or 35 site-visit measurements ($7\text{ sites} \times 5\text{ visits}$) as independent observations violates conditional independence, artificially deflating standard errors and $p$-values in ANOVA and GLMs. Because only one site (C) is unmanaged, any statistical test comparing C against G and M cannot separate the management effect from the idiosyncratic soil, microtopography, or historical land-use of that single 1-ha parcel.
* **WHAT:** Re-specify plot- and visit-level analyses as linear mixed-effects models (`lme4` in R) with `Site` and `Visit` as random intercepts, report site-level means and standard deviations ($N = 3$ for G, $N = 3$ for M, $N = 1$ for C), and explicitly acknowledge that comparisons involving the single control site C are descriptive ($N = 1$ true replicate).
* **HOW:** For vegetation height, total cover, and Shannon indices, fit `lmer(response ~ Management + (1 | Site) + (1 | Visit))` and evaluate fixed effects using Kenward-Roger or Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. For cumulative species richness, compute sample-based rarefaction curves (using `iNEXT` or `vegan::specaccum`) standardized to equal sampling effort (1 site / 8 plots) rather than summing 3 sites for G and M against 1 site for C.

#### 2. Eliminate Data Leakage and Overfitting in the Ordination and Machine Learning Analyses
* **WHY:** Random 80/20 or 75/25 splitting of 224 subplot-visits places plots from the same site into both training and test sets, while `max_depth = 30` with SMOTE oversampling memorizes noise and site identity. Furthermore, Methods line 82 states LDA used 3 predictors (`MVH`, `TVC`, `Shannon-Weaver index`), whereas Table 6 and Figure 3 actually include 5 predictors (`VG`, `TVC`, `DPl`, `DPo`, `DB`).
* **WHAT:** Replace the `max_depth = 30` decision tree and SMOTE pipeline with either a shallow, regularized classification tree (`max_depth <= 3`, `min_samples_leaf >= 10`) evaluated via leave-one-site-out cross-validation (LOSO-CV) on M vs. G sites, or a standard ecological Indicator Species Analysis (`indicspecies::multipatt` in R) with site-restricted permutation tests. Reconcile the LDA predictor list between Methods and Table 6.
* **HOW:** In `scikit-learn`, use `GroupKFold(n_splits=6)` grouped by `Site` (excluding the single C site from cross-validated accuracy claims, since a class with $N=1$ site cannot be simultaneously present in disconnected training and test site folds) and constrain `DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=3, min_samples_leaf=10, class_weight='balanced')` without SMOTE. Alternatively, run Dufrêne-Legendre Indicator Value (`IndVal`) analysis in R to identify indicator plant species for G, M, and C.

#### 3. Resolve Numerical Contradictions and Incomplete Tables
* **WHY:** Multiple numbers in the text conflict with one another or with the tables, undermining technical reliability.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Audit and correct each of the following discrepancies:
  1. **Pollinator species total:** Reconcile "31 insect pollinator species" (line 93) with "The recorded 32 pollinator species (20 wild bees and 12 hoverflies)" (line 121).
  2. **Bird species total:** Clarify the relationship between "61 bird species" (line 93) and "46 species of birds were possibly breeding on the surveyed sites, 31 of which were confirmed or probable breeders" (line 123)—specify whether the 15 additional species were migrating/overflying species that Methods line 71 stated were excluded.
  3. **Plot count arithmetic:** Explain how "eight quadratic plots ($1\times 1\text{ m}$) were designated at each site during the first field trip, resulting in 224 plots in total" (line 67). Since $8\text{ plots} \times 7\text{ sites} = 56\text{ plots}$, clarify that the 56 permanent plots were sampled across 4 of the 5 field visits ($56 \times 4 = 224$ plot-samples).
  4. **Table 2 column sums:** In Table 2 (Relative cover % of SBTs), the columns sum to $65.88\%$ (G), $73.04\%$ (M), and $72.87\%$ (C). Include the omitted SBT categories (e.g., competitors `C`, Ruderal competitors `RC`, Alien `A`) so that relative cover sums to $100\%$.
  5. **Table 3 & line 125:** Rewrite line 125 to remove the self-contradiction regarding plant Shannon diversity between M and G sites, and add standard deviations/errors to every cell in Table 3.
  6. **Table 5 caption truncation:** Line 179 ends mid-sentence (`Significance is expressed as`). Complete the footnote defining `*` and `***` and report the exact sample size ($N$) and residual degrees of freedom.

#### 4. Align Biological Claims with the Study System (Sheep/Goats vs. Cattle)
* **WHY:** The study sites were grazed by sheep and goats, not cattle, and pollinators and birds showed no statistically significant differences in Shannon diversity across management types.
* **WHAT:** Remove the unsupported conclusion about cattle substituting for *Bos primigenius* (line 236), and temper the Abstract and Discussion to state that management effects on alpha diversity were statistically significant only for vegetation structure (`MVH`, `TVC`) and plant diversity (between M and C), whereas pollinator and bird alpha diversity did not differ significantly across treatments.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract (line 8) and Conclusions (line 236) to accurately reflect sheep/goat grazing and mowing effects on plant structure and community turnover (Jaccard dissimilarity) rather than claiming uniform diversity gains across all three taxa or making claims about cattle grazing.
