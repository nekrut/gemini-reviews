### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This resting-state fMRI study investigates local spontaneous brain activity and seed-based functional connectivity (rsFC) of the default mode network (DMN) in 56 non-manic patients with bipolar disorder (BD; 30 euthymic with $\text{HAMD} \le 7$ and 26 depressed with $\text{HAMD} > 7$) compared against 71 age-, sex-, and education-matched healthy controls (HCs), after excluding 23 participants for excessive head motion. Using whole-brain voxel-wise comparisons in DPABI/SPM12 (controlling for age, sex, education, and mean framewise displacement [FD]), the authors identify decreased fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation (fALFF, 69 voxels, cluster $P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.004$) and degree centrality (DC, 187 voxels, cluster $P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.017$) in the left precuneus, alongside a sub-threshold reduction in regional homogeneity (ReHo, 106 voxels, cluster $P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.135$). Using the 47-voxel intersection of these three maps (and confirming with the 57-voxel fALFF $\cap$ DC overlap and the independent Human Brainnetome Atlas precuneus ROIs 153+154), they report reduced rsFC between the left precuneus and the medial prefrontal cortex (MPFC) and reduced negative rsFC (anticorrelation) with the left putamen/insula. Data are available from the authors upon request, though no public repository link or analysis code is provided.

The study is methodologically transparent in reporting the non-significant cluster-level FWE $p$-value for ReHo ($P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.135$) and commendable for including an independent atlas-based confirmation analysis (Brainnetome A31, regions 153+154) to address the circularity of defining a seed ROI from group differences on the same dataset. However, three substantive issues weaken the primary conclusions: (1) the putamen/insula finding—framed in the Abstract, Discussion, and Conclusions as reduced anticorrelation between the DMN and the salience network (SN) and a "trait-like feature for BD"—completely disappears when using the unbiased, independent Brainnetome precuneus seed (Table 3, where only the MPFC cluster survives) and is critically confounded by global signal regression (GSR), which mathematically forces zero-mean correlation distributions and induces artificial anticorrelations; (2) defining the primary 47-voxel seed from the between-group DC difference on the exact same subjects is circular for the subsequent rsFC test, because DC is defined as the sum of whole-brain voxel-wise Pearson correlations ($r > 0.2$), guaranteeing that a cluster selected for lower DC in BD will exhibit lower rsFC in BD; and (3) 51 of 56 BD patients were receiving psychotropic medications (mood stabilizers, antipsychotics, antidepressants), and BD-I vs. BD-II subtypes were not distinguished, confounding disease effects with pharmacological effects. These issues can be resolved by re-running the rsFC pipeline without GSR, relying on the independent Brainnetome atlas seed as the primary analysis, and testing medication load covariates.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Patients with BD exhibit decreased local spontaneous activity (fALFF) and whole-brain degree centrality (DC) in the left precuneus, and decreased intra-DMN connectivity between the precuneus and MPFC: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The whole-brain reductions in fALFF ($P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.004$) and DC ($P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.017$) survive stringent voxel-wise $P < 0.001$ and cluster-level FWE $P < 0.05$ correction while controlling for age, sex, education, and mean FD. Crucially, the reduced rsFC between the precuneus and MPFC replicates when using the independent, anatomically defined Brainnetome Atlas precuneus seed (Nos. 153+154; Peak $T = 4.50$, 126 voxels, $P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.014$, Table 3), demonstrating that the anterior-posterior DMN dysconnectivity is not merely an artifact of circular seed selection. However, support remains moderate rather than strong because 91% of patients ($51/56$) were medicated (28 on mood stabilizers, 27 on antipsychotics, 19 on antidepressants) and no medication-naive sensitivity analysis or chlorpromazine/lithium equivalent covariate adjustment was performed.

* **Patients with BD show convergent abnormalities across all three local indices (fALFF, ReHo, and DC) in the left precuneus: [Overstated].**  
  The Abstract and Conclusions claim convergent reductions across fALFF, ReHo, and DC, but Table 2 and the Results text (Line 131) explicitly show that ReHo failed to reach cluster-level FWE significance ($P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.135$, 106 voxels) and was only included in the 47-voxel overlap by relaxing the statistical threshold to cluster-level uncorrected $P < 0.001$. Furthermore, as the authors note in the Discussion (Line 205), fALFF and DC are strongly correlated across subjects ($r = 0.671, P < 0.001$), so they do not represent three independent lines of evidence.

* **BD patients exhibit reduced negative functional connectivity (anticorrelation) between the left precuneus (DMN) and the left putamen/insula (salience network), representing a trait-like feature of BD: [Fragile].**  
  This conclusion is fragile for three reasons. First, the primary 47-voxel and 57-voxel seeds were defined by significant between-group differences in DC on the exact same dataset; because DC is the sum of supra-threshold voxel-wise rsFC across the brain, using a DC-selected cluster as a seed for a subsequent two-sample $t$-test on voxel-wise rsFC in the same subjects is statistically circular ("double-dipping"). Second, when this circularity is removed in Confirmation Analysis (ii) using the independent Brainnetome Atlas precuneus seed (Table 3, bottom row), the putamen/insula cluster completely disappears (only MPFC remains significant), proving that the putamen/insula finding depends on the circularly selected 47/57-voxel seed. Third, the preprocessing pipeline regressed out the global mean signal (GSR; Section "fMRI data preprocessing", Line 66), which mathematically centers every subject's whole-brain correlation distribution around zero; when positive intra-DMN correlations (precuneus–precuneus and precuneus–MPFC) decrease in BD, GSR mathematically shifts the remaining correlations upward, creating or inflating apparent reductions in negative connectivity ("anticorrelation").

* **Local precuneus activity and precuneus–putamen dysconnectivity are independent of mood state and represent candidate diagnostic biomarkers for BD: [Overstated].**  
  Concluding that local precuneus activity is unaffected by clinical symptoms because Pearson correlations with HAMD were non-significant ($P > 0.017$) conflates absence of evidence in a heterogeneous, medicated cross-sectional sample ($N = 56$, median $\text{HAMD} = 5$) with evidence of state-independence. Moreover, in the subgroup comparison (euthymic $n = 30$ vs. depressed $n = 26$), the authors state (Line 195) that the depressed subgroup only showed consistent patterns "upon reducing the threshold," which reflects low statistical power rather than confirmed trait invariance, and no out-of-sample classification (sensitivity/specificity/AUC) was performed to support the "candidate biomarker for diagnosis" claim.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Make the Independent Brainnetome Atlas Seed the Primary rsFC Analysis to Eliminate Circularity
* **WHY:** Defining a 47-voxel or 57-voxel seed from a between-group $t$-test on Degree Centrality (DC) and then testing that exact seed for between-group differences in voxel-wise rsFC on the same 127 subjects is circular, because DC is a direct mathematical function of the voxel-wise rsFC matrix. This circularity explains why the local precuneus ($T = 5.29$) and putamen/insula ($T = 4.92$) clusters appear with the data-driven seed but vanish when using the independent Brainnetome A31 seed (Table 3).
* **WHAT:** Promote the Brainnetome A31 (regions 153+154, or left A31 region 153 alone to match the left-lateralized fALFF/DC finding) rsFC analysis to the primary text and temper claims regarding the putamen/insula cluster.
* **HOW:** Re-run the Brainnetome seed analysis using Left A31 (region 153) alone (since regions 153+154 combined bilateral precuneus, which may have diluted left-lateralized effects) and report both Left A31 and Bilateral A31 in Table 3. Explicitly note in the Abstract and Discussion that while precuneus–MPFC hypoconnectivity replicates with an independent anatomical atlas seed, the precuneus–putamen/insula finding is restricted to the functionally defined sub-cluster.

#### 2. Repeat the rsFC Analysis Without Global Signal Regression (GSR)
* **WHY:** Regressing out the global mean signal forces the sum of all voxel-wise correlations to be zero or negative, which is well known in resting-state fMRI to introduce artifactual negative correlations ("anticorrelations") and to couple reductions in positive network connectivity with apparent shifts in negative connectivity.
* **WHAT:** Perform a sensitivity analysis of the seed-based rsFC maps preprocessed without GSR (using CompCor or white-matter/CSF regression only).
* **HOW:** In DPABI v3.1, re-run the nuisance regression step excluding the global mean signal regressor while retaining the Friston-24 motion parameters, white matter, and CSF signals. Extract the mean rsFC values in the MPFC and left putamen/insula clusters for both BD and HC groups to verify whether the putamen/insula correlation is genuinely negative in HCs and significantly altered in BD without GSR.

#### 3. Control for Medication Load and Report BD Subtypes
* **WHY:** With $51/56$ BD patients taking mood stabilizers ($n=28$), antipsychotics ($n=27$), or antidepressants ($n=19$)—particularly given that antipsychotics directly target striatal $D_2$ receptors in the putamen—striatal/insular connectivity differences between BD and HC may be driven by antipsychotic or mood-stabilizer exposure.
* **WHAT:** Compute a composite medication load index (or binary indicators for antipsychotic, mood stabilizer, and antidepressant use) and test whether fALFF, DC, and rsFC differ as a function of medication.
* **HOW:** Using the clinical records in Table 1, include binary covariates for antipsychotics, mood stabilizers, and antidepressants (or Sackeim composite medication load) in a multiple regression predicting extracted left precuneus fALFF, DC, precuneus–MPFC rsFC, and precuneus–putamen rsFC within the BD group ($n = 56$). Report the partial $r$ and $p$-values in the Results.

#### 4. Directly Compare Euthymic (EP) and Depressed (DP) Subgroups
* **WHY:** Comparing EP ($n = 30$) vs. HC ($n = 71$) and DP ($n = 26$) vs. HC ($n = 71$) separately and observing that one passes threshold while the other requires "reducing the threshold" (Line 195) does not test whether EP and DP differ from each other (the interaction fallacy).
* **WHAT:** Conduct a direct two-sample $t$-test (or ANCOVA) comparing EP ($n = 30$) directly against DP ($n = 26$) on the extracted precuneus fALFF, DC, and rsFC values.
* **HOW:** Extract the mean Z-values from the precuneus (fALFF, DC) and the MPFC and putamen/insula rsFC clusters for all 56 BD patients, and report the direct EP vs. DP comparison ($t$-statistic, $p$-value, and Cohen's $d$ with 95% confidence intervals) alongside a Bayesian $t$-test ($\text{BF}_{01}$) to quantify evidence for state-invariance.

#### 5. Technical and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Minor reporting omissions and typographical errors in the text and tables should be corrected.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Abstract & Results (Line 131 vs. Line 21):** Explicitly state in the Abstract that the precuneus reduction was statistically significant after FWE correction for fALFF and DC, whereas ReHo showed a trend-level uncorrected overlap ($P_{\text{FWE}} = 0.135$).
  * **Table 1 (Line 117):** Since duration of illness ($45.78 \pm 68.49$ months) has a standard deviation larger than its mean (indicating strong positive skew), report its median and interquartile range `(median (Q1, Q3))` consistent with Age, HAMD, and Mean FD.
  * **Abbreviations (Line 252):** Fix the typo `"ractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation"` to `"fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation"`.
