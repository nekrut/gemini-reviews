### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This preclinical study investigates whether maternal and neonatal gut microbiome dysbiosis mediates thermal and mechanical pain hypersensitivity in 3-week-old male C57BL/6 mouse offspring exposed prenatally and postnatally to methadone (`MET`, dams rendered dependent on escalating hydromorphone for 14 days pre-gestation and transitioned to methadone $10\text{ mg/kg}$ s.c. b.i.d. throughout pregnancy and lactation until P21) compared to saline controls (`CTRL`). Using 16S rRNA V4 amplicon sequencing (`DADA2`, `SILVA v138`, `MicrobiomeAnalyst`, `BugBase`, `PICRUSt`), behavioral nociceptive assays (tail-flick thermal latency and manual von Frey mechanical withdrawal threshold), 26-plex serum cytokine profiling (`Luminex`), `FITC-dextran` gut permeability, blinded colon histopathology, whole-brain bulk RNA-seq (`DESeq2`, `Ingenuity Pathway Analysis`), and a midbrain 84-gene neuropathic/inflammatory pain qPCR array (`RT2 Profiler`), the authors show that `MET` offspring exhibit thermal and mechanical hyperalgesia (with thermal hyperalgesia persisting to 12 weeks), elevated serum `IL-17a`, mild colonic mucosal inflammation, brain transcriptomic upregulation of neuropathic pain and neuroinflammation pathways, and fecal depletion of *Lactobacillus*, *Bifidobacterium*, and *Lachnospiraceae* alongside expansion of *Akkermansia*, *Bacteroides*, *Clostridium sensu stricto 1*, and *Lachnoclostridium*. Crucially, the authors perform three orthogonal microbiome interventions: (1) fecal microbiota transplantation (`FMT`) from 3-week-old `MET` vs. `CTRL` pups into antibiotic-pretreated naive 3-week-old male mice, which transfers thermal hyperalgesia ($-16.26\%$ vs. $+5.39\%$ tail-flick latency); (2) maternal `VSL#3` probiotic gavage (`E19` to `P21`), which restores neonatal *Lactobacillus*/*Bifidobacterium*, normalizes serum `IL-17a` and colon histology, reverses whole-brain and midbrain (`Oprm1`, `Htr2a`) transcriptional changes, and rescues 3-week thermal and mechanical pain thresholds; and (3) P3 cross-fostering between `MET` and `CTRL` dams (`FCM` vs. `FMM`), which rescues thermal pain hypersensitivity in `MET` pups nursed by `CTRL` dams (`MET FCM`). Metadata are deposited in BioStudies (`S-BSST1226`).

The experimental design is impressive in its biological breadth and causal triangulation: demonstrating sufficiency via `FMT` into drug-naive recipients and necessity/rescue via both maternal `VSL#3` supplementation and postnatal cross-fostering provides strong, convergent support for a microbiome-mediated component of opioid-induced developmental hyperalgesia. The authors also follow good behavioral practices (blinded scoring of withdrawal, pup retrieval, and colon histology, and drawing pups from $\ge 3$ litters per group). However, several statistical and technical issues should be addressed to solidify the work: (1) statistical tests (`t`-tests, two-way ANOVAs, `PERMANOVA`, `DESeq2`) treat individual pups ($n = 8\text{–}15$) as the unit of replication without modeling litter/dam random effects, even though methadone and `VSL#3` are administered to the dam; (2) the midbrain `RT2 Profiler` PCR array normalizes target expression against 5 automatically selected genes (`Actb`, `P2rx7`, `Kcnq2`, `Comt`, `Ednra`), four of which are canonical nociceptive genes on the pain panel rather than housekeeping genes; (3) the whole-brain RNA-seq DEG counts for the exact same `MET vs. CTRL` ($n=5$ vs. $n=5$) comparison conflict between Figure 7b (`3,516` DEGs) and Figure 8b (`986` DEGs); and (4) raw sequencing reads (`FASTQ` files for 16S rRNA and whole-brain RNA-seq) lack NCBI SRA / ENA accession numbers.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Prenatal and lactational methadone exposure induces thermal and mechanical hyperalgesia and alters maternal and neonatal gut microbiome beta diversity (depleting *Lactobacillus*/*Bifidobacterium* and enriching *Akkermansia*/*Bacteroides*/*Clostridium sensu stricto 1*): [Well Supported].**  
  Both behavioral nociceptive assays (tail-flick and von Frey, Fig. 3a–b, Fig. 5g–h, Fig. 9j) and Bray–Curtis `PERMANOVA` analyses across dams and 3-week-old male offspring (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2b, $p \le 0.002$) consistently replicate across independent cohorts of animals. While litter-level clustering should be modeled explicitly to prevent deflated standard errors, the authors mitigated single-litter domination by sampling across at least 3–5 independent litters per group.

* **Gut microbiome dysbiosis causally contributes to thermal and mechanical pain hypersensitivity in `MET` offspring, as demonstrated by `FMT` transfer into naive recipients, maternal `VSL#3` probiotic rescue, and postnatal cross-fostering (`MET FCM`): [Well Supported].**  
  The convergence of three independent interventions—particularly the `FMT` experiment showing that cell-free/filtered fecal suspensions from `MET` offspring reduce tail-flick latency in antibiotic-pretreated drug-naive recipients (Fig. 3d, eliminating direct pharmacological carryover of methadone), the maternal `VSL#3` rescue of both tail-flick and von Frey thresholds (Fig. 5g–h), and the cross-fostering rescue in `MET FCM` vs. `MET FMM` (Fig. 9j)—provides strong causal evidence. Notably, the finding that `CTRL` pups fostered onto methadone dams (`CTRL FMM`) did *not* develop thermal hyperalgesia (Fig. 9j) nuances this conclusion, indicating that postnatal exposure to a methadone dam (and breastmilk methadone/microbiota) from P3 onward is insufficient on its own without *in utero* (or P0–P2) programming.

* **Prenatal methadone exposure elevates systemic `IL-17a` and colonic mucosal inflammation, which are normalized by maternal `VSL#3` probiotic supplementation: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Out of 26 serum cytokines/chemokines assayed at P21, `IL-17a` is elevated in `MET` offspring and reduced to control levels in `MET+PRO` (Fig. 6a), accompanied by parallel improvements in blinded colonic H&E histological scores (Fig. 6c–d), while `FITC-dextran` barrier permeability is equally high in both `CTRL` and `MET` 3-week-old pups relative to adults (Fig. 6b). However, because 26 cytokines were tested simultaneously, the text should clarify whether the `IL-17a` two-way ANOVA $p$-value survives FDR correction across the 26-plex panel.

* **Whole-brain RNA-seq demonstrates that maternal `VSL#3` supplementation reverses `MET`-induced transcriptional dysregulation in neuropathic pain, opioid signaling, and synaptogenesis pathways: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The whole-brain RNA-seq ($n=5\text{–}6$ per group) shows clear separation on PCA (Fig. 7a, Fig. 8a) and biologically coherent `IPA` pathway reversals between `MET vs. CTRL` and `MET+PRO vs. MET` (Fig. 7d, Fig. 8d, Fig. S11D). However, there is an unexplained discrepancy in the number of differentially expressed genes reported for `MET vs. CTRL`: Figure 7b (and text line 37) reports `3,516` DEGs (`1,671` upregulated, `1,845` downregulated at $\text{FDR} < 0.05, |\log_2\text{FC}| \ge 0.5$), whereas the Figure 8b Venn diagram caption (line 53) reports `986` DEGs (`585` upregulated, `401` downregulated) for the same `MET vs. CTRL` comparison.

* **Midbrain `RT2 Profiler` PCR array confirms downregulation of `Bdnf`, `Edn1`, `Oprm1`, `Scn10a`, and `Slc6a2` in `MET` offspring and rescue of `Oprm1` and `Htr2a` by probiotics: [Fragile].**  
  In Methods ("RT2 profiler", line 71), the authors state that the 84-gene pain array was normalized using 5 genes automatically selected from the catalog array with minimal variation: `Actb, P2rx7, Kcnq2, Comt, Ednra`. While `Actb` is a genuine housekeeping reference gene, `P2rx7` (purinergic receptor P2X7), `Kcnq2` (potassium channel Kv7.2), `Comt` (catechol-O-methyltransferase), and `Ednra` (endothelin receptor type A) are primary neuropathic/inflammatory pain target genes on the `PAMM-162Z` panel itself. Normalizing pain genes against other pain genes on the panel can introduce normalization artifacts if those genes shift subtly across groups; normalization should be restricted to the dedicated housekeeping genes on the plate (`Actb`, `B2m`, `Gapdh`, `Gusb`, `Hsp90ab1`).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Account for Litter/Dam Clustering in Offspring Statistical Models
* **WHY:** In developmental toxicology and maternal-intervention studies where the dam (or co-housed pair of dams) is randomized to methadone/saline and `VSL#3`/vehicle, pups from the same litter share intrauterine environment, maternal care, and cage microbiome. Treating multiple pups from the same litter as independent biological replicates in `t`-tests and two-way ANOVAs can inflate Type I error rates.
* **WHAT:** Re-verify key behavioral (tail-flick, von Frey) and cytokine (`IL-17a`) comparisons using linear mixed-effects models with `Litter_ID` (or `Dam_ID`) as a random intercept, or by analyzing litter means, and explicitly state the exact number of independent litters represented in each group (`n = X pups from Y litters`) in all figure captions.
* **HOW:** In R (`lme4` / `lmerTest`), fit `lmer(Latency ~ Opioid * Probiotic + (1 | Dam_ID))` for Figure 5g–h, Figure 6a, and Figure 9j, and update the figure captions to report both pup count and litter count.

#### 2. Re-Normalize the Midbrain `RT2 Profiler` PCR Array Using Only Dedicated Housekeeping Genes
* **WHY:** Using `P2rx7`, `Kcnq2`, `Comt`, and `Ednra` (four pain-pathway target genes) alongside `Actb` as reference normalizers in the $\Delta\Delta C_t$ calculation (Methods line 71) risks distorting fold-change estimates for the remaining pain genes on the `PAMM-162Z` array.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the `RT2 Profiler` $\Delta\Delta C_t$ analysis normalizing strictly to the standard housekeeping gene panel (`Actb`, `B2m`, `Gapdh`, `Gusb`, `Hsp90ab1`) present on row H of the `PAMM-162Z` plate.
* **HOW:** In the Qiagen GeneGlobe portal (or directly in R from the raw $C_t$ spreadsheet), select only the housekeeping gene wells as reference genes, recompute fold regulation and Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted $p$-values for `Ngf`, `Bdnf`, `Edn1`, `Oprm1`, `Scn10a`, `Slc6a2`, and `Htr2a`, and update Supplementary Fig. S12.

#### 3. Reconcile the `MET vs. CTRL` DEG Count Discrepancy Between Figure 7b (`3,516` DEGs) and Figure 8b (`986` DEGs)
* **WHY:** Figure 7b reports `3,516` DEGs (`1,671` up, `1,845` down) between `MET` ($n=5$) and `CTRL` ($n=5$), whereas the Figure 8b caption reports `986` DEGs (`585` up, `401` down) for `MET vs CTRL` on the same samples, leaving it unclear whether a different $\log_2\text{FC}$ or unadjusted vs. FDR $p$-value threshold was applied in Figure 8b.
* **WHAT:** Clarify the exact statistical thresholds (`padj < 0.05` vs. raw `p < 0.05`, and $|\log_2\text{FC}| \ge 0.5$ vs. $\ge 1.5$) used in Figure 7b, Figure 8b, and the Methods (where line 106 states both `adjusted P < .05, and log2 fold change >= 0.5` and `|log2FoldChange| >= 0.5 & p-value < 0.05`).
* **HOW:** Standardize the DEG definition across Methods line 106, Figure 7, and Figure 8 (using `DESeq2` Benjamini–Hochberg `padj < 0.05` and $|\log_2\text{FC}| \ge 0.5$), and explain in the Figure 8b caption why its `MET vs CTRL` count differs from Figure 7b (or update the Venn diagram so both figures use the exact same threshold).

#### 4. Deposit Raw 16S rRNA and Whole-Brain RNA-Seq Reads in NCBI SRA / ENA and Fix the Truncated BioStudies URL
* **WHY:** While metadata are placed in BioStudies (`S-BSST1226`), the printed URL is truncated (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/studies/S-BSST122`, missing the final `6`), and no NCBI SRA, ENA, or GEO accession numbers are provided for the raw 16S rRNA MiSeq reads or the whole-brain HiSeq 4000 RNA-seq reads.
* **WHAT:** Deposit the demultiplexed 16S rRNA and RNA-seq `FASTQ` files (and raw gene/ASV count matrices) in NCBI SRA/GEO or ENA, and provide the exact accession numbers and corrected BioStudies link (`S-BSST1226`) in the Data Availability Statement.
* **HOW:** Upload the raw sequencing files to SRA/ENA or link them directly inside BioStudies `S-BSST1226`, and fix the URL in lines 54 and 71 of the Data Availability section.
