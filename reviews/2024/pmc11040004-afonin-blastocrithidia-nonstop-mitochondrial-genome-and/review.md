### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates how wholesale nuclear stop-codon reassignment in the trypanosomatid *Blastocrithidia nonstop* (where UAA and UAG encode glutamate and UGA encodes tryptophan, with UAA also serving as the sole termination codon) affects the mitochondrial (kinetoplast) genome, tRNA import, U-indel RNA editing, and respiratory chain complexes. Combining Illumina sequencing of total DNA, purified kDNA, poly(A)-enriched RNA, kRNA-enriched RNA, and mitochondrial small RNA libraries with `T-Aligner` transcript reconstruction, northern blotting of digitonin-fractionated organelles, native PAGE in-gel complex I activity assays, and AlphaFold2/Swiss-Model structural modeling of the RNA editing substrate-binding complex (RESC), the authors show that: (1) the *B. nonstop* maxicircle retains the standard protistan mitochondrial code (UGA = Trp; 8 UAA and 3 UAG stop codons) and encodes no tRNAs; (2) nuclear-encoded read-through $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA}}$ and $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{CUA}}$ are imported into the mitochondrion alongside canonical $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}$; (3) *B. nonstop* possesses the smallest known natural minicircle repertoire in trypanosomatids (41 classes encoding 42 gRNAs, plus 3 maxicircle gRNAs), which exclusively and non-redundantly direct productive editing of *A6*, *RPS12*, *COIII*, *COII*, and *CYb*; (4) complex I cryptogenes (*ND3*, *ND7*, *ND8*, *ND9*, *MURF2*, *G3*, *G4*) lack cognate gRNAs and fail to produce translatable ORFs or complex I enzymatic activity, yet accumulate abundant non-canonically edited reads; and (5) nuclear-encoded RNA editing proteins (65/71 *T. brucei* orthologs) remain conserved despite carrying frequent ~16 aa surface/loop insertions. All raw reads, assemblies (`OQ909994–OQ910035`), edited mRNAs (`OQ911728–OQ911732`), and custom scripts are publicly available.

This is a thorough, multi-disciplinary study that integrates comparative genomics, small-RNA/mRNA transcriptomics, organelle biochemistry, and structural modeling to resolve a central question in genetic code evolution. A few analytical and interpretive points should be strengthened: (1) the relaxed `findgrna` alignment showing that 79–86% of non-productive complex I editing events match non-cognate gRNAs lacks a shuffled-gRNA null control to quantify the background rate of chance G:U-rich matches; (2) the codon usage comparison across 11 mitochondrial mRNAs (Figure 3) relies on visual inspection without formal statistical testing; and (3) a few mechanistic interpretations in the Discussion (claiming that testing mitochondrial $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}$ charging is experimentally unrealistic, describing tolerated surface insertions as "not functionally neutral," and linking internal sense-codon divergence in *COI*/*ND1* to stop-codon read-through) should be refined.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* ***B. nonstop* retains a standard protistan mitochondrial genetic code and imports nuclear suppressor $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA}}$ and $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{CUA}}$ into the mitochondrion: [Well Supported].**  
  Maxicircle assembly and `T-Aligner` ORF reconstructions confirm that all 11 translatable mitochondrial mRNAs (6 unedited, 5 edited) use UGA as tryptophan and terminate at canonical UAA (8 genes) or UAG (3 genes) without internal UAA/UAG sense codons. Organellar fractionation northern blots across three cell densities ($2\times 10^8$, $3\times 10^8$, and $4\times 10^8$ cells) with RNase A protection and cytosolic (5.8S rRNA) / mitochondrial (9S rRNA) controls convincingly demonstrate that suppressor $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA/CUA}}$ are enriched in the mitochondrial fraction relative to 5.8S rRNA at levels matching or exceeding canonical $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUC/CUC}}$ (Figure 2).

* **Loss of productive editing of complex I cryptogenes and loss of complex I NADH dehydrogenase activity are driven by an ultra-reduced repertoire of 41 minicircle classes lacking complex I gRNAs: [Well Supported].**  
  De novo minicircle assembly from both total DNA and purified kDNA libraries recovers exactly 41 minicircle classes (42 minicircle gRNAs + 3 maxicircle gRNAs, verified by small-RNA-seq). Sequential cascade mapping (Figure 7, Supplementary Figure S4) accounts for 40 of the 42 minicircle gRNAs across *A6*, *RPS12*, and *COIII* with almost zero redundancy, leaving only 2 unassigned gRNAs—far too few to edit the seven complex I cryptogenes. This genomic and transcriptomic evidence is independently corroborated by the absence of most nuclear-encoded complex I subunits in the proteome and the absence of high-molecular-weight NADH dehydrogenase activity in native PAGE (Figure 5).

* **Non-productive U-indel editing of complex I cryptogene transcripts (79–86% of events) is explained by transient, non-cognate binding of gRNAs belonging to other cascades: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Comparing alignment length and G:U wobble counts between cognate and non-cognate gRNA:read pairs (Supplementary Figure S5) is informative. However, the `findgrna` search used highly permissive parameters (`--seed_length 16 --length 21 --gu 17 --mm 4 --anchor 2`; line 63). Because gRNAs are purine-rich and edited mRNAs are U-rich, allowing up to 17 G:U wobble pairs and 4 mismatches in a 21-nt window will match a substantial fraction of U-indel reads purely by chance. Without a permutation control (e.g., shuffled gRNAs), the fraction of complex I editing events genuinely templated by non-cognate gRNAs versus untemplated/background alignment matches remains unquantified.

* **Nuclear-encoded RNA editing complexes (RECC, RESC, PPsome) are structurally conserved and tolerate widespread ~16 aa insertions on solvent-exposed loops and flexible termini: [Well Supported].**  
  Reciprocal BLAST, phylogenetics (Supplementary Figure S6), and AlphaFold2/Swiss-Model superpositions onto the *T. brucei* RESC-A/RESC-B cryo-EM structures (`8FN6`) clearly show that the 29% average protein length expansion in *B. nonstop* maps almost exclusively to flexible regions, surface-exposed loops, or cavity-facing extensions rather than disrupting subunit interfaces (Figure 8). However, the statement in the Discussion (line 178) that *"the fact that so few RESC protein-RNA or protein-protein interactions are apparently disrupted by these insertions... suggests that these insertions are not functionally neutral"* inverts standard evolutionary terminology: the restriction of insertions to unconstrained surface loops is evidence that purifying selection purged disruptive core insertions, leaving behind insertions that are selectively neutral (or nearly neutral).

* **Presence of suppressor $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}$ in the mitochondrion may accelerate internal amino acid substitution rates in unedited maxicircle genes (*COI*, *ND1*, *ND4*, *ND5*): [Overstated].**  
  In the Discussion (line 186), the authors speculate that incomplete inactivation of imported UAA/UAG-decoding $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}$ could "accelerate aa sequence evolution" in *COI* and *ND1*. Because UAA and UAG do not occur internally within the coding sequences of *COI* or *ND1*, translational read-through of terminal UAA/UAG codons would only append C-terminal extensions to translated polypeptides; it provides no direct mutational or selective mechanism to accelerate sense-codon amino acid substitutions throughout the interior of *COI* and *ND1*.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Add a Shuffled-gRNA Null Control to Calibrate Non-Cognate gRNA Alignment Rates on Complex I Cryptogenes
* **WHY:** Under the relaxed `findgrna` settings (`--length 21 --gu 17 --mm 4 --anchor 2`, line 63), G:U wobble pairing allows G and A in any gRNA to pair with U insertions, creating a high baseline probability of spurious matches against U-rich edited reads.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the false-positive alignment rate by running the exact same `findgrna` search against synthetic control sets of dinucleotide-shuffled (or reverse, non-complemented) gRNAs.
* **HOW:** Generate 100 replicates of the 41 minicircle gRNAs shuffled within their guiding regions (preserving length and base/dinucleotide composition), run `findgrna --seed_score 20 --seed_length 16 --length 21 --score 25 --gu 17 --mm 4 --anchor 2` against the extracted complex I reads ($\ge 10$ edits), and compare the empirical 79–86% match rate and alignment score/length distributions (Supplementary Figure S5) against the shuffled null distribution.

#### 2. Statistically Test Mitochondrial Codon Usage Differences Across Species (Figure 3)
* **WHY:** Figure 3 highlights putatively over- and under-utilized codons in *B. nonstop* and *Leishmania* spp. using visual arrows and asterisks without testing whether these deviations exceed binomial/multinomial sampling noise across only 11 mitochondrial genes.
* **WHAT:** Compute Relative Synonymous Codon Usage (RSCU) and perform Fisher's exact tests (with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction) comparing codon counts for each amino acid family in *B. nonstop* against the pooled reference trypanosomatids.
* **HOW:** Tabulate the total codon counts across the 11 translatable maxicircle ORFs per species, run a $2 \times k$ Fisher's exact test per synonymous codon family (`B. nonstop` vs. other species), and restrict the arrows/asterisks in Figure 3 to codons with FDR-adjusted $q < 0.05$.

#### 3. Clarify the Feasibility of Testing Mitochondrial $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}$ Aminoacylation Status
* **WHY:** In the Discussion (line 176), the authors propose that imported suppressor $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA/CUA}}$ may remain uncharged by mitochondrial Glu-aaRS (or undergo disabling modification), but state that *"pursuing evidence for such a mechanism is not experimentally realistic, as it would require probing for unfeasible numbers of potential known individual nucleotide modifications."* While mapping every unknown RNA modification by mass spectrometry is indeed labor-intensive, testing whether mitochondrial $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA/CUA}}$ is aminoacylated in vivo does not require identifying modifications—it can be tested directly by acid-urea PAGE northern blotting (comparing deacylated vs. untreated mitochondrial RNA using the exact oligonucleotide probes already in hand from Figure 2).
* **WHAT:** Revise line 176 to distinguish between exhaustive RNA modification mapping and direct aminoacylation assays (acid-urea northern blotting).
* **HOW:** Modify the concluding sentences of paragraph 1 in the Discussion (line 176) to note that while de novo identification of inhibitory nucleotide modifications is challenging, directly testing whether mitochondrial $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA/CUA}}$ is charged in organello via acid-urea PAGE northern blotting is a feasible next step.

#### 4. Refine Evolutionary Terminology Around RESC Insertions and *COI*/*ND1* Divergence in the Discussion
* **WHY:** (1) Calling surface-restricted insertions "not functionally neutral" (line 178) confuses purifying selection *against* core insertions with positive/functional selection *for* surface insertions. (2) Speculating that UAA/UAG suppressor tRNAs accelerate internal sense-codon divergence in *COI* and *ND1* (line 186) lacks a coherent molecular mechanism.
* **WHAT:** Clarify both passages in the Discussion.
* **HOW:** (1) On line 178, replace *"suggests that these insertions are not functionally neutral"* with *"indicates that the spatial placement of these insertions is constrained by purifying selection, restricting tolerated insertions to solvent-exposed surfaces and flexible loops where they are functionally neutral."* (2) On line 186, attribute the elevated sequence divergence of maxicircle genes (*COI*, *ND1*, *ND2*, *ND4*, *ND5*) to relaxed purifying selection on respiratory chain complexes (following the loss of complex I) or elevated mitochondrial mutation rate rather than stop-codon read-through.
