**Manuscript Title:** Mendelian randomization based on genome-wide association studies and expression quantitative trait loci, predicting gene targets for the complexity of osteoarthritis as well as the clinical prognosis of the condition

**Journal / Preprint:** *Front Med (Lausanne)* (2024; `PMC11238174` / `PMID:38994346` / `10.3389/fmed.2024.1409439`)

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study combines two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) of blood expression quantitative trait loci (`eQTLGen`, $N = 31,684$) against an osteoarthritis (OA) GWAS (`ebi-a-GCST007090`, 24,955 cases and 378,169 controls) with microarray differential expression (`limma`), weighted gene co-expression network analysis (`WGCNA`), `CIBERSORT` immune deconvolution, and supervised machine learning (`Random Forest`, `LASSO`, `SVM-RFE`) across six small human synovial tissue GEO datasets (3 training datasets: $n=57$ [27 healthy controls, 30 OA]; 3 validation datasets: $n=43$ [21 healthy controls, 22 OA]). Intersecting 318 nominally significant MR genes ($p < 0.05$), 2,258 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), and 8 WGCNA modules yields 22 "core genes," which are narrowed by the three machine learning algorithms to two genes, `MAT2A` and `RBM6`. The authors construct a two-gene diagnostic nomogram and perform western blotting and ELISA on primary synoviocytes isolated from $n=2$ amputation controls and $n=4$ surgical OA patients (plus `MAT2A` knockdown in control synoviocytes), reporting reduced `MAT2A`, `RBM6`, and S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) levels alongside elevated `TGF-β1`, `SMAD3`, `SMAD4`, `α-SMA`, and `COL1A1`. Public database URLs are listed, but no analysis scripts, trained model weights, or uncropped western blot images are provided.

Despite combining computational screening with wet-lab assays on primary human synoviocytes, the manuscript suffers from severe methodological flaws across both its dry-lab and wet-lab components. First, the MR screen tests 5,430 genes at an uncorrected threshold of $p < 0.05$; the two highlighted genes (`RBM6` at $p = 0.046$ and `MAT2A` at $p = 0.028$) fail any multiple-testing correction and rely on blood eQTLs without colocalization analysis against synovial tissue. Second, the machine learning and nomogram pipeline suffers from multi-stage data leakage—selecting DEGs and WGCNA modules, running `RF`/`LASSO`/`SVM-RFE`, and evaluating ROC/nomogram calibration on the exact same $n=57$ training samples—while mischaracterizing cross-sectional end-stage synovial biopsy classification as a "clinical prognosis model" for "early OA diagnosis, functional recovery evaluation." Third, the Discussion reports immune-cell correlation directions that contradict the paper's own differential expression and `CIBERSORT` results. Fourth, the wet-lab experiments ($n=2$ vs. $n=4$ patients, conflicting `siRNA` vs. `ShMAT2A` nomenclature, and absence of any SAM rescue or TGF-$\beta$1 inhibition experiment) cannot support the causal mechanistic claim that `MAT2A` blocks TGF-$\beta$1/Smad3/4-driven fibrosis via SAM synthesis.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Mendelian randomization establishes a significant causal relationship between `RBM6` and `MAT2A` gene expression and osteoarthritis risk: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The authors test 5,430 genes (26,152 cis-eQTL SNPs from blood in `eQTLGen`) against OA and filter solely on unadjusted IVW $p < 0.05$ (`RBM6`: $\text{OR} = 0.967$, 95% CI $0.935\text{–}0.999$, $p = 0.046$; `MAT2A`: $\text{OR} = 0.912$, 95% CI $0.840\text{–}0.990$, $p = 0.028$). Under a standard Bonferroni threshold for 5,430 tested genes ($\alpha = 0.05 / 5,430 = 9.2 \times 10^{-6}$) or Benjamini–Hochberg FDR $< 0.05$, neither gene is statistically significant (at $\alpha = 0.05$, ~271 false-positive genes are expected by chance alone, close to the 318 genes found). Moreover, no genetic colocalization (`coloc` or `HEIDI`) was performed to verify that the blood cis-eQTL and OA GWAS signals share a causal variant rather than reflecting linkage disequilibrium confounding, and section 2.1 claims a "bidirectional MR analysis" was performed even though no reverse MR (OA $\to$ gene expression) is ever reported.

* **Machine learning (`RF`, `LASSO`, `SVM-RFE`) and a two-gene nomogram based on `MAT2A` and `RBM6` provide a robust clinical diagnostic and prognostic model facilitating early OA diagnosis and functional recovery evaluation: [Overstated].**  
  First, the feature selection pipeline leaks information across stages: DEGs ($p < 0.05$, $|\log_2 \text{FC}| > 0.585$), WGCNA trait-correlated modules, `RF`, `LASSO`, `SVM-RFE`, and the training ROC/nomogram/calibration/DCA curves are all fit and evaluated on the same 57 merged microarray samples (`GSE55235`, `GSE55457`, `GSE82107`). Second, the input tissue is invasive surgical synovial biopsy tissue from established/end-stage OA versus healthy controls, not a non-invasive blood test or an early subclinical cohort, and the datasets contain zero longitudinal follow-up or functional recovery outcomes—making claims of "clinical prognosis," "early OA diagnosis," and "functional recovery evaluation" unsupported by the study design.

* **`RBM6` and `MAT2A` expression levels correlate positively with M0 macrophages and activated mast cells and negatively with resting mast cells and activated NK cells: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 3.9 (Fig. 9F–G), `RBM6` and `MAT2A` are reported as *significantly downregulated* in OA compared to HC ($p = 3.6 \times 10^{-5}$ and $1.2 \times 10^{-5}$). In Section 3.6 (Fig. 6B), M0 macrophages are *significantly elevated* in OA ($p = 0.006$), while activated NK cells, resting mast cells, and activated mast cells are *significantly elevated* in HC ($p = 0.001\text{–}0.005$). Yet in the Discussion (line 252), the authors state that both `RBM6` and `MAT2A` are *positively* correlated with M0 macrophages ($R = 0.40$ and $R = 0.47$) and activated mast cells ($R = 0.48$ and $R = 0.58$) and *negatively* correlated with resting mast cells ($R = 0.66$ and $R = 0.55$, written without minus signs). Two genes that are strongly downregulated in OA cannot simultaneously have positive rank correlations across all samples with a cell type (M0 macrophages) that is strongly upregulated in OA unless group labels were swapped or correlations were misread.

* **`MAT2A` and `RBM6` protein levels are reduced in OA synoviocytes, and `MAT2A` knockdown reduces intracellular SAM and increases `TGF-β1`, `SMAD3`, `SMAD4`, `α-SMA`, and `COL1A1`: [Fragile].**  
  While the western blot and ELISA trends (Fig. 10) are directionally consistent with the microarray data, the sample size is only $n=2$ healthy amputation donors vs. $n=4$ OA donors, no biological replicate counts or exact statistical tests (e.g., ANOVA / $t$-test $p$-values and degrees of freedom) are specified in Methods 2.11–2.12 or Figure 10, and the Methods describe transient `siRNA` transfection (Section 2.10) whereas the Results and Figure 10 label the group `ShMAT2A` (short hairpin RNA).

* **`MAT2A` prevents synovial fibrosis by regulating SAM synthesis to inhibit the `TGF-β1`-induced `Smad3/4` signaling pathway: [Overstated].**  
  Observing that `MAT2A` knockdown lowers SAM and increases total `TGF-β1`, `SMAD3`, `SMAD4`, `α-SMA`, and `COL1A1` does not prove that SAM depletion is the causal mediator or that `Smad3/4` activation is required. Proving this linear pathway (`MAT2A` $\to$ `SAM` $\dashv$ `TGF-β1/Smad3/4` $\to$ fibrosis) requires a rescue experiment (supplementing exogenous SAM to `siMAT2A` synoviocytes to test whether it restores normal `TGF-β1`/`p-SMAD3`/`α-SMA`/`COL1A1` levels) and measuring phosphorylated active `p-SMAD3` rather than only total `SMAD3`/`SMAD4` protein.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Apply Multiple-Testing Correction and Bayesian Colocalization to the eQTL Mendelian Randomization Screen
* **WHY:** Screening 5,430 genes at an uncorrected IVW $p < 0.05$ yields ~270 false positives by chance under the null hypothesis, and blood cis-eQTLs can be associated with OA merely through linkage disequilibrium with nearby causal variants rather than shared genetic regulation in synovium.
* **WHAT:** Apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across all 5,430 tested genes, perform `coloc` Bayesian colocalization between `eQTLGen` (and `GTEx` musculoskeletal/fibroblast tissues) and the OA GWAS (`ebi-a-GCST007090`), and clarify that `RBM6` ($p = 0.046$) and `MAT2A` ($p = 0.028$) are only nominally associated prior to multiple-testing adjustment.
* **HOW:** Using `p.adjust(p_ivw, method = "BH")` in R on Supplementary Table S5, report the exact FDR $q$-values for all 318 genes, run `coloc.abf()` using a $\pm 500\text{ kb}$ window around `MAT2A` and `RBM6` to report posterior probability $PP.H_4$, and remove the unperformed "bidirectional MR" claim from Section 2.1.

#### 2. Audit and Correct the `CIBERSORT` Immune Correlation Signs and Batch-Integration Pipeline
* **WHY:** In Section 4 (line 252), the authors report positive correlations with negative prose (`"negatively correlated with activated NK cells, R = 0.51... and resting mast cells, R = 0.66"`) and claim that `RBM6` and `MAT2A` (which are lower in OA) correlate positively with M0 macrophages (which are higher in OA). Additionally, `GSE1919` (`GPL91`), `GSE55235`/`GSE55457`/`GSE12021` (`GPL96`), and `GSE82107`/`GSE206848` (`GPL570`) come from different microarray generations; applying `SVA`/`ComBat` across studies with unbalanced case/control ratios or running `CIBERSORT` on ComBat-shifted log-expression values (rather than non-log linear expression) can distort deconvolution fractions.
* **WHAT:** Re-run `CIBERSORT` on non-log-transformed expression matrices within each dataset prior to batch merging, recompute Spearman correlations separately within OA and HC groups (to avoid Simpson's paradox across disease states), and fix the sign errors in the text.
* **HOW:** Inspect the correlation script for Figure 6C, ensure the exact signed $R$ values (positive vs. negative) match the heatmap color bar in Fig. 6C, and report within-group correlations in OA samples separately from HC samples.

#### 3. Perform Rescue Experiments (Exogenous SAM Supplementation and `p-SMAD3` Quantification) to Test the Proposed Pathway
* **WHY:** Knocking down `MAT2A` and measuring total `TGF-β1`, total `SMAD3`, and total `SMAD4` is purely correlative; Smad signaling activation is canonically mediated by C-terminal phosphorylation (`p-SMAD3` Ser423/425) and nuclear translocation, and without a rescue control, off-target or SAM-independent effects of `MAT2A` depletion cannot be ruled out.
* **WHAT:** Add an exogenous SAM supplementation rescue group (`siMAT2A + SAM`) and blot for phosphorylated `p-SMAD3` alongside total `SMAD3`.
* **HOW:** Treat `siMAT2A`-transfected human synovial fibroblasts with exogenous SAM (e.g., $0.5\text{–}2.0\text{ mM}$ for 48–72 h) and quantify whether SAM supplementation reverses the induction of `TGF-β1`, `p-SMAD3 / SMAD3` ratio, `α-SMA`, and `COL1A1` across at least $n \ge 4$ independent biological donors per group, reporting exact statistical test methods and two-sided $p$-values.

#### 4. Reframe "Clinical Prognosis" and "Early Diagnosis" Claims to Match Cross-Sectional Synovial Biopsy Data
* **WHY:** All six GEO datasets (`GSE55235`, `GSE55457`, `GSE82107`, `GSE1919`, `GSE12021`, `GSE206848`) are cross-sectional case-control comparisons of synovial membrane biopsies (healthy vs. established OA), with zero longitudinal survival, disease progression, or post-operative functional recovery data. Calling a logistic regression nomogram fit on cross-sectional case-control status a "clinical prognosis model" that "facilitates early OA diagnosis, functional recovery evaluation" is clinically and statistically inaccurate.
* **WHAT:** Replace all references to "prognosis model," "early OA diagnosis," and "functional recovery evaluation" in the Abstract, Introduction, Results (Section 2.8, 3.9), Discussion, and Conclusion with accurate descriptions ("cross-sectional synovial tissue classification model").
* **HOW:** Edit the Abstract, Section 2.8, Section 3.9, and Section 5 to state that `MAT2A` and `RBM6` distinguish OA from healthy synovial tissue in cross-sectional transcriptomic datasets, and note that prospective blood or synovial-fluid validation in early-stage OA cohorts is required before any diagnostic or prognostic utility can be claimed.

#### 5. Reconcile Technical Inconsistencies (`siRNA` vs. `ShMAT2A`) and Deposit Reproducible Code and Raw Blots
* **WHY:** Section 2.10 states that transient transfection was performed with a synthetic double-stranded `siRNA` (`5'-ACACAUUGGAUAUGAUGAUTT-3'`) using Lipofectamine 2000, whereas Sections 3.10–3.11, Section 4, and Figure 10 label the group `ShMAT2A` (implying lentiviral/plasmid short hairpin RNA). Furthermore, no statistical analysis section describes what tests (`t`-test, one-way ANOVA with post-hoc Tukey, or Mann–Whitney) were used in Figure 10, and no R scripts are deposited.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Standardize the nomenclature between Section 2.10 (`siRNA`) and Figure 10 (`ShMAT2A` $\to$ `siMAT2A`), and specify whether a scrambled negative-control `siRNA` (`si-NC`) was used for the control group (currently `ShMAT2A` is compared against untreated `HC` rather than `HC + si-NC`, leaving lipofection toxicity uncontrolled).
  2. Add a "Statistical Analysis" subsection in Materials and Methods specifying the exact statistical test, sample sizes ($n$ biological donors vs. technical replicates), and multiple-comparison adjustment used for the ELISA and Western blot bar graphs in Fig. 10A–D.
  3. Deposit the complete R scripts (MR filtering, `limma`/`SVA` batch correction, `WGCNA`, `RF`/`LASSO`/`SVM-RFE`, and `rms` nomogram) in a public repository and include full uncropped western blot membranes in the Supplementary Material.
