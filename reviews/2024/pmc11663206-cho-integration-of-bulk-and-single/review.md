### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study integrates single-cell RNA-seq data from 7 bladder cancer (BC) patients (`GSE135337`, restricted to 1,517 non-epithelial tumor microenvironment [TME] cells out of 36,300 total cells) with bulk RNA-seq and overall survival (OS) data from 89 patients with advanced BC treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors (`GSE176307`). Using the `Scissor` algorithm (Cox regression family, $\alpha = 0.1$), the authors identify 115 `Scissor-` cells associated with favorable OS (predominantly M1/M2 macrophages and plasma B cells) and 173 `Scissor+` cells associated with poor OS (monocytes and fibroblasts). Differential expression testing between `Scissor-` and background cells yields 15 upregulated genes ($\text{FDR} < 0.05$, $\log_2 \text{FC} > 1.5$), from which 10 genes (`SSR4`, `RGS1`, `HLA-DRB5`, `APOE`, `C1QB`, `C1QA`, `APOC1`, `JCHAIN`, `C1QC`, `DERL3`) are averaged as $z$-scores to define a bladder cancer gene signature (`BC-GS`). Across `GSE176307` ($n=89$) and two validation cohorts (`IMvigor210` bladder-tissue subset, $n=195$; `UC-GENOME`, $n=101$), a high `BC-GS` score (median split) is associated with prolonged OS ($\text{HR} = 0.49, 0.70$, and $0.36$, respectively) and further stratifies survival within the high tumor mutational burden (`TMB`) subgroup. Custom analysis scripts are publicly available on GitHub.

The integration of scRNA-seq and ICI-treated bulk cohorts via `Scissor` and the validation across two external urothelial cancer immunotherapy datasets (`IMvigor210` and `UC-GENOME`) provide a useful empirical finding: macrophage/complement (`C1QA/B/C`, `APOE`, `APOC1`) and plasma cell (`JCHAIN`, `SSR4`, `DERL3`) abundance in the TME predicts favorable survival on PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, especially when combined with high `TMB`. However, several methodological and conceptual issues weaken the manuscript: (1) cross-cohort leakage during feature selection (selecting 10 of 15 genes based on being "stably overexpressed across the datasets" and presenting the `GSE176307` discovery dataset as an "independent dataset" in survival plots); (2) an internal contradiction in the differential expression pipeline (claiming to use pseudobulk methods while running `Seurat::FindMarkers` at the single-cell level, and applying `limma::voom` to 10 pre-normalized `TPM` values); (3) a mismatch between the actual biological identity of the 10 `BC-GS` genes (complement/lipoprotein macrophage and ER/plasma-cell genes) and the Abstract claim that "Genes in the BC-GS were predominantly involved in CD8+ T cell activation, antigen presentation, and immune checkpoint pathways"; and (4) lack of multivariable Cox regression adjusting for standard clinical covariates. All of these issues can be resolved through computational re-analysis and textual revision.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **High `BC-GS` expression is associated with longer overall survival in patients with advanced bladder cancer receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors across `IMvigor210` ($n=195$) and `UC-GENOME` ($n=101$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The direction of the association replicates across `IMvigor210` ($\text{HR} = 0.70$, 95% CI $0.49\text{–}0.99$, $p = 0.043$) and `UC-GENOME` ($\text{HR} = 0.36$, 95% CI $0.20\text{–}0.67$, $p = 0.00081$). However, two caveats temper this support: first, `GSE176307` ($\text{HR} = 0.49$, $p = 0.008$) is the discovery cohort used in `Scissor` to supervise the selection of `Scissor-` cells against OS, so its survival $p$-value is circular and cannot be counted as an "independent dataset" (as labeled in Results lines 103 and 109). Second, Methods line 63 states that out of 15 upregulated DEGs, *"10 stably overexpressed genes... across the datasets were carefully selected for further analysis,"* implying that expression patterns in the validation cohorts influenced the filtering from 15 to 10 genes.

* **Combining `BC-GS` with `TMB` improves prognostic stratification and resolves survival heterogeneity within high-`TMB` patients (`HH` vs. `LH`): [Well Supported].**  
  In all three cohorts (`GSE176307`, `IMvigor210`, and `UC-GENOME`), patients with both high `BC-GS` and high `TMB` (`HH`) experience significantly longer OS than patients with high `TMB` but low `BC-GS` (`LH`, log-rank $p = 0.001$, $p < 0.0001$, and $p = 0.0025$, Fig. 4c). Biologically, this aligns with the requirement for both neoantigen load (`TMB`) and functional antigen-presenting/effector immune infiltration (`BC-GS`) to achieve durable ICI responses.

* **The 10 genes comprising the `BC-GS` are "predominantly involved in CD8+ T cell activation, antigen presentation, and immune checkpoint pathways": [Overstated].**  
  The Abstract (line 8) and Introduction (line 24) state that the genes *in* the `BC-GS` are predominantly involved in $\text{CD8}^+$ T cell activation, antigen presentation, and immune checkpoint pathways. In reality, as shown in the authors' own Reactome enrichment (Fig. 2b) and Discussion (line 139), the 10 `BC-GS` genes are lipid-associated macrophage complement/apolipoprotein genes (`C1QA`, `C1QB`, `C1QC`, `APOE`, `APOC1`), MHC class II (`HLA-DRB5`), G-protein signaling (`RGS1`), and plasma B-cell secretory/ER genes (`JCHAIN`, `SSR4`, `DERL3`)—none of which are $\text{CD8}^+$ T cell markers or immune checkpoints. The association with $\text{CD8}^+$ T effector and checkpoint gene sets (Fig. 3a–b) is a *extrinsic correlation* across bulk tumors in `GSE176307`, not a property of the 10 `BC-GS` genes themselves.

* **Single-cell differential expression and `limma::voom` normalization were properly specified to derive the `BC-GS` score: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  There are two technical misspecifications in the Methods:
  1. Line 53 states: *"To identify the DEGs in Scissor- cells compared to that in other cells without Scissor+ cells, the 'FindMarkers' function in Seurat with the DESeq test argument was used [18]. To mitigate bias toward highly expressed genes, pseudobulk methods were used instead of single-cell differential expression approaches [19]."* `Seurat::FindMarkers(..., test.use = "DESeq2")` treats each of the 115 `Scissor-` cells and 1,229 background cells as independent replicates ($N = 1,344$); it is a single-cell test, *not* a patient-level pseudobulk aggregation (which requires summing raw counts by patient ID across the 7 donors in `GSE135337` before running `DESeq2`).
  2. Line 63 states: *"The TPM expression values of these 10 genes were subjected to transformation using the 'voom' function of the LIMMA R package (v3.54.2) and subsequently converted to z-scores."* The `limma::voom` transformation is designed for full-transcriptome raw integer read counts to estimate the count-size mean-variance trend; applying `voom` to a 10-gene matrix of already length-normalized `TPM` values is mathematically invalid.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Feature-Selection Leakage and Separate the Discovery Cohort (`GSE176307`) from Independent Validation (`IMvigor210`, `UC-GENOME`)
* **WHY:** Because `GSE176307` survival data were used by `Scissor` to identify the `Scissor-` cells, evaluating OS on `GSE176307` is an in-sample training check rather than independent validation. Furthermore, filtering the 15 `Scissor-` upregulated genes down to 10 genes based on behavior "across the datasets" leaks information from the validation cohorts.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate the full, unfiltered 15-gene `Scissor-` signature alongside the 10-gene `BC-GS` in `IMvigor210` and `UC-GENOME`, explicitly label `GSE176307` as the *Discovery/Training Cohort* and `IMvigor210`/`UC-GENOME` as the *Validation Cohorts* throughout the text and figures, and clarify the exact rule used to drop 5 of the 15 upregulated genes.
* **HOW:** Compute the 15-gene average $z$-score in `IMvigor210` ($n=195$) and `UC-GENOME` ($n=101$) and report its Cox hazard ratio, 95% CI, and log-rank $p$-value in the text or a supplementary table to demonstrate that the prognostic signal does not depend on post-hoc pruning from 15 to 10 genes.

#### 2. Replace the Misspecified 10-Gene `voom`-on-TPM Step with Standard $\log_2(\text{TPM} + 1)$ $Z$-Scores and Clarify the `DESeq2` Single-Cell vs. Pseudobulk Procedure
* **WHY:** Passing a 10-row matrix of `TPM` values into `limma::voom` violates `voom`'s assumptions (which require raw counts and whole-transcriptome library sizes) and introduces unnecessary distortion before $z$-scoring. Similarly, describing `Seurat::FindMarkers(test.use = "DESeq2")` as a "pseudobulk method" is factually incorrect unless counts were first summed per patient sample.
* **WHAT:** Compute the `BC-GS` score directly as the mean of gene-wise $z$-scores of $\log_2(\text{TPM} + 1)$ in `GSE176307` and `IMvigor210` (matching the $\log_2$-normalized $z$-scores already used in `UC-GENOME`), and accurately describe the DEG test performed in `Seurat`.
* **HOW:** In the GitHub script, replace `voom(tpm_10_genes)` with `scale(log2(t(tpm_10_genes) + 1))` and verify that the survival curves and hazard ratios in `GSE176307` and `IMvigor210` remain consistent. In Methods line 53, clarify whether `FindMarkers` was run on single cells or on donor-aggregated pseudobulk profiles.

#### 3. Test `BC-GS` and `TMB` as Continuous Predictors in Multivariable Cox Models Controlling for Clinical Confounders
* **WHY:** Dichotomizing `BC-GS` and `TMB` at cohort-specific medians discards statistical power and makes the cutoff dependent on cohort composition. Moreover, neither `BC-GS` nor `BC-GS + TMB` is tested in a multivariable Cox model adjusting for established clinical prognostic factors (e.g., ECOG performance status, liver/visceral metastasis, baseline hemoglobin, PD-L1 immune cell IHC score in `IMvigor210`).
* **WHAT:** Fit multivariable Cox proportional hazards models across `GSE176307`, `IMvigor210`, and `UC-GENOME` including continuous `BC-GS`, continuous $\log_{10}(\text{TMB})$, their interaction term (`BC-GS * TMB`), and available clinical covariates.
* **HOW:** Add a table reporting univariate and multivariable hazard ratios (per 1 SD increase in `BC-GS` and `TMB`) and $p$-values adjusting for age, sex, ECOG performance status, metastatic site, and (in `IMvigor210`) PD-L1 IC0/1/2+ status, as well as the full `IMvigor210` cohort ($n=348$, including upper-tract urothelial carcinoma) as a sensitivity analysis alongside the $n=195$ bladder-only subset.

#### 4. Correct the Biological Framing of the `BC-GS` Gene Composition in the Abstract and Introduction
* **WHY:** Stating that *"Genes in the BC-GS were predominantly involved in CD8+ T cell activation, antigen presentation, and immune checkpoint pathways"* misleads readers about what the 10 signature genes actually encode.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Abstract (line 8) and Introduction (line 24) to accurately state that the 10 `BC-GS` genes reflect complement-producing lipid-associated macrophages (`C1QA/B/C`, `APOE`, `APOC1`), MHC class II antigen presentation (`HLA-DRB5`), and plasma B cells (`JCHAIN`, `SSR4`, `DERL3`), and that high `BC-GS` *correlates with* elevated $\text{CD8}^+$ T effector, antigen presentation, and immune checkpoint pathway expression in bulk tumors.
* **HOW:** Replace *"Genes in the BC-GS were predominantly involved in CD8+ T cell activation, antigen presentation, and immune checkpoint pathways"* in the Abstract with *"Genes in the BC-GS primarily mark complement-expressing macrophages and plasma B cells, and tumors with high BC-GS scores exhibited enriched CD8+ T cell effector, antigen presentation, and immune checkpoint pathway expression."*
