### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study phenotypes eight seedling root system architecture (RSA) traits (TRL, TRSA, RAD, NTP, TPRL, TLRL, TTRL, and BLL) across 379 accessions spanning six *Brassica* species from the Triangle of U—*B. napus* (AACC, $n = 106$: 68 IPK + 25 Canadian canola + 13 CCD), *B. juncea* (AABB, $n = 64$), *B. rapa* (AA, $n = 60$), *B. nigra* (BB, $n = 66$), *B. oleracea* (CC, $n = 55$), and *B. carinata* (BBCC, $n = 28$)—grown for 14 days in a roll-paper semi-hydroponic system (four experiments $\times$ four replicates) and imaged with `WinRHIZO`. After excluding the 66 *B. nigra* (BB) accessions, the remaining 313 accessions are genotyped on the 19K *Brassica* SNP array (yielding 6,213 filtered SNPs: 5,103 on the A-genome and 1,110 on the C-genome) to estimate linkage disequilibrium (LD), `STRUCTURE` clustering ($K = 3$), and marker-trait associations (MTAs) via six `TASSEL 5.0` GLM/MLM models (PCA, Q, Q+D, Q+K, PCA+D, PCA+K) at a Bonferroni threshold of $0.05 / 6,213$. The authors report that *B. napus* and *B. oleracea* have the largest root systems while *B. nigra* has the smallest, and claim 79 significant SNP markers associated with six RSA traits across 18 chromosomes. No public repository accession numbers for the 19K SNP genotypes or raw root scans are provided in the manuscript text.

While the multi-species semi-hydroponic root phenotyping dataset across 379 accessions is a substantial empirical effort, the cross-species genomic and GWAS design is fundamentally flawed due to structural ploidy/subgenome missingness across the Triangle of U: (1) the authors pool five distinct species with non-overlapping genomic constitutions—*B. rapa* (AA, lacking the C-genome), *B. oleracea* (CC, lacking the A-genome), *B. juncea* (AABB, lacking the C-genome), *B. carinata* (BBCC, lacking the A-genome), and *B. napus* (AACC)—into a single joint `STRUCTURE` and `TASSEL` GWAS analysis across A- and C-genome markers, yet impose a $<5\%$ missingness filter across all 313 accessions (`missing data for > 5% of the accessions` excluded, line 48); biologically, the 83 A-genome-absent accessions (*B. oleracea* + *B. carinata*, 26.5% of the panel) have no A-genome chromosomes, and the 124 C-genome-absent accessions (*B. rapa* + *B. juncea*, 39.6% of the panel) have no C-genome chromosomes, meaning any marker present in $>95\%$ of all 313 samples is cross-hybridizing homeologously to the B, C, or A subgenomes; (2) pooling distinct species with large species-level mean differences in root size (Table 2) into a single GWAS conflates inter-specific taxonomic divergence with intra-specific allelic effects; and (3) numerous mathematical and statistical errors appear in the LD, root classification, and candidate-gene mapping sections. Re-analyzing the GWAS separately within each species/subgenome is essential.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Among the six *Brassica* species grown in a 14-day semi-hydroponic system, *B. napus* and *B. oleracea* develop the largest and most branched root systems, whereas *B. nigra* develops the smallest and *B. juncea* / *B. carinata* have the largest average root diameter: [Well Supported].**  
  The 4-experiment $\times$ 4-replicate semi-hydroponic `WinRHIZO` measurements across 379 accessions (Tables 1–2, Figures 2–4) clearly separate the larger, more branched root systems of *B. napus* and *B. oleracea* from the smaller root systems of *B. nigra* and the thicker average diameter (RAD $= 0.41\text{ cm}$) of the B-genome allotetraploids *B. juncea* and *B. carinata*. Note, however, that early 14-day seedling root vigor is strongly influenced by seed size/endosperm maternal provisioning, which was not measured or controlled as a covariate.

* **Joint `STRUCTURE` and `TASSEL` GWAS across the 313 multi-species accessions identifies 3 ancestral clusters and 79 credible SNP marker-trait associations across the A and C genomes: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the 313-accession panel combines five species with incompatible subgenomes: 60 *B. rapa* (AA) and 64 *B. juncea* (AABB) completely lack the C-genome ($124/313 = 39.6\%$), while 55 *B. oleracea* (CC) and 28 *B. carinata* (BBCC) completely lack the A-genome ($83/313 = 26.5\%$). Applying a global missing-data filter of $\le 5\%$ across all 313 accessions (Section "SNP genotyping", line 48) is impossible unless array probes on the A-genome are cross-hybridizing to C/B homeologs in *B. oleracea*/*B. carinata* (and vice versa), which introduces severe homeologous genotyping artifacts into `STRUCTURE`, the kinship matrix $K$, LD decay ($r^2$ extended to $9,100\text{ kb}$ on the C-genome), and the GWAS models. Second, although the Results text (line 224) states that `PCA + K` showed the best Q-Q calibration and was used to call MTAs, Table 4 pools hits from uncalibrated naive `PCA`, `Q`, and `Q+D` models (e.g., `Bn_A02_p5574727` detected only in `PCA`; `Bn_A08_p20968239` detected only in `Q`; lines 251, 268–274) to reach the total of 79 SNPs, contradicting the Abstract (line 8: `"Two general linear models were tested"`) and Results (line 224).

* **Candidate genes underlying the GWAS hits (such as *HDT2-like* on A02, *AHK2* on A03, and *Root hair specific 17* on A06) directly regulate *Brassica* root system architecture: [Fragile].**  
  In Table 4, the reported `Marker Position (Start / End)` coordinates do not match the physical positions encoded in the SNP marker names (e.g., `Bn_A03_p9765420` is at ~9.77 Mb on A03, but Table 4 lists Start `55863` and End `55931`; `Bn_A09_p30678275` is at ~30.68 Mb on A09, but Table 4 lists `28355–28373`; `Bn_A09_p1011107` is an A09 marker mapped to linkage group `C08` at `2194–2259`). These short intervals (`17 bp` to `300 bp`, e.g., `28467–28487 = 20 bp`) are actually the local `BLASTN` alignment coordinates within a scaffold or cDNA entry rather than chromosomal coordinates, and given an average LD decay of 440–1,400 kb on the A-genome and 2,500–9,100 kb on the C-genome (Table 3), a single array SNP cannot resolve causal genes to a single ORF without local haplotype/expression evidence.

* **Root size classifications across the six species predict field drought adaptation, phosphorus uptake efficiency, salinity tolerance, and resistance to soilborne pathogens (*Plasmodiophora brassicae*, *Sclerotinia*, *Leptosphaeria*): [Overstated].**  
  No drought, salinity, nutrient-deficiency, or pathogen-inoculation experiments were performed in this study; all 379 accessions were grown in nutrient-replete half-strength Hoagland solution on germination paper for 14 days. Extrapolating 14-day paper-roll primary/lateral root lengths to field salinity tolerance or clubroot/stem-rot disease escape is speculative.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Separate GWAS and LD Analyses by Subgenome Presence (*A-Genome Panel* vs. *C-Genome Panel* and Within-Species)
* **WHY:** Including *B. oleracea* (CC) and *B. carinata* (BBCC) when analyzing A-genome SNPs—and including *B. rapa* (AA) and *B. juncea* (AABB) when analyzing C-genome SNPs—treats inter-specific subgenome absence/homeologous cross-hybridization as segregating allelic variation. This explains why C-genome marker retention dropped to 1,110 SNPs (13.8%) with an inflated LD block size of 4,705 kb (up to 9,100 kb on C01) and why *B. oleracea* and *B. carinata* appeared as "admixtures" in the $K = 3$ `STRUCTURE` plot (Figure 5).
* **WHAT:** Split the association mapping and LD estimation into subgenome-specific panels: (a) an A-genome analysis restricted to the 230 A-genome-bearing accessions (*B. napus* AACC, *B. rapa* AA, *B. juncea* AABB), and (b) a C-genome analysis restricted to the 189 C-genome-bearing accessions (*B. napus* AACC, *B. oleracea* CC, *B. carinata* BBCC), including `Species` as an explicit fixed effect (plus a *B. napus*-only analysis, $n = 106$).
* **HOW:** In `TASSEL 5.0`, subset the genotype matrix for the 5,103 A-genome SNPs to the 230 AA/AABB/AACC accessions and the C-genome SNPs (re-filtered at $<5\%$ missingness within the 189 CC/BBCC/AACC accessions, which will recover thousands of true C-genome SNPs previously discarded because they were missing in AA/AABB species). Run `MLM (Species + PCA + K)` on rank-based inverse-normal transformed RSA traits (since all 8 traits failed the Anderson-Darling normality test at $p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$, line 104).

#### 2. Audit and Fix Mathematical and Coordinate Errors in Root Size Thresholds, Table 3, and Table 4
* **WHY:** Several numerical calculations in the Results and Tables are internally inconsistent:
  1. **Results, line 99 (Root size classification):** States `"Based on a median TRL value of 137.52 cm per plant ± 2 standard errors (SE) of 7.53, 177 accessions were classified as having large-sized roots (TRL >130.52 cm), 29 accessions were identified as having medium-sized roots (TRL ranging from 115.45 to 130.52 cm), and 173 genotypes were deemed as having small-sized root systems (TRL <115.45 cm)"`. First, `137.51 cm` is the **mean** in Table 1 (line 87), not the median, and with $\text{SD} = 90.10$ and $N = 379$, $\text{SE} = 90.10 / \sqrt{379} = 4.63\text{ cm}$ (not $7.53$). Second, even using $7.53$, $137.52 \pm 2(7.53)$ gives $[122.46, 152.58]$, whereas the text intervals $[115.45, 130.52]$ are centered at $122.99\text{ cm}$ ($122.99 \pm 7.53$), meaning the center was $122.99\text{ cm}$ and the half-width was $1 \times 7.53\text{ cm}$.
  2. **Table 3 (lines 196–198):** The sum of `Pairwise comparisons of all linked SNP markers` for the A-genome ($251,738$) and C-genome ($45,441$) is $297,179$, yet the `AC-genome` total row reports a *smaller* number (`224,259`), while `Number of SNP pairs in significant LD` ($134,687 + 27,474 = 162,161$) is reported as `169,182 (75.4%)` in the `AC-genome` row.
  3. **Discussion (line 9 of chunk 2):** States `"the 1,110 filtered set of SNP markers on the C-genome was distributed over 302.5 Mb or 905.5 cM"`—`302.5 Mb` is the **A-genome** length from Table 3, whereas the C-genome length is `452.8 Mb`.
  4. **Table 4 (lines 239–280):** Replace the `Start` and `End` columns (which currently list local BLAST hit query/subject offsets like `28467–28487`) with the actual physical chromosome coordinates (in bp) on the *B. napus* Darmor-bzh / EnsemblPlants reference assembly, and restrict Table 4 to SNPs passing Bonferroni significance in the kinship-controlled mixed models (`PCA+K` / `Q+K`) rather than naive `PCA`/`Q` GLMs.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Correct the TRL classification center and SE formula on line 99, fix the `AC-genome` summation row in Table 3, fix `302.5 Mb` $\to$ `452.8 Mb` in the Discussion, and update Table 4 with true chromosomal coordinates and MLM-verified associations.

#### 3. Correct Statistical Citations and Provide Direct Genotype/Phenotype Data Accessions
* **WHY:** In the Methods ("Genome-wide association studies", line 63), the Bonferroni cutoff ($0.05 / M$) is attributed to `"Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995"` (which is the false discovery rate [FDR] paper, not Bonferroni). In the Data Availability Statement (lines 25 and 60), the text retains the generic journal template placeholder (`"The names of the repository/repositories and accession number(s) can be found in the article/ Supplementary Material"`) without naming any repository or accession number for the 19K SNP array data.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Cite Dunn (1961) for the Bonferroni correction (or apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at $q \le 0.05$), replace Duncan's multiple range test in Table 2 with Tukey's HSD test, and deposit the 313-accession $\times$ 6,213-SNP genotype matrix and 379-accession RSA phenotype table in Dryad, Figshare, or Zenodo with a permanent DOI in the Data Availability Statement.
