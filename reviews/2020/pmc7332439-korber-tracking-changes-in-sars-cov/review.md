### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript describes a real-time genomic surveillance pipeline (`cov.lanl.gov`) tracking SARS-CoV-2 Spike mutations across 28,576 QC-filtered sequences from GISAID (as of 29 May 2020) and integrates epidemiological time-series modeling, structural mapping, clinical cohort analysis ($N = 999$ linked patients from an English NHS Foundation Trust), and in vitro VSV/lentiviral pseudotype entry and neutralization assays to characterize the Spike D614G substitution. Using hierarchical geographic partitioning, pre-onset vs. post-delay Fisher's exact tests, and permutation-calibrated isotonic logistic regression, the authors show that the 4-mutation G-clade haplotype (C241T, C3037T, RdRp P323L, Spike D614G) recurrently increased in frequency across continents (5/5), countries (16/17 by Fisher's test; 30/31 subcountries/states by isotonic regression, binomial $p = 2.98\times 10^{-9}$), and counties/cities (11/12 and 17/19), even where D614 outbreaks were already well established and after stay-at-home orders. Clinically, G614 is associated with a lower diagnostic E-gene RT-PCR cycle threshold ($\Delta\text{Ct} = -0.78, p = 0.037$) after adjusting for extraction method, age, and sex, without increasing hospitalization odds ($p = 0.60$). Experimentally, G614 increases VSV and lentiviral pseudotype infectious titers by 2.6- to 9.3-fold ($p = 9\times 10^{-11}$) across Vero, 293T-ACE2, 293T-ACE2-TMPRSS2, and TZM-bl/ACE2 cells while remaining fully sensitive to convalescent sera ($n = 6$). Forensic quality control also exposes a nanopore ARTIC amplicon-81 adapter-trimming artifact causing spurious S943P calls. Processed data are deposited on Mendeley Data (`10.17632/hn3h9gdrgj.1`) and interactive tools are hosted at `cov.lanl.gov`.

This is a thorough, multi-disciplinary study that pairs global bioinformatics surveillance with clinical and experimental validation, transparently discusses exceptions (Iceland travel-screening shift and Santa Clara DPH vs. Stanford community structure), and includes aDedicated "Limitations of Study" section. A few statistical and methodological caveats warrant attention: (1) in the clinical viral load regression ($\Delta\text{Ct} = -0.78, p = 0.037$), calendar time / symptom duration at swabbing is an unmodeled confounder because D614 cases were sampled earlier in the epidemic wave while G614 cases were sampled later (when testing expanded and patients may have been swabbed earlier in their infection course), and the mid-April switch to heat-inactivated RT-PCR (`Method_2`, $+3.20\text{ Ct}$, $p < 2\times 10^{-16}$) is collinear with the temporal replacement of D614 by G614; (2) in the "unmasking" hospitalization mediation analysis (STAR Methods lines 340–354), replacing Ct with the linear regression residuals (`Corrected Ct`) in a multivariable model that already includes `G614` and `Method_2` is algebraically redundant and slightly misstated (Wait: `Method_2` was dropped in the second table, which confounds the residual); and (3) the 400-permutation floor ($p \ge 0.0025$) in the isotonic regression limits granular multiple-testing correction across regions. Addressing these statistical points will further strengthen the clinical and quantitative analyses.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The G614 haplotype recurrently displaced D614 across hierarchical geographic regions worldwide, including regions with established D614 epidemics and post-lockdown windows: [Well Supported].**  
  The dual statistical framework—combining a 2-week lagged Fisher's exact test after a 15-sequence/3-minor-variant onset threshold (Fig. 1B) with isotonic logistic regression (`cgam`) across regions with $\ge 5$ counts of each variant and $\ge 14$ sampling days (Fig. 3, binomial sign test $p = 2.98\times 10^{-9}$)—provides strong, consistent evidence across geographic scales, and the two apparent outliers (Iceland and Santa Clara County, Fig. S4) are investigated and explained via sampling-frame metadata.

* **Spike G614 increases pseudotyped viral infectivity by 2.6- to 9.3-fold across multiple cell lines independently of TMPRSS2, without conferring resistance to polyclonal convalescent sera: [Well Supported].**  
  Infectivity increases are replicated across two distinct pseudotyping platforms (VSV-$\Delta$G-GFP and HIV-1 lentiviral vectors normalized to p24 content) and four target cell lines in two independent laboratories (Fig. 6A–C, random-effects log-normal GLM $p = 9\times 10^{-11}$), while neutralization curves across six convalescent donor sera show equivalent or slightly improved neutralization of G614 (Fig. 6D–E). As the authors appropriately note in Limitations (line 141), single-cycle Spike pseudotypes cannot isolate the linked RdRp P323L mutation or fully recapitulate authentic SARS-CoV-2 transmission in primary airway epithelia.

* **Apparent Spike S943P clusters in GISAID resulted from untrimmed Oxford Nanopore adapter homology in ARTIC amplicon 81: [Well Supported].**  
  The BAM-level forensic inspection comparing overlapping ARTIC amplicons 80 and 81 before and after primer/adapter soft-clipping (Fig. S7, lines 362–377) is a textbook example of rigorous bioinformatic quality control.

* **In 999 clinical patients, G614 is associated with higher upper-respiratory viral loads ($\Delta\text{Ct} = -0.78, p = 0.037$) and shows no association with hospitalization ($p = 0.60$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the non-association with hospitalization ($p = 0.60$) and strong effects of age ($z = 15.79, p < 2\times 10^{-16}$) and male sex ($z = 8.21, p = 2.3\times 10^{-16}$) are clear, the viral load effect ($\Delta\text{Ct} = -0.78 \pm 0.37\text{ Ct}, p = 0.037$) is modest and sensitive to two unmodeled temporal confounders (Pillars 2, 3, and 4):
  1. *Temporal collinearity with the PCR protocol switch and epidemic phase:* In mid-April 2020, the hospital switched from MagnaPure96 nucleic acid extraction (`Method_1`) to heat inactivation (`Method_2`), which shifts Ct upward by $+3.20\text{ Ct}$ ($p < 2\times 10^{-16}$, line 318)—more than 4-fold larger than the $-0.78\text{ Ct}$ G614 effect. Because G614 was simultaneously sweeping from minority to majority status during March–April 2020, almost all `Method_2` samples are G614 (which is why G614 vs. D614 is non-significant within `Method_2`, line 320). Furthermore, as the first UK wave progressed from early March to May, days from symptom onset to swabbing and patient admission criteria evolved; without controlling for sampling date or days post-symptom onset, a $0.78\text{ Ct}$ shift ($p = 0.037$) is fragile.
  2. *Redundant / misspecified residualization in the hospitalization model (lines 340–354):* To test whether Ct "masks" a direct effect of G614 on hospitalization, the authors regress Ct on `Method` and `G614`, take the residuals (`Corrected Ct`), and fit `Hospitalization ~ G614 + Corrected_Ct + Male + Age` (dropping `Method_2`). First, to estimate the *total* (unmasked) effect of `G614` on hospitalization without conditioning on the intermediate mediator (`Ct`), one simply fits `Hospitalization ~ G614 + Male + Age` (omitting `Ct` altogether!). Second, by dropping `Method_2` from the second logistic regression (lines 342–352) while keeping `G614`, the `G614` coefficient changes only because `Method_2` was omitted, not because `Ct` was residualized (by Frisch-Waugh-Lovell theorem, replacing a covariate $M$ with $M - \hat{M}(X)$ in a linear predictor that still includes $X$ leaves the likelihood and predicted values identical and shifts $\beta_X$ by exactly $\beta_M \hat{\gamma}_{M|X}$).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Calendar Date of Sampling in the Clinical Ct Regression and Simplify the Hospitalization Mediation Test
* **WHY:** In the $N = 999$ clinical cohort, G614 frequency increased monotonically with calendar date while diagnostic capacity, patient presentation timing, and the RT-PCR extraction protocol (`Method_1` vs. `Method_2`) changed over time. Moreover, the residualization approach in STAR Methods (lines 340–354) is a non-standard way to test mediation when simply omitting the mediator (`Ct`) gives the total effect of `G614` on hospitalization directly.
* **WHAT:** Add sampling date (and interaction `G614 * Method_2`) to the Ct linear model to verify whether the $\Delta\text{Ct} = -0.78$ ($p = 0.037$) association holds after adjusting for epidemic week, and report the total-effect logistic regression for hospitalization (`Hospitalization ~ G614 + Male + Age`).
* **HOW:** 
  1. In R, fit `lm(Ct ~ G614 + Method_2 + splines::ns(SampleDate, df=3) + Sex + Age)` and `lm(Ct ~ G614 * Method_2 + Sex + Age)` on the $N = 999$ dataset and report the adjusted `G614` coefficient and $p$-value.
  2. Replace the `Corrected Ct` residual table (lines 342–353) with the standard total-effect logistic regression `glm(Hospitalized ~ G614 + Male + Age, family = binomial)` (without `Ct`), which directly tests whether G614 affects hospitalization when not conditioning on viral load.

#### 2. Increase Permutation Replicates in Isotonic Regression and Report FDR-Adjusted $q$-Values Across Geographic Regions
* **WHY:** In STAR Methods (line 297), the isotonic logistic regression (`cgam`) uses only $B = 400$ random permutations per region, truncating all $p$-values at a lower bound of $1/400 = 0.0025$. Furthermore, in Figure 1B and Figure 3A, regions are first pre-filtered by nominal $p < 0.05$ before running the across-region binomial sign test.
* **WHAT:** Increase the number of permutations to $B = 10,000$ per region, report Benjamini-Hochberg FDR $q$-values across all tested regions in Figure 3A / Data S1, and run the across-region binomial sign test on *all* regions meeting the sample-size inclusion criteria (not just those filtered at $p < 0.05$).
* **HOW:** Re-run the `cgam` permutation loop with `n_perm = 10000` (which takes only a few minutes for $\sim 50$ regions), compute `p.adjust(p, method = "BH")`, and report the binomial test on the sign of the fitted isotonic slope across all eligible regions regardless of their individual $p$-value threshold.

#### 3. Deposit the Alignment and Isotonic Regression Scripts Directly Without Conditioning on Funder Permission
* **WHY:** In the Data and Code Availability statement (line 220), the authors state that code for alignment generation and isotonic regression *"will be available through https://cov.lanl.gov, at also GitHub, once permission from our funders is obtained."* Withholding core statistical and alignment scripts pending post-publication funder approval impedes immediate reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Include the R script for the `cgam` isotonic logistic regression and the `align_to_ref.pl` wrapper directly in the Mendeley Data archive (`doi:10.17632/hn3h9gdrgj.1`) or as a supplemental code zip file (`Data S2`).
