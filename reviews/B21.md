### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

#### Executive Summary
This manuscript presents `UFBoot2`, an accelerated and statistically refined successor to the `UFBoot` ultrafast bootstrap approximation implemented in `IQ-TREE` v1.6. `UFBoot2` introduces four main improvements: (1) accelerating Felsenstein's pruning likelihood calculation via reversible-model root-Optimization shortcuts (adopted from `RAxML`, yielding theoretical 4x DNA and 20x protein speedups during branch-length optimization) and SSE/AVX SIMD vectorization across 2–4 alignment sites, (2) resolving polytomy-induced over-support by uniformly sampling among candidate trees within $\varepsilon_{\text{boot}} = 0.5$ log-likelihood units of the best RELL score for each bootstrap replicate, (3) mitigating branch-support inflation under severe substitution model misspecification via an optional hill-climbing nearest-neighbor interchange post-optimization (`UFBoot2+NNI`, invoked via `-bnni`) directly on each bootstrap alignment, and (4) implementing partition-aware phylogenomic resampling (`-bsam GENE` and `-bsam GENESITE` alongside default site-within-partition resampling). Across 115 TreeBASE benchmark alignments (70 DNA, 45 protein; `10.5281/zenodo.854445`), 100 four-taxon star-tree null simulations (15,000 bp), 5,690 PANDIT DNA simulations under matched and severely misspecified models (`10.5281/zenodo.854445`), and 15 empirical phylogenomic datasets (highlighted by the 21-species, 225-gene, 171,077-site metazoan alignment of Salichos and Rokas, 2013), `UFBoot2` and `UFBoot2+NNI` achieve median speedups of 778x and 424x over standard nonparametric bootstrap (`SBS`) and 8.4x and 5.0x over `RAxML` rapid bootstrap (`RBS`), eliminate false positives ($\ge 95\%$) on star trees (capping support at $\le 88\%$), and correct the liberal bias of `UFBoot` under severe model violations. Software is freely available in `IQ-TREE` v1.6 (`http://www.iqtree.org`) and benchmark alignments are archived on Zenodo.

The paper is concise, focused, and empirically rigorous in testing both null calibration (4-taxon star trees) and probability calibration ($f_M(x)$, the empirical fraction of true clades among branches supported at $x\%$) across 5,690 PANDIT simulations. The primary limitations are: (1) an asymmetric baseline omission in Figure 1B (`RAxML` `RBS` is omitted under severe model misspecification because `RAxML` lacks simple sub-models like JC/K2P, even though `RBS` could have been evaluated by simulating under a complex model and inferring under GTR vs. partitioning/rate heterogeneity omissions, or by running `SBS` under the same constraint), (2) the unexamined sensitivity of the polytomy tie-breaker to the fixed $\varepsilon_{\text{boot}} = 0.5$ log-likelihood threshold across varying alignment lengths, and (3) reporting the 14 additional phylogenomic resampling comparisons as "data not shown" (line 65).

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

#### Detailed Rubric Evaluation

* **Claim 1 — `UFBoot2` and `UFBoot2+NNI` achieve median speedups of 778x (range 200–1,848x) and 424x (range 233–749x) over `SBS`, and 8.4x (range 1.5–51.2x) and 5.0x (range 0.8–32.6x) over `RAxML` `RBS` across 115 TreeBASE DNA and protein alignments: [Well Supported].**  
  Lines 27 and 51 (and Supplementary Figures S1–S3) document the runtime distributions across 70 DNA and 45 protein MSAs archived at `10.5281/zenodo.854445`, with exact command lines provided in Supplementary Table S1.

* **Claim 2 — Post-optimizing RELL bootstrap trees via NNI hill-climbing (`UFBoot2+NNI`, `-bnni`) eliminates the branch-support overestimation of `UFBoot`/`UFBoot2` under severe model violations while remaining 424x faster than `SBS`: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 1A,B on 5,690 PANDIT DNA simulations demonstrates that while standard `UFBoot2` is nearly unbiased (tracking the $y = x$ diagonal) under the true model (Figure 1A), it becomes anti-conservative (curve below the diagonal) under severe model misspecification (Figure 1B). Adding the NNI step (`UFBoot2+NNI`) restores conservative/near-unbiased calibration (slightly above the diagonal, closer to $y = x$ than `SBS`) at only a 2x median runtime cost over `UFBoot2`.

* **Claim 3 — Randomly selecting among candidate RELL trees within $\varepsilon_{\text{boot}} = 0.5$ log-likelihood units of the maximum prevents false-positive resolution of polytomies ($\le 88\%$ support across 100 four-taxon star-tree simulations of 15,000 sites): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Line 37 confirms that on the Simmons and Norton (2014) 4-taxon star-tree benchmark ($L = 15,000$ bp, terminal branches 0.05 under JC, analyzed under JC and GTR+$\Gamma$), `UFBoot2` never assigns $\ge 95\%$ support to nonexistent internal branches (maximum observed $\le 88\%$). However, the choice of a fixed absolute RELL log-likelihood tolerance $\varepsilon_{\text{boot}} = 0.5$ is only tested on 4-taxon star trees; on large trees with thousands of taxa, RELL log-likelihood variance across bootstrap resamples scales with $\sqrt{L}$ and tree size, and no sensitivity analysis is shown to verify that $\varepsilon_{\text{boot}} = 0.5$ does not excessively depress support for real short branches.

* **Claim 4 — Phylogenomic resampling strategies (`MSA-site`, `gene`, and `gene-site`) yield substantial support discrepancies on empirical phylogenomic datasets (e.g., 100% vs. 80% on the metazoan backbone, and $\ge 95\%$ vs. $\le 10\%$ on 14 other datasets), motivating running all three strategies: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Figure 2 clearly demonstrates the divergence between site/gene resampling (>95%) and gene-site resampling (80%) along the Protostomia/Deuterostomia backbone of the 21-taxon, 225-gene metazoan dataset (Salichos and Rokas, 2013). However, the results for the 14 additional empirical phylogenomic datasets (line 65), including the striking claim that certain branches drop from $\ge 95\%$ under two strategies to $\le 10\%$ under the third, are cited as `(data not shown)` rather than tabulated in the Supplementary Material.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### Major Issues, Minor Issues & Concrete Recommendations

#### 1. Tabulate the 14 "Data Not Shown" Phylogenomic Resampling Comparisons in the Supplementary Material (Major Issue)
* **WHY:** Line 65 states that across 14 published empirical phylogenomic datasets, *"we observed more discrepancies between resampling strategies (data not shown). Exceptionally, for some data sets, a number of branches showed almost no support ($\le 10\%$) for one resampling but high supports ($\ge 95\%$) for the other two resampling strategies."* Withholding these already-computed results as "data not shown" prevents readers from verifying which datasets and clades exhibit extreme sensitivity to gene vs. site resampling.
* **WHAT:** Add a Supplementary Table S2 summarizing the 14 empirical datasets and the distribution of branch-support differences across `MSA-site`, `gene`, and `gene-site` resampling.
* **HOW:** For each of the 14 datasets (already analyzed by the authors), list the number of taxa, number of genes, total sites, fraction of internal branches with $\ge 95\%$ support under each of the three `-bsam` modes, and the number of discordant branches ($\ge 95\%$ vs. $< 80\%$ or $\le 10\%$).

#### 2. Evaluate `RBS` Under Model Misspecification Using a Model Pair Supported by Both `RAxML` and `IQ-TREE` (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** In Figure 1B, `RAxML` rapid bootstrap (`RBS`) is omitted from the severe model misspecification panel with the note *"RBS not shown because RAxML does not support simpler models."* While standard `RAxML` v8 primarily exposes `GTR` for DNA (though RAxML v8.1+ added `--JC69` and `--K80` options), model misspecification can also be evaluated symmetrically by simulating under a complex codon/heterotachy/mixture model and inferring under unpartitioned `GTR` (without $+\Gamma$) in both `RAxML` and `IQ-TREE`.
* **WHAT:** Include an `RBS` curve in Figure 1B (either using `RAxML`'s `--JC69`/`--K80` flags or under rate-heterogeneity omission `GTR+I+G` simulated $\to$ `GTR` inferred) so all four methods (`SBS`, `RBS`, `UFBoot2`, `UFBoot2+NNI`) are compared symmetrically in both panels.
* **HOW:** Run `RAxML` `RBS` on the PANDIT subset where the true model is `GTR+I+G` and inference is restricted to `GTR` (or using `--JC69`), and plot the resulting calibration curve $f_{\text{RBS}}(x)$ in Figure 1B.

#### 3. Clarify the Command-Line Flag Typo (`-bcor 1`) for the Star-Tree Simulation (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** In line 37 ("Correction for Polytomies"), the authors state: *"For each MSA, we performed UFBoot2 runs under JC and GTR+$\Gamma$, each with 1,000 bootstrap replicates and up to 1,000 search iterations (invoked in IQ-TREE via '-bcor 1' option)."* In `IQ-TREE`, `-bcor` sets the minimum correlation coefficient for theUFBoot convergence criterion (default 0.99), whereas `-nm` sets the maximum number of iterations (default 1000) and `-beps` sets $\varepsilon_{\text{boot}}$ (default 0.5). Setting `-bcor 1` forces the search to run all 1,000 iterations without early stopping.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Clarify in line 37 that `-bcor 1.0` (alongside `-bb 1000 -nm 1000`) was used specifically to disable the early-stopping correlation rule so that all 1,000 search iterations were executed, and state the flag (`-beps`) that controls $\varepsilon_{\text{boot}}$.
