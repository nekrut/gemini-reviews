### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the temporal dynamics of nonadaptive amino acid convergence ($C$) relative to paired divergence ($D$) across 629 vertebrate mitochondrial genomes (13 concatenated protein-coding genes, 3,596 sites) and compares empirical $C/D$ trajectories against homogeneous substitution models (JC, WAG, codon-based $Z$), site-heterogeneous mixture models (CAT-60), and thermodynamic epistatic simulations (the Stokes–Fisher [SF] model on a 300-residue purple acid phosphatase structure, PDB 1QHW). Using ancestral sequence reconstructions inferred via PLEX under an mtMam+$\Gamma_5$ model along a CO1-derived Bayesian phylogeny, the authors demonstrate that empirical $C/D$ ratios decline from ~0.40 at short inter-branch patristic distances to <0.20 at large distances (Fig. 1). Specifically, when conditioning on branch pairs that share the same inferred ancestral amino acid (Fig. 2A), $C/D$ still declines significantly over time (from ~0.45 to ~0.20), whereas under the time-homogeneous site-specific CAT-60 model, conditioning on identical ancestral states causes $C/D$ to *increase* over time due to survivorship bias toward low-entropy conserved sites (Fig. 6A). The authors conclude that site-specific amino acid propensities fluctuate over evolutionary time due to epistatic coevolution ("evolutionary Stokes shift") and that excess molecular convergence among closely related lineages cannot be assumed to be adaptive when evaluated against standard time-homogeneous null models.

The analytical distinction between spatial site heterogeneity (CAT-60) and temporal constraint fluctuation (SF) conditioned on identical ancestral states is a sharp theoretical and empirical contribution. However, several methodological choices warrant scrutiny: (1) ancestral states on the mitochondrial tree are inferred using a site-homogeneous empirical matrix (mtMam+$\Gamma_5$), which can introduce systematic ancestral reconstruction bias that varies with patristic distance and site conservation; (2) the tree topology and branch lengths are estimated solely from CO1 and applied to all 13 concatenated proteins without accounting for topological or branch-length uncertainty; (3) display plots filter branch pairs by ad hoc minimum divergence cutoffs ($D \ge 20, 7, 10$) and posterior probability thresholds (>90%), which can truncate variance differently across patristic distance bins; and (4) neither the concatenated alignment, the inferred phylogeny, nor the simulation/calculation scripts are deposited in a public repository. These issues can be addressed through targeted computational controls and archival deposition.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Nonadaptive amino acid convergence ($C/D$) decreases strongly with evolutionary distance in vertebrate mitochondrial proteins: [Well Supported].**  
  Across 629 vertebrate mitochondrial proteomes, $C/D$ drops from ~0.4 at short patristic distances to <0.2 at distances >1.5 replacements/site (Fig. 1), with non-overlapping 99% credible intervals integrated over ancestral state posterior distributions (Supplementary Fig. S2, Table S1) and robustness to alignment software (ClustalX vs. PRANK, Supplementary Fig. S7).

* **Time-homogeneous site-heterogeneous models (CAT-60) predict an *increase* in $C/D$ over time for sites with identical ancestral amino acids, opposite to the empirical mitochondrial decline: [Well Supported].**  
  The mathematical and simulation demonstration in Fig. 6A is exact: if site profiles are static over time, conditioning on the ancestral state remaining unchanged across increasing patristic distances progressively enriches for low-entropy (highly constrained) sites, driving expected $C/D$ upward. The observed downward slope in empirical mitochondrial data for identical ancestral states (99% CI: $[-0.109, -0.094]$, Fig. 2A, Supplementary Fig. S3) therefore falsifies static site-independent profile models as a complete explanation.

* **Once ancestral amino acids have diverged at a site, site-specific information about convergence probabilities is completely erased ($C/D \approx 0.08$, indistinguishable from site-resampled substitutions): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the permutation test in Supplementary Fig. S4 shows that resampling descendant states across all sites sharing the same ancestral amino acid reproduces the flat $C/D \approx 0.08$ curve of Fig. 2B, this analysis conditions on inferred substitutions with >90% posterior probability under the homogeneous mtMam+$\Gamma_5$ model. At sites where ancestors have already diverged, multiple substitutions on intervening branches make ancestral state reconstruction under a site-homogeneous model most prone to regression toward stationary background frequencies, which could artificially homogenize inferred $C/D$ toward the global matrix average.

* **The mitochondrial $C/D$ decay is specifically explained by epistatic "evolutionary Stokes shift" dynamics rather than nonepistatic temporal heterogeneity (covarion/heterotachy) or mutational asymmetry: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The Stokes–Fisher thermodynamic simulation matches the empirical mitochondrial curve closely (Fig. 4B), and the authors acknowledge that SF is an illustrative model. However, vertebrate mitochondrial genomes experience pronounced, lineage-specific strand-asymmetric directional mutation pressure (which the authors note in Methods as the reason for building the tree from CO1 alone). Diverging mutational spectra across clades or non-epistatic covarion shifts in functional constraint could also generate decaying $C/D$ from identical ancestral states without requiring thermodynamic pairwise contact epistasis.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Evaluate Ancestral Reconstruction Bias by Inferring Mitochondrial Substitutions Under a Site-Heterogeneous Model
* **WHY:** In the empirical analysis, substitutions and ancestral states are inferred using PLEX under a single site-homogeneous matrix (mtMam+$\Gamma_5$). Because mtMam ignores site-specific amino acid profiles, ancestral reconstruction accuracy degrades with patristic distance, potentially distorting the partition between "same" (Fig. 2A) and "different" (Fig. 2B) ancestral states.
* **WHAT:** Verify that the decreasing $C/D$ slope for identical ancestral states (Fig. 2A) and the CAT-60 vs. empirical contrast (Fig. 6A) hold when ancestral states are reconstructed under a site-heterogeneous mixture model (e.g., CAT or C20/C60 profile mixture) or when CAT-60 sequences are simulated on the exact 629-taxon mitochondrial tree and passed through the identical PLEX mtMam+$\Gamma_5$ inference pipeline.
* **HOW:** Simulate alignments of 3,596 sites along the 629-taxon mitochondrial phylogeny under the CAT-60 model, run PLEX with mtMam+$\Gamma_5$ on those simulated alignments exactly as done for the real data (including the >90% posterior probability filter), and plot the inferred $C/D$ for same and different ancestral states alongside Fig. 2A–B and Fig. 6A.

#### 2. Control for Lineage-Specific Mitochondrial Mutational Asymmetry Across the 13 Genes
* **WHY:** As noted in Materials and Methods, vertebrate mitochondrial genes exhibit strong, clade-specific strand-asymmetric cytosine deamination and mutational gradients (Krishnan et al. 2004). If closely related branches share similar nucleotide mutation biases while distantly related branches experience divergent mutational spectra, amino acid $C/D$ will decay with patristic distance even under constant selective constraints.
* **WHAT:** Test whether the decay in $C/D$ for identical ancestral amino acids persists in CO1 alone (the least asymmetric gene) and when stratifying amino acid replacements by codon GC/AT-skew direction.
* **HOW:** Recompute the identical-ancestor $C/D$ vs. patristic distance regression separately for CO1 vs. the most strand-asymmetric mitochondrial genes (e.g., ND2, ND6, ATP8), or compare empirical $C/D$ against a nucleotide-level strand-asymmetric codon simulation along the mitochondrial tree.

#### 3. Assess Sensitivity to Display Cutoffs ($D \ge 20$) and Ratio-of-Sums vs. Mean-of-Ratios Bias
* **WHY:** In Figs. 1, 3, and 4, branch pairs with fewer than $D \ge 20$ (or $D \ge 7, 10$) divergent events are excluded from the scatter plots and running averages. Because $D$ is proportional to the product of the two branch lengths, filtering on $D \ge 20$ systematically excludes short-branch pairs unless they have unusually high substitution rates, and taking the ratio $C/D$ on small counts introduces positive small-sample ratio bias ($E[C/D] > E[C]/E[D]$).
* **WHAT:** Report the pooled ratio $\sum C / \sum D$ across patristic distance bins without truncating on $D \ge 20$, and verify that the variance and decay curve in Fig. 1 and Fig. 3 are invariant to the $D$ threshold.
* **HOW:** Calculate binned $\sum C / \sum D$ (summing $C$ and $D$ across all eligible branch pairs within each 0.03 patristic distance window, including $D < 20$ pairs) integrated over the MCMC posterior samples, and overlay this curve on Fig. 1 and Fig. 3A–B.

#### 4. Clarify Parameter Notation and Typographical Inconsistencies in Equations and Text
* **WHY:** Several minor mathematical and editorial inconsistencies appear in the text and equations.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. In Equation (4), the denominator uses $N_{\text{Eff}}$ whereas the immediately following line defines it as $N_e$ ("with $N_e$, the effective population size set equal to $10^6$"); standardize the notation to $N_e$.
  2. In the "Relationship of Convergence with Time under Different Evolutionary Models" section, correct the typo "The results for that CAT model are especially notable" to "The results for the CAT model are especially notable".
  3. In Materials and Methods ("Phenomenological Substitution Models"), the text refers to "summing over all possible amino acids (or codons) for sequences $k$, $i$, and $j$", where sequence $k$ (presumably the common ancestor of $i$ and $j$) was never defined in the preceding sentences; explicitly define $k$.

#### 5. Deposit the Alignment, Phylogeny, and Simulation Code in a Public Archive
* **WHY:** Reproducing the 629-taxon mitochondrial analysis and the Stokes–Fisher / phenomenological model calculations requires the curated 3,596-site alignment, the MrBayes CO1 tree, and the custom simulation scripts, none of which are linked in an external repository.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Deposit the concatenated 629-taxon alignment, the CO1 tree with branch lengths, the PLEX configuration files, and the SF/phenomenological calculation scripts on Dryad, Zenodo, or GitHub, and add a Data and Code Availability statement.
