### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

#### Executive Summary
This manuscript presents PLINK 1.9 (and the architectural specification for PLINK 2.0), a complete C/C++ algorithmic and systems overhaul of the ubiquitous PLINK 1.07 whole-genome association and population-genetics toolkit. The authors introduce five core computational innovations: (1) 64-bit word and SSE2 vector bit-level parallelism (`popcount` / `popcount2`) over packed 2-bit BED genotypes for Hamming/identity-by-state (IBS) distance and linkage disequilibrium ($r$) dot products, (2) a 4-table ($2^{15}$-entry, 1 MB L2/L3-cache-resident) 20-marker partial-sum lookup algorithm for variance-standardized genomic relationship matrices (GRM), (3) an $O(\sqrt{n})$-time, $O(1)$-space early-termination algorithm for Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (SNP-HWE) and $2 \times 2$ / $2 \times 3$ Fisher's exact tests exploiting super-geometric tail decay below IEEE-754 double-precision ($2^{-53}$), (4) accelerated Gabriel et al. haplotype block estimation using analytic cubic roots of Hill's diplotype equation and unimodal likelihood bounding, and (5) streaming variant-window memory management, multithreaded PThreads/Win32 execution, and integrated third-party algorithms (`PERMORY`, `BOOST`, `CASSI`, coordinate-descent LASSO, `pigz`). Benchmarks across 7 hardware platforms (32/64-bit Linux, macOS, Windows; 2 to 512 cores; 1 GB to 512 GB RAM) and 3 datasets (`synth1` with $N=1,000, M=100,000$; `synth2` with $N=10,000, M=88,025$; and 1000 Genomes Phase 1 with $N=1,092, M=39,637,448$) demonstrate 10- to 91,000-fold speedups over PLINK 1.07 and Haploview 4.2, alongside competitive double-precision cross-platform performance against GCTA 1.24. All benchmark datasets and source snapshots are archived in GigaDB (`10.5524/100116`) and GitHub under GPLv3.

This is a model bioinformatics software paper. Rather than treating software acceleration as opaque engineering, the authors write out the exact bit-vector encodings, bitwise boolean identities, lookup-table decompositions, and numerical underflow bounds in the main text, and benchmark them across both commodity laptops (1–4 GB RAM) and 512-core servers against strong external baselines (`GCTA 1.24`, `Haploview 4.2`, `PERMORY 1.1`). The only minor blemishes are a couple of omitted `PERMORY` ratio entries in Table 6, an unquantified empirical accuracy/error bound table in the main text for the $O(\sqrt{n})$ Fisher exact test truncation (deferred to a separate mathematical manuscript), and a minor author-list duplication in the metadata header.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

#### Detailed Rubric Evaluation

* **Claim 1 — Bitwise parallelism (`XOR`/`AND` masking and SSE2 `popcount`/`popcount2`) accelerates IBS matrix computation, complete-linkage clustering ($O(n^2 \log n)$), and LD pruning (`--indep-pairwise`) by 2 to 3 orders of magnitude while scaling to 39.6 million variants within commodity RAM: [Well Supported].**  
  The bitwise derivations for IBS over 960-marker blocks and pairwise LD correlation $r$ (mapping $v_i w_i \in \{-1, 0, 1\}$ to 2-bit addition-friendly codes via `popcount2(((X XOR Y) AND (10101010..._2 - Z)) OR Z)`) are mathematically exact. Tables 2 and 4 confirm 320x–4,200x speedups for IBS matrices and 360x–8,700x speedups for `--indep-pairwise`, completing full 1000 Genomes Phase 1 LD pruning ($M=39.6\text{M}$) in 14.5k seconds (~4 hours) on a 12-core Mac Pro where PLINK 1.07 runs out of memory on a single chromosome.

* **Claim 2 — The 4-table ($2^{15}$-entry) 20-marker partial-sum lookup algorithm computes double-precision variance-standardized GRMs across all platforms and low-memory machines within 2–5x of 64-bit-Linux-only single-precision GCTA 1.24: [Well Supported].**  
  Table 3 shows that on 32-bit Linux, macOS, and Windows (where GCTA 1.02 is required or GCTA runs out of memory on `synth2p` and `chr1snp`), PLINK 1.9 is 9.5x–123x faster and succeeds in 1–8 GB RAM. On `Linux64-512` (where GCTA 1.24 uses hardware-optimized single-precision BLAS), the authors transparently report ratios of 0.46 (`synth1p`), 0.20 (`synth2p`), and 0.64 (`chr1snp`), honestly highlighting GCTA 1.24's speed advantage on large 64-bit Linux nodes alongside PLINK 1.9's advantages in double precision and memory footprint.

* **Claim 3 — Early-termination SNP-HWE and $2 \times 2$ / $2 \times 3$ Fisher's exact tests reduce time complexity from $O(n)$ to $O(\sqrt{n})$ (and $O(n)$ for $2 \times 3$) in $O(1)$ space while preserving 10+ significant digits of precision: [Well Supported].**  
  Figures 1 and 2 and the accompanying hypergeometric ratio derivation $\frac{(n_1 - k)(n_2 - k)}{(k+1)(k+2)}$ clearly explain why terms beyond the $2^{-53} \ell_{\max}$ ellipsoidal annulus underflow IEEE-754 double precision during direct summation. Integrating this exact test into `max(T)` permutation testing (`--fisher`, Table 6) yields 2,900x–91,000x speedups over PLINK 1.07.

* **Claim 4 — Analytic cubic root-finding for Hill's diplotype equation, unimodal CDF bounding, and 19:1 recombination-pair window skipping accelerate Gabriel et al. haplotype block identification (`--blocks`) by 1,000x–3,000x over PLINK 1.07 and >10,000x over Haploview 4.2: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 3 and Table 5 demonstrate that evaluating $L(D' = 0.90) < L(D' = \hat{x}) / 220$ suffices to classify recombination pairs in 2 evaluations instead of 101 whenever the cubic derivative has a single interior extremum, reducing `synth1` (`--ld-window-kb 1000`) runtime from 7,394.4 s (PLINK 1.07) and ~45,000 s (Haploview 4.2) to 9.8 s on `Mac-12`.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### Major Issues, Minor Issues & Concrete Recommendations

#### 1. State the Maximum Relative Error Bound of the $2^{-53}$ Tail Truncation in the Main Text (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** In the "Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and Fisher's exact tests" section (line 27), the authors note that stopping when terms drop below $2^{-53}$ of the running partial sum leaves the 10th significant digit unaffected and mention that formal error bounds are in a separate manuscript in preparation. A concise 1–2 sentence bound in the main text would make the numerical analysis self-contained.
* **WHAT:** State the worst-case relative error bound when summing at most $O(\sqrt{n})$ geometric tail terms below $2^{-53}$ (or $K \cdot 2^{-53} \lesssim 10^{-11}$ for $N \le 10^7$).
* **HOW:** Add a parenthetical note in the Fisher's exact test subsection stating the maximum empirical discrepancy observed between `FEXACT` and the early-termination algorithm across all tested variants in `synth1`, `synth2`, and `1000g`.

#### 2. Complete the Missing Ratio Entries in Table 6 for `PERMORY 1.1` vs. `PLINK 1.90` (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** In Table 6 (`Association analysis max(T) permutation test times`), the `Ratio` column reports `PLINK 1.07 / PLINK 1.90` for rows where `PERMORY 1.1` was not run (`Mac-2`, `Mac-12`, `Linux32-2`), omits the ratio entirely for rows where both `PLINK 1.07` and `PERMORY 1.1` were run (`Linux64-512`, `Win32-2`, `Win64-2` under `--trend`), and switches to reporting `PERMORY 1.1 / PLINK 1.90` in the bottom three rows (`--assoc lin (QT)`).
* **WHAT:** Split or explicitly label the `Ratio` column in Table 6 so readers can distinguish `1.07 / 1.90` speedups from `PERMORY / 1.90` speedups across all rows.
* **HOW:** Report both ratios (e.g., `6.1k / 102` for `Linux64-512` under `--trend`: $17,000 / 2.8 = 6,071\text{x}$ vs. PLINK 1.07 and $285.0 / 2.8 = 101.8\text{x}$ vs. PERMORY 1.1) or add a footnote clarifying the denominator in each block.
