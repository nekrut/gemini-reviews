* **Synthesis:** This landmark study presents the largest genome-wide association study (GWAS) meta-analysis conducted to date, analyzing 5,380,080 individuals across 281 cohorts and five major genetic ancestry groups (EUR: 4,080,687; EAS: 472,730; HIS: 455,180; AFR: 293,593; SAS: 77,890) across 1,385,132 HapMap 3 (HM3) common SNPs to map the genetic architecture of human adult height. Through conditional and joint (`GCTA-COJO`) analysis, out-of-sample replication in the Estonian Biobank ($N = 49,160$), stratified GREML (`GCTA`) variance partitioning in eight independent holdout cohorts across all five ancestries, within-sibship validation in UK Biobank sibling pairs, and systematic sample-size downsampling from $N = 0.13\text{M}$ to $5.3\text{M}$, the authors demonstrate that 12,111 quasi-independent genome-wide significant (GWS) SNPs concentrated within 7,209 non-overlapping segments covering ~21.6% of the genome (647.5 Mb) account for virtually 100% of common-SNP heritability ($h^2_{\text{SNP}}$) in Europeans and $>90\%$ in non-Europeans. The methodological execution, cross-ancestry empirical validation, and biological synthesis (`ACAN` allelic series, `MAGMA`/`DEPICT`, `S-LDSC`, and `SMR`) are outstanding; the primary methodological limitations involve the reliance on a single European LD reference panel ($N = 348,501$ UKB EUR) when running `GCTA-COJO` and `SBayesC` on the trans-ancestry meta-analysis (`META_FE`), the contribution of cross-chromosome assortative mating to between-family vs. within-sibship polygenic score variance, and a few minor internal sample-size transcription discrepancies between the Methods and Figure 4 captions.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Evaluation & Empirical Audit

* **Claim 1:** Meta-analysis of 5.31 million individuals identifies 12,111 quasi-independent GWS SNPs (9,920 marginally GWS and 2,191 secondary conditional signals in `META_FE`, plus 12,179 in `META_EUR`) that replicate with high effect-size concordance in the Estonian Biobank ($N = 49,160$, $\rho_b = 0.931$ for marginal SNPs and $\rho_b = 0.803$ for secondary conditional SNPs) and cluster into 7,209 non-overlapping genomic segments ($\pm 35\text{ kb}$) covering 21.6% (647.5 Mb) of the genome, with local signal density reaching 25 GWS SNPs per 100 kb at the *ACAN* locus.
  * **Support Level:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Audit Trail:** Table 1, Figure 1a–c, Figure 2a–d, Extended Data Figures 1–4, Supplementary Notes 2–3, and Supplementary Tables 3–6.
  * **Assessment:** The out-of-sample replication in the Estonian Biobank using the regression-slope and sampling-error-corrected correlation estimator ($\hat{\rho}_b$, Equations 1–2), together with explicit verification that multiple common SNPs at *ACAN* remain jointly significant alongside the known exon 12 variable number tandem repeat (VNTR), provides rigorous empirical validation of both the primary and allelic-series conditional signals.

* **Claim 2:** Common genetic variation underlying human height is saturated at the locus level: stratified GREML (`GCTA`) shows that HM3 SNPs located within $\pm 35\text{ kb}$ of the 12,111 GWS SNPs (21% of the genome) explain $45.5\%\ (\text{s.e.} = 0.8\%)$ out of $45.7\%\ (\text{s.e.} = 0.6\%)$ total $h^2_{\text{SNP}}$ (~99.6%) in holdout Europeans and 90–100% of $h^2_{\text{SNP}}$ in non-European holdout cohorts, while downsampling shows that functional (`S-LDSC`) and gene-set (`MAGMA`/`DEPICT`) enrichments plateau below $N = 1\text{M}$ whereas causal variant resolution and `SMR` eQTL gene discovery continue to increase with sample size.
  * **Support Level:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Audit Trail:** Figure 3a–f, Figure 5a–c, Extended Data Figures 6, 8, and 9, and Supplementary Tables 8–17.
  * **Assessment:** The variance partitioning in independent cohorts (UKB holdout EUR $N = 14,583$, UKB SAS $N = 7,792$, UKB EAS $N = 2,265$, UKB AFR $N = 6,955$, WGHS, BioMe, PAGE, and WHI) combined with the two-stratum GREML model (`GWS loci` vs. `rest of the genome`) and empirical downsampling from $N = 130,000$ to $5,314,291$ convincingly establishes that additional sample sizes in Europeans mainly refine within-locus fine-mapping and secondary alleles rather than uncovering novel genomic regions.

* **Claim 3:** Polygenic scores derived from GWS SNPs (`PGS_GWS`) and genome-wide HM3 SNPs (`PGS_HM3` via `SBayesC`) explain 40.2% and 44.7% of phenotypic height variance in holdout Europeans (rising to 54.2%–55.2% when combined with mid-parental height in 981 UKB trios, and achieving $R^2 = 33\%$ within 17,492–17,942 UKB sibling pairs), while explaining 10–20% (`PGS_GWS`) and 14–24% (`PGS_HM3`) in non-European ancestries, where ~84% of the EUR-to-AFR loss of `PGS_GWS` accuracy is attributable to linkage disequilibrium (LD) and minor allele frequency (MAF) differences.
  * **Support Level:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Audit Trail:** Figure 4a–b, Extended Data Figure 7, Supplementary Note 4, and Supplementary Tables 18–20.
  * **Assessment:** The evaluation across multiple independent holdout cohorts, trio benchmarking against mid-parental height, within-sibship difference regressions ($\Delta y \sim \Delta\text{PGS}$), and analytical decomposition of cross-ancestry accuracy loss ($29.7\% / 35.5\% \approx 84\%$ due to LD and MAF under Lopez-Cortegano et al. 2019) are comprehensive and well calibrated.

## 3. Technical & Methodological Deep-Dive

### 1. Single-Ancestry (EUR) LD Reference Misspecification in Trans-Ancestry Conditional (`GCTA-COJO`) and Bayesian (`SBayesC`) Analyses
Both `GCTA-COJO` and `SBayesC` require an external linkage disequilibrium (LD) correlation matrix $\mathbf{R}$ to transform marginal GWAS summary statistics into conditional or joint multi-SNP effect estimates. For the trans-ancestry meta-analysis (`META_FE`, $N = 5,314,291$, which comprises 75.8% EUR and 24.2% non-EUR participants), the authors used an LD reference panel composed exclusively of $N = 348,501$ unrelated European UK Biobank individuals.
* When summary statistics derive from a multi-ancestry fixed-effect meta-analysis at loci with substantial cross-ancestry effect-size or LD heterogeneity (the authors note a mean Cochran's $Q$ heterogeneity statistic of 34% across ancestry groups for the 12,111 GWS SNPs, and only 56% of the 2,191 secondary COJO signals in `META_FE` physically overlap `<10 kb` with `META_EUR` COJO signals), inverting an exclusively European LD matrix against trans-ancestry marginal $Z$-scores at $N = 5.31\text{M}$ can generate phantom secondary conditional hits (`COJO` false positives) because the off-diagonal covariance of the trans-ancestry score statistic is a sample-size-weighted mixture $\sum_k w_k \mathbf{R}_k \neq \mathbf{R}_{\text{EUR}}$.
* This exact mismatch is empirically visible in Extended Data Figure 7: when lowering the `GCTA-COJO` selection threshold in `META_FE` to include less stringent conditional SNPs, out-of-sample prediction accuracy in African-ancestry cohorts *decreases* (`PGS_FE` performs worse than `PGS_EUR` at liberal thresholds), which the authors correctly attribute to LD mismatch between the EUR reference panel and the ~25% non-EUR fraction of `META_FE`. Using ancestry-matched meta-analytic LD covariance matrices (or running `COJO`/`SBayesC` within ancestry groups followed by multi-ancestry joint modeling such as `PRS-CSx` or `CT-SLEB`) would eliminate reference-LD inflation of secondary trans-ancestry conditional signals.

### 2. Assortative Mating and Cross-Chromosome Gametic Phase Disequilibrium in GREML $h^2_{\text{SNP}}$ and Between-Family PGS Variance
In Europeans, `PGS_GWS` explains 40.2% of height variance across unrelated individuals (`between-family`) but 33.0% of height variance within sibling pairs (`within-family`, Figure 4b)—a relative attenuation of $1 - 33.0/40.2 = 17.9\%$ in variance explained (~9.4% in correlation). While residual population stratification and indirect parental ("dynastic") effects contribute to this gap, human height is subject to strong positive phenotypic assortative mating ($r \approx 0.20\text{–}0.25$ between spouses). Under equilibrium assortative mating, directional trait-increasing alleles across unlinked chromosomes become positively correlated (gametic phase disequilibrium), inflating the equilibrium genetic variance in the population by a factor of $1 / (1 - r h^2_{\text{eq}}) \approx 1.15\text{–}1.20$ relative to the random-mating (Mendelian segregation) genetic variance observed within full-sibling pairs. Because both between-family PGS $R^2$ and standard GREML $h^2_{\text{SNP}}$ capture this cross-chromosome covariance in unrelated individuals—and because the GREML estimator in `GCTA` ignores positive correlations between causal effects on different chromosomes—explicitly distinguishing equilibrium assortative-mating variance from within-family Mendelian additive variance would sharpen the interpretation of the 40.2% vs. 33.0% PGS gap and the $h^2_{\text{SNP}}$ ceiling.

### 3. Internal Numerical & Typographical Inconsistencies to Correct
1. **UK Biobank Sibling-Pair Sample Size Discrepancy (`17,492` vs. `17,942`):** In the Figure 4b caption (page 6), the within-family validation sample is reported as *"17,492 independent EUR sibling pairs from the UKB"*, whereas in the Methods under *"Hold-out sample from the UK Biobank"* (page 13) and *"Within-family prediction"* (page 16), it is reported twice as *"17,942 sibling pairs"* (a digit transposition between `492` and `942`).
2. **PAGE Cohort Sample Sizes in Methods vs. Figure 4a Caption:** In the Figure 4a caption (page 6), the PAGE prediction cohorts are listed as *"HIS (n = 5,798 from the PAGE study) and AFR (n = 6,955 from the UKB and n = 8,238 from the PAGE study)"*. However, in the Methods under *"Between-family prediction"* (page 16), these two sample sizes are swapped: *"8,238 HIS and 5,798 AFR participants from the PAGE study"*. Furthermore, earlier in the Methods under *"Samples used for prediction and variance partitioning analyses"* (page 13), the PAGE HIS sample is listed as *"4,939 HIS individuals from the PAGE study (mean age: 59 years; 38.7% male)"*. These cohort counts should be harmonized across the text and captions.
3. **Equation (1) Subscript Typo:** On page 14, Equation (1) writes $\sin^{-1}(E[\rho_{bt}])$ on the left-hand side, whereas the immediately following sentence defines *"where $E[\rho_b]$ represents the expected value of $\rho_b$ under the assumption that the genetic correlation equals 1"* (without the extra subscript $t$).
