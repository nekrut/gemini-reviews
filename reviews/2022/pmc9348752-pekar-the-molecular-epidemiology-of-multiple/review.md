### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the ancestral haplotype, rooting, and early zoonotic transmission dynamics of SARS-CoV-2 using 787 complete lineage A and B genomes (plus 20 putative intermediate C/C or T/T genomes) sampled globally through 14 February 2020. By auditing sequencing coverage and laboratory provenance, reconstructing a 15-segment non-recombinant sarbecovirus progenitor (`recCA`), developing a non-reversible random-effects substitution model in BEAST v1.10.5, and coupling phylodynamic inference with individual-based SAPHIRE epidemic simulations (`FAVITES-COVID-Lite`) on a 5-million-node scale-free network via rejection sampling, the authors conclude that: (1) early C/C and T/T intermediate haplotypes are sequencing/bioinformatic artifacts or later convergent mutations; (2) highly revertant lineage A haplotypes (e.g., WA1/A.1 and 20SF012) reflect C-to-T mutational bias rather than the pandemic root ($\text{BF} > 100$); and (3) lineages B and A resulted from at least two separate zoonotic introductions into humans ($\text{BF} = 60.0\text{--}61.6$), with the lineage B primary case occurring around 18 November 2019 (95% HPD: 23 October–8 December) and the lineage A primary case around 25 November 2019 (95% HPD: 29 October–14 December). Sequence accessions, raw reads for re-examined genomes (`PRJNA806767`, `PRJNA802993`), BEAST XMLs, simulation outputs (Dryad), and analysis code (Zenodo) are publicly available.

This is a thorough and technically rigorous study whose forensic audit of putative C/C and T/T transitional genomes and non-reversible phylodynamic modeling of C-to-T reversion bias convincingly resolve why outgroup similarity alone misleads early SARS-CoV-2 rooting. The main methodological caveats lie in the epidemic simulation assumptions used to compute the Bayes factor ($\text{BF} \approx 60$) favoring two introductions over one: (1) the `FAVITES-COVID-Lite` simulations assume spatially homogeneous genomic ascertainment proportional to hospitalizations on a static network, whereas real-world December 2019–January 2020 sequencing was heavily enriched for targeted cluster investigations (specifically the Huanan market), which can inflate the apparent size of the lineage B polytomy; and (2) the Bayes factor formulation does not penalize the two-introduction hypothesis for the prior probability that an animal reservoir harboring both A and B (separated by two mutations) spills over both lineages within ~7 days without successfully transmitting intermediate C/C or T/T haplotypes. Testing spatially clustered ascertainment/superspreading in the single-introduction simulations and explicitly parameterizing within-reservoir diversity in the Bayes factor calculation can be done directly within the authors' existing simulation pipeline.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Claim 1: Putative intermediate C/C and T/T haplotypes sampled before mid-February 2020 are sequencing artifacts or later convergent mutations rather than ancestral transitional forms. [Well Supported]**  
  The authors inspect raw read depth, shared rare private mutations from co-sequenced lineage A/B samples, and primary laboratory records for all 20 early C/C and T/T genomes (Table S1, Fig. S1). Confirming $\le 10\times$ read depth or ambiguous base calls at positions 8782/28144 in the Korean, Singaporean, Wuhan, and Sichuan sequences—combined with clear phylogenetic evidence of later convergent C8782T/T28144C mutations in the Diamond Princess, New York, and San Diego clades—decisively refutes the claim that these 20 sequences represent an early human transitional population.

* **Claim 2: Revertant genomes within lineage A (such as WA1/A.1 and 20SF012) arose via C-to-T hypermutation rather than representing the ancestral SARS-CoV-2 haplotype. [Well Supported]**  
  By reconstructing the 15-region non-recombinant sarbecovirus ancestor (`recCA`, 381 substitutions from Hu-1), the authors show that 82.6% (19/23) of early mutations toward `recCA` are C-to-T transitions occurring at nearly three-fold higher density at `recCA`-divergent sites (6.04% vs. 2.17%). Incorporating these empirical C-to-T and G-to-T biases into a non-reversible random-effects substitution model in BEAST rejects A.1 and A+C29095T as the MRCA with $\text{BF} > 100\text{--}1000$ across unconstrained, no-market, and `recCA`-rooted analyses (Table 1).

* **Claim 3: The dual-polytomy phylogenetic structure of lineages A (35.2%) and B (64.8%) strongly favors at least two separate zoonotic introductions over a single introduction ($\text{BF} = 60.0\text{--}61.6$). [Moderately Supported]**  
  The incompatibility between the unconstrained molecular clock (which favors a lineage B root, $\text{BF} = 48.1$) and `recCA`-informed rooting (which favors a lineage A root, $77.3\%$) provides strong phylogenetic evidence that the A/B common ancestor predates human transmission. However, the exact magnitude of the simulation-derived Bayes factor ($\text{BF} \approx 60$) rests on two idealized assumptions in the `FAVITES-COVID-Lite` model. First, the simulations assume that sequenced genomes are sampled uniformly across ascertained hospitalizations on a single scale-free network, finding that a single introduction produces the observed two-clade topology in only $0.1\%$ (from C/C, Fig. 2B) or $0.5\%$ (from A or B, Fig. 2C) of runs. In reality, intensive targeted sampling and contact tracing centered on the Huanan market outbreak in late December 2019–January 2020 selectively amplified the representation of the market-associated lineage B polytomy. Second, the Bayes factor calculation assigns equal prior probability (0.5 vs. 0.5) to one vs. two successful introductions and treats the two introductions as independent draws of haplotype A and haplotype B without modeling the evolutionary waiting time or lack of C/C intermediate spillovers from the animal reservoir.

* **Claim 4: The primary human infection of lineage B occurred around 18 November 2019 (95% HPD: 23 October–8 December), with minimal cryptic circulation (<33 cumulative infections at tMRCA) prior to December 2019. [Well Supported]**  
  Coupling the BEAST tMRCA posterior for lineage B (median 15 December 2019) and lineage A (median 20 December 2019) with the SAPHIRE coalescent rejection sampler yields narrow, consistent primary-case windows across wide variations in doubling time ($1.35\text{--}5.44\text{ days}$), ascertainment rates, and index-case definitions (Tables S6–S10), and aligns with negative retrospective serology and ILI surveillance in Wuhan prior to December 2019.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Model Spatially Clustered Ascertainment and Market Superspreading in the Single-Introduction Simulations
* **WHY:** In `FAVITES-COVID-Lite`, genomes are sampled uniformly at random from ascertained cases across the 5-million-node network. However, early sequencing in Wuhan was heavily skewed toward patients and contacts linked to the Huanan market (all lineage B). If a single introduction of lineage A (or C/C) mutated to lineage B shortly before entering a high-contact market setting that was subsequently targeted for intensive sequencing, the probability of observing two large basal polytomies (Fig. 2B–C) could be substantially higher than the $0.1\%\text{--}0.5\%$ baseline.
* **WHAT:** Add a simulation sensitivity analysis that incorporates two-tier ascertainment (higher sequencing probability for a focal superspreading cluster vs. background community cases) under the single-introduction hypothesis.
* **HOW:** Using the existing `FAVITES-COVID-Lite` coalescent trees, designate the first node that triggers a high-degree hub transmission event (degree $\ge 50$) at two mutations from the root as a focal "market cluster," enrich taxon sampling from that clade by $3\times\text{--}5\times$ relative to background lineages during the first 3 weeks of sampling, and recompute the empirical frequencies of topologies 2B and 2C and the resulting Bayes factor.

#### 2. Incorporate Within-Reservoir Coalescent Priors into the Two-Introduction Bayes Factor
* **WHY:** The current Bayes factor calculation (Methods, line 175) assumes that under the two-introduction model, one introduction is deterministically or equiprobably lineage B and the other is lineage A, without accounting for the probability that two random draws from a shallow animal reservoir differ by exactly two mutations (C8782T and T28144C) while the ~6 extinct introductions (out of 8 total estimated introductions, line 133) leave no surviving intermediate C/C or T/T lineages.
* **WHAT:** Formulate an explicit within-reservoir population genetic prior for the haplotype composition (A, B, C/C, T/T) of spillover events as a function of reservoir effective population size ($N_e\tau$).
* **HOW:** Parameterize the probability that two successful independent spillovers separated by $\Delta t \approx 1\text{--}2\text{ weeks}$ draw distinct haplotypes A and B (rather than both drawing the dominant reservoir haplotype, e.g., both A or both B) across a grid of reservoir diversity values, and report how the Bayes factor for two distinct-haplotype introductions scales with the frequency of A vs. B in the reservoir.

#### 3. Clarify the Impact of the Unsequenced 10 December Vendor Case on Lineage B Primary Case Timing
* **WHY:** In the primary analysis (line 113), the earliest symptom onset constraint for lineage B (10 December, hospitalized 16 December) comes from a Huanan market seafood vendor for whom no viral genome was sequenced, relying on a lineage B environmental sample (`EPI_ISL_408512`) collected weeks later from the same stall.
* **WHAT:** Highlight in the main text (not only in Table S7) the exact shift in the lineage B primary case date and the A-vs.-B ordering probability ($64.6\%$, Fig. 3D) when conditioning strictly on the earliest *genomically confirmed* lineage B patient (onset 13 or 16 December, sequenced 24 December).
* **HOW:** Add one sentence in the *Timing the introductions of lineages A and B* section reporting the median primary case date and 95% HPD from Table S7 when restricting the index constraint to sequenced patients only.
