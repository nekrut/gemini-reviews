### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript applies a tumor-cell mutation-ordering algorithm (`SCITE`, referred to as the Mutational Order Approach, MOA) to binary presence/absence matrices of common single-nucleotide variants (SNVs with global frequency $>1\%$) across two 2020 GISAID snapshots of SARS-CoV-2 genomes (29,681 genomes through July 7, 2020 with 49 SNVs, and 68,057 genomes through October 12, 2020 with 84 SNVs), and tracks the resulting mutational fingerprints across 172,480 genomes through December 30, 2020 (`http://sars2evo.datamonkey.org/`). By polarizing the 49 SNVs against three distant animal sarbecovirus outgroups (`RaTG13`, `RmYN02`, and pangolin `MT121216.1`), the authors infer a putative progenitor genome (`proCoV2`) that differs from `Wuhan-Hu-1` at three positions ($\alpha_1$: 18060, $\alpha_2$: 8782, $\alpha_3$: 28144—corresponding to Pango lineage A.1 / WA1) and also place a three-mutation North American clade ($\mu_1$: 2416, $\mu_2$: 19524, $\mu_3$: 23929, termed the $\nu$ lineage) basal to `Wuhan-Hu-1`. From these 3–6 base differences, they conclude that `Wuhan-Hu-1` was a derived descendant, that `proCoV2` originated 5.8–8.1 weeks before December 24, 2019 (in late October 2019, with a speculated index case in September 2019), and that `proCoV2` circulated globally for months before and after the Wuhan outbreak.

While the browser-based clade-fingerprinting dashboard (`sars2evo.datamonkey.org`) is a convenient visualization utility for tracking common 2020 haplotype frequencies, the manuscript's central evolutionary conclusion—that `proCoV2` (lineage A.1) and the North American $\nu$ sublineage are ancestral to `Wuhan-Hu-1` and push the pandemic origin back to September–October 2019—is a methodological artifact of circular outgroup polarization combined with frequency-thresholded infinite-sites ordering. Specifically, because SARS-CoV-2 experiences strong host-driven C$\to$U hypermutation (accounting for >80% of reversions toward animal sarbecoviruses), derived C$\to$U mutations that arose in January–February 2020 within Lineage A in North America (`C18060U` in WA1/A.1, and `C2416U + C19524U + C23929U` in the North American A.1 sub-clade) reverted those sites to the `U` base present in `RaTG13`. Because the authors hard-coded the outgroup `U` state as ancestral (`0`) and the `Wuhan-Hu-1` `C` state as derived (`1`), and because `SCITE` places high-frequency `"1"` alleles (91–98.6% global frequency) near the root of a directed mutation tree starting from the `"all-0"` vector, `SCITE` was mathematically forced to place the February 2020 North American revertants at the root of the entire pandemic. Correcting this requires replacing naive outgroup-consensus binary coding with a non-reversible nucleotide substitution model that accounts for C$\to$U reversion bias and sampling dates, which overturns the `proCoV2` and $\nu$-lineage rooting claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Claim 1: The Mutational Order Approach (MOA / `SCITE`) reconstructs the SARS-CoV-2 progenitor (`proCoV2`), which differs from `Wuhan-Hu-1` by three mutations ($\alpha_1$: U18060C, $\alpha_2$: U8782C, $\alpha_3$: C28144U) and gave rise to an even earlier-diverging North American $\nu$ lineage ($\mu_1$: U2416C, $\mu_2$: U19524C, $\mu_3$: U23929C). [Methodologically Flawed]**  
  `SCITE` (Jahn et al. 2016) is a single-cell tumor phylogeny algorithm that takes a binary $0/1$ matrix where `0` is the known normal somatic reference state and `1` is a derived somatic mutation, assuming an infinite-sites model where the root is by definition the `"all-0"` genotype and ancestral mutations have higher `"1"` frequencies than their descendants. In *Materials and Methods* (line 190), the authors coded every site matching the `RaTG13`/`RmYN02`/pangolin consensus as `0` (ancestral) and the alternate human allele as `1` (derived), and filtered the alignment to variants with $>1\%$ global frequency in July 2020. Because SARS-CoV-2 undergoes rampant APOBEC-mediated C$\to$U transitions, any derived sub-clade within Lineage A (`U8782, C28144`) that acquired C$\to$U mutations at sites where `RaTG13` happens to carry a `U`—namely `C18060U` (Lineage A.1, which seeded the heavily sequenced Washington State outbreak, reaching 4.9% of GISAID) and `C2416U + C19524U + C23929U` (a North American offshoot reaching ~1.5% of GISAID)—was assigned `"0"` (ancestral) at those sites, while the remaining 95.1%–98.6% of global SARS-CoV-2 genomes (including all December 2019 Wuhan genomes) were assigned `"1"` (derived)! Feeding a binary matrix where $\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3$ have $98.1\%\text{--}98.6\%$ `"1"`s and $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$ have $90.8\%\text{--}95.1\%$ `"1"`s into `SCITE` deterministically forces the `"all-0"` vector to be the root and forces the mutation order $\mu_{1..3} \to \alpha_1 \to \alpha_2 \to \alpha_3 \to \text{Wuhan-Hu-1}$. The result is not an empirical discovery of an ancient progenitor; it is a circular consequence of mis-polarizing C$\to$U reversions in heavily sequenced North American clades as ancestral `"0"` states.

* **Claim 2: The inferred mutation order is independently validated by the temporal timestamps of first variant appearance (47 of 49 mutations having $t_f(j) \ge t_f(i)$). [Methodologically Flawed]**  
  In Table 1 (lines 55–64) and *Materials and Methods* ("Temporal Concordance", chunk 2, line 22), the authors define the timestamp $t_f$ of a mutation as the collection date of the earliest genome carrying the `"1"` allele. Because `Wuhan-Hu-1` and the 18 earliest Chinese genomes sampled on December 24–30, 2019 carry the `"1"` allele (`C` at 2416, 19524, 23929, 18060, 8782 and `U` at 28144), Table 1 assigns `Time (days) = 0` to all six basal mutations ($\mu_1, \mu_2, \mu_3, \alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$)! In reality, the genomes carrying the putative ancestral `"0"` states at $\alpha_1$ (`U18060`, `proCoV2`) and $\mu_1\text{--}\mu_3$ ($\nu$ lineage) were only sampled **12 to 59 days later** (in mid-January and late February 2020 in Washington State and outside Hubei, line 119). Scoring `Time = 0` for the `"1"` allele when the `"1"` allele is present in 100% of Day-0 genomes while the putative ancestral `"0"` state does not appear until weeks or months later reverses the chronological arrow and creates a tautological "concordance" ($0 \ge 0$) at the base of the tree.

* **Claim 3: Post-hoc reversal of `0/1` coding for low-COI sites (such as position 3037) corrects outgroup homoplasy without biasing the root. [Methodologically Flawed]**  
  In *Materials and Methods* (line 194 and chunk 2, lines 1–4), the authors note that when outgroup polarization yielded a Co-Occurrence Index (COI) $<70\%$—as occurred for `C3037U` ($\beta_1$, which co-occurs with `D614G` on the Lineage B background, giving COI $= 60\%$ under outgroup `U=0` coding)—they manually reversed the coding to `C=0, U=1` (raising COI to $100\%$). However, when the exact same type of C$\to$U mutation (`C18060U`, `C2416U`, `C19524U`, `C23929U`) occurred on the *Lineage A* (`U8782, C28144`) background, it was nested within the outgroup-matching Lineage A state and therefore automatically had COI $>84\%$ without reversal! Thus, the authors' COI filter selectively corrected C$\to$U reversions that occurred in Lineage B (`3037`) while preserving and mis-rooting on every C$\to$U reversion that occurred in Lineage A (`18060`, `2416`, `19524`, `23929`).

* **Claim 4: Because `proCoV2` differs from `Wuhan-1` by three bases, `proCoV2` circulated in late October 2019 (5.8–8.1 weeks before December 24, 2019), with a putative index case in September 2019 and pre-December circulation in Italy. [Overstated]**  
  Even setting aside the erroneous root polarization, dividing a point count of $k = 3$ substitutions by a clock rate ($6.64\times 10^{-4}\text{--}9.27\times 10^{-4}\text{ substitutions/site/year}$) to claim a 5.8–8.1 week divergence interval ignores the massive Poisson variance of small mutation counts ($\text{Var}(k) = 3$) and substitutes a point-rate division for a formal coalescent model. Furthermore, citing a 409-bp Italian Spike RT-PCR amplicon as "confirming the existence of proCoV2 before the first coronavirus detection in China" (line 134) contradicts the immediately preceding sentence acknowledging that the 409-bp fragment spans *none* of the 49 diagnostic positions and cannot distinguish `proCoV2`, `Wuhan-Hu-1`, or any other SARS-CoV-2 lineage.

* **Claim 5: A McDonald–Kreitman test comparing human polymorphisms (>1% frequency) against divergence from bat `RaTG13` demonstrates altered selective regimes ($P \ll 0.01$). [Methodologically Flawed]**  
  Comparing $P_N/P_S = 32/17 = 1.88$ among variants $>1\%$ frequency in an exponentially expanding human pandemic against $D_N/D_S = 170/958 = 0.18$ between human SARS-CoV-2 and bat `RaTG13` (chunk 2, line 46) violates the assumptions of the McDonald–Kreitman test: (1) `RaTG13` is separated by ~1,100 substitutions (~15–20% synonymous divergence with regional recombination), where synonymous sites have experienced multiple hits and strong purifying selection over decades in bats; and (2) truncating human polymorphisms to global frequencies $>1\%$ during a rapid pandemic wave samples founder/hitchhiking alleles rather than a stationary site-frequency spectrum.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Outgroup-Consensus Binary Polarization with a Non-Reversible Substitution Model Accounting for C$\to$U Hypermutation
* **WHY:** Coding any human allele that matches `RaTG13`/`RmYN02`/`MT121216.1` (~1,100 mutations distant) as ancestral (`0`) ignores the >3-fold excess of C$\to$U host RNA-editing transitions in SARS-CoV-2, causing derived C$\to$U revertants in Lineage A (`C18060U`, `C2416U`, `C19524U`, `C23929U`) to be inverted into false ancestral nodes.
* **WHAT:** Test the directionality of $\alpha_1$ (`18060`) and $\mu_1\text{--}\mu_3$ (`2416, 19524, 23929`) using a phylogenetic likelihood model that incorporates empirical SARS-CoV-2 substitution asymmetries (specifically the elevated C$\to$U rate) and tip sampling dates, rather than fixing `RaTG13` states as `0` in `SCITE`.
* **HOW:** Compare the log-likelihood (and AIC, as the authors already did for site 28657 in chunk 2, line 3) of coding `18060`, `2416`, `19524`, and `23929` with `C=0, U=1` vs. `U=0, C=1` under a directional nucleotide substitution model (e.g., in `IQ-TREE` or `HyPhy` with non-reversible rate matrix $Q$ estimated on the 29KG tree) and verify that `C18060U` and `C2416U/C19524U/C23929U` are derived C$\to$U changes nested within Lineage A rather than ancestors of `Wuhan-Hu-1`.

#### 2. Correct the Temporal Concordance Definition to Test the First Appearance of the Ancestral (`0`) vs. Derived (`1`) State
* **WHY:** Defining the timestamp $t_f$ of a mutation solely by when the `"1"` state was first sampled assigns `Day 0` (December 24, 2019) to every site where `Wuhan-Hu-1` has `"1"`, masking the fact that the putative ancestral `"0"` haplotypes (`proCoV2` and $\nu$) did not appear until January and February 2020.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate temporal concordance by comparing the earliest sampling date of each node's actual multi-locus haplotype (the ancestor carrying `"0"` vs. the descendant carrying `"1"`).
* **HOW:** Add a column to Table 1 reporting the earliest collection date of a genome assigned to the *parent* haplotype (`"0"` state at the focal mutation, `"1"` at preceding mutations) alongside the earliest collection date of the *descendant* haplotype (`"1"` state). This will immediately reveal the 12-to-59-day temporal inversion at $\mu_1\text{--}\mu_3$ and $\alpha_1$.

#### 3. Account for Spatiotemporal Sequencing Ascertainment and Poisson Uncertainty in Divergence Timing
* **WHY:** In `SCITE`, variant frequency in the sample matrix determines node height, but GISAID genome counts in 2020 were heavily skewed toward specific countries (e.g., the UK and Washington State, USA), inflating the global frequency of the Washington State A.1 (`C18060U`) clade above the 1% cutoff. Moreover, dividing $k=3$ mutations by a substitution rate without Poisson confidence intervals understates timing uncertainty.
* **WHAT:** Perform geographic/temporal downsampling of the 29KG dataset and compute exact Poisson credible intervals for mutation-count divergence times.
* **HOW:** Subsample the 29KG dataset to equal numbers of genomes per country/month (or weight genomes inversely by regional sequencing intensity) before applying the $>1\%$ variant filter, and compute the 95% gamma/Poisson confidence interval for the time required to accumulate $k$ mutations at rate $r \in [6.64, 9.27] \times 10^{-4}\text{ s/s/y}$.

#### 4. Temper Unsupported Speculations on September–October 2019 Origins and Remove the Uninformative 409-bp Fragment Citation
* **WHY:** Claiming in the Abstract and main text that the progenitor spread worldwide months before December 2019 (back to September–October 2019) and citing a 409-bp Italian Spike fragment that covers zero informative SNVs overreaches the data.
* **WHAT:** Reframe the paper around what `MOA` and the `sars2evo.datamonkey.org` tool reliably accomplish—clade fingerprinting and spatiotemporal tracking of high-frequency 2020 variants—and remove or caveat the pre-December 2019 origin claims.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract, *The Progenitor Genome and the Index Case*, and *Estimated Timing of MRCA and the Index Case* sections to acknowledge that outgroup-based binary polarization cannot distinguish an ancestral `U` state from a derived C$\to$U reversion in Lineage A.1, and delete the claim that the 409-bp Italian PCR fragment "can only confirm the existence of proCoV2" (line 134).

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Several sample-size numbers and methodological descriptions are inconsistent across sections.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Sample Size Discrepancy (Main Text vs. Materials and Methods):** Line 31 and line 178 state that the 29KG dataset contains `29,681` genomes, whereas line 192 and chunk 2 lines 1, 3, and 17 repeatedly write `29,861` genomes (transposing `68` and `86`). Similarly, chunk 2 line 44 refers to the `68KG` dataset (`68,057` genomes) as `64KG` twice (*"both 29KG and 64KG analyses"*). Standardize the exact genome counts throughout.
  * **Bootstrap Grouping Disclosure (Materials and Methods, chunk 2, line 17):** Clearly state in the main text (line 99)—not only in the Methods—that the underlined `>95%` bootstrap confidence levels in Figure 2 were computed after post-hoc collapsing $\beta_1\text{--}\beta_3$, $\epsilon_1\text{--}\epsilon_3$, and $\alpha_{1a}\text{--}\alpha_{1d}$ into single composite nodes because their internal ordering had low bootstrap support.
