### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript retrospectively screens stored plasma samples from 959 asymptomatic heavy smokers (aged 55–65 years, 92.9% with $\ge 30$ pack-years) enrolled across Italy between September 2019 and March 2020 in the prospective SMILE lung cancer screening trial. Using a proprietary, in-house receptor-binding domain (RBD) ELISA (`VM-IgG-RBD` and `VM-IgM-RBD`) and a qualitative cytopathic effect (CPE) microneutralization assay, the authors report anti-SARS-CoV-2 RBD antibodies in 111 of 959 individuals (11.6%), including 23/162 (14.2%) in September 2019 and 27/166 (16.3%) in October 2019, with 6 of the 111 RBD-positive samples scoring positive in qualitative microneutralization. Raw optical density (OD) values, dilution titers, and assay validation data are not publicly deposited ("available upon request").

The central conclusion—that SARS-CoV-2 was already circulating widely across 13 Italian regions in September–October 2019 at 14–16% seroprevalence—is fundamentally invalid and rests on uncalibrated false-positive IgM reactivity and a base-rate fallacy in geographic sampling. First, the study includes no pre-pandemic negative control cohort (e.g., 2016–2018 sera) to establish assay specificity, and 94.6% (105/111) of the ELISA-positive samples failed to neutralize live virus. Second, the serological time series violates basic viral immunology: despite an alleged 14.2–16.3% IgM-driven prevalence in September–October 2019, IgG seroprevalence remained flat at background noise levels (0.9–2.4%) across all six months, 109 of 111 positive samples were positive for only a single isotype, and overall seropositivity dropped to 2.8% in January 2020. Third, the claim that antibody positivity clustered in Lombardy (53.2% of positive cases, mirroring Lombardy's 57.1% share of official March 2020 COVID-19 cases) is a pure artifact of cohort recruitment: 51.2% (491/959) of all trial participants were recruited in Lombardy, yielding a per-participant positivity rate in Lombardy (59/491 = 12.0%) that is statistically indistinguishable from the rest of Italy (52/468 = 11.1%, $p = 0.66$). Addressing these fatal flaws requires blinded re-testing with orthogonal antigens (full-length Spike, Nucleocapsid) alongside pre-2018 negative controls, quantitative plaque-reduction neutralization titers ($\text{PRNT}_{50}/\text{PRNT}_{90}$), and retracting the unsupported claims of September 2019 pandemic circulation.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **SARS-CoV-2 circulated in 14.2%–16.3% of asymptomatic individuals in Italy in September–October 2019: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Table 1, 97 of the 111 positive calls (87.4%) are driven by IgM (`VM-IgM-RBD`), whereas IgG positivity is only 1.7% overall (1.9% in September, 2.4% in October, 1.1% in November, 2.0% in December, 0.9% in January, and 1.9% in February), and in five of the six months (September, October, November, January, February) zero individuals were positive for both IgG and IgM ($3+20=23$, $4+23=27$, $3+23=26$, $1+2=3$, $2+19=21$). If 14–16% of the cohort had been infected in September–October 2019, class-switching over 14–21 days would have produced a cumulative IgG seroprevalence of $\ge 15\text{–}30\%$ by November 2019–February 2020 rather than a flat 0.9–2.4% baseline, and prevalence would not have decayed to 2.8% in January 2020. Furthermore, a 14–16% prevalence in autumn 2019 is nearly six-fold higher than the 2.5% national seroprevalence measured by the Italian Ministry of Health ($n = 64,660$) *after* the devastating spring 2020 wave (cited by the authors on page 4). In a cohort of older heavy smokers with chronic airway inflammation, ~10% isolated IgM reactivity in an uncalibrated ELISA reflects false-positive background and cross-reactivity (e.g., rheumatoid factor or endemic human coronaviruses OC43, HKU1, 229E, and NL63).

* **The proprietary RBD-based ELISA is a valid, specific surrogate for live-virus neutralization in this cohort: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The authors state that "an excellent correlation between the neutralization titer and the IgG, IgM, and immunoglobulin A ELISA response against the RBD of the S-protein was observed, confirming that the RBD-based ELISA can be used as a valid surrogate for neutralization." Yet their own results directly contradict this assertion: when all 111 RBD-ELISA-positive plasma samples were tested in the CPE microneutralization assay, **only 6 of 111 (5.4%) scored positive**, meaning **94.6% (105/111) had no detectable neutralizing activity**. Even for the 6 qualitative microneutralization positives, no dilution titers ($\text{ID}_{50}$ or $\text{IC}_{50}$), serum toxicity controls, or blinded pre-2019 negative control sera are reported.

* **The geographic distribution of RBD-positive individuals closely mirrors the official regional incidence of COVID-19 in Italy, with the highest burden (53.2%) in Lombardy: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The authors emphasize that 59 of the 111 positive cases (53.2%) resided in Lombardy, comparing this to Lombardy's 57.1% share (5,791/10,141) of official Italian COVID-19 cases up to March 10, 2020 (Figure 2A). However, as reported in the same paragraph, **491 of the 959 enrolled participants (51.2%) were residents of Lombardy**. Consequently, the seropositivity rate inside Lombardy is $59 / 491 = 12.02\%$ (95% CI: $9.3\%\text{–}15.2\%$), and the seropositivity rate across the remaining 19 Italian regions is $52 / 468 = 11.11\%$ (95% CI: $8.4\%\text{–}14.3\%$). A two-sided Fisher's exact test or chi-square test between Lombardy and non-Lombardy participants yields $p = 0.69$ (odds ratio $= 1.09$). The apparent geographic concentration in Lombardy is entirely an artifact of recruiting half the cohort in Lombardy; the underlying per-capita positivity rate is spatially uniform across Italy, consistent with a constant ~11–12% assay false-positive rate.

* **Early widespread circulation in autumn 2019 occurred unnoticed in a less symptomatic form before the Wuhan outbreak: [Overstated].**  
  The SMILE cohort consists exclusively of 55–65-year-old individuals with $\ge 30$ pack-years of smoking history—a high-risk demographic for severe COVID-19 pneumonia. An epidemic infecting 14–16% of this demographic across 13 Italian regions in September–October 2019 without generating severe respiratory clusters, ICU admissions, or durable IgG antibodies is incompatible with known SARS-CoV-2 clinical severity and molecular clock estimates placing the global ancestor of SARS-CoV-2 in late 2019.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Establish Assay Specificity Using Blinded Pre-Pandemic (2015–2018) Negative Control Sera
* **WHY:** When estimating seroprevalence in a pre-pandemic window where true prevalence is near zero, any assay with $< 99.5\%$ specificity will be dominated by false positives. By Rogan-Gladen adjustment ($p_{\text{true}} = \frac{p_{\text{obs}} + \text{Sp} - 1}{\text{Se} + \text{Sp} - 1}$), an observed positivity of $p_{\text{obs}} = 11.6\%$ is completely explained by an assay specificity of $\text{Sp} = 88.4\%$.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate the `VM-IgG-RBD` and `VM-IgM-RBD` assays on a matched panel of at least 300 archived plasma samples collected in 2015–2018 from heavy smokers, and apply Rogan-Gladen correction to all monthly prevalence estimates in Table 1.
* **HOW:** Assay pre-2018 archived lung-screening plasma samples in blinded, randomized plate order alongside the 2019–2020 SMILE samples. Report the exact empirical false-positive rate ($1 - \text{Sp}$) for IgM and IgG separately, set the OD cutoff at $\ge \text{mean} + 3\text{–}5\,\text{SD}$ of the pre-2018 smoking controls (targeting $\ge 99.5\%$ specificity), and recompute Table 1 with 95% confidence intervals accounting for specificity uncertainty.

#### 2. Perform Orthogonal Multi-Antigen Confirmation and Quantitative Neutralization Titration
* **WHY:** Single-antigen RBD IgM ELISAs are prone to non-specific binding, and 94.6% of the RBD-positive samples failed qualitative live-virus neutralization.
* **WHAT:** Require orthogonal confirmation against full-length Spike (S1/S2) and Nucleocapsid (N) proteins, as well as quantitative neutralization titers ($\text{PRNT}_{50}$ or $\text{IC}_{50} \ge 1:20$), before classifying any pre-pandemic sample as seropositive.
* **HOW:** Re-test all 111 ELISA-positive samples and a random subset of 111 ELISA-negative samples using commercial, FDA/CE-validated IgG and IgM assays targeting SARS-CoV-2 Nucleocapsid and trimeric Spike, plus seasonal human coronavirus (OC43, HKU1, 229E, NL63) Spike proteins to quantify cross-reactivity. For the microneutralization assay, report the exact serial two-fold dilution curve (from 1:10 to 1:640) for all 111 samples and pre-2018 controls.

#### 3. Correct the Geographic Analysis for Regional Cohort Recruitment Denominators
* **WHY:** Comparing the regional numerator of positive SMILE subjects (59/111 = 53.2% in Lombardy) to national COVID-19 case shares without dividing by the regional SMILE recruitment denominator (491/959 = 51.2% in Lombardy) creates a misleading visual and statistical correlation in Figure 2.
* **WHAT:** Replace raw positive counts in Figure 2 and the main text with region-specific and province-specific seroprevalence proportions ($n_{\text{pos}, i} / N_{\text{recruited}, i}$) and exact binomial 95% confidence intervals.
* **HOW:** In Supplementary Tables S3 and S4 and Figure 2A–B, compute the regional prevalence rate for Lombardy ($59/491 = 12.0\%$) versus non-Lombardy regions ($52/468 = 11.1\%$) and test for spatial heterogeneity using a permutation or Fisher's exact test. Explicitly acknowledge in the Results and Abstract that per-subject seropositivity did not significantly differ between Lombardy and the rest of Italy.

#### 4. Reconcile Longitudinal IgG Kinetics and Reframe the Manuscript's Claims
* **WHY:** The absence of IgG accumulation from September 2019 (1.9%) to February 2020 (1.9%) and the drop in total positivity from October 2019 (16.3%) to January 2020 (2.8%) are incompatible with true SARS-CoV-2 transmission.
* **WHAT:** Test longitudinal follow-up blood draws from the 97 IgM-positive individuals in the SMILE trial to verify whether IgG seroconversion ever occurred, and remove claims that the data establish September 2019 SARS-CoV-2 circulation.
* **HOW:** Because SMILE is a prospective screening trial with longitudinal follow-up, assay subsequent plasma timepoints from the September–November 2019 IgM-positive subjects to determine whether any developed durable anti-RBD and anti-Nucleocapsid IgG. Temper the title, abstract, and discussion to reflect that unconfirmed IgM reactivity in autumn 2019 likely represents cross-reactive or non-specific antibody binding unless confirmed by seroconversion and neutralization.

#### 5. Technical and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Numerical discrepancies between the text and figure captions and inaccurate biosafety descriptions reduce technical reliability.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Page 3 (Main text vs. Figure 2A caption):** The main text states "5791/10,141 (57.1%) patients with COVID-19," whereas the Figure 2A caption states "The national distribution includes 10,149 patients with COVID-19." Reconcile the denominator (`10,141` vs. `10,149`).
  * **Page 3 (Paragraph 4):** The text states that the live-virus CPE microneutralization assay was performed "in a high-containment biosafety level 2 laboratory." Propagating live SARS-CoV-2 for CPE neutralization requires Biosafety Level 3 (BSL-3) containment; clarify whether a BSL-3 facility was used or whether a pseudotyped lentivirus/VSV assay was performed at BSL-2.
  * **Data Availability:** Deposit the anonymized patient-level table containing collection dates, province, smoking pack-years, raw `VM-IgG-RBD` and `VM-IgM-RBD` optical density values, cutoff thresholds, and microneutralization titers.
