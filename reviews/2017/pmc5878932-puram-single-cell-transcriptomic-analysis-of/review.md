### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents a single-cell transcriptomic and functional dissection of primary tumors and matched lymph node (LN) metastases in human head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). Using full-length SMART-Seq2 scRNA-seq across 5,902 quality-controlled cells (2,215 malignant and 3,363 non-malignant stromal and immune cells) from 18 HPV-negative oral cavity HNSCC patients (including five matched primary–LN pairs), whole-exome sequencing (WES), immunohistochemistry (IHC), in vitro perturbations in SCC9 cells, and bulk TCGA RNA-seq deconvolution, the authors characterize both inter- and intra-tumoral heterogeneity. Non-negative matrix factorization (NNMF) across the 10 most deeply sampled tumors identifies seven recurrent intra-tumoral malignant expression programs: two cell-cycle programs, stress/immediate-early response, hypoxia, two epithelial differentiation programs, and a partial epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (p-EMT) program characterized by extracellular matrix and mesenchymal genes (*TGFBI*, *LAMC2*, *LAMB3*, *PDPN*, *VIM*, *ITGA5*) alongside retained epithelial marker expression and absence of canonical EMT transcription factors (except *SNAI2*, which does not covary across single cells within a tumor). Using IHC, ligand–receptor analysis, FACS repopulation, and CRISPR-Cas9 knockout/overexpression of *TGFBI*, the authors show that p-EMT cells localize to the invasive leading edge of tumor nests adjacent to cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and are dynamically induced by TGF$\beta$ signaling. Finally, deconvolving bulk TCGA HNSCC profiles reveals that the canonical TCGA "Mesenchymal" subtype is driven by high CAF and myocyte admixture within basal tumors ("malignant-basal"), and that malignant-specific p-EMT scores independently predict nodal metastasis, higher pathological grade, extracapsular extension, and lymphovascular invasion. Raw sequencing data (dbGaP: phs001474.v1.p1), processed expression matrices (GEO: GSE103322), and analysis scripts (GitHub: `Trinity_CTAT`) are publicly available.

This is a thorough, multi-modal study that resolves a long-standing question in epithelial cancer genomics—demonstrating that bulk "mesenchymal" tumor subtypes reflect stromal fibroblast infiltration while a distinct, metastable p-EMT state at the tumor–stromal interface tracks locoregional metastasis. The conclusions are well supported across orthogonal experimental systems, and the Discussion candidly notes key limitations (such as the 10-tumor deep scRNA-seq sample size and the absence of p-EMT in classical/atypical subtypes that still metastasize). A few statistical and modeling refinements—specifically regarding log-scale linear regression in the bulk deconvolution model, extreme-quantile dichotomization of TCGA tumors, and reporting biological-replicate variance rather than single-experiment technical error bars in functional assays—would further strengthen the quantitative presentation.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Malignant HNSCC cells exhibit recurrent intra-tumoral expression heterogeneity across seven meta-programs, including an in vivo partial EMT (p-EMT) state that retains epithelial markers and lacks canonical EMT transcription factors except *SNAI2* (Results lines 106–125, Figure 3A–C, Figures S3–S4): [Well Supported].**  
  Malignant cells are rigorously separated from non-malignant cells via three concordant criteria (sliding-window chromosomal CNV inference validated against WES, a 14-gene epithelial score, and global clustering). The NNMF meta-programs are validated across parameter ranges ($k=5\text{--}15$) and against 10,000 expression-matched permutation control gene sets. One technical caveat noted in STAR Methods (lines 161–163) is that two reverse transcriptases (SuperScript II and Maxima) were used, and five malignant clusters (including MEEI28, processed solely with Maxima) were sequenced with only one enzyme; however, the intra-tumoral NNMF programs are computed *within* each tumor and are therefore unaffected by inter-tumor enzyme differences.

* **p-EMT cells spatially localize to the leading edge of primary and metastatic tumor nests in proximity to CAFs and respond dynamically to TGF$\beta$–TGFBI signaling in vitro (Results lines 127–147, Figures 3D–H and 4, Figures S4–S5): [Well Supported].**  
  Multi-marker IHC across multiple tumors confirms peripheral localization of p63$^+$/PDPN$^+$/LAMB3$^+$/LAMC2$^+$/TGFBI$^+$ cells at the tumor–stroma interface vs. SPRR1B$^+$/CLDN4$^+$ epithelial differentiation at the core. FACS sorting of $\text{TGFBI}^{\text{high}}$ and $\text{TGFBI}^{\text{low}}$ SCC9 cells shows plastic re-equilibration within 4–7 days, and TGF$\beta$3 stimulation, pathway inhibition, *TGFBI* overexpression, and CRISPR-Cas9 *TGFBI* knockout (>98.8% indels) consistently modulate Matrigel invasion and proliferation. However, the bar plots in Figures 3E, 3F, 4I, and S4F–G display SEM and ANOVA/$t$-test $p$-values from a single "representative experiment" ($n=3\text{--}4$ technical wells) rather than across the $n=3\text{--}4$ independent biological experiments, and Figure 4H applies a two-sample $t$-test across individual cells ($p < 10^{-16}$) without accounting for culture-well clustering.

* **The TCGA "Mesenchymal" HNSCC subtype reflects elevated CAF and myocyte abundance within basal tumors ("malignant-basal") rather than an intrinsic mesenchymal state of malignant cells (Results lines 166–181, Figure 6, Figure S7A–D): [Well Supported].**  
  Single-cell subtype scoring shows that no malignant cells map to the TCGA Mesenchymal signature, whereas CAFs, myofibroblasts, and myocytes strongly express it; blinded pathological review of 203 TCGA H&E slides independently confirms ~2.7-fold higher stromal content in Mesenchymal vs. Basal TCGA tumors ($p < 0.0001$).

* **Deconvolved malignant-specific p-EMT scores in TCGA malignant-basal tumors independently predict nodal metastasis, lymphovascular invasion, and extracapsular extension (Results lines 183–196, Figure 7, Figure S7E–J): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Both univariate hypergeometric comparisons (Figure 7C) and bivariate binomial logistic regressions controlling for CAF score and $\text{p-EMT} \times \text{CAF}$ interaction (Figure S7I) support the association of p-EMT with nodal stage ($N+$), extracapsular extension, and lymphovascular invasion. Two methodological choices slightly weaken the analysis: (1) in STAR Methods ("Inferring Cancer-cell Specific Expression"), bulk deconvolution regresses log-transformed expression $Ex(i,g)$ linearly on estimated cell-type scores $Fr_t(i)$ and restricts predictors $T_g$ via a hard 2-fold expression cutoff, whereas transcript mixing in bulk RNA-seq is additive on the linear TPM scale; and (2) in Figure 7C ("p-EMT Stratification of TCGA samples"), tumors were ranked by $(\text{p-EMT} - \text{Epi.Diff.2})$ rather than p-EMT alone, the middle 20% of tumors were discarded to compare top 40% vs. bottom 40%, and unadjusted $p < 0.05$ hypergeometric tests were reported across seven clinical features.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report Biological-Replicate Summary Statistics and Account for Cell Clustering in In Vitro Assays
* **WHY:** Reporting error bars (SEM) and ANOVA/$t$-test $p$-values from technical wells of a single "representative experiment" (Figures 3E, 3F, 4I, S4F–G, S5N, S5P; STAR Methods lines 222, 227) underestimates inter-experiment variability, and testing single cells as independent units in Figure 4H ($t$-test $p < 10^{-16}$) inflates degrees of freedom.
* **WHAT:** Aggregate technical replicate wells within each independent experiment ($n=3$ or $n=4$) and compute mean $\pm$ SEM and $t$-test/ANOVA $p$-values across the independent biological experiments; for Figure 4H, add a permutation or mixed-effects test at the replicate/batch level.
* **HOW:** Using the existing Matrigel invasion and CellTiter-Glo data from all $n=3\text{--}4$ independent runs, plot the experiment-level means as individual points overlaid on the bar graphs in Figures 3E–F and 4I, and report the biological-replicate ANOVA/paired $t$-test $p$-values in the legends.

#### 2. Verify TCGA Clinical Associations Using Continuous p-EMT Scores Without Middle-Quintile Exclusion
* **WHY:** In Figure 7C, ranking malignant-basal TCGA tumors by the composite difference $(\text{p-EMT} - \text{Epi.Diff.2})$ and dropping the middle 20% of patients (comparing top 40% vs. bottom 40%, STAR Methods page 45) discards information and conflates high p-EMT with low epithelial differentiation.
* **WHAT:** Test the continuous p-EMT score (and continuous Epi.Diff.2 score separately) across all $n=225$ malignant-basal TCGA tumors without excluding the middle 20%, and report Benjamini–Hochberg FDR-adjusted $p$-values across the seven clinical endpoints.
* **HOW:** In the logistic regression framework already implemented in `fitglm` for Figure S7I, fit models on all 225 malignant-basal tumors using continuous `p-EMT`, `Epi.Diff.2`, and `CAF` scores, report odds ratios, 95% confidence intervals, and BH-adjusted $q$-values for the seven clinical features in Table S7 or Figure 7C, and verify that linear-scale (TPM) vs. log-scale residual deconvolution yields concordant tumor rankings.

#### 3. Editorial Cleanup of Duplicate Supplementary Figure Legends

* **WHAT & HOW:** Remove the duplicated block of Supplementary Figure legends (lines 174–298 at the end of the manuscript) and fix the minor patient ID typo on line 70 of STAR Methods (`"MEE22 77/M"` $\to$ `"MEEI22 77/M"`).
