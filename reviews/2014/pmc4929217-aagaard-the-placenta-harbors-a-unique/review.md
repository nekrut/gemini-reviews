### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study characterizes the putative bacterial microbiome of human placental parenchymal specimens collected at delivery from 320 subjects in a population-based case-cohort design (`PeriBank`), using V1–V3 16S rDNA 454 pyrosequencing ($n = 320$) and nested whole-genome shotgun (WGS) Illumina HiSeq sequencing ($n = 48$). Comparing placental taxonomic and `HUMAnN` metabolic profiles against non-pregnant reference body sites (oral, skin, nasal, vaginal, and stool) from the Human Microbiome Project (HMP), the authors conclude that the normal human placenta harbors a unique, metabolically active microbiome dominated by *Escherichia coli* and oral-like commensals (*Prevotella tannerae*, *Neisseria*, *Fusobacteria*, *Tenericutes*), with phylum-level Bray–Curtis dissimilarity $< 0.3$ to the oral microbiome. Using UniFrac PERMANOVA, `LEfSe`, and `Boruta` feature selection, they further report that placental microbiome composition is significantly associated with preterm birth ($<37$ weeks, $P = 0.001$; enriched for *Burkholderia*) and remote first- or second-trimester maternal antenatal infection ($P = 0.006$; enriched for *Streptococcus*, *Acinetobacter*, *Thioalkalivibrio*, and *Pseudoalteromonas*). Raw 16S and WGS reads are deposited in SRA (`PRJNA238913`) and dbGaP.

While the clinical cohort (`PeriBank`, $n = 320$) is well annotated and the question of intrauterine microbial seeding is clinically important, the study's core conclusions are undermined by three major methodological flaws: (1) unsequenced/unsubtracted DNA extraction and library reagent contamination ("kitome") in an ultra-low-biomass tissue ($>99\%$ human DNA), (2) an asymmetric bioinformatics comparison that runs lax `MG-RAST` BLASTX matching on placental WGS reads while comparing them against `MetaPhlAn` marker-gene profiles from HMP body sites at the coarse phylum level, and (3) biological implausibility of the top identified taxa. Specifically, finding *Escherichia coli* as the most abundant resident species across normal term placentas without inflammation, alongside soil/kit contaminants (*Burkholderia*, *Paenibacillus*, *Acinetobacter*), an obligate soda-lake haloalkaliphile (*Thioalkalivibrio*), and a marine bacterium (*Pseudoalteromonas*), is a hallmark of reagent contamination and permissive database matching rather than a physiological placental microbiota. Re-analyzing the WGS reads alongside sequenced extraction/library negative controls using a uniform, stringent taxonomic profiler is required to determine whether any endogenous microbial signal exceeds background noise.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Healthy and complicated human placentas harbor a unique, resident commensal microbiome dominated by *Escherichia coli*: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  As noted in the Materials and Methods (line 143), $>99\%$ of placental WGS reads are human. In such ultra-low-biomass extracts, trace bacterial DNA present in `MO-BIO PowerSoil` extraction columns, PCR enzymes, and Illumina library reagents dominates the non-human read fraction. Although one non-template blank extraction was performed per 11 samples (line 131), the authors state only that *"no 16S sequences were generated from nontemplate controls (fig. S4)"* based on gel electrophoresis/amplification threshold, and they neither present nor subtract sequenced WGS or 16S negative-control profiles. Recombinant *E. coli* DNA is the most ubiquitous contaminant in molecular biology enzymes and reagents, and live *E. coli* colonization of the placental parenchyma in healthy term pregnancies without chorioamnionitis is incompatible with the potent endotoxic inflammatory response triggered by *E. coli* lipopolysaccharide at the maternal–fetal interface.

* **The placental microbiome is taxonomically most similar to the human oral microbiome (phylum-level Bray–Curtis dissimilarity $< 0.3$; `SourceTracker` 90% oral): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the placental WGS reads were classified using `MG-RAST` against the M5NR database with highly permissive parameters ($\text{e-value} \le 1$, percent identity $\ge 50\%$, alignment length $\ge 15$ amino acids; line 143), whereas the HMP reference body-site WGS profiles were generated using `MetaPhlAn` clade-specific marker genes (line 136). Comparing two different taxonomic profilers across cohorts confounds body-site differences with algorithmic bias. Second, computing Bray–Curtis dissimilarity at the **phylum** level (Figure 1) obscures species-level incompatibility: the placental WGS profile is dominated by *E. coli* (phylum *Proteobacteria*), which is a gut/reagent organism rarely found in the oral cavity, yet at the phylum level *E. coli* artificially matches oral *Proteobacteria* (*Neisseria*, *Haemophilus*).

* **Preterm birth ($<37$ weeks) and remote maternal antenatal infections (such as first-trimester UTIs) significantly structure the placental microbiome and enrich for specific bacterial genera: [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 3B and Figure 4B–D, the genera identified by `Boruta` and `LEfSe` as driving the separation between preterm vs. term birth and antenatal infection vs. control include *Burkholderia* and *Paenibacillus* (Figure 3B)—two well-documented `MO-BIO PowerSoil` kit and water contaminants—as well as *Acinetobacter*, *Thioalkalivibrio* (an extremophilic sulfur-oxidizing bacterium restricted to hypersaline alkaline lakes), and *Pseudoalteromonas* (a marine bacterium) in Figure 4D (line 77). The presence of marine and soda-lake extremophiles as top biomarkers of maternal UTI indicates that short, low-identity `MG-RAST` alignments ($\ge 15$ aa, $\ge 50\%$ identity) and batch/reagent effects between case and control extractions drive the apparent group differences. Furthermore, as noted in the Discussion (line 88), all women in the antenatal infection group received antibiotic treatment (`Table S3`), confounding infection history with antibiotic exposure.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Process Placental and HMP WGS Reads Through an Identical, Stringent Taxonomic Pipeline (`MetaPhlAn` / `Kraken2`)
* **WHY:** Comparing `MG-RAST` (with $\text{e-value} \le 1$, $\ge 50\%$ identity over 15 amino acids) on placental samples against `MetaPhlAn` on HMP body sites introduces severe false-positive and methodological confounding, and computing Bray–Curtis similarity at the phylum level conflates unrelated bacteria (e.g., *E. coli* vs. oral *Neisseria*).
* **WHAT:** Re-profile the 48 placental WGS datasets using `MetaPhlAn` (the exact tool used for the HMP reference cohort) and recompute Bray–Curtis dissimilarity and `SourceTracker` at the species and genus levels.
* **HOW:** Run `MetaPhlAn` on the `BMTagger`-filtered non-human placental WGS reads from the 48 subjects, require a minimum species-level marker coverage threshold, and recalculate species-level and genus-level Bray–Curtis PCoA and `SourceTracker` proportions against the HMP oral, vaginal, gut, skin, and nasal datasets.

#### 2. Sequence and Explicitly Subtract Negative Extraction and Library Controls (`decontam`)
* **WHY:** Absence of a visible 16S PCR band on an agarose gel (`Fig. S4`) does not mean extraction kits and sequencing reagents are free of bacterial DNA; in low-biomass WGS and 30–35 cycle 16S sequencing, reagent DNA ("kitome") is sequenced whenever carrier human DNA allows library preparation to succeed.
* **WHAT:** Report the full taxonomic read counts of all sequenced 16S and WGS blank extraction controls (1 blank per 11 samples, line 131) and apply formal statistical decontamination before downstream testing.
* **HOW:** Deposit the raw fastq/sff files for all sequenced blank controls into SRA (`PRJNA238913`), tabulate every taxon detected in the blanks in a supplementary table, run the `decontam` R package (frequency and prevalence methods) to flag reagent contaminants (including *E. coli*, *Burkholderia*, *Paenibacillus*, *Acinetobacter*, *Thioalkalivibrio*, and *Pseudoalteromonas*), and test whether the weighted/unweighted UniFrac PERMANOVA results for preterm birth (Figure 3A) and antenatal infection (Figure 4A) remain significant after removing reagent-associated OTUs and controlling for extraction kit lot/sequencing run.

#### 3. Audit Extraction Batch and Kit Lot Effects Between Cases and Controls
* **WHY:** If preterm birth cases, remote infection cases, and term controls were extracted or sequenced in separate batches or using different `MO-BIO PowerSoil` kit lots, kit-lot variation in contaminant DNA directly produces statistically significant PERMANOVA $p$-values ($P = 0.001$ and $P = 0.006$) and `Boruta`/`LEfSe` hits.
* **WHAT:** Test whether DNA extraction date, kit lot, or 454/Illumina sequencing run correlates with preterm birth or antenatal infection case status and beta-diversity ordination.
* **HOW:** Include `Extraction_Batch` and `Sequencing_Run` as covariates in a multivariable `vegan::adonis2(UniFrac_dist ~ Batch + Preterm_Birth + Antenatal_Infection)` model and report the marginal variance explained ($R^2$) and $p$-value for the clinical variables after conditioning on technical batch.

#### 4. Clarify Matching Criteria and Temper Claims of Resident Viable Colonization
* **WHY:** Line 112 states that *"All case-cohort subject specimens were matched for gestational age, mode of delivery..."*, whereas preterm cases ($35.0$ weeks) and term controls ($39.3$ weeks) differ in gestational age by design (line 27). Furthermore, DNA detection alone in a low-biomass tissue cannot establish a viable, metabolically active resident microbiome versus transient cell-free microbial DNA or reagent background.
* **WHAT:** Correct the matching description in the Materials and Methods and reframe the Abstract and Discussion to distinguish low-level microbial DNA sequences from a confirmed viable microbiome.
* **HOW:** Revise line 112 to clarify that gestational-age matching applied only to the remote antenatal infection case-cohort (and not the preterm birth case-cohort), and temper claims in the Abstract and Discussion to state that low-abundance microbial DNA sequences were detected and require culture/viability and contamination-controlled confirmation.
