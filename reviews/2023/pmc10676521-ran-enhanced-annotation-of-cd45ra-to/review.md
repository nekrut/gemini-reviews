### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents `scCD45RA`, a Python package that predicts binary surface CD45RA protein status ($\text{CD45RA}^+$ vs. $\text{CD45RA}^-$) in human T cells from short-read single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) gene expression counts. Using a healthy PBMC CITE-seq dataset (GSE144434; 5,559 cells before T-cell filtering) where surface CD45RA antibody-derived tag (ADT) counts provide ground-truth labels via Otsu thresholding, the authors select differentially expressed genes and `hnRNPLL`-correlated transcripts to train six supervised classifiers: logistic regression (LR), linear SVM, radial basis function (RBF) SVM, a stacked LR+SVM classifier, and two multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). The models are evaluated on a 20% internal holdout of GSE144434 (RBF-SVM test accuracy 89.94%), an external sorted $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD45RA}^-$ dataset (GSE150132; reported accuracy 99.96%), and an external PBMC CITE-seq dataset (GSE164378; RBF-SVM accuracy 82.31%, precision 93.34%, recall 70.73%). The package is publicly available on GitHub and PyPI (`pip install sccd45ra`), and all three datasets are available in GEO.

Inferring CD45RA isoform status from standard 3'/5' short-read scRNA-seq is a practical problem for T-cell subset annotation (distinguishing Naive from $\text{T}_{\text{CM}}$ and $\text{T}_{\text{EM}}$ from $\text{T}_{\text{EMRA}}$). However, the manuscript's headline claims and evaluation framework are undermined by major methodological flaws and internal contradictions. First, the headline 99.96% accuracy reported in the Abstract is computed on GSE150132, which consists exclusively of sorted $\text{CD45RA}^-$ negative cells (0% positive instances, as noted in the footnotes to Tables 4 and 5); a trivial dummy classifier that predicts $\text{CD45RA}^-$ for every cell achieves 100.00% accuracy on this dataset. Conversely, on the only genuine two-class external validation dataset (GSE164378), accuracy drops to 82.31% (recall 70.73%), with 27.4%–41.2% misclassification inside the target Naive/$\text{T}_{\text{CM}}$ and $\text{T}_{\text{EM}}/\text{T}_{\text{EMRA}}$ clusters—a result omitted from the Abstract and Tables 3–5. Second, the Abstract and Section 3.2 state that the RBF-SVM was selected for `scCD45RA`, whereas the Discussion (line 176) states the exact opposite—that LR and RBF-SVM are *not* the terminal models incorporated into the package due to overfitting concerns. Third, preprocessing (`SCTransform`, Otsu thresholding) was performed prior to train/test splitting, intermediate cells within $t^* \pm 0.5$ were excluded, Seurat v4 benchmark metrics were omitted from all performance tables, equations in Sections 2.5–2.6 are missing, and Table 2 scrambles the MLP hyperparameter names and values. These issues can be resolved through a rigorous re-evaluation and transparent reporting using the existing codebase.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The RBF-SVM classifier achieves 99.96% accuracy (and MLP achieves 99.74% accuracy) on an unseen dataset (Abstract, Section 3.2, Table 3): [Overstated].**  
  The "unseen dataset" (GSE150132) consists entirely of CyTOF-sorted $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD45RA}^-$ T cells with zero $\text{CD45RA}^+$ cells (lines 27, 140, 160). Because there are no positive instances, precision, recall, and ROC AUC are undefined (Tables 4–5 footnotes), and 99.96% accuracy merely reflects specificity on a single-class negative control where a constant negative predictor achieves 100% accuracy. Presenting 99.96% in the Abstract as the primary generalization metric while omitting the true two-class external CITE-seq validation result (GSE164378: 82.31% accuracy, 70.73% recall; Section 3.3, line 169) misleads readers regarding out-of-sample performance.

* **`scCD45RA` accurately resolves hard-to-classify Naive/$\text{T}_{\text{CM}}$ and $\text{T}_{\text{EM}}/\text{T}_{\text{EMRA}}$ clusters and outperforms Seurat v4 multimodal reference mapping (Sections 3.2–3.3, Figures 1–4, Tables 6–7): [Fragile].**  
  In the internal test split (Table 6), RBF-SVM misclassification jumps from 2.19% (training) to 31.13% (testing) in Naive/$\text{T}_{\text{CM}}$ cluster C1, and from 0.65% (training) to 21.59% (testing) in $\text{T}_{\text{EM}}/\text{T}_{\text{EMRA}}$ cluster C3, indicating substantial overfitting on the exact clusters where CD45RA inference is needed. In the external two-class CITE-seq dataset GSE164378 (line 169), misclassification reaches 41.21% in $\text{CD4}^+$ Naive/$\text{T}_{\text{CM}}$ (Cluster 0), 27.42% in $\text{CD8}^+$ Naive/$\text{T}_{\text{CM}}$ (Cluster 7), and 36.47% in $\text{CD8}^+$ $\text{T}_{\text{EM}}/\text{T}_{\text{EMRA}}$ (Cluster 4). Moreover, comparisons against Seurat v4 reference mapping (lines 152, 160; Figures 1f, 2b, 3b) are purely visual: no quantitative accuracy, precision, recall, or F1 scores for Seurat v4 are computed or included in Tables 3–7.

* **Excluding cells near Otsu's threshold ($t^* \pm 0.5$) and optimizing feature thresholds ($t_{DE}, P_{hn}$) yields a leak-free, well-calibrated classifier (Sections 2.2–2.7): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Three pipeline choices introduce optimistic bias: (1) `SCTransform` normalization and Otsu threshold calculation ($t^*$) were applied to the full GSE144434 dataset before the 80/20 train-test split (lines 34, 39, 44); (2) removing cells with intermediate CD45RA ADT levels in $(t^* - 0.5, t^* + 0.5)$ (line 39) excises the biological transition zone between $\text{CD45RA}^+$ and $\text{CD45RA}^-$ states, artificially inflating separability in the remaining extreme cells; and (3) differential expression (`Monocle 3` GLM) and `hnRNPLL` Spearman correlations were computed across the entire training set prior to 5-fold cross-validation (lines 68–85), leaking fold validation labels into feature ranking during Bayesian hyperparameter optimization.

* **Choice of the terminal model deployed in the `scCD45RA` Python package (Abstract & Section 3.2 vs. Section 4): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The manuscript directly contradicts itself on which classifier is implemented in the software tool. The Abstract (line 7) and Section 3.2 (line 160) state: *"Based on the overall performance, we chose the support vector machine with a radial basis function kernel as the model implemented in our Python package scCD45RA."* Conversely, the Discussion (line 176) argues that the large training-versus-testing gap in Table 6 is not a problem because *"it is crucial to highlight that LR and SVM (RBF) are not the terminal models incorporated into our Python package."*

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace the Single-Class Headline Metric in the Abstract and Tables 3–5 with Two-Class External CITE-seq Validation
* **WHY:** Reporting 99.96% accuracy on GSE150132 ($\text{CD4}^+\text{CD45RA}^-$ sorted cells) in the Abstract as the primary unseen benchmark is statistically uninformative because 100% of true labels are negative.
* **WHAT:** Report the full evaluation metrics (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, Specificity, F1, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC) for all six classifiers AND the Seurat v4 reference-mapping baseline on the two-class external CITE-seq dataset GSE164378 in Tables 3–5, and update the Abstract to report both GSE164378 performance and GSE150132 negative-control specificity.
* **HOW:** Run all six trained models and Seurat v4 `MapQuery()` on the 33,456 T cells of GSE164378. Add a column `GSE164378 (Unseen Two-Class CITE-seq)` to Tables 3, 4, and 5 alongside a row for `Seurat v4 Reference Mapping`, relabel the current `Unseen (%)` column in Table 3 as `GSE150132 (CD45RA− Specificity, %)`, and revise the Abstract to state the exact accuracy (82.31%), precision (93.34%), and recall (70.73%) on GSE164378.

#### 2. Eliminate Pre-Split Leakage and Evaluate Performance Without Excluding Intermediate ($t^* \pm 0.5$) Cells
* **WHY:** Normalizing and thresholding before splitting, computing DEGs across the whole training set before cross-validation, and deleting intermediate cells in $(t^* - 0.5, t^* + 0.5)$ inflate accuracy estimates and leave performance on biological transition cells unknown.
* **WHAT:** Perform train/test splitting on raw counts prior to `SCTransform` and Otsu thresholding, move DEG and `hnRNPLL` correlation calculations inside each cross-validation fold, and report test-set accuracy both with and without the $t^* \pm 0.5$ exclusion zone.
* **HOW:** Split GSE144434 T cells 80/20 on raw UMI counts first. Fit `SCTransform` and Otsu's threshold $t^*$ strictly on the 80% training split. Inside each of the 5 CV folds, compute the DEG list and `hnRNPLL` correlations using only the 4 training folds. Finally, evaluate the trained classifier on all cells in the 20% test set of GSE144434 and in GSE164378 thresholded directly at $t^*$ (without dropping $(t^* - 0.5, t^* + 0.5)$), and plot classifier probability calibration against continuous CITE-seq CD45RA ADT levels. Also fix the Stacked classifier (line 97) to train the meta-LR on out-of-fold cross-validated probability predictions (`sklearn.ensemble.StackingClassifier`) rather than in-sample training predictions.

#### 3. Reconcile the Contradiction on Which Model Is Implemented in `scCD45RA`
* **WHY:** Readers and users cannot tell whether `scCD45RA` runs the RBF-SVM (as stated in lines 7 and 160) or another model (as claimed in line 176).
* **WHAT & HOW:** Inspect the `cd45ra_infer` function in the `scCD45RA` repository, verify which serialized model weight file is loaded, and make the Abstract (line 7), Section 3.2 (line 160), and Discussion (line 176) consistent. If RBF-SVM is indeed the deployed model, delete the contradictory sentence in line 176 and transparently discuss the ~22–41% error rate observed in Naive/$\text{T}_{\text{CM}}$ and $\text{T}_{\text{EM}}/\text{T}_{\text{EMRA}}$ clusters (Table 6 and Section 3.3).

#### 4. Fix Missing Equations, Scrambled Table 2 Values, and Reversed Inequality Signs
* **WHY:** Multiple equations are blank, Table 2 assigns learning rates and dropout rates to layer/node counts, and statistical significance inequalities are inverted.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Section 2.5 & 2.6 (lines 51–75):** Restore the five missing mathematical equations for the quasi-Poisson GLM linear predictor (line 52), variance function $\text{Var}(Y_{ij}) = \phi \mu_{ij}$ (line 58), likelihood ratio test statistic $D = 2(\ell_{\text{full}} - \ell_{\text{null}})$ (line 62), fold change (line 71), $\log_2\text{FC}$ (line 73), and GLM coefficient difference $\log_2\text{FC} = (\beta_1 - \beta_2)/\ln 2$ (line 75). Also clarify how a quasi-Poisson log-link GLM ($\mu_{ij} > 0$) was applied to `SCTransform`-corrected residuals, which contain negative values.
  2. **Table 2 (lines 211–214):** The MLP hyperparameter values are mapped to the wrong parameter names (`Nlayers = 1e-4, Nnodes = 0.1, η = 3, ρ = 105` and `Nlayers = 2.262e-4, Nnodes = 0.1, η = 1, ρ = 87`). Correct Table 2 so that `Nlayers` = 3 and 1, `Nnodes` = 105 and 87, learning rate $\eta = 1\times 10^{-4}$ and $2.262\times 10^{-4}$, and dropout rate $\rho = 0.1$ and $0.1$, matching the parameter ranges in line 103.
  3. **Section 3.1 (lines 115 and 123):** In line 115, change `q-value ≥ 0.05` to `q-value < 0.05` (matching line 63). In line 123, change `P-value > = .05` to `P-value < 0.05` (matching line 78).
  4. **Section 2.1 / 2.2 / 4 (Sample Sizes):** Report the exact number of T cells retained after `CD19`/`CD3` filtering and `Scrublet` doublet removal for each of the three datasets (GSE144434, GSE150132, and GSE164378), and reconcile the GSE164378 cell counts across lines 32 (80k downsampled), 107 (162,000), and 178 (33,456 profiles).
  5. **Figure 3c Caption (lines 162, 285):** Change *"clusters 1 and 3 of the CITE-seq data"* to *"clusters 5, 10, and 11 of the unseen dataset"* to match line 160.
