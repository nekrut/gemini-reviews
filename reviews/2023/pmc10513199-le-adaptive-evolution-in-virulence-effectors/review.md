### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the evolutionary genomics, structural diversity, transcriptional regulation, and host-specificity role of the MAX (*Magnaporthe* Avrs and ToxB-like) effector family across 120 *Pyricularia oryzae* genomes spanning 14 host plant genera (54 newly assembled genomes and 66 public assemblies). Using PSI-BLAST, TM-align structural alignment, and iterative HMMER searches, the authors identify 58–78 MAX effector genes per isolate grouped into 80 orthogroups (94 single-copy orthologous groups). Quantitative RT-PCR of all 67 MAX genes in reference isolate Guy11 across two rice cultivars (Maratelli and Kitaake) demonstrates biotrophy-specific induction (64–78% expressed at 2–4 dpi) modulated by host genotype. Population genomic analyses show that MAX effectors are predominantly accessory "shell" genes (80% of orthogroups) with significantly elevated nonsynonymous nucleotide diversity ($\pi_N/\pi_S = 1.826$ vs. $0.448$ in non-effector genes; 26 MAX orthologs with $\pi_N/\pi_S > 1$) and elevated divergence against outgroup *Pyricularia* sp. LS ($d_N/d_S = 0.977$ vs. $0.584$; 24/66 MAX orthologs with $d_N/d_S > 1$). Mapping polymorphism and divergence onto 3D homology models reveals enrichment of diversifying selection in coils and turns and solvent-exposed residues. Finally, transgenic knock-in of three *Setaria*-lineage MAX effectors absent from the *Oryza*-infecting lineage (`MAX79`, `MAX83`, `MAX89`) into Guy11 fails to trigger avirulence across 22 diverse rice cultivars, demonstrating that lineage-specific effector loss is not universally driven by escape from host R-gene recognition. All assemblies, alignments, homology models, and raw reads are publicly available in ENA (PRJEB47684), Zenodo (`10.5281/zenodo.7689273`, `10.5281/zenodo.8052494`), and an institutional model server.

This is a thorough interdisciplinary study combining comparative genomics, structural biology, population genetics, and experimental plant pathology—including transparent reporting of negative transgenic knock-in results that falsify a simplistic "all losses are AVR escapes" hypothesis. The primary methodological caveats are: (1) estimating $d_N/d_S$ by averaging 12 pairwise `codeml` (`runmode = -2`) comparisons between ingroup isolates and the outgroup rather than using a tree-based codon model, which conflates fixed inter-species divergence with intra-specific polymorphism and treats shared ancestral branch length as independent across pairs; (2) computing $\pi_N$ and $d_N/d_S$ on ultra-short structural sub-partitions (`lseff >= 10 bp`, i.e., 3–4 codons) where ratio estimators have extreme variance, alongside a copy-paste numerical error in the reported $\pi_N$ values for beta strands 3–5; and (3) expressing knock-in transgenes under a heterologous promoter (*pAVR-Pia*). These points can be addressed through computational sensitivity checks and text revisions.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **MAX effectors constitute a highly plastic pangenome compartment (80% shell orthogroups) structured by host lineage and massively induced during biotrophic infection in a host-genotype-dependent manner (Figs. 2–4): [Well Supported].**  
  The authors carefully rule out assembly quality artifacts (showing MAX gene counts do not correlate with N50 or assembly size, $P = 0.39$ for study of origin, unlike strongly significant host-of-origin effects, $P = 3 \times 10^{-8}$) and manually curate missing/chimeric annotations with RNA-seq reads and BLASTn searches. The qRT-PCR time-course across three independent infection experiments (5 biological replicates each) on Maratelli and Kitaake convincingly establishes biotrophy-specific, host-modulated expression.

* **MAX effectors experience widespread multiallelic balancing selection ($\pi_N/\pi_S > 1$) and recurrent directional selection ($d_N/d_S > 1$) concentrated in coils, turns, and exposed surface residues (Fig. 6): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The genome-wide contrast showing elevated $\pi_N$, $\pi_N/\pi_S$, and $F_{ST}$ in MAX effectors relative to non-MAX secreted proteins and background genes (Fig. 6A–B) and the binomial GLMM showing higher polymorphism probability in exposed (60–100% solvent accessibility) residues are robust. However, two methodological issues affect the divergence and domain-level claims:
  1. $d_N/d_S$ was calculated using pairwise `codeml` (`runmode = -2`) between 12 randomly chosen *P. oryzae* sequences and the outgroup *Pyricularia* sp. LS (line 81 in Methods). Pairwise outgroup $d_N/d_S$ spans the entire ancestral branch connecting *P. oryzae* and *Pyricularia* sp. LS plus tip polymorphisms, and averaging 12 non-independent pairs does not test for recurrent selective sweeps within *P. oryzae* nor provide a formal likelihood-ratio test against neutrality ($d_N/d_S = 1$).
  2. In the structural sub-partition analysis (line 142 in Results, line 77 in Methods), filtering segments at `lseff >= 10 bp` (just 3.3 codons) produces noisy $\pi_N$ and $d_N/d_S$ estimates, and line 142 reports identical values to four decimal places for the third, fourth, and fifth beta strands ($\pi_N = 0.0341$, $\pi_N = 0.0341$, and $\pi_N = 0.0341$).

* **Loss of `MAX79`, `MAX83`, and `MAX89` in the *Oryza*-infecting lineage does not trigger receptor-mediated resistance across 22 diverse rice cultivars (Fig. 5): [Well Supported].**  
  Using *AVR-Pia* and *AVR1-CO39* as positive controls that reliably trigger *Pi-a*-mediated resistance in cultivar Aichi Asahi, the authors test three independent transgenic lines for each of `MAX79`, `MAX83`, and `MAX89` (cloned from *Setaria* isolate US0071 under the *AVR-Pia* promoter and verified by qRT-PCR in S7 Fig) across 22 rice cultivars representing global rice diversity. While testing additional landraces or native promoters could theoretically uncover rare recognition specificities, the experiment strongly supports the conclusion that effector loss during host shifts can result from loss of target utility or neutral drift rather than ubiquitous R-gene recognition.

* **Twenty-five MAX effectors predicted by prior studies were missed by the current pipeline, while 19 novel MAX orthologs were identified (Section 1 of Results, S2 Table): [Well Supported].**  
  The authors transparently benchmark their PSI-BLAST + TM-align + HMMER pipeline (which enforces the canonical disulfide bridge spacing of two cysteines $<33\text{–}48$ amino acids apart) against four prior studies (refs. 15–17, 38) in S2 Table.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace or Supplement Pairwise Outgroup `codeml` Averages with Phylogenetic Codon Models and Formal Likelihood-Ratio Tests
* **WHY:** Averaging 12 pairwise `codeml` (`runmode = -2`) comparisons between ingroup isolates and outgroup *Pyricularia* sp. LS (Methods, line 81) treats shared evolutionary history along the outgroup stem branch as 12 independent replicates, conflates intraspecific polymorphism ($\pi_N/\pi_S$) with interspecific divergence ($d_N/d_S$), and lacks a statistical test of whether $d_N/d_S > 1$ is significantly greater than 1.0 (a short gene can yield point estimate $d_N/d_S = 1.2$ purely by stochastic sampling of 2–3 synonymous sites).
* **WHAT:** Run a tree-based codon model (`codeml` M0, M1a vs. M2a, or M7 vs. M8, or McDonald-Kreitman tests comparing intraspecific polymorphism to fixed outgroup divergence) on the 66 single-copy MAX orthologs with outgroup sequences.
* **HOW:** Using the existing codon alignments in Zenodo (`10.5281/zenodo.8052494`) and RAxML gene trees, run `codeml` with `runmode = 0` to estimate the branch $d_N/d_S$ on the stem branch vs. ingroup branches (and test $d_N/d_S > 1$ via a 1-d.f. likelihood ratio test against a null model fixing $\omega = 1$), or report site-level positive selection posterior probabilities (BEB from M8) mapped directly onto the structural features (coils/turns vs. extended beta strands). This also avoids having to compute $d_N/d_S$ on tiny concatenated structural fragments (`lseff >= 10 bp`).

#### 2. Apply Multiple-Testing Correction to the 67 Gene-Wise Mann-Whitney Tests Between Rice Cultivars (Fig. 2B)
* **WHY:** In Fig. 2B and Methods (line 115), differences in maximum median expression between Kitaake and Maratelli across the 67 MAX genes were evaluated using individual Mann-Whitney $U$-tests at unadjusted $P < 0.05$. Furthermore, extracting "for each variety, gene, and experimental replicate, values corresponding to the day post-inoculation with the highest median expression" gives only $n = 3$ experimental replicate medians per variety (or $n = 15$ leaf samples if biological replicates were pooled across the 3 experiments).
* **WHAT:** Clarify the exact sample size ($n = 3$ independent experiments vs. $n = 15$ pooled leaf samples) used in the Mann-Whitney $U$-tests in Fig. 2B (noting that a two-sided Mann-Whitney test on $n = 3$ vs. $n = 3$ cannot mathematically achieve $P < 0.05$, as the minimum possible two-sided exact $P$-value is $2 / \binom{6}{3} = 0.10$, whereas Fig. 2B reports $P < 0.0001$), and apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across the 67 tested genes.
* **HOW:** In Methods (line 115) and the Fig. 2B caption, clarify that the Mann-Whitney tests were performed across the $n = 15$ leaf biological replicates (3 experiments $\times$ 5 leaves) at the peak dpi, supplement with a linear mixed model accounting for experiment batch (`Expression ~ Cultivar + (1 | Experiment)`), and report BH-adjusted $q$-values in Fig. 2B.

#### 3. Correct the Duplicated Numerical Values in the Structural Domain Analysis
* **WHY:** Line 142 of Results states: *"The third, fourth, and fifth beta strands displayed the highest levels of non-synonymous diversity ($\pi_N = 0.0341$, $\pi_N = 0.0341$, and $\pi_N = 0.0341$, respectively)"*. Having all three distinct structural domains equal $0.0341$ is a copy-paste error from S6 Table.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Check S6 Table for the exact mean/median $\pi_N$ values of beta strands 3, 4, and 5 and insert the correct numbers into line 142.

#### 4. Discuss the Cysteine-Spacing Filter and Heterologous Promoter Caveats
* **WHY:** First, the annotation pipeline (Fig. 1, Methods line 52) retained only proteins with two conserved cysteines $<33\text{–}48$ amino acids apart during HMMER rounds 1–2, which automatically excludes cysteine-less MAX variants (or variants with modified disulfide spacing) and likely explains part of the 25 MAX effectors predicted by sequence/AlphaFold studies (refs. 15–17, 38) that were missed here. Second, driving `MAX79`, `MAX83`, and `MAX89` from the *AVR-Pia* promoter in Guy11 tests recognition during biotrophy, but native promoter timing or epistatic interactions in the *Setaria* isolate background could differ.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Add 1–2 sentences in the Discussion (lines 32 and 37) noting that: (a) structure predictions via AlphaFold2/ESMfold without hard cysteine-spacing filters can recover diverged MAX-fold effectors lacking canonical disulfide bridges (such as *PWL2*), and (b) while *pAVR-Pia* drives strong biotrophic expression matching canonical MAX kinetics, native-promoter or deletion assays in *Setaria*-infecting isolates would complement the heterologous knock-in screen.
