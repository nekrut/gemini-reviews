## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis
The manuscript reports a multi-locus genome-wide association study (GWAS) and ridge-regression best linear unbiased prediction (`rrBLUP`) genomic prediction (GP) analysis of 236 CIMMYT maize testcross hybrids evaluated across seven optimal and three managed-drought environments in Kenya using 215,542 genotyping-by-sequencing (GBS) SNPs. While multi-environment field phenotyping of 236 testcrosses and publicly archiving the genotype/phenotype data (`hdl.handle.net/11529/10549140`) provide a useful empirical resource for tropical maize breeding, the analytical execution and manuscript reporting contain major methodological and numerical defects: (1) a critical reference-genome assembly mismatch where SNPs called on `B73 AGPv2` coordinates (lines 73, 92) are mapped onto a mixture of `B73 RefGen_v4` (`Zm00001d...`) and `B73 NAM-5.0 / v5` (`Zm00001eb...`) gene models in Table 2 without coordinate liftover; (2) use of shrinkage BLUPs rather than de-regressed BLUPs or BLUEs as phenotypes for GWAS (line 85); (3) widespread mathematical inconsistencies in Table 1 (`H^2` and `CV%`) and QTN summary counts (lines 178, 186); and (4) repeated claims in the Abstract, Discussion, and Conclusion that "integration of genomic selection with GWAS results leads to an increase in prediction accuracy" despite the fact that no GWAS-assisted genomic prediction model was ever run in this study.

## 2. Direct Appraisal of Core Claims
* **Claim 1: Multi-environment trials of 236 maize testcrosses reveal significant genotypic and G×E variance with broad-sense heritability ranging from `0.24` (PH) to `0.87` (EPO) under drought and `0.21` (EPP) to `0.74` (AD) under optimal conditions** — `[Fragile]`
  The variance components, coefficients of variation (`CV%`), and broad-sense heritabilities ($H^2$) reported in Table 1 (lines 116–136) contain multiple mathematical inconsistencies:
  1. **Broad-sense heritability ($H^2$) miscalculation for Plant Height (`PH`) under drought:** In Table 1 (line 125, Drought Conditions across $n_E = 3$ environments and $n_R = 2$ replications), `PH` has genetic variance $\sigma_G^2 = 140.23$, genotype-by-environment variance $\sigma_{GE}^2 = 56.48$, and error variance $\sigma_e^2 = 260.31$. Under the standard multi-environment heritability formula $H^2 = \frac{\sigma_G^2}{\sigma_G^2 + \sigma_{GE}^2/n_E + \sigma_e^2/(n_E n_R)}$, this yields $H^2 = \frac{140.23}{140.23 + 56.48/3 + 260.31/6} = 0.693$, yet Table 1 reports `H^2 = 0.24` (lower than `GY`, which has $\sigma_G^2 = 0.06, \sigma_{GE}^2 = 0.28, \sigma_e^2 = 0.54$ and $H^2 = 0.25$!).
  2. **Coefficient of Variation (`CV%`) errors for `ASI`:** Under Optimal Conditions (line 131), `ASI` has `Mean = 0.74` and $\sigma_e^2 = 1.44$ ($\sqrt{\sigma_e^2}/\text{Mean} = 1.20/0.74 = 162.2\%$), yet Table 1 reports `CV (%) = 13.10`. Under Drought Conditions (line 122), `ASI` has `Mean = 2.77` and $\sigma_e^2 = 2.17$ ($\sqrt{\sigma_e^2}/\text{Mean} = 1.47/2.77 = 53.2\%$), yet Table 1 reports `CV (%) = 95.88`.
  3. **Inconsistent phenotypic selection efficiency in Discussion (chunk 2 line 53):** The text states "the phenotypic selection efficiency is 0.50 (square root of heritability of GY) under DS and 0.73 under optimum conditions." While $\sqrt{0.25} = 0.50$ under DS, under optimal conditions $H^2 = 0.46$ (Table 1 line 128), whose square root is $\sqrt{0.46} = 0.68$, not `0.73`.
* **Claim 2: Seven multi-locus GWAS models identify 219 (drought) and 265 (optimum) QTNs, including 172 stable QTNs detected by at least two methods** — `[Moderately Supported]`
  Applying six multi-locus models from the `mrMLM` package (`mrMLM`, `FASTmrMLM`, `FASTmrEMMA`, `pLARmEB`, `pKWmEB`, `ISIS EM-BLASSO` at $\text{LOD} \ge 3$) alongside `FarmCPU` while controlling for population structure ($K = 4$ PCs and VanRaden kinship matrix) is a reasonable multi-locus screening strategy. However, two methodological and reporting problems affect these results:
  1. **Use of shrinkage BLUPs as GWAS input (line 85):** Performing GWAS on empirical BLUPs (rather than BLUEs, whereas BLUEs were already computed and used for genomic prediction in line 99) compresses phenotypic variance toward the mean—especially for low-heritability traits like `GY` under drought ($H^2 = 0.25$)—and induces double-shrinkage when fitted inside mixed-model GWAS frameworks.
  2. **Arithmetic discrepancies in reported QTN counts (line 186):** Under drought, the QTN counts across the seven models (`62 + 45 + 37 + 36 + 16 + 12 + 7`) sum to `215`, contradicting the total of `219` stated in line 178. Under optimal conditions (line 186), the authors list only **seven** QTN numbers (`40, 37, 36, 36, 32, 25, and 24`, summing to `230`) for **eight** traits (`EPO, EH, ASI, GY, EPP, PH, SD and AD`), omitting one trait of 35 QTNs needed to reach the stated total of `265`. Additionally, line 161 reports SNP density in units of `Mbp` ("varied from 84.89 Mbp for chromosome 4 to 116.79 Mbp for chromosome 5") instead of `SNPs/Mbp`.
* **Claim 3: Candidate gene mapping within $\pm 4.75\text{ kb}$ of peak QTNs identifies 43 genes, including `Zm00001eb041070` (`AP2-EREBP 60`) at `qGY_DS1.1` (`S1_216149215`) and `Zm00001eb364110` (`TCP 20`) at `qASI_DS8.2` (`S8_167256316`)** — `[Methodologically Flawed]`
  There is a fundamental reference-genome coordinate mismatch in the candidate-gene and haplotype pipeline. In Materials and Methods, the authors explicitly state that raw GBS reads were called "after mapping to **B73 AGPv2** coordinates" (line 73) and that candidate genes within $\pm 4.75\text{ kb}$ were annotated "using the B73 maize reference genome (**B73 RefGen_V2**)" (line 92). However, in `B73 RefGen_v2`, gene models use `GRMZM2G...` or `AC...` identifiers. In contrast, Table 2 (lines 212–253) reports a mixture of **B73 RefGen_v4** (`Zm00001d...`, e.g., `Zm00001d045665`, `Zm00001d023278`) and **B73 RefGen_v5 / NAM-5.0** (`Zm00001eb...`, e.g., `Zm00001eb041070`, `Zm00001eb364110`) gene identifiers, and the Figure 6B–C caption (chunk 2 line 1) misspells `Zm00001eb041070` as `Zm00001ed41070`. Because chromosomal physical coordinates shift by several megabases between `B73 AGPv2`, `AGPv4`, and `RefGen_v5`, looking up `AGPv2` SNP coordinates directly against `v4` and `v5` gene models within a narrow $\pm 4.75\text{ kb}$ window—or mixing assembly versions without `CrossMap`/`liftOver` coordinate conversion—invalidates the physical proximity and intragenic haplotype claims (`qGY_DS1.1` at `S1_216149215`).
* **Claim 4: `rrBLUP` genomic prediction achieves moderate-to-high accuracy (`0.24–0.61` under drought; `0.37–0.72` under optimum), and integrating GS with GWAS increases prediction accuracy** — `[Overstated]`
  While the 5-fold cross-validation `rrBLUP` predictive abilities (Figure 7, chunk 2 line 6) are plausible for a 236-line panel, the manuscript repeatedly claims in the Abstract (line 21), Discussion (chunk 2 line 53), and Conclusion (chunk 2 line 58) that "Integration of genomic selection with GWAS results leads not only to an increase in the prediction accuracy, but also to validate the function of the identified candidate genes." No integrated GWAS+GS analysis (such as fitting GWAS-discovered QTNs as fixed effects in `rrBLUP` or weighting the genomic relationship matrix by QTN variance) was performed anywhere in the Materials and Methods or Results.

## 3. Essential Revisions & Methodological Critiques
1. **Resolve the B73 reference genome version mismatch (`AGPv2` vs. `RefGen_v4` vs. `RefGen_v5`):**
   - Clarify which exact B73 reference assembly (`AGPv2`, `AGPv4`, or `Zm-B73-REFERENCE-NAM-5.0 / v5`) was used to call the SNP physical positions (e.g., `S1_216149215`).
   - If the GBS SNPs are on `AGPv2` coordinates (lines 73, 92), either convert all SNP coordinates to `B73 RefGen_v5` using `liftOver` / `CrossMap` before querying $\pm 4.75\text{ kb}$ windows and running `Haploview`/`geneHapR` on `Zm00001eb...` gene models, or use an統一 single assembly version across Table 2 (eliminating the mix of `Zm00001d...` v4 and `Zm00001eb...` v5 IDs, and fixing the typo `Zm00001ed41070` in the Figure 6 caption).
2. **Re-run GWAS on BLUEs and audit all numerical tables and QTN counts:**
   - Replace shrinkage BLUPs with the across-environment BLUEs as the phenotypic input to the seven GWAS models (line 85) to avoid double-shrinkage of genetic effects.
   - Recalculate and correct the `H^2` for `PH` under drought (`0.24` vs. `0.69` in Table 1 line 125), the `CV (%)` values for `ASI` under drought and optimal conditions (`95.88%` and `13.10%` in Table 1 lines 122, 131), the QTN sums in line 186 (`215` vs. `219` under drought; restore the missing 8th trait count under optimal conditions), and the SNP density units (`SNPs/Mbp` in line 161).
3. **Either perform the claimed GWAS-assisted genomic prediction or remove the unsubstantiated claim:**
   - Either conduct a formal cross-validated benchmark comparing baseline `rrBLUP` against `rrBLUP + fixed QTN covariates` (where QTNs are discovered strictly within each 80% training fold to avoid data leakage into the 20% test fold), or remove all statements claiming that integrating GS with GWAS increased prediction accuracy in this study (Abstract line 21, Discussion chunk 2 line 53, Conclusion chunk 2 line 58).
   - In Methods (line 99), correct the definition of predictive ability to state that Pearson's $r$ was computed between predicted GEBVs and observed BLUEs in the testing set, and when comparing genomic prediction against phenotypic selection ($\sqrt{H^2}$, chunk 2 line 53), report true prediction accuracy $r_{\text{GP}} = r(\text{GEBV}, \text{BLUE}) / \sqrt{H^2}$.
