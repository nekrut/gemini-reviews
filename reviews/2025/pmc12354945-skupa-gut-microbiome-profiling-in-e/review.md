### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the gut microbiome in murine models of chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). First, the authors longitudinally profile fecal 16S rRNA (V3–V4) amplicon sequences in transgenic `Eμ-TCL1` mice ($n = 13$) and wild-type C57BL/6J (`WT B6`) controls ($n = 7$) at 4, 7, 10, and 12 months, alongside small intestinal/colonic histology, immunohistochemistry (human TCL1 and murine CD19), tight-junction immunofluorescence (ZO-1, CLDN2, CLDN3, CLDN7), and plasma ELISAs (CLDN2, CLDN3, sCD14, zonulin) at 12 months. Second, using the aggressive syngeneic `Eμ-TCL1` adoptive transfer (AT) model in female `WT B6` recipients ($n = 10$), they track microbiome changes over 7 weeks post-engraftment and test whether broad-spectrum antibiotic ablation (vancomycin, neomycin, metronidazole, amphotericin B, and ampicillin; $n = 10$ vs. $n = 10$ water controls) alters leukemic progression and peripheral/splenic immune populations. Raw 16S data and QIIME2/R scripts are deposited in NCBI SRA and GitHub.

While the syngeneic AT time-course (Figure 5) and the transient delay in peripheral blood CLL burden at weeks 3 and 5 under antibiotic treatment (Figure 6B) in vendor-matched recipient mice are informative, several experimental design confounds and contradictions between the Abstract and Results undermine the manuscript's primary claims: (1) in the 12-month transgenic study (Figures 1–4), `Eμ-TCL1` mice were bred in-house whereas `WT B6` controls were purchased from The Jackson Laboratory and housed separately, confounding genotype with vendor/maternal origin and explaining why `Eμ-TCL1` alpha and beta diversity were already distinct at baseline (4 months, where only $n = 1$ WT sample passed QC) and showed **no significant change over time** (Figures 1E–F); (2) the Abstract claims that antibiotic treatment yielded *"more tumor-reactive CD8+ T cells that coexpressed fewer inhibitory receptors (e.g., PD-1, LAG-3, and TIM-3)"* in the spleen, directly contradicting Results line 137, which reports that these splenic CD8+ T-cell differences were **not statistically significant**; and (3) comparing anti-human TCL1 immunohistochemistry between human-*TCL1* transgenic mice and wild-type mice lacking the human transgene (Figure 3B) is a transgene control rather than a fair comparison of B-cell infiltration, especially since murine CD19 histoscores did not differ significantly in either the small intestine or colon (Figure 3C). Re-framing the non-significant splenic and longitudinal transgenic claims and adding proper statistical tests for beta diversity and repeated measures are necessary.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Transgenic `Eμ-TCL1` mice develop a progressive, disease-associated gut dysbiosis over time compared with `WT B6` controls: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the Abstract states that a dysbiotic microbiome *"develops in mice over time,"* but Results line 114 explicitly reports that *"there is no significant increase in diversity between Eμ-TCL1 mice over time as disease progresses (Fig. 1E and F)"* and that 4-month and 12-month profiles are similar. Second, `Eμ-TCL1` mice were bred in-house while `WT B6` controls were purchased from The Jackson Laboratory (`JAX:000664`) and housed in separate cages (lines 27, 33). Vendor-purchased JAX C57BL/6J mice possess a restricted, facility-specific microbiota that differs markedly from in-house bred colonies regardless of genotype. Without littermate `WT` controls or co-housing, the difference in Shannon diversity and Bray–Curtis clustering between `Eμ-TCL1` and `WT B6` (Figures 1C–D, 2A) cannot be attributed to CLL rather than vivarium/maternal origin. Moreover, only a single 4-month `WT B6` sample ($n = 1$) passed the Q28 sequencing filter (line 89), precluding valid baseline comparison at 4 months.

* **The aggressive syngeneic `Eμ-TCL1` adoptive transfer model induces longitudinal gut microbiome shifts enriched in Proteobacteria: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Unlike the transgenic comparison, the syngeneic AT experiment (Figure 5) uses age- and source-matched female `WT B6` recipients sampled longitudinally from baseline (engraftment) through week 7, showing a progressive shift in Bray–Curtis ordination at weeks 5–7 and transient Proteobacteria expansion at weeks 3–5. However, no sham-injected (non-leukemic) age-matched longitudinal control group was sequenced in parallel to separate vivarium aging/handling drift over 7 weeks from leukemia-driven shifts, and no formal PERMANOVA ($R^2$, $p$-value) or linear mixed-effects test is reported for Figure 5D.

* **Antibiotic-mediated gut flora ablation transiently delays peripheral CLL expansion in the syngeneic AT model: [Well Supported].**  
  In randomized `WT B6` recipients ($n = 10$ Abx vs. $n = 10$ water), antibiotic treatment significantly reduced circulating $\text{CD45}^+\text{CD19}^+\text{CD5}^+$ leukemic burden and MDSC proportions at weeks 3 and 5 post-engraftment before converging at week 7 (Figures 6B–F). Future work should note whether oral/systemic metronidazole or ampicillin exerts any direct off-target effects on engrafted `Eμ-TCL1` lymphocyte proliferation independent of the microbiota.

* **Delayed CLL onset in antibiotic-treated mice is accompanied by more tumor-reactive splenic $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells co-expressing fewer inhibitory receptors (PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3): [Overstated].**  
  The Abstract (line 7) and Discussion (line 148) state as a main finding that splenic $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells in antibiotic-treated mice were more tumor-reactive and co-expressed fewer inhibitory receptors (PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3). However, Results line 137 states the opposite regarding statistical support: *"Antibiotic-receiving mice displayed minimally reduced T cell dysfunction, evoking a **non-significant change** in the percentage of total CD8+ and antigen-experienced (CD44+) CD8+ T cells co-expressing inhibitory receptors PD-1, TIM-3, and LAG-3 (Supplementary Fig. S5B)... **No significant change** in percentage of progenitor-exhausted T cells was witnessed... Overall, more definitive conclusions were not able to be made because of the limited sample size analyzed."* Presenting statistically non-significant trends at the study endpoint (when tumor burden in blood and spleen had already equalized between arms) as positive findings in the Abstract is unwarranted.

* **Advanced `Eμ-TCL1` mice exhibit GI tract CLL infiltration and altered intestinal barrier markers: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Serosal/mucosal tumor nodules on H&E (Figure 3A), decreased mucosal ZO-1 and increased CLDN2 by immunofluorescence (Supplementary Figure S4), and elevated plasma CLDN2 and sCD14 (Figures 4A,D) support barrier perturbation in 12-month-old `Eμ-TCL1` mice. However, two caveats require correction: (1) in Figure 3B, staining for **human TCL1** is positive in `Eμ-TCL1` mice and absent in `WT B6` mice simply because `Eμ-TCL1` mice carry the human *TCL1* transgene while wild-type mice lack the gene; when stained for endogenous murine CD19 (Figure 3C, Supplementary Figure S3C), there was **no significant difference** in small intestine or colon histoscores between `Eμ-TCL1` and `WT B6` mice; and (2) in Figure 4C, commercial "zonulin" ELISAs target pre-haptoglobin 2 (*HP2*), a gene absent from the mouse genome, and are known to cross-react with complement C3/properdin rather than measuring tight-junction zonulin.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Remove or Qualify Non-Significant Splenic $\text{CD8}^+$ T-Cell Claims in the Abstract and Discussion
* **WHY:** Reporting in the Abstract (line 7) and Discussion (line 148) that antibiotic ablation produced *"more tumor-reactive CD8+ T cells that coexpressed fewer inhibitory receptors (e.g., PD-1, LAG-3, and TIM-3)"* directly contradicts Results line 137 and Supplementary Figure S5B–D, where these differences were statistically non-significant and spleens were harvested at week 7/9 after leukemic burden had already equalized between groups.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Abstract and Discussion to accurately reflect the statistically significant peripheral blood findings (higher circulating $\text{CD3}^+$ T-cell and non-malignant B-cell proportions and lower M-MDSC/G-MDSC proportions at weeks 3–5; Figures 6C–F) while explicitly noting that endpoint splenic $\text{CD8}^+$ exhaustion markers did not reach statistical significance.
* **HOW:** Replace the penultimate sentence of the Abstract with: *"Immunophenotyping of peripheral blood during the period of delayed disease expansion (weeks 3–5) showed higher proportions of T cells and non-malignant B cells and reduced myeloid-derived suppressor cells, whereas endpoint splenic CD8+ T-cell exhaustion profiles did not differ significantly."*

#### 2. Explicitly Acknowledge the Vendor/Housing Confound and Lack of Temporal Progression in the Transgenic `Eμ-TCL1` Cohort
* **WHY:** In-house bred `Eμ-TCL1` mice and Jackson Laboratory (`JAX:000664`) `WT B6` mice housed separately differ in baseline microbiota due to maternal transmission and facility origin. Furthermore, Figure 1E–F and line 114 show that `Eμ-TCL1` microbiome diversity did not change significantly between 4 and 12 months.
* **WHAT:** Temper claims in the Abstract, Results (Section 1 header, line 112), and Discussion that CLL *generates* a progressive dysbiosis over 12 months in the transgenic model, and explicitly discuss the non-littermate vendor/cage confound.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract ("develops in mice over time") and Discussion (line 146) to state that while longitudinal microbiome progression is demonstrated within the syngeneic AT model (Figure 5), differences between in-house `Eμ-TCL1` transgenic mice and vendor-purchased `WT B6` controls were already present at 4 months and may reflect colony/maternal background in addition to genotype.

#### 3. Add Formal PERMANOVA Tests for Beta Diversity and Linear Mixed-Effects Models for Longitudinal Data
* **WHY:** None of the Bray–Curtis PCoA plots (Figures 1D, 1F, 5D, 6G) report a statistical test (PERMANOVA $R^2$ and $p$-value). In addition, applying separate unpaired Welch $t$-tests at each longitudinal time point (Figures 1C, 6C–F, 6H) ignores within-mouse repeated measures and inflates Type I error across multiple time points.
* **WHAT:** Run PERMANOVA (`vegan::adonis2` or `qiime diversity beta-group-significance`) on all Bray–Curtis distance matrices, and analyze longitudinal curves (Figures 1C, 6C–F, 6H) using two-way repeated-measures ANOVA or linear mixed-effects models with Holm–Šidák/Dunnett post-hoc correction.
* **HOW:** Using the existing QIIME2 Bray–Curtis artifacts in the `DIG-CLL` repository, compute `adonis2` $F$-statistics, $R^2$, and permutation $p$-values (blocking by mouse ID for longitudinal comparisons in Figures 1F and 5D) and annotate them directly on Figures 1D, 1F, 5D, and 6G.

#### 4. Clarify Interpretation of Human TCL1 vs. Murine CD19 IHC and the Mouse "Zonulin" ELISA
* **WHY:** Wild-type mice lack the human *TCL1* transgene, so comparing anti-human TCL1 staining between `Eμ-TCL1` and `WT B6` mice (Figure 3B, $P < 0.001$) is expected by definition, whereas endogenous murine CD19 histoscores showed no significant difference in diffuse small intestinal or colonic mucosa (Figure 3C). In addition, mice lack the haptoglobin-2 (*HP2*) gene that encodes zonulin, and commercial mouse "zonulin" ELISA kits cross-react with complement/properdin proteins.
* **WHAT:** Clarify in the Results (lines 123–125) that human TCL1 IHC identifies transgenic leukemic cells specifically within focal serosal/mucosal nodules (whereas overall mucosal B-cell density by CD19 is unchanged), and note the known cross-reactivity caveat of commercial zonulin ELISAs in mice.
* **HOW:** Add a qualifying sentence in Section 2 of the Results (line 123 and line 125) addressing both points.
