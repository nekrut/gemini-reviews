### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates how luminal microbial metabolites from post-inflammatory dysbiosis interact with the colonic epithelium to sensitize primary afferent neurons. The authors show in vivo that four daily intracolonic instillations of microbe-free fecal supernatants from dextran sodium sulfate-treated donor mice ($\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$) induce persistent visceral hypersensitivity to colorectal distension (measured via abdominal withdrawal reflex [AWR] and visceromotor response [VMR]) in naive male C57BL/6N recipient mice. Moving to an in vitro system combining murine colonic crypt-derived epithelial organoids and primary dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, the authors report that $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ reduces organoid growth area without lowering MTT viability, downregulates mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation transcripts while upregulating protease and growth factor transcripts (bulk RNA-seq, $n=4$ per group), and exhibits depleted short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) and hypoxanthine ($^1\text{H}$ NMR metabolomics, $n=5$ per group). In whole-cell patch-clamp recordings ($6\text{--}12$ neurons across $n=4$ experiments) and immunofluorescence assays ($n=4\text{--}6$ slides), neither $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ alone nor conditioned medium from $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$-treated organoids ($\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}}$) alone alters DRG action potential firing, whereas the combined treatment ($\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}} + \text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$) increases action potential frequency and c-Fos/CGRP immunoreactivity. Raw RNA-seq data (GEO: GSE294757) and processed datasets (Mendeley Data) are publicly deposited.

The combination of colonic organoids with primary DRG electrophysiology is a useful experimental platform, and the in vivo transfer of visceral hypersensitivity by cell-free $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ is clear. However, several statistical and experimental design flaws weaken the central mechanistic conclusions: (1) the claim of an integrated/synergistic requirement for both $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}}$ and $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ is not tested with a two-way factorial interaction model and omits the necessary cross-combination controls ($\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}} + \text{FS}_{\text{CTR}}$ and $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,CTR}} + \text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$); (2) differential gene expression in RNA-seq is defined using an unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ threshold across the transcriptome; (3) the DRG subpopulation analysis uses an arbitrary 25th-percentile fluorescence cutoff that mathematically forces constant subpopulation proportions (~60% double-positive) and misclassifies $\text{CGRP}^+/\text{IB4}^+$ C-fibers as myelinated $\text{A}\delta$ fibers; and (4) donor fecal pellets were pooled prior to homogenate preparation, making the $n=5$ metabolomic replicates technical aliquots rather than biological replicates. Most of the statistical issues and textual overstatements can be resolved through re-analysis of the existing data, though the missing cross-condition electrophysiological controls should be explicitly acknowledged if not experimentally added.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Intracolonic instillation of microbe-free $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ transfers visceral hypersensitivity to naive recipient mice (Section 3.1, Figure 1, Figure S2): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Both semiquantitative AWR scores and quantitative EMG-based VMR recordings show elevated responses to colorectal distension at 1 h, Day 7, and Day 10 in $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$-treated mice relative to saline and $\text{FS}_{\text{CTR}}$. However, ordinal AWR scores (0–4) across four graded distension volumes (50–200 $\mu\text{L}$) were analyzed by separate one-way ANOVAs rather than an ordinal or repeated-measures mixed-effects model, and the exact number of mice per group is omitted from both Section 2 and the Figure 1 caption.

* **$\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ impairs colonic organoid growth and downregulates mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation pathways without reducing cell viability (Sections 3.2 and 3.6, Figures 2 and 6): [Well Supported].**  
  Morphometric area reductions ($n=5$ independent experiments) and GSEA pathway enrichments (FDR-adjusted $p < 0.05$, showing coordinated suppression of all 13 mitochondrially encoded electron transport chain genes and Hallmark Oxidative Phosphorylation) are consistent and statistically sound.

* **Modulation of DRG neuron hyperexcitability and c-Fos/CGRP expression requires the combined action of $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ and $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}}$ (Sections 3.3 and 3.4, Figures 3 and 4): [Fragile].**  
  The authors test $\text{FS}_{\text{CTR}}$ vs. $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$, $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,CTR}}$ vs. $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}}$, and $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,CTR}} + \text{FS}_{\text{CTR}}$ vs. $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}} + \text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$, but never test the mismatched combinations ($\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}} + \text{FS}_{\text{CTR}}$ or $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,CTR}} + \text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$) and do not fit a two-way interaction model ($\text{FS} \times \text{CM}$ across current steps). In addition, patch-clamp data ($6\text{--}12$ cells from $n=4$ replicates) appear to pool individual cells without accounting for within-replicate clustering, and DRG cultures were harvested from the entire spinal column (Section 2.7) rather than colon-innervating thoracolumbar (T10–L1) and lumbosacral (L6–S1) ganglia.

* **$\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}} + \text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ activates all four sensory fiber subpopulations, with $\text{CGRP}^+/\text{IB4}^+$ $\text{A}\delta$ fibers comprising ~60% of DRG neurons (Section 3.5, Figure 5): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 2.14 (line 104), positive staining for CGRP and IB4 was defined as any neuron exceeding the 25th percentile of the sample fluorescence intensity distribution. Gating two markers at the 25th percentile guarantees that 75% of cells are called positive for each marker, which mechanically produces $\sim 0.75 \times 0.75 = 56.25\%$ double-positive cells and $\sim 0.75 \times 0.25 = 18.75\%$ single-positive cells in every group regardless of true antigen expression. Furthermore, in adult mouse DRG, $\text{CGRP}^+/\text{IB4}^+$ co-labeling marks a subset of polymodal unmyelinated C-fibers, not myelinated $\text{A}\delta$ nociceptors.

* **Specific candidate proteases (*Klk6*, *Capn2*, *Ctsl*) and neuromodulators (*Il1a*, *Bdnf*, *Ereg*, *Gnrh1*) are differentially expressed in $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$-treated organoids and drive neuronal sensitization (Section 3.6, Figure 7): [Overstated].**  
  Unlike the GSEA pathway analysis, individual differentially expressed genes (1,485 DEGs) were called using an unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ threshold in DESeq2 (Section 2.15, line 111; Figure 6A and Figure 7 captions). Across $\sim 20,000$ expressed transcripts, an unadjusted $\alpha = 0.05$ cutoff yields up to 1,000 false positives by chance, and no protein-level quantification in conditioned media or receptor-blocking experiments on DRG neurons were performed.

* **$^1\text{H}$ NMR metabolomics demonstrates significant depletion of SCFAs and hypoxanthine in $\text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ compared to $\text{FS}_{\text{CTR}}$ (Section 3.7, Figure 8): [Fragile].**  
  Sections 2.3 and 2.8 state that fecal pellets from donor mice were pooled prior to homogenization. Consequently, the $n=5$ replicates analyzed by NMR (Section 2.16) represent technical subsamples of a single pooled homogenate per group rather than independent biological donors, invalidating the variance estimate used in the $t$-tests and PLS-DA.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Apply False Discovery Rate (FDR) Correction to Individual DEG Calls in RNA-seq
* **WHY:** Calling 1,485 DEGs at unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ (Section 2.15, line 111) inflates false discoveries across the transcriptome and undermines the candidate signaling gene panels in Figure 7.
* **WHAT:** Filter and report DEGs in Figure 6A and Figure 7 using Benjamini–Hochberg FDR-adjusted $p_{\text{adj}} < 0.05$ (or $p_{\text{adj}} < 0.10$) alongside $|\log_2\text{FC}| \ge 0.58$.
* **HOW:** Using the existing DESeq2 output table in Supplementary Dataset 1, replace the raw `pvalue` filter with `padj <= 0.05`, regenerate the volcano plot (Figure 6A) and gene panels (Figure 7A–D), and clearly distinguish in the text which candidate neuromodulators and proteases survive multiple-testing correction.

#### 2. Replace Percentile-Based Immunofluorescence Gating with Negative-Control Thresholding and Correct Fiber Nomenclature
* **WHY:** Defining marker positivity as $>25\text{th}$ percentile of each group's fluorescence distribution (Section 2.14, line 104) is circular and forces fixed proportions (~60% $\text{CGRP}^+/\text{IB4}^+$) in Figure 5A,B. Moreover, $\text{CGRP}^+/\text{IB4}^+$ neurons are a subset of C-fibers, not $\text{A}\delta$ fibers.
* **WHAT:** Re-threshold the existing ROI fluorescence data using the concurrent no-primary-antibody negative control slides (described in Section 2.14, line 100) and revise the anatomical labels.
* **HOW:** Set the positivity threshold for CGRP and IB4 channels at the mean $+ 3\,\text{SD}$ (or 99th percentile) of the background-subtracted ROI intensity measured on the negative control slides. Re-compute the proportions of $\text{CGRP}^+\text{IB4}^-$, $\text{CGRP}^-\text{IB4}^+$, $\text{CGRP}^+\text{IB4}^+$, and $\text{CGRP}^-\text{IB4}^-$ neurons and their within-group c-Fos intensities in Figure 5, and relabel $\text{CGRP}^+/\text{IB4}^+$ as "polymodal $\text{CGRP}^+/\text{IB4}^+$ C-nociceptors" rather than $\text{A}\delta$ fibers.

#### 3. Re-analyze Electrophysiology and In Vivo CRD Data with Mixed-Effects Models and Clarify Missing Cross-Controls
* **WHY:** Separate one-way ANOVAs ignore repeated current injections within neurons (Figure 3), clustering of $6\text{--}12$ neurons within $n=4$ biological preps, and repeated distension volumes across ordinal AWR scores (Figure 1). Furthermore, comparing two nominal $p$-values ($\text{FS}$ alone non-significant vs. $\text{CM}+\text{FS}$ significant) does not formally test for interaction.
* **WHAT:** Fit linear mixed-effects models (LMMs) for patch-clamp excitability and VMR, and cumulative link mixed models (or two-way repeated-measures non-parametric tests) for ordinal AWR scores.
* **HOW:** For Figure 3, model evoked action potential count using a two-way LMM with fixed effects for Treatment Group, Current Step, and their interaction, and random intercepts for Experimental Replicate ($n=4$) and Neuron ID; also test the $2 \times 2$ interaction between $\text{FS}$ presence and $\text{CM}$ source across the groups available, and explicitly state in the Discussion that testing $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}} + \text{FS}_{\text{CTR}}$ and $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,CTR}} + \text{FS}_{\text{DSS}}$ is required in future work to determine whether $\text{CM}_{\text{FS\,DSS}}$ requires dysbiotic vs. healthy fecal metabolites.

#### 4. Clarify Biological vs. Technical Replication and Anatomical Scope of DRG Cultures
* **WHY:** Pooling fecal pellets across donor mice before homogenization (Sections 2.3 and 2.8) means NMR replicates ($n=5$) do not capture inter-animal microbiome variance, and harvesting DRGs from the whole spinal column (Section 2.7) means most recorded neurons do not innervate the colon.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state in Sections 2.3, 2.8, and 2.16 whether the $n=5$ NMR samples were extracted from 5 independent donor cages/mice or 5 aliquots of one pooled batch; report exact animal numbers ($n$ per group) for Figure 1 and Figure S2; and discuss the use of pan-spinal DRGs and male-only mice as limitations in Section 4.
* **HOW:** Add sample sizes ($n$ mice per group) to Figure 1 and Figure S2 captions, report PLS-DA cross-validation metrics ($R^2Y, Q^2$) and permutation $p$-values in Figure 8A,C, and add a paragraph in the Discussion noting that colon-innervating afferents represent a fraction of total spinal DRGs and that sex-specific visceral pain mechanisms remain to be tested in female mice.
