### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This prospective cohort study within the Tohoku Medical Megabank Community-Based Cohort Study (TMM CommCohort, Miyagi Prefecture, Japan) examines whether baseline circulating plasma metabolites mediate the association between polygenic risk scores (PRSs) and incident type 2 diabetes (T2DM). Using external multi-ancestry GWAS summary statistics, the authors tuned a genome-wide PRS (PRSice-2) and pathway-specific PRSs (PRSet + LASSO across 9,270 pathways) in Type 2 survey participants ($n = 9,801$) and evaluated them in Type 1 survey participants ($n = 12,461$; median follow-up 4.3 years; 354 incident T2DM cases), alongside 43 baseline plasma metabolites quantified by NMR spectroscopy. Modified Poisson regression identified positive associations with incident T2DM for the genome-wide PRS ($\text{RR} = 2.09$ per 1-SD, $95\%\text{ CI}: 1.68$–$2.60$), the nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS ($\text{RR} = 1.32$, $95\%\text{ CI}: 1.03$–$1.70$), and 14 metabolites, while glycine was inversely associated ($\text{RR} = 0.51$). Causal mediation analysis reported that seven metabolites partially mediated $1.0\%$–$7.0\%$ of the genome-wide PRS effect on T2DM incidence. Data and code are not publicly archived and require contacting the corresponding author.

Profiling 43 NMR metabolites and genome-wide genotypes in 12,461 prospective East Asian participants is a valuable empirical resource. However, the study's conclusions are weakened by major analytical flaws and internal inconsistencies across sections: (1) baseline plasma glucose is treated as a "metabolomic mediator" of future T2DM (which is itself defined by crossing a blood glucose/HbA1c threshold), and the mediation models for the remaining 42 metabolites do not adjust for baseline glucose, HbA1c, or fasting duration prior to non-fasting blood draws; (2) the Abstract, Article Highlights, Results text, and Table 2 contradict one another on which metabolites significantly mediate the PRS effect (with Article Highlights claiming mediation by alanine and valine despite Table 2 showing $0.0\%$ mediation for alanine, and five of the seven claimed mediators having $95\%$ CIs bounded by $0.0\%$); and (3) $55\%$ of baseline participants ($n = 15,262$) were excluded due to loss to follow-up without inverse-probability attrition weighting. These issues can be resolved through rigorous statistical re-analysis and careful textual reconciliation.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Genome-wide T2DM PRS ($\text{RR} = 2.09$) and the nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS ($\text{RR} = 1.32$) are positively associated with incident T2DM in a Japanese population independent of lifestyle factors: [Moderately Supported].**  
  External GWAS summary statistics (Suzuki et al.) and an independent tuning subcohort ($n = 9,801$ Type 2 survey) prevent PRS overfitting in the Type 1 target cohort ($n = 12,461$). However, excluding $15,262$ participants ($55\%$ of the eligible baseline cohort) who did not attend the follow-up survey introduces selection bias (as shown in Additional File 3, included participants were older, leaner, and less likely to smoke). Note also that in Discussion line 350, the authors mistakenly argue that restricting to repeat attendees "avoid[s] healthy volunteer bias," whereas excluding non-attendees actually induces healthy-responder attrition bias.

* **Fourteen baseline circulating metabolites are positively associated and glycine is inversely associated with incident T2DM, and 14 metabolites are linearly associated with PRS: [Fragile].**  
  Blood samples were collected under non-fasting conditions at municipal health check-ups (Methods line 92), yet neither time since last meal nor baseline glycemic status (HbA1c) was controlled for when modeling postprandial-sensitive amino acids, glycolysis intermediates, and ketone bodies. Furthermore, there is a direct numerical contradiction in Results line 210: the text states that "PRS was linearly and positively associated with 14 metabolites including..." and then lists **19 metabolites** (`succinic acid, 3-hydroxyisobutyric acid, 2-aminobutyric acid, 3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid, methionine, 2-ketoisocaproic acid, creatine, 2-hydroxybutyric acid, cysteine, arginine, ornithine, tyrosine, histidine, leucine, lysine, valine, glutamine, glucose, and 3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid`), followed by a sentence claiming `glutamic acid` was among them. In addition, Methods line 120 states that logistic regression was used with "the standardized PRS (per 1-SD increase), which was used as the outcome variable"—indicating that the continuous PRS was dichotomized at $> +1\text{ SD}$ ($n = 1,990$, $16.0\%$ of the cohort, mislabeled in Table 1 as "PRS 1-SD increase"), discarding continuous genetic information.

* **Seven circulating metabolites—including glucose, alanine, valine, leucine, glycine, 2-ketoisocaproic acid, 2-aminobutyric acid, 2-hydroxybutyric acid, 3-hydroxyisobutyric acid, and 3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid—significantly mediate 1–7% of the PRS effect on incident T2DM: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, including **baseline plasma glucose** as a mediator of incident T2DM (diagnosed by blood glucose $\ge 200\text{ mg/dL}$ or HbA1c $\ge 6.5\%$) is circular: individuals with high T2DM PRS already exhibit higher baseline glucose and HbA1c at enrollment ($P < 0.001$ in Table 1), meaning baseline glucose simply reflects pre-existing subclinical dysglycemia closer to the diagnostic cutoff. Second, because baseline dysglycemia and insulin resistance elevate branched-chain amino acids and ketoacids, failing to adjust the single-metabolite mediation models in Table 2 for baseline HbA1c/glucose confounds amino acid mediation with baseline prediabetes. Third, the manuscript gives three mutually contradictory lists of significant mediators:
  * **Article Highlights (line 46):** claims "glucose, alanine, and valine" mediate $1$–$7\%$ of the effect, whereas Table 2 (lines 263, 265) reports **alanine** has Natural Indirect Effect $= 1.00$ ($0.99$–$1.01$) and Proportion Mediated $= 0.0\%$ ($-1.0$ to $2.0\%$), and **valine** has NIE $= 1.01$ ($0.99$–$1.01$).
  * **Abstract (line 21):** lists `2-hydroxybutyric acid` (whose NIE 95% CI in Table 2 is $0.99$–$1.02$, non-significant) and omits `3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid`.
  * **Results (line 221) & Table 2:** lists `3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid` instead of `2-hydroxybutyric acid`, while `3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid`, `arginine`, and `lysine` also have NIE $= 1.01$ ($1.00$–$1.02$) in Table 2 but are ignored, and no FDR correction was applied across the 43 mediation tests.

* **For the nitrogen compound transport pathway PRS, no metabolites showed a statistically significant natural direct effect (Table 3): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Results line 221, the authors state that "no metabolites showed a statistically significant natural direct effect (Table 3)." Inspection of Table 3 shows the exact opposite: the **Natural Direct Effect** is statistically significant ($\text{RR} \approx 1.34$–$1.38$, $95\%\text{ CIs} > 1.00$) for nearly every metabolite, whereas the **Natural Indirect Effect** is null ($1.00$ [$0.99$–$1.01$]) across all metabolites.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Separate Baseline Glycemic Status from Non-Glycemic Metabolomic Mediators and Control for Fasting Time
* **WHY:** Treating baseline blood glucose as a metabolomic mediator of a glucose-defined endpoint is circular, and unadjusted baseline dysglycemia (HbA1c) and non-fasting blood draw timing confound branched-chain amino acid and ketone body levels.
* **WHAT:** Remove plasma glucose from the set of candidate non-glycemic metabolomic mediators (or treat baseline glucose/HbA1c as a baseline covariate), adjust all 42 remaining metabolite regressions and mediation analyses for baseline HbA1c and hours since last meal, and perform a sensitivity analysis excluding baseline prediabetic participants ($\text{HbA1c} \ge 5.7\%$ or glucose $\ge 110\text{ mg/dL}$).
* **HOW:** Re-run the modified Poisson regressions (Figure 1) and mediation models (Table 2) adding `baseline_HbA1c` and `fasting_hours` to the covariate vector (`age, sex, education, PC1–PC10, energy_intake, BMI, smoking, alcohol, physical_activity`), and report a joint (multiple-mediator) mediation model for the branched-chain amino acid / ketoacid pathway (leucine, valine, 2-ketoisocaproic acid, 3-methyl-2-oxobutyric acid, 3-hydroxyisobutyric acid) rather than 43 unadjusted single-mediator models.

#### 2. Replace Dichotomized PRS Logistic Models with Continuous Linear/Spline Models
* **WHY:** In Table 1 and Figure 2 (Methods line 120), the continuous standardized PRS was dichotomized at $> +1\text{ SD}$ ($n = 1,990$ vs. $n = 10,471$) and modeled via logistic regression while mislabeling the binary upper tail as "PRS 1-SD increase." Dichotomizing a continuous polygenic score sacrifices statistical power and misleads readers about the exposure scale.
* **WHAT:** Relabel the Table 1 column from `PRS 1-SD increase` to `High PRS (> +1 SD)` (and `Low/Intermediate PRS (≤ +1 SD)`), and re-estimate the associations between continuous standardized PRS (exposure) and continuous standardized metabolites (outcomes) in Figure 2 using multivariable linear regression with restricted cubic splines.
* **HOW:** Fit linear models `metabolite_SD ~ rcs(PRS_SD, 3) + covariates` across all $N = 12,461$ participants, apply Benjamini–Hochberg $\text{FDR} < 0.05$ across the 43 metabolites, and report $\beta$ (change in metabolite SD per 1-SD increase in PRS) and FDR-adjusted $q$-values.

#### 3. Address Follow-Up Attrition Bias via Inverse-Probability Weighting
* **WHY:** Excluding $15,262$ baseline participants ($55\%$) who did not attend the follow-up visit introduces attrition bias (Additional File 3).
* **WHAT:** Apply inverse probability of attrition weighting (IPAW) to the modified Poisson regressions in the Type 1 cohort.
* **HOW:** Using all baseline Type 1 participants without baseline diabetes ($N = 12,461 + 15,262 = 27,723$), fit a logistic regression model predicting follow-up attendance from baseline demographics, lifestyle factors, baseline glucose/HbA1c, and PRS; multiply the robust Poisson regression weights by the stabilized inverse attendance probabilities and verify robustness of the PRS and metabolite RRs.

#### 4. Reconcile Contradictions Across Abstract, Highlights, Results, and Tables
* **WHY:** Multiple direct conflicts between text sections and tables undermine the credibility of the reported findings.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Article Highlights (line 46):** Remove `alanine` and `valine` from the list of significant mediators, as Table 2 reports $0.0\%$ mediation for alanine and a non-significant indirect effect ($0.99$–$1.01$) for valine.
  * **Abstract (line 21) vs. Results (line 221) vs. Table 2:** Harmonize the exact list of significant mediating metabolites based on FDR-adjusted indirect effect $p$-values, and explain why metabolites with identical NIE $= 1.01$ ($1.00$–$1.02$) in Table 2 (`3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid`, `arginine`, `lysine`) were excluded while others were included.
  * **Results (line 210) vs. Abstract (line 21):** Fix the count in line 210 where the text states "14 metabolites" but lists **19** metabolites, and clarify whether `glutamic acid` was associated with PRS.
  * **Results (line 221):** Change "no metabolites showed a statistically significant natural direct effect (Table 3)" to "natural **indirect** effect (Table 3)."
  * **Table 2 & Table 3 Typos:** Correct `3-Hyudroxybutyric acid` (lines 257, 314) to `3-Hydroxybutyric acid`, `3-methyl-2-oxiovaleric acid` (lines 21, 199, 210) to `3-methyl-2-oxovaleric acid`, `Glycerol` CI `(-1.0–.0)` (line 255) to `(-1.0–0.0)`, `Serine` CI `(-2.0–60)` (line 316) to `(-2.0–6.0)`, and `Lactic acid` CI `(-6.03.0)` (line 324) to `(-6.0–3.0)`.
