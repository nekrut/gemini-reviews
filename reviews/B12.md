### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript constructs a single-cell transcriptomic atlas of the human lung by profiling ~75,000 cells (65,662 via 10x Chromium and 9,404 via Smart-Seq2 [SS2]) across proximal, medial, and distal uninvolved lung tissue and matched peripheral blood from $N=3$ surgical lobectomy patients, supplemented by bulk RNA-seq of 21 FACS-purified peripheral blood immune subsets and spatial validation via immunohistochemistry and single-molecule fluorescence *in situ* hybridization (smFISH). By magnetically and flow-sorting tissue suspensions to balance epithelial, endothelial, stromal, and immune compartments and performing iterative per-subject Louvain clustering in Seurat, the authors define 58 transcriptionally distinct cell populations (41 of 45 classical histological cell types plus 14 newly described populations/subtypes and 3 proliferative states). They derive optimal marker panels (via Matthews Correlation Coefficient), transcription factor enrichments, hormone/chemokine/CellPhoneDB ligand-receptor networks, and disease/viral-receptor expression maps. Comparing the human SS2 profiles against ~35,500 mouse lung cells from *Tabula Muris Senis* (plus 522 lineage-labeled mouse cells), they report that 17 of the 58 human molecular types (including 12 of 14 newly identified types) are absent in mouse and that only ~6% of expressed orthologous genes maintain fully conserved cellular expression patterns across species. Raw human data are deposited under controlled access in EGA (`EGAS00001004344`), mouse data in SRA (`PRJNA632939`), processed objects on Synapse and `cellxgene`, and full analysis code on GitHub (`krasnowlab/HLCA`).

As a primary anatomical and transcriptomic resource, the human lung cell census is well-validated: combining high-throughput 10x droplet capture with deep full-length SS2 sequencing, compartment-balancing MACS/FACS enrichment, and extensive smFISH tissue localization across independent donors establishes the spatial reality of key novel stromal, endothelial, and epithelial subpopulations (e.g., alveolar vs. adventitial fibroblasts, fibromyocytes, Bro1/Bro2 bronchial vessels, AT2-s). However, the cross-species evolutionary claims—that mice have evolutionarily lost or failed to evolve 17 of 58 human lung cell types (including 86% of newly identified types) and that 94% of expressed genes have diverged in cellular expression pattern—are overstated relative to the asymmetric experimental design. Specifically, only 1 of the 3 human patients (Patient 3) had proximal bronchus tissue profiled, human lungs underwent elastase + Liberase digestion with dedicated compartment enrichment whereas mouse lungs were dissociated with Liberase alone without equivalent anatomical microdissection of large cartilaginous airways or bronchial vasculature, and cross-species differential expression (`MAST` on single cells without donor-level pseudobulk random effects) and ad hoc binarization thresholds severely inflate false-positive evolutionary divergence and "expression switching." These issues can be resolved through matched anatomical/sampling controls, donor-level pseudobulk statistical modeling, and tempered evolutionary framing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Identification and spatial localization of 58 molecular cell populations across the four human lung compartments: [Well Supported].**  
  Combining MACS/FACS compartment balancing with both 10x and SS2 platforms captures cell types spanning five orders of magnitude in abundance (from capillaries down to ionocytes at 0.01%). Key newly defined subtypes (AT2-s, Bas-px, Bas-d, Cil-px, Bro1/Bro2, Cap-a, alveolar vs. adventitial fibroblasts, fibromyocytes, lipofibroblasts, and IGSF21+/EREG+/TREM2+ dendritic cells) are orthogonally validated by smFISH and immunostaining in independent tissue sections (Figs. 1–2, Extended Data Figs. 3–5).

* **Derivation of optimal marker panels (~200 genes), master transcription factors (e.g., *MYRF* in AT1, *TBX5* in pericytes), and disease/viral-receptor expression maps: [Well Supported].**  
  Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) ranking demonstrates that 2–4 markers per population saturate classification accuracy (Extended Data Fig. 5b), and smFISH confirms selective expression of *MYRF* in 97% of *AGER*+ AT1 cells, *TBX5* in 92% of *COX4I2*+ pericytes, *KCNK3* in 91% of pericytes, and *SERPINA1* in 93% of AT2 cells (Extended Data Figs. 5c–d, 8c–e).

* **Mice lack 17 of the 58 human lung molecular cell types (29%), including 12 of the 14 newly discovered types, reflecting major evolutionary diversification of mammalian lung cell types: [Overstated].**  
  Attributing the absence of 17 clusters in the mouse dataset to evolutionary gain/loss ignores stark anatomical and technical asymmetries between the human and mouse datasets: (1) human proximal bronchi (containing pseudostratified epithelium, submucosal glands, bronchial vessels Bro1/Bro2, Bas-px, Cil-px, serous and mucous cells) were dissected from cartilaginous intrapulmonary bronchi, whereas mouse intrapulmonary airways lack submucosal glands and bronchial systemic circulation and the mouse whole-lung dissociation did not dissect extrapulmonary trachea/mainstem bronchi; (2) human lungs were digested with Liberase DL + elastase to release matrix-embedded stromal cells, whereas mouse lungs were digested with Liberase DL alone (Methods, line 134); (3) human proximal tissue came from a single donor ($N=1$, Patient 3), and two of the 58 clusters (clusters 45 and 56) were only found in a single human subject; and (4) clustering resolution was tuned manually per subject and compartment rather than jointly integrated across species.

* **Only ~6% of expressed lung genes have conserved cellular expression patterns between mouse and human, while 94% have undergone evolutionary gain/loss, expansion/contraction, or cell-type switching: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To classify genes into Types 0–3 (Fig. 4e, Extended Data Fig. 12h), the authors binarize each gene as "expressed (1)" or "not expressed (0)" in each of ~40 aligned cell types using hard thresholds on median non-zero expression and percent positive cells, and require a gene to have identical binary calls across *every single one* of the ~40 cell types (`all elements equal 2 or 0`) to be classified as "Type 0 (conserved)". Under independent binary classification noise across $K \approx 40$ cell types (especially when many SS2 cell types have very small sample sizes, down to $n=5$ cells), even if every cell type had a 95% probability of concordant binary calling between species, the probability of zero discrepancies across 40 cell types is $0.95^{40} = 12.9\%$. Thus, the reported "94% evolutionary divergence" is largely a mathematical artifact of requiring 100% binary concordance across dozens of unequally sampled clusters, compounded by using cell-level `MAST` $p$-values (which treat thousands of cells from $N=3$ humans as independent biological replicates, inflating Type I error).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Hard Multi-Cluster Binarization and Cell-Level `MAST` Tests with Donor-Level Pseudobulk Models for Cross-Species Divergence
* **WHY:** Requiring identical binary thresholding across ~40 cell types guarantees that >90% of genes will be flagged as "diverged" (Types 1–3) due to sampling noise and dropout in small SS2 clusters ($n \ge 5$ cells). Simultaneously, running `MAST` across all pooled cells treats cells from the same patient ($N=3$) as independent replicates, producing artificially tiny $p$-values ($P < 10^{-5}$) for donor-specific or batch effects.
* **WHAT:** Re-evaluate cross-species gene expression conservation using donor-level pseudobulk profiles (aggregating cells by subject $\times$ cell type) and a continuous correlation / random-effects model that accounts for cluster sample size and dropout uncertainty.
* **HOW:**
  1. For each homologous cell type with $\ge 2$ donors per species, compute donor-level pseudobulk $\log(\text{CPM}+1)$ matrices and run `DESeq2` or `limma-voom` with donor as the unit of replication to recalculate the number of $\ge 20$-fold diverged genes in Fig. 4b and Extended Data Fig. 11a.
  2. To estimate the false-discovery rate of the Type 0/1/2/3 binary classification scheme, perform a within-species split-half control: split the human SS2 dataset into two halves (e.g., Patient 1/2 vs. Patient 3, or random 50/50 cell splits matched to the mouse cell counts per cluster) and run the exact same `V = (a - b) + 2ab` binarization pipeline. Report the baseline percentage of genes classified as "Type 0 conserved" between two human replicates versus between human and mouse.

#### 2. Control for Anatomical Region (Proximal vs. Distal) and Dissociation Protocol (Elastase) in the "Missing Mouse Cell Types" Analysis
* **WHY:** Many of the 17 human lung clusters reported as "missing" in mouse (e.g., Bas-px, Cil-px, serous, mucous, Bro1, Bro2, fibromyocytes) are enriched in or restricted to the human proximal bronchus sample (Patient 3) or depend on elastase digestion of dense elastic lamina. In mice, submucosal glands and pseudostratified basal/ciliated architectures reside in the extrapulmonary trachea rather than intrapulmonary lobes.
* **WHAT:** Stratify the 17 "missing" cell types by whether they are restricted to proximal cartilaginous airways/submucosal glands ($N=1$ human sample, P3), whether they exist in mouse trachea datasets (e.g., *Tabula Muris* trachea), or whether they represent finer sub-clustering splits of shared canonical types.
* **HOW:** Add a column to Supplementary Table 6 categorizing each of the 17 human-only clusters into: (a) anatomical structure present in human intrapulmonary bronchi but restricted to extrapulmonary trachea in mouse (e.g., serous, mucous, bronchial vessels), (b) states/subtypes whose markers are co-expressed within a broader mouse cluster (e.g., lipid-handling genes *Plin2/Apoe* inside mouse alveolar fibroblasts vs. separate human LipF), and (c) candidate lineage-specific cell types found in distal alveoli sampled comparably in both species.

#### 3. Quantify Inter-Patient Reproducibility for Proximal-Specific Clusters and Immune Residency Scores
* **WHY:** Proximal lung tissue (`Proximal 3`) was sequenced from only a single patient (Patient 3), and matched peripheral blood scRNA-seq was collected from two patients (Patients 1 and 3). Consequently, proximal-versus-distal cell abundance ratios in Extended Data Fig. 3e and lung-versus-blood residency classifications in Fig. 2a cannot separate inter-individual variation from anatomical enrichment in scRNA-seq counts.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state in the main text and Fig. 2a / Extended Data Fig. 3e legends that scRNA-seq proximal enrichment is derived from $N=1$ donor (while smFISH validations were replicated in $N \ge 2$ independent donors), and report per-patient breakdowns for the immune residency and egression scores.
* **HOW:** In Fig. 2a and 2c, plot or tabulate the lung-vs.-blood enrichment and egression signature scores separately for Patient 1 and Patient 3 to show concordant directionality across both individuals with matched blood.
