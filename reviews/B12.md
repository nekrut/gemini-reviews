### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

#### Executive Summary
This manuscript synthesizes the statistical architecture, modeling philosophy, and expanded functional capabilities of the `limma` R/Bioconductor package for gene expression analysis across both microarrays and RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq). The authors detail how `limma` unifies multi-factor linear modeling (`lmFit`, `contrasts.fit`) with parametric empirical Bayes variance moderation (`eBayes`, incorporating mean-variance trends and outlier-robust gene-wise shrinkage), observation- and sample-level precision weighting (`voom`, `arrayWeights`, `voomWithQualityWeights`), consensus intrablock random-effects correlation (`duplicateCorrelation`, `intraspotCorrelation`), minimum-fold-change hypothesis testing (`treat`), exon-level differential splicing (`diffSplice`), and higher-level pathway and signature inference (`genas` for bivariate biological correlation of log-fold-changes, `camera` for inter-gene correlation-adjusted competitive gene set testing, `roast`/`mroast`/`romer` for residual-space rotation testing, `goana`, and `propTrueNull`). Illustrative diagnostic and summary displays are demonstrated on published RNA-seq (`Pickrell et al.`, `GSE52870`) and two-colour microarray (`Ritchie et al.`, `GSE2593`) datasets (Figures 3 and 4). The package is openly distributed via Bioconductor with a >120-page user guide containing 10 fully worked, reproducible case studies.

As a comprehensive methodological review and software flagship paper, this work clearly articulates *why* specific statistical choices are made—for instance, why parametric empirical Bayes with variance-trend modeling and residual-space rotation (`roast`) outperform naive sample-permutation tests in small, blocked, or multifactorial experiments where exchangeability is violated. Because the primary validation of individual estimators (`voom` [10], `camera` [56], `roast` [55], `normexp` [22, 23], robust `eBayes` [18]) was established in dedicated statistical papers, this article focuses on their unified integration rather than presenting new simulation tables; the only notable omission is a quantitative benchmark or guidance on the minimum count/sample-size regime required for the `diffSplice` exon-level linear model and `duplicateCorrelation` consensus assumption to remain well-calibrated on sparse RNA-seq data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

#### Detailed Rubric Evaluation

* **Claim 1 — Precision weighting of $\log_2$-counts-per-million via `voom` (and `voomWithQualityWeights`) unlocks the full `limma` linear modeling, random-effects blocking, and empirical Bayes pipeline for RNA-seq data with accuracy comparable to negative-binomial frameworks and superior speed: [Well Supported].**  
  Lines 47 and 53 (and Figure 3A) explain how `voom` captures the empirical mean-variance relationship of log-CPM values and converts fitted variances into inverse-variance observation weights $w_{gj}$ passed into `lmFit`. The statistical calibration and power relative to `edgeR`/`DESeq` are backed by Law et al. (2014) [10] and independent SEQC benchmarks [8, 21].

* **Claim 2 — Trended and outlier-robust parametric empirical Bayes variance moderation (`eBayes`) increases effective degrees of freedom in small-sample studies while protecting against hyper-variable outlier genes: [Well Supported].**  
  Line 47 and line 71 describe how `limma` shrinks gene-wise residual variances $s_g^2$ toward a global or abundance-trended prior $s_0^2(A_g)$ while assigning individualized, reduced shrinkage weights to hyper-variable genes (Phipson et al., 2013 [18]), preventing outlier genes from generating false positives when squeezed toward a small prior variance.

* **Claim 3 — Higher-order gene set and signature methods (`camera`, `roast`/`mroast`, `romer`, `genas`) overcome the invalid conditional-independence assumptions of naive rank/overlap tests and the exchangeability failures of sample permutation in complex designs: [Well Supported].**  
  Line 47 ("Parametric modelling versus permutation methods") and lines 77 / 1 (chunk 2) provide a rigorous critique of sample-label permutation in multi-factor experiments (where permuting all samples tests an uninteresting composite null and fails under blocking or unequal quality weights) and naive gene-ranking tests (`geneSetTest`), showing how `camera` corrects for inter-gene correlation via a variance inflation factor [56] and how `roast` performs exact multivariate normal rotation in the orthogonal residual space of the nuisance covariates [55, 58].

* **Claim 4 — Exon-level linear modeling via `diffSplice` provides fast, well-calibrated differential splicing detection across both exon microarrays and RNA-seq exon counts: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Line 71 ("Testing for differential splicing") introduces `diffSplice`, `plotSplice`, and `topSplice` as a major new capability for RNA-seq and exon arrays, claiming that *"This approach is considerably faster than alternative approaches to differential splicing, making large-scale surveys of differential exon usage feasible."* Unlike `voom`, `camera`, and `roast`, however, no citation to a standalone validation study or internal simulation benchmark is provided in the manuscript to document the Type I error, power, or runtime of `diffSplice` relative to established exon-usage tools (e.g., `DEXSeq`) particularly when individual exons have low or zero read counts.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### Major Issues, Minor Issues & Concrete Recommendations

#### 1. Provide Validation References or Empirical Calibration Details for `diffSplice` on Low-Count RNA-Seq Exons (Major Issue)
* **WHY:** The Abstract (line 9), Introduction (line 23), and Results (line 71) highlight differential splicing analysis (`diffSplice`) of RNA-seq data as one of the two major new expansions of `limma`. However, whereas every other methodological module cites a peer-reviewed validation paper, `diffSplice` has no citation or mathematical description of how exon-level log-fold-changes are contrasted against gene-level log-fold-changes, nor how `voom` precision weights behave when short exons have zero or near-zero read counts.
* **WHAT:** State the mathematical test statistics computed by `diffSplice` (exon-level moderated $t$-tests and gene-level Simes-adjusted or $F$-test summaries) and provide a benchmark reference or brief runtime/FDR comparison against `DEXSeq`.
* **HOW:** Add a short paragraph or table in "Testing for differential splicing" (line 71) specifying the `diffSplice` model equations, recommended minimum exon count filtering (`filterByExpr`), and execution time on a standard RNA-seq dataset.

#### 2. Discuss the Scope of the Homogeneous Intrablock Correlation Assumption in `duplicateCorrelation` (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** Line 71 ("Blocking and random effects") notes that `duplicateCorrelation` constrains all genes to share a single consensus intrablock correlation $\rho$ (estimated by averaging $\tanh^{-1}(r_g)$ across genes). While effective for technical array replicates, in repeated-measures clinical cohorts or multi-tissue designs, subject-level heritability/correlation can vary substantially between housekeeping genes and immune or cell-cycle genes.
* **WHAT:** Briefly state the operating assumptions and shrinkage benefits of pooling $\rho$ across genes, and note when a gene-specific mixed model might diverge from a single consensus $\rho$.
* **HOW:** Add 2–3 sentences in "Blocking and random effects" explaining why shrinking gene-wise block correlations to a single consensus value stabilizes small-sample generalized least squares (GLS) fits.
