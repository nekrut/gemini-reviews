### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Alnaji et al. investigate the longitudinal emergence, competition, and co-evolution of deletion-containing viral genomes (DelVGs) and wild-type (WT) influenza A virus (`rPR8`) across 72 serial passages in MDCK cells initiated at $\text{MOI} = 10\text{ TCID}_{50}/\text{cell}$ in two replicate lineages (`L1` and `L2`), supplemented by six additional replicate lineages analyzed at passage 34. Using hemagglutination (HA) and $\text{TCID}_{50}$ assays alongside Illumina junction-spanning next-generation sequencing (NGS), the authors document sustained Von Magnus oscillations in particle-to-infectivity ratios (`Fig. 1A`), stochastic junction formation across replicate lineages (`Fig. 1C`, `Fig. S1B`), a sharp contraction in Shannon entropy of polymerase-segment DelVGs (`Fig. 2`), and selective sweeps of 1–2 dominant `PB2` DelVGs (`129_2092` [`DI250`] in `L1`; `128_2094` [`DI248`] and `129_2096` in `L2`; `Fig. 3`). Reconstituting three `L1` `PB2` clones (`DI250`, `DI143`, `DI289`) with WT virus over 16 passages failed to reproduce the dominance of `DI250` (`Fig. 4B`). Variant calling on junction-spanning reads identified a convergent `PB2` mutation (`A94G`, encoding `T23A`) that swept to high frequency after passage 15 specifically within dominant `PB2` DelVGs (`DI250` in `L1`, `DI248` in `L2`) while remaining absent from WT `PB2` (`Fig. 5`). Reverse-genetics characterization showed that `A94G` severely attenuates WT `PR8` and reverts upon passage (`Fig. 6A`), but significantly increases `DI250` polymerase inhibition in a minigenome assay ($p = 0.0028$, `Fig. 6B` left), reduces infectious WT plaque yield in co-infection ($p = 0.0162$, `Fig. 6B` right), and accelerates `DI250` accumulation over 14 passages (`Fig. 6C`, `Fig. 7A–B`). Raw sequencing data (`PRJNA1366758`), custom code (`10.5281/zenodo.20421313`), and a multi-sheet Source Data workbook (`MOESM4_ESM.xlsx`) with embedded Excel formulas are publicly provided.

The identification of `PB2 A94G` as an adaptive DelVG mutation exhibiting antagonistic pleiotropy (beneficial in defective interfering `PB2` RNA, deleterious in full-length WT `PB2`) is a solid empirical finding supported by orthogonal virological assays (`Fig. 5`, `Fig. 6A–B`). However, a forensic audit of the statistical tests, bioinformatic filters, and embedded spreadsheet formulas in `MOESM4_ESM.xlsx` reveals multiple quantitative and methodological flaws that undermine the secondary conclusions and pipeline validations:
1. **Chi-square tests applied to percentages ($N=100$) instead of raw counts (`Fig. S3B` and `Fig. S7`):** In both the reading-frame enrichment analysis (`Fig. S3B`) and the simulated NGS pipeline validation (`Fig. S7`), Excel's `CHISQ.TEST` was applied to percentage values summing to $100$ rather than raw counts. At `L1 P72` ($N=7$ junctions: raw counts `4, 2, 1`), this inflates the $\chi^2$ statistic by $100/7 = 14.29\times$ (from the true $\chi^2 = 2.00, p = 0.3679$ to $\chi^2 = 28.57, p = 6.25 \times 10^{-7}$), creating the spurious `p < 0.001` result highlighted in `Fig. S3B` and discussed in the main text. Conversely, in `Fig. S7` (`DI10`, $T = 194,246$ aligned reads), feeding percentages into `CHISQ.TEST` divides $\chi^2$ by $1,942.5\times$ (turning $\chi^2 = 3,188.4, p < 10^{-300}$ from an $18\%$ relative bias into $\chi^2 = 1.64, p = 0.200$) to claim "no significant difference."
2. **Unpaired two-sample $t$-test on complementary proportions ($X_i$ vs. $100 - X_i$) and un-barcoded amplicon deduplication in the packaging assay (`Fig. 7C` & `Fig. S6B`):** In `Fig. 7C`, the percentages of `DI250` ($X_i$) and `DI250:A94G` ($Y_i = 100 - X_i$) within the same $n=3$ co-transfection libraries ($\rho = -1.00$) are compared via an unpaired two-sample Student's $t$-test (`T.TEST(W28:Y28, Z28:AB28, 2, 2)`, $\text{df}=4, p = 0.436$). Worse, removing sequence duplicates from un-barcoded 379-nt Nanopore RT-PCR amplicons differing at a single nucleotide (`A94G`) discarded all error-free reads except one per allele and retained only **$42\text{--}50$ error-bearing reads per sample** (`min_freq = 1/42` to `1/50`).
3. **Time-series and compartment pooling in `Fig. S5C` and `Fig. S5E`, cross-passage packaging ratios (`Fig. S5F`), and $>4\text{--}14\times$ input skew in `Fig. 4B`:** Homoscedastic unpaired $t$-tests (`type=2`, misreported as Welch's $t$-test) are run by pooling 14 oscillating passages (`Fig. S5C`, masking a $5.5$- to $32.4$-fold NP reduction at `P8–P9`) or pooling intracellular 6 hpi lysates with extracellular 24 hpi supernatants that differ by 100-fold (`Fig. S5E`). Moreover, the three-way competition in `Fig. 4B` suffered from a $3.9$- to $13.8$-fold lower starting copy number for `DI250` at `Seed`/`P2` (`MOESM4_ESM.xlsx`, sheet `Fig4`) and was never repeated with `DI250:A94G`. All of these issues can be resolved through statistical re-analysis, unfiltered amplicon allele counting, and text/figure corrections using existing data, though proving that `A94G` reverses the `DI250` vs. `DI143`/`DI289` hierarchy would require a direct three-way competition experiment.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Convergent emergence and antagonistic pleiotropy of `PB2 A94G` (`T23A`) in DelVG vs. WT backgrounds (`Figs. 5, 6A–B`): Well Supported.**  
  The emergence of `A94G` in `DI250` (`L1`) and `DI248` (`L2`) after passage 15 while remaining absent from WT `PB2` (`Fig. 5`), its rapid attenuation ($>100$-fold lower `PB2` RNA at `P1–P2`, `Fig. 6A`) and reversion in recombinant WT `PR8`, and its enhancement of polymerase interference in minigenome (`Firefly/Renilla` reduced from $0.144 \pm 0.013$ to $0.068 \pm 0.008$, two-tailed Welch's $t$-test $p = 0.0028$, `Fig. 6B` left) and co-infection plaque assays ($2.07 \times 10^5$ vs. $1.05 \times 10^5\text{ PFU/mL}$, $n=5$, Welch's $t$-test $p = 0.0162$, `Fig. 6B` right) are internally consistent and backed by raw data in `MOESM4_ESM.xlsx` (`Fig 6A & B`, rows 25–56).

* **Deletion length and reading frame do not govern DelVG competitive success, with a significant `+0` frame bias at `P72` in `L1` (`Fig. S3A–B`) and validated WT:DelVG read quantification (`Fig. S7`): Methodologically Flawed.**  
  1. *Chi-square tests on percentages (`Fig. S3B`):* In `MOESM4_ESM.xlsx` (`Fig S3`, cells `AK35:AM46`), `_xlfn.CHISQ.TEST` is applied to percentage columns (`+0`, `+1`, `+2` summing to $100\%$) against expected values of `=100/3` ($33.33\%$). At `L1 P72`, only $N=7$ unique junctions remain (`4` in `+0` [$57.14\%$], `2` in `+1` [$28.57\%$], `1` in `+2` [$14.29\%$]; `Fig S3 (sheet 1)`). On the true integer counts `[4, 2, 1]` vs. `[7/3, 7/3, 7/3]`, Pearson's $\chi^2 = 2.00$ ($p = 0.3679$). Feeding percentages treats $N=7$ as $N=100$, multiplying $\chi^2$ by $100/7 = 14.29\times$ to yield $\chi^2 = 28.57$ ($p = 6.25 \times 10^{-7}$), which is reported as `<0.001` in red in `Fig. S3B` and discussed on p. 4. Similarly, at `L2 P34` ($N=43$, raw counts `[10, 20, 13]`), feeding percentages inflates $\chi^2$ from $3.67$ ($p = 0.1593$) to $8.55$ ($p = 0.01394576$). Conversely, at `P8` ($N > 200$), using percentages deflates $\chi^2$ by $>2\times$.  
  2. *Simulated WT:DelVG validation (`Fig. S7`):* In `Fig 2 & S7 (Sheet 1)` cells `AG62:AG64`, the authors compare expected vs. observed WT:DelVG read percentages in three simulated libraries (`DI10`: $40.02\%$ observed vs. $33.96\%$ expected across $T = 194,246$ aligned reads; `DI100`: $73.65\%$ vs. $79.02\%$ across $T = 126,647$ reads; `DI200`: $82.30\%$ vs. $86.21\%$ across $T = 102,376$ reads) by running `CHISQ.TEST` on the percentages (`AC62:AD62` vs. `X62:Y62`), obtaining $p = 0.200, 0.187, 0.256$ and claiming *"no significant difference"* (`p. 11`). On the actual read counts (`R62:S62`), $\chi^2 = 3,188.4, 2,211.7,$ and $1,319.1$ (all $p < 10^{-280}$), with an $18\%$ relative overestimation of DelVG proportion at low DelVG abundance (`DI10`).  
  3. *Unweighted junctions and microdeletion inclusion (`Fig. S3A–B`):* Every detected junction is weighted equally ($1/N$) regardless of read count (`NGS RS`), even though by `P72` a single in-frame 379-nt species (`DI250`, `129_2092`) accounts for $>85\%$ of all `PB2` reads. In addition, `Fig S3 (sheet 1)` includes tiny microdeletions of 25–43 nt (`DI length = 2298–2316 nt` out of `2341 nt`, e.g., `148_192` at `R30`) alongside canonical ~1,900-nt internal deletions.

* **`A94G` has no effect on viral packaging efficiency (`Fig. 7C`, `Fig. S5F`, `Fig. S6B`): Fragile / Methodologically Flawed.**  
  1. *Unpaired two-sample $t$-test on complementary proportions (`Fig. 7C`):* In `Fig 7C & S6B` cell `AD28`, the packaged proportions of `DI250` (`W28:Y28` $= [50.0, 53.4, 38.1]$) and `DI250:A94G` (`Z28:AB28` $= [50.0, 46.6, 61.9] = 100 - \text{DI250}$) from the same three co-transfection supernatants are compared using `_xlfn.T.TEST(W28:Y28, Z28:AB28, 2, 2)` ($p = 0.436$, $\text{df}=4$). Because $Y_i \equiv 100 - X_i$, the two groups are perfectly negatively correlated ($\rho = -1.00$), violating two-sample independence and inflating the $t$-statistic by $\sqrt{2}$ relative to a one-sample $t$-test of $X_i$ against $\mu_0 = 50\%$ ($t = -0.611, \text{df}=2, p = 0.603$).  
  2. *Amplicon deduplication artifact (`Fig. 7C` & `Fig. S6B`):* In `Fig 7C & S6B` cell `L5`, the authors state that for Nanopore sequencing of the 379-nt RT-PCR amplicon, *"duplicate reads were removed, and only reads with a length ≥ 370 nt... and a quality score ≥ 30 were retained."* Because PCR amplicons of `DI250` and `DI250:A94G` share identical primer termini and differ at only a single base (`A94G`), sequence deduplication without UMIs collapses error-free reads and retains only reads containing random Nanopore/PCR errors at other positions. In the LoFreq variant tables (`Fig 7C & S6B`, rows 24–371), the minimum allele frequencies across the six libraries are exact reciprocals of **$42$ to $50$ total reads** (`1/50 = 0.0200` in `1_Control_1:10`; `1/46 = 0.021739` in `2_Control_1:1` and `3_Control_10:1`; `1/48 = 0.020833` in `4_Rep1`; `1/45 = 0.022222` in `5_Rep2`; `1/42 = 0.02381` in `6_Rep3`), with 41–70 spurious error calls per 379-nt amplicon.  
  3. *Cross-passage packaging index (`Fig. S5F`) and compartment-pooled $t$-tests (`Fig. S5C`, `Fig. S5E`):* In `Fig 7 & S5E` (`AD32:AG33`), the packaging index is computed across passages (`P4-External / P3-Internal` and `P5-External / P4-Internal`), spanning an entire re-infection cycle between 6 hpi of passage $n$ and 24 hpi of passage $n+1$; at `P5 Ex / P4 In`, the ratio for `DI250:A94G` (`0.545, 0.481`) is less than half the mean of `DI250` (`1.472, 0.731`), driven by a $23$-fold discrepancy between `DI250` replicates at `P4-internal` (`1,600` vs. `37,138` copies/$\mu$L). Meanwhile, the `NP` control tests in `Fig. S5C` (`AE63`: `T.TEST(AC27:AC40, AC63:AC76, 2, 2)`, $p = 0.576$) and `Fig. S5E` (`AB40`: `T.TEST(Y40:Y44, Z40:Z44, 2, 2)`, $p = 0.843$) use homoscedastic Student's $t$-tests (`type=2`, despite the legends claiming Welch's $t$-test) that pool 14 oscillating passages spanning $3.5$ logs of viral titer (`Fig. S5C`) or pool 6 hpi intracellular lysates with 24 hpi extracellular supernatants differing by $100$-fold (`Fig. S5E`). In `Fig. S5C`, this time-pooled test obscures a $5.5$-fold (`P8`: $7.09 \times 10^4$ vs. $3.92 \times 10^5$) to $32.4$-fold (`P9`: $3.38 \times 10^3$ vs. $1.09 \times 10^5$) reduction in `NP` copies by `DI250:A94G`, contradicting the main text (`p. 7`).

* **Failure of clonal `DI250` to outcompete `DI143` and `DI289` in three-way competition (`Fig. 4B`) is explained by the absence of `A94G`, and five-log Von Magnus oscillations are sustained throughout 72 passages (`Fig. 1A`): Overstated / Fragile.**  
  1. *Starting input imbalance and missing three-way control (`Fig. 4B`):* Although the Methods (`p. 9`) specify equal input of $1\text{ PB2-GE/cell}$ for each DIP (`DI250`, `DI143`, `DI289`), the qPCR data in `MOESM4_ESM.xlsx` (`Fig4`, rows 25–26, 36, 46) show that at `Seed`/`P2`, `DI250` started at $1,035$ copies/$\mu$L (`Rep 1 Seed`), $952$ (`Rep 1 P2`), $202$ (`Rep 2 P2`), and $870$ (`Rep 3 P2`)—which is **$3.9$- to $13.8$-fold lower** than `DI143` ($2,056\text{--}4,438$ copies/$\mu$L) and `DI289` ($2,786\text{--}4,847$ copies/$\mu$L). Moreover, standard curve slopes in `Fig4` (`R55–R58`) correspond to amplification efficiencies of $117.7\%$ for `WT` ($\text{slope} = -2.960$) and $114.2\%$ for `DI143` ($\text{slope} = -3.024$), versus $92.3\%$ for `DI250` ($\text{slope} = -3.520$). Crucially, the authors never repeated the three-way competition substituting `DI250:A94G` against `DI143` and `DI289`.  
  2. *Below-LOD HA imputation in `Fig. 1A`:* In `Fig 1A & Fig S1` (`O25:P96`), passages with undetectable hemagglutination ($<1\text{ HAU}$) were assigned an imputed floor of `0.5 HAU` ($= 2.515 \times 10^7$ particles/mL). When infectious titers simultaneously crashed to $180\text{--}560\text{ TCID}_{50}/\text{mL}$ (e.g., `L2` `P9` at `R33` and `P15` at `R39`), dividing the constant `0.5 HAU` floor by a low $\text{TCID}_{50}$ generated apparent particle-to-infectivity ratios of $4.49 \times 10^4$ to $1.40 \times 10^5$ despite zero detected hemagglutination. In addition, the HA dilution scheme shifted between `P1–P8` ($10 \times 2^k$: `40, 80, 160, 320`) and `P9–P72` ($2^k$: `0.5, 1, 2, 4, ..., 256`).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Percentage-Based `CHISQ.TEST` Calls with Raw-Count Goodness-of-Fit / Exact Multinomial Tests and Abundance-Weighted Metrics (`Fig. S3A–B`, `Fig. S7`)
* **WHY:** Applying Pearson's $\chi^2$ test to percentages summing to $100$ scales the test statistic by $100/N$, creating severe false positives when $N < 100$ (`L1 P72`, $N=7$, $p$ inflated from $0.368$ to $6.25 \times 10^{-7}$; `L2 P34`, $N=43$, $p$ inflated from $0.159$ to $0.0139$) and false negatives when $N > 100$ (`Fig. S7`, $T > 10^5$, $p$ deflated from $<10^{-280}$ to $>0.18$). Furthermore, unweighted junction lists ignore the selective sweep of `DI250` ($>85\%$ of `PB2` reads).
* **WHAT:** Recompute all reading-frame tests in `Fig. S3B` on raw junction counts (using an exact multinomial test when expected counts $<5$, as at `P72` where $E_i = 7/3 = 2.33$ and $3/3 = 1.00$), report read-abundance-weighted deletion length and reading-frame distributions alongside unweighted counts after filtering out microdeletions ($<200$ nt deleted), and report the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE $= 4.4\text{--}6.1$ percentage points) in `Fig. S7` instead of a $N=100$ percentage $\chi^2$ test.
* **HOW:** In `MOESM4_ESM.xlsx` sheet `Fig S3`, replace the percentage values in `AK27:AM30` and `AK512:AM515` with raw integer counts $O_{ij}$ and expected counts $E_{ij} = N_i / 3$. Reconcile the discrepancy between the `L1` sample sizes in `Fig S3` (`N = 479, 83, 68, 3` in `AJ27:AJ30`) and those plotted in `Fig. S3B` (`N = 222, 115, 54, 7` from `Fig S3 (sheet 1)`). In `Fig S3 (sheet 1)`, weight each junction's retained length (`E7:E1530`) and frame (`J7:L1530`) by its normalized read support (`D7:D1530`).

#### 2. Re-Quantify Nanopore Packaging Libraries Without Un-Barcoded Sequence Deduplication and Use a One-Sample / Binomial Mixed Model (`Fig. 7C`, `Fig. S6B`)
* **WHY:** Deduplicating identical full-length reads ($\ge 370$ nt) from an un-barcoded 379-nt PCR amplicon where `DI250` and `DI250:A94G` differ by a single base (`A94G`) discards almost all error-free amplicon reads and leaves only $42\text{--}50$ reads per sample that survived deduplication because they carried random Nanopore/PCR errors at other sites. Testing $X_i$ against $100 - X_i$ via an unpaired two-sample $t$-test (`AD28`) violates sample independence.
* **WHAT:** Re-run allele counting at position 94 (`A` vs. `G`) on all full-length ($\ge 370$ nt, $Q \ge 20\text{--}30$) aligned Nanopore amplicon reads **without removing sequence duplicates**, calibrate against the `1:10`, `1:1`, and `10:1` plasmid spike-in controls (`Fig. S6B`), and test for deviation from the `1:1` input ratio using a one-sample $t$-test of $\text{logit}(p_i)$ (or $p_i$) against $0.50$ ($\text{df} = 2$) or a beta-binomial / logistic mixed-effects model across replicates.
* **HOW:** Using the raw FASTQ files deposited under `PRJNA1366758` for `1_Control_1:10` through `6_Rep3`, skip the deduplication step in the `minimap2` + `LoFreq` pipeline (`Fig 7C & S6B`, cell `L5`), extract the exact `A` and `G` base counts at position 94 across all quality-filtered full-length amplicon reads, and replace `_xlfn.T.TEST(W28:Y28, Z28:AB28, 2, 2)` in `AD28` with a one-sample test of `W28:Y28` against $50\%$.

#### 3. Replace Time- and Compartment-Pooled $t$-Tests with Passage-Resolved / Paired Longitudinal Models and Within-Passage Packaging Ratios (`Fig. 6C`, `Fig. 7`, `Fig. S5C/E/F`)
* **WHY:** Pooling 14 serial passages (`P1–P14`) that oscillate across $3.5$ orders of magnitude into a single unpaired homoscedastic $t$-test (`Fig 6C and S5B&C` cell `AE63`, `T.TEST(..., 2, 2)`) or pooling 6 hpi intracellular lysates with 24 hpi extracellular supernatants (`Fig 7 & S5E` cell `AB40`) conflates longitudinal/compartment variance with treatment error, while `P(n+1) Ex / P(n) In` (`Fig. S5F`) compounds packaging with an intervening infection cycle.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate `NP` and `DelVG` trajectories on the $\log_{10}$ scale using a two-way repeated-measures ANOVA / linear mixed model (`Condition × Passage`) or passage-paired comparisons, and compute the within-passage packaging ratio $(\text{DelVG}/\text{NP})_{\text{P}_n\text{ External (24 hpi)}} \div (\text{DelVG}/\text{NP})_{\text{P}_n\text{ Internal (6 hpi)}}$ for `P3` and `P4`.
* **HOW:** In `Fig 7 & S5E`, compute `P3-External / P3-Internal` (`=AD26/AD27`: `2.15, 5.82` for `DI250` vs. `1.10, 0.95` for `DI250:A94G`) and `P4-External / P4-Internal` (`=AD28/AD29`: `22.52, 1.64` for `DI250` vs. `0.69, 0.96` for `DI250:A94G`), or clarify in the Methods and `Fig. 7` / `Fig. S5F` legends if the labels `P4-External` and `P5-External` actually denote the 24 hpi supernatants harvested at the end of `P3` and `P4` before transfer. In `Fig. S5C`, correct the legend's statement *"with error bars indicating the mean"* to state SD or range across the $n=2$ biological replicates.

#### 4. Temper Mechanistic Claims on Three-Way Clonal Competition (`Fig. 4B`) and censor Below-LOD HA Ratios (`Fig. 1A`)
* **WHY:** In `Fig. 4B` (`MOESM4_ESM.xlsx`, sheet `Fig4`), `DI250` began with a $3.9$- to $13.8$-fold lower copy number at `Seed`/`P2` than `DI143` and `DI289`, and the three-way competition was not repeated with `DI250:A94G`. In `Fig. 1A`, imputing `0.5 HAU` when hemagglutination is below the limit of detection creates artificial particle:infectivity spikes whenever $\text{TCID}_{50}$ drops below $10^3\text{ TCID}_{50}/\text{mL}$.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly acknowledge the lower measured starting copy number of `DI250` at `Seed`/`P2` in `Fig. 4B` (and either perform the `DI250:A94G` + `DI143` + `DI289` + `WT` three-way passage experiment or qualify the conclusion that `A94G` alone explains the `Fig. 4B` hierarchy reversal), and mark passages with undetectable HA ($\text{HAU} < 1$, imputed as `0.5`) as open/censored symbols in `Fig. 1A` and `Fig. S1A`.
* **HOW:** In `Fig 1A & Fig S1`, flag all rows where `O25:P96 = 0.5` as upper-bound ratios ($< 5.03 \times 10^7 / \text{TCID}_{50}$), and document the change in initial HA dilution factor between `P1–P8` and `P9–P72`.
