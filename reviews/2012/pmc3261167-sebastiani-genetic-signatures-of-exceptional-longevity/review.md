### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents a genome-wide association study (GWAS) and an ensemble Naive Bayes genetic risk classifier for exceptional longevity across a discovery cohort of 801 centenarians from the New England Centenarian Study (NECS, median age 104) and 914 genetically matched controls (241 NECS controls and 673 Illumina `iControlDB` controls), followed by evaluation in two replication sets: Replication Set 1 (ELIX: 253 nonagenarians/centenarians, median age 100, and 341 matched `iControlDB` controls) and Replication Set 2 (60 NECS centenarians, median age 107, and 2,863 unmatched `iControlDB` controls). Testing 243,980 SNPs across four genetic models (general, allelic, dominant, recessive), the authors report genome-wide significance at `rs2075650` in *TOMM40/APOE* ($\log_{10}\text{BF} = 6.31, P = 1.03\times 10^{-8}$), construct an ensemble of 281 nested Naive Bayes classifiers that achieves 89% sensitivity and specificity in the discovery set (and 60%/58% in Replication Set 1, rising in older age strata and reaching 78%/61% in Replication Set 2), and cluster individual risk trajectories into 26 "genetic signatures" of longevity.

While the replication of the protective *TOMM40/APOE* locus (`rs2075650`, $\text{OR} = 0.47\text{--}0.55$) across cohorts is biologically sound and well established, the manuscript's primary claims regarding the 281-SNP polygenic classifier and the 26 genetic signatures are invalidated by severe differential genotyping platform confounding and in-sample feature-selection overfitting. As revealed in Table 2, cases and controls were genotyped on systematically different Illumina microarray platforms across different laboratories: 278 of the 861 NECS centenarians (32.3%) and 44 of the 253 ELIX centenarians (17.4%) were genotyped on the redesigned `Human610-Quad` or `Human1M` arrays, whereas only 4 of the 914 discovery controls (0.4%) and **0 of the 3,483 replication controls (0.0%)** were genotyped on `610-Quad` or `1M` (with all 3,613 `iControlDB` controls genotyped externally on older `HumanHap300` and `550` arrays). Compounded by a permissive quality-control filter allowing up to a **20% minor allele frequency discrepancy between array types** and Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium $\chi^2 < 50$ ($P > 1.5\times 10^{-12}$), differential array artifacts between cases and controls directly drive SNP selection in the discovery set and persist into both replication sets (where 39 of the 60 Replication Set 2 centenarians were also genotyped on the `610-Quad` array). Resolving these fatal flaws requires restricting all single-SNP and risk-prediction analyses strictly to array-matched case–control strata, re-genotyping the remaining 253 model SNPs on an orthogonal platform across both cases and controls, and performing feature selection strictly inside outer cross-validation folds.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Claim 1: An ensemble of 281 SNPs discriminates centenarians from controls with 89% sensitivity and specificity in the discovery cohort and 60–85% sensitivity in independent replication cohorts. [Methodologically Flawed]**  
  This claim suffers from two compounding methodological failures:
  1. **Differential Genotyping Array Confounding Across Discovery and Replication Sets:** Table 2 shows a stark platform asymmetry between cases and controls. In the NECS discovery/replication pool, 102 centenarians were genotyped on the Illumina `610-Quad` array and 176 on the `1M` array (32.3% combined), compared to just 4 NECS controls on `610-Quad` and 0 controls on `1M`, while 100% of Illumina `iControlDB` controls (`Illumina I, II, III`) were genotyped on `300` and `550` arrays at external laboratories. The authors explicitly note (chunk 2, line 78) that the `610-Quad` array underwent *"substantial design changes"* compared to the `370`, `1M`, `550`, and `300` arrays, and that in 32 duplicate samples run on `370` and `610-Quad`, 915 SNPs had discordant genotypes and 28 SNPs had allele frequency shifts $>0.05$. Yet their SNP inclusion criteria (chunk 2, lines 72–74) permitted inter-array MAF differences up to $0.20$ and control HWE $\chi^2$ up to $50$. When screening 243,980 SNPs for the largest case–control genotype differences, platform-specific calling errors on `610-Quad` and `1M` (which exist almost exclusively in centenarians) are systematically enriched in the top 281 SNPs. Crucially, because 44/253 ELIX cases (Replication Set 1) and 39/60 Replication Set 2 cases were *also* genotyped on the `610-Quad` array against `300/550` controls (0% `610-Quad`), the exact same technical confounding carries over into the replication cohorts—explaining why sensitivity in Replication Set 2 jumps to 82% in the 39 `610-Quad` centenarians vs. 71.5% in the 21 non-`610-Quad` centenarians (line 67).
  2. **In-Sample Feature Selection Leakage:** The reported 89% sensitivity and specificity in the discovery set (Fig. 4B, Fig. 7A) is evaluated on the exact same 801 cases and 914 controls used to screen 243,980 SNPs across 4 genetic models and estimate the Naive Bayes genotype conditional probabilities. Even in the authors' 2/3-train, 1/3-test resampling check (chunk 2, line 143, Fig. S4A), the candidate SNPs were pre-selected and ordered using the full discovery set prior to splitting, leaking test-fold labels into feature selection. When applied to the matched Replication Set 1 (ELIX), overall discrimination drops to $\text{AUC} = 0.58$ (60% sensitivity, 58% specificity; line 65).

* **Claim 2: TaqMan validation of 28 SNPs confirms that microarray genotyping errors do not affect the 281-SNP genetic risk model. [Overstated]**  
  In *Materials and Methods* (chunk 2, lines 164–168), the authors re-genotyped only 30 of the 281 SNPs (~10.7%, of which 2 failed QC, leaving 28 SNPs) in 688 NECS centenarians and 221 NECS controls. First, 90% of the 281 SNPs in the classifier (253 SNPs) were never validated by TaqMan. Second, the TaqMan validation completely excluded the 673 `iControlDB` controls that make up 73.6% of the discovery control group and 100% of the replication control groups (`Illumina I, II, III` genotyped on `300/550` chips). Validating 10% of SNPs on internal NECS samples does not test for array discordance in the remaining 90% of SNPs or between BU arrays (`370/610/1M`) and external `iControlDB` arrays (`300/550`).

* **Claim 3: Clustering the cumulative 281-step Naive Bayes posterior trajectories reveals 26 distinct biological "genetic signatures" of exceptional longevity that correlate with survival age. [Methodologically Flawed]**  
  The "genetic risk profile" plotted for each individual (Figs. 6 and 8) is the sequence of cumulative Naive Bayes posterior probabilities $p_k = P(\text{EL} \mid \Sigma_k)$ for $k = 1, \dots, 281$, where SNPs are added one by one in decreasing order of discovery-set Bayes factor (`MBF`), and the ensemble score is $\frac{1}{281}\sum_{k=1}^{281} p_k$. Because $\Sigma_k$ is a cumulative product of up to 281 likelihood ratios, the trajectory $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_{281}$ is an order-dependent cumulative random walk in log-odds space where SNP 1 (`rs2075650`) receives $281\times$ the weight of SNP 281 in the ensemble average, and early SNPs dominate the polynomial shape of the curve clustered by `CAGED`. Consequently, clustering the shape of $p_k$ over $k=1\dots 281$ predominantly groups individuals by their genotypes at the first few SNPs (and by whether they were genotyped on the `610-Quad`/`1M` arrays) rather than identifying biologically distinct multi-locus pathways.

* **Claim 4: SNP `rs2075650` in *TOMM40/APOE* is significantly associated with exceptional longevity ($\text{OR} = 0.49, P = 1.03\times 10^{-8}$) and replicates across independent cohorts. [Well Supported]**  
  The depletion of the minor `G` allele of `rs2075650` (a known proxy for the $\varepsilon 4$ allele of *APOE*) in centenarians ($15\%$ vs. $24\%\text{--}27\%$ in controls; $\text{OR} = 0.47\text{--}0.55$) reaches genome-wide significance in the discovery set, replicates cleanly in ELIX ($P = 4.68\times 10^{-4}$) and against Coriell NINDS controls ($P = 3.86\times 10^{-6}$, where both cases and Coriell controls were genotyped on the `370` array; Tables 1 and 2), was verified by TaqMan, and is fully consistent with the established biology of *APOE* in Alzheimer's disease and cardiovascular mortality.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Genotyping Platform Confounding by Stratifying and Filtering by Array Type
* **WHY:** Because 32% of NECS cases (and 17% of ELIX cases, 65% of Replication 2 cases) were genotyped on `610-Quad` or `1M` arrays while ~100% of controls were genotyped on `370`, `300`, or `550` arrays, any SNP with array-specific genotype calling shifts on `610-Quad` or `1M` creates a spurious case–control association that replicates across all three cohorts.
* **WHAT:** Perform a strict within-case inter-array association test (`370` vs. `610-Quad` vs. `1M` among centenarians, and `370` NECS controls vs. `300/550` Illumina controls), purge all SNPs showing platform differences, and re-run the entire GWAS and Naive Bayes classifier restricting strictly to `370`-genotyped cases vs. `370`-genotyped controls (NECS controls + Coriell NINDS controls).
* **HOW:**
  1. For each of the 243,980 SNPs (and specifically for the 281 model SNPs in Table S1), compute a $3 \times 3$ genotype contingency $\chi^2$ test across the `370` ($n=583$), `610-Quad` ($n=102$), and `1M` ($n=176$) NECS centenarians, and a $3 \times 3$ test across the `370` NECS controls ($n=237$), `300` Illumina controls, and `550` Illumina controls. Exclude any SNP with $P < 0.01$ or inter-array MAF difference $>0.02$ (tightening the current permissive $<0.20$ filter by 10-fold, and tightening HWE from $\chi^2 < 50$ to $P > 10^{-4}$).
  2. Re-evaluate the 281-SNP classifier (and re-train from scratch) using *only* the 583 NECS `370` cases vs. 237 NECS `370` controls (plus the 867 Coriell `370` controls), and test it on the 209 ELIX `370` cases (excluding the 44 `610-Quad` ELIX cases and the 39 `610-Quad` Replication 2 cases).

#### 2. Implement Strict Outer Cross-Validation Without Feature-Selection Leakage
* **WHY:** Selecting and ranking the 281 SNPs on the full discovery set before running the 2/3-train, 1/3-test split (Fig. S4A) leaks the test labels into the feature selection step, inflating apparent discovery accuracy to 89% when true out-of-sample accuracy in ELIX is 58–60% ($\text{AUC} = 0.58$).
* **WHAT:** Perform genome-wide SNP ranking, Bayesian network LD pruning, and forward SNP selection strictly inside each 2/3 training split without touching the 1/3 held-out test split.
* **HOW:** In each of 100 outer cross-validation splits of the platform-matched discovery set, compute the single-SNP Bayes factors across all 243,980 SNPs using *only* the 2/3 training subjects, select the top $K$ SNPs, fit the Naive Bayes parameters on the 2/3 training set, and report the out-of-fold sensitivity, specificity, and AUC on the untouched 1/3 test set.

#### 3. Replace Order-Dependent Cumulative Ensemble Weighting with a Standard Log-Odds Classifier
* **WHY:** Averaging $P(\text{EL} \mid \Sigma_k)$ across nested sets $k = 1, \dots, 281$ arbitrarily weights the $i$-th ranked SNP by $(282 - i)/281$ and makes the `CAGED` polynomial clustering of trajectory shapes dependent on the exact ordering of SNPs.
* **WHAT:** Compare the single full 281-SNP Naive Bayes model $P(\text{EL} \mid \Sigma_{281})$ (which weights each SNP solely by its log-likelihood ratio) against a regularized logistic regression (LASSO/elastic net) model, and cluster individuals directly on their 281-dimensional genotype log-odds vector rather than on cumulative partial sums.
* **HOW:** Compute the individual log-likelihood ratio contributions $\ell_{ij} = \log \frac{P(G_{ij} \mid \text{EL})}{P(G_{ij} \mid \text{AL})}$ for each subject $i$ and SNP $j \in \{1, \dots, 281\}$, and perform clustering directly on the matrix $L = [\ell_{ij}]$ after removing platform-confounded SNPs.
