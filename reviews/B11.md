### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

#### Executive Summary
This foundational resource manuscript presents the GENCODE 7 reference human genome annotation produced for the ENCODE Project (and adopted by the 1000 Genomes and International Cancer Genome Consortia). GENCODE 7 merges manual curation on finished genomic BACs by the HAVANA team (18 chromosomes completed, with chr14–19 in progress) with automated whole-genome predictions from the Ensembl pipeline via the `HavanaAdder` merge engine and `AnnoTrack` quality-control tracking system. Across 20,687 protein-coding loci, 9,640 long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) loci (15,512 transcripts), 8,801 small ncRNA loci, and 11,224 manually annotated pseudogene loci (7,183 three-way Level 1 consensus pseudogenes across Yale `Pseudopipe`, UCSC `RetroFinder`, and HAVANA), GENCODE 7 catalogs 161,375 transcripts stratified into three confidence tiers: Level 1 (experimentally validated by targeted `RT-PCR-seq` or three-way pseudogene consensus; 9,019 transcripts), Level 2 (manual HAVANA/merged annotation; 118,657 transcripts), and Level 3 (automated Ensembl-only predictions; 33,699 transcripts). The authors validate the primary annotation deliverable across five orthogonal modalities: (1) ENCODE CAGE clusters and polyA site/signal features for 5'/3' completeness, (2) comparative cross-database overlap against RefSeq, UCSC Genes, CCDS, and AceView, (3) structural and functional domain conservation across 84,408 translated CDS isoforms using the `APPRIS` suite, (4) targeted `RT-PCR-seq` across 8 human tissues (confirming 82% of 5,871 tested exon-exon junctions and 73% of novel Illumina Human Body Map 2.0 `HBM` RNA-seq predictions), and (5) evolutionary coding potential (`PhyloCSF` across 33 placental mammals) and tandem mass spectrometry (`X!Tandem` against GPM and PeptideAtlas at 1% target-decoy FDR, detecting 83,054 tryptic peptides across 8,098 protein-coding genes). All GENCODE releases, GTF files, and evidence tracks are openly available at `gencodegenes.org`, Ensembl, and the UCSC Genome Browser.

Anchored on the technical validation of its primary resource deliverable, GENCODE 7 is an exceptionally thorough, multi-institutional reference annotation that transparently documents the incomplete 5'/3' ends of current transcript models (only 35% of coding TSSs and 15% of lncRNA TSSs overlap CAGE clusters; 16.8% of lncRNAs have polyA support), the prevalence of partial EST-supported alternative transcripts (flagged with `start_not_found` / `end_not_found`), and the high proportion of alternative translated isoforms (59.9%) that lose or damage Pfam/PDB structural domains relative to the constitutive `APPRIS` isoform. Minor issues include internal numerical inconsistencies in protein-coding gene counts across sections (20,687 in Abstract vs. 20,700 in Mass Spectrometry), a discrepancy between text and Figure 2 regarding the modal exon count of protein-coding transcripts (five exons in line 63 vs. four exons in line 107), and an uncritical inference that 3,127 two-exon unannotated HBM RNA-seq assemblies represent bona fide novel lncRNAs rather than fragmented short-read reconstruction artifacts.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

#### Detailed Rubric Evaluation

* **Claim 1 — GENCODE 7 provides 20,687 protein-coding loci (with 140,066 transcripts and 33,977 unique CDSs absent from RefSeq) and 11,224 manually curated pseudogenes (7,183 Level 1 three-way consensus loci, with 63 of 162 tested candidates confirmed transcribed by RT-PCR-seq): [Well Supported].**  
  Figures 3, 4, and 7 and the accompanying HAVANA/Ensembl merge rules substantiate the locus and transcript counts, explaining why AceView's ~20,000 excess coding loci are inflated by 31,057 single-exon models and spurious CDS assignments to pseudogenes (such as *PTENP1*). The pseudogene consensus pipeline (combining `Pseudopipe`, `RetroFinder`, and HAVANA with 50 bp overlap criteria and manual verification of unitary pseudogenes against mouse genome assembly errors) is rigorously validated.

* **Claim 2 — Targeted `RT-PCR-seq` across eight tissues confirms 82% of 5,871 tested low-confidence exon-exon junctions and provides 5-fold higher junction verification sensitivity (82% vs. 16%) than deep unbiased RNA-seq (~10.5 billion total reads across HBM and ENCODE): [Well Supported].**  
  Lines 145–148 and the Methods section (lines 45–53, chunk 2) specify exact wet-lab amplification parameters, normalized cDNA inputs, and strict mapping criteria (`Bowtie 0.12.5`, 0 mismatches, unique mapping, $\ge 10$ reads spanning the junction with $\ge 4$ nt or $\ge 8$ nt overhang for 35-nt and 75-nt reads). This provides direct experimental support for rare and tissue-restricted splice junctions.

* **Claim 3 — Orthogonal proteomic (`X!Tandem` at 1% target-decoy FDR) and structural (`APPRIS`) analyses confirm peptide translation for 8,098 (~40%) protein-coding genes (plus 194 genes with multiple splice isoforms and 8 NMD-tagged isoforms), while showing that 59.9% of annotated alternative coding isoforms lose or damage functional/structural domains: [Well Supported].**  
  Lines 125–129 and 167–169 (plus line 1 of chunk 2) provide transparent, biologically grounded quality control on the translated transcriptome. Rather than claiming that all 84,408 annotated CDS transcripts produce stable, distinct proteins, the authors demonstrate via `APPRIS` (`Pfamscan`, `Matador3D`, `firestar`) that 71.5% of "putative", 62.7% of "novel", and 79.5% of NMD-predicted transcripts lose functional or structural domain integrity.

* **Claim 4 — The two-exon modal peak of GENCODE 7 lncRNAs (9,640 loci) is a bona fide biological characteristic rather than an annotation artifact, and 3,127 two-exon Illumina HBM RNA-seq models outside GENCODE represent novel unannotated lncRNA loci: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In lines 63 and 161, the authors argue that because 3,127 of 3,689 unannotated Illumina HBM RNA-seq gene models reconstructed by Ensembl/Exonerate have two exons and lack `PhyloCSF` coding signal ($\ge 60$), this corroborates the two-exon distribution of manually curated GENCODE lncRNAs (Figure 2) and indicates ~3,127 novel lncRNAs. However, the authors themselves note that 85% of GENCODE lncRNAs lack 5' CAGE support, 83.2% lack 3' polyA support (line 63), and short-read de novo/ab initio transcript assembly (evaluated in RGASP, line 31 of chunk 2) frequently fragments low-abundance multi-exonic transcripts into two-exon splice-junction fragments. Thus, two-exon HBM short-read assemblies are subject to the exact same incomplete-coverage bias and cannot independently prove that the two-exon peak is unaffected by transcript truncation.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### Major Issues, Minor Issues & Concrete Recommendations

#### 1. Control for Transcript Completeness (CAGE + PolyA Support) When Evaluating the Two-Exon Distribution of lncRNAs and HBM Models (Major Issue)
* **WHY:** Line 63 acknowledges that ~85% of GENCODE lncRNAs lack 5' CAGE support and ~83% lack 3' polyA support ("indicating that the majority of transcripts are incomplete"), yet concludes that the 2-exon modal peak in Figure 2 *"does not appear to be an artifact or the product of incomplete annotation"* because Illumina HBM short-read models also peak at 2 exons. Because short-read RNA-seq assemblers (`Exonerate`/`Scripture`) preferentially reconstruct single-intron (2-exon) fragments when read coverage drops across low-expressed transcripts, this comparison is confounded by expression level and 5'/3' truncation.
* **WHAT:** Stratify the exon-count distribution in Figure 2 by transcript completeness (comparing the subset of full-length lncRNAs supported by *both* a 5' CAGE cluster and a 3' polyA feature against unsupported partial lncRNAs).
* **HOW:** Re-plot the lncRNA exon-number curve in Figure 2 for the doubly bounded (5' CAGE + 3' polyA) GENCODE 7 lncRNA subset and report its modal and median exon count in the "Analyzing long noncoding transcript annotation" section.

#### 2. Reconcile Numerical Discrepancies in Exon Counts, LncRNA Sub-Biotypes, and Protein-Coding Gene Totals Across Sections (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** Several summary statistics vary across different sections of the manuscript without explanation:
  1. **Modal exon count of protein-coding transcripts:** Line 63 states *"with their distribution peaking at two and five exons, respectively (see Fig. 2)"*, whereas line 107 states *"Transcripts annotated at protein-coding loci demonstrate a peak at four exons per transcript... (see Fig. 2)"*.
  2. **LncRNA locus sum:** Line 9 (Abstract) and line 59 state **9,640** lncRNA loci, whereas the four sub-biotypes summed in line 69 (`5,058 lincRNA + 3,214 antisense + 378 sense intronic + 930 processed transcripts`) sum to **9,580** loci (leaving 60 `sense_overlapping` or `<200 bp` loci uncounted in the summary sentence).
  3. **Protein-coding gene count:** Line 9 (Abstract) reports **20,687** protein-coding loci, whereas line 169 (Mass spectrometry) states *"8098 of the **20,700** annotated protein-coding genes"*.
  4. **NMD peptide-supported isoforms:** Line 1 of chunk 2 states *"we validated the expression of **eight** isoforms that were tagged as candidates for NMD degradation"*, whereas line 27 of chunk 2 (Discussion) states *"mass spectrometry analysis within the project has identified **four** transcripts, annotated to be subject to NMD, that produce peptides"*.
  5. **Pseudogene parent counts:** Line 77 states *"11,224 pseudogenes based on GENCODE 7, level 2. ... identified parents for 9369 pseudogenes ... whereas the parents for the remaining 1847 pseudogenes are still ambiguous"*, where $9,369 + 1,847 = 11,216$ (8 pseudogenes unaccounted for).
* **WHAT & HOW:** Harmonize these five sets of numbers across the Abstract, Results, and Discussion, or add brief parenthetical notes explaining the exact filtering difference (e.g., 60 `sense_overlapping` lncRNA loci in line 69; 4 genes vs. 8 isoforms for NMD peptide hits).
