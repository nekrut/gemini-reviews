## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript introduces DeepRNA-DTI, a multi-task deep learning framework that takes primary RNA nucleotide sequences (encoded via pretrained RNA-FM) and small-molecule molecular graphs (encoded via pretrained Mole-BERT) and applies multi-head cross-attention to simultaneously predict binary drug–target interactions (DTI) and compound-specific nucleotide-level binding sites. Because nucleotide-level structural binding annotations (from 938 PDB complexes) are much scarcer than binary DTI pairs ($21,546$ interactions across $1,334$ RNAs and $3,574$ compounds from R-SIM, R-BIND, SMMRNA, and ROBIN), the authors evaluate four multi-task training regimens and show that pooled learning with binding-site oversampling prevents catastrophic forgetting and improves both DTI (`AUC = 0.892`) and binding-site (`AUC = 0.898`) performance. The model is further benchmarked on the RSApred leave-one-out dataset (`AUC = 0.949` vs. `0.869`), evaluated on two out-of-distribution PDB complexes (`8XZM` with ligand Tanimoto $< 0.3$ and `4Y1M` with RNA Needleman–Wunsch similarity $< 0.7$), and applied to screen $48,232,772$ compounds from the Enamine REAL library against oncogenic pre-miR-21.

A forensic audit confirms that all tables and statistical comparisons are internally consistent and transparently reported, including candid caveats regarding comparison against structure-based compound-agnostic predictors (RNAsite and RNABind). However, four methodological and mathematical points should be addressed:

1. **Incomplete Binary Focal Loss Formula in the Methods Text (First half, line 131):** The manuscript defines the binding-site focal loss as $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Focal}} = -\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L} (1-P_i)^\gamma y_i \log(P_i)$. Because $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$, this expression omits the negative-class penalty $-(1-y_i)P_i^\gamma \log(1-P_i)$. Minimizing $-\sum (1-P_i)^\gamma y_i \log(P_i)$ alone would trivially drive $P_i \to 1$ for every nucleotide in the RNA sequence ($0$ loss for all-positive predictions). The authors should write the complete binary focal loss equation used in their code.
2. **Permissive $10\text{ \AA}$ Distance Cutoff for Binding Site Definition (First half, line 63; Second half, Table 2):** Nucleotides are labeled as positive binding sites if any atom lies within $10\text{ \AA}$ of a ligand atom. In compact folded RNA motifs (where stems and hairpins have diameters of $20\text{–}25\text{ \AA}$), a $10\text{ \AA}$ sphere around a small molecule encompasses both direct first-shell contact nucleotides ($\le 4.0\text{–}5.0\text{ \AA}$) and second-shell bystanders, substantially increasing the positive base rate and simplifying the classification task relative to standard $4\text{ \AA}$ contact cutoffs.
3. **Transductive Overlap in Main 5-Fold Cross-Validation and Pre-miR-21 Split (First half, line 127; Second half, line 29):** The primary 5-fold cross-validation (`80%/10%/10%`) splits RNA–compound pairs randomly rather than clustering by RNA sequence identity or ligand Bemis–Murcko scaffold. As noted in the pre-miR-21 section (second half, line 29), five pre-miR-21 sequences (177 compounds) were present in the training set while one pre-miR-21 sequence (36 compounds) was in the test set. Furthermore, negative pairs for the PDB subset were generated by $1:1$ random pairing (first half, line 65), which can introduce false negatives or trivial degree biases if certain RNAs or ligands have unequal positive/negative ratios.
4. **Absence of Experimental Validation for Pre-miR-21 Virtual Screening Hits:** While rediscovery of a known pre-miR-21 scaffold (`Scaffold 1`) and high DTI scores ($>0.8$) on four external binders from Shortridge et al. provide encouraging retrospective validation, the nine novel chemotypes identified from Enamine REAL remain unverified experimentally.

**Editorial Verdict:** **Minor Revision.** This is a well-executed, reproducible computational study whose text and methodological disclosures are largely transparent; correcting the focal loss equation and adding a sensitivity analysis across distance cutoffs ($4\text{ \AA}$, $6\text{ \AA}$, $10\text{ \AA}$) or cold-RNA/cold-scaffold splits would further strengthen the paper.

### Specific Conclusions and Assessment of Support
* **Claim 1:** Pooled multi-task learning with binding-site oversampling outperforms independent learning, sequential fine-tuning, and alternating training, achieving `AUC = 0.892` (DTI) and `AUC = 0.898` (Binding Site) in 5-fold CV. `[Well Supported]` — Supported by controlled comparisons across all four training paradigms in Figure 2 and pretrained embedding ablations in Figure 3.
* **Claim 2:** DeepRNA-DTI outperforms DeepH-DTA, GraphDTA, and RSApred on the RSApred benchmark (`AUC = 0.949` vs. `0.869`) and maintains stable performance across six RNA subtypes without subtype-specific models. `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by Table 1 and Figure 4 across aptamers, miRNAs, repeatable elements, riboswitches, rRNAs, and viral RNAs.
* **Claim 3:** DeepRNA-DTI accurately localizes compound-specific binding regions on unseen ligands (`8XZM`, `AUC = 0.985`) and low-homology RNAs (`4Y1M`, `AUC = 0.838`), and outperforms RNAsite (`0.743`) and RNABind (`0.675`). `[Moderately Supported]` — The authors appropriately caveat in Table 2 and the Discussion that RNAsite and RNABind use a $4\text{ \AA}$ compound-agnostic cutoff whereas DeepRNA-DTI uses a $10\text{ \AA}$ compound-specific cutoff, making the numerical AUC comparison indicative rather than apples-to-apples.
* **Claim 4:** Screening 48.2 million Enamine REAL compounds against pre-miR-21 yields predicted binders enriched for RNA-like physicochemical properties and identifies nine novel pre-miR-21-targeting scaffolds. `[Moderately Supported]` — Computationally supported by subsampled 1-nearest-neighbor analysis ($61.5\%$ RNA-binder nearest neighbors vs. $38.5\%$ protein-binder across 10 balanced subsamples), SHAP descriptor analysis, and retrospective recovery of Shortridge et al. active compounds, though prospective wet-lab binding confirmation is not performed.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Major Methodological and Statistical Defects

* **Defect 1: Omission of the Negative-Class Term in the Binary Focal Loss Equation.**
  * *Location:* Section "Training strategy" (first half, line 131).
  * *Forensic Evidence:* Line 131 writes:
    $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Focal}} = -\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L} (1-P_i)^\gamma y_i \log(P_i).$$
    In binary per-nucleotide classification where $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ and $P_i \in (0, 1)$ is the predicted sigmoid probability for nucleotide $i$, the above sum evaluates to $0$ whenever $y_i = 0$, providing zero gradient to push non-binding nucleotides toward $P_i = 0$. The standard binary focal loss requires both positive and negative terms:
    $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Focal}} = -\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L} \left[ \alpha y_i (1-P_i)^\gamma \log(P_i) + (1-\alpha)(1-y_i) P_i^\gamma \log(1-P_i) \right].$$
    The equation in the manuscript should be corrected to match the actual implementation, and the values of the focusing hyperparameter $\gamma$ (and $\alpha$, if used) should be stated in the main text.

* **Defect 2: Broad $10\text{ \AA}$ Distance Cutoff for Defining Nucleotide Binding Sites.**
  * *Location:* Section "Dataset curation and preprocessing" (first half, line 63) and Table 2 (second half, lines 4–9).
  * *Forensic Evidence:* A $10\text{ \AA}$ heavy-atom distance threshold is more than twice the standard van der Waals / hydrogen-bonding contact distance ($4.0\text{ \AA}$) used in RNAsite and RNABind. Because small-molecule pockets in RNA typically span $10\text{–}15\text{ \AA}$, a $10\text{ \AA}$ radius around every ligand atom labels a broad regional neighborhood of the RNA sequence as "binding," which inflates ROC-AUC relative to strict contact-residue prediction. Reporting binding-site AUC and AUPRC at $4.0\text{ \AA}$, $6.0\text{ \AA}$, and $10.0\text{ \AA}$ cutoffs would clarify the spatial resolution of the cross-attention weights.

* **Defect 3: Random Pair Splitting in 5-Fold Cross-Validation Without Sequence/Scaffold Clustering.**
  * *Location:* Section "Training strategy" (first half, line 127) and Section "Massive AI screening reveals novel pre-miR-21 targeting scaffolds" (second half, line 29).
  * *Forensic Evidence:* Splitting the $21,546$ DTI pairs randomly (`80%/10%/10%`) places identical or near-identical RNA targets (such as the five training pre-miR-21 sequences and one test pre-miR-21 sequence) and shared chemical series across train and test folds. While the authors include two cold-start PDB examples (`8XZM` and `4Y1M`) and external pre-miR-21 binders, reporting systematic cold-RNA and cold-compound 5-fold CV benchmarks alongside random-split CV would better quantify out-of-distribution generalization.
