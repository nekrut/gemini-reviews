## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict
This manuscript investigates the evolutionary dynamics of 20 plastid-encoded photosystem I (PSI) and photosystem II (PSII) protein subunits across 773 angiosperm species spanning 356 genera and 51 orders. By mapping substitutions onto a plastid phylogenomic tree and combining codon-based selection tests (`FUBAR`/`FEL`) with structure-based $\Delta\Delta G$ predictions (`PRODIGY-LIG` and `BAlaS`), the authors demonstrate that strong purifying selection maintains the core reaction-center proteins (`PsaA`, `PsaB`, `PsbA`, `PsbD`) and their prosthetic group binding pockets, while episodic and recurrent adaptive substitutions cluster primarily at protein–protein interfaces and in the rapidly turned-over PSII reaction center D1 subunit (`PsbA`). The study is clearly written, quantitatively transparent, and fully reproducible via open code and alignments. However, two methodological and biological caveats require attention: first, translating plastid genomic DNA directly via `Pal2Nal` ignores widespread plastid C-to-U mRNA editing in angiosperms—directly affecting the flagship stabilizing mutation reported in Table 1 (`PsbF S26F`, $\Delta\Delta G = -2.7\text{ kcal/mol}$), which is the classic chloroplast C-to-U RNA-editing site where genomic `UCU` (Ser26) is edited to `UUU` (Phe26) at the transcript level across most angiosperms; second, simulating null expectations under a site-homogeneous `JTT+F+R7` model overestimates the neutral amino acid alphabet available at constrained transmembrane positions and thus inflates the statistical significance of homoplasy.

### Claim-by-Claim Evaluation
* **Claim 1:** *Plastid-encoded PSI and PSII subunits are highly conserved across 773 angiosperms, with `dN/dS` ratios averaging 10-fold higher at variable sites (mean `0.21`) than across full sequences (`0.022`), and two-thirds of all amino acid residues remaining completely invariant.* **[Well Supported]** — Backed by a curated 773-taxon dataset (`6,732` total substitutions mapped onto a `GTR+F+R7` plastome phylogeny) and site-level codon models (`FUBAR` and `FEL`).
* **Claim 2:** *At least 30% (`2,068`) of all amino acid substitutions across photosystem subunits are adaptive, because they occur recurrently along the phylogeny significantly more often than expected by chance under a `JTT+F+R7` Monte Carlo simulation ($P < 0.001$).* **[Overstated]** — While recurrent forward substitutions ($X \to Y$ occurring up to 62 times independently) are enriched for true adaptive convergence and corroborated at 92 codons by `FUBAR`/`FEL` ($d_N/d_S > 1$), simulating the null distribution under a site-homogeneous `JTT+F+R7` model assumes that every variable site can sample all 20 amino acids according to global equilibrium frequencies (`+F`). In membrane proteins, site-specific biophysical constraints restrict neutral/nearly neutral drift at hydrophobic helices to 2–3 biochemically similar residues (`I/L/V` or `A/G/S`), producing high rates of neutral homoplasy that violate a site-homogeneous null (Zou & Zhang 2015).
* **Claim 3:** *Adaptive substitutions (`573` substitutions at `254` residue positions) are entirely absent from prosthetic-group-binding pockets ($\Delta\Delta G_{\text{LIG}} \approx 0$) and are concentrated at inter-subunit interfaces (`25%`) and `PsbA` (D1), including `PsbF S26F` ($\Delta\Delta G_{\text{PPI}} = -2.7\text{ kcal/mol}$, Table 1).* **[Fragile]** — The structural finding that cofactor-binding pockets (`Chl a`, pheophytin, $\beta$-carotene, plastoquinone, $\text{Fe}_4\text{S}_4$) are invariant across angiosperms is compelling and well supported. However, the single largest interface-stabilizing substitution reported in Table 1 (`PsbF S26F`, interface `F:E`, $\Delta\Delta G = -2.7\text{ kcal/mol}$) is a known biological artifact of translating unedited plastid DNA: codon 26 of `psbF` (`UCU` $\to$ `UUU`, Ser26 $\to$ Phe26) is a canonical plastid C-to-U mRNA editing site in angiosperms (spinach, *Arabidopsis*, pea, etc., where the mature translated protein in vivo is already Phe26; see PDB `5L8R` where spinach `PsbF` residue 26 is Phe despite the genomic `TCT` codon!). Lineages carrying genomic `TTT` (`F26`) have merely replaced the C-to-U RNA-editing requirement via DNA-level `C -> T` substitution without altering the translated PsbF protein subunit in vivo.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1. Methodological & Biological Defects
1. **Unaccounted Plastid C-to-U mRNA Editing Confounds Genomic Amino Acid Translations (Table 1 line 67, Methods line 115):**
   * In angiosperm chloroplasts, several photosystem transcripts (`psbF`, `psbE`, `psbL`, `psbB`, `psbZ`, `psaI`) undergo site-specific C-to-U mRNA editing before translation. Because the authors translated genomic CDS directly (`Pal2Nal`, line 115), genomic `C <-> T` transitions at editing sites are misclassified as nonsynonymous amino acid substitutions (`S <-> F`, `P <-> L`, `T <-> I`, `H <-> Y`) when the translated polypeptide in vivo remains invariant (`F`, `L`, `I`, `Y`).
   * Specifically, Table 1 highlights `PsbF S26F` (occurring 18 times independently) as the strongest stabilizing interface mutation in the dataset ($\Delta\Delta G = -2.7\text{ kcal/mol}$ at the `PsbF:PsbE` cytochrome $b_{559}$ interface). In spinach (`Spinacia oleracea`, the exact species of the reference cryo-EM structure `5L8R` used by the authors!), genomic `psbF` codon 26 is `TCT` (Ser), which is post-transcriptionally edited to `TTT` (Phe26) in chloroplast mRNA, so the cryo-EM structure `5L8R` contains Phe26 in vivo. Modeling the genomic `S26F` substitution on `5L8R` measures the artificial destabilization of placing an unedited Ser26 into the native Phe26 pocket rather than an evolutionary change in the mature protein.
2. **Site-Homogeneous Null Model (`JTT+F+R7`) Inflates Homoplasy Significance (lines 38–42, 128–133):**
   * The Monte Carlo simulation generates 1,000 alignments along the 773-species tree using `JTT+F+R7` (where all sites share the same 20-state stationary amino acid frequency vector `+F`). Because real transmembrane helices tolerate only a small subset of hydrophobic side chains (`I, L, V, M, F, A`) at any given position under purifying selection, neutral substitutions are forced into the same 2–3 states repeatedly across 773 species. Comparing empirical recurrence against a site-homogeneous `JTT+F` null rather than a site-heterogeneous profile model (`CAT`, `C20`/`C60`, or site-specific amino acid profiles) overestimates the fraction of recurrent substitutions driven by positive selection (`2,068` substitutions).

### 3.2. Internal Numerical & Textual Inconsistencies
1. **Reversed $p$-Value Inequality on Line 47 vs. Figure 4B Caption (line 50):**
   * Line 47 states: *"Similarly, there was no significant difference in the level of adaptive evolution between PSI and PSII subunits (**t-test, P < 0.05**; Fig. 4B)."* Reporting `P < 0.05` for a non-significant difference reverses the inequality sign; the Figure 4B caption (line 50) correctly states `Two-sided two-sample t-test, P > 0.05 (PSI n = 5 and PSII n = 15)`.
2. **Unexplained Drop in Total Substitution Denominator (`6,732` vs. `6,522`, line 29 vs. line 39):**
   * Line 29 reports that across all 20 proteins, *"a total of **6,732** amino acid substitutions were identified, all occurring within the 1,020 variable amino acid residues."* Ten lines later, line 39 states: *"While 46% of all amino acid substitutions occurred on a single branch of the phylogenetic tree, the remaining **54% (3,620/6,522)** were recurrent."* No explanation is given for why the denominator of total amino acid substitutions drops by 210 substitutions from `6,732` to `6,522`.
3. **Duplicate `PsbD` and Missing `PsbB` in Figure 4C Caption (line 50 vs. line 55):**
   * The Figure 4C caption (line 50) lists the 9 core proteins as: *"Core proteins included PsaA, PsaB, PsaC, PsbA, **PsbD**, PsbC, **PsbD**, PsbE, and PsbF."* Here `PsbD` is listed twice and `PsbB` (CP47, correctly listed on line 55) is omitted.

## 4. Quantitative Rubric Scoring
* **Methodological & Statistical Rigor (2.2 / 3.5):** Strong integration of 773-taxon phylogenomics, ancestral reconstruction, `FUBAR`/`FEL` codon models, and cryo-EM $\Delta\Delta G$ calculations, reduced by the failure to filter canonical plastid C-to-U mRNA editing sites (`PsbF S26F`) and the reliance on a site-homogeneous `JTT+F` null for homoplasy detection.
* **Claim Calibration & Evidence Alignment (1.7 / 2.5):** The core conclusions regarding extreme conservation of cofactor-binding pockets and accelerated interface evolution in `PsbA` (D1) are well calibrated, though the claim that $\ge 30\%$ of all plastid photosystem substitutions are adaptive is inflated by neutral homoplasy and RNA-editing losses.
* **Biological & Evolutionary Insight (1.4 / 2.0):** Connects photodamage-driven D1 turnover (`PsbA`) to selective pressure on PSII reaction-center interfaces across 773 angiosperms, though overlooking plastid RNA editing misses an important aspect of plastid gene expression biology.
* **Reproducibility & Data Hygiene (1.9 / 2.0):** Exemplary data and code hygiene: full alignments, trees, tables, and custom Python scripts are openly archived with persistent identifiers on Zenodo (`10.5281/zenodo.13750849`) and GitHub.
