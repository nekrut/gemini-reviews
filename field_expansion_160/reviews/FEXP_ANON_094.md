## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict
This manuscript presents `gSELECT`, an open-source Python tool (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`) that evaluates the discriminative signal of data-driven or user-defined gene sets in binary single-cell RNA-seq comparisons using mutual information (`scikit-learn` `mutual_info_classif`) and repeated Monte Carlo train/test splits of a small neural network classifier (`TensorFlow/Keras` MLP with hidden layers `16 -> 8 -> 1`). The authors illustrate `gSELECT` across three scenarios: (1) a negative-control dataset where human `H358` lung cancer cells (`GSE137912`, `n = 1,057` cells) are duplicated and perturbed with 1% Gaussian noise; (2) male vs. female cortical/hippocampal neurons in adult mouse SMART-Seq v4 data (`GSE185862`, `n = 73,363` cells); and (3) alpha vs. beta pancreatic islet cells in the integrated `panc8` dataset (`GSE81076`, `GSE85241`, `GSE86469`, `E-MTAB-5061`).

The software addresses a practical exploratory use case—allowing biologists to rapidly test whether a small literature-derived or hypothesis-driven gene panel separates two annotated cell groups—and the code and benchmark datasets are openly available. However, forensic evaluation of the methodology and quantitative results exposes several significant statistical and algorithmic flaws:
1. **Default Full-Dataset Mutual Information Data Leakage:** By default (`split_Before_MI = False`), `gSELECT` computes mutual information rankings across **100% of the dataset (including future test cells)** before performing the 80/20 Monte Carlo train/test splits for the MLP classifier (Methods lines 167–175; Discussion line 126). Evaluating high-dimensional feature selection (`p > 20,000` genes) when features are pre-selected on the combined train+test set violates fundamental cross-validation hygiene and inflates test accuracy in weak-signal regimes.
2. **Severe Numerical Contradiction and Anti-Learning Artifact in Scenario 1 (Negative Control):** In Scenario 1 (two identical `H358` groups of `1,057` cells each with 1% positive Gaussian noise added to Group 1), the authors claim in the Discussion (line 137) and Table 6 that balanced accuracy is *"around 50%, which equals the accuracy of mere guessing."* However, in the Results (lines 382–385 and Figure 2C), they report that as top MI genes are added, **training balanced accuracy rises to `~58%` while test balanced accuracy plunges to `~26%` (`0.26`)**! For a balanced binary classification problem, `26%` test accuracy is `24` percentage points *below* random guessing (`50%`)—a classic anti-learning / label-inversion artifact caused by computing MI on the full dataset or fitting to strictly positive noise perturbations (`N(0,1)` truncated or shifted to add `1%` to Group 1) across correlated splits. Even worse, in lines 374–378 (Figure 2B), the authors state that across `2,114` total cells (an 80/20 split has `423` test cells), an average of `313.5` samples were misclassified when achieving `~50%` balanced accuracy—a mathematical impossibility (`313.5 / 423 = 74.1%` error rate, or `313.5 / 2114 = 14.8%` error rate; neither equals `50%`!).
3. **Cell-Level Pseudoreplication Across Donor Mice in Scenario 2 (`GSE185862`):** When classifying male vs. female cells (`73,363` cells) after removing sex-chromosome markers (`Xist`, `Tsix`, `Eif2s3y`, `Ddx3y`, `Uty`, `Kdm5d`), the authors split individual cells randomly into 80% train and 20% test sets rather than splitting by biological donor (mouse). Consequently, the residual `~72%` test accuracy achieved with 25–50 autosomal genes (`Figure 4B`) reflects donor/batch fingerprinting (cells from the same mouse appearing in both train and test sets) rather than true autosomal sex dimorphism.
4. **Volcano Plot Typo and Overstated Novelty Claims:** In Figure 5E (caption line 15), both downregulated (blue) and upregulated (red) genes are defined as `(log2 fold change < -1)`. Furthermore, the authors repeatedly claim that *"to the best of our knowledge, at the time of publication, gSELECT is the only available tool that allows direct input of custom gene sets to evaluate their classification performance"* (lines 28, 227), overlooking numerous established R/Python single-cell classification and gene-set scoring frameworks (`scPred`, `SingleCellNet`, `AUCell`, `Garnett`, `scikit-learn` pipelines).

### Specific Conclusions and Assessment of Support
* **Claim 1:** In Scenario 1 (1% Gaussian noise negative control), `gSELECT` yields random-chance performance (`~50%` balanced accuracy) and correctly diagnoses the absence of biological signal. — `[Methodologically Flawed]` (Figure 2C and lines 382–385 actually show test balanced accuracy dropping to `~26%` while training accuracy reaches `~58%`, and line 376 reports `313.5` misclassified samples, which contradicts `50%` error on both `n = 423` test cells and `n = 2,114` total cells.)
* **Claim 2:** In Scenario 2 (`GSE185862`), excluding canonical sex-linked genes (`Tsix`, `Xist`, `Eif2s3y`, `Ddx3y`, `Uty`, `Kdm5d`) leaves a residual autosomal sex-specific signal yielding `~72%` test accuracy at 25–50 genes. — `[Fragile]` (Random cell-level train/test splitting mixes cells from the same donor mice and technical batches across train and test splits; the `72%` accuracy likely captures individual mouse/batch effects rather than sex biology.)
* **Claim 3:** In Scenario 3 (`panc8`), `INS`, `GCG`, and `GC` are the top MI-ranked markers separating alpha and beta islet cells (`>90%` accuracy with `INS` alone, `~99%` with top 10 genes), and exhaustive search of `{INS, SOX9, KRT19, B2M, ALDOA}` shows that `INS + B2M + ALDOA` (`0.94`) outperforms ductal markers `SOX9 + KRT19` (`0.51`). — `[Well Supported]` (Canonical endocrine vs. ductal biology is accurately recovered and clearly demonstrated in Table 5 and Figures 5–7.)
* **Claim 4:** `gSELECT` is the first and only tool capable of evaluating the classification performance of custom gene sets or ranking gene combinations. — `[Overstated]` (Wrapper feature selection [exhaustive/greedy forward selection] and custom gene-panel classification are standard capabilities in `scikit-learn` [`SequentialFeatureSelector`], `mlxtend`, and single-cell packages.)

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Numerical Inconsistency and Anti-Learning (`26%` Test Accuracy) in Scenario 1
* **Location:** Section `Scenario 1: Very similar groups` (lines 363–392, Figure 2B–C) vs. Discussion (line 137) and Table 6 (line 179).
* **Audit Finding:**
  1. **Misclassification Count Arithmetic Error:** Dataset 1 consists of `1,057` untreated `H358` cells duplicated into two groups (`2,114` cells total). With `gSELECT`'s default 80/20 train/test split (`test_size = 0.2`), the test set contains `423` cells (`0.2 * 2114 = 422.8`). In lines 374–378 (and Figure 2B), when evaluating all non-constant genes, the authors state: *"The balanced accuracy remains close to 50 % across all sweeps... Consistently, the average number of misclassified samples across runs is 313.5, with a standard deviation of 65.5."* If `313.5` out of `423` test cells are misclassified, the accuracy is `25.9%`, not `50%`! Conversely, if `313.5` out of all `2,114` cells are misclassified, the accuracy is `85.2%`, not `50%`!
  2. **Unexplained `26%` Test Accuracy (`Figure 2C`):** In lines 382–385, when evaluating the top MI-ranked genes on the 1% noise data, the authors report: *"While the training accuracy rises slightly (stabilising at ∼58 %), the test accuracy remains low across all gene counts, plateauing at around 26 %."* In binary classification, a balanced accuracy of `26%` is not "chance level"—it means the classifier is systematically predicting the **wrong** label `74%` of the time! Moreover, the authors state they added 1% Gaussian noise to Group 1 via $x_{ij}^{\text{new}} = x_{ij} + 0.01 \cdot x_{ij} \cdot |z_{ij}|$ (or positive noise so expression remained non-negative, lines 366–370), which creates a systematic positive mean shift in Group 1 across every expressed gene. The authors never reconcile why Figure 2C shows `26%` test accuracy while Table 6 claims `Balanced accuracy ≈ 50%`.

### Defect 2: Default Feature Selection Data Leakage (`split_Before_MI = False`)
* **Location:** Methods (`Workflow and Structure of gSELECT`, lines 167–175) and Discussion (`Intended use of gSELECT`, line 126).
* **Audit Finding:** The authors explicitly state in Discussion line 126: *"The default pipeline for gSELECT calculates mutual information on the complete set of expression data; however, it also includes an option to split the dataset before computing mutual information..."* Computing mutual information (`mutual_info_classif`) on the entire dataset before splitting into training and test sets leaks test-set label associations into the gene ranking step. In high-dimensional single-cell datasets (`>20,000` genes), selecting the top $k$ genes using all cells guarantees that even under a global null hypothesis, the selected genes will exhibit spurious correlation with the labels across the full dataset. Feature ranking must strictly occur inside each training split by default.

### Defect 3: Cell-Level Pseudoreplication in Multi-Donor scRNA-Seq (`Scenario 2`)
* **Location:** Section `Scenario 2: Subtle differences between groups` (lines 410–490, Figure 3–4) and Discussion (lines 141–144).
* **Audit Finding:** In Scenario 2, the authors classify male vs. female cells across `73,363` cortical and hippocampal neurons from `GSE185862` (Yao et al., 2021). When the 6 sex-chromosome genes (`Tsix`, `Xist`, `Eif2s3y`, `Ddx3y`, `Uty`, `Kdm5d`) are excluded, test accuracy recovers to `~72%` using 25–50 autosomal genes (`Figure 4B`). Because `gSELECT` performs random cell-level train/test splits without grouping by biological replicate (`mouse_id` / donor animal), thousands of cells from the exact same male or female mouse are placed into both the training set and the test set. The MLP classifier can trivially achieve `72%` accuracy by learning mouse-specific or batch-specific autosomal expression signatures rather than true sex-specific biology.

### Defect 4: Dense Matrix Conversion Bottleneck and Greedy Search Unregularized Metric
* **Location:** Section `Computational constraints and scalability` (lines 204–220).
* **Audit Finding:**
  1. `gSELECT` converts sparse `AnnData` count matrices (`.h5ad`) into dense NumPy arrays in memory (line 204), causing severe RAM bottlenecks on modern atlas-scale scRNA-seq datasets (`>100,000` cells) especially when `ThreadPoolExecutor` spawns multiple concurrent classification sweeps.
  2. In exhaustive and greedy subset search (lines 50, 210–218), candidate gene subsets are ranked by their test-set balanced accuracy across Monte Carlo sweeps without a separate validation split (a three-way train/validation/test split), meaning the maximum accuracy over $2^n - 1$ subsets (`1,023` models for $n=10$) is subject to multiple-testing selection bias (winner's curse).

### 5. Constructive and Doable Suggestions for Improvement
1. **Make `split_Before_MI = True` the Mandatory Default:** Compute `mutual_info_classif` strictly within the 80% training split of each Monte Carlo sweep so that test cells never leak into feature ranking.
2. **Audit and Fix Scenario 1 (`Fig. 2B–C`):** Investigate why test balanced accuracy drops to `26%` in Figure 2C (e.g., check whether class weights or probability threshold inversion occurred when training accuracy was near `50%`) and clarify the denominator (`423` test cells vs. `2,114` total cells) for the `313.5` misclassified samples reported in Figure 2B. Additionally, add zero-mean symmetric noise (or randomly permute group labels on the `1,057` cells) so the negative control has a true theoretical Bayes accuracy of `50%`.
3. **Add Donor/Batch-Aware Group Splitting (`GroupShuffleSplit`):** In single-cell datasets with multiple biological donors (such as `GSE185862`), support `scikit-learn`'s `GroupShuffleSplit` (grouped by `donor_id` or `sample_id`) so that all cells from a given animal or patient are assigned exclusively to either train or test.
4. **Use a Three-Way Split (`Train / Validation / Test`) for Combinatorial Subset Search:** Rank the $2^n - 1$ candidate gene subsets using validation-split accuracy and report unbiased generalization accuracy on a locked holdout test split.
