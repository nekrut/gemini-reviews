## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict
This study characterizes genetic polymorphism in the complete `CMC` (`Gp60–70`) gene across 104 clinical-clade *Sporothrix* isolates (*S. brasiliensis*, *S. schenckii*, *S. globosa*, and *S. luriei*), compares its discriminatory power against `ITS1/2+5.8S` and calmodulin (`CAL`), maps `CMC` to a 3.4 Mb chromosome via PFGE and Southern blotting (demonstrating its absence in *S. mexicana* and 12 other environmental *Sporothrix* genomes), and evaluates population-level conservation of linear B-cell epitopes, previously tested vaccine peptides (`KPVQHALLTPLGLDR` and `ZR8`), and N-linked glycosylation sequons. The wet-lab dataset (94 newly sequenced `CMC` alleles deposited in GenBank, PFGE Southern blots, and empirical conservation rates of candidate diagnostic/vaccine peptides) is a genuinely useful resource for sporotrichosis surveillance and serology. However, the evolutionary and bioinformatic sections suffer from three notable defects: (1) the authors repeatedly claim that negative `Fu's Fs` values (`-12.606` and `-10.264`) indicate positive selection driven by urban polyaromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) pollution, directly contradicting their own finding in Results (line 124) that `CMC` is under strong purifying selection ($\pi_S = 0.10995$ vs. $\pi_N = 0.02316$, $\pi_N/\pi_S = 0.211$); (2) neutrality statistics (`Fu's Fs = -9.415`, `Tajima's D = -0.41730`) are computed on the pooled 104-isolate alignment spanning four distinct, strongly differentiated species ($F_{ST} = 0.66\text{–}0.84$), violating the single-population assumption of coalescent tests; and (3) spectral evolutionary coupling analysis (`LERI`/`SAEC`) is applied separately to the clinical *Sporothrix* alignment ($\sim 1\text{–}2\%$ protein divergence), which lacks the effective sequence depth ($M_{\text{eff}}$) required to distinguish biochemical coevolution from shared phylogenetic ancestry.

### Claim-by-Claim Evaluation
* **Claim 1:** *The `CMC` gene resolves 79 unique haplotypes across 104 clinical Sporothrix isolates ($Hd = 0.990$) and outperforms `ITS` ($Hd = 0.8221$, 14 haplotypes) and `CAL` ($Hd = 0.9091$, 22 haplotypes) on a matched 56-isolate panel (`CMC` $Hd = 0.9857$, 44 haplotypes; combined MLSA $Hd = 0.9942$, 50 haplotypes).* **[Well Supported]** — Directly demonstrated by comparative sequencing, phylogenetic trees, and median-joining haplotype networks across the matched 56-isolate panel (Table 2, Figs. 2–5).
* **Claim 2:** *`CMC` localizes to a conserved 3.4 Mb chromosomal band in clinical-clade species (*S. brasiliensis*, *S. schenckii*, *S. globosa*, *S. luriei*) but is absent in *S. mexicana* and 12 other environmental Sporothrix species, with its closest homologs found in Ophiostoma piceae and other Sordariomycetes.* **[Well Supported]** — Demonstrably supported by PFGE + $\alpha\text{-}^{32}\text{P}$ Southern blot hybridization (Fig. 6) and whole-genome BLAST verification across 13 environmental *Sporothrix* assemblies.
* **Claim 3:** *Three N-glycosylation sequons (`NGS` at 62, `NNT` at 225, `NGT` at 373/374) and the phage-displayed Gp70 vaccine peptide `KPVQHALLTPLGLDR` (82.69%, 86/104 isolates) are broadly conserved across clinical Sporothrix species, whereas the `ZR8` peptide (`LKFLALASVISATSA`) is conserved in S. brasiliensis (87.50%, 28/32) but rare across the genus (26.92%, 28/104).* **[Well Supported]** — Quantified directly from the 104 aligned population sequences (lines 31, 62–64 of chunk 2).
* **Claim 4:** *High `CMC` polymorphism and negative `Fu's Fs` values in S. brasiliensis (`-12.606`) and S. schenckii (`-10.264`) indicate positive selection on `CMC` enzymatic activity driven by urban aromatic hydrocarbon pollutants (Abstract line 5; Results line 122–124; Discussion lines 40–44).* **[Overstated]** — As shown in Results line 124, synonymous diversity in `CMC` ($\pi_S = 0.10995$) is nearly 5-fold higher than nonsynonymous diversity ($\pi_N = 0.02316$; $\pi_N/\pi_S = 0.211$), demonstrating net purifying selection on the amino acid sequence. Negative `Fu's Fs` reflects an excess of rare (predominantly synonymous) haplotypes resulting from recent demographic expansion (such as the post-1990s Brazilian cat-transmitted epizootic) or purifying/background selection, and no site- or branch-level $d_N/d_S$ test or PAH growth assay is performed to support pollutant-driven positive selection.
* **Claim 5:** *Spectral evolutionary coupling analysis (`LERI`/`SAEC`) identifies Sporothrix-specific coevolving residue communities within `CMC` that differ from environmental Sordariomycetes and form conformational epitopes (lines 21–26, 54 of chunk 2; Fig. 9b–e).* **[Methodologically Flawed]** — Global covariance/coupling methods (`ECA`/`SAEC`) require large alignments of diverse homologs ($M_{\text{eff}} \gg 100$) to estimate pairwise residue couplings reliably. Within the clinical *Sporothrix* clade, sequences share $>98\%$ amino acid identity across only four species; running `SAEC` on this low-divergence alignment captures species-specific fixed differences (phylogenetic structure) rather than true compensatory coevolution.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1. Methodological & Statistical Defects
1. **Internal Contradiction Between Purifying Selection ($\pi_N/\pi_S = 0.21$) and Claims of Positive Selection via `Fu's Fs` (Results lines 122–124 vs. Abstract line 5 & Discussion lines 40–44):**
   * Results line 124 reports that `CMC` exhibits high synonymous nucleotide diversity ($\pi_S = 0.10995$) and low nonsynonymous diversity ($\pi_N = 0.02316$), explicitly concluding that this pattern indicates **purifying selection**. Yet in lines 122, 124, Abstract line 5, and Discussion lines 40–44, the authors interpret negative `Fu's Fs` values (`-12.606` in *S. brasiliensis*, `-10.264` in *S. schenckii*) as evidence of **positive selection** driven by polyaromatic hydrocarbon pollutants. `Fu's Fs` evaluates haplotype frequency spectra regardless of codon degeneracy; when $\pi_N/\pi_S = 0.21$, an excess of rare haplotypes is driven by demographic expansion (consistent with the clonal/epizootic expansion of cat-transmitted *S. brasiliensis*) and purifying selection, not positive diversifying selection on protein function.
2. **Application of Single-Population Neutrality and Gene-Flow Estimators Across Distinct Species (lines 122–124, 143, Table 2):**
   * In Table 2 (`Overall CMC 1` and `CMC 2`) and Results lines 122–124, `Fu's Fs` (`-9.415`), `Strobeck's S`, and `Tajima's D` (`-0.41730`) are calculated on the pooled 104-isolate dataset comprising four distinct species (*S. brasiliensis*, *S. schenckii*, *S. globosa*, *S. luriei*). Pooling highly subdivided species ($F_{ST} = 0.662\text{–}0.844$, Table 3) violates panmixia and artificially inflates pairwise nucleotide differences ($k = 61.400$) relative to segregating sites ($S = 361$).
   * Similarly, in line 143, the authors report Nei's 1973 haplotype-based $G_{ST}$ as showing `"a high level of gene flow (Nm = 8.90)"` across the three species before noting that sequence-based $\gamma_{ST}$ ($N_m = 0.16$) and $F_{ST}$ ($N_m = 0.08$) show limited gene flow. Because $G_{ST}$ treats every unique multi-SNP haplotype as an equally distant allele, when within-species haplotype diversity $H_S = 0.9645$ approaches 1.0, $G_{ST}$ is mathematically bounded near zero ($G_{ST} \le (1 - H_S)$) even when species share zero alleles (Hedrick 2005); computing $N_m = 8.90$ from raw $G_{ST}$ on high-diversity sequence haplotypes is a classic population-genetic artifact.
3. **Underpowered Evolutionary Coupling Analysis (`LERI`/`SAEC`) on Low-Divergence Clinical Isolates (line 109; chunk 2 lines 21–26, Fig. 9):**
   * Applying spectral evolutionary coupling (`SAEC`) separately to the clinical *Sporothrix* dataset (where protein sequences are nearly identical within species and span only 4 species) versus 69 Sordariomycetes sequences conflates sample size and tree topology with changes in intramolecular residue coupling networks.

### 3.2. Internal Numerical & Formatting Inconsistencies
* **Inconsistent Decimal Separator (`−12,606` vs. `5.691`) in Table 2 (lines 130–136):** In Table 2, the `Fu's Fs statistic` column uses commas as decimal separators for the five `CMC` rows (`−12,606`, `−10,264`, `−1,648`, `−9,415`, `−1,738`)—which in English notation reads as negative twelve thousand—while using periods in the main text (`−12.606`, `−10.264`, `−1.648`, `−9.415`) and in the `ITS` (`5.691`) and `CAL` (`7.163`) rows of the very same column.
* **PCR Master Mix dNTP Concentration Unit Error (line 65):** Line 65 states that the $2\times$ PCR Master Mix buffer included `"400 mmol/L each dNTP"` instead of `400 μmol/L` (`0.4 mmol/L`). Line 96 also reports storing nylon membranes at `"− 4 °C"` (likely `4 °C` or `−20 °C`).

## 4. Quantitative Rubric Scoring
* **Methodological & Statistical Rigor (1.8 / 3.5):** Strong experimental molecular typing (104 full-gene Sanger sequences, MLSA comparison, PFGE karyotyping + Southern blotting), offset by misinterpreting negative `Fu's Fs` as positive selection despite $\pi_N/\pi_S = 0.21$, pooling distinct species in neutrality tests, reporting unadjusted haplotype $G_{ST}$ ($N_m = 8.90$), and running `SAEC` coevolution analysis on a low-divergence alignment.
* **Claim Calibration & Evidence Alignment (1.3 / 2.5):** Diagnostic barcoding, chromosomal localization, and vaccine-peptide conservation claims are well supported, whereas the environmental PAH-driven positive selection narrative and coevolutionary residue-community claims are overstated.
* **Biological & Evolutionary Insight (1.5 / 2.0):** Demonstrating the presence of `CMC` on a 3.4 Mb chromosome across the pathogenic clade and its complete absence across 13 environmental *Sporothrix* genomes—alongside the contrasting population conservation of the `KPVQHALLTPLGLDR` (82.7%) and `ZR8` (26.9%) immunogenic peptides—provides actionable insight for sporotrichosis diagnostics and vaccine design.
* **Reproducibility & Data Hygiene (1.6 / 2.0):** All 12 primer sequences, thermal cycling profiles, PFGE pulse-time schedules, reference genome accessions, and GenBank/strain metadata (Tables S1–S5) are thoroughly documented.
