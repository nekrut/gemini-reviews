## 1. Blinded Metadata Perception
* **Perceived Field & Subfield:** Clinical Neuroimaging / Resting-State fMRI (Migraine Without Aura, Degree Centrality, and Granger Causality Analysis)
* **Perceived Study Design:** Cross-sectional case-control resting-state fMRI study comparing patients with migraine without aura (MwoA, $n = 50$ analyzed out of $53$ recruited) and healthy controls (HCs, $n = 48$ analyzed out of $50$ recruited) using voxel-wise degree centrality (DC) and bivariate lag-based Granger causality analysis (GCA) in RESTplus v1.25.
* **Tone & Structural Assessment:** Formulaic clinical resting-state fMRI manuscript with major omissions in physiological noise regression, contradictory statistical thresholds between the Methods and Results, complete absence of any table reporting peak MNI coordinates or test statistics, a sign-inverted interpretation of its primary clinical correlation, and un-corrected exploratory correlation testing across dozens of region-by-scale pairs.

## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study compares voxel-wise resting-state fMRI Degree Centrality (DC, correlation threshold $r > 0.25$) between $50$ patients with migraine without aura (MwoA) and $48$ healthy controls (HCs), and subsequently uses three clusters showing group DC differences (left inferior temporal gyrus [ITG], right amygdala, and right superior parietal gyrus [SPG]) as seeds for voxel-wise Granger causality analysis (GCA). The authors report decreased DC in the left ITG and increased DC in the right amygdala and right SPG in MwoA patients, alongside altered directional effective connectivity (EC) between these seeds and occipital/cerebellar/thalamic regions, and correlation of two pathways with MIDAS and HIT-6 clinical scores.

Forensic audit of the preprocessing pipeline, statistical reporting, and clinical correlations identifies multiple severe methodological defects:
1. **Complete Omission of White Matter, CSF, and Head-Motion Nuisance Covariate Regression in Voxel-Wise Degree Centrality:** In Section "Preprocessing" (line 75), the 7-step preprocessing pipeline includes only slice-timing, rigid-body realignment (excluding $>2.5\text{ mm}$ or $>2.5^\circ$), spatial normalization, bandpass filtering ($0.01\text{–}0.08\text{ Hz}$), and linear detrending. There is **no nuisance regression** of white matter (WM) signal, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) signal, global signal, or Friston-24 head-motion parameters, nor any framewise displacement (FD) scrubbing or group FD comparison. Because voxel-wise DC counts the number of whole-brain voxels exceeding $r > 0.25$ with each target voxel, un-regressed sub-threshold head motion and respiratory/cardiac physiological fluctuations globally inflate voxel-to-voxel correlations and dominate group DC differences.
2. **Direct Contradiction in Cluster-Forming Voxel Threshold (`p < 0.001` vs. `p < 0.01`) and Complete Absence of Any MNI Coordinate / Statistic Table:** In Section "Statistical Analysis" (line 90), the authors state that Gaussian Random Field (GRF) correction was applied at *"single voxel level p < 0.001 and cluster level p < 0.05"*. However, in Section "Results" (line 125) and the caption of Figure 3 (line 139), the threshold is reported as *"GRF, clustering level p < 0.05, voxel level p < 0.01"*—a tenfold more permissive primary threshold known to yield inflated false-positive cluster rates under GRF theory (Eklund et al., 2016). Moreover, the manuscript contains **zero tables** of neuroimaging results: neither peak MNI coordinates ($x, y, z$), cluster sizes ($k$), nor peak $t$-values are reported anywhere in the text or tables for either the DC analysis or the 6 directional GCA models.
3. **Hemodynamic Confounding and Circular Seed Selection in Lag-Based Granger Causality on Slow-TR ($2.0\text{ s}$) Bandpass-Filtered ($0.01\text{–}0.08\text{ Hz}$) BOLD Data:** Applying bivariate lag-based GCA ($1\text{ TR} = 2.0\text{ s}$ order) to slow-sampled ($2.0\text{ s}$), narrowband-filtered ($0.01\text{–}0.08\text{ Hz}$) BOLD signals between distant structures (inferior temporal gyrus, amygdala, thalamus, cerebellum, and occipital cortex) mistakes regional differences in vascular hemodynamic response function (HRF) time-to-peak ($1\text{–}2\text{ s}$ latency differences across brain territories) for neural information flow. Furthermore, selecting the three GCA seeds directly from clusters that survived group DC comparison in the exact same $98$ subjects introduces selection bias.
4. **Sign-Inverted Interpretation of the Primary Clinical Correlation, Uncorrected Multiple Testing Across $>50$ Pairs, and Numerical Errors in Table 1:** In Results (line 144) and Figure 4A, MwoA patients show *decreased* EC from the left ITG to the right IOG (line 119), yet within MwoA patients, EC(L_ITG $\to$ R_IOG) is *positively* correlated with MIDAS disability score ($r = +0.486, p = 0.000$). In the Discussion (line 162), the authors interpret this positive correlation backwards as *"suggesting that the lower ITG to the IOG outflow has a greater impact on the lives of migraine patients"*—when in reality $r = +0.486$ indicates that *higher* (more normal/elevated) ITG-to-IOG outflow is associated with *higher* MIDAS disability! Additionally, Table 1 (line 105) reports an impossible $p = 0.999$ for Education ($13.98 \pm 2.69$ vs. $13.68 \pm 3.58$, where two-sample $t_{96} = 0.470$ gives $p = 0.639$).

### Claim-by-Claim Audit Summary
* **Claim 1:** *"Compared with HCs, patients with MwoA had decreased DC values in the left ITG and increased DC values in the right amygdala and right SPG."* — **`[Fragile]`** (Computed without WM, CSF, or head-motion covariate regression; primary voxel threshold contradicts between `p < 0.001` in Methods and `p < 0.01` in Results; no MNI coordinates, cluster sizes, or peak $t$-statistics are reported anywhere in the manuscript.)
* **Claim 2:** *"In the MwoA group, the brain regions with decreased EC from the left ITG included the left lingual gyrus, right middle occipital gyrus (MOG), and right inferior occipital gyrus (IOG)... the brain regions with increased EC from the right SPG included the left cerebellum and left thalamus; the brain region with decreased EC to the right SPG included the right calcarine."* — **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** (Lag-based autoregressive GCA on $2.0\text{ s}$ TR, $0.01\text{–}0.08\text{ Hz}$ filtered BOLD data across cortical, thalamic, and cerebellar vascular territories is confounded by regional HRF latency variation; cluster-forming threshold dropped to `voxel p < 0.01` GRF; no coordinates or test statistics are provided.)
* **Claim 3:** *"Correlation analysis showed that the EC from the left ITG to the right IOG was positively correlated with MIDAS scores (r = 0.486, p = 0.000), suggesting that the lower ITG to the IOG outflow has a greater impact on the lives of migraine patients."* — **`[Overstated]`** (First, the directional interpretation in Discussion line 162 is mathematically inverted: because MIDAS measures migraine disability, a *positive* correlation $r = +0.486$ means *lower* ITG-to-IOG outflow is associated with *lower* disability, contradicting the group reduction in MwoA. Second, correlations were screened across 7 neuroimaging clusters and 8+ clinical scales [$\ge 56$ tests] without multiple-testing correction. Third, `MIDAS` was omitted from the list of covariates/scales in Methods line 90, while `VLSQ-8` was listed in Methods lines 48 and 90 but omitted from Table 1.)

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1. Omission of Nuisance Covariate Regression (WM, CSF, Motion) in Voxel-Wise Degree Centrality (Pillar 3 — Critical)
* **Location:** Section "Preprocessing" (line 75) and Section "DC Calculation" (line 79).
* **The Defect:** The authors list the entire fMRI preprocessing workflow in 7 numbered steps (line 75):
  1. Discard first 10 volumes;
  2. Slice-timing correction;
  3. Head movement correction (excluding translation $>2.5\text{ mm}$ or rotation $>2.5^\circ$);
  4. Spatial normalization to MNI space ($3 \times 3 \times 3\text{ mm}^3$);
  5. Spatial smoothing ($6\text{ mm}$ FWHM, applied only before GCA);
  6. Bandpass filtering ($0.01\text{–}0.08\text{ Hz}$);
  7. Detrending to remove linear trends.
* **Quantitative Impact:** Critically, **no nuisance regression** (neither rigid-body/Friston-24 motion parameters, white matter signal, CSF signal, nor scrubbing of high-motion frames) was performed prior to computing whole-brain voxel-wise Pearson correlations at $r > 0.25$. In resting-state fMRI, sub-millimeter in-scanner head motion and physiological cardiac/respiratory pulsations induce widespread, distance-dependent artifactual correlations across grey matter voxels (Power et al., 2012; Yan et al., 2013), directly biasing voxel-wise Degree Centrality. Nor do the authors report mean Framewise Displacement (FD) for the MwoA ($n = 50$) and HC ($n = 48$) groups.

### 3.2. Contradictory GRF Voxel Thresholds (`p < 0.001` vs. `p < 0.01`) and Total Omission of Neuroimaging Result Tables (Pillar 2 & Pillar 5 — Critical)
* **Location:** Section "Statistical Analysis" (line 90) vs. Section "Results" (line 125) and Figure 3 caption (line 139).
* **The Defect:**
  1. **Threshold Contradiction:** Line 90 states: *"A single voxel level p < 0.001 and cluster level p < 0.05 (using GRF around two-tailed tests) were set as the threshold."* However, Results line 125 (`voxel level p < 0.01, cluster level p < 0.05, tuned by GRF`) and Figure 3 caption line 139 (`GRF, clustering level p < 0.05, voxel level p < 0.01`) report a 10-fold looser cluster-forming threshold of $p < 0.01$ (and Figure 2 caption line 117 fails to state the voxel-level threshold altogether). Under Gaussian Random Field theory, a cluster-forming threshold of $p < 0.01$ violates high-threshold asymptotic assumptions and produces family-wise false-positive rates of $30\%\text{–}70\%$ (Eklund et al., 2016).
  2. **Missing Neuroimaging Coordinate & Statistic Tables:** Remarkably, the manuscript contains **only Table 1 (Demographics)**. There is no table anywhere in the manuscript reporting the peak MNI coordinates ($x, y, z$), Automated Anatomical Labeling (AAL) labels, cluster voxel extents ($k$), or peak $t$-values for the 3 DC clusters (Figure 2) or the 8 GCA clusters (Figure 3). Readers and meta-analysts cannot verify where in the ITG, amygdala, SPG, thalamus, or cerebellum these clusters are located.

### 3.3. Methodological Invalidity of Lag-Based Granger Causality on Filtered $2.0\text{ s}$ TR BOLD fMRI (Pillar 3 — Major)
* **Location:** Section "GCA Analysing" (line 84) and Section "Results: GCA Analysis" (lines 119–139).
* **The Defect:** The authors apply coefficient-based bivariate Granger causality with a 1-step autoregressive time lag ($\text{TR} = 2.0\text{ s}$) to BOLD timeseries that were bandpass-filtered to $0.01\text{–}0.08\text{ Hz}$ (periods of $12.5\text{–}100\text{ s}$).
  1. Regional variations in neurovascular coupling and hemodynamic response latency across cortical, subcortical (amygdala/thalamus), and cerebellar territories easily span $1.0\text{–}2.5\text{ s}$, which exceeds neural synaptic conduction delays ($10\text{–}50\text{ ms}$) by two orders of magnitude and confounds the sign of autoregressive lag coefficients in BOLD GCA (Smith et al., 2011; Webb et al., 2013).
  2. Selecting the three seeds (left ITG, right amygdala, right SPG) based on their significant group DC differences in the same $50$ MwoA and $48$ HC subjects (line 84) and then testing group GCA differences on the exact same timeseries data is non-independent.

### 3.4. Sign-Inverted Clinical Interpretation, Uncorrected Multiple Testing, and Demographic Table Arithmetic Error (Pillar 4 & Pillar 5 — Major)
* **Location:** Table 1 (line 105), Methods (lines 48, 90), Results (line 144), Figure 4 (line 146), and Discussion (line 162).
* **The Defect:**
  1. **Sign-Inverted Interpretation of $r = +0.486$ (Line 162):** MwoA patients have *lower* EC from the left ITG to the right IOG than HCs (line 119). Yet within MwoA patients, EC(L_ITG $\to$ R_IOG) correlates *positively* with MIDAS disability score ($r = 0.486$, line 144). Higher MIDAS scores reflect *greater* migraine-related disability. Thus, patients with *lower* (more abnormal) EC(L_ITG $\to$ R_IOG) actually had *lower* MIDAS disability! In Discussion line 162, the authors state the exact opposite: *"EC from the left ITG to the right IOG was positively correlated with MIDAS scores, suggesting that the lower ITG to the IOG outflow has a greater impact on the lives of migraine patients."*
  2. **Uncorrected Multiple Correlation Screening and Inconsistent Scale Lists:** Line 90 states that Spearman's correlation (corrected for education, sex, and age) was run across 8 clinical scales (`SAS`, `SDS`, `MoCA`, `frequency`, `duration`, `HIT-6`, `VAS`, `VLSQ`). Notably, `MIDAS` is omitted from line 90's list despite being the primary reported correlation in line 144, whereas `VLSQ-8` (Visual Light Sensitivity Questionnaire-8, described in line 48 as a key visual photophobia scale) is completely missing from Table 1 and Results! Across 3 DC seeds + 8 GCA target clusters ($11$ neural features) and $9$ clinical scales ($99$ partial correlation tests), no false discovery rate (FDR) or Bonferroni correction was applied; the HIT-6 correlation ($r = -0.351, p = 0.012$, line 144) would not survive correction for even a single scale across the 11 brain clusters ($\alpha = 0.05/11 = 0.0045$).
  3. **Impossible $p$-Value in Table 1 (Line 105):** For `Education (years)`, MwoA ($n = 50$) is $13.98 \pm 2.69$ and HC ($n = 48$) is $13.68 \pm 3.58$, yet Table 1 reports $p = 0.999$. A two-sample $t$-test yields $t_{96} = \frac{13.98 - 13.68}{\sqrt{2.69^2/50 + 3.58^2/48}} = \frac{0.30}{0.642} = 0.467$, corresponding to $p = 0.641$ (not $0.999$, which would require $\Delta\bar{x} < 0.001$). Additionally, line 144 and Figure 4A report `p = 0.000` instead of $p < 0.001$ (for $r = 0.486, n = 50$ with 3 covariates, $t_{45} = 3.73, p = 5.3 \times 10^{-4}$).

## 4. Quantitative Rubric Scoring

* **Rigor (0.9 / 3.5):** Complete omission of white matter, CSF, and head-motion nuisance regression prior to voxel-wise degree centrality; contradictory GRF voxel thresholds (`p < 0.001` in Methods vs. `p < 0.01` in Results); hemodynamic confounding in lag-based GCA on $2.0\text{ s}$ TR bandpass-filtered BOLD data; uncorrected correlation testing across dozens of feature-scale pairs; arithmetic error in Table 1 ($p = 0.999$).
* **Claims (0.9 / 2.5):** Directional interpretation of the primary MIDAS correlation ($r = +0.486$) is inverted in the Discussion; directional "causal" claims from slow-TR BOLD GCA overstate what autoregressive lag models can resolve.
* **Biology (1.0 / 2.0):** The clinical cohort ($n = 50$ MwoA vs. $48$ HCs during the interictal period) is reasonably sized and assessed with multiple headache and neuropsychological scales, though the visual light sensitivity scale (`VLSQ-8`) collected in Methods is omitted from all tables and analyses.
* **Reproducibility (0.9 / 2.0):** Complete absence of any neuroimaging table reporting MNI peak coordinates, cluster sizes, or $t$-values makes the neuroimaging findings impossible to verify or include in coordinate-based meta-analyses; data are available only upon request (`Data Availability Statement`, line 183).
