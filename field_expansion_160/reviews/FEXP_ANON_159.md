## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study investigates the atomic-level interactions of three phenolic environmental endocrine-disrupting chemicals—`4-sec-butylphenol` (co-crystal PDB: `6I66`), `tetrahydro-2-naphthol` (co-crystal PDB: `6I67`), and the lignin-derived bisphenol substitute `2,2-bis(4-hydroxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenyl)propane` (`BP(2,2)(Me)`, docked via AutoDock 4.2 into `6I66`)—with the human estrogen-related receptor $\gamma$ ($\text{hERR}\gamma$) ligand-binding domain. For each complex, three independent $250\text{ ns}$ explicit-solvent Amber18 (`ff14SB`/`GAFF`/`TIP3P`) simulations were performed, and the last $200\text{ ns}$ of each replica were concatenated ($600\text{ ns}$ total per system, $3,000$ frames) to compute RMSD, RMSF, dynamical cross-correlation matrices ($C_{ij}$), principal component modes, and binding free energies via both MM-PBSA (with normal-mode entropy on 100 frames) and SIE (`Sietraj`).

A forensic audit of Tables 1 and 2 confirms that all thermodynamic sums ($\Delta E_{\text{MM}}$, $\Delta G_{\text{sol}}$, $\Delta G_{\text{polar}}$, $\Delta G_{\text{nonpol}}$, $\Delta H$, $\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ in MM-PBSA, and the scaled $\alpha(\Delta G_{\text{nonpol}} + \Delta G_{\text{pol}}) + C$ relation in SIE with $\alpha = 0.1048, C = -2.89\text{ kcal/mol}$) and experimental $\Delta G_{\text{exp}}$ conversions ($-9.49$ and $-8.98\text{ kcal/mol}$ from $\text{EC}_{50} = 109$ and $260\text{ nM}$) are mathematically consistent. Nevertheless, four scientific and presentation defects weaken the manuscript:

1. **Physically Misinterpreted Mechanism for Weakened `BP(2,2)(Me)` Binding Affinity (Abstract, line 5; Section 2.2, Table 1; Section 2.3, line 84; Conclusion, line 139):** Both the Abstract and Conclusion state: *"What is striking is that the hydrophobic methoxide groups of BP(2,2)(Me) can help to reduce the binding energy of PEDCs with hERR$\gamma$."* Here, the authors use the phrase "reduce the binding energy" to mean **weaken the net binding affinity** ($\Delta G_{\text{bind}} = -2.97\text{ kcal/mol}$ for `BP(2,2)(Me)` vs. $-5.94\text{ kcal/mol}$ for `4-sec-butylphenol` in Table 1). However, inspecting the energy decomposition in Table 1 reveals that the nonpolar van der Waals and surface-area interactions of `BP(2,2)(Me)` are actually **twice as favorable** ($\Delta G_{\text{nonpol}} = -50.20\text{ kcal/mol}$) as those of `4-sec-butylphenol` ($\Delta G_{\text{nonpol}} = -25.62\text{ kcal/mol}$) and `tetrahydro-2-naphthol` ($-25.10\text{ kcal/mol}$) due to the larger molecular surface and methyl contacts! Why, then, is the net binding affinity $\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ of `BP(2,2)(Me)` weaker ($-2.97\text{ kcal/mol}$)? Because burying its four polar methoxy oxygens and two phenolic hydroxyls inside the hydrophobic $\text{hERR}\gamma$ pocket without strong hydrogen bonding ($\Delta E_{\text{ele}} = -5.59\text{ kcal/mol}$ vs. $-13.02\text{ kcal/mol}$) incurs a massive **polar desolvation penalty** ($\Delta G_{\text{PB}} = +32.29\text{ kcal/mol}$, giving $\Delta G_{\text{polar}} = +26.70\text{ kcal/mol}$ vs. $+4.47\text{ kcal/mol}$) and a larger **conformational entropy penalty** ($-T\Delta S = +20.52\text{ kcal/mol}$ vs. $+15.21\text{ kcal/mol}$). Calling the methoxy groups "hydrophobic groups that reduce binding energy" obscures the actual thermodynamic mechanism shown in Table 1 (uncompensated polar desolvation of the four ether oxygens plus entropic loss).
2. **Sign-Reversed Prose When Comparing Negative Per-Residue Energies (Section 2.3, lines 80–84):** Throughout Section 2.3, the authors confuse algebraic sign with interaction strength when comparing negative energy values: e.g., line 82 states *"causes the backbone electrostatic energy of Leu268 to be $-0.58\text{ kcal/mol}$, so the total energy is increased by $0.32\text{ kcal/mol}$"* (meaning the interaction became $0.32\text{ kcal/mol}$ *more favorable*, i.e., more negative) and *"the energy of Ala272 with tetrahydro-2-napthol decreases by $0.27\text{ kcal/mol}$. This results from the formation of a relatively weak interaction"* (meaning the magnitude $|\Delta G|$ decreased by $0.27\text{ kcal/mol}$, i.e., $\Delta G$ became less negative).
3. **Omitted Mathematical Equations in Methods Sections 3.4, 3.5, and 3.6 (Lines 109–127):** Lines 109, 116, and 125 introduce equations with *"The calculation formulas are presented as follows:"*, *"The calculation formulas are as follows:"*, and *"The formula is as follows:"*, followed by definitions of the variables ($\Delta E_{\text{ele}}, \rho, D_{\text{in}}, \alpha, \gamma, C, C_{ij}, \Delta\mathbf{r}_i$), but the displayed equations themselves are missing from the manuscript text.
4. **Absence of *Apo* $\text{hERR}\gamma$ Simulation and Mischaracterization of $\text{hERR}\gamma$ as Having a "Catalytic Function" (Section 2.1, line 29):** Line 29 states *"This is mainly because the catalytic function of hERR$\gamma$ requires a stable three-dimensional structure."* $\text{hERR}\gamma$ is a constitutively active nuclear hormone receptor transcription factor, not an enzyme with catalytic activity. Moreover, without an *apo* $\text{hERR}\gamma$ trajectory, the study cannot determine whether `BP(2,2)(Me)` stabilizes or destabilizes the active AF-2 Helix 12 conformation relative to the unliganded receptor.

**Editorial Verdict:** **Minor-to-Moderate Revision.** The MD/MM-PBSA/SIE calculations are numerically sound and internally consistent, but the thermodynamic explanation of `BP(2,2)(Me)` desolvation, the sign terminology for negative free energies, the missing equation blocks, and the "catalytic function" misnomer must be corrected.

### Specific Conclusions and Assessment of Support
* **Claim 1:** Three $250\text{ ns}$ MD simulations per complex show that `4-sec-butylphenol`, `tetrahydro-2-naphthol`, and `BP(2,2)(Me)` form stable complexes with the $\text{hERR}\gamma$ LBD (mean backbone RMSD $2.17–2.31\text{ \AA}$) stabilized primarily by van der Waals interactions with hydrophobic pocket residues (`Leu268`, `Leu271`, `Ala272`, `Leu309`, `Val313`, `Tyr326`, `Leu345`, `Phe435`) and a persistent hydrogen bond with `Glu275` ($86.93\%$ occupancy for `4-sec-butylphenol`). `[Well Supported]` — Directly supported by the multi-replica MD trajectories, Tables 1 and 2, and per-residue decomposition in Figures 2–4.
* **Claim 2:** Both MM-PBSA ($\Delta G_{\text{bind}} = -5.94, -5.67, -2.97\text{ kcal/mol}$) and SIE ($\Delta G_{\text{bind}} = -6.68, -6.54, -5.68\text{ kcal/mol}$) reproduce the experimental rank order showing weaker $\text{hERR}\gamma$ binding by the lignin-derived bisphenol `BP(2,2)(Me)` compared to `4-sec-butylphenol` and `tetrahydro-2-naphthol`. `[Well Supported]` — All thermodynamic components in Tables 1 and 2 are arithmetically consistent and align with experimental $\Delta G_{\text{exp}}$ ($-9.49$ and $-8.98\text{ kcal/mol}$).
* **Claim 3:** The methoxy groups of `BP(2,2)(Me)`, acting as hydrophobic groups, reduce the binding energy of `BP(2,2)(Me)` with $\text{hERR}\gamma$. `[Overstated]` — While the methyl carbons of the methoxy groups enhance nonpolar van der Waals attraction ($\Delta E_{\text{vdW}} = -46.47\text{ kcal/mol}$), the net binding free energy $\Delta G_{\text{bind}}$ ($-2.97\text{ kcal/mol}$) is weakened by the large polar desolvation penalty ($\Delta G_{\text{PB}} = +32.29\text{ kcal/mol}$; $\Delta G_{\text{polar}} = +26.70\text{ kcal/mol}$) of burying the four methoxy oxygens and two hydroxyls without strong electrostatic compensation ($\Delta E_{\text{ele}} = -5.59\text{ kcal/mol}$), combined with higher entropic cost ($-T\Delta S = +20.52\text{ kcal/mol}$).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Major Methodological and Statistical Defects

* **Defect 1: Confused Thermodynamic Interpretation of `BP(2,2)(Me)` Methoxy Groups and Sign Ambiguity in Free Energy Comparisons.**
  * *Location:* Abstract (line 5), Section 2.2 (Table 1, lines 39–51), Section 2.3 (lines 80–84), and Conclusion (line 139).
  * *Forensic Evidence:*
    1. Table 1 shows that moving from `4-sec-butylphenol` to `BP(2,2)(Me)` changes $\Delta G_{\text{nonpol}}$ from $-25.62$ to $-50.20\text{ kcal/mol}$ ($\Delta\Delta G_{\text{nonpol}} = -24.58\text{ kcal/mol}$, strongly favorable), changes $\Delta G_{\text{polar}} = \Delta E_{\text{ele}} + \Delta G_{\text{PB}}$ from $+4.47$ to $+26.70\text{ kcal/mol}$ ($\Delta\Delta G_{\text{polar}} = +22.23\text{ kcal/mol}$, strongly unfavorable), and changes $-T\Delta S$ from $+15.21$ to $+20.52\text{ kcal/mol}$ ($\Delta(-T\Delta S) = +5.31\text{ kcal/mol}$, unfavorable), yielding a net $\Delta\Delta G_{\text{bind}} = -24.58 + 22.23 + 5.31 = +2.96\text{ kcal/mol}$ (from $-5.94$ to $-2.97\text{ kcal/mol}$). Thus, the weaker net binding of `BP(2,2)(Me)` is driven by polar desolvation ($\Delta G_{\text{PB}}$) and conformational entropy ($-T\Delta S$), whereas the nonpolar interactions of the methoxy methyls actually *strengthen* $\Delta E_{\text{vdW}}$.
    2. In Section 2.3 (lines 80–84), phrases such as *"energies that contribute more than $-0.5\text{ kcal/mol}$"*, *"offer over $-1\text{ kcal/mol}$"*, *"total energy is increased by $0.32\text{ kcal/mol}$"*, and *"decreases by $0.27\text{ kcal/mol}$"* use algebraic inequalities and directional verbs backward for negative quantities.

* **Defect 2: Missing Displayed Equations in Methods Sections 3.4, 3.5, and 3.6.**
  * *Location:* Section 3.4 (line 109–111), Section 3.5 (line 116–120), and Section 3.6 (line 125–127).
  * *Forensic Evidence:* In all three sections, the text states *"The calculation formulas are [presented] as follows:"*, followed immediately by the definitions of symbols ($\Delta E_{\text{ele}}, \Delta E_{\text{vdW}}, \Delta G_{\text{PB}}, \Delta G_{\text{SA}}, \gamma, \beta$; $E_{\text{Coul}}, E_{\text{vdW}}, \Delta G_{\text{R}}, G_{\text{cav}}, \rho, D_{\text{in}}, \alpha, C$; and $C_{ij}, \Delta\mathbf{r}_i$), with the equation lines themselves completely absent.

* **Defect 3: Lack of an Unliganded (*Apo*) $\text{hERR}\gamma$ Control Trajectory and Misstatement of Receptor Function.**
  * *Location:* Section 2.1 (line 29).
  * *Forensic Evidence:* Line 29 refers to *"the catalytic function of hERR$\gamma$."* As a nuclear receptor transcription factor, $\text{hERR}\gamma$ has no enzymatic catalytic activity; its biological readout is coactivator recruitment mediated by the agonist conformation of Helix 12 (AF-2). Including an *apo* $\text{hERR}\gamma$ control simulation is needed to quantify whether ligand binding stabilizes or perturbs H12 relative to the constitutively active *apo* state.

### 3.2 Minor Presentation, Structural, and Editorial Issues
* **Residue Numbering Typo (Section 2.3, line 84):** Lists `Met33` instead of `Met332` (*"Tyr326, Tyr330, Met33, Leu342"*).
* **Inconsistent Ligand Spelling:** `tetrahydro-2-napthol` (missing `h`) is used throughout the text except at line 29 (`tetrahydro-2-naphthol`).

## 4. Quantitative Rubric Scoring

* **Methodological & Statistical Rigor (1.7 / 3.5):** Three $250\text{ ns}$ independent MD replicas per complex, redocking validation (`Figure S10`), and dual MM-PBSA + SIE free energy estimations with exact arithmetic consistency in Tables 1 and 2 provide a solid computational foundation; deducted for missing equation blocks in the Methods and lack of an *apo* control.
* **Claim-Evidence Alignment (1.3 / 2.5):** The residue hotspot and relative affinity claims are supported by Tables 1 and 2, but the headline claim regarding how the methoxy groups of `BP(2,2)(Me)` reduce binding energy conflates favorable nonpolar van der Waals contacts with unfavorable polar desolvation.
* **Biological & Domain Validity (1.1 / 2.0):** Identifying `BP(2,2)(Me)` as a weaker $\text{hERR}\gamma$ binder than industrial alkylphenols is relevant to safer bisphenol design, though referring to the "catalytic function" of a nuclear receptor is a domain error.
* **Reproducibility & Transparency (1.4 / 2.0):** PDB IDs (`6I66`, `6I67`), docking grid coordinates/dimensions, force fields, simulation parameters, and SIE/MM-PBSA constants are fully specified.
