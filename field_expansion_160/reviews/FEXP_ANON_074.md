## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This methodological manuscript introduces `PRS-PGx-TL`, a transfer-learning framework designed to construct pharmacogenomic (PGx) polygenic risk scores from two-arm randomized clinical trial (RCT) data by initializing prognostic ($G$) weights from pre-trained large-scale disease PRS models (`Lassosum` or `PRS-CS`) and updating both prognostic ($\boldsymbol{\beta}_G$) and predictive ($\boldsymbol{\beta}_{GT}$, genotype-by-treatment interaction) weights via a two-dimensional $L_2$-penalized gradient descent algorithm (Algorithm 1) across six implementation variants (M1–M6). Using extensive spike-and-slab simulations varying cross-cohort genetic correlation ($\rho_{DT}$), prognostic-predictive correlation ($\rho_E$), interaction scaling ($\gamma$), polygenicity ($p_{\text{causal}}$), heritability ($h_D^2$), and imperfect causal-variant overlap, the authors show that M1–M2 maximize overall $R^2$ while M3–M6 maximize predictive ($G \times T$) interaction power. Applied via nested $5 \times 4$-fold cross-validation to $N = 5,661$ subjects in the IMPROVE-IT trial (1-month LDL-C response), `PRS-PGx-TL` increases overall $R^2$ slightly ($0.048 \to 0.052$ for Lassosum; $0.072 \to 0.075$ for PRS-CS) and reduces the $\text{PRS}_{GT} \times T$ interaction $P$-value from non-significant ($0.388$ and $0.063$) to $2.83 \times 10^{-6}$ (Lassosum-M5) and $9.60 \times 10^{-5}$ (PRS-CS-M5), prioritizing known lipid-response loci on chromosome 19 (*APOE* `rs7412`, *PVRL2* `rs7254892`). Open-source code and an R package (`PRSPGxTL`) are publicly provided on GitHub.

**Editorial Verdict: Minor-to-Moderate Revision (Strong / Sound).** This is a well-motivated, clearly structured methodological contribution with comprehensive simulation benchmarks, a real clinical trial validation (`IMPROVE-IT`), and open-source software. However, a mathematical audit of Equations (3)–(7) and the nested cross-validation inference procedure identifies three important theoretical and statistical issues that should be addressed: (1) **Zero-centered vs. prior-centered $L_2$ regularization in Eq. (3) and Eq. (7)**, where penalizing $\lambda \|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2$ toward $\mathbf{0}$ (rather than $\lambda \|\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{b}^{(0)}\|_2^2$ toward the pre-trained weights $\mathbf{b}^{(0)}$) causes the pre-trained disease initialization $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ to decay exponentially at rate $(1 - \lambda^{\text{new}})^r$ whenever $\lambda^{\text{new}} \in \{0.5, 0.99\}$; (2) **Post-cross-validation pooling bias** in Table 2, where out-of-fold predictions from the 5 correlated outer CV folds in IMPROVE-IT are concatenated to compute parametric OLS interaction $P$-values without accounting for shared training data across folds or scale misalignment between base and target phenotypes; and (3) **Verbatim duplicate paragraphs** in the Methods section.

### Assessment of Specific Conclusions
* **Claim 1: `PRS-PGx-TL` outperforms baseline disease PRS (`PRS-Dis`: `Lassosum`, `PRS-CS`, `C+T`) and zero-initialized direct `PRS-PGx` across simulation scenarios (Figs. 2–3, Figs. S1–S6):** `[Well Supported]`  
  The simulation design systematically varies $\rho_{DT} \in \{0.2, 0.5, 0.8\}$, $\rho_E \in \{0.2, 0.5, 0.8\}$, $\gamma \in \{0.5, 1, 5\}$, $p_{\text{causal}}$, $h_D^2$, and partial causal overlap (60% and 80%), evaluating performance on an independent holdout test set ($N = 1,000$) across 1,000 replicates. Crucially, Figure S3 includes the essential ablation baseline (`PRS-PGx` initialized at $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{(0)} = \mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\beta}_{GT}^{(0)} = \mathbf{0}$ under the exact same 2D gradient descent algorithm), confirming that transferring $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ improves overall $R^2$ and interaction detection.
* **Claim 2: Zero-initializing the interaction weights ($\boldsymbol{\beta}_{GT}^{(0)} = \mathbf{0}$ in M1, M3, M5) outperforms initializing $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{GT}^{(0)} = \boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ (M2, M4, M6):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Because main disease effects and drug-by-genotype interaction effects do not share identical signs or magnitudes across all SNPs ($\rho_E < 1$), initializing $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{GT}^{(0)} = \mathbf{0}$ avoids injecting false-positive main-effect noise into the interaction vector.
* **Claim 3: In IMPROVE-IT ($N = 5,661$), `PRS-PGx-TL` substantially improves $\text{PRS}_{GT} \times T$ interaction significance ($P = 2.83 \times 10^{-6}$ for Lassosum-M5; $P = 9.60 \times 10^{-5}$ for PRS-CS-M5) and patient stratification:** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  While the quantile stratification trends (Fig. 4) and chromosome 19 *APOE*/*PVRL2* signals are biologically plausible, the incremental variance explained by the interaction term in IMPROVE-IT is very small ($\Delta R^2_{\text{cond}} = 0.001\text{ to }0.004$, i.e., $0.1\%\text{--}0.4\%$ of variance in Table 2), and the parametric interaction $P$-values in Table 2 are computed by pooling out-of-fold predictions across the 5 outer folds of nested CV, which can inflate significance compared to a permutation test or external holdout cohort.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. [Major] Mathematical Formulation of the $L_2$ Penalty: Shrinking Toward Zero ($\lambda \|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2$) vs. Shrinking Toward the Pre-Trained Prior ($\lambda \|\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{b}^{(0)}\|_2^2$)
* **Location:** Methods Section "PRS-PGx-TL", Equations (3)–(7) (Lines 49–55) and Algorithm 1.
* **Defect:** In Eq. (3), the authors define the penalized objective function as:
  $$\text{loss}(\mathbf{b}) = (\mathbf{Y} - \mathbf{C}\boldsymbol{\delta} - \mathbf{X}\mathbf{b})^T(\mathbf{Y} - \mathbf{C}\boldsymbol{\delta} - \mathbf{X}\mathbf{b}) + \lambda \mathbf{b}^T \mathbf{b}$$
  which leads to the update rule in Eq. (7) and Algorithm 1:
  $$\mathbf{b}^{(r+1)} = (1 - \lambda^{\text{new}})\mathbf{b}^{(r)} + \eta^{\text{new}} \mathbf{X}^T (\mathbf{Y} - \mathbf{X}\mathbf{b}^{(r)}), \quad \lambda^{\text{new}} \in \{0, 0.5, 0.99\}.$$
  Notice the mathematical behavior of this update rule on the pre-trained prognostic weights $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{(0)} = \boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ in Models M1–M4:
  * Because the $L_2$ penalty in Eq. (3) penalizes $\|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2$ (toward $\mathbf{0}$) rather than the transfer-learning deviation $\|\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{b}^{(0)}\|_2^2$ (toward $\mathbf{b}^{(0)}$), whenever $\lambda^{\text{new}} = 0.99$, the factor $(1 - \lambda^{\text{new}}) = 0.01$ **wipes out 99% of the pre-trained disease weights $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ on the very first iteration ($r = 1$)**! Even for $\lambda^{\text{new}} = 0.5$, $(1 - 0.5)^r = 2^{-r}$ erases $>99.9\%$ of $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ within $10$ iterations.
  * Thus, under Eq. (7), information from $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ in M1–M4 is preserved *only* when $\lambda^{\text{new}} = 0$ (unpenalized early stopping) or at $r = 1\text{--}2$ (which explains why Figure S10 peaks at very small $r$ and why M5—which freezes $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G = \boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ so it cannot be shrunk to zero—outperforms M3 on IMPROVE-IT in Table 2!).
* **Remediation:** Explicitly discuss the distinction between zero-centered ridge shrinkage ($\lambda \|\mathbf{b}\|_2^2$) and prior-centered proximal transfer shrinkage ($\lambda \|\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{b}^{(0)}\|_2^2$, where the update retains $+\lambda^{\text{new}} \mathbf{b}^{(0)}$ so $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G$ is shrunk toward $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ rather than $\mathbf{0}$), and report which values of $\lambda^{\text{new}} \in \{0, 0.5, 0.99\}$ were selected by cross-validation in M1–M4 vs. M5–M6.

### 2. [Major] Uncalibrated Base-to-Target Scale Factor in Models M5/M6 and Pooled Out-of-Fold $P$-Value Inflation in Nested CV (Table 2)
* **Location:** Results Section "Workflow of PRS-PGx-TL" (Line 32), Section "IMPROVE-IT PGx GWAS data analysis results" (Lines 31–44, Table 2), and Algorithm 1.
* **Defect:**
  1. **Base-to-target scale mismatch in M5/M6:** In Algorithm 1, Models M5 and M6 fix $\boldsymbol{\beta}_G = \boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ (estimated from cross-sectional LDL-C levels in GLGC) and update $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{GT}$ using the residual $\mathbf{Y} - \mathbf{G}\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}} - (\mathbf{G} \times \mathbf{T})\boldsymbol{\beta}_{GT}^{(r)}$, where $\mathbf{Y}$ is the covariate-adjusted 1-month log-fold change in LDL-C in IMPROVE-IT. Because cross-sectional LDL-C (SD units) and 1-month log-fold LDL-C response have different phenotypic scales and heritabilities, subtracting raw $\mathbf{G}\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ without first fitting an OLS scaling scalar $\hat{c}_G (\mathbf{G}\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}})$ on the training fold leaves scale-mismatched prognostic residuals in the gradient update.
  2. **Post-CV pooling inference bias:** In Table 2, the authors concatenate out-of-fold $\text{PRS}_G$ and $\text{PRS}_{GT}$ predictions across the 5 outer CV folds ($N = 5,661$) and compute a single standard OLS $P$-value for $\text{PRS}_{GT} \times T$. Because the 5 outer training folds overlap by $75\%$ with one another and each fold uses different tuned hyperparameters $(\eta_k, \lambda_k, r_k)$ yielding different score variances across folds, standard parametric $t$-test $P$-values on concatenated CV predictions can be anti-conservative (moreover, notice in Table 2 that for `PRS-Dis` the sign of $\hat{\beta}_{GT}$ is negative [$-0.018$ and $-0.044$], whereas for `PRS-PGx-TL` it is positive [$+0.008$ to $+0.028$] because $\text{PRS}_{GT}$ is trained directly to correlate positively with $Y$ in the treatment arm).
* **Remediation:** Standardize or regress out $\hat{c}_G \mathbf{G}\boldsymbol{\beta}_G^{\text{pre}}$ within each training fold before updating $\boldsymbol{\beta}_{GT}$ in M5/M6, standardize out-of-fold PRS predictions within each outer fold before concatenation (or report the median/mean test statistic across folds alongside a permutation-based $P$-value), and explain why $\hat{\beta}_{GT}$ flips sign between `PRS-Dis` and `PRS-PGx-TL` in Table 2.

### 3. [Minor] Verbatim Duplicate Paragraphs in Methods Section "Existing disease PRS methods"
* **Location:** Methods Section "Existing disease PRS methods" (Paragraphs 1–4, `ContentOffset=92160` Lines 36–43).
* **Defect:** The entire descriptions of the three baseline methods (`C + T`, `Lassosum`, and `PRS-CS`, ~450 words total) appear inline inside the first paragraph of "Existing disease PRS methods" (Line 36) and are then repeated word-for-word as three standalone paragraphs immediately below (Lines 38, 40, and 42).
* **Remediation:** Delete the duplicated text in Paragraph 1 of Section "Existing disease PRS methods" so that the descriptions of `C + T`, `Lassosum`, and `PRS-CS` appear only once.
