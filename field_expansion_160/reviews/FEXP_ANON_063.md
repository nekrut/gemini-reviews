## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict
This study evaluates whether genetically predicted dietary intakes of four antioxidants (vitamins A, C, E, and carotene) causally decelerate biological aging measured by four established epigenetic clocks (`HannumAge`, `IEAA`, `GrimAge`, `PhenoAge`). The manuscript is clearly structured, reports all $4 \times 4 = 16$ exposure–outcome pairs transparently in `Table 1` and `Figure 2` (rather than selectively hiding null traits), and appropriately concludes that vitamins A, E, and carotene have no causal effect on any of the four epigenetic clocks. However, forensic audit identifies three methodological and provenance flaws that weaken the primary positive claims regarding vitamin C:

1. **False Database Provenance (Attributing UK Biobank 24-Hour Recall GWAS Summary Statistics to the "FinnGen Project Database"):** In Section 2.1 (line 28), the authors state: *"Summary-level data of these 4 dietary antioxidants were got from FinnGen Project Database (`https://r11.finngen.fi/`). The datasets about vitamin C, vitamin E and carotene include 64,979 European participants and 9,851,867 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). The data about vitamin A involves 62,991 European participants and 9,851,867 SNPs."* This provenance statement is factually incorrect: FinnGen is a Finnish hospital/registry biobank that does not collect 24-hour dietary nutrient intake questionnaires on $64,979$ individuals with exactly $9,851,867$ SNPs. Those exact sample sizes ($N = 64,979$ for vitamin C, vitamin E, and carotene; $N = 62,991$ for retinol/vitamin A) and exact SNP count ($9,851,867$) are the well-known **UK Biobank** 24-hour dietary recall GWAS datasets generated by the MRC-IEU (`ukb-b-19390`, `ukb-b-12915`, `ukb-b-16202`, `ukb-b-9596`)! Worse, on line 28 the authors argue that sample overlap was avoided *because* the exposure came from FinnGen and the outcome came from the 28-cohort McCartney et al. meta-analysis—a justification that collapses once it is recognized that the exposure GWAS is actually from UK Biobank.
2. **Uncorrected Multiple Testing Across 16 Exposure–Outcome Pairs and Overstatement of the Marginal `GrimAge` Association ($P = 0.0504$):** The study tests $4 \text{ exposures} \times 4 \text{ outcomes} = 16 \text{ hypotheses}$ with zero Bonferroni ($\alpha = 0.05 / 16 = 0.003125$) or Benjamini–Hochberg FDR adjustment. While the inverse association between dietary vitamin C and `HannumAge` ($\beta = -0.7851, 95\%\text{ CI } [-1.2662, -0.3041], P = 0.0014$) narrowly survives Bonferroni correction ($0.0014 < 0.003125$), the claimed association between vitamin C and `GrimAge` ($\beta = -0.4658, 95\%\text{ CI } [-0.9324, 0.0007], P = 0.0504$) does not even meet the nominal $P < 0.05$ cutoff ($P = 0.0504 > 0.05$), is non-significant across all four complementary MR methods (`Figure 3B`: MR-Egger $P = 0.452$, weighted median $P = 0.145$, simple mode $P = 0.598$, weighted mode $P = 0.619$), and collapses in the leave-one-out sensitivity plot (`Figure 4B`) whenever any of several individual SNPs (e.g., `rs9276435`, `rs76719001`, `rs144428479`) are excluded.
3. **Relaxed Instrument Threshold ($P < 5 \times 10^{-6}$) on Self-Reported 24-Hour Dietary Recall Rather Than Circulating Antioxidant Biomarkers, Plus Unreported Horizontal Pleiotropy in `Table 1`:** Because single-day 24-hour dietary recall questionnaires in UK Biobank (`N = 64,979`) have low heritability and yielded insufficient genome-wide significant SNPs ($P < 5 \times 10^{-8}$), the authors relaxed the instrument threshold 100-fold to $P < 5 \times 10^{-6}$ (line 32), selecting 11–16 SNPs that reflect behavioral dietary recall rather than genetically regulated circulating ascorbate/tocopherol/retinol concentrations (e.g., `SLC23A1` for plasma vitamin C). Furthermore, Section 2.3 (line 41) states that *"When significant horizontal pleiotropy was found, the MR-Egger method was used as the main method in place of IVW"*, yet `Table 1` (line 88) shows statistically significant directional pleiotropy for `Carotene -> IEAA` (MR-Egger intercept $P = 0.0272 < 0.05$) while Section 3.1/3.3 claims no horizontal pleiotropy was observed and continues to report IVW.

### Specific Conclusions and Assessment of Support
* **Claim 1:** Genetically predicted dietary vitamin C intake is negatively associated with `HannumAge` ($\beta = -0.7851, P = 0.0014$; `Results`, p. 5, lines 57–63; `Table 1`; `Figure 3A`). **`[Moderately Supported]`** — The IVW association ($P = 0.0014$) survives Bonferroni correction across 16 tests ($0.0014 < 0.0031$), shows consistent negative direction across all five MR estimators (`Figure 3A`), and has no detected heterogeneity ($P = 0.8925$) or MR-Egger intercept pleiotropy ($P = 0.6494$). However, it relies on 14 relaxed-threshold ($P < 5 \times 10^{-6}$) SNPs from a UK Biobank 24-hour dietary recall phenotype misattributed to FinnGen.
* **Claim 2:** Dietary vitamin C intake has a marginal causal effect on `GrimAge` ($\beta = -0.4658, P = 0.0504$; `Abstract`, line 6; `Results`, p. 5, lines 57–63; `Figure 3B`; `Figure 4B`). **`[Overstated]`** — Fails both multiple-testing correction and the nominal $P < 0.05$ threshold ($P = 0.0504$, $95\%\text{ CI } [-0.9324, +0.0007]$ crosses zero), is non-significant in all four sensitivity estimators, and is driven by single HLA-region/outlier variants (`rs9276435`) in the leave-one-out plot (`Figure 4B`).
* **Claim 3:** Dietary intakes of vitamin A, vitamin E, and carotene have no causal associations with `HannumAge`, `IEAA`, `GrimAge`, or `PhenoAge` (`Results`, p. 5, lines 55–57; `Table 1`; `Figure 2`). **`[Well Supported]`** — Transparently documented across all 12 exposure–outcome pairs in `Table 1` and `Figure 2` ($P = 0.088\text{–}0.890$).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Misattribution of UK Biobank 24-Hour Dietary Recall GWAS Datasets to the "FinnGen Project Database"
* **Severity:** Major
* **Location:** `Section 2.1 Exposure and Outcome Data` (line 28) and `Data Availability Statement` (line 138).
* **WHY:** Accurate GWAS provenance is essential both for reproducibility and for assessing sample overlap between exposure and outcome cohorts.
* **WHAT:** Line 28 states: *"Summary-level data of these 4 dietary antioxidants were got from FinnGen Project Database (`https://r11.finngen.fi/`). The datasets about vitamin C, vitamin E and carotene include 64,979 European participants and 9,851,867 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). The data about vitamin A involves 62,991 European participants and 9,851,867 SNPs. The outcome data were collected from a recent meta-analysis containing 28 cohorts and 34,710 European participants [19]. This ensured that there was no sample overlap between exposure and outcome data."* In reality, the four exposure datasets with $N = 64,979$ and $N = 62,991$ and $9,851,867$ SNPs are the MRC-IEU **UK Biobank** 24-hour recall nutrient intake GWAS (`ukb-b-19390`, `ukb-b-12915`, `ukb-b-16202`, `ukb-b-9596`), not FinnGen R11.
* **HOW:** Correct Section 2.1 and the Data Availability Statement to cite the UK Biobank / MRC-IEU OpenGWAS accession IDs (`ukb-b-19390`, `ukb-b-12915`, `ukb-b-16202`, `ukb-b-9596`), and verify whether any UK Biobank subcohorts were included in the 28-cohort McCartney et al. (2021) epigenetic clock meta-analysis.

### Defect 2: Uncorrected Multiple Testing Across 16 Hypotheses and Overinterpretation of `Vitamin C -> GrimAge` ($P = 0.0504$)
* **Severity:** Major
* **Location:** `Abstract` (line 6), `Section 2.3` (lines 38–42), `Section 3.2` (lines 55–68), `Figure 3B`, and `Figure 4B`.
* **WHY:** Testing 4 exposures against 4 outcomes creates 16 parallel hypothesis tests; treating $P = 0.0504$ as a positive "marginal causal association" without multiple-testing correction inflates Type I error.
* **WHAT:** For `Vitamin C -> GrimAge`, the IVW confidence interval includes zero ($95\%\text{ CI } -0.9324\text{ to }+0.0007, P = 0.0504$), the Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across the 16 tests in `Table 1` is $q = 0.0504 \times 16 / 2 = \mathbf{0.403}$ (far above $0.05$), and `Figure 4B` demonstrates that omitting single SNPs such as `rs9276435` (located in the highly pleiotropic MHC class II region on chromosome 6!) shifts the overall estimate toward the null. Moreover, in Section 3.3 (line 103), the authors claim *"Moreover, our leave-one-out test revealed no potential outliers for vitamin C (Figure 4)"*, which fails to acknowledge that the $95\%$ CI in `Figure 4B` crosses zero whenever almost any single SNP is dropped, and that `rs9276435` is an HLA-region variant (`HLA-DQB1/HLA-DQA1`) likely reflecting immune confounding rather than vitamin C metabolism.
* **HOW:** Apply Bonferroni ($\alpha = 0.0031$) or Benjamini–Hochberg FDR ($q < 0.05$) across all 16 tests in `Table 1`, reclassify `Vitamin C -> GrimAge` ($P = 0.0504, q = 0.403$) as null/non-significant, and perform a sensitivity analysis excluding MHC variant `rs9276435`.

### Defect 3: Behavioral Dietary Recall Instruments ($P < 5 \times 10^{-6}$) vs. Circulating Plasma Biomarker Instruments, and Unreported Pleiotropy for `Carotene -> IEAA`
* **Severity:** Minor to Moderate
* **Location:** `Section 2.2` (line 32), `Section 2.3` (line 41), `Section 3.1/3.3` (lines 51, 88, 103), and `Table 1`.
* **WHY:** SNPs selected at $P < 5 \times 10^{-6}$ for self-reported 24-hour dietary intake capture dietary preferences and socioeconomic confounding rather than physiological vitamin absorption/transport (`SLC23A1` for vitamin C, `CYP2R1/GC` for vitamin D, `SCARB1/APOA5` for vitamin E/carotenoids). Additionally, pre-specified methodological rules must be followed consistently.
* **WHAT:**
  1. None of the 14 SNPs in `Figure 4A/B` include the canonical functional vitamin C transporter variant (`rs33972313` in `SLC23A1`); instead, they are sub-threshold behavioral recall variants ($P < 5 \times 10^{-6}$).
  2. In `Table 1` (line 88), `Carotene -> IEAA` has an MR-Egger intercept $P = \mathbf{0.0272} < 0.05$, indicating significant directional pleiotropy, yet Section 3.1 (line 51) and Section 3.3 (line 103) state that no horizontal pleiotropy was detected, violating the rule stated in Section 2.3 (line 41) to switch to MR-Egger when pleiotropy is significant.
* **HOW:** Validate the `Vitamin C -> HannumAge` finding using genetic instruments for **circulating plasma ascorbate levels** (e.g., Zheng et al. / Fenland & EPIC-InterAct GWAS, $N \approx 52,018$, anchored by `SLC23A1`), and accurately report the significant MR-Egger intercept ($P = 0.0272$) for `Carotene -> IEAA` in Section 3.3.
