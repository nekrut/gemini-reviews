## 1. Blinded Metadata Perception
* **Perceived Field & Subfield:** Human Neuroimaging / Resting-State fMRI of Somatosensory & Neuromodulatory Stimulation (2 Hz Rhythmic Muscle Stimulation vs. 100 Hz Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation)
* **Perceived Study Design:** Within-subject crossover resting-state fMRI pilot study in $N = 8$ healthy young adults (5 females, 3 males; ages 18–26), acquiring three 6-minute rsfMRI scans before and after 45 minutes of 2 Hz right hand muscle stimulation vs. 100 Hz skin TENS across two visits separated by 2 weeks, analyzed in CONN v22.a.
* **Tone & Structural Assessment:** Exploratory small-sample ($N = 8$) human rsfMRI paper containing multiple disqualifying methodological, coordinate-space, and statistical defects: (1) doubling of the degrees of freedom (`T(14)` instead of `T(7)`) in the between-condition comparison of a within-subject $N = 8$ crossover study; (2) systematic anterior–posterior ($+y \leftrightarrow -y$) sign inversion across nearly all MNI coordinates in Table 1, placing the manually generated spherical ROIs in the wrong brain structures; (3) concatenation of three discontinuous 6-minute raw EPI runs via `3dTcat` *prior* to slice-timing, motion realignment, and bandpass filtering; (4) severe text–table contradictions where ROIs highlighted in the Abstract, Results, and Discussion are completely absent from Tables 2A and 2C; and (5) use of an uncorrected $p < 0.05$ voxel-level cluster-forming threshold in the seed-to-voxel analysis.

## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study investigates resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) changes in $N = 8$ healthy young adults before and after 45 minutes of 2 Hz electrical hand muscle stimulation compared with 100 Hz skin TENS (baseline control) in a counterbalanced two-visit crossover design. Using 21 selected cortical, subcortical, and brainstem regions of interest (ROIs) in CONN v22.a, the authors report post-stimulation connectivity increases following 2 Hz muscle stimulation (and differential effects against 100 Hz skin stimulation) involving the insula, putamen, caudate, thalamus, anterior cingulate, hypothalamus, amygdala, hippocampus, and periaqueductal gray (PAG), alongside seed-to-voxel hypothalamus connectivity changes.

Forensic audit reveals five foundational methodological and mathematical defects that invalidate the statistical inferences and anatomical localization of the study:
1. **Pseudoreplicated Degrees of Freedom (`T(14)` Instead of `T(7)`) in the $2 \times 2$ Within-Subject Crossover Comparison (Tables 2C and 4C):** Only **$N = 8$ participants** were enrolled, and each completed both visits (2 Hz and 100 Hz) in a within-subject crossover design (lines 26–33). While the single-condition post-vs.-pre contrasts (Tables 2A, 2B, 4A, 4B) correctly use paired degrees of freedom $\text{df} = n - 1 = 7$ (`T(7)`), the between-condition differential contrasts in **Table 2C** (lines 137–146) and **Table 4C** (lines 218–233) report every test statistic with **`T(14)`** ($\text{df} = 2n - 2 = 16 - 2 = 14$). Treating the $8$ paired subject sessions as $16$ independent participants in a two-sample $t$-test violates within-subject error partitioning and artificially doubles the degrees of freedom.
2. **Systematic Anterior–Posterior ($+y \leftrightarrow -y$) Coordinate Inversion in Table 1 Placing Manually Created Spherical ROIs in the Wrong Anatomy:** Table 1 (lines 53–77) is titled *"Talairach atlas coordinates (MNI152 atlas) for the 21 selected ROIs"*, conflating Talairach and MNI152 coordinate spaces. Worse, inspection of the $y$-coordinates in Table 1 reveals that **the anterior–posterior ($y$) sign is inverted across nearly the entire table**:
   - `Accumbens` is listed at $y = -11.5 / -12.2$ (posterior to the AC), whereas the human nucleus accumbens in MNI152 space is at $y \approx +10\text{ to }+12\text{ mm}$!
   - `Cingulate, Anterior` is listed at $y = -18.3$, whereas `Cingulate, Posterior` is listed at $y = +36.6$!
   - `Hippocampus` is listed at $y = +23.2 / +21.0$, whereas the hippocampus in MNI152 space is at $y \approx -21\text{ to }-23\text{ mm}$!
   - Crucially, the authors states (lines 48–50) that for 5 custom brainstem/diencephalic regions (`Hypothalamus`, `PAG`, `Raphe Magnus`, `Lateral Medulla`, and `Subthalamic Nucleus`), they **manually created $4\text{–}6\text{ mm}$ spherical masks in FSLeyes at the coordinates in Table 1**. Because `Medulla, lateral` was generated at $(\pm 4.0, \mathbf{+43.7}, -54.4)$ instead of $y = -43.7$, that sphere lies $87\text{ mm}$ anterior to the medulla—outside the brain inside the submandibular/jaw region! Similarly, `Periaqueductal gray` at $(0.9, \mathbf{+29.2}, -10.2)$ lies $58\text{ mm}$ anterior to the midbrain aqueduct (in the orbitofrontal/subcallosal space instead of $y \approx -29.2$)!
3. **Concatenation of Three Discontinuous 6-Minute Raw EPI Runs Prior to Preprocessing (`3dTcat`):** Line 78 states that the three separate 6-minute functional runs were concatenated into a single 18-minute NIFTI file using `3dTcat` *before* uploading the raw data into CONN for slice-timing correction, motion realignment, cubic detrending, and bandpass filtering ($0.008\text{–}0.09\text{ Hz}$). Concatenating raw discontinuous runs prior to preprocessing introduces step-function discontinuities across run boundaries that corrupt temporal interpolation (slice-timing and $0.008\text{–}0.09\text{ Hz}$ bandpass filtering) and invalidates the subsequent modeling of "3 sessions" (lines 89–92) on an already-concatenated single run.
4. **Major Text–Table Contradictions and Truncated Tables (Tables 2A, 2C, and 4C):**
   - The Abstract (line 5), Results (line 106), and Discussion (line 239) claim that the differential 2 Hz vs. 100 Hz analysis (Table 2C) revealed significant involvement of the **left and right hypothalamus, right amygdala, right hippocampus, and PAG**. Yet **Table 2C** (lines 135–148) contains **none** of those five regions—it lists only Caudate, Thalamus, Putamen, Insula, and Anterior Cingulate, and **abruptly cuts off** after the header `Cluster 2 (F(2,13) = 9.01, p-unc = 0.003507, p-FDR = 0.007014)` on line 148 with zero connections listed under Cluster 2!
   - Similarly, Discussion line 243 claims: *"In our rsfMRI analysis, right insula–left putamen connectivity was enhanced following rhythmic (2 Hz) muscle stimulation"*, yet `Right Insula – Left Putamen` is completely absent from Table 2A (lines 114–129)!
5. **Use of an Invalid `p < 0.05` Uncorrected Cluster-Forming Threshold in Seed-to-Voxel Analysis (Figure 3, Table 3):** In lines 152 and 160, the hypothalamus seed-to-voxel maps were thresholded at a primary voxel threshold of **`p < 0.05 uncorrected`** with cluster-size $p\text{-FDR} < 0.05$. Under Gaussian Random Field theory, a cluster-forming threshold of $p < 0.05$ violates high-threshold assumptions so severely that false-positive rates exceed $50\%\text{–}80\%$ (Woo et al., 2014; Eklund et al., 2016); indeed, Table 3 (lines 171–204) shows that the peak-level FWE-corrected $p$-value (`p-FWE` column) is **`1.000000`** for 10 out of the 11 reported clusters!

### Claim-by-Claim Audit Summary
* **Claim 1:** *"A differential contrast of 2 Hz sensory-driven rhythmic stimulation compared to 100 Hz control baseline revealed localized connectivity increases within three functional clusters involving the bilateral caudate, bilateral thalamus, bilateral putamen, bilateral insula, bilateral hypothalamus, anterior cingulate, right hippocampus, right amygdala, and PAG."* — **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** (First, Table 2C erroneously uses two-sample degrees of freedom `T(14)` instead of paired `T(7)` for $N = 8$ subjects. Second, the hypothalamus, hippocampus, amygdala, and PAG are completely absent from Table 2C, which is truncated after the `Cluster 2` header. Third, the manually created spherical mask for the PAG in Table 1 was placed at $y = +29.2$ instead of $y = -29.2$ due to an anterior–posterior sign inversion.)
* **Claim 2:** *"Right insula–left putamen connectivity was enhanced following rhythmic (2 Hz) muscle stimulation, suggesting increased integration between visceral sensory inputs and motor control frameworks."* — **`[Overstated]`** (Directly contradicted by Table 2A [lines 114–129], where `Insular cortex, Right` connects only to `Thalamus, Right`, `Insular cortex, Left`, `Caudate, Left`, and `Putamen, Right`—not `Putamen, Left`.)
* **Claim 3:** *"Seed-to-voxel analysis of the left and right hypothalamus revealed significant functional connectivity increases with the right medial frontal gyrus, right postcentral gyrus, cerebellar tonsil, and culmen after 2 Hz rhythmic muscle stimulation."* — **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** (Thresholded at an invalid primary voxel threshold of $p < 0.05$ uncorrected in $N = 8$ subjects, where 10 of 11 clusters have peak $p\text{-FWE} = 1.000000$ in Table 3.)

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1. Systematic Anterior–Posterior ($+y \leftrightarrow -y$) Coordinate Sign Inversion in Table 1 Placing Manual Brainstem/Midbrain Spheres Outside Target Anatomy (Pillar 3 & Pillar 5 — Critical)
* **Location:** Section "Selection of ROIs" (lines 48–51) and Table 1 (lines 53–77).
* **The Defect:** In standard MNI152 coordinates (origin at the anterior commissure), $+y$ is anterior and $-y$ is posterior. In Table 1 (`Talairach atlas coordinates (MNI152 atlas) for the 21 selected ROIs`), the $y$-coordinate sign is reversed across virtually every structure:
  - `Accumbens, Left/Right`: $(-9.5, \mathbf{-11.5}, -7.1)$ and $(9.2, \mathbf{-12.2}, -6.9)$ — true MNI152 $y \approx +11.5\text{ to }+12.2$.
  - `Cingulate, Anterior`: $(0.8, \mathbf{-18.3}, 24.8)$ vs. `Cingulate, Posterior`: $(0.9, \mathbf{+36.6}, 30.0)$ — Anterior Cingulate is placed posterior to the AC and Posterior Cingulate is placed $36.6\text{ mm}$ anterior!
  - `Hippocampus, Left/Right`: $(-25.3, \mathbf{+23.2}, -13.9)$ and $(26.8, \mathbf{+21.0}, -14.1)$ — true MNI152 $y \approx -21\text{ to }-23$.
  - `Periaqueductal gray (PAG)`: $(0.9, \mathbf{+29.2}, -10.2)$ — true midbrain PAG is at $y \approx -29.2, z \approx -10.2$.
  - `Raphe magnus`: $(0.8, \mathbf{+26.6}, -39.5)$ — true pontomedullary raphe magnus is at $y \approx -26.6, z \approx -39.5$.
  - `Medulla, lateral, Left/Right`: $(-4.0, \mathbf{+43.7}, -54.4)$ and $(4.0, \mathbf{+43.7}, -54.4)$ — true lateral medulla is at $y \approx -43.7, z \approx -54.4$.
* **Quantitative Impact:** While the 16 pre-defined Harvard-Oxford / AAL masks inside CONN use internal atlas masks (making Table 1's inverted $y$-coordinates a severe reporting error for those 16 ROIs), lines 48–50 explicitly state that for the **5 manually generated spherical ROIs** (`Hypothalamus`, `Periaqueductal gray`, `Raphe magnus`, `Lateral medulla`, and `Subthalamic nucleus`), *"spherical boundaries inside the brain region were manually created in FSLeyes using MNI coordinates derived from the neurosynth platform... and adjusted along constraints of standard atlases (see Table 1 for details)."* If the manual spheres for PAG ($y = +29.2, z = -10.2$), Raphe Magnus ($y = +26.6, z = -39.5$), and Lateral Medulla ($y = +43.7, z = -54.4$) were created at the Table 1 coordinates, they were placed $53\text{–}87\text{ mm}$ anterior to the brainstem (in the subcallosal, pharyngeal, and submandibular spaces, respectively), explaining why Lateral Medulla and Raphe Magnus yielded zero connections in every single analysis!

### 3.2. Pseudoreplicated Degrees of Freedom (`T(14)` Instead of `T(7)`) in the Between-Condition Crossover Contrasts (Pillar 2 & Pillar 3 — Critical)
* **Location:** Section "Participants" (line 26), Section "Experimental design" (lines 31–33), Table 2C (lines 137–146), and Table 4C (lines 218–233).
* **The Defect:** The study includes $N = 8$ participants who each underwent both the 2 Hz muscle stimulation visit and the 100 Hz skin TENS visit in a counterbalanced within-subject crossover design. In Tables 2A, 2B, 4A, and 4B (single-visit After vs. Before contrasts), the paired $t$-tests have $\text{df} = n - 1 = 7$ (`T(7)`). However, when comparing the 2 Hz change against the 100 Hz change in **Table 2C** and **Table 4C**, the test statistics are reported as **`T(14)`** (and `F(2, 13)` for the omnibus cluster test in line 136).
* **Quantitative Impact:** Degrees of freedom $\text{df} = 14 = 2(8) - 2$ correspond to an **independent two-sample $t$-test** treating the 8 subjects in the 2 Hz condition and the same 8 subjects in the 100 Hz condition as $16$ independent individuals! In a within-subject $2 \times 2$ design with $N = 8$, the Condition $\times$ Time interaction contrast (`[2Hz_After - 2Hz_Before] - [100Hz_After - 100Hz_Before]`) is a single-sample paired $t$-test across the 8 subjects with $\text{df} = 7$, not $\text{df} = 14$.

### 3.3. Raw Concatenation of Discontinuous EPI Runs via `3dTcat` Prior to Preprocessing and Bandpass Filtering (Pillar 3 — Major)
* **Location:** Section "Preprocessing" (lines 78–83) and Section "Processing" (lines 89–92).
* **The Defect:** Line 78 states: *"The three functional runs were concatenated into one long timeseries file (NIFTI; *.nii format) using AFNI software plugin 3dTcat (Cox, 1996). All raw data were then uploaded into the CONN toolbox for subsequent analyses."*
* **Quantitative Impact:** Concatenating three separate 6-minute EPI runs into one continuous 18-minute file *before* preprocessing violates signal continuity at the two inter-run boundaries during slice-timing correction, rigid-body motion realignment, cubic detrending across the full 18 minutes, and infinite-impulse-response/FFT temporal bandpass filtering ($0.008\text{–}0.09\text{ Hz}$). Furthermore, lines 89–92 state that in CONN, *"To account for simple main baseline effects of each run (sessions 1, 2, and 3) on the BOLD signal, their boundaries were modeled by three continuum conditions (`rest`, `session1`, `session2`, and `session3`) using rectangular (boxcar) functions"*—which means the authors uploaded each 18-minute concatenated file as a single session or mis-specified session boundaries after already smearing the run discontinuities through the $0.008\text{–}0.09\text{ Hz}$ filter.

### 3.4. Truncated Tables and Direct Contradictions Between the Text and Tables 2A / 2C (Pillar 4 & Pillar 5 — Major)
* **Location:** Abstract (line 5), Results (lines 97, 106), Table 2A (lines 114–129), Table 2C (lines 135–148), Table 4C (lines 218–233), and Discussion (lines 239, 243).
* **The Defect:**
  1. **Missing ROIs & Truncated Table 2C:** Results line 106 states: *"This differential analysis for the 2 Hz rhythmic muscle activation condition additionally revealed significant involvement of the left and right hypothalamus ROIs, right amygdala, right hippocampus, and PAG."* Yet **Table 2C** (lines 135–148) ends abruptly at line 148 (`Cluster 2 (F(2,13) = 9.01, p-unc = 0.003507, p-FDR = 0.007014)`) with the entire contents of Cluster 2 and Cluster 3 omitted from the published table! The identical truncation occurs in **Table 4C** (line 233), which ends immediately after `Cluster 2 (F(2,13) = 13.71...)` with no rows underneath.
  2. **Non-Existent Connection Claimed in Discussion (Line 243):** Discussion line 243 states: *"In our rsfMRI analysis, right insula–left putamen connectivity was enhanced following rhythmic (2 Hz) muscle stimulation"*. In Table 2A (lines 114–129), `Insular cortex, Right` – `Putamen, Left` does not exist (nor does it exist in Table 4A, where `Insular cortex, Right – Putamen, Left` has $T(7) = 0.65, p\text{-unc} = 0.535, p\text{-FDR} = 0.672$, line 209!).

### 3.5. Invalid `p < 0.05` Uncorrected Voxel Threshold in Seed-to-Voxel Analysis (Pillar 2 — Major)
* **Location:** Section "Examination of the hypothalamus through seed-to-voxel analyses" (lines 152, 160), Figure 3, and Table 3 (lines 166–204).
* **The Defect:** Because no clusters survived standard cluster-forming thresholds in this $N = 8$ sample, the authors lowered the voxel-level cluster-forming threshold in the hypothalamus seed-to-voxel analysis to **`p < 0.05 uncorrected`** (`p < 0.05, uncorrected; cluster size p < 0.05, FDR corrected`, lines 152 and 160). At $p < 0.05$ uncorrected with $8\text{ mm}$ smoothing, spatially contiguous noise clusters routinely exceed parametric GRF extent expectations (Woo et al., 2014; Eklund et al., 2016). In Table 3, 10 of the 11 clusters have peak-level `p-FWE = 1.000000`.

## 4. Quantitative Rubric Scoring

* **Rigor (0.6 / 3.5):** Disqualifying statistical and methodological errors: doubling degrees of freedom from `T(7)` to `T(14)` in a within-subject $N = 8$ crossover contrast; anterior–posterior ($+y \leftrightarrow -y$) coordinate inversion across Table 1 placing manual brainstem/midbrain spherical ROIs outside target anatomy; concatenating discontinuous raw EPI runs via `3dTcat` prior to preprocessing; and using a $p < 0.05$ uncorrected primary cluster-forming threshold.
* **Claims (0.7 / 2.5):** Major text–table contradictions (claiming right insula–left putamen enhancement in Discussion when $p\text{-FDR} = 0.672$, and claiming hypothalamus, amygdala, hippocampus, and PAG effects from a truncated, pseudoreplicated Table 2C).
* **Biology (0.8 / 2.0):** The physiological question—comparing low-frequency (2 Hz) ergoreceptive muscle stimulation against high-frequency (100 Hz) cutaneous TENS in a crossover design—is well-motivated by acupuncture and exercise pressor reflex literature, but the execution in $N = 8$ healthy subjects with inverted MNI coordinates compromises biological validity.
* **Reproducibility (0.8 / 2.0):** Tables 2C and 4C are physically truncated mid-table (`Cluster 2` header with zero rows below); Table 1's $y$-coordinates have inverted signs while conflating Talairach and MNI152 labels; raw data are not publicly deposited (`available on reasonable request`, line 284).
