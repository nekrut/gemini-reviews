## 1. Blinded Metadata Perception
* **Perceived Field & Subfield:** Movement Disorders Neuroimaging / Parkinson's Disease Freezing of Gait (Multi-Band Resting-State fMRI Regional Fluctuation Metrics and Wearable Gait Kinematics)
* **Perceived Study Design:** Cross-sectional clinical neuroimaging and wearable gait sensor study comparing Parkinson's disease patients with freezing of gait (PDFoG, $n = 39$), Parkinson's disease patients without freezing of gait (PDnFoG, $n = 40$), and healthy controls (HC, $n = 44$) across four regional low-frequency fluctuation metrics (`ALFF`, `fALFF`, `PerAF`, and `wavelet-ALFF`) and three frequency bands (`conventional`, `slow-5`, and `slow-4`), alongside wearable inertial sensor gait testing (TUG, Narrow Path Walking, Turning, and Freeze Index).
* **Tone & Structural Assessment:** Comprehensive and transparently written clinical neuroimaging manuscript (notably reporting null Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-corrected brain–gait correlations honestly and performing smoothing-kernel and motion-covariate sensitivity checks), but compromised by four major statistical and data-hygiene flaws: (1) use of an invalid `voxel-level p < 0.05` cluster-forming threshold under Gaussian Random Field (GRF) theory across 36 unadjusted whole-brain statistical maps; (2) inclusion of `Duration of illness` (which is identically zero/undefined in Healthy Controls and averages $8.51\text{ years}$ in PDFoG vs. $3.11\text{ years}$ in PDnFoG) as a linear ANCOVA covariate in pairwise **Patient vs. Healthy Control** $t$-tests, creating an extrapolation artifact that erased true basal ganglia reductions in PDFoG; (3) an uncleaned outlier/typo in Table 1 (`TUG: Gait speed` in PDnFoG = $1.84 \pm 6.27\text{ m/s}$) that inflated the group standard deviation 25-fold and masked a highly significant TUG gait speed deficit; and (4) text–table swaps in Sections 3.2.1 and 3.2.4.

## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study evaluates spontaneous regional brain activity in Parkinson's disease patients with freezing of gait (PDFoG, $n = 39$), PD patients without freezing of gait (PDnFoG, $n = 40$), and healthy controls (HC, $n = 44$) during the dopaminergic OFF state ($>12\text{ h}$ withdrawal) using four rs-fMRI metrics (`ALFF`, `fALFF`, `PerAF`, `wavelet-ALFF`) across three frequency bands (`conventional`: $0.01\text{–}0.08\text{ Hz}$; `slow-5`: $0.01\text{–}0.027\text{ Hz}$; `slow-4`: $0.027\text{–}0.073\text{ Hz}$), paired with wearable inertial gait kinematics and Freeze Index (FI) measurements. The authors conclude that PDFoG is characterized by widespread cortical and cerebellar hyperactivity (most consistently in the bilateral postcentral gyri, visual cortex, and cerebellar lobule VI) without basal ganglia reductions, whereas PDnFoG is characterized by reduced basal ganglia activity (putamen, caudate, pallidum), and report no significant FDR-corrected correlations between regional fMRI metrics and quantitative gait parameters.

While the study has clear strengths—including scanning and performing wearable gait assessments in the L-dopa OFF state, applying Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction ($q = 0.05$) to the brain–behavior correlations and transparently reporting the null result (Section 3.3, line 338), and conducting validation analyses with $4\text{ mm}$ and $8\text{ mm}$ smoothing kernels and mean Framewise Displacement (Section 3.4)—forensic audit uncovers four critical methodological and statistical errors:
1. **Extrapolation Artifact from Including `Duration of Illness` as a Covariate in Pairwise Comparisons Against Healthy Controls (`PDFoG vs HC` and `PDnFoG vs HC`):** In Section 2.5 (line 136), the authors state that all pairwise group comparisons (`PDFoG vs PDnFoG`, `PDFoG vs HC`, and `PDnFoG vs HC`) were conducted using two-sample $t$-tests with **`Duration of illness` included as a covariate**. However, Healthy Controls (`HC`) do not have Parkinson's disease (`Duration of illness = NA / 0` in Table 1, line 107), whereas `PDFoG` has a mean duration of $8.51 \pm 4.20\text{ years}$ and `PDnFoG` has a mean duration of $3.11 \pm 2.51\text{ years}$. Including a disease-only variable as a covariate in a **Patient vs. HC** linear model ($Y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{Group} + \beta_2 \text{Duration} + \varepsilon$, where $\text{Duration} \equiv 0$ for all HCs) induces extreme multicollinearity ($R^2 \approx 0.81$ between Group and Duration in `PDFoG vs HC`) and forces $\beta_1$ to estimate the difference between HCs and a hypothetical PDFoG patient at **$\text{Duration} = 0\text{ years}$** (extrapolating $8.51\text{ years}$ below thePDFoG sample mean!). Crucially, the authors admit in Discussion line 362 that **significant putamen reductions in PDFoG vs. HC were present before regressing out disease duration**, and vanished only when `Duration of illness` was entered as a covariate! Thus, a central biological claim of the paper—that PDFoG lacks basal ganglia hypoactivity while PDnFoG exhibits reduced basal ganglia activity—is a statistical artifact of improper covariate extrapolation against healthy controls.
2. **Invalid `voxel-level p < 0.05` Cluster-Forming Threshold Across 36 Unadjusted Whole-Brain Tests:** In Section 2.5 (line 136), Gaussian Random Field (GRF) correction was applied using a primary cluster-forming threshold of **`voxel-level p < 0.05, cluster-level p < 0.05, two-tailed`**. Parametric GRF cluster-extent inference is mathematically invalid at a primary threshold of $p < 0.05$ because the Euler characteristic approximation requires high thresholds ($p < 0.001$), resulting in empirical family-wise error rates of $50\%\text{–}70\%$ per map (Woo et al., 2014; Eklund et al., 2016; Chen et al., 2018). Compounding this, the authors bypassed a 3-group omnibus ANCOVA and executed **36 separate whole-brain pairwise $t$-tests** ($4\text{ metrics} \times 3\text{ bands} \times 3\text{ pairs}$) with no multiple-comparison adjustment across pairs, bands, or metrics.
3. **Uncleaned Data Entry Error in Table 1 (`TUG: Gait speed = 1.84 ± 6.27 m/s` in PDnFoG) Masking a True Group Difference:** In Table 1 (line 126), `TUG: Gait speed (m/s)` is $0.65 \pm 0.29\text{ m/s}$ in `PDFoG` ($n = 39$), but is reported as **`1.84 ± 6.27 m/s`** in `PDnFoG` ($n = 40$), leading to a non-significant Welch's $t$-test ($t = 1.20, \text{df} = 39.17, p = 0.236$) and the claim in Results line 217 that *"there was no significant difference in gait speed (m/s)... between the two groups"* during TUG. Physiologically and mathematically, a standard deviation of $6.27\text{ m/s}$ ($22.6\text{ km/h}$!) in Parkinson's patients walking a 5-meter TUG is impossible, and is contradicted by the very same table's `TUG: Stride length` ($95.23 \pm 25.17\text{ cm}$, line 128) and `TUG: Gait cycle` ($1.15 \pm 0.14\text{ s}$, line 129), which deterministically imply a true PDnFoG TUG gait speed of $\text{Stride Length} / \text{Gait Cycle} = 0.9523\text{ m} / 1.15\text{ s} \approx \mathbf{0.83 \pm 0.25\text{ m/s}}$ (matching their Narrow Path Walking gait speed of $0.85 \pm 0.25\text{ m/s}$ in line 132, where $t = 3.33, p = 0.001$!). A single decimal-shift outlier of $\approx 40\text{ m/s}$ among the $n = 40$ PDnFoG subjects shifted the sample mean by $+40/40 = +1.00\text{ m/s}$ (from $0.84$ to $1.84\text{ m/s}$) and inflated the sample SD to $\approx 40/\sqrt{40} = 6.32\text{ m/s}$.
4. **Severe Clinical Confounding Between `PDFoG` and `PDnFoG` and Text–Table Swaps in Results:** `PDFoG` patients had nearly triple the disease duration ($8.51 \pm 4.20$ vs. $3.11 \pm 2.51\text{ years}, t = 6.91, p < 0.001$), nearly double the daily levodopa dose (`LEDD`: $686.18$ vs. $375.98\text{ mg}, p < 0.001$), and substantially worse overall motor severity (`UPDRS III`: $40.0$ vs. $28.4, p < 0.001$; `H-Y stage`: $3.0$ vs. $2.25, p < 0.001$). Neither `LEDD` nor `UPDRS III` was controlled for in the `PDFoG vs PDnFoG` neuroimaging contrast. Additionally, the text in Section 3.2.1 (line 232) swaps the `slow-5` and `slow-4` basal ganglia and cortical findings from Table 2, and Section 3.2.4 (lines 331–333) mislabels `PDFoG vs HC` and `PDnFoG vs HC` clusters from Table 5.

### Claim-by-Claim Audit Summary
* **Claim 1:** *"PD patients with FoG experienced more extensive changes in regional brain activity than those without FoG, primarily affecting cortical regions [bilateral postcentral gyri and visual cortex] and the cerebellum [lobule VI]."* — **`[Fragile]`** (While bilateral postcentral gyrus increases appear consistently across metrics and smoothing kernels, all whole-brain maps rely on an invalid `voxel p < 0.05` GRF primary threshold across 36 unadjusted tests, and `PDFoG` vs. `PDnFoG` is heavily confounded by a $5.4\text{-year}$ difference in disease duration, a $310\text{ mg/day}$ difference in LEDD, and an $11.6\text{-point}$ difference in UPDRS-III motor severity.)
* **Claim 2:** *"Conversely, PDnFoG patients primarily showed reduced activity in the basal ganglia [whereas PDFoG patients showed an absence of basal ganglia impairment relative to HCs]."* — **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** (Directly caused by the statistical error of regressing out `Duration of illness` in pairwise `Patient vs HC` comparisons where all HCs have `Duration = 0`, which extrapolated the `PDFoG` group [$8.51\text{ yr}$] three times further than `PDnFoG` [$3.11\text{ yr}$]; as acknowledged in Discussion line 362, PDFoG patients did show significant putamen reductions relative to HCs prior to this invalid covariate adjustment.)
* **Claim 3:** *"No significant correlation was found between FoG-related regional activity amplitudes and sensor-based gait parameters, FI_max or FI_avg in PDFoG patients."* — **`[Well Supported]`** (Appropriately tested using Spearman rank correlation with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction at $q = 0.05$ and transparently reported as null without p-hacking.)

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1. Extrapolation Artifact from Including `Duration of Illness` as a Covariate in `Patient vs. Healthy Control` Contrasts (Pillar 2 & Pillar 3 — Critical)
* **Location:** Section 2.5 (line 136), Table 1 (line 107), and Discussion (line 362).
* **The Defect:** Line 136 states that pairwise independent-samples $t$-tests were run for `PDFoG vs PDnFoG`, `PDFoG vs HC`, and `PDnFoG vs HC` with **`Duration of illness` included as a covariate**. In Healthy Controls (`HC`), `Duration of illness` is structurally zero (`NA` in Table 1). In `PDFoG`, `Duration of illness` is $8.51 \pm 4.20\text{ years}$, whereas in `PDnFoG` it is $3.11 \pm 2.51\text{ years}$.
* **Quantitative Impact:** In a two-group ANCOVA comparing patients ($G = 1$) to healthy controls ($G = 0$, where $D_i \equiv 0$ for all $G_i = 0$), the model
  $$Y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 G_i + \beta_2 D_i + \varepsilon_i$$
  estimates the slope $\beta_2$ exclusively from variance within the patient group, and evaluates the group effect $\beta_1 = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid G=1, D=0] - \mathbb{E}[Y \mid G=0, D=0]$ at **$D = 0\text{ years}$**! For `PDFoG vs HC`, evaluating $\beta_1$ at $D = 0$ extrapolates $8.51\text{ years}$ ($2.03$ standard deviations) outside the observed PDFoG duration distribution, inflating the variance of $\hat{\beta}_1$ by the variance inflation factor $\text{VIF} = 1 / (1 - R^2_{G,D}) \approx 1 / (1 - 0.808) = \mathbf{5.21}$! In Discussion line 362, the authors explicitly note that *"significant differences in putamen activity between PDFoG patients and HCs were present when disease duration was not regressed out as a covariate"*. Thus, the disappearance of basal ganglia hypoactivity in `PDFoG vs HC` is a textbook covariate-extrapolation artifact.

### 3.2. Invalid `voxel-level p < 0.05` Cluster-Forming GRF Threshold Across 36 Uncorrected Whole-Brain Models (Pillar 2 — Critical)
* **Location:** Section 2.5 ("Statistical analysis", line 136) and Tables 2–5.
* **The Defect:** All whole-brain maps were thresholded using Gaussian Random Field (GRF) theory at **`voxel-level p < 0.05, cluster-level p < 0.05, two-tailed`** (corresponding to $|t| > 1.99$).
* **Quantitative Impact:** At a primary cluster-forming threshold of $p < 0.05$ ($|t| > 1.99$), random Gaussian noise fields form large spatially contiguous clusters that violate the high-threshold asymptotic assumptions of GRF theory, producing false-positive rates exceeding $50\%\text{–}70\%$ per test (Woo et al., 2014; Eklund et al., 2016; Chen et al., 2018). Moreover, performing 3 pairwise $t$-tests without a prior 3-group omnibus $F$-test across 4 metrics and 3 frequency bands ($36$ whole-brain maps) further compounds family-wise error.

### 3.3. Uncleaned Data Entry Outlier in Table 1 (`TUG: Gait speed = 1.84 ± 6.27 m/s`) Corrupting the Parametric Group Comparison (Pillar 4 & Pillar 5 — Major)
* **Location:** Table 1 (line 126) and Section 3.1 (line 217).
* **The Defect:** In Table 1 (line 126), `TUG: Gait speed (m/s)` is reported as $0.65 \pm 0.29\text{ m/s}$ for `PDFoG` ($n = 39$) and **`1.84 ± 6.27 m/s`** for `PDnFoG` ($n = 40$), with Welch's $t = 1.20, \text{df} = 39.17, p = 0.236$.
* **Quantitative Verification:**
  1. In the exact same TUG trial (Table 1, lines 125–129), `PDnFoG` patients had `Step length` $= 47.90 \pm 12.59\text{ cm}$, `Stride length` $= 95.23 \pm 25.17\text{ cm}$, `Cadence` $= 103.23 \pm 23.69\text{ steps/min}$, and `Gait cycle` $= 1.15 \pm 0.14\text{ s}$. By basic kinematics ($v = \text{Stride length} / \text{Gait cycle}$), the true TUG gait speed of `PDnFoG` is $0.9523\text{ m} / 1.15\text{ s} = \mathbf{0.83\text{ m/s}}$ (with $\text{SD} \approx 0.25\text{ m/s}$), which is virtually identical to their Narrow Path Walking (`NPW`) gait speed of **$0.85 \pm 0.25\text{ m/s}$** in line 132!
  2. Notice that the reported Welch degrees of freedom in line 126 are **`df = 39.17`**—practically equal to $n_{\text{PDnFoG}} - 1 = 39.00$ because $\frac{s_1^2/n_1}{s_2^2/n_2} = \frac{0.29^2/39}{6.27^2/40} = 0.0022$, meaning $99.8\%$ of the variance came from `PDnFoG`! A single decimal-point typo in one of the $40$ PDnFoG subjects (e.g., an entry of $\approx 40.5\text{ m/s}$ instead of $0.405\text{ m/s}$, or $84\text{ cm/s}$ entered without dividing by $100$) increases the mean of $n = 40$ values from $0.83\text{ m/s}$ by $+40/40 = +1.00\text{ m/s}$ to $1.83\text{ m/s}$ and produces a sample SD of $\sqrt{40^2 / 39} = \mathbf{6.40\text{ m/s}}$! Because the authors applied a parametric Welch's $t$-test without inspecting the raw distribution, they falsely concluded that TUG gait speed does not differ between PDFoG and PDnFoG ($p = 0.236$), when the true difference ($0.65 \pm 0.29$ vs. $\approx 0.83 \pm 0.25\text{ m/s}$) is highly significant ($t_{77} \approx 2.96, p \approx 0.004$).

### 3.4. Text–Table Contradictions in Sections 3.2.1 and 3.2.4 (Pillar 5 — Minor/Moderate)
* **Location:** Section 3.2.1 (line 232) vs. Table 2 (lines 150–167), and Section 3.2.4 (lines 331–333) vs. Table 5 (lines 175–198).
* **The Defect:**
  1. **Swapped Frequency Bands in Section 3.2.1 (Line 232):** Line 232 states that relative to HCs, `PDnFoG` showed reduced ALFF in the `right putamen` in the `conventional` and `slow-5` bands and the `right pallidum` in the `slow-4` band, and increased ALFF in the `left fusiform gyrus` (`slow-5`) and `left middle temporal gyrus` (`slow-4`). In **Table 2** (lines 159–160, 166–167), `Temporal_Mid_L` and `Pallidum_R` are in **`Slow-5`**, whereas `Fusiform_L` and `Putamen_R` are in **`Slow-4`**!
  2. **Mislabeled Group Contrasts in Section 3.2.4 (Lines 331–333):** Line 331 states *"relative to PDnFoG patients, PDFoG patients showed increased wavelet-ALFF in... the right cuneus and right cerebellar lobule VI in the conventional and slow-4 bands; and the left cerebellar lobule VI, right inferior frontal gyrus (pars orbitalis), and right calcarine fissure... in the slow-5 band"*, which in **Table 5** (lines 175–178, 184–188, 193–196) is actually the **`PDFoG vs HC`** comparison! Conversely, line 333 states *"compared with HCs, PDFoG patients exhibited higher wavelet-ALFF in the left inferior frontal gyrus (pars orbitalis) in the conventional band, the left inferior temporal gyrus in the slow-5 band, and the left cerebellar lobules IV and V in the slow-4 band"*, which in **Table 5** (lines 179, 189, 197) is actually the **`PDnFoG vs HC`** comparison!

## 4. Quantitative Rubric Scoring

* **Rigor (1.1 / 3.5):** While preprocessing (Friston-24, WM, CSF, global signal regression), smoothing-kernel/FD sensitivity analyses, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control on the correlation analyses are well-executed, the primary whole-brain analyses suffer from an invalid `voxel p < 0.05` GRF cluster-forming threshold across 36 unadjusted tests, a major ANCOVA extrapolation error (`Duration of illness` regressed out in `Patient vs HC` contrasts where HCs have `Duration = 0`), and an uncleaned decimal-shift outlier in Table 1 (`1.84 ± 6.27 m/s`).
* **Claims (1.1 / 2.5):** The authors commendably report the null FDR-corrected brain–gait correlations without spin and acknowledge limitations candidly, but their central claim that PDFoG spares the basal ganglia while PDnFoG reduces basal ganglia activity is an artifact of regressing out disease duration against healthy controls, and Sections 3.2.1 and 3.2.4 contain text–table swaps.
* **Biology (1.3 / 2.0):** Conducting both rs-fMRI and quantitative wearable sensor gait paradigms (TUG, Narrow Path Walking, 360° Turning, and vertical ankle Freeze Index) in the dopaminergic OFF state ($>12\text{ h}$ withdrawal) in $79$ PD patients and $44$ HCs is clinically well-designed, though `PDFoG` and `PDnFoG` were not matched on disease duration ($8.51$ vs. $3.11\text{ yr}$), LEDD, or UPDRS-III motor severity.
* **Reproducibility (1.3 / 2.0):** Acquisition parameters, RESTplus v1.27 preprocessing formulas, wearable sensor algorithms, and complete cluster coordinate tables (Tables 2–5) are thoroughly documented, though raw MRI/sensor data require contacting the authors.
