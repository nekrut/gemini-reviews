## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript models the potential distribution of nine flightless Iberian ant-like fly species (*Tachydromia*, Diptera: Hybotidae)—five species with $<5$ occurrences pooled into a multi-species complex ($n = 68$) and four species modeled individually (*T. lusitanica*, $n = 20$; *T. ebejeri*, $n = 20$; *T. semiaptera*, $n = 14$; *T. iberica*, $n = 20$)—and computes their spatial intersection with 13 native *Quercus* oak species and four Iberian biogeographic zones. While compiling primary entomological distribution records for micro-endemic, wing-reduced dipterans is valuable for natural history, the quantitative ecological modeling and spatial overlap analyses suffer from severe small-sample overfitting, post-hoc model cherry-picking, an outright mathematical contradiction between the text's Intersection over Union (`IoU`) definition and the directional containment ratios reported in Tables 4 and 5, and inconsistent descriptions of the modeling pipeline.

### Claim-by-Claim Evaluation

1. **Claim 1:** Ensemble species distribution models for the four individual *Tachydromia* species achieve near-perfect predictive performance (`ROC = 0.99`, `Sensitivity = 100%`, `TSS = 0.92–0.94`, `KAPPA = 0.65–0.80`; Section 3.1, Table 3) and accurately resolve species-specific climatic niches across the Iberian Peninsula. **`[Methodologically Flawed]`**
   * **Audit Verification:** The four individual species have total sample sizes of $n = 20, 20, 14,$ and $20$ presence records across a 1 km grid of the Iberian Peninsula (Table 1). Under the 80%/20% random split-sample procedure described in Section 2.5 (line 87), each model is trained on only 11 to 16 presences across 6 environmental predictors (~1.8 to 2.7 events per predictor) and evaluated on just **2.8 to 4 test presences**. Fitting 10 high-capacity machine-learning and statistical algorithms (including `ANN`, `RF`, `GBM` with 2,000 trees, `GAM`, `CTA`, `MARS`, `FDA`, `GLM`, `MAXENT`, and `SRE`) across 50 pseudo-absence × cross-validation runs (500 models per dataset), selecting the top 35 models with the highest test TSS on 3–4 test points (Section 2.6, line 94), and then evaluating the resulting ensemble across the full dataset with 10,000 background pseudo-absences guarantees severe overfitting and metric inflation (`Sensitivity = 100%` and `ROC = 0.99` across every single individual species in Table 3). Moreover, predictor selection via Random Forest on all 27 variables (Section 2.4, line 68) was conducted prior to the final modeling splits, introducing feature-selection leakage.
2. **Claim 2:** Spatial overlap between *Tachydromia* species and *Quercus* species (Table 4) and Iberian biogeographic Life zones (Table 5) was quantified using the Intersection over Union (`IoU`, Jaccard similarity index; Section 2.7, lines 109–111), demonstrating high `IoU` values such as `1.00` between *T. semiaptera* and *Quercus pyrenaica* and `0.94` between *T. iberica* and the Eurosiberian zone. **`[Methodologically Flawed]`**
   * **Audit Verification:** Section 2.7 (lines 109–111) explicitly defines the overlap metric as *"the Intersection Over Union (IoU) method [97]. This method is based on the Jaccard index that establishes the ratio between the overlapping area and the union of the areas, ranging from 0 to 1 (Equation (1))."* (Note that Equation 1 itself is missing from line 110). However, the captions and numerical entries of Table 4 (line 201) and Table 5 (line 213) state: *"Metrics from 0 to 1 calculated by dividing the area intersected and the total area of the target species."* That is the directional containment fraction $\frac{|A_{\text{Tachydromia}} \cap A_{\text{Reference}}|}{|A_{\text{Tachydromia}}|}$, **not** the Jaccard index / `IoU` $\frac{|A_{\text{Tachydromia}} \cap A_{\text{Reference}}|}{|A_{\text{Tachydromia}} \cup A_{\text{Reference}}|}$. This mathematical discrepancy is proven directly by the tables:
     - In Table 5, the four Life-zone values in every column sum to exactly $1.00$ (e.g., *Tachydromia* group: $0.66 + 0.14 + 0.18 + 0.02 = 1.00$; *T. lusitanica*: $0.76 + 0.18 + 0.06 + 0.00 = 1.00$; *T. semiaptera*: $0.55 + 0.18 + 0.27 + 0.00 = 1.00$; *T. iberica*: $0.94 + 0.03 + 0.03 + 0.00 = 1.00$). Because the four Life zones partition the Iberian Peninsula, directional fractions of a species' range sum to $1.00$, whereas `IoU` values cannot unless the species occupies the entire peninsula.
     - In Table 4, *T. semiaptera* (a restricted central-western species) has an overlap score of `1.00` with *Quercus pyrenaica* and `0.96` with *Quercus robur* (which spans northern and western Iberia). Under true `IoU`, a value of `1.00` is mathematically possible if and only if the two species have identical binary range maps across the entire Iberian Peninsula ($|A_1 \cap A_2| = |A_1 \cup A_2|$).
3. **Claim 3:** Pooling five rare species with $<5$ records alongside the four more common species into a single 9-species genus complex (`Tachydromia` group, $n = 68$) yields a biologically meaningful niche model for conservation planning of the rare micro-endemics (Section 2.2, lines 47–49; Section 4.1, lines 221–228). **`[Overstated]`**
   * **Audit Verification:** Of the 68 records in the pooled `Tachydromia` group, 55 records ($80.9\%$) belong to the four common species (`20 + 18 + 11 + 6 = 55` in Table 1 after 1 km grid thinning, or `20 + 20 + 14 + 20 = 74` raw records), while the five rare species (*T. stenoptera*, *T. cantabrica*, *T. hispanica*, *T.romatizans*/*T.ansoain*, etc.) contribute only 13 records combined ($1\text{--}4$ records each). Consequently, the pooled `Tachydromia` group habitat suitability map is overwhelmingly driven by the four common species and cannot resolve distinct micro-endemic refugia or conservation priorities for the five rare species.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Extreme Small-Sample Overfitting ($n = 14\text{--}20$), Test-Set Cherry-Picking, and Random Pseudo-Absence Inflation (`[Critical / Fatal]`)
* **Location:** Section 2.2 (Table 1, line 53), Section 2.4 (lines 67–76), Section 2.5 (lines 84–88), Section 2.6 (lines 93–98), and Section 3.1 (Table 3, line 124).
* **Mechanism:**
  1. For each individual species ($n = 14$ for *T. semiaptera* and $n = 20$ for the other three), 6 environmental predictors were selected (`aspect`, `elevation`, `bio2`, `bio4`, `bio14`, `bio18`). Fitting 10 algorithms—including neural networks (`ANN`), Random Forests (`RF`), Generalized Boosted Regression (`GBM` with 2,000 trees), and Generalized Additive Models (`GAM`)—on 11–16 training presence points violates minimum events-per-variable requirements by an order of magnitude.
  2. Ten thousand ($10,000$) background pseudo-absences were randomly sampled across the entire Iberian Peninsula without spatial buffering or target-group bias correction (Section 2.4, line 67; Section 2.5, line 85). For narrow mountain/forest endemics clustered in northwest/central Iberia, random peninsula-wide pseudo-absences trivially separate presences from arid southern/eastern background points, inflating `ROC` and `Specificity`.
  3. In Section 2.6 (line 94), out of the 500 individual models fitted per species (10 algorithms $\times$ 5 PA datasets $\times$ 10 evaluation runs), the authors cherry-picked the top 35 models (`n = 35`) based on test-set `TSS` rank (computed on just 3–4 held-out test presences) and then evaluated the ensemble on the full dataset in Table 3, yielding `Sensitivity = 100%` and `ROC = 0.99` for all four species.

### Defect 2: Mathematical Contradiction Between `IoU` (Jaccard Index) in Text and Directional Range Containment in Tables 4 & 5 (`[Critical]`)
* **Location:** Section 2.7 (lines 109–111) vs. Section 3.6 (Table 4, line 201; Table 5, line 213).
* **Mechanism:** Section 2.7 states that spatial overlap was computed using Intersection over Union (`IoU`, Jaccard index), where the denominator is the spatial union of the two binary suitability maps. Not only is `Equation (1)` missing from line 110, but the actual values in Tables 4 and 5 are calculated by dividing the intersection area by the total area of the target *Tachydromia* species alone ($\frac{|A_{\text{Tachydromia}} \cap A_{\text{Ref}}|}{|A_{\text{Tachydromia}}|}$). Because *Quercus pyrenaica* and *Quercus robur* have much larger distributions across Iberia than *T. semiaptera*, directional containment equals `1.00` and `0.96` even though true `IoU` is small. Interpreting directional containment as symmetric `IoU` distorts the biogeographic conclusions in Sections 3.6 and 4.3.

### Defect 3: Internal Pipeline Contradictions and Severe Manuscript Preparation / Editing Defects (`[Major]`)
* **Location:** Abstract (lines 5–8), Section 2.3 (line 61), Section 2.4 (line 68), Section 2.5 (line 87), Section 2.6 (line 94), Section 3.1 (line 119), Section 3.5.2 (lines 183–194), and Data Availability Statement (line 400).
* **Mechanism:**
  1. **Duplicate / Contradictory Abstracts:** Lines 5–6 and lines 7–8 contain two different abstracts pasted back-to-back. The first abstract (line 5) states *"using an ensemble of 9 different species distribution models"*, whereas the second abstract (line 7) and Section 2.3 (line 61) state *"ten different techniques"*, and Section 2.6 (line 94) states *"the top 10% percentile best models for the seven best-performing techniques considering the TSS rank (n = 35)"* (note: $10\%$ of 7 techniques $\times$ 50 runs is 35 models, meaning 3 of the 10 techniques were silently dropped).
  2. **Inconsistent Cross-Validation Splits:** Section 2.4 (line 68) specifies *"twenty evaluation rounds using the 70/30 train-test split method"*, whereas Section 2.5 (line 87) specifies *"ten rounds of cross-validation repeats, split in 80% for training the data and 20% for model testing"*.
  3. **Missing Species Subheadings in Section 3.5.2:** In Section 3.5.2, the subheading for *Tachydromia ebejeri* is missing before line 187 (so the paragraph beginning *"For T. ebejeri, aspect is the top variable..."* is merged under `3.5.2.1. Tachydromia lusitanica`), and the subheading for *Tachydromia iberica* is missing before line 193 (so the paragraph beginning *"Aspect is also the top-ranked variable to explain T. iberica distribution..."* is merged under `3.5.2.2. Tachydromia semiaptera`).
  4. **Misnumbered Table Citation:** Section 3.1 (line 119) cites `(Table 1)` for the `KAPPA`, `TSS`, and `ROC` scores, which are actually in `Table 3` (`Table 1` lists species occurrence counts).
  5. **Unfulfilled Data Availability Placeholder:** Line 400 states *"Upon acceptance of the manuscript, all set of data supporting results will be made publicly available at GitHub platform"* without any repository URL (although Supplementary Materials on line 397 provides a Zenodo link for supplementary tables/figures).
