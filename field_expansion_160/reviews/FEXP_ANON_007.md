## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study investigates whether `EIGENSTRAT` (a PCA-based population stratification correction originally developed for human diploid GWAS) can identify viral genetic variants associated with zoonotic spillover in small, phylogenetically and geographically stratified viral datasets. The authors quantify two dimensions of phylogenetic stratification using Pagel's $\lambda$: allelic phylogenetic signal ($\lambda_A$) and host-state phylogenetic clustering ($\lambda_S$). Using pure birth-death tree simulations ($N = 100$ and $1,000$ tips, $1,000$ independent binary loci) and hybrid simulations appending a single synthetic locus onto the empirical Sierra Leone LASV polymerase (L-gene) phylogeny ($N = 92$: 67 human samples mostly from Kenema Government Hospital [Eastern/Southern Provinces, Clades A & B, 2009–2013] and 25 *M. natalensis* rodent samples, 11 of which were collected in the Northern Province in 2019–2020 and form Clade C), the authors show that when $\lambda_S > 0.99$ (full LASV dataset), `EIGENSTRAT` fails to control Type I error, exceeding the nominal $\alpha = 10^{-4}$ threshold by two orders of magnitude ($\sim 10^{-2}$). Removing Clade C (11 rodents, 1 human) reduces $\lambda_S$ from $0.99$ to $0.52$ and lowers the simulated Type I error rate (OR $= 0.06$), reducing the number of candidate nonsynonymous L-gene SNPs from 13 to 2 (SNPs 1 and 10).

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision (Mixed / Salvageable).** The authors deserve credit for honestly demonstrating via their own hybrid simulations that `EIGENSTRAT` suffers catastrophic Type I error inflation on the full LASV dataset, for depositing their simulation/analysis code on OSF (`https://osf.io/c7tqp/`) and Bitbucket (`https://bitbucket.org/aobwhitlock/lassaspillover`), and for contributing 30 new rodent LASV segment sequences to GenBank. However, the study's methodological premise and empirical SNP claims suffer from three fundamental flaws: (1) benchmarking a Euclidean PCA method (`EIGENSTRAT` with 2 PCs) in complete isolation without comparing it against established *tree-aware* microbial/viral GWAS methods (`treeWAS`, `hogwash`, `pyseer` with phylogenetic kinship matrix, or phylogenetic logistic regression / homoplasy counting) that were specifically designed to solve clonal phylogenetic stratification; (2) an unrealistic, non-evolutionary simulation mechanism for the causal spillover locus (simulating host state and allele state independently along the tree and then post-hoc flipping ancestral alleles to derived in human tips with probability $\phi$), which manufactures terminal-branch homoplasies that are trivially easy to detect when the allele is rare but biologically impossible when the allele is common; and (3) unresolved spatio-temporal confounding and residual Type I error inflation even after removing Clade C, where the remaining 14 rodent sequences (10 unlabelled from 2012, 3 Eastern, 1 Southern) still have $\lambda_S = 0.52$ and a simulated false positive rate of $\sim 10^{-3}$ to $10^{-2}$ at high $\lambda_A$ (Figure 4a)—meaning the 2 surviving candidate SNPs in the ~2,200-codon L gene remain well within the expected false-positive envelope.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1 (`EIGENSTRAT` fails to control Type I error under severe phylogenetic host stratification [$\lambda_S \to 1, \lambda_A \to 1$], and larger sample sizes [$N=1,000$ vs. $100$] exacerbate false-positive rates under unmitigated stratification): [Well Supported].**  
  Both the pure simulations (Figure 2a,b) and the LASV hybrid simulations (Figure 4a) clearly demonstrate exponential inflation of Type I error as $\lambda_S$ and $\lambda_A$ increase, consistent with theoretical expectations when linear PCs fail to capture deep coalescent branch structure.
* **Claim 2 (Removing the geographically confounded Clade C [$n=12$: 11 Northern Province rodents from 2019–2020 and 1 human] reduces $\lambda_S$ from $0.99$ to $0.52$ and lowers the Type I error rate by an order of magnitude without reducing power at $\phi = 0.75$): [Well Supported].**  
  Clade C is almost entirely composed of Northern Province rodents sampled 6–10 years after the KGH human samples; pruning this uninformative, purely confounded clade reduces false positives while preserving the contrast in Clades A and B.
* **Claim 3 (Confining the `EIGENSTRAT` analysis to Clades A and B reliably identifies two candidate spillover-associated nonsynonymous SNPs [SNPs 1 and 10] in the LASV polymerase gene): [Fragile].**  
  Look closely at Figure 4a (line 112): even *without* Clade C ($\lambda_S = 0.52$), the Type I error rate of `EIGENSTRAT` at nominal $p < 10^{-4}$ only stays near $10^{-4}$ when $\lambda_A \approx 0$, and rises to $\sim 2 \times 10^{-3}$ – $5 \times 10^{-3}$ as $\lambda_A \to 1$. In Figure 5 (line 124), SNPs 1 and 10 are clade-structured polymorphisms with high $\lambda_A$ (present in a subset of Clade A/B tips) tested across hundreds of polymorphic sites in a dataset with only $14$ remaining rodent sequences! Neither the exact nucleotide/amino-acid coordinates, exact $p$-values, nor the number of independent homoplasic occurrences on the phylogeny are reported for SNPs 1 and 10.
* **Claim 4 (Detection power of `EIGENSTRAT` is highest [97%] when the spillover allele is rare [<25% frequency] and drops to 9% when the allele is common [>75% frequency]): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This counterintuitive behavior is an artifact of the authors' post-hoc allele-flipping simulation protocol (line 45): the authors first evolve a neutral locus on the tree, and then at the tips, *only for human samples carrying the ancestral allele (`0`)*, they flip `0` $\to$ `1` with probability $\phi = 0.75$. When the derived allele `1` is already at $>75\%$ frequency in both rodents and humans due to neutral clade fixation, flipping 75% of the remaining human `0`s shifts the human frequency from, say, $0.80$ to $0.95$ (an odds ratio / frequency difference too small to pass $p < 10^{-4}$ at $N = 100$). Conversely, when `1` is rare ($<5\%$ in rodents), flipping 75% of human `0`s to `1` creates a massive artificial jump ($5\%$ in rodents vs. $>75\%$ in humans) via dozens of independent terminal-branch mutations on human tips!

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

1. **Pillar 3 & 5 Violation — Absence of Any Phylogenetic / Microbial GWAS Baseline Comparison (Methods lines 28–31):**  
   `EIGENSTRAT` adjusts for population structure by regressing out the top $K=2$ linear principal components of the genotype matrix. In non-recombining (or low-recombination) viral phylogenies, top PCs only separate the deepest 2–3 basal clades (Clades A, B, and C, as shown in Figure 3) and leave all sub-clade phylogenetic correlations unadjusted. Purpose-built phylogenetic GWAS methods—such as `treeWAS` (Collins & Didelot 2018), `pyseer` (Lees et al. 2018, using the full pairwise patristic distance / kinship matrix), `hogwash` (Saund & Snitkin 2020), or phylogenetic generalized linear mixed models—explicitly condition on the tree topology and branch lengths. Evaluating `EIGENSTRAT` without comparing its Type I/II error curves against even a single tree-based association test leaves readers with a suboptimal tool for viral data.
2. **Pillar 3 Violation — Flawed Post-Hoc Tip-Flipping Simulation of the Causal Spillover Locus (Methods line 45, Results line 89):**  
   In nature, if a viral allele increases the probability of rodent-to-human spillover, then human spillover cases are *preferentially drawn from reservoir lineages that already carry the derived allele* (or the mutation arises during within-host human adaptation). Instead, the authors simulated host identity (human vs. rodent) along the tree *independently* of the causal locus, and then post-hoc flipped ancestral alleles in human tips to the derived state with probability $\phi$. This creates two severe distortions: (a) it forces the derived allele frequency in humans to be at least $\phi + (1-\phi)f_{\text{anc}}$ regardless of tree structure, decoupling the causal locus from the evolutionary process when $f$ is low, and (b) it makes the effect size $(\Delta f = \phi(1 - f))$ shrink linearly to zero as the neutral allele frequency $f \to 1$, which is why power artificially collapses at high allele frequencies (line 89).
3. **Pillar 2 & 4 Violation — Severe Spatio-Temporal Confounding in the Remaining 14 Rodent Samples After Clade C Removal (Results lines 94–96, 124–129):**  
   After removing Clade C (11 rodents, 1 human), the L-segment dataset contains 66 human samples (60 from KGH in 2009–2013, 2 from 1976, 4 from 1975–2000) and only **14 rodent samples** (3 from Eastern Province in 2019–2020, 1 from Southern Province in 2019–2020, and 10 from 2012 with *no geographic metadata*, all clustering in Clade A). Thus, Clade B (Southern Province / Bo district human referrals, ~15–20 human samples) contains only **1 rodent sample**! Any SNP that differentiates Clade B (or a sub-clade of Clade A lacking the 2012 unlabelled rodents) from the rest of Clade A will appear "human-associated" simply because Clade B has 1 rodent vs. ~15+ humans.
4. **Pillar 7 Violation — Omission of Quantitative Coordinates, $p$-Values, and Phylogenetic Homoplasy Counts for Candidate SNPs 1 and 10 (Results lines 124–129, Data Accessibility line 147):**  
   While the authors invoke Dual Use Research of Concern (DURC) to justify withholding the plotting script for Figure 5 and the exact residue numbers of SNPs 1 and 10 (line 147), they also omit the statistical test outputs for SNPs 1 and 10: neither their `EIGENSTRAT` $\chi^2$ statistics, nominal $p$-values, minor allele frequencies, Pagel's $\lambda_A$ values, nor the number of independent branches on which they mutate are reported in the text.

### Constructive and Doable Suggestions for Improvement

1. **Benchmark `EIGENSTRAT` Against a Phylogenetic Kinship LMM (`pyseer` / `GEMMA`) or `treeWAS`:**  
   * **WHY:** Two principal components only capture basal splits between Clades A, B, and C, leaving within-clade genealogy uncorrected.  
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Run `treeWAS` (or a linear mixed model using the patristic covariance matrix from the RAxML-NG L-gene phylogeny) on the exact same simulated and LASV datasets in the Bitbucket repository, and compare false-positive and false-negative curves against `EIGENSTRAT`.
2. **Fix the Causal Spillover Simulation to Condition Host Sampling on Genotype Rather Than Flipping Tips Post-Hoc:**  
   * **WHY:** Flipping `0` $\to$ `1` with probability $\phi$ in human tips after evolving host state independently makes the odds ratio depend on background allele frequency $f$ and creates artificial terminal homoplasies.  
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Simulate the neutral and causal loci along the viral phylogeny first, and then assign tip host state (`human` vs. `rodent`) with a log-odds offset $+\beta X_{\text{causal}}$ (or rejection-sample spillover events proportional to relative risk $RR$ while preserving overall clade sampling fractions).
3. **Report Summary Test Statistics and Homoplasy Counts for Candidate SNPs 1 and 10:**  
   * **WHY:** Even if exact nucleotide coordinates are withheld for DURC reasons, readers cannot evaluate the statistical support for SNPs 1 and 10 without their `EIGENSTRAT` $p$-values, Pagel's $\lambda_A$, and parsimony transition counts on the Clade A+B tree.  
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Report the `EIGENSTRAT` $p$-value, $\lambda_A$, and number of independent phylogenetic origins for SNPs 1 and 10 alongside the simulated false-positive rate at their specific $(\lambda_S = 0.52, \lambda_A, \text{MAF})$ coordinates.
