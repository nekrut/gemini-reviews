## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study examines the ecological, temporal, and phylogenetic drivers of Aleutian mink disease virus (AMDV) spillover from invasive feral American mink ($n = 1,303$ carcasses from 25 trapping sites, 1995–2022; overall seroprevalence $67.0\%$) to six native mustelid species ($n = 446$ roadkill/hunted carcasses across Poland; overall seroprevalence $28.0\%$: polecat $55\%$ [17/31], stone marten $39\%$ [53/134], weasel $26\%$ [10/38], pine marten $25\%$ [31/125], otter $21\%$ [3/14], and badger $11\%$ [11/104]). In Stage 1, the authors fit a binomial generalized additive model (`mgcv` GAM) to the American mink data with thin-plate splines for `year` ($k=15$) and `longitude × latitude` ($k=25$) plus `season` and `sex`. In Stage 2, they plug the GAM-predicted mink seroprevalence at each native mustelid's coordinates into a fixed-effects binomial GLM alongside `phylogenetic distance to American mink`, `year`, `season`, and `sex`. They report that native mustelid seroprevalence increases with local mink seroprevalence ($0 \to 0.54$), increases 15-fold over time ($0.040$ in 1995 to $0.604$ in 2022, despite mink seroprevalence declining from $0.957$ in 2006 to $0.600$ in 2020), and decreases with phylogenetic distance from American mink ($0.682$ in closely related *Mustela* vs. $0.323$ in distantly related badgers).

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision (Mixed / Salvageable).** A 27-year dataset of 1,749 wild carnivore carcasses across seven species is a substantial and valuable field resource, including the first serological detection of AMDV antibodies in least weasels (*Mustela nivalis*). However, the two-stage statistical modeling pipeline suffers from four severe methodological flaws that artificially deflate standard errors and produce implausibly narrow confidence intervals (e.g., reporting $1995$ native mustelid seroprevalence as `0.040, 95% CI = 0.037–0.042`—a $\pm 0.25\%$ margin of error on a binary outcome with $N=446$ total samples over 27 years!):
1. **Phylogenetic pseudoreplication ($N = 446$ individuals treated as independent observations of $K = 6$ species without a species random effect):** In the Stage 2 GLM (line 40), `phylogenetic distance` is a species-level trait with only 6 distinct values (actually 5, since *Martes martes* and *Martes foina* are sister species equidistant from *Neogale vison*), yet the binomial GLM omits a `(1 | species)` random intercept, treating all 446 individuals as independent degrees of freedom for testing a 6-species phylogenetic hypothesis.
2. **Unpropagated generated-regressor uncertainty and spatial-temporal decoupling in Stage 1 $\to$ Stage 2:** The Stage 1 GAM predicts mink seroprevalence from `year` + `lat × lon` + `season` + `sex`, and that point prediction is plugged into the Stage 2 GLM as an exact fixed covariate without propagating GAM prediction variance (via bootstrap or joint Bayesian modeling) and without clarifying whether the Stage 1 GAM prediction for each native mustelid was evaluated at a fixed reference year/season or at the mustelid's collection year (which would make it collinear with `year` and `season` in Stage 2).
3. **Differential secondary antibody conjugate confounding across species:** Two different ELISA secondary antibodies at different dilutions (`AffiniPure Goat Anti-Cat IgG` at `1:4500` vs. `goat anti-ferret IgG` at `1:20,000`, line 33) were used without calibrating species-specific binding affinity across Mustelinae, Guloninae (*Martes*), Lutrinae (*Lutra*), and Melinae (*Meles*).

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1 (AMDV antibodies are present in all six studied native mustelid species in Poland [28% overall], including a first report in least weasels [*Mustela nivalis*, 26%]): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The serological detection is supported by VP2 ELISA and prior PCR confirmation in 3 of the 6 species, and the authors candidly discuss potential cross-reactivity with other amdoparvoviruses and lower PCR positivity ($10\%\text{–}20\%$) in native mustelids (lines 62, 70).
* **Claim 2 (Native mustelid AMDV seroprevalence correlates positively with local feral American mink AMDV seroprevalence): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The spatial gradient in Poland (high in the north, near zero in the south) is strong enough that a positive association between mink and native mustelid seroprevalence is biologically and empirically plausible, even though the Stage 2 GLM standard errors are underestimated because Stage 1 GAM kriging uncertainty was not propagated.
* **Claim 3 (AMDV seroprevalence in native mustelids is driven by phylogenetic distance to American mink [compatibility hypothesis: 0.682 in closely related species vs. 0.323 in distantly related species]): [Fragile].**  
  Because there are only 6 native species ($n=14$ in otter, $n=31$ in polecat, $n=38$ in weasel, $n=104$ in badger, $n=125$ in pine marten, $n=134$ in stone marten) and the GLM omitted a species random effect (`(1 | species)`), the model treats $N = 446$ animals as 446 independent phylogenetic data points ($df = 440$ instead of $df = 4$). Moreover, the two *Martes* species have identical phylogenetic distance to mink yet differ notably in raw seroprevalence ($39\%$ stone marten vs. $25\%$ pine marten), and the secondary ELISA antibody (`goat anti-ferret IgG`, raised against *Mustela putorius furo*) has highest binding affinity to *Mustela* (polecat/weasel) and lowest affinity to *Meles* (badger), directly confounding the phylogenetic gradient!
* **Claim 4 (Over 27 years, AMDV seroprevalence in native mustelids increased 15-fold from 0.040 [95% CI: 0.037–0.042] in 1995 to 0.604 [95% CI: 0.568–0.638] in 2022): [Overstated].**  
  The reported 95% confidence intervals (`0.037–0.042` in 1995, width $0.005$; and `0.952–0.962` for mink in 2006, width $0.010$) are mathematically impossible for genuine binomial marginal predictions on $N=446$ samples across 27 years. A binomial proportion of $p = 0.04$ with width $\pm 0.0025$ would require $N \approx 23,600$ observations! The authors appear to have computed the mean and 95% CI of the *fitted values across individual specimens or grid points* ($\bar{\hat{p}} \pm 1.96 \cdot \text{SE}(\hat{p}_i)$) rather than the Wald/profile confidence interval of the regression prediction ($\hat{p} \pm 1.96 \cdot \text{SE}_{\text{model}}$). Furthermore, space and time are confounded if northern Poland (high prevalence) was sampled more heavily in later years or if particular species (e.g., polecats/stone martens vs. badgers) were collected in different decades.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

1. **Pillar 4 Violation — Mathematically Impossible 95% Confidence Intervals on Model Predictions (Results lines 48, 55):**  
   In line 48, the GAM-predicted seroprevalence for American mink in 2006 is reported as `0.957 (CI 95% = 0.952–0.962)` and in the breeding season as `0.846 (CI 95% = 0.833–0.858)`. In line 55, the GLM-predicted seroprevalence for native mustelids in 1995 is reported as `0.040 (CI 95% = 0.037–0.042)` and in 2022 as `0.604 (CI 95% = 0.568–0.638)`. With only $N = 446$ native mustelids total over 27 years (an average of $\sim 16.5$ animals per year), the true logistic regression standard error at the 1995 boundary of the time series guarantees a 95% CI spanning at least $[0.01, 0.12]$, not $[0.037, 0.042]$. Reporting the standard error of the sample mean of predicted values across observations instead of the regression parameter uncertainty understates uncertainty by an order of magnitude.
2. **Pillar 3 Violation — Phylogenetic Pseudoreplication in the Stage 2 GLM (Methods line 40, Results line 55):**  
   Testing the effect of a species-level predictor (`phylogenetic distance to American mink`) across $N = 446$ individuals from 6 species in a standard binomial GLM without a random intercept for species (`glmer(..., (1 | species))` or `MCMCglmm`) commits classic pseudoreplication, inflating the effective sample size from 6 species to 446 individuals.
3. **Pillar 3 & 4 Violation — Unpropagated Uncertainty and Spatio-Temporal Specification Ambiguity in the Two-Stage GAM-to-GLM Pipeline (Methods lines 38–40):**  
   * **Generated regressor bias:** Plugging $\hat{P}_{\text{mink}}(x_i, y_i)$ from the Stage 1 GAM into the Stage 2 GLM as a deterministic covariate ignores estimation variance from the GAM spline surface (`k = 25` spatial basis functions on only 25 mink trapping sites!).Wait—look at line 28 and line 38: American mink were collected from **25 trapping sites** (line 28), and the authors set `k = 25` for the 2D `longitude × latitude` thin-plate spline interaction *plus* `k = 15` for `year`! A 2D spatial spline with `k = 25` on 25 spatial sites interpolates site-level noise.
   * **Temporal vs. spatial prediction ambiguity:** Line 38 states that the Stage 1 GAM included `year`, `longitude × latitude`, `season`, and `sex`, and then assigned "a predicted AMDV seroprevalence of American mink to each location of native mustelids." If that prediction varied only by `(lon, lat)` (holding `year` constant), it ignores that mink seroprevalence declined from $0.96$ to $0.60$ over time; if it varied by `(lon, lat, year, season)`, then plugging it into a Stage 2 GLM that *also* includes `year` and `season` (line 40) induces structural dependency between the Stage 1 temporal spline and the Stage 2 linear `year` slope.
4. **Pillar 2 & 6 Violation — Serological Assay Bias Across Divergent Mustelid Subfamilies (Methods line 33):**  
   Line 33 notes that either `AffiniPure Goat Anti-Cat IgG (1:4500)` or `goat anti-ferret IgG (1:20,000)` was used to detect antibodies on dried blood filter paper strips. Because domestic ferrets (*Mustela putorius furo*) are conspecific with the European polecat (*M. putorius*, $55\%$ seropositive) and congeneric with the weasel (*M. nivalis*, $26\%$), whereas badgers (*Meles meles*, $11\%$ seropositive) belong to the divergent subfamily Melinae, anti-ferret IgG secondary antibodies have systematically higher affinity for *Mustela* IgG than for *Meles* IgG, which directly mimics the hypothesized phylogenetic compatibility effect!
5. **Pillar 7 Violation — Missing `Age` Term in Stage 1 GAM Methods vs. Results:**  
   Methods line 38 states that the mink GAM included `year`, `longitude × latitude`, `season`, and `sex` (never mentioning `age`). Yet Results line 48 reports GAM results comparing `adults (0.846)` vs. `subadult individuals (0.710; Table 1, Figure 2)`.

### Constructive and Doable Suggestions for Improvement

1. **Refit the Stage 2 Model as a Generalized Linear Mixed Model (GLMM) with a Species Random Effect and Propagate Stage 1 Uncertainty:**  
   * **WHY:** Testing phylogenetic distance across 446 individuals without `(1 | species)` treats individuals within the same species as independent replicates of evolutionary divergence, and plugging in point predictions from a `k=25` GAM on 25 sites ignores first-stage estimation error.  
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Fit a binomial GLMM (`lme4::glmer` or `mgcv::gam` with `bs="re"` for species) including `(1 | species)` (and `(1 | site)` for mink), reduce the mink spatial spline basis dimension to `k = 10–12` (so $k < \text{number of trapping sites}$), and compute Stage 2 confidence intervals using a non-parametric bootstrap that resamples both Stage 1 mink data and Stage 2 mustelid data.
2. **Recompute Model-Based 95% Confidence Intervals Properly:**  
   * **WHY:** Intervals such as `0.040 (95% CI = 0.037–0.042)` in 1995 reflect the standard error of the mean of fitted values across individuals rather than the uncertainty of the logistic regression curve.  
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Use `predict(..., type = "link", se.fit = TRUE)` in R to compute $\text{logit}^{-1}(\hat{\eta} \pm 1.96 \cdot \text{SE}(\hat{\eta}))$ at fixed reference covariate values, and update all CIs in the Abstract, Results (lines 48, 55), and Figures 2–3.
3. **Control for Secondary Conjugate Type (`Anti-Cat` vs. `Anti-Ferret`) and Species-by-Year Sampling Skew:**  
   * **WHY:** If anti-ferret IgG binds *Mustela* IgG more avidly than *Meles* (badger) IgG, or if badgers were predominantly sampled in earlier years / southern regions, the phylogenetic and temporal trends will be confounded.  
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Include `conjugate_type` (`anti-cat` vs. `anti-ferret`) as a covariate in the GLM, report a cross-tabulation of sample counts by species $\times$ decade $\times$ region, and clarify in Methods line 38 how `age` (adult vs. subadult, line 48) was included in the mink GAM.
