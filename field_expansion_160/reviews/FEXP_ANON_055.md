## 1. Blinded Metadata Perception

* **Perceived Field & Subfield:** Translational Radiation Oncology / Gastrointestinal Pathology / Mucosa-Associated Microbiome in Rectal Cancer
* **Study Design:** Retrospective cohort study profiling mucosa-associated 16S rDNA (V3–V4) from microdissected FFPE proximal resection margins (sigmoid colon) across rectal carcinoma patients stratified by neoadjuvant therapy modality, clinical radiation colitis, histologic radiation injury score, and College of American Pathologists (CAP) pathologic treatment effect score.
* **Model System & Sample Size:** Human rectal carcinoma surgical resection specimens ($N = 57$ eligible patients from 100 screened at the University of Iowa, 2015–2021: No neoadjuvant therapy $n = 9$, Neoadjuvant chemotherapy only $n = 8$, Neoadjuvant chemoradiation without clinical colitis $n = 16$, Neoadjuvant chemoradiation with clinical colitis $n = 24$; among the $n = 40$ chemoradiation patients, $n = 14$ achieved complete pathologic response [score 0] and $n = 26$ had incomplete response [scores 1–3]).
* **Perceived Methodological Maturity:** Sound pathological design with blinded triplicate histologic scoring, an explicit *a priori* power calculation, and an exemplary two-stage FFPE decontamination validation protocol; moderated by unadjusted clinical confounders (loperamide exposure, total neoadjuvant therapy, surgical waiting interval, and age) and an anatomical gap between the sampled proximal sigmoid resection margin and pre-surgical distal rectal biopsies needed for "watch-and-wait" clinical decision-making.

## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This retrospective cohort study investigates the relationship between mucosa-associated bacteria, radiation colitis, and pathologic treatment response in 57 rectal carcinoma patients who underwent surgical resection following no neoadjuvant therapy ($n = 9$), chemotherapy alone ($n = 8$), or standard long-course neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (50.4 Gy in 28 fractions; $n = 40$, comprising 24 with clinical radiation colitis requiring loperamide and 16 without colitis; 14 complete pathologic responders [pCR, CAP score 0] and 26 incomplete responders [CAP scores 1–3]). Using V3–V4 16S rDNA sequencing of microdissected nonneoplastic sigmoid colon mucosa from the proximal surgical margin of FFPE blocks (NCBI SRA `PRJNA1029660`), coupled with an empirical FFPE decontamination protocol (benchmarked against 10 paired fresh-frozen/FFPE colon samples plus `decontam`), the authors find that global unweighted UniFrac $\beta$ diversity differs across treatment/colitis groups (PERMANOVA $P = 0.006$) and strongly separates pCR from incomplete responders ($P \le 0.001$). Blinded triplicate histologic scoring of radiation injury shows an inverse linear relationship between mucosal injury severity and $\alpha$ diversity (Figure 3C). Taxonomically, `Bacillaceae` is enriched in patients with clinical radiation colitis and incomplete response, while `Streptococcus`, `Lachnospiraceae`, and `Bacillaceae` are depleted and PICRUSt2-predicted $\text{NAD}^+$ salvage, cobinamide salvage, and arginine/ornithine pathways are enriched in complete responders.

**Editorial Verdict: Minor-to-Moderate Revision (Strong / Sound).** This is a well-conceived, carefully executed translational pathology study. Its standout methodological strengths include: (1) a rigorous two-tiered FFPE decontamination strategy that empirically compared 10 paired fresh-frozen and FFPE colonic mucosa specimens from the same institution alongside `decontam` (removing ~25% of reads, predominantly unclassifiable kingdom-level artifact sequences); (2) blinded triplicate histopathologic scoring of radiation injury by two gastrointestinal pathologists and a pathology resident; and (3) full deposition of raw sequencing reads (`PRJNA1029660`). The primary limitations that should be addressed are: (a) clinical confounding in the "radiation with colitis" definition, which required loperamide prescription (an antimotility opioid agonist that directly alters gut transit and microbiota) and had twice the rate of sequential chemotherapy + chemoradiation ($50\%$ vs. $25\%$) and a longer median interval to surgery ($108$ vs. $75$ days); (b) absence of multivariable PERMANOVA (`adonis2`) adjustment for these covariates and false-discovery-rate correction on taxon-level Kruskal–Wallis/LEfSe tests; and (c) framing proximal *sigmoid colon* surgical margin data in the Abstract as a biomarker for nonoperative ("watch-and-wait") management without noting in the Abstract that the sampled tissue was proximal sigmoid resection margin rather than endoscopic rectal biopsy or stool.

### Specific Conclusions and Assessment of Support

* **Unweighted UniFrac $\beta$ diversity of proximal-margin colonic mucosa differs significantly between complete (CAP score 0) and incomplete (CAP scores 1–3) pathologic responders to neoadjuvant chemoradiation: [Well Supported].**  
  Within the uniform 50.4 Gy/28-fraction chemoradiation cohort ($n = 40$: 14 pCR vs. 26 incomplete responders), unweighted UniFrac PERMANOVA demonstrates a clear separation ($P \le 0.001$, Figure 4B), supported by rigorous FFPE decontamination and adequate sequencing depth (median 51,488 reads/sample).

* **Histopathologic radiation injury severity (blinded triplicate scoring) is inversely associated with mucosal microbiome $\alpha$ diversity: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Three independent blinded observers scored six histological parameters of radiation injury, revealing a statistically significant negative linear slope with $\alpha$ diversity (Figure 3C), though dichotomization at the median score of 5 showed no significant difference in unweighted UniFrac $\beta$ diversity ($P = 0.2$, Figure 3D).

* **Enrichment of `Bacillaceae` is specifically driven by radiation-induced colitis, and depletion of `Streptococcus`, `Lachnospiraceae`, and `Bacillaceae` marks complete pathologic response: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the group differences in relative abundance are clear (Figures 2C and 4C–F), "radiation with colitis" was operationally defined by clinical symptoms *plus loperamide prescription* (line 34), and 12/24 (50%) of colitis patients received sequential systemic chemotherapy before chemoradiation compared to 4/16 (25%) of non-colitis patients (line 39). Without multivariable adjustment, loperamide-induced colonic stasis and sequential chemotherapy cannot be fully disentangled from radiation mucosal inflammation.

* **Posttreatment mucosal microbiome markers from this study can guide clinical decisions to manage rectal carcinoma nonoperatively ("watch-and-wait"): [Overstated].**  
  All samples were microdissected from the proximal surgical resection margin in the **sigmoid colon** ($10\text{–}25\text{ cm}$ proximal to the irradiated rectal tumor bed) *after* proctectomy. While the Discussion (line 152) appropriately notes that nonresection specimen types (stool or endoscopic biopsies) must be tested in future studies, the Abstract and translational summary overstate the immediate applicability to nonoperative management without mentioning the sigmoid margin sampling site.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Confounding by Loperamide Exposure, Total Neoadjuvant Therapy (TNT), Age, and Time to Resection (Pillars 2, 3, 5)
* **Defect:** (a) Section "Study design and case selection" (line 34) defines "radiation with colitis" ($n = 24$) by severe symptoms *and prescription of loperamide*, an antimotility drug that slows colonic transit time and directly reshapes mucosal bacterial communities. (b) Line 39 states that $12/24$ ($50\%$) of the radiation colitis group received chemotherapy followed by chemoradiation compared to $4/16$ ($25\%$) of the no-colitis group, and Table 1 shows a longer median interval from radiotherapy to resection in the colitis group ($108$ days [IQR 74–217] vs. $75$ days [IQR 63–133], $P = 0.2$) as well as significant age differences across the four cohorts ($P = 0.007$).
* **WHY:** Longer intervals between radiation completion and surgery are independently associated with higher pathologic complete response (pCR) rates and time-dependent microbiome recovery, while loperamide and pre-radiation systemic chemotherapy directly alter mucosal flora.
* **WHAT:** Run a multivariable PERMANOVA (`vegan::adonis2`) in the chemoradiation cohort ($n = 40$) to verify that the association between unweighted UniFrac $\beta$ diversity and pathologic complete response (Figure 4B) remains statistically significant after controlling for interval to resection, sequential chemotherapy, age, and loperamide/colitis status.
* **HOW:** Using the QIIME 2 unweighted UniFrac distance matrix and metadata from Table 1 / Supplementary Table S2, execute `adonis2(dist_matrix ~ pCR + interval_to_resection + sequential_chemo + colitis_loperamide + age, by = "margin", permutations = 9999)` and report the marginal $R^2$ and $P$-value for `pCR`.

### 2. Multiple Hypothesis Testing Corrections and Inconsistent LEfSe Threshold Reporting (Pillars 4, 7)
* **Defect:** (a) Methods lines 85 and 95 state that LEfSe differential abundance testing used *"a cutoff LDA score of >2.0... according to the default settings of LEfSe,"* whereas the Figure 2B legend (line 111) states *"using a default LDA effect score cutoff of 3.0."* (b) Phylum- and taxon-level Kruskal–Wallis and Mann–Whitney tests (Figures 2A, 2C–E, 4D–F) and PICRUSt2 pathway LEfSe tests (Figure 5) report unadjusted $P$-values without Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction.
* **WHY:** Screening hundreds of 16S features and PICRUSt2 pathways with unadjusted Kruskal–Wallis tests at $\alpha = 0.05$ inflates the false-positive discovery rate, and inconsistent LDA cutoffs ($2.0$ vs. $3.0$) create ambiguity about feature selection criteria.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Reconcile the exact LDA threshold used in Figures 2B, 4C, and 5A (`2.0` vs. `3.0`); compute and report Benjamini–Hochberg FDR-adjusted $q$-values alongside the nominal $P$-values for the key taxa (`Bacillaceae`, `Alcaligenaceae`, `Fusobacteriaceae`, `Streptococcus`, `Lachnospiraceae`) and PICRUSt2 pathways; and note whether the taxon differences survive `ANCOM-BC` or `MaAsLin2` compositional testing.

### 3. Anatomical Framing in the Abstract and Terminology Cleanup (`PCA` vs. `PCoA`) (Pillars 1, 7)
* **Defect:** (a) The Abstract states that microbiome profiling was performed *"at the proximal margin of resection"* and concludes that *"Posttreatment microbiome $\beta$ diversity markers of complete pathologic response may be useful in decisions to manage rectal carcinoma nonoperatively,"* without clarifying in the Abstract that the proximal margin is unirradiated **sigmoid colon** tissue rather than the rectal tumor bed. (b) Figure 1B, Figure 3D, Figure 4B legends, and Methods line 95 repeatedly expand the abbreviation `PCoA` as *"principal component analysis (PCoA)"* instead of **principal coordinate analysis (PCoA)** (metric multidimensional scaling on a UniFrac distance matrix).
* **WHY:** In rectal cancer surgery (LAR/APR), the proximal resection margin in the sigmoid colon is anatomically distinct from the distal rectum accessible via digital rectal exam/proctoscopy during "watch-and-wait" surveillance, and conflating Principal Component Analysis (Euclidean covariance decomposition) with Principal Coordinate Analysis (distance matrix eigen-decomposition) is a mathematical terminology error.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Specify *"sigmoid colon proximal margin"* in the Abstract and temper the Abstract's closing sentence to state that *if validated in nonoperative rectal biopsy or fecal specimens*, posttreatment microbiome markers could aid nonoperative management; and replace all instances of *"principal component analysis (PCoA)"* with *"principal coordinate analysis (PCoA)"*.
