## 1. Blinded Metadata Perception

* **Perceived Subfield:** Plant Comparative & Population Genomics / Polyploid Genome Assembly (Juglandaceae: *Cyclocarya paliurus*).
* **Study Design:** De novo chromosome-scale genome assembly of three *C. paliurus* individuals—two diploids representing protandrous (`PA-dip`, $2n=2x=32$) and protogynous (`PG-dip`, $2n=2x=32$) heterodichogamous floral morphs and one haplotype-resolved autotetraploid (`PA-tetra`, $2n=4x=64$)—integrated with cytogenetics, floral developmental transcriptomics/hormone profiling (5 stages), leaf triterpenoid UPLC-MS/MS and RNA-seq, and whole-genome resequencing of 45 individuals (10 diploids, 35 tetraploids, plus 1 *Juglans regia* outgroup).

## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This resource and evolutionary genomics study presents three chromosome-scale genome assemblies for the relict medicinal tree *Cyclocarya paliurus*: two diploids (`PA-dip`, 586.6 Mb, Contig N50 1.93 Mb; `PG-dip`, 583.5 Mb, Contig N50 1.39 Mb; 16 pseudo-chromosomes) and one allele-aware autotetraploid (`PA-tetra`, 2.38 Gb, Contig N50 431 kb; 64 pseudo-chromosomes resolved into four homologous haplotype sets via ALLHiC). The assemblies are supported by mitotic karyotyping, meiotic pachytene/diakinesis DAPI imaging demonstrating quadrivalent synapsis, flow cytometry, Smudgeplot (`AAAB` = 57%), and high BUSCO completeness (95.2%–96.4%). Using these references, the authors investigate two post-*Vitis* whole-genome duplications (WGD1 at ~67.6–50.7 MYA and WGD2 at ~11.2–10.5 MYA), cytochrome P450 gene family expansions implicated in cyclocaric acid B biosynthesis, gibberellin ($GA_3$) and co-expression modules across five floral development stages in protandrous vs. protogynous morphs, leaf physiological/transcriptomic differences between ploidies, and population structure/demography across 45 resequenced trees.

**Editorial Verdict: Minor-to-Moderate Revision (Strong / Sound Resource with Secondary Analytical Caveats).** Anchoring the evaluation on the primary deliverable, the three chromosome-level assemblies—especially the phased 64-chromosome autotetraploid assembly backed by cytogenetic quadrivalent verification and complete public data deposition—constitute a high-value genomic resource. However, several downstream population-genetic and transcriptomic analyses exhibit methodological flaws that require re-analysis or careful qualification: (1) calling "dosage-effect genes" via standard FPKM/DESeq2 relative library normalization (which identifies more genes downregulated [759] than upregulated [691] in tetraploids) conflates relative transcript concentration with absolute per-cell gene dosage; (2) applying a diploid variant-calling and SweeD pipeline to ~10$\times$ autotetraploid resequencing data (~2.5$\times$ per haplotype) and mislabeling SweeD positive-sweep outputs as "purifying selection" (line 111) undermines the selective sweep catalog; and (3) pooling structured diploid and tetraploid subpopulations ($K=2$) into a single panmictic SFS in ANGSD/Stairway Plot induces artificial demographic bottlenecks, while the 11.2–10.5 MYA $K_s$ date for WGD2 conflicts with the population-genetic inference of a single recent autotetraploid origin.

### Specific Conclusions and Assessment of Support

* **Chromosome-scale assembly of `PA-dip` (16 chromosomes), `PG-dip` (16 chromosomes), and haplotype-resolved autotetraploid `PA-tetra` (64 chromosomes), with cytogenetic and k-mer proof of autotetraploidy: [Well Supported].**  
  High PacBio (115–223$\times$), Illumina (131–237$\times$), and Hi-C (103–215$\times$) coverage, meiotic quadrivalent imaging (Fig. S2), 13-mer chromosome clustering (Fig. S14), Smudgeplot `AAAB` dominance (Fig. S4D), and BUSCO scores (>95.2% assembly, >94.4% annotation) thoroughly validate the primary genome assemblies.
* **Gibberellin ($GA_3$) accumulation at stage S0 and associated WGCNA co-expression modules correlate with asynchronous male/female floral development in heterodichogamous PA and PG morphs: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Replicated RNA-seq ($n=3$ per stage/sex/morph across S0–S4) and hormone assays show a clear $GA_3$ divergence at S0 and plausible candidate TFs (`Trihelix-1`, `ERF066`, `ERF090`, `WRKY55`), though causal genetic determinants (e.g., structural variants or eQTLs distinguishing PA and PG loci between `PA-dip` and `PG-dip`) are not mapped.
* **691 "dosage-effect" genes in tetraploids drive enhanced photosynthesis and cyclocaric acid B accumulation: [Fragile].**  
  Physiological measurements ($n=5$, chlorophyll, $P_n$, stomatal size) and UPLC-MS/MS confirm phenotypic differences between sampled tetraploid and diploid trees. However, standard RNA-seq normalization (FPKM/DESeq2 mapped solely to the `PA-dip` reference) measures relative transcript proportions rather than absolute per-cell dosage, and actually identifies 759 genes significantly higher in diploids vs. 691 higher in tetraploids. Moreover, the proposed CYP716A/CYP72A/CYP71A biosynthetic steps for cyclocaric acid B rely on homology rather than enzymatic characterization.
* **Population resequencing of 45 individuals reveals two Pliocene/Pleistocene bottlenecks and 25.88 Mb (diploid) / 165.18 Mb (tetraploid) under "purifying selection" via SweeD: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, SweeD computes composite likelihood ratios for *positive selective sweeps* (hitchhiking), not purifying selection (misstated in Results, line 111), and using an arbitrary top-5% threshold guarantees flagging 5% of any genome without neutral demographic calibration. Second, sequencing autotetraploids at ~10$\times$ total depth yields only ~2.5$\times$ coverage per chromosome copy, causing severe allele dropout under diploid GATK/SAMtools callers or when aligning to the 64-chromosome reference with a read-depth $\ge 5$ filter. Third, pooling strongly structured diploid and tetraploid clades ($K=2$) into one 45-sample SFS violates the panmixia assumption of Stairway Plot, generating spurious $N_e$ drops.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Mischaracterization of SweeD Selective Sweeps as "Purifying Selection" and Uncalibrated Top-5% Cutoff (Pillars 3, 4 & 5)
* **WHY:** In Section "Population genetics uncovers evolutionary history" (line 111), the manuscript states: *"we further analyzed selective sweeps based on the SweeD analysis in both diploid and auto-tetraploid genomes. A total of 25.88 Mb and 165.18 Mb genomic sequences were under purifying selection in diploid and tetraploid genomes, respectively."* SweeD (Pavlidis et al. 2013) implements the Kim & Stephan composite likelihood ratio test for **positive directional selection (selective sweeps)**, not purifying (background) selection. Furthermore, taking the top 5% of CLR windows on an autotetraploid genome where ~10$\times$ short reads were mapped across 64 phased chromosomes (~2.5$\times$ coverage per homologous chromosome, while filtering out SNPs with depth $<5$) introduces massive missing-data and allele-dropout artifacts into the SFS.
* **WHAT:** Correct "purifying selection" to "putative positive selective sweeps," address the read-depth mismatch on the 64-chromosome reference, and calibrate SweeD CLR thresholds against demographic null simulations.
* **HOW:** Map the 35 tetraploid resequencing samples to the monoploid `PA-dip` reference using polyploid-aware genotype likelihoods (`ANGSD` or `GATK -ploidy 4` on high-depth sites), or restrict SFS/sweep inference to the diploid population and calibrate the 99th percentile CLR threshold using `ms`/`msprime` neutral coalescent simulations parameterized by the inferred demographic history.

### 2. Violation of Panmixia in Stairway Plot Demographic Reconstruction and $K_s$ Dating Conflict (Pillars 3 & 6)
* **WHY:** PCA and ADMIXTURE (Fig. 4B–D) demonstrate a deep split ($K=2$) between the 10 diploid and 35 tetraploid individuals. Computing a single joint 45-individual site frequency spectrum in ANGSD for Stairway Plot (line 115) folds inter-ploidy population structure into the SFS (creating an excess of intermediate-frequency and fixed variants), which Stairway Plot mathematically misinterprets as ancient changes in effective population size ($N_e$). Additionally, dating `WGD2` to 11.2–10.5 MYA via $K_s$ (line 71) contradicts the meiotic quadrivalent behavior, 13-mer clustering, and close SNP tree proximity of `PA-tetra` to extant diploids.
* **WHAT:** Run Stairway Plot separately on the diploid subpopulation (and separately on the tetraploid subpopulation using polyploid-aware SFS estimation) rather than pooling structured populations, and reconcile the 11.2–10.5 MYA $K_s$ peak with autotetraploid origin timing.
* **HOW:** Re-estimate the unfolded SFS in ANGSD exclusively within the non-admixed diploid group (polarizing with *J. regia*) to verify whether the Pliocene (4.5–3.0 MYA) and Pleistocene (0.6–0.16 MYA) bottlenecks persist without ploidy admixture; clarify in the text that the `WGD2` $K_s$ peak reflects divergence among standing allelic/paralogous lineages rather than a 10-MYA reproductive isolation split.

### 3. Confounding of Relative Differential Expression (FPKM) with Ploidy "Dosage Effects" (Pillars 1, 3 & 5)
* **WHY:** In Section "Dosage effect contributes to enhanced photosynthesis..." (line 97), any gene with $\text{FC} \ge 2$ and $P \le 0.05$ between tetraploid and diploid leaves under standard DESeq2/FPKM normalization is labeled a "dosage-effect gene," even though 759 genes show higher relative expression in diploids vs. 691 in tetraploids. Because standard RNA-seq normalization scales libraries to equal total read counts, a uniform 2$\times$ genome-wide dosage increase yields $\text{FC} = 1.0$; the 691 and 759 DEGs simply represent trans-regulated differentially expressed genes between the sampled tetraploid and diploid genotypes.
* **WHAT:** Rename the 691 upregulated and 759 downregulated genes as "ploidy-associated differentially expressed genes (DEGs)" or quantify true allelic dosage across the four phased subgenomes of `PA-tetra`.
* **HOW:** Map the `PA-tetra` leaf RNA-seq reads to the 64-chromosome allele-defined `PA-tetra` reference (Table 1: genes with 1, 2, 3, and 4 retained alleles) and test whether total gene expression in `PA-tetra` scales monotonically with retained allelic copy number ($1\text{ vs. }2\text{ vs. }3\text{ vs. }4$ alleles).
