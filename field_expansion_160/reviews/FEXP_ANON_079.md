## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict
This manuscript attempts to infer causal effects of $594$ oral microbiome taxa ($309$ tongue dorsum and $285$ salivary taxa) on six autoimmune diseases (`RA`, `T1D`, `IBD`, `MS`, `SLE`, `SS`) using two-sample Mendelian Randomization. Forensically, the study violates the foundational population-homogeneity and exclusion-restriction assumptions of two-sample MR. First, the study commits a **trans-ethnic two-sample MR mismatch**: the oral microbiome exposure GWAS (`CNGBdb`, Liu et al. 2021, $N \approx 1,915\text{--}2,017$ Chinese participants from Shenzhen) is **100% East Asian**, whereas the autoimmune disease outcome GWAS datasets (`IEU OpenGWAS` / `GWAS Catalog` cohorts for `RA`, `T1D`, `IBD`, `MS`, `SLE`, and `SS`) are **100% European**, and LD clumping was performed via the `IEUGWAS` platform (`Section 2.3`, `Lines 42, 50`). Because linkage disequilibrium patterns, allele frequencies, and tag-SNP architectures differ substantially between East Asian and European populations—especially across immune and MHC loci—trans-ethnic two-sample MR using relaxed $P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$ tag-SNPs is invalid. Second, the headline associations (`Treponema A -> RA` at $P = 3.72 \times 10^{-9}$, `Leptotrichia massiliensis -> T1D` at $P = 3.50 \times 10^{-7}$, `Streptococcus -> IBD` at $P = 3.29 \times 10^{-6}$, and `Eikenella -> RA` at $P = 1.57 \times 10^{-5}$) are textbook artifacts of **horizontal pleiotropy at major autoimmune/HLA loci** combined with ultra-sparse instrument sets: the authors themselves admit in `Section 2.5` (`Line 68`) that several analyses were **"limited to two SNPs (insufficient for formal pleiotropy testing)"** where neither MR-Egger nor MR-PRESSO can run, and admit (`Lines 68, 97, 101`) that Open Targets Genetics directly flagged horizontal pleiotropy for `Leptotrichia massiliensis`, `Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae`, and `Streptococcus`—yet still report them as significant causal taxa in the Abstract and Results! Third, the Abstract (`Line 5`) and Results (`Line 81`) falsely label Benjamini–Hochberg $\text{FDR } q \le 0.10$ associations (`q = 0.073` to `0.083`) derived from $P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$ instruments as **"genome-wide significant causal effects"**. Finally, `Section 2.3` (`Lines 34–54`) prints both of its numbered methodological lists twice verbatim, and all cited main figures and tables (`Figures 1–3`, `Table 1`) are missing from the manuscript body.

* **Claim 1:** *"After multiple testing correction (q ≤ 0.1), our study identified four tongue dorsum and five salivary taxa with genome-wide significant causal effects on four ADs (IBD, MS, RA, and T1D)."* — **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** (First, associations passing a lenient $\text{FDR } q \le 0.10$ using $P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$ instruments are not "genome-wide significant"; second, the exposure GWAS is East Asian while the outcome GWASs are European; third, the authors admit in `Lines 68, 97, 101` that 3 of these 9 taxa—`Leptotrichia massiliensis`, `Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae`, and `Streptococcus`—exhibited horizontal pleiotropy in Open Targets Genetics and had only 2 SNPs).
* **Claim 2:** *"For T1D, the genus Campylobacter A (OR = 0.4539, 95% CI: 0.3046–0.6763, p = 9.86 × 10^-5, q = 0.073) and the species Leptotrichia massiliensis (OR = 0.5067, 95% CI: 0.3887–0.6606, p = 3.50 × 10^-7, q = 0.0005) were associated with a significantly reduced risk."* — **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** (In `Line 97`, the authors explicitly state that *"Due to potential horizontal pleiotropy identified for Leptotrichia massiliensis via Open Targets Genetics, its causal interpretation was excluded"*, yet they still present it as a headline protective causal taxon in `Abstract Line 5` and `Section 3.3 Line 88`).
* **Claim 3:** *"Regarding RA, our results indicated that the genus Eikenella (OR = 2.0086, 95% CI: 1.4666–2.7510, p = 1.57 × 10^-5, q = 0.0252) and the genus Treponema A (OR = 2.1322, 95% CI: 1.6626–2.7343, p = 3.72 × 10^-9, q = 1.18 × 10^-5) were significantly associated with an increased risk."* — **`[Fragile]`** (Derived from trans-ethnic East Asian-to-European MR with relaxed $P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$ instruments on an HLA-dominated autoimmune phenotype where 2-SNP IVW ratios cannot be tested for directional pleiotropy via MR-Egger or MR-PRESSO).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Major Methodological, Statistical, and Editorial Defects

1. **Unacknowledged Trans-Ethnic Two-Sample MR Mismatch (East Asian Exposure GWAS vs. European Outcome GWASs + European Clumping Reference; `Sections 2.2–2.3`, `Lines 26–54`):**
   * The oral microbiome exposure GWAS (`CNGBdb`, Liu et al. 2021; $2,017$ tongue dorsum and $1,915$ salivary samples) was conducted in an **East Asian (Chinese)** cohort (`Line 26`, `Line 129`), whereas the six autoimmune disease outcome datasets (`RA`, `SLE`, `SS`, `T1D`, `IBD`, `MS` from `IEU OpenGWAS` and `GWAS Catalog`) are **European-ancestry** cohorts.
   * Worse, in `Section 2.3` (`Lines 42, 50`), LD clumping ($r^2 < 0.001$, window $= 10,000\text{ kb}$) was executed through the `IEUGWAS` platform using its standard reference panel, and no ancestral harmonization check was performed until `Section 5` (`Line 129`), where the authors belatedly note as a limitation that *"all oral microbiome GWAS datasets were derived from Asian populations, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic groups"*—missing the fundamental methodological point that **mixing an East Asian exposure GWAS with European outcome GWASs inside a two-sample MR estimator violates the same-population assumption of two-sample MR**. Because sub-threshold $P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$ SNPs are rarely the causal variant itself, differences in LD structure between East Asians and Europeans invalidate the ratio estimator $\hat{\beta}_{ZY}^{\text{EUR}} / \hat{\beta}_{ZX}^{\text{EAS}}$.

2. **Two-SNP Instrument Sets and Confirmed Horizontal Pleiotropy Reported as Headline Causal Hits (`Section 2.5 Line 68`, `Sections 3.3–3.5 Lines 88–101`):**
   * Why do tiny oral microbiome sub-cohorts ($N \approx 1,915$) with relaxed $P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$ instruments yield extreme IVW $P$-values such as $3.72 \times 10^{-9}$ (`Treponema A -> RA`) and $3.50 \times 10^{-7}$ (`Leptotrichia massiliensis -> T1D`)?
   * In `Section 2.5` (`Line 68`), the authors reveal the cause:
     > *"For analyses limited to two SNPs (insufficient for formal pleiotropy testing), we manually queried the Open Targets Genetics database to evaluate potential confounding associations. Taxa linked to SNPs with documented pleiotropic effects (e.g., Leptotrichia massiliensis, Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae, and Streptococcus) were excluded from causal interpretation."*
   * When an exposure has **only 2 SNPs**, neither `MR-Egger` (which requires $\ge 3$ SNPs) nor `MR-PRESSO` (which requires $\ge 4$ SNPs) can be computed! If even one of those two $P < 10^{-5}$ SNPs lies in or near a major autoimmune susceptibility locus (such as the MHC/HLA region on chromosome 6p21 or `PTPN22`/`INS`/`IL23R`, where single variants have $P < 10^{-50}$ for T1D, RA, or IBD), the two-SNP IVW estimate simply reflects the massive primary autoimmune locus association divided by noise.
   * Worse, despite stating in `Line 68` that `Leptotrichia massiliensis`, `Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae`, and `Streptococcus` *"were excluded from causal interpretation"* due to confirmed horizontal pleiotropy, the authors **still count them among the "four tongue dorsum and five salivary taxa"** in the Abstract (`Line 5`) and `Section 3.2` (`Line 81`), and prominently report their ORs and $P$-values in `Sections 3.3–3.5` (`Lines 88, 92, 100`)!

3. **Misrepresentation of $\text{FDR } q \le 0.10$ Hits from $P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$ Instruments as "Genome-Wide Significant Causal Effects" (`Abstract Line 5`, `Section 3.2 Line 81`):**
   * Both `Abstract Line 5` and `Section 3.2 Line 81` claim: *"After multiple testing correction (q ≤ 0.1), our study identified four tongue dorsum and five salivary taxa with **genome-wide significant** causal effects on four ADs."*
   * Neither the instrument selection threshold ($P < 1 \times 10^{-5}$, `Line 41`) nor the MR multiple-testing threshold ($\text{FDR } q \le 0.10$, where `Campylobacter A` has $q = 0.073$, `Veillonella` has $q = 0.0742$, and `Bacteroidaceae F0040` has $q = 0.0829$) is "genome-wide significant" ($P < 5 \times 10^{-8}$).

4. **Verbatim Duplicated Text Blocks in `Section 2.3` and Missing Tables/Figures (`Lines 34–54`, `Lines 81–85`):**
   * In `Section 2.3` (`Lines 34–54`), the three numbered MR assumptions (`Lines 34–36` and `Lines 37–39`) and the six numbered IV selection criteria (`Lines 41–46` and `Lines 49–54`) are **printed twice in a row verbatim**—a glaring copy-paste editing failure.
   * Furthermore, `Section 3.2` (`Lines 81–85`) cites `Figure 1`, `Figure 2`, `Figure 3`, and `Table 1`, none of which are present in the manuscript text, and `Section 2.2` (`Line 28`) never provides the GWAS Catalog / IEU OpenGWAS accession IDs or case/control sample sizes for any of the six autoimmune disease outcomes in the main text.
