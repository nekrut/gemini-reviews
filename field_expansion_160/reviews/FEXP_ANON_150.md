## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study purports to train three machine learning models—Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)—on 3,092 natural compounds from the Selleckchem library (2,164 training, 928 testing) to predict glycogen synthase kinase-3 beta (GSK3$\beta$) inhibition. Consensus "active" predictions across all three models (181 compounds) are docked into GSK3$\beta$ (PDB ID: `8AUZ`) via PyRx/AutoDock Vina, identifying artemisinin (`CID 68827`, $-8.6\text{ kcal/mol}$), chrysin ($-8.5\text{ kcal/mol}$), dihydroartemisinin ($-8.3\text{ kcal/mol}$), costunolide ($-8.2\text{ kcal/mol}$), and daidzein ($-8.1\text{ kcal/mol}$). Finally, the artemisinin–GSK3$\beta$ complex is evaluated via a 100 ns GROMACS MD simulation and MM-PBSA calculation.

**Editorial Verdict: Reject (Fundamentally Invalid, 1.8 / 10.0).** This manuscript exhibits fatal mathematical impossibilities in its machine learning performance tables, circular heuristic label generation with direct target leakage, and physically impossible molecular dynamics metrics. First, in Table 2, the reported classification `Accuracy` values violate the fundamental laws of probability: for any binary classifier, overall Accuracy is a convex combination of Sensitivity (Recall) and Specificity ($\text{Acc} = \pi\cdot\text{Sens} + (1-\pi)\cdot\text{Spec}$) and **must** lie strictly between Sensitivity and Specificity. Yet Table 2 reports RF Training Accuracy = $88.40\%$ and Test Accuracy = $83.60\%$ when Sensitivity = $85\%$ and Specificity = $87\%$ in both splits (outside the $[85\%, 87\%]$ interval in both directions!), SVM Training Accuracy = $72.50\%$ when Sensitivity = $64\%$ and Specificity = $71\%$, and KNN Training Accuracy = $80.10\%$ when Sensitivity = $76\%$ and Specificity = $78\%$. Moreover, Precision, Recall, Specificity, and F1-Score are reported as 100% identical to two decimal places between the Training and Test sets for all three algorithms. Second, Section 2.2 (line 51) admits that **no experimental GSK3$\beta$ bioactivity labels existed** in the dataset (labels were generated by property heuristics), while line 55 simultaneously claims that **`pIC50` was the top-ranked input feature** used by the Random Forest classifier (direct target leakage). Third, the MD trajectory of GSK3$\beta$ (a ~42 kDa globular kinase whose physical radius of gyration is $\sim 2.2\text{ nm}$ and SASA is $\sim 175\text{ nm}^2$) is reported with an unphysical $R_g$ of $5.5\text{--}5.9\text{ nm}$, a SASA of $1,500\text{--}2,000\text{ nm}^2$ ($10\times$ too large), and a backbone RMSD of $1.38\text{ nm}$ ($13.8\text{ \AA}$).

### Specific Conclusions and Assessment of Support

* **Random Forest outperforms SVM and KNN in predicting GSK3$\beta$ inhibitors, achieving 88.4% training accuracy, 83.6% test accuracy, 85% sensitivity, and 87% specificity: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  It is mathematically impossible for binary classification accuracy ($\text{TP}+\text{TN})/(P+N)$ to be $88.40\%$ or $83.60\%$ when Sensitivity ($\text{TP}/P = 0.85$) and Specificity ($\text{TN}/N = 0.87$) are $85\%$ and $87\%$, because any weighted average of $0.85$ and $0.87$ with non-negative weights summing to 1 must lie in $[0.85, 0.87]$. The same mathematical impossibility occurs in SVM (Train) ($\text{Acc} = 72.50\% > \max(64\%, 71\%)$) and KNN (Train) ($\text{Acc} = 80.10\% > \max(76\%, 78\%)$). Furthermore, all four secondary metrics (Precision, Recall, Specificity, F1) are identical between Train and Test sets across all three models in Table 2.

* **Feature selection via RFE-RF identified `pIC50`, MW, ALogP, HBA, and McGowan Volume as the most informative predictors of GSK3$\beta$ potency across 3,092 natural compounds: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Line 51 explicitly states: *"As no experimentally validated GSK3$\beta$ inhibitor labels were included, active/inactive compound definitions were derived through property-driven heuristics."* Line 49 titles Figure 1 *"Synthetic QSAR feature scatter plot."* Yet line 55 states that `pIC50` was the #1 input predictor feature in the RF model, and Figure 3d plots *"predicted vs. actual pIC50 values."* Training a classifier on heuristic property thresholds and using `pIC50` as an input feature $X$ constitutes circular data leakage, and screening the exact same 3,092 Selleckchem compounds on which the models were trained/tested (line 117) violates train/deployment separation.

* **100 ns MD simulations and MM-PBSA calculations confirm that artemisinin forms a compact, stable complex with GSK3$\beta$ ($R_g = 5.5\text{ nm}$, $\text{SASA} = 1500\text{ nm}^2$, $\Delta G = -316.13\text{ kJ/mol}$ at pH 8.0): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  A single GSK3$\beta$ catalytic domain (~360–420 amino acids) has a physical radius of gyration of $\sim 2.15\text{ nm}$ and a SASA of $\sim 170\text{ nm}^2$. The reported $R_g = 5.5\text{--}5.9\text{ nm}$, $\text{SASA} = 1,500\text{--}2,000\text{ nm}^2$, backbone $\text{RMSD} = 1.38\text{ nm}$ ($13.8\text{ \AA}$), and $\text{RMSF}$ peaks of $1.6\text{ nm}$ ($16\text{ \AA}$) are physically incompatible with a folded monomeric GSK3$\beta$ domain. Additionally, artemisinin has zero ionizable functional groups and zero H-bond donors ($\text{C}_{15}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_5$), making its purported $-86.20\text{ kJ/mol}$ electrostatic energy and massive pH-dependent binding shift ($-316.13\text{ kJ/mol}$ at pH 8.0) physically implausible.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Mathematical Impossibility in Table 2 Classification Metrics ($\text{Accuracy} \notin [\text{Sensitivity}, \text{Specificity}]$ and Train = Test Duplication)
* **Location:** Section 2.3, Table 2 (lines 63–71), and Table 3 (lines 73–78).
* **Defect:**
  1. By the law of total probability, $\text{Accuracy} = \frac{\text{TP}+\text{TN}}{P+N} = \frac{P}{P+N}\text{Sensitivity} + \frac{N}{P+N}\text{Specificity}$. Thus, $\min(\text{Sens}, \text{Spec}) \le \text{Accuracy} \le \max(\text{Sens}, \text{Spec})$. In Table 2:
     - **SVM (Train):** $\text{Sens} = 0.64$, $\text{Spec} = 0.71$, but $\text{Accuracy} = 72.50\% > 71.0\%$ (impossible).
     - **RF (Train):** $\text{Sens} = 0.85$, $\text{Spec} = 0.87$, but $\text{Accuracy} = 88.40\% > 87.0\%$ (impossible).
     - **RF (Test):** $\text{Sens} = 0.85$, $\text{Spec} = 0.87$, but $\text{Accuracy} = 83.60\% < 85.0\%$ (impossible).
     - **KNN (Train):** $\text{Sens} = 0.76$, $\text{Spec} = 0.78$, but $\text{Accuracy} = 80.10\% > 78.0\%$ (impossible).
  2. Every single value of `Precision`, `Recall (Sensitivity)`, `Specificity`, and `F1-Score` in Table 2 is identical between the `(Train)` and `(Test)` columns for SVM (`0.62, 0.64, 0.71, 0.63`), RF (`0.84, 0.85, 0.87, 0.845`), and KNN (`0.75, 0.76, 0.78, 0.755`), even though Train and Test accuracies differ by $3.8\text{--}4.8\%$.
  3. **KNN AUC contradiction:** Table 2, Table 3, and line 98 report KNN `AUC-ROC = 0.837 ± 0.014`, whereas the caption to Figure 4c (line 101) reports `AUC of 0.736`.

### 2. Admission of Missing Experimental Bioactivity Labels Combined with `pIC50` Target Leakage
* **Location:** Section 2.2 (lines 49–55), Section 2.5 (line 117), and Section 3 (line 180).
* **Defect:**
  1. Line 51 states: *"As no experimentally validated GSK3$\beta$ inhibitor labels were included, active/inactive compound definitions were derived through property-driven heuristics."* Figure 1 is explicitly captioned *"Synthetic QSAR feature scatter plot."* Yet Section 3 (line 180) contradicts this by claiming *"we trained these three models using a dataset of known GSK3$\beta$ inhibitors"*, and line 55 lists `pIC50` as one of the five PaDEL/RFE input features used to predict potency. Using `pIC50` as an input predictor $X$ is direct target leakage, and training a model to recover heuristic physicochemical thresholds (`ALogP`, `HBA`, `McGowan Volume`) from those exact same descriptors is circular.
  2. In Section 2.5 (line 117), the trained models are applied to screen the *exact same* 3,092 Selleckchem compounds used as the $2164 + 928 = 3092$ training and test set (70% training-set overlap).

### 3. Physically Impossible Biomolecular Dimensions in MD Trajectory Analysis ($R_g$, SASA, RMSD, RMSF)
* **Location:** Sections 2.6.1–2.6.4 (lines 140–167) and Figure 7.
* **Defect:** For the GSK3$\beta$–artemisinin complex (PDB `8AUZ`), the authors report:
  - Backbone $\text{RMSD} = 1.38 \pm 0.07\text{ nm}$ ($13.8\text{ \AA}$) and complex $\text{RMSD} = 1.45 \pm 0.06\text{ nm}$ ($14.5\text{ \AA}$) (line 142–146).
  - Per-residue $\text{RMSF}$ ranging from $0.2\text{ nm}$ to $1.6\text{ nm}$ ($16\text{ \AA}$) (line 154).
  - Radius of gyration $R_g$ starting at $5.9\text{ nm}$ ($59\text{ \AA}$) and stabilizing at $5.5\text{--}5.6\text{ nm}$ ($55\text{--}56\text{ \AA}$) (line 161).
  - Solvent-accessible surface area ($\text{SASA}$) starting at $2,000\text{ nm}^2$ ($200,000\text{ \AA}^2$) and dropping to $1,500\text{ nm}^2$ ($150,000\text{ \AA}^2$) (line 166).
  A monomeric GSK3$\beta$ kinase domain (~350–420 residues) has $R_g \approx 2.15\text{ nm}$ and $\text{SASA} \approx 170\text{ nm}^2$; the reported $R_g$ ($5.6\text{ nm}$) and $\text{SASA}$ ($1500\text{--}2000\text{ nm}^2$) are off by factors of $\sim 2.6\times$ and $\sim 10\times$, respectively, and a $25\%$ SASA collapse ($2000 \to 1500\text{ nm}^2$) with a $13.8\text{ \AA}$ backbone RMSD is inconsistent with a native folded crystal structure.

### 4. Internal Contradictions in Methods ("181 Unidentified Inulin Derivatives", RF Hyperparameters, and Water Models)
* **Location:** Section 2.3.2 (line 90) vs. Section 4.3.2 (second chunk, line 20); Section 4.6 (second chunk, line 60); Section 4.7 vs. Section 4.8 (second chunk, lines 65 vs. 70).
* **Defect:**
  1. **Unexplained "inulin derivatives" in Section 4.6:** Line 60 (second chunk) states: *"Molecular docking was conducted to explore the binding interactions of 181 unidentified inulin derivatives with GSK3$\beta$ (PDB ID: 8AUZ)."* Nowhere else in the paper is inulin (a fructan polysaccharide) mentioned; Section 2.5 claims the 181 compounds were diverse Selleckchem natural products (sesquiterpenes and flavonoids).
  2. **RF hyperparameter mismatch:** Section 2.3.2 (line 90) reports RF optimization with `n_estimators = 200, max_depth = 10`, whereas Section 4.3.2 (second chunk, line 20) reports `ntree = 500 trees and mtry = M/3`.
  3. **MD water model mismatch:** Section 4.7 (second chunk, line 65) states the 100 ns GROMOS96 54a7 simulation used the **SPC** water model, whereas Section 4.8 (line 70) states the exact same trajectory used a **TIP3P** water box.
  4. **Missing equations in Methods:** Equations in Sections 4.3.1 (line 13), 4.3.2 (line 22), 4.3.3 (line 31), and 4.4 (line 39) are completely blank in the text while their variables ($w, C, \xi_i, T_i(X), N, y_i, \text{TP}, \text{TN}$) are defined underneath.
