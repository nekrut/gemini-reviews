## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict
This manuscript uses univariable and multivariable Mendelian randomization (MVMR) to test whether genetically predicted serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D (`25(OH)D`) levels protect against laryngeal cancer (`ieu-b-4913`) independently of smoking pack-years and weekly alcohol consumption. While incorporating MVMR to adjust for smoking and alcohol is conceptually appropriate for head and neck cancer epidemiology, forensic audit uncovers three severe methodological and mathematical defects that distort the reported effect sizes, instrument strength metrics, and reverse MR results:

1. **Unrecognized Linear Probability Model (BOLT-LMM) Scale Distortion (`OR = 0.9993`) and Nearly 100% UK Biobank Sample Overlap:** The authors report that higher serum `25(OH)D` is associated with a statistically significant reduction in laryngeal cancer risk with an odds ratio of **$\text{OR} = 0.9993$ ($95\%\text{ CI } 0.9987\text{–}0.9999, P = 0.019$)** in univariable IVW and **$\text{OR} = 0.9992$ ($95\%\text{ CI } 0.9986\text{–}0.9998, P = 0.008$)** in MVMR (`Table 2`, `Figure 2`). They then spend multiple paragraphs in the Popular Summary (line 11), Discussion (line 119), and Conclusion (line 125) puzzling over why the protective effect size is minuscule ($\text{OR} = 0.9993$, i.e., a $0.07\%$ relative change). In reality, `ieu-b-4913` is a UK Biobank GWAS analyzed with a **linear mixed model (`BOLT-LMM`) on a binary $0/1$ case-control trait** where there are only **273 laryngeal cancer cases out of 372,289 individuals** (case prevalence $\mu = 273 / 372,289 = 0.0007333$). Because `BOLT-LMM` outputs effects on the raw linear probability scale ($\beta_{\text{linear}}$) rather than the logistic log-odds scale, exponentiating $\beta_{\text{linear}} = -0.0007$ directly yields $\exp(-0.0007) = 0.9993$, which is **not** a true odds ratio! Transforming from the linear probability scale to the log-odds scale via $\beta_{\text{log-odds}} \approx \beta_{\text{linear}} / [\mu(1 - \mu)] = -0.0007 / 0.0007328 \approx -0.955$ yields an actual approximate odds ratio of $\text{OR} \approx \exp(-0.955) \approx \mathbf{0.38}$. Furthermore, the exposure (`ebi-a-GCST90000618`, UK Biobank $N = 496,946$), outcome (`ieu-b-4913`, UK Biobank $N = 372,289$), and smoking covariate (`ukb-b-10831`, UK Biobank $N = 142,387$) are all derived from **UK Biobank**, creating nearly 100% sample overlap—directly contradicting the authors' claim in the Discussion (line 123) that *"the overlap of the samples between exposure and outcome in our study is unknown."*
2. **Mathematically Impossible $R^2$ Values ($70\%$ and $80\%$ of Variance Explained) in Table 2 and Duplicated Sample Sizes in Table 1:** In `Table 2` (lines 76–77), the authors report that 92 SNPs explain **$R^2 = 0.7$ ($70\%$)** of the phenotypic variance in serum 25(OH)D, and that 89 SNPs (after removing 3 SNPs!) explain **$R^2 = 0.8$ ($80\%$)** of the variance in serum 25(OH)D. First, removing 3 SNPs from an instrument set cannot *increase* the cumulative variance explained from $0.7$ to $0.8$. Second, all genome-wide significant SNPs for serum 25(OH)D in UK Biobank explain roughly $3\%\text{–}8\%$ ($R^2 \approx 0.03\text{–}0.08$, or perhaps $0.7\%\text{–}0.8\%$ after specific filtering), never $70\%\text{–}80\%$. Third, plugging $N = 496,946$, $k = 92$, and $R^2 = 0.7$ into the authors' own $F$-statistic formula on line 57 ($F = \frac{N - k - 1}{k} \times \frac{R^2}{1 - R^2}$) yields $F = \frac{496,853}{92} \times \frac{0.7}{0.3} = \mathbf{12,601.5}$—which contradicts the $F = 32.974$ reported in the adjacent column of `Table 2`! Conversely, $F = 32.974$ corresponds to $R^2 = \frac{92 \times 32.974}{496,853 + 92 \times 32.974} = \mathbf{0.00607}$ ($0.61\%$), proving that the authors confused percentages ($0.6\text{–}0.8\%$) with proportions ($0.7\text{–}0.8$). In addition, `Table 1` (lines 50, 52) copies the sample size (`496,946` and `335,394`) into the `Number of SNPs` column for two of the four datasets.
3. **Mislabeled and Undocumented Reverse MR Analysis (`Table 3`):** In `Table 3` (lines 99–108), the authors test laryngeal cancer as the exposure (`nSNP = 12`) and continuous serum 25(OH)D as the outcome. Although the column header is labeled `Beta (95% CI)`, the reported values (`8.8656 [0.0484–1624.4057]`, `10.1728 [0.0828–1249.1583]`, `0.7701 [0.0001–8004.5739]`) are strictly positive numbers spanning seven orders of magnitude—demonstrating that the authors exponentiated the linear regression slopes (`exp(beta)`) on a continuous outcome and mislabeled them as `Beta`. Moreover, the Methods never explain what $P$-value threshold was used to extract 12 SNPs from a GWAS with only 273 cases (`ieu-b-4913` has zero genome-wide significant SNPs at $P < 5 \times 10^{-8}$), nor why the MR-Egger row in `Table 3` is blank (`-`).

### Specific Conclusions and Assessment of Support
* **Claim 1:** Higher genetically predicted serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D levels are associated with lower laryngeal cancer risk both before ($\text{OR} = 0.9993, P = 0.019$) and after ($\text{OR} = 0.9992, P = 0.008$) multivariable MR adjustment for smoking pack-years and weekly alcohol intake (`Results`, pp. 5–6, lines 69–91; `Table 2`; `Figure 2`). **`[Fragile]`** — While the directional association ($P = 0.019$ univariable, $P = 0.008$ MVMR) is consistent across IVW, MR-Egger ($P = 0.029$), weighted median ($P = 0.036$), and weighted mode ($P = 0.032$), the outcome GWAS contains only 273 cases in 372,289 UK Biobank participants (100% sample overlap with the UK Biobank vitamin D exposure GWAS), and the reported $\text{OR} = 0.9993$ is a raw exponentiated linear probability slope (`BOLT-LMM`) rather than a valid logistic odds ratio.
* **Claim 2:** The protective effect of serum 25(OH)D on laryngeal cancer is clinically very small because $\text{OR} = 0.9993$ (`Popular Summary`, line 11; `Discussion`, line 119; `Conclusion`, line 125). **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** — This interpretation rests on mistaking a linear probability risk difference of $-0.07\%$ per SD on a rare binary outcome (prevalence $0.073\%$) for an odds ratio; on the true log-odds scale, a $-0.0007$ risk difference corresponds to an approximate $\text{OR} \approx 0.38$.
* **Claim 3:** Reverse Mendelian randomization confirms that laryngeal cancer has no causal effect on serum 25(OH)D levels (`Results`, p. 7, lines 97–108; `Table 3`). **`[Methodologically Flawed]`** — Conducted with 12 undocumented SNPs extracted from an underpowered 273-case linear-model GWAS, with exponentiated values (`0.0001` to `8004.57`) mislabeled as `Beta (95% CI)` on a continuous outcome and MR-Egger omitted.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Untransformed BOLT-LMM Linear Probability Slopes Reported as Odds Ratios (`OR = 0.9993`) and Unacknowledged 100% UK Biobank Sample Overlap
* **Severity:** Critical
* **Location:** `Popular Summary` (line 11), `Materials and methods` (lines 43–53), `Results` (lines 69–91, `Table 2`, `Figure 2`), and `Discussion` (lines 119, 123).
* **WHY:** When a binary disease outcome with ultra-low case prevalence ($\mu = 273 / 372,289 = 0.000733$) is analyzed by linear mixed modeling (`BOLT-LMM`, as in MRC-IEU UK Biobank pipeline `ieu-b-4913`), the SNP regression coefficient $\beta_{\text{LMM}}$ measures the absolute change in case probability ($0$ to $1$), which scales linearly with $\mu(1 - \mu)$. Exponentiating $\beta_{\text{LMM}}$ directly yields $\exp(\beta_{\text{LMM}}) \approx 1 + \beta_{\text{LMM}} \approx 0.9993$, which is not a logistic odds ratio and cannot be interpreted as a $0.07\%$ relative risk change.
* **WHAT:** Throughout the manuscript (`Abstract`, `Popular Summary`, `Table 2`, `Figure 2`, `Discussion`), the authors report $\text{OR} = 0.9993$ ($95\%\text{ CI } 0.9987\text{–}0.9999$) and write: *"Although this result suggests that the protective effect of serum 25(OH)D levels against LC may be small..."* (line 11). Furthermore, on line 123 they state: *"Fifth, the overlap of the samples between exposure and outcome in our study is unknown, which can lead to weak instrumental bias."* Yet `Table 1` shows that `ebi-a-GCST90000618` ($N = 496,946$ UK Biobank Europeans), `ieu-b-4913` ($N = 372,289$ UK Biobank Europeans), and `ukb-b-10831` ($N = 142,387$ UK Biobank Europeans) are all UK Biobank cohorts, meaning sample overlap is virtually $100\%$.
* **HOW:** Transform the `BOLT-LMM` linear scale effect estimates and standard errors of `ieu-b-4913` to the log-odds scale using $\hat{\beta}_{\text{log-odds}} = \frac{\hat{\beta}_{\text{LMM}}}{\mu(1 - \mu)}$ (where $\mu = 273 / 372,289 = 0.0007333$) before computing odds ratios, and explicitly quantify potential winner's curse / sample-overlap bias across the shared UK Biobank cohort or replicate in an independent non-UKB head-and-neck cancer GWAS (e.g., FinnGen).

### Defect 2: Mathematically Impossible $R^2$ Values (`0.7` and `0.8`) in Table 2 and Corrupted SNP Counts in Table 1
* **Severity:** Major
* **Location:** `Table 1` (lines 48–53), `Materials and methods` (line 57), and `Table 2` (lines 74–78).
* **WHY:** Under the formula $F = \frac{N - k - 1}{k} \times \frac{R^2}{1 - R^2}$ (line 57), $R^2$, $N$, $k$, and $F$ are deterministically linked, and removing instruments ($k = 92 \to 89$) cannot increase cumulative $R^2$.
* **WHAT:**
  1. In `Table 2` (lines 76–77), row 1 (`nSNP = 92`) reports $R^2 = 0.7$ and $F = 32.974$, while row 2 (`nSNP = 89`, after removing 3 SNPs) reports $R^2 = 0.8$ and $F = 47.768$. Solving $F = \frac{N - k - 1}{k} \frac{R^2}{1 - R^2}$ for $R^2$ given $N = 496,946$, $k = 92$, and $F = 32.974$ yields $R^2 = \mathbf{0.00607}$ ($0.61\%$), and for $k = 89, F = 47.768$ yields $R^2 = \mathbf{0.00848}$ ($0.85\%$). Even then, 89 SNPs Subset from 92 SNPs cannot have a larger sum of $R^2$ unless $R^2$ was miscalculated, and reporting $0.7$ and $0.8$ (i.e., $70\%$ and $80\%$ of phenotypic variance) is off by two orders of magnitude.
  2. In `Table 1` (lines 50, 52), the column `Number of SNPs` for `ebi-a-GCST90000618` and `ieu-b-73` is printed as `496,946` and `335,394`—which are the exact numbers from the `Sample size` column rather than the number of genotyped/imputed SNPs in those GWAS datasets.
* **HOW:** Recompute cumulative $R^2 = \sum_{j=1}^k 2 \text{EAF}_j (1 - \text{EAF}_j) \beta_j^2$ accurately as a proportion ($\sim 0.006\text{–}0.008$ or $3\text{–}5\%$ depending on standardization), explain why the 89-SNP set has a higher mean $F$-statistic than the 92-SNP set, and report the true total SNP counts in `Table 1`.

### Defect 3: Corrupted Reverse MR Table (`Table 3`) Reporting Exponentiated Values as `Beta` and Omitting Instrument Selection Criteria
* **Severity:** Major
* **Location:** `Results` (lines 97–108, `Table 3`) and `Materials and methods` (lines 55–57).
* **WHY:** In reverse MR where continuous serum 25(OH)D (`ebi-a-GCST90000618`) is the outcome, the causal effect parameter is a linear regression slope $\beta \in (-\infty, +\infty)$ centered at $0$ under the null, not an odds ratio centered at $1$.
* **WHAT:** In `Table 3`, the column is titled `Beta (95% CI)`, yet the values for IVW, Weighted median, Simple mode, and Weighted mode are `8.8656 (0.0484–1624.4057)`, `10.1728 (0.0828–1249.1583)`, `0.7701 (0.0001–8004.5739)`, and `1.4487 (0.0014–1536.3406)`—which are exponentiated $\exp(\hat{\beta}_{\text{reverse}})$ values! Taking natural logarithms recovers the actual $\hat{\beta}$ values: $\ln(8.8656) = +2.182$ ($\text{SE} = 2.658, Z = 0.821, P = 0.412$). Moreover, `MR Egger` is left completely blank (`-`) without explanation, and the Methods never disclose what relaxed $P$-value cutoff was used to select 12 SNPs for laryngeal cancer (`ieu-b-4913`), nor whether those 12 SNPs were transformed from the `BOLT-LMM` scale before running reverse MR.
* **HOW:** Report true unexponentiated $\hat{\beta}$ (SD change in 25(OH)D per unit log-odds of laryngeal cancer) in `Table 3`, document the $P$-value threshold and $F$-statistics for the 12 laryngeal cancer SNPs, and explain why MR-Egger was omitted.
