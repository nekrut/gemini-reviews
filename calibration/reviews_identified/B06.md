### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript experimentally tests whether site-specific thermodynamic amino acid preferences are conserved over billions of years of protein evolution or erased by epistatic coevolution ("evolutionary Stokes shift"), using laboratory-resurrected Precambrian thioredoxins (dating back to the Last Bacterial Common Ancestor, LBCA, ~4 Ga, 55% sequence identity to *Escherichia coli*) and class A $\beta$-lactamases (dating back to PNCA, ~3 Ga, 37–59% sequence identity to *E. coli* TEM-1 and *Bacillus licheniformis*). Using differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), X-ray crystallography (PDB 4ULX for LPBCA L90K thioredoxin), and 23 replicate 15-day stationary-phase competition assays in *E. coli*, the authors evaluate three datasets: (1) 21 chemically conservative mutations ($I\leftrightarrow V$ and $E\leftrightarrow D$) across *E. coli* and LBCA thioredoxins, finding a strong correlation in unfolding energetics ($r = 0.89, \text{slope} = 1.03 \pm 0.21, P = 9 \times 10^{-8}$, with 18/21 site preference rankings conserved; Figs. 3–4); (2) the chemically radical L90K/K90L exchange across four thioredoxin nodes, showing that leucine is always thermodynamically favored over lysine via a conserved local structural switch that buries Leu and exposes Lys (Fig. 4B, Fig. 10), even though extant *E. coli* retains Lys90 due to an organismal fitness advantage in stationary phase (22/23 competitions won by wild-type over K90L; Fig. 8); and (3) the clinical global suppressor M182T/T182M across modern and Precambrian $\beta$-lactamases, demonstrating that Thr182 (which forms a canonical N-cap hydrogen bond) is universally favored by ~5–7 °C over Met182 (Figs. 6, 9).

The calorimetric and crystallographic experiments are executed with high biophysical rigor, including concentration-dependence, scan-rate, and reversibility controls (Supplementary Tables S2–S3) and direct structural confirmation of the L90K conformational switch (PDB 4ULX). However, the evolutionary generalization in the title and abstract—that site-specific amino acid preferences remain broadly conserved throughout evolutionary history despite local sequence divergence—rests on a non-random sample of mutations: 21 conservative methyl/methylene changes ($I\leftrightarrow V, E\leftrightarrow D$) plus two targeted case studies (position 90 in thioredoxin and the known global suppressor position 182 in $\beta$-lactamase). Furthermore, in the 21-mutation dataset, partitioning the mutations into the 14 sites where the ancestral and extant wild-type residue is identical (blue points) versus the 7 sites where the wild-type residue actually substituted between LBCA and *E. coli* (red points) reveals a systematic thermodynamic shift favoring the extant residue in *E. coli* ($\Delta\Delta\Delta G$ in Fig. 3B), and ancestral sequence reconstruction uncertainty (maximum posterior state vs. alternative ancestral sequences) is not explicitly tested for the targeted mutations. These scope and statistical analysis issues can be addressed through stratified statistical tests on the existing calorimetric data and careful framing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Thermodynamic effects ($\Delta\Delta G$ and $\Delta T_m$) of 21 conservative $I\leftrightarrow V$ and $E\leftrightarrow D$ mutations are strongly correlated ($r = 0.89$) between extant *E. coli* and resurrected LBCA thioredoxins: [Well Supported].**  
  DSC measurements across 21 variants in both backgrounds show high linear concordance ($r = 0.89, P = 9 \times 10^{-8}$, Fig. 3A), with 18 of 21 binary preference rankings conserved across ~4 billion years (45% sequence divergence; Fig. 4A), and the three sign reversals (positions 4, 60, 61) occurring only at sites with near-zero $\Delta\Delta G$.

* **Conservation of the energetic preference for Leu over Lys at thioredoxin position 90 is mediated by a reversible local structural switch, while Lys90 persists in *E. coli* due to an in vivo fitness advantage: [Well Supported].**  
  DSC profiles across LBCA, LPBCA, LGPCA, and *E. coli* confirm that Leu90 is always more stabilizing than Lys90 (by $\Delta T_m \approx 4\text{--}15\,^\circ\text{C}$, Fig. 4B). The newly solved crystal structure of LPBCA-L90K (PDB 4ULX, Fig. 10B) directly captures the conformational rearrangement that exposes Lys90 to solvent, and 23 independent 15-day batch competition assays in $\Delta trxA \Delta trxC$ *E. coli* confirm that wild-type Lys90 outcompetes the hyperstabilized K90L variant in 22/23 replicates ($P < 10^{-5}$, Fig. 8).

* **The clinical TEM-1 $\beta$-lactamase global suppressor M182T represents a reversion to the ancestral, thermodynamically preferred N-cap threonine residue: [Well Supported].**  
  Across three Precambrian nodes (PNCA, GNCA, GPBCA) and two divergent modern $\beta$-lactamases (*E. coli* TEM-1 and *B. licheniformis*, 37% identity), T182 is consistently more stable than M182 by $\Delta T_m \approx 5\text{--}7\,^\circ\text{C}$ (Fig. 6B–C), and crystal structures of the ancestral enzymes confirm the canonical Ncap–N3 hydrogen bond formed by Thr182 (Fig. 9).

* **Site-specific amino acid preferences are generally conserved throughout evolutionary history, with evolutionary Stokes shifts insufficient to erase ancestral rankings: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While supported for the 21 conservative $I/V$ and $E/D$ exchanges and the two specific focal sites (Trx 90 and Bla 182), extrapolating this to general proteome-wide preference conservation has three caveats: (1) 21 of the 23 tested sites examine only single-methylene/methyl shifts ($I\leftrightarrow V, E\leftrightarrow D$) that preserve charge and polarity; (2) in Fig. 3B, 17 of the 21 mutations have positive $\Delta\Delta\Delta G$ (more destabilizing in *E. coli* than in LBCA when measured in the *E. coli* wild-type direction), and at the 7 red sites where the amino acid actually substituted between LBCA and *E. coli*, 4 of 7 sites still prefer the *ancestral* LBCA residue (Fig. 4A, red letters at positions 23, 43, 51, 75), raising the question of whether Bayesian ML ancestral reconstruction under time-homogeneous substitution models favors consensus-stabilizing residues at ancestral nodes; and (3) at Trx position 90, the energetic gap shrinks from $\sim 15\,^\circ\text{C}$ in LBCA to $\sim 4\,^\circ\text{C}$ in *E. coli* (Fig. 4B), demonstrating a massive (~11 °C) Stokes-shift/permissive adjustment that compensates >70% of the initial destabilization.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Separately Quantify Stokes-Shift Adjustment at Substituted (Red) vs. Unchanged (Blue) Sites in the 21-Site Dataset
* **WHY:** In Fig. 3A–B, the 14 unchanged sites (blue, same amino acid in *E. coli* and LBCA) and the 7 substituted sites (red, different amino acid in *E. coli* and LBCA) are pooled to compute the overall correlation ($r = 0.89$) and visual shift ($\Delta\Delta\Delta G$). Under the evolutionary Stokes shift hypothesis, epistatic adjustment to a new occupant should specifically occur at the 7 red sites where the resident amino acid changed, whereas blue sites experienced no change in resident side chain.
* **WHAT:** Perform a formal statistical test comparing the mean and distribution of $\Delta\Delta\Delta G$ (and $\Delta\Delta T_m$) between the 7 substituted (red) sites and the 14 conserved-occupant (blue) sites, and report the separate Pearson/Spearman correlations and slopes for both subsets.
* **HOW:** Using the data in Supplementary Table S1 and Fig. 3B, compute two-sample Wilcoxon and $t$-tests on $\Delta\Delta\Delta G = \Delta\Delta G_{\text{E.coli}} - \Delta\Delta G_{\text{LBCA}}$ (oriented both in the *E. coli* WT$\to$mutant direction and in the fixed $I\to V / E\to D$ chemical direction) for the $n=7$ red vs. $n=14$ blue sites, and report what fraction of the ancestral energetic preference is eroded at substituted sites versus conserved sites.

#### 2. Address Potential Ancestral Sequence Reconstruction (ASR) Consensus Bias on Inferred Preference Conservation
* **WHY:** Maximum-likelihood and Bayesian ancestral sequence reconstruction using site-homogeneous empirical matrices is known to exhibit a bias toward choosing the most globally frequent (and therefore thermodynamically stabilizing "consensus") amino acid at uncertain ancestral sites. In Fig. 4A and Fig. 7, at 4 of the 7 substituted $I/V$ and $E/D$ sites (plus Trx 90 and Bla 182), the ancestral LBCA residue is more stabilizing even in the modern *E. coli* protein—which is the hallmark of a consensus-stabilizing residue.
* **WHAT:** Report the posterior probabilities of the inferred ancestral states at all 21 thioredoxin sites, position 90, and $\beta$-lactamase position 182 across the targeted Precambrian nodes, and compare the ancestral states against the simple alignment consensus of extant sequences.
* **HOW:** Add columns to Supplementary Table S1 (or a new summary table) listing for each mutated position: (1) the marginal posterior probability of the resurrected ancestral residue at LBCA/LPBCA/LGPCA/PNCA/GNCA/GPBCA, (2) the frequency of the ancestral vs. extant residue across the modern alignment (200 thioredoxins, 75 $\beta$-lactamases), and (3) whether the conserved energetic preference corresponds to the global alignment consensus residue.

#### 3. Quantify the Rate of Energetic Adjustment Along the Position 90 Trajectory
* **WHY:** In Fig. 4B, the $\Delta T_m$ preference for Leu over Lys at position 90 decreases monotonically from ~15 °C in LBCA (4 Ga) and ~11 °C in LPBCA (2.5 Ga) to ~6 °C in LGPCA (1.5 Ga) and ~4 °C in *E. coli* (0 Ga). Rather than showing static preference conservation, this trajectory demonstrates that ~73% of the initial destabilizing penalty of L90K was compensated over evolutionary time, even though the zero-crossing threshold was not reached.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the fraction of initial destabilization compensated across the phylogenetic trajectory in Fig. 4B and discuss how this bridges the gap between the authors' model and the Stokes-shift model.
* **HOW:** Regress $\Delta T_m(K\to L)$ against sequence divergence (number of amino acid substitutions separating LBCA, LPBCA, LGPCA, and *E. coli*, shown in the circles of Fig. 4B) and local 6 Å neighborhood divergence (Fig. 10A) to estimate the energetic adjustment per neighboring substitution.

#### 4. Verify Expression Levels and Plasmid Retention in the 15-Day Stationary-Phase Competition Assays
* **WHY:** In the competition experiments (Fig. 8), *trxA* WT and K90L are expressed via leaky basal transcription from an uninduced T7 promoter on multicopy `pET30a(+)` plasmids in a $\lambda\text{DE3}$-lysogenized $\Delta trxA \Delta trxC$ strain over 15 days of nutrient starvation without antibiotic selection mentioned in the minimal medium step. If the hyperstable K90L protein accumulates to higher cellular concentrations (imposing a translational/proteostatic burden during starvation) or alters plasmid copy number/stability over 15 days, the fitness difference could reflect expression dosage rather than intrinsic catalytic/binding superiority of Lys90.
* **WHAT:** Clarify in Materials and Methods whether kanamycin selection was maintained during the 15-day minimal medium competition and whether steady-state intracellular levels of WT and K90L thioredoxin are equivalent under uninduced basal expression.
* **HOW:** Report Western blot or SDS-PAGE quantification (or reference existing expression measurements) of basal WT vs. K90L Trx levels in `FA41(λDE3)` prior to and during stationary phase, and explicitly state the antibiotic regime used during the 15-day incubation.
