### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

#### Executive Summary
This brief software resource paper describes version 11 of the Molecular Evolutionary Genetics Analysis (MEGA11) suite across GUI and command-line (`MEGA-CC`) builds for Windows, macOS, and Linux. Methodological additions integrate four previously published algorithms from the authors' group into the MEGA interface: (1) node-calibration probability densities (normal, lognormal, uniform, exponential) and analytical confidence interval propagation for `RelTime` (Tao, Tamura, Mello, et al., 2020), (2) rapid tip-dating via `RelTime with Dated Tips` (`RTDT`; Miura et al., 2018), (3) a machine-learning test for branch-rate autocorrelation (`CorrTest`; Tao et al., 2019), and (4) neutral evolutionary probability (`EP`) estimation for alleles across multispecies alignments using `RelTime`-derived relative timetrees (Liu et al., 2016; Patel and Kumar, 2019). Technical engineering improvements include site-pattern compression ($m / L$) for Maximum Likelihood and Maximum Parsimony memory reduction, spatiotemporal metadata group tagging, and moving long-running pairwise distance and NJ/UPGMA calculations to background threads to keep the GUI responsive on alignments up to 68,000 genomes $\times$ 30,000 bp.

MEGA11 brings genuinely practical usability and memory improvements to a large user community, and the site-configuration compression (660 MB saved on 229 birds $\times$ 2,728 sites; 4.5 GB saved on 162 mammals $\times$ 11,010 sites) is concrete and verifiable. However, as a standalone technical report, the manuscript relies almost entirely on citations to prior papers for methodological validation, provides no runtime or memory metrics for the 68,000-genome responsiveness test, and makes an unsubstantiated claim in the "Memory Efficient ML Analyses" section that site-pattern compression *"does not have any detrimental impact on phylogenetic estimates and computational times"* without reporting before-and-after wall-clock runtimes (where site-pattern deduplication normally *reduces* ML runtime alongside memory).

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

#### Detailed Rubric Evaluation

* **Claim 1 — Site-pattern deduplication reduces Maximum Likelihood and Maximum Parsimony memory usage by a factor of approximately $m / L$, reducing RAM from 870 MB to 209 MB on 229 birds (2,728 sites) and from 6.8 GB to 2.3 GB on 162 mammals (11,010 sites): [Well Supported].**  
  Line 61 reports exact memory footprints on two published datasets (Claramunt and Cracraft, 2015; Meredith et al., 2011). Grouping identical alignment columns and weighting unique site log-likelihoods by their multiplicity is standard in phylogenetic likelihood engines (e.g., RAxML, IQ-TREE, PhyML) and is mathematically exact.

* **Claim 2 — Probability-density node calibrations (`RelTime`) and rapid tip-dating (`RTDT`) in MEGA11 yield accurate divergence times and well-calibrated confidence intervals accounting for lineage rate heterogeneity, branch-length estimation variance, and calibration uncertainty: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The underlying analytical variance-propagation equations and tip-dating estimators are validated in the cited primary studies (Miura et al., 2018; Tao, Tamura, Mello, et al., 2020). Within this manuscript, however, only GUI wizard screenshots (Figures 1–3) are shown, with no worked numerical validation or runtime comparison on an empirical pathogen or species dataset. Moreover, the manuscript correctly caveats (line 31) that when a user supplies only a pre-computed Newick tree with branch lengths (without the sequence alignment), the variance from sequence divergence estimation is omitted, yielding artificially narrower CIs.

* **Claim 3 — MEGA11 integrates `CorrTest` for branch-rate autocorrelation and Bayesian neutral `Evolutionary Probabilities` (`EP`) where $EP < 0.05$ identifies non-neutral disease or adaptive variants: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Both methods are cited to peer-reviewed publications (Tao et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2016; Patel and Kumar, 2019). However, the blanket operational threshold stated in line 43 (*"Alleles with EP less than 0.05 are nonneutral"*) is presented without discussing sensitivity to taxon sampling depth, alignment errors, or lineage-specific epistatic shifts (compensatory mutations), which can cause benign human alleles to appear improbable in deeper vertebrate alignments.

* **Claim 4 — Moving pairwise distance, selection test, and distance-tree calculations to background threads makes the MEGA11 GUI responsive on alignments of 68,000 genomes $\times$ 30,000 bases: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Line 67 states that a 68,000-genome $\times$ 30,000-base alignment (presumably SARS-CoV-2, though the organism is not explicitly named) was used to test GUI responsiveness during file I/O, `Sequence Data Explorer` navigation, pairwise distance estimation, and distance-based tree building. However, no wall-clock times, peak RAM usage, or thread counts are reported for computing an $\binom{68000}{2} \approx 2.31 \times 10^9$-pair distance matrix and NJ tree.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### Major Issues, Minor Issues & Concrete Recommendations

#### 1. Report Wall-Clock Runtimes and Peak Memory for the 68,000-Genome Stress Test and ML Site Compression (Major Issue)
* **WHY:** In "Enhanced GUI for Exploring Large Data Sets" (line 67), the authors test an alignment of 68,000 sequences $\times$ 30,000 bp, and in "Memory Efficient ML Analyses" (line 61), they report RAM reductions on bird and mammal alignments without wall-clock runtimes. Because a $68,000 \times 68,000$ double-precision pairwise distance matrix requires $\sim 37\text{ GB}$ of RAM (or $\sim 18.5\text{ GB}$ in lower-triangular single precision) and $O(N^3)$ neighbor-joining scales poorly at $N = 68,000$, readers need concrete performance metrics to know what operations are practically feasible.
* **WHAT:** Add a compact benchmark table reporting wall-clock runtime and peak RAM for (a) MEGA10 vs. MEGA11 ML inference on the bird (229 taxa) and mammal (162 taxa) datasets, and (b) file loading, pairwise distance calculation, and tree building on the 68,000 $\times$ 30,000 dataset.
* **HOW:** Record execution time and peak resident set size (RSS) on a specified desktop CPU for these three datasets and include them in Section 3 ("Technological Advances").

#### 2. Warn Users Explicitly When User-Supplied Branch Lengths Underestimate `RelTime` Confidence Intervals (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** Line 31 notes that when users input only a phylogeny with branch lengths (without the MSA), `RelTime` CIs are narrower because sequence-divergence estimation variance cannot be computed. Users interpreting these narrower CIs as higher precision may falsely reject temporal hypotheses.
* **WHAT:** Clarify in the text (and ideally in the `Tree Explorer` output log) how MEGA11 distinguishes alignment-backed CIs from tree-only CIs.
* **HOW:** State in Section 2 ("Calibrating the Clock Using Probability Densities on Node-Constraints") that `MEGA11` flags tree-only CIs in the tabular export so downstream analyses do not conflate omitted sequence-sampling variance with increased dating precision.

#### 3. Contextualize the $EP < 0.05$ Non-Neutrality Cutoff (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** Line 43 states categorically that *"Alleles with EP less than 0.05 are nonneutral"*. While $EP$ measures long-term phylogenetic permissibility under a neutral substitution model on the focal timetree, low $EP$ can also arise from clade-specific covarion/epistatic shifts or incomplete taxon sampling near the focal species.
* **WHAT:** Temper the wording in "Calculating Neutral Evolutionary Probabilities" to frame $EP < 0.05$ as a phylogenetic conservation filter rather than an absolute proof of non-neutral fitness effects.
* **HOW:** Revise line 43 to: *"Alleles with EP less than 0.05 are evolutionarily unexpected under a site-independent neutral model, whereas evolutionarily permissible alleles show much higher EPs."*

#### 4. Editorial and Bibliographic Cleanup (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** Minor reference typos should be cleaned up.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Line 132 (References, `Liu et al. 2016`): Fix the title typo *"A molecular evolutionary reference or the human variome"* to *"A molecular evolutionary reference for the human variome"*.
  2. Line 137 (References, `Miura et al. 2018`): Fix the citation volume/article number (`PLoS Comput Biol. 16:24` -> `PLoS Comput Biol. 14(6):e1006248` or verify the exact journal/article ID).
