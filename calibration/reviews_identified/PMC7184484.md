# Review of PMC7184484

**Manuscript Title:** Extreme Genomic CpG Deficiency in SARS-CoV-2 and Evasion of Host Antiviral Defense.  
**Authors:** Xuhua Xia  
**Journal / Preprint:** Molecular Biology and Evolution (2020) | DOI: 10.1093/molbev/msaa094  
**Overall Score:** **3.0 / 10.0** (Flawed / Overstated)  
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.7/3.5 | Claims: 0.5/2.5 | Biology: 0.6/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.2/2.0

---

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Xia computes the genomic dinucleotide relative abundance index $I_{CpG} = P_{CpG} / (P_C P_G)$ and overall GC content across GenBank assemblies of Betacoronaviruses (927 genomes with host annotations) and Alphacoronaviruses. Observing that SARS-CoV-2 and its closest bat relative BatCoV RaTG13 exhibit low $I_{CpG}$ ($\sim 0.41$) and low GC% compared to other *Rhinolophus* betacoronaviruses, and that pantropic canine coronaviruses (CCoVs, Alphacoronavirus) and pangolin coronaviruses show comparably low $I_{CpG}$, the author proposes that the ancestor of SARS-CoV-2 and RaTG13 infected the digestive tract of a mammalian intermediate host—specifically suggesting feral dogs/canids ingesting bat meat—where Zinc Finger Antiviral Protein (ZAP) selected against CpG dinucleotides, followed by anal-oral transmission in canids and spillover into humans. Supplementary spreadsheets of viral $I_{CpG}$ and GC% values are provided.

While documenting dinucleotide composition across coronavirus clades is a straightforward descriptive exercise, the manuscript's central evolutionary and zoonotic conclusions are methodologically invalid and contradicted by the paper's own comparative data. The study performs zero statistical tests, omits phylogenetic comparative controls, conflates whole-clade ancestral composition with recent host-specific selection, relies on a single cell-culture isolate (KC175339) to claim rapid CpG rebound, and constructs a speculative narrative of canid intestinal evolution and anal-licking transmission that has no basis in sequence evidence. A complete overhaul—restricting the scope to a phylogenetically controlled analysis of synonymous vs. non-synonymous dinucleotide mutational bias across Sarbecoviruses and removing the unsubstantiated canid origin narrative—is required.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **SARS-CoV-2 and BatCoV RaTG13 have the lowest genomic $I_{CpG}$ and GC% among sequenced Betacoronaviruses, shared with pangolin sarbecoviruses ($I_{CpG} = 0.3929$): [Well Supported].**  
  As a purely descriptive summary of whole-genome nucleotide and dinucleotide frequencies across the 927 betacoronavirus sequences in GenBank and GISAID (Figures 1–2), the calculation of $I_{CpG}$ and GC% correctly identifies the SARS-CoV-2 / RaTG13 / pangolin CoV subclade as sitting at the lower tail of betacoronavirus CpG representation.

* **The low $I_{CpG}$ of SARS-CoV-2 indicates that its ancestor evolved in a mammalian digestive tract—specifically canid intestine via ingestion of bat meat and anal-to-respiratory transmission—before jumping to humans: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This hypothesis is contradicted by the manuscript's own phylogenetic facts. BatCoV RaTG13 (sampled from a *Rhinolophus affinis* bat in 2013) and the pangolin coronavirus (EPI_ISL_410721) already possess the exact same low $I_{CpG}$ ($\sim 0.39–0.41$) and low GC% while diverging by 4% to >10% across the genome (representing decades of evolutionary separation in wildlife reservoirs). By parsimony, low $I_{CpG}$ is an ancestral property of the SARS-CoV-2/RaTG13/pangolin sarbecovirus lineage in bats and pangolins, not a recent adaptation in feral dogs. Furthermore, equating two distantly related viral genera (an Alphacoronavirus in dogs and a Betacoronavirus in humans/bats) based solely on a bivariate plot of whole-genome GC% and $I_{CpG}$ without testing receptor usage (canine CCoV uses aminopeptidase N, not ACE2) or isolating any SARS-like virus from canids is ecologically and molecularly unfounded.

* **Tissue-specific ZAP expression in the mammalian digestive tract drives stronger selection against viral CpG than in the respiratory tract: [Fragile].**  
  The author compares two camel coronavirus clades (MERS-CoV vs. Embecovirus HKU23) and two canine coronavirus clades (Alphacoronavirus CCoV vs. Betacoronavirus CRCoV) without any phylogenetic comparative model (effectively $N = 2$ independent taxonomic comparisons confounded by viral genus/subgenus divergence) and without measuring or citing actual ZAP protein expression levels in camel or canine intestinal versus respiratory epithelium. Moreover, Figure 3C cites human *ACE2* mRNA expression across tissues rather than *ZC3HAV1* (ZAP) expression, which does not test whether ZAP activity is higher in the intestine than in the lung.

* **A single cell-culture propagated CCoV genome (KC175339, $I_{CpG} = 0.4986$) demonstrates rapid evolutionary rebound of CpG when ZAP selection is relaxed in vitro: [Overstated].**  
  Inferring an evolutionary trajectory from a single unvalidated GenBank accession (KC175339) based on a personal communication that the strain was passaged in cell culture is invalid. In Figure 3B, KC175339 sits on a long phylogenetic branch separated from the low-$I_{CpG}$ clade; without sequencing the pre-passage parental clinical isolate, there is no evidence that KC175339 ever had a low $I_{CpG}$ of $\sim 0.40$ or that its higher GC% (38.17%) and $I_{CpG}$ (0.4986) accumulated during cell culture passaging rather than representing its natural wild-type composition or a recombination event.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Remove the Speculative Canid Origin Narrative and Align Claims with Ancestral State Reconstruction
* **WHY:** Because BatCoV RaTG13 (in *Rhinolophus* bats) and pangolin-CoV share the low $I_{CpG}$ signature with SARS-CoV-2, the trait clearly evolved in the common ancestor of the SARS-CoV-2-like sarbecovirus clade prior to any human spillover, rendering the canid intestinal hypothesis and behavioral speculations ("licking of anal regions in canids") scientifically baseless.
* **WHAT:** Delete Paragraph 12 of the Results (lines 41–42) and all claims implicating feral dogs/canids as the intermediate host of SARS-CoV-2. Reframe the paper around ancestral dinucleotide evolution within the *Sarbecovirus* subgenus.
* **HOW:** Reconstruct ancestral GC% and $I_{CpG}$ values along a time-calibrated or maximum-likelihood phylogeny of *Sarbecovirus* (including RaTG13, pangolin CoVs, and bat SARS-related CoVs) using continuous-trait maximum likelihood (`ape::ace` or `phytools::fastAnc` in R) to show exactly on which internal branch the shift in $I_{CpG}$ occurred.

#### 2. Separate Mutational Bias ($C \to U$ Deamination) from ZAP-Mediated Selection Using Codon-Position Controls
* **WHY:** Whole-genome $I_{CpG}$ is heavily confounded by overall GC content and directional mutational pressure (such as host APOBEC-driven $C \to U$ deamination, which depletes both C and CpG and increases U content). Attributing low $I_{CpG}$ solely to ZAP-mediated RNA degradation without distinguishing mutational bias from natural selection is unwarranted.
* **WHAT:** Compute $I_{CpG}$ separately at fourfold degenerate (synonymous) sites, non-degenerate coding sites, and frame-straddling (3-1) dinucleotide positions, and compare $I_{CpG}$ against all other 15 dinucleotides (especially $I_{UpG}$ and $I_{CpA}$, the expected products of CpG deamination).
* **HOW:** Partition every coronavirus coding sequence into codon positions (1-2, 2-3, and 3-1), calculate position-specific $I_{CpG}$ and synonymous substitution spectra along the branch leading to the RaTG13/SARS-CoV-2 clade, and test whether CpG depletion exceeds the neutral expectation generated by the empirical $C \to U$ substitution rate using a codon permutation null model (1,000 replicates preserving amino acid sequence and mononucleotide composition).

#### 3. Apply Phylogenetic Comparative Methods to Test Tissue Tropism Associations
* **WHY:** Treating individual GenBank genomes within the same viral clade as independent data points violates phylogenetic independence (pseudoreplication), and comparing Alphacoronavirus CCoV to Betacoronavirus CRCoV confounds tissue tropism with deep sub-family divergence.
* **WHAT:** Perform a formal Phylogenetic Generalized Least Squares (PGLS) regression testing whether enteric vs. respiratory tropism significantly predicts $I_{CpG}$ across the *Coronaviridae* phylogeny.
* **HOW:** Build an RdRp/ORF1ab phylogeny across unique coronavirus species, code primary tissue tropism (enteric vs. respiratory) as a binary predictor, and fit a PGLS model (`caper::pgls` or `phylolm` in R) estimating Pagel's $\lambda$ and the $p$-value for tissue tropism while controlling for genomic GC%.

#### 4. Analyse Actual Tissue-Specific *ZC3HAV1* (ZAP) Expression Data
* **WHY:** The central mechanistic premise of the paper is that ZAP expression is higher in intestinal epithelium than in respiratory tissue, yet Figure 3C plots *ACE2* expression (from Fagerberg et al. 2014) and never examines *ZC3HAV1* (ZAP) expression.
* **WHAT:** Query the same human tissue RNA-seq atlas (Fagerberg et al. 2014 / Human Protein Atlas / GTEx) for *ZC3HAV1* (and its cofactor *TRIM25* / *KHNYN*) across lung, bronchus, duodenum, small intestine, and colon.
* **HOW:** Replace or supplement Figure 3C with a boxplot of normalized *ZC3HAV1* expression (TPM/FPKM) across respiratory and gastrointestinal tissues and test whether gastrointestinal tissues actually express higher levels of ZAP than respiratory tissues.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Unverified personal communications, typos in gene names, and undefined formulas reduce technical clarity.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Results (line 31):** Either provide paired pre- and post-passage sequences for CCoV isolate KC175339 to prove in vitro CpG evolution, or remove the claim that KC175339 gained CpG during cell culture propagation.
  2. **Results (line 39):** Correct the typo `"ABOBEC3G"` to `"APOBEC3G"`.
  3. **Figure 1 Caption (line 56):** Correct the formula typo `ICpG(=PCpG/PCPG)` to clearly indicate multiplication of marginal nucleotide frequencies in the denominator: $I_{\text{CpG}} = P_{\text{CpG}} / (P_{\text{C}} \cdot P_{\text{G}})$.
