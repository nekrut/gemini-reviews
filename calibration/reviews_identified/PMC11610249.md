* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 1.0/3.5 | Claims: 0.9/2.5 | Biology: 1.0/2.0 | Reproducibility: 0.9/2.0

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Xu et al. perform a genome-wide association study (GWAS) and genomic prediction (GP) analysis of salt tolerance across 563 soybean (*Glycine max*) germplasm accessions from the public USDA GRIN collection (150 salt-tolerant coded as `1` and 413 salt-susceptible coded as `9`) genotyped with 34,181 filtered SNPs from the SoySNP50K array (`soybase.org`). Using GAPIT 3 (`GLM`, `MLM`, `FarmCPU`, and `BLINK`, with $K = 4$ PCA/NJ subpopulations `Q1–Q4`), the authors apply a Bonferroni significance threshold of $-\log_{10}(0.05 / 34,181) = 5.83$ (referred to in the text as "LOD") and report 10 significant SNPs on chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 7, and 16, identifying 11 candidate genes within a 3–5 kb window (`Glyma.Wm82.a2.v1`). They then evaluate genomic prediction accuracy (Pearson $r$ over 100 random cross-validation splits) using `maBLUP`, `gBLUP`, `cBLUP`, and `sBLUP` across all 34,181 SNPs, as well as `rrBLUP`, `Bayes A`, `Bayes B`, `Bayes LASSO`, and `Bayes Ridge Regression` across eight randomly sampled SNP subsets (`r10` to `r10000`) and the 10 GWAS-significant SNPs (`m10`, reporting $r = 0.36 - 0.40$).

While the underlying USDA GRIN phenotype and SoySNP50K genotype datasets are publicly accessible, the analytical execution and statistical interpretation suffer from three major methodological flaws: (1) **9 of the 10 reported GWAS hits (including all hits on chromosomes 1, 2, and 7) were detected almost exclusively by the naive Generalized Linear Model (`GLM`), which omits the kinship matrix $K$, and failed to reach significance in kinship-corrected models (`MLM` and `BLINK`)**—only the chromosome 3 locus (`Gm03_43220331_ss715586399`, matching the canonical *GmCHX1* / *Ncl* salt-tolerance region) and a single SNP on chromosome 16 (`Gm16_7722217_ss715625494`) were supported by `FarmCPU`; (2) **the genomic prediction benchmark of the 10 GWAS-derived markers (`m10`, $r = 0.38$) suffers from textbook in-sample feature-selection data leakage**: the 10 SNPs were selected using a GWAS conducted on all 563 accessions before running the 100 cross-validation splits on the exact same 563 accessions, artificially inflating `m10` accuracy relative to random SNP sets; and (3) **a binary qualitative phenotype (`Tolerant` vs. `Susceptible`) was arbitrarily numerically encoded as `1` and `9` and analyzed with Gaussian linear models and two-sample $t$-tests**, while the manuscript contains numerous internal contradictions in model names, candidate gene windows (3 kb vs. 4 kb vs. 5 kb), SNP counts per chromosome, and broken table references.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Ten SNPs across chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 7, and 16 are robustly associated with salt tolerance in soybean:** **[Overstated].**  
  Inspecting Table 1 shows that **zero** SNPs passed significance in `MLM` or `BLINK`. Eight of the 10 SNPs were detected *only* by `GLM` (which controls only for $Q_1-Q_4$ PCs without a polygenic kinship matrix $K$, producing severe QQ-plot inflation in structured soybean landraces), one SNP (`Gm16_7722217`) was detected only by `FarmCPU` (with an unadjusted $t$-test $-\log_{10} P$ of only $2.75$), and only **one** SNP (`Gm03_43220331_ss715586399` on chromosome 3) passed the threshold in both `GLM` and `FarmCPU`. Thus, only the chromosome 3 QTL (and potentially the chromosome 16 `FarmCPU` hit) resists kinship confounding, whereas the chromosomes 1, 2, and 7 `GLM`-only signals are almost certainly false positives driven by cryptic relatedness and country-of-origin structure (e.g., 46/159 Japanese accessions are tolerant).

* **The GWAS-derived 10-SNP marker set (`m10`) achieves higher genomic prediction accuracy ($r = 0.36 - 0.40$) than up to 10,000 genome-wide SNPs ($r = 0.28 - 0.31$), demonstrating superior efficiency for genomic selection:** **[Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section "Genomic prediction for genomic selection of salt tolerance" (line 137) and Table 2, the `m10` marker set was chosen from the GWAS performed on the **entire panel of 563 accessions** and then evaluated via 100 random training/validation splits drawn from those same 563 accessions. Selecting features on the combined training + validation dataset before cross-validation is a classic data-leakage error (winner's curse / lookahead bias) that guarantees upward bias in validation $r$.

* **Eleven candidate genes within 5 kb of the significant SNPs on chromosomes 1, 2, 3, and 16 underlie soybean salt tolerance:** **[Fragile].**  
  Cultivated soybean (*Glycine max*) is an autogamous species with extensive linkage disequilibrium ($r^2 > 0.2$ typically extending $100 - 500\text{ kb}$ in euchromatic regions). Restricting the candidate gene search to $\pm 4 - 5\text{ kb}$ around array SNPs from the 50K chip (average spacing ~1 SNP per 28 kb) misses causal genes in the same LD block. Strikingly, on chromosome 3 (~43.2 Mb)—the one genuinely replicated locus—the authors list four genes (`Glyma.03G230400` to `Glyma.03G230700`) without even mentioning whether the well-cloned causal soybean salt-tolerance cation/proton antiporter gene *GmCHX1* / *GmSALT3* (located in this exact chromosome 3 region) lies in the LD block.

* **Phylogenetic clustering of the 104 salt-tolerant accessions from six countries demonstrates geographic structuring of salt-tolerant germplasm:** **[Moderately Supported].**  
  While the neighbor-joining and maximum-likelihood trees (Fig. 2, Fig. 5) show that accessions from Japan, China, and India cluster by geographic origin, this reflects general demographic history and isolation-by-distance across the USDA collection rather than trait-specific adaptive clustering, which is also why unadjusted `GLM` GWAS produces spurious hits.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Feature-Selection Leakage in the `m10` Genomic Prediction Cross-Validation
* **WHY:** Selecting the 10 most significant SNPs (`m10`) using a GWAS on all 563 lines and then splitting those 563 lines into training and validation sets leaks validation-set phenotypes into the marker-selection step, inflating $r = 0.38$ in Table 2.
* **WHAT:** Perform nested (out-of-fold) GWAS marker selection strictly inside each training split during the 100 cross-validation iterations, and report the true out-of-sample prediction accuracy alongside the training-validation split ratio.
* **HOW:** In each of the 100 random splits (specify the exact split ratio, e.g., 80% training = 450 lines, 20% validation = 113 lines, which is currently omitted from Methods line 137):
  1. Run GAPIT (`FarmCPU` / `BLINK`) on the 450 training lines only.
  2. Select the top 10 SNPs from the training-only GWAS.
  3. Fit `rrBLUP`, `BA`, `BB`, `BL`, and `BRR` on the 450 training lines using those 10 SNPs and predict the held-out 113 validation lines.
  4. Report the mean and SE of $r$ across the 100 strictly out-of-sample replicates in Table 2.

#### 2. Restrict GWAS Claims to Kinship-Controlled Models (`MLM`, `MLMM`, `FarmCPU`, `BLINK`) and Use a Binomial/Probit Link for Binary Phenotypes
* **WHY:** A naive `GLM` without a kinship matrix $K$ does not control for polygenic background relatedness in soybean, and encoding a binary trait (`Tolerant` vs. `Susceptible`) as `1` and `9` violates Gaussian residual assumptions in linear regression and Pearson correlation.
* **WHAT:** Remove `GLM`-only hits from the primary QTL claims unless supported by at least one kinship-controlled model (`MLM`, `MLMM`, `FarmCPU`, or `BLINK`), and evaluate threshold/probit models (`BGLR(response_type = "ordinal")`) and area under the ROC curve (AUROC) for the binary phenotype.
* **HOW:** Re-run `BGLR` with `response_type = "ordinal"` on the binary `T/S` vector, report AUROC and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) alongside Pearson $r$ in Table 2, and separate Table 1 into kinship-supported QTLs (Chr 3 and Chr 16) vs. suggestive `GLM`-only signals.

#### 3. Expand the Candidate Gene Window to Match Empirical Local LD Decay on Chromosome 3
* **WHY:** Searching only $\pm 4 - 5\text{ kb}$ around array SNPs ignores the actual LD block size in soybean (~100–250 kb) and fails to connect the chromosome 3 hit (`43.21 - 43.22 Mb`) to known functional salt-tolerance genes in that interval.
* **WHAT:** Compute pairwise $r^2$ around `Gm03_43213208` and `Gm03_43220331` across the 563 accessions to define the empirical LD block ($r^2 > 0.2$), and list all annotated `Wm82.a2.v1` genes within that LD block.
* **HOW:** Use PLINK (`--r2 --ld-snp Gm03_43220331_ss715586399 --ld-window-kb 500`) to determine the physical boundaries of the chromosome 3 haplotype block and discuss its physical relationship to the canonical *GmSALT3* / *GmCHX1* locus.

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** The manuscript has pervasive contradictions between the Abstract, Methods, Results, Conclusion, and Tables that must be corrected.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Terminology error ("LOD" vs. $-\log_{10} P$):** Throughout the paper (lines 125, 164, 172, Table 1), the authors redefine $-\log_{10}(P\text{-value})$ from frequentist $t$-tests and linear regressions as `"LOD (logarithm of odds)"`. $-\log_{10}(P)$ is not an LOD score ($\text{LOD} = \log_{10}[L(\hat{\theta})/L(0)]$). Replace `"LOD"` with `$-\log_{10}(P)$` throughout the text, tables, and figure axes.
  * **Contradictory GWAS model lists (Abstract line 70 vs. Methods line 125):** The Abstract states `"using four models, including MLM, MLMM, FarmCPU, and BLINK"`, whereas Methods line 125 and Results line 164 state `"BLINK, FarmCPU, GLM, and MLM"` (dropping `MLMM` and adding `GLM`). Make the model list consistent.
  * **Contradictory SNP counts in Conclusion (line 277):** Line 277 states `"we identified 10 SNPs associated with salt tolerance: four SNP markers on chromosome 1 (Gm01_4306329_ss715579436, Gm01_4312808_ss715579441, and Gm01_4336306_ss715579451)..."`—it says "four SNP markers on chromosome 1" but lists only three, and $4 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 11 \ne 10$. Change `"four SNP markers on chromosome 1"` to `"three SNP markers on chromosome 1"`.
  * **Inconsistent candidate gene window sizes and chromosome lists:** Abstract line 75 says `"within a 5 kb window... on chromosomes 1, 3, and 16"` (omitting chromosome 2, which has `Glyma.02G195400`); Table 1 header says `"Linked gene (0-4kb)"` and footnote says `"less than 4 kb"`; Discussion line 270 says `"within 4 kb"` for Chr 3 and Chr 2, `"within 5 kb"` for Chr 1, and `"within 3 kb"` for Chr 16. Standardize the window definition.
  * **Broken Table cross-references (lines 172, 231, 233):** Line 172 cites `(Table 2)` three times for the GWAS LOD and $t$-test results of chromosomes 3, 7, and 16, and lines 231 and 233 cite `(Tables 1, 2)` for candidate genes. Table 2 contains only Genomic Prediction $r$-values; all those results are in **Table 1** (and Supplementary Table 2).
  * **Missing `cBLUP` result (line 137 vs. line 238):** Methods line 137 states that four GAPIT models (`maBLUP`, `gBLUP`, `cBLUP`, `sBLUP`) were run, but Results line 238 and Discussion line 272 report $r$-values only for three (`0.46, 0.60, and 0.44 for the maBLUP, gBLUP, and sBLUP models`). Report the `cBLUP` $r$-value as well.
  * **Table 1 formatting (line 191):** The second row for `Gm03_43220331_ss715586399` (`| 6.03 | FarmCPU | 9.33 |`) is misaligned across columns in Table 1; align it properly under the `LOD`, `Model`, and `Lod_(t-test)` columns.
  * **Grammar in Abstract (line 70):** Fix the duplicated clause `"Employing four distinct models—we performed a genome-wide association study (GWAS) using four models, including MLM, MLMM, FarmCPU, and BLINK in GAPIT 3, we conducted a Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS)..."`
