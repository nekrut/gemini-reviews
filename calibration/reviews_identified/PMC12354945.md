### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Skupa et al. profile longitudinal fecal 16S rRNA (V3–V4) microbiome dynamics, small intestinal and colonic histopathology (TCL1/CD19 IHC and tight junction IF), plasma intestinal permeability markers (CLDN2, CLDN3, zonulin, sCD14), and peripheral/splenic immunophenotypes across two murine models of chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL): the spontaneous transgenic E$\mu$-TCL1 model ($n = 13$ vs. $n = 7$ commercial WT C57BL/6J controls sampled at 4, 7, 10, and 12 months) and the syngeneic E$\mu$-TCL1 adoptive transfer (AT) model ($n = 10$ tracked over 7 weeks, plus an antibiotic microflora-ablation experiment comparing $n = 10$ antibiotic-treated vs. $n = 10$ water-control mice). The authors report higher Shannon alpha diversity and distinct Bray–Curtis beta diversity in transgenic E$\mu$-TCL1 mice relative to WT controls, enrichment of Proteobacteria at late disease stages in both models, leukemic B-cell infiltration (`TCL1+`/`CD19+` nodules) in the small intestine accompanied by elevated plasma CLDN2, zonulin, and sCD14, and a transient delay in peripheral blood CLL burden at weeks 3 and 5 post-engraftment in antibiotic-treated AT mice. Raw 16S sequencing data (BioProject `PRJNA1187802`) and analysis scripts (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`) are publicly deposited.

The histopathologic demonstration of direct intestinal CLL infiltration (`TCL1+` serosal/mucosal nodules) paired with altered epithelial tight junction protein expression (`ZO-1` loss and `CLDN2` upregulation) is an interesting biological observation. However, the microbiome and immunological conclusions are severely compromised by experimental design confounders, statistical inconsistencies, and internal numerical discrepancies: (1) **the transgenic E$\mu$-TCL1 vs. WT comparison is completely confounded by provenance and separate housing (vendor/colony and cage effects)**—E$\mu$-TCL1 mice were bred in-house whereas WT B6 controls were purchased from The Jackson Laboratory and housed in separate cages (lines 27, 33), which fully explains why the microbiome separation is already maximal at baseline (4 months, before significant CLL expansion) and never changes over time within E$\mu$-TCL1 mice (Fig. 1E–F); (2) at the 4-month time point, **only a single ($n = 1$) WT B6 sample passed sequencing quality control** (line 35, line 89), invalidating any statistical inference at disease initiation; (3) the Abstract claims that antibiotic-treated mice had "more tumor-reactive CD8+ T cells that coexpressed fewer inhibitory receptors (e.g., PD-1, LAG-3, and TIM-3)," whereas the Results section (line 137) explicitly reports that these splenic T-cell differences were **not statistically significant**; and (4) the text and figure captions contradict each other on basic sample sizes (e.g., $n = 7$ WT B6 mice enrolled in Methods line 27 vs. $n = 10$ WT B6 mice at 12 months in the Fig. 2A LEfSe caption).

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Diseased E$\mu$-TCL1 mice exhibit leukemic B-cell infiltration in the small intestine and colon accompanied by altered tight junction marker expression (decreased ZO-1, increased mucosal and plasma CLDN2, zonulin, and sCD14):** **[Well Supported].**  
  Swiss-roll histopathology, quantitative QuPath `TCL1` immunohistochemistry (Fig. 3A–B, $P < 0.001$), mucosal immunofluorescence (Fig. S4), and plasma ELISAs (Fig. 4A–D, $P < 0.01$ to $P < 0.001$) consistently demonstrate GI tract leukemic involvement and barrier remodeling at 12 months. The authors also appropriately note the caveat that monocytes can independently secrete sCD14 in the presence of CLL cells.

* **Transgenic E$\mu$-TCL1 mice develop a CLL-specific dysbiotic gut microbiome with higher alpha diversity and distinct beta diversity compared with WT B6 mice:** **[Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In murine microbiome studies, vendor of origin, maternal lineage, and cage allocation are the dominant drivers of gut community structure. Here, E$\mu$-TCL1 mice were bred in-house while WT C57BL/6J controls were purchased from The Jackson Laboratory (`JAX:000664`) and housed in separate cages for 12 months (lines 27, 33). Crucially, within the E$\mu$-TCL1 cohort, alpha diversity (Fig. 1E) and Bray–Curtis beta diversity (Fig. 1F) show **no significant change between 4, 7, 10, and 12 months** even as peripheral CLL burden rises from near zero to 36.96% (line 114). Thus, the difference between E$\mu$-TCL1 and WT B6 mice (Fig. 1C–D) reflects an existing baseline colony/vendor difference between in-house transgenic mice and Jackson Lab WT mice rather than a disease-induced shift. Furthermore, only $n = 1$ WT B6 sample passed QC at 4 months (line 89).

* **Broad-spectrum antibiotic ablation transiently delays peripheral CLL expansion at weeks 3 and 5 in the syngeneic adoptive transfer model:** **[Moderately Supported].**  
  In the randomized AT experiment ($n = 10$/arm, all sourced from Jackson Lab), peripheral blood `% CD45+/CD19+/CD5+` cells are significantly lower at weeks 3 and 5 in the antibiotic group before converging at week 7 (Fig. 6B). However, daily high-dose oral gavage of vancomycin, neomycin, metronidazole, and amphotericin B plus continuous ampicillin/metronidazole in drinking water can directly alter hydration, systemic inflammation, and hepatic/splenic drug metabolism; without a germ-free or fecal microbiota transplant (FMT) control, direct systemic effects of the 5-drug antimicrobial regimen cannot be excluded.

* **Delayed CLL onset in antibiotic-treated mice is accompanied by splenic tumor-reactive $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells co-expressing fewer inhibitory receptors (PD-1, LAG-3, TIM-3):** **[Overstated].**  
  The Abstract (line 7) states as a positive finding that `"this delay in disease was accompanied by more tumor-reactive CD8+ T cells that coexpressed fewer inhibitory receptors (e.g., PD-1, LAG-3, and TIM-3)."` Yet the Results section (line 137) explicitly states the opposite: `"Antibiotic-receiving mice displayed minimally reduced T cell dysfunction, evoking a non-significant change in the percentage of total CD8+ and antigen-experienced (CD44+) CD8+ T cells co-expressing inhibitory receptors PD-1, TIM-3, and LAG-3 (Supplementary Fig. S5B)... No significant change in percentage of progenitor-exhausted T cells was witnessed... Overall, more definitive conclusions were not able to be made because of the limited sample size analyzed."` Also, spleens were harvested at week 9 (Fig. 6A caption) or week 7 (line 29, 135), when tumor burden between groups had already equalized, not during the week 3–5 delay window.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Account for Colony/Vendor and Cage Confounding in the Transgenic E$\mu$-TCL1 Microbiome Analysis
* **WHY:** Comparing in-house-bred E$\mu$-TCL1 mice against Jackson-purchased WT B6 mice housed in separate cages confounds genotype with maternal/vendor microbiota and cage-level drift. Because Shannon diversity and Bray–Curtis PCoA do not change longitudinally from 4 to 12 months within the E$\mu$-TCL1 cohort (Fig. 1E–F), attributing the WT vs. E$\mu$-TCL1 separation to CLL progression is invalid.
* **WHAT:** Re-analyze the 16S dataset using linear mixed-effects models that include cage ID as a random effect and test specifically for *within-cohort longitudinal slope* ($\text{Time} \times \text{Genotype}$ or microbiome shift vs. individual `% CD19+CD5+` leukemic burden), and explicitly acknowledge the in-house vs. Jackson Lab vendor confounder in the Results and Discussion.
* **HOW:** In R (`lme4` / `vegan::adonis2`), run PERMANOVA on the Bray–Curtis matrix stratified by mouse ID and cage ID (`adonis2(dist ~ CD5_CD19_burden + Age, strata = MouseID)`) across the 4-, 7-, 10-, and 12-month E$\mu$-TCL1 samples to identify which specific amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) covary with individual leukemic burden independently of baseline colony identity.

#### 2. Apply Compositional, FDR-Controlled Differential Abundance Methods and Report Exact PERMANOVA Statistics
* **WHY:** The manuscript reports PCoA plots (Figs. 1D, 1F, 5D, 6G) without a single PERMANOVA $R^2$ or $P$-value, relies on unadjusted Welch $t$-tests at individual time points, and uses unadjusted LEfSe and PICRUSt2 at the coarse phylum level without false discovery rate (FDR) correction.
* **WHAT:** Report PERMANOVA ($R^2$, $F$, $P$-value) for every PCoA plot, and validate phylum- and genus-level LEfSe hits using a compositionally aware method (e.g., ANCOM-BC2 or MaAsLin2) with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction.
* **HOW:** Using the QIIME2 feature table rarefied/filtered in `[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`, run `qiime diversity beta-group-significance` (PERMANOVA, 999 permutations) for Figs. 1D, 1F, 5D, and 6G, and report BH-adjusted $q$-values for all taxa and PICRUSt2 pathways in Figs. 2, 5F, and 7.

#### 3. Align the Abstract and Discussion with the Non-Significant Splenic T-Cell Results
* **WHY:** Presenting statistically non-significant fluctuations in splenic $\text{CD8}^+$ PD-1/LAG-3/TIM-3 and CD39 expression (Results line 137, Fig. S5B–D) as positive findings in the Abstract (line 7) and Discussion (line 8) violates statistical reporting standards.
* **WHAT:** Remove or qualify the claim in the Abstract and Discussion that antibiotic ablation increased tumor-reactive $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells and reduced inhibitory receptor co-expression.
* **HOW:** Revise Abstract line 7 to state: `"Peripheral blood immunophenotyping showed that this delay in disease was accompanied by preserved non-malignant B- and T-cell proportions and reduced myeloid-derived suppressor cells, whereas splenic CD8+ T-cell exhaustion markers at study endpoint did not differ significantly between groups."`

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Multiple sample-size contradictions, broken URLs, and cross-reference typos exist across the text and figure captions.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Sample size contradiction (Methods line 27 vs. Fig. 2A caption line 37):** Methods line 27 states `WT B6 control mice (n = 7; two males, five females) were housed separately and monitored over 12 months`, yet the Fig. 2A caption (line 37) states `transgenic Eμ-TCL1 mice (n = 12) and WT B6 mice (n = 10) at 12 months of age`. Explain how $n = 7$ WT B6 mice grew to $n = 10$ at 12 months, and list the exact number of mice passing 16S QC at each time point (4, 7, 10, 12 months).
  * **Spleen harvest endpoint discrepancy (Methods line 29/31 vs. Fig. 6A caption line 45):** Methods line 29 says `At the study end (7 weeks after engraftment), mice were euthanized`, whereas the Fig. 6A caption says `Spleens were harvest at 9 weeks after engraftment for further immunophenotyping`. Reconcile whether the antibiotic study ended at 7 weeks or 9 weeks.
  * **SRA BioProject ID mismatch (line 106):** Line 106 lists `BioProject ID PRJNA1187802 at https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/1178702` (`1187802` in text vs. `1178702` in the URL). Correct the typographical mismatch so both point to the valid NCBI accession.
  * **Broken figure cross-reference (Part 1, line 141):** `"corresponding with the gradual rise in CLL burden (Fig. 5I; Supplementary Fig. S7B and S7C)"`—Fig. 5 only has panels A–F; this refers to **Fig. 6I**.
  * **Typos:** Line 119 heading `"Intestinal barrier is potentially comprised"` -> `"compromised"`; Line 12 (Part 2) `"examine casual relationships"` -> `"causal relationships"`; Line 43/24 `"Bray–Curis distance"` -> `"Bray–Curtis distance"`.
