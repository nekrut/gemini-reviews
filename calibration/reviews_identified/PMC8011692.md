### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Inizan et al. investigate whether viral genetic factors contributed to the increased clinical severity and frequent hepatic involvement observed during the 2016–2017 dengue virus serotype 1 (DENV-1) outbreak in New Caledonia compared to the 2013–2014 outbreak. Using a cohort of 86 qRT-PCR-confirmed patients (17 retrospectively biobanked from 2013–2014 and 69 prospectively recruited in 2016–2017), the authors generated 24 near-complete DENV-1 genomes (3 from 2013–2014 and 21 from 2016–2017, supplemented with 2 GenBank sequences from 2014) and evaluated in vitro replication kinetics, viral titers, and cell mortality in human hepatocarcinoma HuH7 cells for 13 clinical isolates (6 from 2013–2014 and 7 from 2016–2017). Phylogenetic analysis showed that 2016–2017 DENV-1 sequences formed a monophyletic cluster separated from 2013–2014 sequences by 26 synonymous mutations and four amino acid substitutions in non-structural proteins (NS1 Y886H, NS4A V2157A, NS5 K2623R, and NS5 G2875A), while sequences from severe ($n=5$) and non-severe ($n=15$) 2016–2017 cases were phylogenetically interspersed without distinguishing mutations. In HuH7 cells, 2016–2017 isolates showed no statistically significant differences in infection rates, supernatant viral output, or cytopathogenicity compared to 2013–2014 isolates. The 24 assembled genomes are publicly available in GenBank (MW315172–MW315195).

While the genomic surveillance data and negative in vitro findings are useful for documenting DENV-1 lineage continuity in the South Pacific, the manuscript's title and primary narrative overreach the empirical evidence. All within-outbreak genomic comparisons and between-outbreak hepatic cell assays yielded null results, none of the 13 viral isolates tested in HuH7 cells were derived from severe cases, and major confounding differences in host secondary infection rates between cohorts (39% vs. 5.9% DENV IgG positivity) are left unadjusted. These issues can be resolved without generating new wet-lab data by reframing the title and abstract to reflect the null virological findings, performing formal statistical enrichment tests for the epitope predictions, and explicitly modeling host serostatus and sampling attrition.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **DENV-1 strains from the 2016–2017 New Caledonia outbreak form a distinct monophyletic cluster differing from 2013–2014 strains by 26 synonymous and 4 non-synonymous substitutions: [Well Supported].**  
  Maximum-likelihood phylogenetic reconstruction in IQ-TREE (Figure 1, Supplementary Figure 2) with 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates clearly separates the 21 genomes sequenced in 2016–2017 from the 5 genomes (3 newly sequenced, 2 from GenBank) from 2013–2014 within DENV-1 genotype I, with four fixed non-structural amino acid replacements (NS1 Y886H, NS4A V2157A, NS5 K2623R, NS5 G2875A).

* **Severe clinical presentation in 2016–2017 is not associated with specific viral genetic signatures among sequenced DENV-1 strains: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Within the 2016–2017 sequenced subset, the 5 genomes from severe patients and 15 genomes from non-severe patients are interspersed across the phylogeny with no shared severity-associated mutations (Figure 1). Support is moderated by the small sample of severe genomes ($n=5$) and restriction of successful whole-genome sequencing to high-viremia sera (median $2.95 \times 10^7$ copies/mL vs. $2.37 \times 10^5$ copies/mL in the full cohort; Table 1).

* **DENV-1 strains from 2016–2017 exhibit reduced replicative fitness and cytopathogenicity in HuH7 hepatic cells that may have contributed to DENV-1 decline in 2018: [Overstated].**  
  Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney rank-sum tests across days 1, 2, and 3 post-infection show no statistically significant differences between 2013–2014 ($n=6$) and 2016–2017 ($n=7$) isolates for percent infected cells (Figure 2A), day-2 supernatant titers (Figure 2B), or cell mortality (Figure 2C). Treating noisy, non-significant differences in small samples as directional biological trends—and extrapolating them to explain island-wide serotype replacement in 2018—conflates sampling noise with true fitness shifts. Furthermore, Table 1 ("Analysis 2") reveals that 0 of the 13 isolated strains came from severe patients ($8/13$ non-severe, $5/13$ missing severity data), precluding direct inference about severe hepatic disease.

* **Four amino acid substitutions in 2016–2017 DENV-1 modified viral T- and B-cell antigenicity to sustain an outbreak of enhanced severity: [Overstated].**  
  This claim, highlighted in the title and abstract, rests solely on uncalibrated *in silico* predictions from NetCTL 1.2 and BepiPred 2.0 without experimental immunogenicity data or statistical null testing. Moreover, there is a positional discrepancy between the Abstract ("Three of these mutations occurred in predicted T cell epitopes") and the Results/Discussion, which report the mutations at polyprotein positions 886, 2157, 2623, and 2875 but list predicted T-cell epitope residues at positions 887, 2158, and 2871 (Results) and describe them as occurring "near" or "in the vicinity of" T-cell epitopes (Discussion). Crucially, Table 1 shows that prior DENV IgG seropositivity rose from $1/17$ (5.9%) in 2013–2014 to $24/62$ (39%) in 2016–2017, providing a direct epidemiological explanation (secondary infection) for increased severity that is overshadowed by the speculative viral-evolution narrative.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Align the Title, Abstract, and Discussion with the Empirical Null Results
* **WHY:** The title (*"Viral evolution sustains a dengue outbreak of enhanced severity"*) implies a demonstrated causal link between viral mutations and outbreak severity, contradicting the manuscript's own findings that severe and non-severe strains are genetically indistinguishable and that 2016–2017 isolates do not differ significantly in hepatic cell replication or cytopathogenicity.
* **WHAT:** Revise the title, abstract, and discussion to accurately frame the study as showing lineage evolution between outbreaks *without* increased intrinsic hepatic tropism or cytopathogenicity, pointing instead to host immunity and secondary infections.
* **HOW:** Change the title to a descriptive statement (e.g., *"Lineage evolution of dengue virus serotype 1 between the 2013–2014 and 2016–2017 outbreaks in New Caledonia does not increase intrinsic hepatic cytopathogenicity"*). In the Abstract and Results, replace claims that 2016–2017 isolates "tend to be less infectious/cytopathogenic" with a direct statement that no statistically significant differences in replication kinetics or cell death were detected in HuH7 cells.

#### 2. Quantify Statistical Enrichment and Exact Peptide Coordinates for Predicted Epitopes
* **WHY:** Because NetCTL 1.2 and BepiPred 2.0 predict numerous 9-mer T-cell and linear B-cell epitopes across the ~3,392-amino-acid DENV-1 polyprotein, observing 3 of 4 substitutions within or near predicted epitopes may easily occur by chance under neutral drift. In addition, the text inconsistently refers to mutated sites (886, 2157, 2875) versus nearby residues (887, 2158, 2871).
* **WHAT:** Report the exact 9-mer peptide boundaries, NetCTL/BepiPred scores for both 2013–2014 (ancestral) and 2016–2017 (derived) sequences, and a permutation $p$-value testing whether 4 substitutions fall in predicted epitopes more often than expected by chance.
* **HOW:** Using the complete DENV-1 polyprotein sequences from 2013–2014 and 2016–2017: (i) run NetCTL 1.2 (HLA-A24) and BepiPred 2.0 on both genotypes to quantify whether the 4 substitutions actually change predicted MHC binding affinity, C-terminal cleavage, TAP transport, or B-cell propensity scores; (ii) compute the fraction $p$ of all polyprotein residues covered by predicted epitopes and calculate the exact binomial probability of $\ge 3$ of 4 random mutations hitting those regions; and (iii) resolve the coordinate mismatch between positions 886/2157/2875 and 887/2158/2871 in a dedicated table.

#### 3. Account for Confounding by Secondary Flavivirus Infection and Subsample Ascertainment
* **WHY:** Prior heterotypic dengue infection is a well-established determinant of severe dengue. In Table 1, DENV IgG prevalence is 6.6-fold higher in the 2016–2017 cohort (39% vs. 5.9%), and ZIKV IgG is 12% vs. 0%, yet this shift is not formally compared across cohorts or sub-analyses. Furthermore, the sequenced ($n=24$) and isolated ($n=13$) subsets have ~100-fold higher median viral loads than the parent cohort and exclude severe cases in Analysis 2.
* **WHAT:** Add formal statistical comparisons of baseline clinical/serological variables between the 2013–2014 and 2016–2017 cohorts and between the sequenced/isolated subsets and unsequenced patients.
* **HOW:** In Table 1, add a column of $p$-values (Fisher's exact test for categorical variables such as DENV IgG, ZIKV IgG, and serotype distribution; Mann–Whitney test for viral load and days post-onset) comparing 2013–2014 vs. 2016–2017 across all three panels ("All patients", "Analysis 1", and "Analysis 2"). Explicitly state in the Results section for Analysis 2 that $0/13$ tested viral isolates originated from severe dengue patients.

#### 4. Report Exact $p$-values, Effect Sizes, and Passage History for HuH7 Replication Assays
* **WHY:** Figure 2 labels all comparisons simply as `ns` without reporting exact Mann–Whitney $U$ statistics, exact $p$-values, or confidence intervals, and Methods notes that isolates underwent "no more than four passages in C6/36," which could introduce passage-number confounding when normalized against a multi-passaged 2014 control strain.
* **WHAT:** Provide exact test statistics, $p$-values, and per-isolate passage numbers for the HuH7 experiments.
* **HOW:** Update Figure 2 and the Results text to include exact two-sided Mann–Whitney $p$-values and Hodges–Lehmann median difference estimates with 95% confidence intervals at days 1, 2, and 3. Add the exact C6/36 passage number (1 to 4) for each of the 13 isolates to Supplementary Table 1 and check via Spearman correlation whether passage count correlates with day-3 HuH7 infection rate.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Several numerical discrepancies between the main text and Table 1 reduce confidence in data curation.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **ZIKV IgG denominator mismatch (Results vs. Table 1):** The Results text states *"Anti-ZIKV IgG were found in 0/17 (0%) and 7/60 (12%) patients"*, whereas Table 1 reports `0/6 (0%)` for 2013–2014 ZIKV IgG and DENV&ZIKV IgG. Reconcile the 2013–2014 denominator (6 vs. 17).
  2. **Co-seropositivity fraction mismatch (Results vs. Table 1):** The Results text states *"Among those anti-ZIKV IgG positive, 5/6 (83.3%) had both anti-ZIKV and DENV IgG"*, whereas Table 1 lists 7 ZIKV IgG-positive patients (`7/60`) and `5/59` with both DENV and ZIKV IgG. Clarify that 1 of the 7 ZIKV IgG-positive patients lacked a DENV IgG measurement (giving $5/6$ among dual-tested ZIKV+ patients).
  3. **Zero viral load in qRT-PCR-positive cohort (Table 1):** Table 1 lists the 2016–2017 viral load IQR as `2.37 × 10^5 [0 - 1.05 × 10^7]`, whereas Methods states that only qRT-PCR-positive patients were included. Replace `0` with the actual lower quartile (or limit of quantification) for qRT-PCR-positive samples, or clarify if some patients were included via NS1/IgM with undetectable RNA.
