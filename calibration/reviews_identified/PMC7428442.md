# Peer Review: SARS-CoV-2 Has Been Circulating in Northern Italy Since December 2019: Evidence From Environmental Monitoring (PMC7428442)

**Overall Score:** **3.6 / 10.0** (Flawed / Overstated)
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.9/3.5 | Claims: 0.7/2.5 | Biology: 1.0/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.0/2.0

## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis

La Rosa et al. analyze 40 archival composite raw wastewater samples collected between October 9, 2019 and February 28, 2020 from five wastewater treatment plants (WTPs) across three northern Italian cities (Milan, Turin, and Bologna), alongside 24 pre-pandemic blank control samples collected between September 2018 and June 2019. Using four molecular assays—the standard Corman et al. (2020) `E` and `RdRp` real-time RT-qPCR assays, a newly designed nested RT-PCR targeting a 332 bp fragment of `ORF1ab` (nsp14), and a newly designed real-time RT-qPCR targeting the same `ORF1ab` (nsp14) region—the authors report that while 0 of 40 samples tested positive with the reference `E` and `RdRp` RT-qPCR assays, 18 of 40 samples tested positive by nested RT-PCR (confirmed by Sanger sequencing of the 332 bp amplicon) and 26 of 40 tested positive by the new `ORF1ab` RT-qPCR. Notably, positive detections are reported in Milan and Turin on December 18, 2019 (by nested RT-PCR and/or RT-qPCR) and in Bologna on December 10, 2019 (by RT-qPCR only), leading the authors to conclude that SARS-CoV-2 was circulating at substantial community levels across three distinct regions of northern Italy more than two months before the first autochthonous Italian case was diagnosed on February 20, 2020.

Extraordinary retrospective claims of pre-pandemic viral circulation require rigorous molecular and epidemiological validation. Upon forensic examination of the assay performance, Table 2 data, and quantitative viral concentration trajectories, the study's central conclusions are undermined by severe technical anomalies: (1) complete failure (0/40 positive) of the internationally validated Corman `E` and `RdRp` RT-qPCR assays, even on late-February 2020 samples supposedly containing $3.3 \times 10^4$ to $5.6 \times 10^4$ genome copies/L (`g.c./L`); (2) high discordance between the authors' two in-house `ORF1ab` assays targeting the exact same genomic region (11 samples positive by single-step RT-qPCR failed to amplify in 75-cycle nested RT-PCR, and 3 samples positive by nested RT-PCR were completely negative by RT-qPCR); (3) use of a high-copy synthetic DNA positive control (`10^4–10^5 copies/uL` stock diluted to `10^2`) containing the exact nested PCR target sequence inside the laboratory, creating a classic nested-PCR carry-over contamination vulnerability where Sanger sequencing of a conserved 332 bp `ORF1ab` amplicon cannot distinguish sample RNA from the synthetic control; and (4) epidemiologically implausible quantitative trajectories in Table 2, in which viral loads in Milan, Turin, and Bologna appear simultaneously in mid-December 2019 at $10^3\text{--}2.9 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$ and then remain flat or decline for two full months across January and mid-February 2020 rather than exhibiting exponential epidemic growth.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Evaluation

* **Claim 1:** SARS-CoV-2 was actively circulating in Milan and Turin by December 18, 2019 and in Bologna by December 10, 2019, at concentrations comparable to late-February 2020 outbreak levels. **[Fragile]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** In Table 2, Bologna sample `3375` (collected December 10, 2019)—nearly three weeks before the first cluster was even recognized in Wuhan, China—is reported to have a viral load of $2.9 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$ by the in-house `ORF1ab` RT-qPCR, which is *higher* than every single Milan sample from January and February 2020 (including February 27, 2020 during the active Lombardy outbreak) and nearly identical to Bologna on January 29, 2020 ($3.3 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$). Yet this same December 10, 2019 Bologna sample (`3375`) with $2.9 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$ tested **negative** by the nested RT-PCR targeting the exact same `ORF1ab` region and **negative** by both Corman `E` and `RdRp` RT-qPCR assays. Similarly, in Turin, samples `3333` and `3335` on December 18, 2019 are reported as positive ($1.2 \times 10^3\text{ g.c./L}$), followed by two months of flat or declining estimates ($7.4 \times 10^2$, $1.2 \times 10^3$, $5.6 \times 10^2$, $6.0 \times 10^2$, $4.7 \times 10^2$, and $2.9 \times 10^2\text{ g.c./L}$ on February 25, 2020) before jumping 200-fold overnight to $5.6 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$ on February 26, 2020. An unmitigated respiratory virus with $R_0 \approx 2.5\text{--}3.5$ introduced into three major metropolitan areas (serving $>2.7\text{ million}$ inhabitants) in early/mid-December 2019 cannot remain at a steady plateau of $5 \times 10^2\text{--}10^3\text{ g.c./L}$ for 10 consecutive weeks without exponential growth or hospital surge.

* **Claim 2:** The newly designed `ORF1ab` nested RT-PCR (18/40 positive) and `ORF1ab` real-time RT-qPCR (26/40 positive) provide specific and reliable detection of SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater where the standard Corman et al. `E` and `RdRp` assays failed completely (0/40 positive). **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** While the authors verified in silico primer specificity and tested cross-reactivity against standard human CoVs (`229E`, `NL63`, `OC43`, `MERS-CoV`, `SARS-CoV`), the internal performance across the 40 wastewater samples (Table 2) reveals severe assay discordance and calibration anomalies:
    1. *Discordance between nested RT-PCR and single-plex RT-qPCR on the same target:* Both assays amplify the exact same `ORF1ab` nsp14 region (`2020-nCoV_Orf1ab` primers `Fw 5'-TGATGATACTCTGACGTTC-3'`, etc.). A two-round nested PCR ($35 + 40 = 75$ cycles total) is theoretically more sensitive than a single-round 45-cycle qPCR. Yet 11 of the 26 RT-qPCR-positive samples—including the highest early sample, Bologna `3375` on December 10, 2019 at $2.9 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$ (5-fold above the LOQ)—tested completely negative by nested RT-PCR, whereas 3 samples positive by nested RT-PCR (`3215` Milan Dec 18, `3218` Milan Jan 10, `3333` Turin Dec 18) yielded zero signal in the RT-qPCR.
    2. *Unexplained failure of Corman `E` and `RdRp` on high-titer samples:* The Corman `E` assay has an analytical limit of detection of $\sim 3.9\text{--}5.2$ copies/reaction and has been successfully used worldwide to detect SARS-CoV-2 in wastewater at concentrations of $10^3\text{--}10^4\text{ g.c./L}$. In Table 2, samples `3375`, `3376`, and `3349` are reported by the in-house RT-qPCR to contain $2.9 \times 10^4$ to $5.6 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$, yet the Corman `E` and `RdRp` assays were 100% negative (0/40) even after BSA addition to reduce inhibition.

* **Claim 3:** Sanger sequencing of the 332 bp nested RT-PCR amplicon from 18 wastewater samples (`MT843229–MT843240`) confirms authentic SARS-CoV-2 RNA in December 2019 wastewater. **[Fragile]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** First, although 18 samples are reported as positive by nested RT-PCR and confirmed by Sanger sequencing, only 12 GenBank accession numbers (`MT843229` to `MT843240` inclusive is 12 accessions, not 18) are provided in Section 3.2. Second, and more critically, the authors state in Section 2.3 that they synthesized and amplified a **"custom synthetic DNA vector containing the sequence frame positions 15251–15670 of the reference genome Wuhan-Hu-1 (NC_045512.2)"** (prepared at $10^4\text{--}10^5\text{ copies/}\mu\text{L}$ and diluted to $10^2\text{ copies/}\mu\text{L}$) as the positive control for the nested PCR. Because this synthetic DNA control lacks engineered diagnostic marker mutations (unlike mutation-tagged controls designed to distinguish control carry-over from wild-type virus) and spans a highly conserved 332 bp region of nsp14 identical to Wuhan-Hu-1, Sanger sequencing of the nested amplicon yields the exact same Wuhan-Hu-1 sequence whether the template originated from authentic viral RNA or trace aerosol/pipette contamination from the synthetic DNA positive control or concurrent February 2020 clinical/wastewater samples.

* **Claim 4:** Twenty-four pre-pandemic wastewater "blank" samples collected between September 2018 and June 2019 tested negative across all assays, ruling out false-positive background amplification. **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Testing 24 archival wastewater extracts from 2018–2019 is an important negative control step demonstrating that background municipal wastewater flora do not non-specifically cross-react with the primers. However, the paper does not specify whether the 24 blank samples from 2018–2019 were extracted and amplified *interleaved* in the same PCR plates alongside the December 2019 / February 2020 samples and the synthetic positive control, or processed in separate batch runs. In nested PCR, batch separation between historical blanks and test samples does not rule out plate-level cross-contamination between February 2020 samples (or the synthetic control) and December 2019 wells on the same plate.

## 3. Technical, Statistical, and Biological Critique

### 3.1 Major Methodological and Technical Issues

1. **Nested PCR Contamination Risk with an Unmarked Synthetic Positive Control and Concurrent High-Titer Samples:**
   Open-tube two-step nested RT-PCR ($35$ first-round cycles followed by transferring $2\ \mu\text{L}$ of unpurified first-round product into $40$ second-round cycles) is exceptionally prone to amplicon and positive-control carry-over contamination. Because the laboratory simultaneously handled (i) a high-copy synthetic plasmid/DNA control matching exact Wuhan-Hu-1 positions 15251–15670, (ii) clinical bronchoalveolar lavage fluid from an Italian COVID-19 patient, and (iii) late-February 2020 outbreak wastewater samples, and because no non-overlapping genomic regions (e.g., `S`, `N`, `ORF8`, or lineage-defining SNPs such as `C241T`, `C3037T`, `C14408T`, `A23403G`) were amplified to verify distinct viral genotypes or rule out synthetic plasmid carry-over, a single conserved 332 bp amplicon cannot definitively authenticate December 2019 circulation.

2. **Extrapolation Below the Limit of Quantification (LOQ) and Extreme Calibration Offset:**
   The authors report that the limit of detection (`LOD`) of the in-house `ORF1ab` RT-qPCR in spiked wastewater was $5.9 \times 10^2\text{ g.c./L}$ (`Ct = 38.29`) and the limit of quantification (`LOQ`) was $5.9 \times 10^3\text{ g.c./L}$. Out of the 26 RT-qPCR-positive wastewater samples in Table 2, **23 samples (88.5%) fall below the LOQ** (ranging from $1.7 \times 10^2$ to $2.3 \times 10^3\text{ g.c./L}$, corresponding to late-cycle $C_t \approx 36\text{--}40$), with 12 samples even falling below the stated `LOD` of $5.9 \times 10^2\text{ g.c./L}$. Moreover, the standard curve slope was $-3.727$ (corresponding to a low amplification efficiency of $85.5\%$, outside the standard MIQE acceptable range of $90\text{--}110\%$). Treating late-cycle signals ($C_t > 37\text{--}39$) in an $85.5\%$-efficiency SYBR/TaqMan wastewater assay as quantitative evidence of community circulation—especially when 11 of those 26 samples failed to amplify in 75-cycle nested PCR—is technically fragile.

3. **Epidemiological Inconsistency of the Quantitative Time Series:**
   In Figure 2 and Table 2, the inferred viral load in Bologna on December 10, 2019 ($2.9 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$) and January 29, 2020 ($3.3 \times 10^4\text{ g.c./L}$) exceeds all Milan samples through February 27, 2020 ($1.7 \times 10^2\text{--}2.3 \times 10^3\text{ g.c./L}$), even though Lombardy (Milan) was the epicenter of the Italian epidemic in late February 2020 while Emilia-Romagna (Bologna) experienced a later rise. Furthermore, the complete absence of exponential growth in viral concentrations between December 10/18, 2019 and late February 2020 across all three cities contradicts the transmission dynamics of SARS-CoV-2.

### 3.2 Minor Issues and Reporting Discrepancies

1. **Discrepancy Between 18 Sequenced Samples and 12 GenBank Accessions:**
   Section 3.2 states that 18 samples tested positive by nested RT-PCR and were confirmed by Sanger sequencing, but lists only 12 GenBank accession numbers (`MT843229–MT843240`), without explaining why 6 of the 18 sequenced amplicons were omitted from GenBank or which Table 2 sample IDs correspond to `MT843229–MT843240`.
2. **Truncated Final Row in Table 2 and Missing Raw $C_t$ Values:**
   Table 2 reports converted genome copies/L (`g.c./L`) rather than the raw duplicate $C_t$ values and standard deviations for each replicate well, and the final row of Table 2 (the fourth Bologna sample, presumably from February 2020) was truncated in the markdown conversion. Reporting raw per-well $C_t$ values and how single-replicate versus duplicate detections were adjudicated at $C_t > 38$ is essential under MIQE guidelines.
3. **Typographical Errors in Figure Captions:**
   In the Figure 1 and Figure 2 captions, *"inhabitants"* is misspelled twice as *"inhibitants"*, and *"quantitative"* is misspelled as *"quatitative"*.
