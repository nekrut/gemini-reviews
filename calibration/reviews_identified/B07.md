### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD v2.1.1), a harmonized resource of 125,748 whole exomes and 15,708 whole genomes from unrelated human individuals spanning six global ancestry groups, yielding 14.9 million exonic and 229.9 million genomic high-quality variants after random-forest quality filtering and relatedness/platform quality control. Focusing on 443,769 high-confidence predicted loss-of-function (pLoF) variants curated by the LOFTEE algorithm, the authors construct a context- and methylation-aware neutral mutational model ($r = 0.979$ for synonymous variants across transcripts after base-level coverage correction) and introduce the loss-of-function observed/expected upper bound fraction (LOEUF, the 90% upper confidence bound on the Poisson observed/expected ratio) for 19,197 protein-coding genes. The authors validate LOEUF against orthogonal benchmarks—including known haploinsufficient vs. autosomal recessive disease genes, 6,735 rare exonic deletion structural variants ($r = 0.13, P = 9.8 \times 10^{-68}$), 389 mouse heterozygous lethal knockouts ($P = 10^{-78}$), human CRISPR essential screens ($P = 9 \times 10^{-71}$), protein-interaction network degree, GTEx multi-tissue and isoform-specific expression—and demonstrate its utility in rare-disease *de novo* burden enrichment (ID/DD, autism, schizophrenia) and common-variant stratified LD-score regression across 658 GWAS traits (276 independent traits). All aggregate variant frequency tables, constraint metrics, browser tools, QC pipelines (`gnomad_qc`), analysis scripts (`gnomad_lof`), LOFTEE software (`loftee`), and a complete Docker container are openly available.

This is an exemplary genomic resource and population-genetic modeling paper. Replacing the dichotomous ExAC pLI threshold with the continuous, uncertainty-penalized LOEUF score directly addresses small-gene variance while retaining interpretability across the constraint spectrum. The primary technical issues are minor: (1) there is a numerical discrepancy between the main text ("the rate of pLoF de novo variation in cases is 15-fold higher in genes belonging to the most constrained LOEUF decile", line 81) and the corresponding Figure 5a legend ("pLoF variants in the most constrained decile of the genome are approximately 11-fold more likely to be found in cases compared to controls"); (2) because LOEUF is defined as the upper bound of the 90% confidence interval around $O/E$, it intentionally conflates truly unconstrained genes with short, underpowered genes ($E < 10$, ~27.9% of genes), which introduces a strong negative correlation with coding sequence length ($\beta = -1.07 \times 10^{-4}, P < 10^{-100}$, Extended Data Fig. 7d) that requires careful adjustment in downstream users' analyses; and (3) comparing mean LOEUF across downsampled ancestry cohorts ($n = 8,128$, Extended Data Fig. 8d, e) using a simple two-sided $t$-test across 927 genes treats genes as independent observations without accounting for shared mutational model calibration on predominantly European coverage/methylation profiles. These points can be refined through straightforward textual and statistical clarifications.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Harmonized joint calling, random-forest filtering, and LOFTEE curation yield a high-precision, high-recall catalogue of 443,769 high-confidence pLoF variants across 141,456 humans: [Well Supported].**  
  Variant calling precision (>99% SNVs, >98.5% indels) and recall (>90% SNVs, >82% indels) are benchmarked against NA12878 and synthetic diploid gold standards (Extended Data Fig. 2), chromosome 20 transmitted singletons in 4,780 trios (>97.8% retention, Extended Data Fig. 3a–d), and 375 validated *de novo* mutations (97.3% SNV and 98% indel recall). LOFTEE removes common annotation artefacts while preserving ClinVar pathogenic variants and enriching for high MAPS singleton fractions (Fig. 2a, b).

* **The methylation- and coverage-corrected mutational model and LOEUF metric accurately quantify continuous selective constraint against heterozygous gene inactivation: [Well Supported].**  
  Incorporating tissue-averaged CpG methylation tiers and logarithmic low-coverage (<40x) calibration achieves $r = 0.979$ between observed and expected synonymous variants across transcripts (Extended Data Fig. 6f). LOEUF strongly separates haploinsufficient, autosomal recessive, and olfactory receptor genes (Fig. 3a), correlates with independent WGS deletion SVs (Fig. 3b), and aligns with mouse heterozygous lethality and human cellular essentiality (Fig. 3c, d).

* **Constrained genes and transcripts exhibit higher protein-network connectivity, broader multi-tissue expression, and enrichment for both rare *de novo* neurodevelopmental variants and common polygenic GWAS heritability: [Well Supported].**  
  Regression analyses control for coding sequence length (or restrict to $\ge 10$ expected pLoFs), and the stratified LD-score regression ($\hat{\tau}^*$ across 276 independent traits, Fig. 5b, c; Extended Data Fig. 10) conditions on 74 baseline functional, LD, and MAF annotations as well as GTEx brain expression and gene size.

* **Lower mean LOEUF in African/African American individuals ($0.49$ vs. $0.62$ in Europeans at matched $n = 8,128$) reflects more efficient purifying selection in a population with larger effective population size ($N_e$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While theoretically consistent with population genetics ($N_e s$) and previous literature, the observed shift in unique variant counts (` observed / expected `) at a fixed sample size of $n = 8,128$ is also shaped by demographic history (exponential recent growth in Europeans vs. higher baseline diversity and different site frequency spectrum shape in African/African American samples, including recent European admixture in African Americans) when the expected count $E$ is calibrated from a single global synonymous saturation curve (Extended Data Fig. 6c, d). If the synonymous calibration curve is fitted per population, part of the neutral SFS discovery difference is absorbed into $E$, whereas if $E$ is scaled slightly differently across demographic histories, $O/E$ shifts.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reconcile the Numerical Discrepancy Between Main Text (15-fold) and Figure 5a Legend (11-fold) for ID/DD De Novo Enrichment
* **WHY:** In the section "Constraint informs disease aetiologies" (line 81), the text states that in 5,305 intellectual disability / developmental disorder cases vs. 2,179 controls, "the rate of pLoF de novo variation in cases is 15-fold higher in genes belonging to the most constrained LOEUF decile, compared with controls (Fig. 5a)." However, the legend of Fig. 5a states: "pLoF variants in the most constrained decile of the genome are approximately 11-fold more likely to be found in cases compared to controls," and the plotted point in Fig. 5a lies just above 10 on the y-axis.
* **WHAT:** Verify the exact rate ratio in the first LOEUF decile for the 5,305 ID/DD cases vs. 2,179 controls and make the number consistent between the main text and the Fig. 5a legend.
* **HOW:** Check whether 15-fold referred to an earlier definition (e.g., pLI > 0.9 or protein-truncating variants before LOFTEE filtering) and update line 81 of the main text (or the Fig. 5a legend) so both report the exact rate ratio plotted in Fig. 5a with its 95% confidence interval.

#### 2. Provide Paired Synonymous $O/E$ Controls in the Cross-Population LOEUF Comparison
* **WHY:** In Extended Data Fig. 8d, e, the authors compare mean pLoF LOEUF across downsampled populations ($n = 8,128$) for 927 (or 865) well-powered genes ($E \ge 10$) and attribute the lower mean LOEUF in African/African American samples (0.49 vs. 0.62, $P = 4.1 \times 10^{-14}$) to increased efficiency of purifying selection under higher $N_e$. However, the number of distinct segregating variants observed in a sample of $n = 8,128$ depends on the neutral site frequency spectrum (recent population expansion vs. long-term $N_e$ and admixture).
* **WHAT:** Confirm how the expected variant count $E$ was calibrated for each downsampled population ($n = 8,128$) and report the corresponding synonymous and missense $O/E$ (and upper-bound) distributions across the same 865 genes in each population.
* **HOW:** Plot the population-specific synonymous $O/E$ ratios alongside Extended Data Fig. 8e to demonstrate that the neutral expectation model is unbiased across all five ancestry groups ($O/E_{\text{syn}} \approx 1.00$ in AFR, AMR, EAS, NFE, SAS), and use a paired test (Wilcoxon signed-rank or paired $t$-test across the 927 matched genes) rather than an unpaired two-sample $t$-test.

#### 3. Release a Companion Length-Decoupled Effect-Size Metric Alongside LOEUF for Downstream Regression Users
* **WHY:** Because LOEUF is the 90% upper confidence bound of $O/E$, a short gene with $O = 0, E = 2$ receives $\text{LOEUF} = 1.34$ (placing it in the least constrained deciles), whereas a long gene with $O = 10, E = 20$ (MLE $O/E = 0.50$) receives a much lower LOEUF. While the authors carefully control for CDS length or filter to $E \ge 10$ in their own regressions, downstream clinical and bioinformatics users frequently use LOEUF deciles directly as a gene-level covariate, inadvertently introducing a strong gene-length bias ($\beta = -1.07 \times 10^{-4}, P < 10^{-100}$) against short conserved genes (such as hormones, antimicrobial peptides, and small transcription factors).
* **WHAT:** Emphasize in the main text and release table usage notes that high LOEUF (>1.0) should not be interpreted as evidence of LoF tolerance unless $E \ge 10$, and highlight the empirical Bayes shrunk point estimate of $O/E$ (or lower bound for LoF tolerance) in the downloadable constraint table.
* **HOW:** Compute a hierarchical Poisson-Gamma empirical Bayes posterior mean of $O/E$ (shrinking noisy small-gene ratios toward the genome-wide prior median of 0.48 rather than pushing all small genes to the upper tail) and include it alongside `oe_lof`, `oe_lof_lower`, and `oe_lof_upper` (`LOEUF`) in the public constraint table so users testing for *unconstrained* genes can use `oe_lof_lower` or the posterior mean.
