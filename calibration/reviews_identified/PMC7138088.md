**Manuscript Title:** Cross-species transmission of the newly identified coronavirus 2019-nCoV.  
**Authors:** Wei Ji, Wei Wang, Xiaofang Zhao, Junjie Zai, Xingguang Li  
**Journal / Preprint:** Journal of Medical Virology (2020) | DOI: 10.1002/jmv.25682  
**Overall Score:** **1.8 / 10.0** (Fundamentally Invalid)  
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.4/3.5 | Claims: 0.3/2.5 | Biology: 0.3/2.0 | Reproducibility: 0.8/2.0

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Ji et al. analyze the reference genome of 2019-nCoV (`MN908947`, SARS-CoV-2) alongside 271 GenBank coronavirus sequences ($>19,000$ bp) to investigate its evolutionary origin, potential recombination history, and intermediate wildlife reservoir. Using `RAxML` (GTR+G) phylogenetics, `SimPlot` sliding-window similarity plots (500-bp window, 30-bp step), and squared Euclidean distances of Relative Synonymous Codon Usage (RSCU) computed in `CodonW` across 2019-nCoV, `bat-SL-CoVZC45`, and eight vertebrate species (*Bungarus multicinctus*, *Naja atra*, *Rhinolophus sinicus*, *Gallus gallus*, *Marmota*, *Homo sapiens*, *Manis javanica*, and *Erinaceus europaeus*), the authors conclude that 2019-nCoV arose via homologous recombination in the Spike glycoprotein between a bat coronavirus and an unknown coronavirus, and that snakes (*B. multicinctus* and *N. atra*) are the most likely wildlife animal reservoir because they exhibit the smallest squared Euclidean RSCU distance ($13.54$ and $16.69$) to 2019-nCoV.

While the basic maximum likelihood tree correctly places 2019-nCoV as a sister lineage to bat SARS-like betacoronaviruses (`bat-SL-CoVZC45` and `bat-SL-CoVZXC21`), the manuscript's central conclusions—that snakes are the intermediate reservoir of SARS-CoV-2 and that Spike arose via homologous recombination—are fundamentally invalid due to catastrophic data bias, invalid evolutionary assumptions, missing statistical tests, and biological implausibility. Specifically, the authors computed RSCU for the two snake species and *Rhinolophus sinicus* from tiny, biased sets of partial GenBank entries (only 38 CDSs for *B. multicinctus*, 64 CDSs for *N. atra*, and 10 CDSs for *R. sinicus*) while using whole-genome CDS annotations ($28,947$ to $49,453$ CDSs) for the other vertebrates; they mistook the intrinsic AU-rich mutational bias of all coronaviruses (shared identically by `bat-SL-CoVZC45` in Table 1) for translational adaptation to ectothermic reptiles; and they inferred Spike recombination solely from a drop in pairwise sequence similarity to `ZC45/ZXC21` without a donor sequence or formal recombination test. The snake reservoir claim and recombination conclusion cannot be salvaged and must be retracted or completely replaced.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **2019-nCoV clusters phylogenetically within Betacoronavirus as a sister lineage to bat SARS-like coronaviruses `bat-SL-CoVZC45` and `bat-SL-CoVZXC21`: [Well Supported].**  
  Among the coronavirus sequences available in GenBank as of mid-January 2020, the `RAxML` GTR+G phylogeny (Figure 1) accurately places `MN908947` closest to the Zhoushan bat sarbecoviruses `ZC45` and `ZXC21`. (Note, however, that Section 2.2 and 3.1 misstate the geographic origin of `ZC45/ZXC21` as Nanjing rather than Zhoushan, Zhejiang, and report conflicting total sequence counts of 272 vs. 276).

* **2019-nCoV shares the most similar synonymous codon usage bias with snakes (*Bungarus multicinctus* and *Naja atra*), indicating that snakes are its likely wildlife viral reservoir: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This claim fails on three independent grounds:
  1. *Severe sample-size and ascertainment artifact in host CDS data (Section 2.3, line 77):* For human, chicken, pangolin, marmot, and hedgehog, the authors calculated RSCU across whole-genome coding sets ($16.7\text{M}–40.7\text{M}$ codons from $28,947–49,453$ genes). In contrast, for *Bungarus multicinctus*, *Naja atra*, and *Rhinolophus sinicus*, they used only **38 CDSs (5,381 codons)**, **64 CDSs (9,587 codons)**, and **10 CDSs (8,081 codons)**, respectively, downloaded ad hoc from GenBank. In GenBank, small sets of cloned elapid snake cDNAs are heavily dominated by three-finger toxins, phospholipase A2, and mitochondrial transcripts that have atypical nucleotide composition and do not represent the snake nuclear genome.
  2. *Falsification by the manuscript's own Table 1 control (`bat-SL-CoVZC45`):* Inspection of Table 1 and Figure 3A shows that the known bat coronavirus `bat-SL-CoVZC45` has virtually identical RSCU values to `2019-nCoV` across all 59 sense codons (squared Euclidean distance between `2019-nCoV` and `bat-SL-CoVZC45` is $\approx 0.30$), and `bat-SL-CoVZC45` is *also* much closer in RSCU to the 38 *B. multicinctus* genes than to *Rhinolophus sinicus* (10 genes) or humans. By the authors' logic, a confirmed bat coronavirus (`bat-SL-CoVZC45`) would also be classified as a snake virus. In reality, all coronaviruses possess strong U/A-ending codon bias ($RSCU > 1.5$ for UUU, UUA, CUU, GUU, UCU, CCU, ACU, GCU) driven by viral genome-wide $C \to U$ mutational pressure and RNA secondary structure, not host tRNA adaptation.
  3. *Biological implausibility:* Betacoronaviruses exclusively infect endothermic mammals; no betacoronavirus has ever been shown to replicate in ectothermic reptiles, and elapid snake ACE2 orthologues lack the critical contact residues required to bind the SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain.

* **Homologous recombination occurred within the 2019-nCoV Spike glycoprotein between Clade A bat coronaviruses (`ZC45/ZXC21`) and an origin-unknown coronavirus, explaining cross-species transmission: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Figure 2, the `SimPlot` curve compares `2019-nCoV` against only three grouped clades (Clade A: `ZC45/ZXC21`; Clade B: `BM48-31/BtKY72`; Clade C: SARS-CoV). A drop in nucleotide similarity between `2019-nCoV` and `ZC45/ZXC21` in the 5' half of the Spike gene (positions 1–783 bp, encoding the hypervariable N-terminal domain) without any crossing of similarity lines (bootscan crossover) or identification of a second parental lineage is the hallmark of accelerated diversifying evolution and lineage sorting in surface glycoproteins, not proof of homologous recombination. Furthermore, the text refers to `"Bootscanning plot analysis (data not shown)"` (line 177) and reports zero statistical tests for recombination (e.g., `RDP4`, `GENECONV`, `3Seq`, or `PHI` test).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Ad Hoc GenBank Gene Snippets with Complete Vertebrate Reference Transcriptomes in RSCU Calculations
* **WHY:** Computing RSCU from 10 bat genes and 38–64 snake genes while using $>28,000$ whole-genome CDSs for mammals/birds introduces extreme sampling bias and invalidates the Euclidean distance rankings in Figure 3B and Table 1.
* **WHAT:** Re-compute host RSCU profiles strictly using complete genome-wide CDS annotations for all comparator species (including published whole genomes of *Naja naja* / *Notechis scutatus* / *Ophiophagus hannah* and *Rhinolophus ferrumequinum* / *R. sinicus*).
* **HOW:** Download the RefSeq/Ensembl whole-genome `.cds.all.fa` files ($>15,000$ CDSs per species) for each vertebrate host, re-calculate genome-wide RSCU and effective number of codons ($ENC$ vs. $GC3s$ plot) in `CodonW`, and verify that snake whole-genome RSCU clusters with other amniotes rather than with AU-rich coronaviruses.

#### 2. Remove the Unsupported Snake Reservoir Claim and Control for Viral Mutational Bias ($GC3$)
* **WHY:** As shown by `bat-SL-CoVZC45` in Table 1, U/A-ending codon preference is an ancestral, clade-wide property of betacoronaviruses caused by directional mutational bias ($C \to U$), rendering raw RSCU Euclidean distance between a single viral genome and host genomes invalid for reservoir identification.
* **WHAT:** Retract the claim that snakes are the wildlife reservoir of 2019-nCoV from the Abstract, Results, and Discussion, and perform a neutrality plot ($GC12$ vs. $GC3$) and $ENC$-plot analysis across the 272 coronavirus genomes to quantify the relative contributions of mutational bias versus translational selection.
* **HOW:** Compute $GC3s$ and $ENC$ for each coronavirus in the alignment and compare against Wright's expected neutral curve $ENC = 2 + GC3s + \frac{29}{GC3s^2 + (1 - GC3s)^2}$ to show that coronavirus codon usage is primarily governed by nucleotide composition rather than host translation machinery.

#### 3. Perform Formal Recombination Testing With Topological Incongruence Verification
* **WHY:** Visual inspection of a single `SimPlot` similarity drop against `ZC45/ZXC21` without a second parental sequence or statistical test cannot distinguish hypervariable S1 divergence from homologous recombination.
* **WHAT:** Run formal recombination detection algorithms (`RDP4` suite: `RDP, GENECONV, BootScan, MaxChi, Chimaera, SiScan, 3Seq`) and construct separate Maximum Likelihood phylogenies for `ORF1ab`, the putative recombinant region (`S` 1–783 bp), and the remainder of `S` / `ORF3a–N`.
* **HOW:** Report Bonferroni-corrected $p$-values across the `RDP4` methods and show side-by-side ML trees with 1,000 bootstrap replicates for the non-recombinant backbone versus the putative Spike segment to test whether tree topologies actually conflict with significant bootstrap support.

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** The manuscript contains contradictory sample counts, incorrect viral CDS counts, and geographic misattributions.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Section 2.1 vs. Section 3.1 (lines 59, 72, 85, 177):** Reconcile the total number of analysed coronavirus genomes: Section 2.1, Figure 1 caption, and Discussion state **272** genomes (`271 + 1`), whereas Section 3.1 line 85 states *"Phylogenetic analysis of 276 coronavirus genomes... Clade C including 267 coronavirus strains"* ($1 + 2 + 2 + 267 = 272 \ne 276$).
  2. **Section 2.3 (line 77):** Correct `"2019-nCoV genome (1CDS's, 9672 codons), bat-SL-CoVZC45 genome (1CDS's, 9680 codons)"`—coronaviruses contain multiple distinct ORFs (`ORF1ab, S, ORF3a, E, M, ORF6, ORF7a, ORF7b, ORF8, N`), not a single concatenated CDS.
  3. **Section 2.2 and 3.1 (lines 66 and 85):** Correct the sampling location of `bat-SL-CoVZC45` and `bat-SL-CoVZXC21` from `"Nanjing, China"` to `"Zhoushan, Zhejiang, China"` (Nanjing was the location of the sequencing institute, not the bat collection site).
  4. **Abstract and Text (lines 35, 51, 88, 90, 177, 183):** Correct recurring grammatical and spelling errors (`"may occurred"` $\to$ `"may have occurred"`, `"bat coronovirus"` $\to$ `"bat coronavirus"`, `"All avaliable"` $\to$ `"All available"`, `"manis javanica"` $\to$ `"Manis javanica"`).
