# Critical Peer Review: Reduced signal for polygenic adaptation of height in UK Biobank (PMC6428572)

**Overall Score:** **9.4 / 10.0** (Exemplary)
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 3.3/3.5 | Claims: 2.4/2.5 | Biology: 1.9/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.8/2.0

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis
Berg et al. re-evaluate one of the most celebrated empirical examples of human polygenic adaptation—directional selection on standing height across Eurasia—by comparing six GWAS summary statistic datasets (`GIANT`, `R15-sibs` and its bug-fixed replacement `R15-sibs-updated`, `UKB-Eur`, `UKB-GB-NoPCs`, `UKB-GB`, and a newly generated 19,268-pair `UKB-sibs` family GWAS) across three distinct population genetic frameworks: (1) polygenic score overdispersion ($Q_X$) and geographic clines (`1000 Genomes` + `Human Origins`), (2) Singleton Density Score (`tSDS`) correlations across the p-value spectrum and within LD-pruned top hits, and (3) bivariate LD Score regression (`LDSC`). The authors demonstrate that the strong signals previously reported in `GIANT` ($p < 10^{-5}$ to $10^{-20}$) and `R15-sibs` ($p \sim 10^{-17}$ to $10^{-75}$) collapse or dramatically attenuate when effect sizes and SNP ascertainments are drawn from homogeneously structured UK Biobank cohorts (`UKB-GB` and `UKB-sibs`). Going beyond empirical refutation, the authors trace the exact statistical mechanisms driving the false positives: residual North-South European stratification (`GBR` vs. `TSI`) in `GIANT`, a computational bug in the `plink` `QFAM` pipeline used for `R15-sibs`, physical linkage covariance among nearby causal/tag SNPs ($pQ_X(k)$ in Appendix 1) when expanding SNP ascertainments (`20k` SNPs), and a previously unrecognized interaction between background selection (`BGS`, parameterized via McVicker's $B$) and allele frequency differentiation that induces spurious bivariate `LDSC` slopes under stratification (Appendix 3). This work is a model of self-correcting quantitative evolutionary biology.

## 2. Granular Claims & Evidence Audit

* **Claim 1:** *"Here we show that signals of selection based on population-level polygenic scores and singleton density scores are dramatically reduced when starting from GWAS data in the UK Biobank, relative to previously-analyzed cohorts... Taken together, these observations lead us to conclude that what once appeared an ironclad example of population genetic evidence for polygenic adaptation now lacks any strong support."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Critique:** The side-by-side comparison across all six GWAS cohorts in `Figure 1` (polygenic score clines) and `Figure 2` (`tSDS` profiles) is exhaustive. Using ~1,700 LD-thinned top loci, the European latitudinal cline $p$-value drops from $3 \times 10^{-7}$ (`GIANT`) and $2 \times 10^{-17}$ (`R15-sibs`) to $p = 0.032$ (`UKB-GB`, with overall $Q_X$ $p = 0.34$) and $p = 0.025$ (`UKB-sibs`, $Q_X$ $p = 0.65$), while the Eurasian longitudinal cline (`5e-14` in `GIANT`) vanishes completely ($p = 0.94$ in `UKB-GB`, $p = 0.72$ in `UKB-sibs`). Similarly, the monotonic genome-wide rank correlation between `tSDS` and height $-\log_{10} p$ observed in `GIANT` ($\rho = 0.079, p = 1.55 \times 10^{-65}$ via 200-block jackknife) and `R15-sibs` ($\rho = 0.097, p = 7.87 \times 10^{-75}$) disappears in `UKB-GB` ($\rho = 0.009, p = 0.077$) and `UKB-sibs` ($\rho = -0.011, p = 0.026$). When `R15-sibs` is replaced by `R15-sibs-updated` (after Robinson & Visscher fixed the `QFAM` bug uncovered during this study), all selection signals in `R15-sibs-updated` also vanish (`Figure 1—figure supplement 1`, `Figure 2—figure supplement 1`, `Figure 4—figure supplement 1`).

* **Claim 2:** *"Specifically, the difference between GIANT and UKB-GB effect sizes is correlated with the difference in allele frequencies between northern and southern European populations (Figure 3B; $r^2 = 0.06, p = 6.6 \times 10^{-24}$)... indicating that a component of the difference between GIANT and UKB-GB is related to the major axis of population structure across Europe."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Critique:** The authors isolate the confounding vector by contrasting allele frequency differences between `1000 Genomes` British (`GBR`) and Tuscan (`TSI`) samples against the difference in estimated effect sizes ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{GIANT}} - \hat{\beta}_{\text{UKB-GB}}$) at 1,648 independent `GIANT`-ascertained SNPs (`Figure 3B`) and similarly for `TSI` vs. `CHB` (`Figure 3—figure supplement 1A`, $r^2 = 0.04, p = 1.43 \times 10^{-15}$). Crucially, when SNPs are ascertained using `UKB-GB` $p$-values instead of `GIANT` $p$-values (`Figure 3—figure supplement 2`), the correlation drops by an order of magnitude ($r^2 = 0.005$ for `GBR-TSI`; $r^2 = 1.8 \times 10^{-4}, p = 0.26$ for `TSI-CHB`), proving that double-stratification bias (ascertaining SNPs whose $p$-values are inflated by structure and evaluating their effect sizes along the same structure axis) amplifies weak per-locus bias into massive polygenic score divergence.

* **Claim 3:** *"Specifically, we demonstrated that background selection—through its correlation with LD Score—can potentially generate a spurious LD Score regression slope."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Critique:** Previously, a positive bivariate `LDSC` slope between two traits (`GIANT`/`R15-sibs` height vs. `SDS` or vs. `[GBR-TSI]`) was interpreted as robust to unlinked stratification because pure neutral drift $F_{ST}$ is independent of `LD Score`, loading only onto the `LDSC` intercept. In `Figure 5` and `Appendix 3`, the authors show empirically and analytically that MAF-standardized squared allele frequency differences (`[GBR-TSI]^2` and `[Irish-British]^2`) and `SDS^2` increase strongly with `LD Score` ($p = 2.5 \times 10^{-25}$). In `Appendix 3` (Equations A1–A11), they derive the variance of standardized allele frequency differences in regions under background selection parameterized by McVicker's $B$-value: lower recombination and stronger purifying selection reduce local $N_e$ ($B < 1$), Simultaneously increasing both `LD Score` and local genetic drift $\propto 1/B$ (`Appendix 3—figures 1–3`). Consequently, whenever a GWAS has residual stratification along an axis correlated with `SDS`, the `BGS`-mediated coupling with `LD Score` creates a false-positive `LDSC` genetic correlation slope.

* **Claim 4:** *"In contrast to standard minimum p-value ascertainment... we observe threat of elevated $Q_X$ ($p = 1.66 \times 10^{-6}$ for 20k; $p = 9.9 \times 10^{-15}$ for HapMap5k) when ascertaining larger numbers of SNPs in UKB-GB... However, the assumptions underlying the $Q_X$ neutrality test are violated when non-independent SNPs are included."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Critique:** Rather than ignoring the fact that expanding `UKB-GB` polygenic scores to ~20,000 (`20k`) or ~5,000 (`HapMap5k`) Fourier/FGWAS windows yields small nominal $Q_X$ $p$-values, the authors develop a rigorous spatial covariance decomposition in `Appendix 1` (Equations A1–A6). They partition $Q_X = V_A^{-1} [ \text{Var}(\vec{Z}_{\text{ind}}) + \sum_{k=1}^{450} \text{Cov}_k + \text{Cov}_\infty ]$, where $pQ_X(k)$ measures the cross-population allele frequency covariance of SNP pairs separated by $k$ windows on the same chromosome (`Appendix 1—figures 1–5`). They show that for `20k` and `R15-sibs-updated-22k`, $pQ_X(k)$ spikes sharply at small $k$ ($k < 20$, physical linkage within a few megabases) and decays to zero for $k \to \infty$ (different chromosomes), demonstrating that packing multiple SNPs per LD neighborhood violates the independent-loci assumption of the multivariate normal null $2 V_A \mathbf{F}$. Even for `HapMap5k` where $pQ_X(\infty) > 0$, eigenvector decomposition into orthogonal $Q_U$ statistics (`Appendix 1—figure 6`) reveals uniform inflation across all principal components of European drift rather than divergence along a specific adaptive axis.

## 3. Concrete Recommendations for Action

1. **Incorporate an LD-aware covariance matrix into the $Q_X$ denominator for dense SNP sets:**
   * In `Appendix 1`, $Q_X$ fails for dense ascertainments (`20k` SNPs) because the denominator $2 V_A = 4 \sum_\ell \alpha_\ell^2 \epsilon_\ell (1 - \epsilon_\ell)$ omits linkage disequilibrium $D_{\ell \ell'}$ between nearby ascertained variants. Replacing the diagonal additive variance $V_A$ with a banded LD-weighted genetic variance $V_{A,\text{LD}} = 2 \sum_{\ell, \ell'} \alpha_\ell \alpha_{\ell'} \text{Cov}(g_\ell, g_{\ell'})$ estimated from a reference panel would provide a formal correction for physical linkage in `pQ_X(k)` for small $k$.
2. **Stratify bivariate `LDSC` by McVicker's $B$ and recombination rate:**
   * Since `Appendix 3` demonstrates that variation in background selection ($B$) and recombination rate drives the correlation between standardized allele frequency differentiation (`[GBR-TSI]^2`) and `LD Score`, directly verify whether adding baseline-LD annotations for $B$-statistics, recombination rate, and allele-age bins to the bivariate `LDSC` model eliminates the residual slope in `UKB-Eur` (`Figure 4—figure supplement 2`).
3. **Report effective sample sizes for `UKB-sibs` relative to `UKB-GB` in power comparisons:**
   * While `UKB-sibs` ($N = 35,524$ individuals in 19,268 sibling pairs) provides an unconfounded within-family benchmark, explicitly report the effective sample size $N_{\text{eff}}$ of the `plink` `QFAM` estimator (which combines between-family and within-family components unless strictly restricted to within-family differences, as analyzed in `Appendix 4`) versus a pure within-sibship difference estimator ($\Delta Y_{\text{sib}} \sim \Delta G_{\text{sib}}$).
