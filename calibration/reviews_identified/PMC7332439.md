### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study combines global genomic surveillance of 28,576 SARS-CoV-2 Spike sequences from GISAID (through May 29, 2020), clinical cohort analysis of 999 patients from Sheffield, UK, and in vitro pseudovirus assays to investigate the rapid global rise of the Spike D614G (A23403G) variant. Using two statistical frameworks—two-period Fisher's exact tests and permutation-based isotonic logistic regression across hierarchical geographic regions—the authors show that the 4-mutation G-clade haplotype (C241T, C3037T, RdRp P323L, and Spike D614G) recurrently displaced the ancestral D614 lineage across continents, countries, states, and cities. In the Sheffield clinical cohort, G614 infection is associated with a modestly lower E-gene RT-PCR cycle threshold ($\Delta\text{Ct} = -0.78, p = 0.037$) after adjusting for a mid-study change in extraction protocol, with no significant difference in hospitalization ($p = 0.60$). In vitro, both VSV-$\Delta$G-GFP and HIV-1 lentiviral particles pseudotyped with G614 Spike exhibit 2.6- to 9.3-fold higher infectious titers across Vero, 293T-ACE2, 293T-ACE2-TMPRSS2, and TZM-bl/ACE2 cells ($p = 9 \times 10^{-11}$), while remaining equally sensitive to neutralization by convalescent human sera ($N = 6$). Clinical and experimental data are archived on Mendeley Data (`doi:10.17632/hn3h9gdrgj.1`) and interactively accessible via `https://cov.lanl.gov/`.

The manuscript is an exemplary model of rapid, multi-modal outbreak science: it pairs population-level genomic surveillance with two orthogonal pseudovirus systems, structural modeling, clinical viral-load data, and a rigorous forensic investigation that exposes an apparent Spike mutation (S943P) as an untrimmed Nanopore adapter artifact in ARTIC amplicon 81 (Figure S7). The primary limitations are statistical and epidemiological: (1) the regional Fisher's exact and isotonic regression permutation tests treat individual sequenced cases within a region as independent observations, ignoring transmission clustering and time-varying travel importation; (2) the clinical Ct regression ($p = 0.037$) omits calendar date of sampling and hospitalization/symptom-duration covariates, which strongly covary with both Ct and the temporal sweep of G614; and (3) the observational and clinical data cannot decouple Spike D614G from the tightly linked RdRp P323L mutation (>99.9% linkage). These limitations are largely acknowledged by the authors and can be refined through cluster-robust/temporal regression adjustments and full code release.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **G614 Spike-pseudotyped VSV and lentiviral particles achieve significantly higher infectious titers in vitro without escaping polyclonal serum neutralization: [Well Supported].**  
  Across two independent vector systems (VSV-$\Delta$G-GFP and p24-normalized HIV-1 lentivirus) and four target cell lines (Figure 6A–C), G614 yields a 2.6- to 9.3-fold increase in infectious titer (mixed-effects log-normal GLM $p = 9 \times 10^{-11}$). Neutralization curves across six convalescent sera (Figure 6D–E) show equivalent or slightly enhanced neutralization of G614 relative to D614.

* **The putative S943P polymorphism in early Belgian and Sheffield raw reads is an amplicon-sequencing adapter artifact: [Well Supported].**  
  By inspecting untrimmed vs. primer-trimmed BAM files across overlapping ARTIC amplicons 80 and 81 (Figure S7), the authors definitively demonstrate that 24389A>C and 24390C>G arise from sequence homology between the Nanopore adapter 5' to amplicon 81 and the Wuhan reference genome.

* **The G614 haplotype recurrently increased in frequency across global geographic subdivisions beyond random neutral drift: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The shift toward G614 occurs in 16/17 countries, 16/16 regions, and 11/12 counties/cities by Fisher's exact test (Figure 1B) and 30/31 subcountries/states by isotonic logistic regression (Figure 3A, binomial $p = 2.98 \times 10^{-9}$). However, the statistical tests within each region assume that sampled individuals are independent ("our model assumption is that the individuals we test within a region are independent"), which is violated by branching transmission chains, household/nosocomial clusters, and massive asymmetric travel seeding from Europe during March 2020. While the cross-region binomial sign test provides macro-level robustness, region-level $p$-values are anti-conservative.

* **G614 is associated with higher upper respiratory tract viral RNA loads (lower RT-PCR Ct) in COVID-19 patients: [Fragile].**  
  In the Sheffield cohort ($N = 999$), the linear model `Ct ~ G614 + Method_2` yields a modest effect ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{G614}} = -0.776 \pm 0.372$, $p = 0.037$; Wilcoxon $p = 0.033$ in Method 1 and non-significant in Method 2). Crucially, the authors' hospitalization model shows that lower Ct (higher viral load) is strongly associated with outpatient status vs. hospitalization ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{Ct}} = +0.0694, p = 0.00014$), reflecting the well-known decline in upper respiratory viral load later in the clinical course when patients are admitted. Because G614 swept through Sheffield over time while testing expanded from hospitalized inpatients to early-stage outpatients/healthcare workers—and while the laboratory switched to `Method_2` ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{Method\_2}} = +3.20, p < 2 \times 10^{-16}$)—omitting calendar date of sampling and outpatient/inpatient status from the Ct model leaves the $p = 0.037$ association vulnerable to temporal and ascertainment confounding.

* **The D614G variant is not associated with increased hospitalization in the Sheffield cohort: [Well Supported].**  
  Both unadjusted comparisons (Fisher's exact $p = 0.66$) and multivariate logistic regressions adjusting for age, sex, extraction method, and raw or residualized Ct ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{G614}} = 0.112, p = 0.601$; residualized $p = 0.887$) show no association between D614G status and hospital admission, while age and male sex show strong, expected effects ($p < 2 \times 10^{-16}$).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Sampling Date and Clinical Setting in the Sheffield RT-PCR Ct Regression
* **WHY:** In the Sheffield dataset, G614 frequency, RT-PCR extraction protocol (`Method_1` vs. `Method_2`), and patient population (inpatient vs. outpatient screening) all shifted over calendar time between March and May 2020. Because hospitalized patients have significantly higher Ct values than outpatients ($p = 0.00014$), secular changes in testing eligibility or epidemic phase can confound the marginal $p = 0.037$ association between G614 and Ct.
* **WHAT:** Re-fit the linear regression model for Ct including sampling date (or epidemic week), patient care setting (`OP` vs. `IP`/`ICU`), and a `G614 * Method_2` interaction term.
* **HOW:** Using the Sheffield clinical table in R, run `lm(CT ~ G614 * Method_2 + Status + Age + Sex + ns(SampleDate, df = 3))` and report the adjusted effect size, 95% confidence interval, and $p$-value for `G614` both overall and stratified by `Method_1` and `Method_2`.

#### 2. Account for Overdispersion and Transmission Clustering in Regional Frequency Trajectory Tests
* **WHY:** Permuting individual sequence labels in the isotonic logistic regression (400 randomizations) and applying Fisher's exact test to raw sequence counts assumes binomial sampling of independent infections. In reality, local outbreaks propagate via branching processes and superspreading events, creating substantial extra-binomial overdispersion even under strict neutrality ($s = 0$).
* **WHAT:** Replace the individual-level permutation null in the regional time-series analysis with a block-bootstrap/day-level permutation or a quasi-binomial / beta-binomial generalized linear model that estimates a regional overdispersion parameter $\phi$, and report the estimated logistic growth rate (selection coefficient $\hat{s}$ per day) alongside $p$-values.
* **HOW:** In R, fit a quasi-binomial GLM `glm(cbind(n_G614, n_D614) ~ day, family = quasibinomial)` (or a hierarchical mixed-effects logistic model across regions `glmer(cbind(n_G614, n_D614) ~ day + (1 + day | region), family = binomial)`) to estimate the mean and variance of the daily log-odds slope across regions while accounting for day-to-day clustering.

#### 3. Explicitly Test the Partial 3-Mutation Haplotype (TTCG) vs. Full 4-Mutation Haplotype (TTTG)
* **WHY:** Figure S5 highlights a crucial natural control: early lineages in China and Germany carried Spike D614G, C241T, and C3037T (*TTCG*) but lacked the RdRp P323L (C14408T) mutation, and failed to expand until replaced by the full 4-mutation *TTTG* haplotype carrying RdRp P323L. This observation directly tests whether Spike D614G alone is sufficient for the epidemiological sweep or whether epistatic/additive synergy with RdRp P323L is required in vivo.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the growth trajectories of the `TTCG` (D614G + P323) and `CCCG` (singleton D614G) haplotypes relative to `TTTG` (D614G + L323) where they co-circulated, and elevate the discussion of RdRp P323L linkage from Figure S5 to the main Results text.
* **HOW:** Apply the isotonic regression and Fisher's exact pipeline specifically to the early German and Chinese datasets stratified by 3-site coding haplotype (`CCCA` vs. `TTCG` vs. `TTTG`) and report the corresponding frequency trajectories in a main-text table or panel.

#### 4. Release the Alignment and Isotonic Regression Source Code
* **WHY:** Under *Data and Code Availability*, the custom Perl alignment script (`align_to_ref.pl`) and R isotonic regression scripts are withheld pending funder approval, limiting full computational reproducibility of the GISAID filtering and permutation pipeline.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Deposit the exact `align_to_ref.pl` script, the `cgam` isotonic logistic regression permutation script, and the anonymized Sheffield regression table (`CT`, `G614`, `Method_2`, `Age`, `Sex`, `Status`) in the existing Mendeley Data repository (`doi:10.17632/hn3h9gdrgj.1`) or GitHub.
