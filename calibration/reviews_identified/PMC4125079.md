## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis

Berg and Coop develop a foundational quantitative and population genetic framework for detecting polygenic adaptation across multiple populations by combining locus-specific additive effect sizes ($\alpha_l$) from genome-wide association studies (GWAS) with population allele frequency trajectories across $M$ populations. Modeling the vector of mean genetic values $\vec{Z}$ across populations under neutrality and genetic drift as a multivariate normal (MVN) distribution parameterized by the empirical genome-wide population coancestry matrix $\mathbf{F}$ and within-ancestral additive genetic variance $V_A$, the authors transform population genetic values via the Cholesky decomposition of the neutral covariance matrix ($\vec{X} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}\vec{Z}'$). This transformation yields two main classes of test statistics: (i) environmental regression statistics ($\beta$, $r^2$, and Spearman's $\rho$) testing whether drift-decorrelated genetic values covary with ecological variables more strongly than matched neutral SNPs, and (ii) an omnibus excess-variance statistic ($Q_X = \vec{X}^T\vec{X} \sim \chi^2_{M-1}$ under neutrality), which naturally partitions into an among-population differentiation component ($F_{ST}$-like diagonal sum) and an across-population linkage disequilibrium (LD)-like signed allelic covariance component, alongside conditional MVN outlier $Z$-scores for individual regions or populations. Applying this framework to 52 Human Genome Diversity Panel (HGDP) populations across six complex traits—height, skin pigmentation, body mass index (BMI), type 2 diabetes (T2D), Crohn's disease (CD), and ulcerative colitis (UC)—the authors demonstrate clear signals of polygenic adaptation for height (particularly within Europe and in conditional regional tests), skin pigmentation (driven primarily by $F_{ST}$-level differentiation at major-effect loci), and inflammatory bowel disease (CD and UC, coupled to historical malaria/pathogen exposure proxies).

The mathematical derivation linking quantitative genetic response equations to the MVN drift model, the explicit decomposition of $Q_X$ into within-locus differentiation versus between-locus cross-population covariance, and the use of frequency-, $B$-statistic-, and distance-matched empirical null SNPs represent a substantial methodological advance. The primary limitations are: (1) high sensitivity of the cross-population LD-like component of $Q_X$ to subtle, uncorrected population stratification in the discovery GWAS (where residual confounding along a continental or sub-continental axis aligns with HGDP allele frequency differences and accumulates across hundreds of loci), combined with the extrapolation of European-ascertained GWAS effect sizes and LD tagging across non-European HGDP populations; (2) absence of formal multiple-hypothesis testing correction across the large grid of traits, regional subsets, and environmental variables tested; and (3) potential reference-panel imputation bias from using fastPHASE with only three HapMap Phase II reference anchors (CEU, YRI, JPT+CHB) to impute missing genotypes across all 52 HGDP populations.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Evaluation

* **Claim 1:** Under neutral genetic drift and small allele frequency changes, the vector of population mean genetic values $\vec{Z}$ follows a multivariate normal distribution $\vec{Z} \sim \text{MVN}(\mu\vec{1}, 2V_A\mathbf{F})$, and the quadratic form $Q_X = (\vec{Z} - \mu)^T (2V_A\mathbf{F})^{-1} (\vec{Z} - \mu)$ follows a $\chi^2_{M-1}$ distribution when standardized by matched neutral loci, while decomposing into an $F_{ST}$-like variance term and an LD-like cross-locus allelic covariance term. **[Well Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Equations (1)–(18) and the Supporting Text derivations are mathematically sound. By mean-centering relative to a reference population $M$ (Equations 6–9) to remove the rank-1 singularity of the ancestral mean, $\mathbf{F}$ becomes a full-rank $(M-1) \times (M-1)$ covariance matrix. Furthermore, Figure 5 demonstrates that while the theoretical $\chi^2_{51}$ null distribution is slightly anti-conservative in the far upper tail when the number of loci $L$ is small (e.g., $L = 4$ for skin pigmentation, where the finite-locus binomial boundary violates normality), evaluating significance against an empirical null distribution constructed from 10,000 sets of SNPs matched on minor allele frequency, background selection $B$-value, and local recombination/clustering distance yields well-calibrated tail probabilities.

* **Claim 2:** Human height exhibits a robust genome-wide signal of polygenic adaptation across HGDP populations ($Q_X = 95.6, P = 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$ globally; $Q_X = 33.4, P = 1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ within Europe), driven primarily by the cross-population LD-like covariance among height-increasing alleles rather than single-locus $F_{ST}$ outliers, with French and Sardinian populations showing opposing conditional outlier shifts within Europe. **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Within Europe, the authors directly address the risk of GWAS population stratification by re-estimating effect sizes in five family-based transmission disequilibrium test (TDT) cohorts from Lango Allen et al. (2010), confirming that the European $Q_X$ signal ($P = 0.0148$) and the French-versus-Sardinian divergence ($P = 9.4 \times 10^{-3}$) persist when using stratification-free family-based directionality and effect estimates. However, for the global and extra-European regional signals (e.g., Central/South Asia conditional $Z = -3.77$, Sub-Saharan Africa conditional $Z = -3.02$), the inference relies on 163–180 lead SNPs ascertained and estimated in European GWAS. Across continental scales, subtle residual stratification in the discovery meta-analysis, combined with differential LD decay between causal variants and European tag SNPs in African and Asian populations, can systematically shift mean polygenic scores without action of natural selection on the focal trait.

* **Claim 3:** Skin pigmentation shows strong polygenic adaptation globally ($Q_X = 113.4, P < 10^{-4}$) and within Eurasia ($Q_X = 63.3, P < 10^{-4}$) as well as a strong latitudinal gradient ($\beta = -0.349, r^2 = 0.256, P = 1 \times 10^{-4}$), driven overwhelmingly by single-locus $F_{ST}$-like differentiation rather than cross-locus covariance. **[Well Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Even though the skin pigmentation score comprises only $L = 4$ major loci (*SLC24A5*, *SLC45A2*, *TYR*, *OCA2/HERC2*) ascertained in European populations, the authors explicitly demonstrate via the $Q_X$ decomposition (Figure 4) that the signal is dominated by the diagonal $F_{ST}$-like component rather than the off-diagonal covariance component, contrasting sharply with height. They also transparently note that convergent evolution of dark or light skin pigmentation via independent loci in East Asia and Africa is missed due to European GWAS ascertainment.

* **Claim 4:** Inflammatory bowel disease loci (Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis) show significant adaptive divergence across Eurasia and East Asia ($Q_X = 87.3, P < 10^{-4}$ globally for UC; $Q_X = 52.5, P = 1 \times 10^{-4}$ in Eurasia for CD) and significant negative association with historical malaria prevalence ($r^2 = 0.140, P = 0.0029$ for CD; $r^2 = 0.101, P = 0.0114$ for UC). **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The decomposition of $Q_X$ and regional conditional $Z$-scores (e.g., East Asia $Z = -4.25$ for UC; Europe $Z = 2.66$) provide coherent evidence of coordinated allele frequency shifts at immune-related GWAS hits. However, unlike the European height analysis, no family-based TDT replication was available to rule out residual East-West or North-South stratification in the discovery GWAS cohorts for CD and UC. Furthermore, the environmental correlations with historical malaria endemicity ($P = 0.0029$ and $P = 0.0114$) are reported as nominal $P$-values without correction for multiple testing across 6 traits and multiple environmental predictors.

* **Claim 5:** Type 2 diabetes and BMI show little to no global or regional excess genetic variance under the $Q_X$ framework, barring a modest regional correlation of BMI genetic values with cold temperatures in Eurasia ($r^2 = 0.168, P = 0.0102$) and a nominal conditional shift for T2D in Sub-Saharan Africa ($Z = 2.13, P = 0.036$). **[Fragile]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The overall null conclusion for T2D and BMI ($Q_X \approx 41.3\text{--}41.9$, $P > 0.70$ globally) is solid and contradicts earlier uncalibrated claims of widespread directional selection on T2D risk alleles. However, the secondary claims of a Eurasian temperature correlation for BMI ($P = 0.0102$) and a Sub-Saharan African conditional outlier shift for T2D ($P = 0.036$) do not survive standard multiple-testing adjustments across the dozens of hypothesis tests performed in Tables 2, 3, and S1.

## 3. Technical, Statistical, and Biological Critique

### 3.1 Major Methodological and Statistical Issues

1. **Vulnerability of the Cross-Locus Covariance Component of $Q_X$ to Subtle GWAS Stratification:**
   As shown in Equation (14), $Q_X$ is the sum of an $F_{ST}$-like diagonal term:
   $$\frac{\sum_l \alpha_l^2 \vec{p}'_l \mathbf{F}^{-1} \vec{p}'_l}{2V_A}$$
   and an $(L^2 - L)$ cross-locus covariance term:
   $$\frac{\sum_{l \neq k} \alpha_l \alpha_k \vec{p}'_l \mathbf{F}^{-1} \vec{p}'_k}{2V_A}$$
   Because the off-diagonal sum aggregates $L(L-1)$ signed pairwise inner products across loci, even tiny systematic biases in the estimated GWAS effect signs or magnitudes $\hat{\alpha}_l$ that correlate with an axis of population differentiation $\vec{p}'_l$ will grow linearly with $L^2$ in the numerator while the denominator scales with $L$. If a discovery GWAS has minor residual population stratification along a geographic gradient (e.g., North–South Europe or Europe–West Asia), $\hat{\alpha}_l$ acquires a small bias proportional to allele frequency differences along that gradient. When projected onto HGDP populations, that shared bias induces positive cross-locus covariance $\alpha_l \alpha_k \vec{p}'_l \mathbf{F}^{-1} \vec{p}'_k$ across all $l \neq k$, inflating $Q_X$ and conditional $Z$-scores even under strict neutrality. While the authors validate the European height signal using family-based TDT estimates, the global signals for height, CD, and UC rely on population-based GWAS summary statistics where this confounding cannot be excluded.

2. **Absence of Multiple-Testing Correction Across Traits, Regions, and Environments:**
   Across the study, the authors evaluate 6 traits across 7 regional partitions for $Q_X$ ($6 \times 7 = 42$ tests), 6 traits across 7 regional conditional $Z$-scores ($42$ tests), and 6 traits across 5 environmental variables (Latitude, Summer/Winter Temperature, Precipitation, Malaria) in multiple regional subsets using both parametric ($\beta / r^2$) and rank ($\rho$) correlations ($>150$ tests). All $P$-values in Tables 2, 3, and S1–S5 are reported as unadjusted nominal values. Under a Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR $< 0.05$) or Bonferroni correction, several highlighted results—such as the Eurasian winter temperature correlation with BMI ($P = 0.0102$), the global precipitation correlation with UC ($P = 0.0108$), and the Sub-Saharan African conditional T2D shift ($P = 0.036$)—are consistent with chance fluctuations under the null.

3. **Reference-Panel Bias in HGDP Genotype Imputation:**
   To include GWAS lead SNPs that were not directly typed on the Illumina 650Y array in HGDP, the authors imputed missing HapMap Phase II genotypes using fastPHASE v1.4 with haplotypes from only three HapMap reference panels: CEU, YRI, and JPT+CHB (plus 5% held-out HGDP samples). For HGDP populations that are genetically divergent from these three anchors—particularly Native American (Pima, Maya, Karitiana, Surui, Colombian), Oceanian (Melanesian, Papuan), Central/South Asian, and San/Mbuti Pygmy populations—phasing and imputing missing GWAS SNPs toward the nearest of the three HapMap reference panels artificially pulls imputed allele frequencies $p_{ml}$ toward CEU, YRI, or JPT+CHB. This can distort the standardized residuals $\vec{X} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}\vec{Z}'$ whenever a non-negligible fraction of trait-associated SNPs are imputed rather than directly genotyped.

### 3.2 Minor Issues and Formatting Artifacts

1. **Missing Values in Converted Markdown Tables 2 and 3:**
   In the manuscript text provided, several cells in Table 2 (e.g., Global and Regional $Q_X$ and $P$-values for Height in Global, Eurasia, Europe, East Asia, America; Skin Pigmentation in Global, Eurasia, Europe, Africa; CD in Eurasia; UC in Global, Eurasia, Europe, East Asia) and Table 3 (conditional $Z$-scores and $P$-values for significant trait-region pairs) are blank because values formatted in boldface in the source XML were stripped during markdown conversion. Fortunately, the exact numerical values for these cells are stated explicitly in the Results prose and Figures 2–3, allowing complete verification of the statistical claims.
2. **Inline Mathematical Symbols in Text:**
   Several inline mathematical symbols (such as $\mathbf{F}$, $V_A$, $\chi^2$, and $Q_X$) were dropped in parts of the plain-text prose during XML-to-markdown conversion, though all numbered display equations (Equations 1–18) and Supporting Text derivations are intact and unambiguous.
3. **Public Code Availability:**
   The authors provide complete R scripts implementing the MVN polygenic adaptation tests, empirical null matching, and HGDP pipelines on GitHub (`https://github.com/jjberg2/PolygenicAdaptationCode`), supporting computational reproducibility.
