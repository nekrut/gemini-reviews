### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript analyzes 137 early SARS-CoV-2 genome sequences downloaded from GISAID (through February 23, 2020) alongside 7 animal coronavirus genomes (including bat RaTG13 and two pangolin coronaviruses) to investigate the evolutionary origin, demographic trajectory, and selective pressures of the early COVID-19 outbreak. The authors compute pairwise inter-species $d_N$ and $d_S$ (Li–Wu–Luo method in MEGA-X and PAML 4), construct a median-joining haplotype network (Network 10) to re-polarize the site frequency spectrum (SFS) against homoplasy/back-mutation relative to RaTG13, compare within-SARS-CoV-2 polymorphism $d_N/d_S$ before and after January 22, 2020, and run BEAST v1.10.4 (HKY + uncorrelated lognormal relaxed clock + Bayesian skyline plot) to estimate the substitution rate ($\mu = 2.4 \times 10^{-3}$/site/year) and time to the most recent common ancestor (TMRCA: December 11, 2019). The sequence accession IDs are listed in the supplement, though custom analysis files and BEAST XML configurations are not deposited.

The authors make one sound methodological observation: polarizing the within-population SARS-CoV-2 SFS using a distant outgroup (`RaTG13`, synonymous divergence $d_S = 0.168$) creates artificial high-frequency derived alleles due to C$\to$T hypermutability and multiple hits, which disappears when mutations are polarized on the internal haplotype network (Figure 1a vs. 1b). However, nearly every major biological and epidemiological conclusion of the paper is undermined by severe methodological flaws and internal contradictions: (1) claiming that SARS-CoV-2 "cryptically circulated within humans for years" based on a ~40-year bat/pangolin RBD divergence estimate, which directly contradicts their own BEAST TMRCA of December 11, 2019; (2) an ad-hoc molecular clock equation that divides the SARS-CoV-2 vs. `Pangolin_2019` RBD $d_S$ by 3 using the RaTG13 ratio while applying a short-term tip rate to deep inter-species divergence; (3) claiming "strong signatures of positive selection" in `orf8` ($d_N/d_S = 8.51$) and `orf3` ($d_N/d_S = 2.68$) based on a single non-singleton SNP per gene after substituting genome-wide $d_S$ for a zero denominator; and (4) attributing a Bayesian skyline plateau and allele-frequency shifts (`L84S`, `G251V`) to the January 23, 2020 Wuhan lockdown despite admitting that no Wuhan genomes in the dataset were sampled after January 5, 2020.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Outgroup polarization with RaTG13 distorts the SARS-CoV-2 site frequency spectrum due to C$\to$T transition bias and multiple substitutions: [Well Supported].**  
  With $d_S = 16.8\%$ between SARS-CoV-2 and RaTG13 and 73% of changes being transitions (45% C$\to$T, 28% A$\to$G), outgroup polarization misassigns six rare C$\to$T polymorphism events (where the minor human allele T matches RaTG13) as high-frequency derived C alleles (Table 2, Figure 1a). Cross-referencing with the median-joining network and sampling dates (Figure 2) confirms an L-shaped singleton-dominated SFS (Figure 1b).

* **SARS-CoV-2 might have cryptically circulated in humans for years prior to December 2019: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This central claim in the Abstract, Discussion, and Conclusion directly contradicts the authors' own BEAST coalescent estimate placing the TMRCA of all 137 human SARS-CoV-2 genomes at December 11, 2019 (95% HPD: November 13 – December 23, 2019) and their singleton-dominated SFS (Figure 1b). Pre-zoonotic recombination between ancestral bat and pangolin coronavirus lineages ~40 years ago in wildlife reservoirs provides zero evidence that the virus circulated in humans during that interval; had it circulated in humans for years without a complete bottleneck in late 2019, human isolates would share a TMRCA years prior to 2019.

* **The Spike RBD similarity between SARS-CoV-2 and Pangolin_2019 reflects an ancient introgression ~40 years ago (95% HPD: 31–69 years): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To calculate the 40-year divergence time on Line 311, the authors use the formula $t = d_S / (\text{rate} \times 2 \times 3)$, dividing the SARS-CoV-2 vs. `Pangolin_2019` RBD $d_S$ ($0.710$) by 3 *"considering dS in RBD is 3-fold of genome average."* First, in Table 1, RBD $d_S$ ($0.710$) between SARS-CoV-2 and `Pangolin_2019` is only $1.51\times$ the genome average ($0.469$), not $3\times$ ($3.04\times$ is the ratio for RaTG13: $0.511/0.168$). Second, dividing observed $d_S$ by 3 has no justification unless third-position mutation rates in the RBD are truly $3\times$ higher than the rest of the genome. Third, extrapolating a 2-month within-outbreak tip rate ($2.9 \times 10^{-3}$/site/year, inflated by unfixed polymorphism) to deep inter-species divergence ($d_S = 0.710$, where K2P/Li–Wu–Luo estimates suffer from transition saturation) invalidates the molecular clock date.

* **Elevated non-singleton $d_N/d_S$ in Episode II vs. Episode I demonstrates genome-wide relaxation of selection alongside strong positive selection on `orf8` and `orf3`: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  After removing singletons, the entire 137-genome dataset contains only 16 nonsynonymous and 13 synonymous polymorphic sites across 29,600 nucleotides (Table 2). The claimed "strong signatures of positive selection" in `orf8` ($d_N/d_S = 8.51$) and `orf3` ($d_N/d_S = 2.68$ in Episode II) in Table 3 rest on a *single* non-singleton nonsynonymous polymorphism in each gene (`L84S` and `G251V`, respectively) and zero synonymous polymorphisms ($d_S = 0$), where the authors artificially plugged the genome-wide $d_S$ ($1.87 \times 10^{-4}$) into the denominator without any statistical test or confidence interval. Similarly, the genome-wide shift from $d_N/d_S = 0.20$ (Episode I) to $0.39$ (Episode II) reflects small-number sampling variance across 29 total non-singleton polymorphisms and lacks a formal permutation or bootstrap test.

* **The plateau in viral effective population size ($N_e$) and shifts in `orf8` 84S and `orf3` 251V frequencies after January 23, 2020 demonstrate the effectiveness of the Wuhan lockdown: [Overstated].**  
  As the authors note on Line 270, *all* 24 sequences from Wuhan in their dataset were sampled on or before January 5, 2020, whereas sequences after January 22, 2020 ($N = 79$) were predominantly sampled from international travelers and secondary clusters outside Wuhan. A plateau in a 5-epoch Bayesian skyline plot (`m = 5`, Figure 4) and shifts in global GISAID allele frequencies between early-January Wuhan hospital samples and February international samples reflect severe spatiotemporal ascertainment bias and founder effects (`G251V` in European/Asian travel clusters), not the causal impact of Wuhan's lockdown on viral $N_e$.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Remove the Contradictory Claim of Multi-Year Cryptic Human Circulation and Fix the RBD Clock Calculation
* **WHY:** A human TMRCA of December 11, 2019 (95% HPD: Nov 13 – Dec 23, 2019) and a star-like phylogeny with 87% singletons are mathematically incompatible with years of cryptic circulation of the sampled lineage in humans prior to late 2019. Furthermore, dividing `Pangolin_2019` RBD $d_S$ ($0.710$) by 3 in the divergence formula conflates the RaTG13 $d_S$ ratio ($3.04\times$) with the `Pangolin_2019` ratio ($1.51\times$).
* **WHAT:** Remove claims in the Abstract, Discussion, and Conclusion that SARS-CoV-2 circulated cryptically in humans for years, and frame the RBD similarity strictly as pre-zoonotic ancestral recombination or lineage sorting in bat/pangolin reservoirs.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract, Discussion (Lines 309–313), and Conclusion (Line 326) to state that the TMRCA of human SARS-CoV-2 isolates dates to late November–December 2019, while the divergence between the SARS-CoV-2 and `Pangolin_2019` RBDs occurred decades earlier in animal reservoirs. If reporting an RBD divergence date, compute codon-model ML $d_S$ (using PAML `codeml` with $\kappa$ estimated rather than Li–Wu–Luo) without the arbitrary factor-of-3 divisor, and note the uncertainty of applying tip-calibrated rates to deep divergence.

#### 2. Compute Bootstrap Confidence Intervals for Polymorphism $p_N/p_S$ and Drop Uncalibrated Zero-$d_S$ Ratios
* **WHY:** Plugging genome-wide $d_S$ into the denominator of `orf8` and `orf3` when gene-specific $d_S = 0$ creates synthetic $d_N/d_S > 1$ values from a single SNP (`L84S` or `G251V`). Moreover, comparing pooled polymorphism ratios between Episode I ($0.20$) and Episode II ($0.39$) without uncertainty intervals mistakes sparse count noise (29 total non-singleton SNPs) for a genome-wide change in selection regime.
* **WHAT:** Report polymorphism counts ($P_N$ and $P_S$) alongside $p_N/p_S$ in Table 3, replace the substituted genome-wide $d_S$ ratios for `orf3` and `orf8` with exact binomial/Fisher's exact tests against neutral site opportunities, and compute 1,000-replicate codon-bootstrap confidence intervals for the Episode I vs. Episode II comparison.
* **HOW:** In MEGA-X or R, bootstrap the 9,555 codons 1,000 times to obtain 95% confidence intervals for $d_N$, $d_S$, and $\Delta(d_N/d_S) = (d_N/d_S)_{\text{Ep II}} - (d_N/d_S)_{\text{Ep I}}$, and test whether 1 nonsynonymous vs. 0 synonymous non-singleton changes in `orf8` (121 codons) or `orf3` (274 codons) deviates significantly from the genome-wide ratio via Fisher's exact test ($p > 0.30$ in both cases).

#### 3. Stratify Allele Frequency and Demographic Analyses by Geographic Location
* **WHY:** Because all 24 Wuhan sequences were collected by January 5, 2020, comparing pre-Jan 22 vs. post-Jan 22 pooled global sequences confounds time (the Jan 23 lockdown) with geography (Wuhan vs. rest of China and international exports).
* **WHAT:** Plot the trajectories of `orf8` 84S and `orf3` 251V (Figure 3) and the Bayesian skyline demographic reconstruction (Figure 4) stratified by geographic cohort (Wuhan vs. Rest of China vs. Rest of World).
* **HOW:** Re-generate Figure 3 separately for sequences from China ($N = 64$) and sequences from outside China ($N = 73$), and re-run the BEAST Bayesian skyline analysis restricted to the 64 Chinese genomes (or using a structured coalescent model) so that international sampling lag does not masquerade as a lockdown-induced plateau in Wuhan.

#### 4. Reconcile Recombination vs. Sequencing/Coinfection Artifacts in the Four-Haplotype Test
* **WHY:** On Line 209, the authors infer at least two intragenomic recombination events from DnaSP's four-haplotype test (between positions 8782, 11083, and 28854), despite already showing in Figure 2 and Line 201 that C$\to$T homoplasy and G11083T (a well-known homoplasic/artifactual site in early SARS-CoV-2 sequencing) occur repeatedly on independent backgrounds.
* **WHAT:** Test whether the four-haplotype violations are driven by recurrent C$\to$T / G11083T homoplasy rather than true homologous recombination.
* **HOW:** Repeat the DnaSP four-haplotype test after excluding hypermutable C$\to$T transition sites and position 11,083, and temper the claim of frequent intragenomic recombination within the 2-month human outbreak accordingly.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Multiple coordinate errors, residue number swaps, arithmetic mismatches in mutation counts, and a date typo in the text cause confusion.
* **WHAT & HOW:**  
  * **Line 143 (Date typo):** Change *"available from GISAID as of 2/23/2019"* to *"2/23/2020."*  
  * **Line 143 (Mutation count arithmetic mismatch):** The text states *"223 mutations were identified with 68 synonymous and 155 nonsynonymous changes"* and then gives singleton counts of `138/56` and non-singleton counts of `16/13`. However, $138 + 16 = 154 \ne 155$ nonsynonymous and $56 + 13 = 69 \ne 68$ synonymous. Reconcile the exact singleton and non-singleton counts so they sum to 223.  
  * **Line 211 (Genomic coordinate error for `orf8` L84S):** Change both occurrences of *"28,814"* / *"28814C"* to *"28,144"* / *"28144C"* to match Table 2 (Line 174) and the `NC_045512` reference coordinate.  
  * **Lines 317, 321, and 326 (Residue number swap for `orf3` G251V):** In four places in the Discussion and Conclusion, `251 V` is miswritten as *"215 V in orf3 protein."* Correct all four occurrences to *"251V."*  
  * **Table 2, Row $\eta$ (Line 186):** At genome position `18,603`, the Major allele is listed as `T` and Minor allele as `A`, but the last column lists the change as `T18338C`. Correct the nucleotide substitution label (`T18338A` or correct the Minor allele column to `C`).
