**Authors:** Minh BQ, Schmidt HA, Chernomor O, Schrempf D, Woodhams MD, von Haeseler A, Lanfear R.  
**Journal / Preprint:** Mol Biol Evol (2020) | DOI: 10.1093/molbev/msaa015

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This software note describes IQ-TREE 2, an updated maximum-likelihood phylogenetic inference package designed for phylogenomic datasets. The authors summarize newly integrated evolutionary models (over 200 time-reversible models, partitioned and mixture models, polymorphism-aware models, and 99 nonreversible Lie Markov DNA models alongside unrestricted DNA `UNREST` and amino acid `NONREV` models) and algorithmic enhancements (rooted tree search for nonreversible models, parallelized quartet likelihood mapping, FastTree-like `-fast` search, terrace awareness via `terraphast`, single-locus tree scheduling `-S`, parallelized branch/topology tests, and memory-saving modes). Performance benchmarks are reported on two empirical phylogenomic datasets: a DNA dataset (Fong et al. 2012; 168 loci) and an amino acid dataset (Whelan et al. 2017; 127 loci). The software, documentation, and workshop tutorials are publicly available at `http://www.iqtree.org`.

As a concise software announcement, the manuscript delivers a practical overview of major engineering advances in maximum-likelihood phylogenetics, particularly the numerically stable implementation of nonreversible amino acid and DNA substitution models and high-throughput parallelization of quartet mapping and branch/topology tests. However, several benchmark comparisons in the main text suffer from incomplete experimental specification, asymmetric baseline configurations, or missing accuracy evaluations—most notably reporting execution times for nonreversible rooting and `-fast` heuristic searches without quantifying topological accuracy or root-placement recovery on the benchmark datasets, and citing a 1,300- to 2,000-fold speedup over PhyML for SH-aLRT without clarifying thread counts or whether PhyML was re-optimizing parameters that IQ-TREE 2 held fixed. These reporting gaps can be resolved through clearer methodological documentation, explicit thread-matched benchmarking tables, and accuracy summaries in the main text.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Substantial runtime speedups for quartet likelihood mapping, branch tests (SH-aLRT), and topology tests (AU test) relative to legacy tools (TREE-PUZZLE, PhyML, CONSEL): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The reported wall-clock reductions (e.g., 282 min in TREE-PUZZLE vs. 1 min on 1 core and 21 s on 4 cores in IQ-TREE 2 for DNA quartet mapping; 173.5 h in PhyML vs. 1.6 min in IQ-TREE 2 for AA SH-aLRT) demonstrate clear algorithmic and engineering gains. However, the main text omits critical baseline symmetry details: for the SH-aLRT comparison against PhyML v3.3.20190321 (over 6,500-fold difference: 173.5 h = 10,410 min vs. 1.6 min, conservatively described as ">2,000-fold"), the text does not state how many CPU cores IQ-TREE 2 used, nor whether PhyML was performing full tree/branch-length optimization prior to branch testing while IQ-TREE 2 evaluated a pre-computed tree. Similarly, the `LG4X` parameter optimization comparison (1.9 min in IQ-TREE 2 vs. 3 min in RAxML-NG and 17.6 min in PhyML-mixtures) does not report final log-likelihoods to verify that all three optimizers converged to equivalent optima.

* **Numerically stable inference of rooted trees under nonreversible DNA (`UNREST`, Lie Markov) and amino acid (`NONREV`) substitution models: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Combining complex eigen-decomposition (via Eigen3) with a fallback to scaling-and-squaring when the rate matrix $Q$ is non-diagonalizable is mathematically sound and addresses known numerical instabilities in computing $P(t) = e^{Qt}$ under nonreversible models. Yet the empirical evaluation on the Fong et al. (2012) DNA and Whelan et al. (2017) AA datasets reports only wall-clock runtimes (2.8 min GTR vs. 10.7 min UNREST; 2.52 h protein GTR vs. 5.8 h NONREV) without reporting whether the inferred root positions are biologically plausible, statistically supported (e.g., via rootstrap or AU tests across `--root-dist`), or susceptible to over-parameterization (379 free exchangeability/rate parameters in a 20-state `NONREV` matrix).

* **Competitive single-locus tree scheduling (`-S`) compared to ParGenes: [Fragile].**  
  On 2 and 4 CPU cores, ParGenes v1.0.1 is faster than IQ-TREE 2 on both the 168-locus DNA dataset (5.98 min vs. 9.2 min on 4 cores) and the 127-locus AA dataset (0.81 h vs. 1.05 h on 4 cores). The authors argue that because IQ-TREE 2 exhibits higher parallel efficiency from 2 to 4 cores (101% and 97.6% vs. 76.7% and 94.4%), "both programs are likely to perform similarly on very large data sets." Extrapolating cluster-scale parity from a 2-to-4 core comparison is unwarranted, especially because IQ-TREE 2 performs internal model selection (`ModelFinder`) plus tree search per locus whereas ParGenes dispatches RAxML-NG with a distinct two-level MPI/OpenMP scheduler designed specifically for hundreds to thousands of cores.

* **Fast tree search (`-fast`) and memory-saving mode (`-mem 0.5`) maintain efficiency with minimal trade-offs: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The `-mem 0.5` benchmark clearly quantifies the runtime penalty of halving RAM usage (21% on DNA, 9% on AA). For `-fast`, the text compares runtime against FastTree2 (82 s vs. 85 s on DNA; 13.9 min vs. 4.3 min on AA) and cites Zhou et al. (2018) to note that FastTree2 produces worse trees than standard ML tools, but fails to report the log-likelihoods or Robinson-Foulds distances of IQ-TREE 2's own `-fast` mode relative to default IQ-TREE 2 or FastTree2 on the two benchmark datasets.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Standardize Benchmark Reporting with Explicit Core Counts and Final Log-Likelihoods
* **WHY:** Comparing wall-clock runtimes across phylogenetic tools without stating thread counts, convergence thresholds, and resulting log-likelihoods makes it impossible to separate algorithmic speedups from hardware parallelism or premature optimization stopping.
* **WHAT:** Add a summary benchmark table in the main text reporting CPU core counts, RAM usage, wall-clock time, and final log-likelihood scores across all compared software packages (`IQ-TREE 2`, `RAxML-NG`, `PhyML-mixtures`, `PhyML`, `FastTree2`, `TREE-PUZZLE`, `CONSEL`, `ParGenes`).
* **HOW:** Extract the final log-likelihoods and exact thread counts from the existing benchmark logs for the Fong et al. (2012) DNA dataset and Whelan et al. (2017) AA dataset, and clarify in the "Fast Branch Tests" section whether PhyML and IQ-TREE 2 were both run with fixed topologies/parameters (`-u` / `-te`) on an identical number of threads.

#### 2. Report Topological Accuracy and Root Recovery for Nonreversible and `-fast` Searches
* **WHY:** Runtime figures alone do not validate that the new `--root-dist` heuristic under `UNREST`/`NONREV` recovers biologically well-supported roots or that `-fast` avoids the topological degradation criticized in FastTree2.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the biological root placement and log-likelihood gains under `UNREST`/`NONREV` (and the sensitivity to the default `--root-dist 2` vs. global root search), and report the log-likelihood and Robinson-Foulds distance of `-fast` trees relative to default IQ-TREE 2 and FastTree2.
* **HOW:** Using the trees already inferred on the Fong et al. (2012) and Whelan et al. (2017) datasets, compare the inferred root branch under `--root-dist 2` versus an exhaustive root search (`--root-dist` set to tree diameter), and tabulate the log-likelihood differences ($\Delta \ln L$) between default IQ-TREE 2, `iqtree2 -fast`, and `FastTree2` evaluated under identical substitution models.

#### 3. Temper or Test the High-Core Extrapolation for Single-Locus Scheduling (`-S`)
* **WHY:** Parallel efficiency measured between 2 and 4 cores ($E = T_2 / (2 T_4)$) is dominated by small-sample bin-packing effects (and cache effects yielding the super-linear 101% efficiency) and cannot predict scaling behavior on 64+ cores where ParGenes' dynamic MPI scheduling operates.
* **WHAT:** Either run the 168-locus DNA and 127-locus AA benchmarks at higher core counts (e.g., 16, 32, and 64 cores) with matched per-locus model specifications, or revise the claim in "Single-Locus Tree Inference" to reflect that ParGenes is 35–50% faster at 2–4 cores while IQ-TREE 2 `-S` provides a convenient single-binary workflow with integrated ModelFinder.
* **HOW:** Replace the speculative sentence ("The upshot of this is that both programs are likely to perform similarly on very large data sets") with a precise statement of the work-equivalence differences (IQ-TREE 2 running ModelFinder + IQ-TREE search vs. ParGenes running RAxML-NG) and the observed 2–4 core runtimes.

#### 4. Provide Exact Dataset Dimensions and Command-Line Reproducibility Details in the Main Text
* **WHY:** The main text repeatedly refers to "the DNA-data set (Fong et al. 2012)" and "the AA-data set (Whelan et al. 2017)" without stating the number of taxa, total alignment length, percentage of missing data, or hardware processor specifications, forcing readers to hunt through external supplements.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Insert a brief paragraph or footnote at first mention specifying the exact dimensions (number of taxa, sites, site patterns, and loci) of both benchmark datasets, the CPU architecture used for timing, and the exact software versions and command-line flags used for every benchmarked tool.
