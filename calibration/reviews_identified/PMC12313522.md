### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Jian et al. evaluate a deep mutational scanning (DMS)-guided screening strategy to identify broadly neutralizing antibodies (bnAbs) against SARS-CoV-2 from early-pandemic B-cell repertoires. Starting from 7,018 monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) isolated across seven human exposure cohorts (1,637 potent autologous neutralizers with $\text{IC}_{50} < 0.05\,\mu\text{g/ml}$, including 141 elicited by ancestral wild-type [WT] exposure and 179 from SARS-CoV-1 + WT vaccination), the authors design 17 single-point and 5 combinatorially mutated B.1 pseudoviruses (`B.1-S1` through `B.1-S5`) incorporating RBD escape hotspots weighted by WT mAb DMS escape, hACE2 binding, RBD expression, and codon usage. Filtering the 141 potent WT-elicited NAbs across `B.1-S1`–`S5` narrows the pool to 5 candidates, 2 of which neutralize XBB.1.5 (a 40% hit rate compared to 2/141 [1.4%] prior to filtering) and 1 of which—`BD55-1205` (an `IGHV3-66` class 1 antibody)—potently neutralizes all tested variants through JN.1 and KP.3. The authors characterize `BD55-1205` via rVSV and authentic XBB.1.5.6 serial passage escape assays, surface plasmon resonance, cryo-EM structures of `BD55-1205` bound to XBB.1.5 Spike (3.5 Å) and RBD (3.3 Å) showing 12 hydrogen bonds to RBD main-chain carbonyl/amide atoms, and in vivo pharmacokinetic/serological evaluation of mRNA-LNP-encoded `BD55-1205-IgG` in human FcRn transgenic (`Tg32-SCID` and `Tg32`) mice. Source data are provided for the primary figures.

The structural, biochemical, and virological characterization of `BD55-1205` is thorough, and the demonstration that extensive main-chain contacts and receptor mimicry confer high resilience to RBD antigenic drift is convincing. However, the evolutionary prediction and enrichment claims rest on a small retrospective sample ($2/5$ XBB.1.5-active mAbs in the WT cohort) and face two methodological caveats: first, the combinatorial design of `B.1-S1`–`S5` ("avoiding multiple mutations in the same epitope") is described qualitatively without a formal mathematical objective function, making it hard to verify that post-hoc knowledge of Omicron sublineages did not subtly inform the specific amino acid choices (e.g., R346T, K444N, F486S in `B.1-S3`); second, the Abstract and Main Text contain a numerical discrepancy regarding the WT cohort denominator ("1,103 SARS-CoV-2 wild-type-elicited mAbs" in the Abstract vs. $n = 141$ potent WT NAbs in Results and Fig. 1–2). Formalizing the combinatorial design algorithm, reporting exact binomial confidence intervals on the enrichment estimates, and reconciling the cohort denominators will strengthen the manuscript.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **`BD55-1205` exhibits broad, potent neutralization against historical and contemporary variants (through JN.1 and KP.3) and a high barrier to viral escape via RBD backbone contacts and hACE2 mimicry: [Well Supported].**  
  Neutralization breadth is replicated across VSV pseudoviruses (Table 1; $\text{IC}_{50} = 0.003$–$0.012\,\mu\text{g/ml}$ from D614G to KP.3) and authentic virus isolates (WT, BA.5.2.1, FL.8, XBB.1.5.6, and JN.3; $\text{IC}_{50} = 0.007$–$0.026\,\mu\text{g/ml}$, Extended Data Fig. 6a). Resistance to escape is supported by head-to-head serial passage in both XBB.1.5 rVSV (retaining activity to passage 6, comparable to a two-mAb cocktail, whereas SA55, BD57-2225, and BD57-1520 escaped at passages 2–3; Fig. 3b) and authentic XBB.1.5.6 (12 passages; Extended Data Fig. 6b,c). The 3.3 Å local cryo-EM structure (RBD + `BD55-1205` + `BD57-0120` Fab) and germline-reversion mutagenesis (`BD55-1205-GLHV`, R31S, P53S, R102Y) directly link mutational tolerance to 12 hydrogen bonds targeting RBD backbone atoms and competition with hACE2 binding.

* **DMS-guided combinatorial pseudovirus screening increases the probability of identifying XBB.1.5-effective bnAbs from 1% to 40% in an early-pandemic WT cohort: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Within the 141 potent WT-elicited NAbs ($\text{IC}_{50} < 0.05\,\mu\text{g/ml}$ against D614G), 2 neutralize XBB.1.5 ($2/141 = 1.42\%$). Screening against the 5 combinatorially designed pseudoviruses (`B.1-S1`–`S5`) retains 5 WT mAbs (`BD-505`, `BD55-1159`, `BD55-1208`, `BD55-279`, `BD55-1205`; Table 1), of which 2 (`BD-505` and `BD55-1205`) have XBB.1.5 $\text{IC}_{50} < 0.05\,\mu\text{g/ml}$ ($2/5 = 40\%$) and only 1 (`BD55-1205`, $1/5 = 20\%$) neutralizes HK.3.1, JN.1, and KP.3. While the hypergeometric enrichment is statistically significant (Fig. 2e), a ratio of $2/5$ carries a wide exact 95% Clopper-Pearson confidence interval ($5.3\%$ to $85.3\%$). Moreover, in the `SARS+WT` cohort (Table 1), 9 antibodies pass the `S1`–`S5` filters, of which 5 (all in epitope group F3) neutralize XBB.1.5–KP.3 while 4 (`BD55-5713`, `BD55-6723`, `BD55-5286`, `BD55-5484`) fail against XBB.1.5 ($\text{IC}_{50} > 10\,\mu\text{g/ml}$). Finally, because the construction of `B.1-S1`–`S5` was performed retrospectively after Omicron sublineages were known, the manuscript needs a strictly algorithmic specification of how the specific mutant combinations and alternate amino acid states (e.g., R346T, K417T, K444N, L452R, E484K, F486S) were selected solely from early-2021 WT DMS data without hindsight leakage.

* **Intravenous delivery of mRNA-LNP encoding `BD55-1205-IgG` achieves high, durable serum neutralizing titres in mice comparable to clinical benchmark `CHKV-24`: [Well Supported].**  
  In homozygous human FcRn transgenic `Tg32-SCID` ($n = 8$ combined across 2 experiments) and immunocompetent `Tg32` ($n = 4$ female, $n = 4$ male) mice dosed at $0.5\,\text{mg/kg}$, `BD55-1205-LA` reached geometric mean peak serum levels of $505\,\mu\text{g/ml}$ at 48 h (vs. $491\,\mu\text{g/ml}$ for `CHKV-24-LS`) and serum $\text{ID}_{50}$ values of $4,496$ (XBB.1.5), $5,138$ (HK.3.1), and $4,608$ (JN.1) (Fig. 5b–e). One minor baseline asymmetry in Methods is that `BD55-1205` used the `LA` (M428L/N434A) Fc modification whereas `CHKV-24` used `LS` (M428L/N434S), which should be noted when comparing elimination half-lives.

* **Generalizability of the DMS prediction platform to newly emerging pathogens ("Disease X") early in a pandemic: [Overstated].**  
  As the authors partially acknowledge in the Discussion, applying this workflow prospectively requires (1) a functional receptor and high-throughput DMS library for the target viral glycoprotein, (2) high-throughput DMS escape profiling across hundreds of early patient-derived mAbs to map immunodominant epitopes before variants emerge, and (3) evolution driven by point-mutation antigenic drift within a single domain rather than epistatic saltations, insertions/deletions, or NTD/SD1 allosteric shifts. Framing this as ready for rapid deployment against "Disease X" in the Abstract and conclusion overreaches the retrospective SARS-CoV-2 validation.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Formalize the `B.1-S1`–`S5` Combinatorial Mutant Selection Rule to Exclude Hindsight Bias
* **WHY:** Because the retrospective study was conducted after BA.1–JN.1 had already circulated, selecting specific multi-mutation combinations (`B.1-S1` through `B.1-S5`) using qualitative criteria ("maintain hACE2 binding and avoid multiple mutations in the same epitope") leaves open the question of whether knowledge of natural Omicron mutations influenced the exact residue and amino-acid choices.
* **WHAT:** Provide the exact mathematical scoring formula, epitope-clustering constraints, and deterministic ranking algorithm used to generate `B.1-S1`–`S5` from the pre-Omicron WT DMS dataset, and test sensitivity to alternative top-ranked DMS combinations.
* **HOW:** Add a step-by-step mathematical specification (and corresponding script) showing how single-site WT DMS escape scores, DMS hACE2 binding ($\Delta \log K_D$), DMS RBD expression, and single-nucleotide accessibility were combined to rank candidate amino acid substitutions at each site, and how the 5 combinatorial genotypes were partitioned across epitope groups.

#### 2. Report Exact Binomial Confidence Intervals and Reconcile Cohort Denominators
* **WHY:** Reporting that the probability of identifying effective bnAbs increased "from 1% to 40%" without sample sizes or confidence intervals obscures that the numerator is $2$ antibodies out of $5$ passing the filter ($95\%\text{ CI}: [5.3\%, 85.3\%]$), and the Abstract states "A retrospective analysis of 1,103 SARS-CoV-2 wild-type-elicited mAbs" whereas Results and Fig. 1–2 analyze $141$ potent WT NAbs (out of $7,018$ total mAbs across 7 cohorts).
* **WHAT:** Reconcile the $1,103$ vs. $141$ WT mAb counts between the Abstract, Main Text, and Fig. 1a, and state exact fractions and 95% Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals alongside all percentage enrichment claims.
* **HOW:** In the Abstract and Results ("The rational strategy to select bnAbs"), explicitly write "$2/141$ ($1.4\%$, $95\%\text{ CI}: 0.2\text{--}5.0\%$) of potent WT-elicited NAbs (from $1,103$ total WT mAbs) to $2/5$ ($40\%$, $95\%\text{ CI}: 5.3\text{--}85.3\%$) for XBB.1.5 and $1/5$ ($20\%$, $95\%\text{ CI}: 0.5\text{--}71.6\%$) for JN.1/KP.3."

#### 3. Account for Repeated Measurements in Serum IgG vs. $\text{ID}_{50}$ Correlation Tests
* **WHY:** In Fig. 5f and Extended Data Fig. 10c, Pearson correlation $P$ values between serum hIgG concentrations and pseudovirus $\text{ID}_{50}$ values are computed using a standard two-sided $t$-test across pooled longitudinal time points from the same 4 mice per group, violating the independence assumption of the standard $t$-test.
* **WHAT:** Recompute the correlation significance accounting for within-animal repeated measures (or report per-time-point / animal-level correlations).
* **HOW:** Use a linear mixed-effects model with mouse ID as a random intercept (or the `rmcorr` repeated-measures correlation package in R) on the longitudinal data in `Source Data Fig. 5` and `Source Data Extended Data Fig. 10`, and update the $R$ and $P$ values in Fig. 5f and Extended Data Fig. 10c.

#### 4. Minor Technical and Editorial Corrections
* **WHY:** Several minor inconsistencies in spike mutation lists, figure cross-references, and statistical methods descriptions detract from technical precision.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Spike mutation list discrepancy (Methods):** In "Pseudovirus design and neutralization assays", the `XBB.1.5` spike list omits `G252V`, `S371F`, `S373P`, `S375F`, `T376A`, `D405N`, `R408S`, `K417N`, `N440K`, `S477N`, `T478K`, `E484A`, `Q498R`, `N501Y`, `Y505H`, `D614G`, `H655Y`, `N679K`, `P681H`, `N764K`, `D796Y`, `Q954H`, and `N969K` because it is written as `BA.2 + ...` (inheriting BA.2 mutations except missing `G252V`), whereas in "Mouse serum pseudovirus neutralization assays" `R493Q` is omitted from the explicit mutation lists for `XBB.1.5`, `JN.1`, and `HK.3.1` (note that relative to Wuhan-Hu-1 reference MN908947, position 493 is Q493, so `R493Q` is a reversion from BA.2 back to WT Q493, which should be stated consistently across both Methods subsections).
  2. **Broken figure cross-reference (Methods):** Under "Cryo-EM sample collection, data acquisition and structure determination", the last sentence reads "All dataset processing is shown in Extended Data Fig. 6." This should be **Extended Data Fig. 7**.
  3. **Typo in Results:** Line 60, "slighted reduced against XBB.1.5+S490Y" $\rightarrow$ "slightly reduced".
