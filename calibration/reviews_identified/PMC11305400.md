# Critical Peer Review: Multiple transatlantic incursions of highly pathogenic avian influenza clade 2.3.4.4b A(H5N5) virus into North America and spillover to mammals (PMC11305400)

**Overall Score:** **7.7 / 10.0** (Strong / Sound)
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 2.5/3.5 | Claims: 1.9/2.5 | Biology: 1.7/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.6/2.0

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis
Erdelyan et al. investigate the 2023 emergence of clade 2.3.4.4b highly pathogenic avian influenza `A(H5N5)` in Atlantic Canada across 41 wildlife cases (pelagic seabirds, corvids, raptors, and 7 mesocarnivores: 4 raccoons, 2 red foxes, and 1 striped skunk), integrating whole-genome Oxford Nanopore sequencing (`GISAID EPI_ISL_18702952`–`18702992`), maximum-likelihood (`IQ-TREE 2`) and Bayesian (`BEAST v1.10.4`) phylodynamics, hemagglutination inhibition (`HI`) antigenic profiling, sialylglycopolymer receptor-binding assays, in vitro neuraminidase and endonuclease inhibitor phenotyping, and ferret pathogenesis/transmission experiments. The genomic and phenotypic characterization is thorough: the authors demonstrate that all 41 Canadian `A(H5N5)` isolates retain a fully Eurasian (`GenoFLU A6`) constellation carrying a characteristic 22-amino-acid NA stalk deletion (`Δ47–68`) and N-linked glycosylation restoration at `HA-N154` via independent `HA-A156T` and `HA-A156S` substitutions, indicating at least two separate transatlantic introductions from Norway. They also show that candidate resistance polymorphisms (`NA-I117T` and `PA-T40A`) do not impair susceptibility to oseltamivir, zanamivir, peramivir, or baloxavir. The primary analytical limitations are (1) over-reliance on unstratified discrete-trait `BSSVS` ancestral state reconstruction (`BEAST`) on opportunistically sampled carcasses ($n = 62$ genomes across 17 host species) to infer directional interspecies transmission networks (`Figure 2B`), and (2) incomplete virological confirmation of the two direct-contact ferret deaths (`Figure 3B,G`), which had undetectable nasal-wash viral titers and no archived tissues for `qRT-PCR`, titration, or immunohistochemistry.

## 2. Granular Claims & Evidence Audit

* **Claim 1:** *"Based on the GenoFLU nomenclature, all A(H5N5)-Δstalk viruses were classified as clade 2.3.4.4b genotype A6 with a fully EA genetic constellation... Both HA-A156 S/T mutations appeared to have occurred independently, indicating two separate incursions of A(H5N5) viruses into Canada from Norway."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Critique:** Whole-genome phylogenetic analysis across all eight segments (`Figure 2A`, `Figure S1`) and `TreeTime`/`BEAST` ancestral reconstruction clearly separate the 40 Canadian `HA-156T` isolates (which cluster with a 2023 Norwegian isolate and trace back to a July 2022 tMRCA) from the Charlottetown red-tailed hawk isolate (`A/Red-Tailed_Hawk/PEI/FAV-0165-1/2023`), which carries `HA-A156S` and clusters directly with two June 2022 Norwegian gull isolates (`A/Glaucous_gull/Norway/2022-07-1148/2022` and `A/Great_black-backed_gull/Norway/2022-07-1141-4T/2022`). Furthermore, the phylogeny confirms that the mammalian-adaptive `PB2-E627K` substitution arose independently in the PEI raccoon (`FAV-0199-1/2023`), the PEI striped skunk (`FAV-0210-1/2023`), and a pair of Nova Scotia gulls (`FAV-0263-6` and `FAV-0263-8`).

* **Claim 2:** *"Northern fulmars appeared to be a source of A(H5N5) virus, while herring gulls and GBBGs were sinks. Additionally, northern fulmars were likely sources of virus for both American crows and red foxes. Crows subsequently acted as the source of virus for raccoons (Figure 2B)."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Fragile]**
  * **Critique:** The host-transition network in `Figure 2B` is inferred using standard asymmetric discrete-trait `BSSVS` diffusion (`Lemey et al., 2009`) on $n = 62$ whole genomes distributed across **17 discrete host states** (an average of 3.6 sequences per host state), retaining transitions with Bayes Factor $\text{BF} \ge 3.0$ and posterior probability $\ge 0.60$. Classic discrete-trait ancestral reconstruction assumes that sampling intensity is proportional to subpopulation prevalence; when applied to opportunistic wildlife carcass submissions (where 13 of the 25 Sable Island sequences happened to be collected from northern fulmars between February and April 2023), the most heavily sampled early host state is mechanically inferred as the ancestral root/trunk state ("source"). Without a structured coalescent model (`MASCOT` or `MultiTypeTree`) or tip-date randomization controls, directional host-to-host transition arrows (`Fulmar -> Crow -> Raccoon`) over-interpret sparse, opportunistically sampled phylogeny tips.

* **Claim 3:** *"Ferrets inoculated with A(H5N5) viruses showed rapid, severe disease onset, with some evidence of direct contact transmission."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Critique:** The donor pathogenicity and neurotropism claims are strongly supported: 100% of intranasally inoculated ferrets ($n = 3$ monitored for survival per virus, plus $n = 3$ necropsied at 4 dpi) reached humane endpoints by 4–5 dpi (`Figure 3B`), with high viral loads in nasal washes (`5.25–5.75 log10 TCID50/mL`), systemic replication in brain (`5.75 log10 TCID50/mL` for `A/Raccoon/PEI/FAV-0193-1/2023`), liver, and intestine (`Figure 3F`), and extensive CNS neuroinvasion documented by IHC (`Figures 4–5`). However, the direct-contact transmission evidence (`n = 3` contact ferrets per virus) is mixed: while 1 of 2 surviving contacts in the raccoon-virus group seroconverted (`HI = 320` at 25 dpi), **none of the 6 contact ferrets ever shed detectable infectious virus in nasal washes (`<1.0 log10 TCID50/mL`) at 1, 3, 5, 7, or 10 dpi (`Figure 3G`)**. Although 1 of 3 contacts in each group was euthanized at day 5 post-cohousing with diarrhea, weight loss, and neurologic signs, the authors explicitly state that *"tissue was not available to confirm this"* (Discussion, line 120). Euthanizing moribund contact ferrets at 5 dpi without collecting brain, lung, or rectal swab/intestinal samples for `qRT-PCR` or `TCID50` leaves the cause of death and route of infection in those two contacts unverified.

* **Claim 4:** *"However, these viruses have maintained receptor binding traits of avian influenza viruses and were susceptible to oseltamivir and zanamivir [and baloxavir]. As such, NA-I117T and PA-T40A do not confer RI to NAIs and CENI, respectively, in the context of A(H5N5) viruses."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Well Supported]**
  * **Critique:** Solid-phase glycan-binding assays (`Figure S2`) confirm strict preference for avian-type $\alpha 2,3$-linked sialylglycopolymers (`3'SLN`) over human-type $\alpha 2,6$-linked (`6'SLN`) receptors. Fluorometric `MUNANA` neuraminidase inhibition assays (for oseltamivir, zanamivir, and peramivir) and both `IRINA` single-cycle and plaque-reduction assays (for baloxavir) confirm $\text{IC}_{50}$ and $\text{EC}_{50}$ fold-changes $\le 2$-fold relative to clade 2.3.4.4b reference strains (`Table S3`), rigorously ruling out phenotypic resistance from the background `NA-I117T` and `PA-T40A` polymorphisms.

## 3. Concrete Recommendations for Action

1. **Validate `BEAST` discrete-trait host and location transitions with tip-state randomization and structured coalescent models:**
   * Perform tip-state swap permutations in `BEAST` to verify that the inferred `Sable Island -> Mainland` and `Northern Fulmar -> Crow` Bayes Factors ($\text{BF} \ge 3.0$) are not driven solely by temporal and numeric sampling imbalances across the 62 genomes, or re-estimate migration rates using an approximation to the structured coalescent (`MASCOT`) with lumped ecological guilds (e.g., pelagic Charadriiformes/Procellariiformes vs. terrestrial scavenging birds vs. mesocarnivores) rather than 17 individual species states.
2. **Reconcile collection date discrepancies between `Table 1` and the main text, and report clinical swab vs. egg-isolate genotypes:**
   * In `Table 1`, the collection dates for `A/American_Crow/PEI/FAV-0035-6/2023` and `A/Raccoon/PEI/FAV-0193-1/2023` are listed as `"February 2023"` and `"May 2023"`, contradicting the Results text (line 35), which states they were collected on **January 9, 2023** and **April 3, 2023**, respectively. Correct `Table 1`, and clarify in the `STAR Methods` whether the Nanopore consensus genomes (`EPI_ISL_18702952`–`18702992`) were sequenced directly from primary clinical swabs/tissues or after `E1` passage in SPF embryonated chicken eggs (which can select against mammalian-type `HA` or `PB2` variants).
3. **Include rectal swabs and necropsy tissue titration for contact ferrets in HPAI transmission protocols:**
   * Because clade 2.3.4.4b H5 viruses frequently exhibit neurotropism and gastrointestinal replication (`3.5 log10 TCID50/mL` in donor intestine; severe diarrhea in contacts) without high upper-respiratory shedding in contacts, standard transmission protocols should mandate both nasal washes and rectal swabs alongside mandatory CNS/lung/GI necropsy titration for any contact animal reaching humane endpoints.
