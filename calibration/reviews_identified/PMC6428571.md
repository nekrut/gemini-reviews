### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Sohail et al. re-evaluate prominent signals of polygenic adaptation on human height across present-day European, global (1000 Genomes, $N = 1,005$; POPRES, 19 populations), and ancient Eurasian ($N = 1,112$ across Hunter-Gatherer, Early Farmer, and Steppe ancestry groups) populations, as well as within-population singleton density score (`tSDS`) signals in the UK10K cohort ($N = 3,195$). Comparing summary statistics from the heterogeneous 79-cohort GIANT Consortium meta-analysis ($N = 253,288$) against homogeneous single-cohort UK Biobank GWAS specifications—including unrelated white British controlling for 10 PCs (`UKB Neale`, $N = 336,474$), linear mixed models (`UKB Loh`, $N = 459,327$), uncorrected multi-ancestry UKB (`UKB all no PCs`), and within-sibling family designs (`UKB sibs WB`)—the authors demonstrate that polygenic adaptation signals constructed from sub-significant SNPs (e.g., ~1,700 LD-independent blocks or $P < 0.01$ clumped SNPs) are heavily inflated by residual North–South European population stratification in GIANT. Specifically, the genome-wide `tSDS` correlation vanishes from Spearman $r = 0.078$ ($P = 1.55 \times 10^{-65}$) in GIANT to $r = 0.009$ (or $-0.009$, $P = 0.077$) in UKB, while the POPRES polygenic overdispersion statistic $Q_X$ for sub-significant SNPs drops from $Q_X = 1100$ ($P_{Q_X} = 10^{-220}$) in GIANT to $Q_X = 64$ ($P_{Q_X} = 5 \times 10^{-7}$) in UKB and disappears completely in UKB sibling estimates. Both data and complete analysis code are archived on GitHub.

This is a landmark forensic population genetics study that diagnoses how subtle, uncorrected allele-frequency clines in GWAS meta-analyses interact with both polygenic risk score aggregation ($Z = \sum_l \beta_l p_l$) and coalescent tip-branch metrics (`tSDS`, which independently varies along the European North–South diversity gradient). The experimental design is exceptionally thorough, constructing positive stratification controls (`UKB all no PCs`), negative family-based controls (`UKB sibs WB`), and null calibration checks (Fig. 4—figure supplement 3, showing that even 10-PC-corrected UKB summary statistics exhibit mild $Q_X$ $P$-value inflation on randomly ascertained SNPs, whereas within-sibling estimates are properly calibrated). Only minor technical and editorial issues exist: a sign discrepancy between the main text and the Fig. 1b caption for the UKB `tSDS` Spearman correlation ($+0.009$ vs. $-0.009$), and the use of an independent-locus Beta-posterior credible interval formula for population polygenic scores that omits GWAS $\hat{\beta}_l$ estimation error (which largely explains why noisy `NG2015 sibs` and `UKB sibs` point estimates appear to have deceptively narrow error bars in Fig. 1—figure supplement 5).

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Polygenic adaptation signals for height based on sub-significant SNPs in GIANT are severely inflated by uncorrected North–South European population stratification:** **[Well Supported].**  
  The authors demonstrate directly that GIANT $\hat{\beta}$ coefficients correlate significantly with 1000 Genomes PC4 and PC11 loadings (which track the GBR–TSI North–South allele frequency difference; Fig. 2a) and that 59% of SNPs in the top GBR–TSI decile are estimated as height-increasing in GIANT compared to ~50% in UKB (Fig. 2—figure supplement 3). Furthermore, deliberately omitting PC correction in a multi-ancestry UK Biobank GWAS (`UKB all no PCs`) reproduces the exact GIANT stratification artifact (Fig. 2—figure supplements 1–3).

* **The genome-wide correlation between Singleton Density Scores (`tSDS`) and GWAS $P$-values in GIANT is an artifact of collinear North–South clines in singleton density and GIANT stratification:** **[Well Supported].**  
  Because Northern Europeans (GBR) have lower genetic diversity and hence fewer singletons (higher SDS for GBR-enriched alleles) than Southern Europeans (TSI) (Fig. 2d), aligning SDS signs to GIANT's stratification-biased $\hat{\beta}$ signs induces a spurious genome-wide slope across all $P$-value bins ($P = 1.55 \times 10^{-65}$). Replacing SDS with raw GBR–TSI allele frequency differences reproduces the `tSDS` curve across all six GWAS datasets (Fig. 3a vs. Fig. 3c).

* **Previously reported family-based GIANT sibling summary statistics (`NG2015 sibs`) suffered from a technical stratification artifact, whereas UK Biobank within-sibling estimates (`UKB sibs WB`) eliminate both PC correlations and sub-significant $Q_X$ overdispersion:** **[Well Supported].**  
  In Fig. 2—figure supplements 1–3 and Fig. 4, the original `NG2015 sibs` summary statistics correlate strongly with GBR–TSI frequency differences, whereas `UKB sibs WB` shows zero correlation with PC loadings and yields uniform $Q_X$ and latitude $P$-values under random SNP permutations (Fig. 4—figure supplement 3).

* **A modest residual signal of polygenic adaptation on height persists when restricting exclusively to LD-independent genome-wide significant SNPs ($P < 5 \times 10^{-8}$):** **[Moderately Supported].**  
  At 506 LD-independent UKB genome-wide significant SNPs oriented using within-sibling $\hat{\beta}$ signs (`UKB sibs WB`), the mean `tSDS` distribution is significantly shifted above zero (Fig. 3b) without a corresponding GBR–TSI allele frequency shift (Fig. 3d). However, as the authors appropriately caution in the Discussion (line 28) and demonstrate in Fig. 4—figure supplement 3, SNP ascertainment via standard GWAS $P < 5 \times 10^{-8}$ and residual fine-scale structure in the UK Biobank still warrant caution when interpreting population-level $Q_X$ differences ($P_{Q_X} = 0.02$).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Propagate GWAS Effect-Size Estimation Variance ($\text{SE}(\hat{\beta}_l)^2$) into the Polygenic Score Credible Intervals
* **WHY:** In Materials and Methods ("Polygenic scores", Part 2 line 64), the 95% credible intervals for population polygenic scores $Z_pop = \sum_l \hat{\beta}_l p_{l,\text{pop}}$ are computed strictly from the binomial/Beta sampling variance of the target population allele frequencies $p_{l,\text{pop}}$, treating the GWAS effect sizes $\hat{\beta}_l$ as fixed constants without estimation error. When $\hat{\beta}_l$ comes from a smaller within-sibling GWAS (`UKB sibs WB` or `NG2015 sibs`), $\text{Var}(\hat{\beta}_l)$ is large and dominates $V_A$, causing the standardized score $(Z - \mu)/\sqrt{V_A}$ to fluctuate wildly while the plotted Beta-only error bars remain tiny (for example, in Fig. 1—figure supplement 5, where `NG2015 sibs` has a Hunter-Gatherer score of $3.86$ with a narrow plotted CI of $[3.60, 4.12]$).
* **WHAT:** Compute and display total variance error bars that propagate both allele-frequency sampling variance and GWAS $\text{SE}(\hat{\beta}_l)^2$ when comparing high-powered (`UKB Neale`) and lower-powered (`UKB sibs WB`) summary statistics.
* **HOW:** For each population $k$, add the estimation noise term $\sum_l \text{SE}(\hat{\beta}_l)^2 (p_{lk} - \bar{p}_l)^2$ to the Beta allele-frequency posterior variance before dividing by $V_A$ (and bias-correct $\hat{V}_A = \sum_l [\hat{\beta}_l^2 - \text{SE}(\hat{\beta}_l)^2] \bar{p}_l (1 - \bar{p}_l)$ so that noisy sibling $\hat{\beta}_l$ estimates do not inflate the denominator $V_A$ and artificially shrink standardized differences).

#### 2. Reconcile the Sign Discrepancy for the UK Biobank `tSDS` Spearman Correlation
* **WHY:** In the Figure 1b caption (Part 1, line 27 and Part 2, line 83), the UK Biobank `tSDS` correlation is reported as **negative**: `"(Spearman r = −0.009, p=0.077)"`. However, in the main text (Part 1, line 58), the exact same statistic is reported as **positive**: `"(ρ = 0.009, p=0.077, Figure 1b)"`. Because the authors note in Figure 2c and Figure 2—figure supplement 4 that residual stratification within the UKB white British subset runs in the *opposite* direction to GIANT, the sign of $r$ matters for interpretation.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Check `Figure 1—source data 1` in the repository (`github.com/uqrmaie1/sohail_maier_2019`) and make the sign of Spearman $\rho$ ($\pm 0.009$) consistent between line 58 and the Figure 1b caption.
