# Review of PMC11662780

**Manuscript Title:** Integrating single-cell RNA-Seq and machine learning to dissect tryptophan metabolism in ulcerative colitis  
**Authors:** Guorong Chen, Hongying Qi, Li Jiang, Shijie Sun, Junhai Zhang, Jiali Yu, Fang Liu, Yanli Zhang, Shiyu Du  
**Journal / Preprint:** Journal of Translational Medicine (2024) | DOI: 10.1186/s12967-024-05934-w  
**Overall Score:** **2.7 / 10.0** (Fundamentally Invalid)  
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.6/3.5 | Claims: 0.5/2.5 | Biology: 0.8/2.0 | Reproducibility: 0.8/2.0

---

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Chen et al. combine publicly available single-cell RNA-seq datasets of ulcerative colitis (UC; `GSE214695` and `GSE125527`, totaling 13 UC and 14 healthy control samples with 39,080 cells) and bulk transcriptomic datasets (`GSE87466` [misprinted as `GSE887466`] and `GSE53306`) to investigate tryptophan metabolism in UC. They score 48 curated tryptophan metabolism-related genes (TrMGs) across single cells using five enrichment algorithms (AUCell, UCell, singscore, ssGSEA, and AddModuleScore), partition all 39,080 cells at the global mean score into "high-expression" and "low-expression" groups, intersect differentially expressed and correlated genes across these two groups (yielding 22 genes), filter for bulk upregulation in UC, and apply five machine-learning feature-selection methods (LASSO, Boruta, GBM, SVM-RFE, and Random Forest) to select three overlapping genes: *CTSS*, *S100A11*, and *TUBB*. They then perform CellChat and Monocle2/CytoTRACE analyses on *CTSS*+ vs. *CTSS*− macrophages and conclude that *CTSS*, *S100A11*, and *TUBB* are *"key regulators of tryptophan metabolism in UC."*

Although investigating cell-type-specific metabolic dysregulation in IBD is biologically worthwhile, the study's analytical pipeline is fatally confounded by cell-type composition, leading to the biologically invalid conclusion that a lysosomal protease (*CTSS*), a calcium-binding calgranulin (*S100A11*), and a ubiquitous cytoskeletal beta-tubulin housekeeping gene (*TUBB*)—none of which is an enzyme, transporter, or transcription factor in the tryptophan/kynurenine/serotonin/indole pathways—are "key regulators of tryptophan metabolism." This artifact occurs because the authors pool all 39,080 cells across 11 distinct lineages, split them at the mean TrMG score (which simply separates macrophages, fibroblasts, and epithelial cells in the "high" group from T, B, and NK lymphocytes in the "low" group; Figure 2f), and run differential expression between the two groups—thereby recovering generic pan-myeloid and cytoskeletal markers rather than tryptophan pathway regulators. In addition, the manuscript contains direct internal contradictions regarding which cell types express *TUBB*, cell-level pseudoreplication, verbatim template text describing non-existent Kaplan-Meier overall survival analyses, a non-existent GEO accession number (`GSE887466`), corrupted abbreviations, and no public code. Resolving these flaws requires redesigning the differential expression analysis *within* each cell type and restricting metabolic regulator claims to actual pathway components.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* ***CTSS*, *S100A11*, and *TUBB* are key regulators of tryptophan metabolism in ulcerative colitis: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  None of the three prioritized genes (*CTSS*, *S100A11*, *TUBB*) belongs to the 48 tryptophan metabolism-related genes (TrMGs) or participates enzymatically in the kynurenine, serotonin, or indole pathways. They were selected because the authors divided all 39,080 cells across all 11 cell types into a "high TrMG score" group (composed primarily of macrophages, fibroblasts, and epithelial cells; Figure 2f) and a "low TrMG score" group (composed of T cells, B cells, plasma cells, and NK cells) and ran `FindMarkers` and Pearson correlation across the pooled cell populations (Figure 2g–i). Comparing macrophages/stromal cells against lymphocytes inevitably flags abundant myeloid and structural genes (*CTSS*, *S100A11*, *TUBB*, *ENO1*, *SOD2*, *ANXA2*, *HLA-DPA1*). Subsequently, in bulk mucosal biopsies, because active UC tissue has heavy macrophage and leukocyte infiltration compared to healthy mucosa, any abundant myeloid/leukocyte transcript (*CTSS*, *S100A11*, *TUBB*) separates UC from control tissue with high AUC and correlates with other myeloid-expressed genes ($r = 0.62$) purely via tissue cell-type composition.

* **Tryptophan metabolic gene set scores are higher in intestinal macrophages, fibroblasts, and epithelial cells than in lymphocytes, and are elevated in UC macrophages: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Scoring the 48 TrMGs (which include myeloid-expressed *IDO1* and *KYNU* and epithelial/enterochromaffin enzymes) via AUCell and UCell validly shows that myeloid, stromal, and epithelial compartments express higher levels of tryptophan-catabolizing enzymes than resting lymphocytes (Figure 2a–d). However, the statistical comparison of TrMG scores between UC ($n = 13$) and HC ($n = 14$) in Figure 2d treats 39,080 individual cells as independent observations rather than aggregating at the patient/donor level (pseudobulk), which inflates statistical significance.

* ***CTSS*, *S100A11*, and *TUBB* are predominantly expressed in macrophages: [Overstated].**  
  The manuscript directly contradicts itself between the Abstract and the Results on this claim. The Abstract (line 58) states: *"Among the key tryptophan metabolism-related genes, CTSS, S100A11, and TUBB were predominantly expressed in macrophages."* However, Results (line 176; Figure 5a–c) states: *"TUBB was predominantly expressed in B cells and fibroblasts, CTSS showing significant expression in macrophages, and S100A11 was highly expressed in macrophages, fibroblasts, and neutrophils."*

* **Five machine-learning algorithms independently validate *CTSS*, *S100A11*, and *TUBB* as optimal UC biomarkers (AUC 0.663–0.926): [Fragile].**  
  The five feature-selection models (LASSO, Boruta, GBM, SVM-RFE, Random Forest) were fed a pre-screened list of only 10–21 candidate genes already filtered for differential expression, without documenting cross-validation folds, hyperparameter tuning, or sample sizes, and the training ROC AUCs (Figure 4h) were evaluated on the exact same cohort used to select the features.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Cell-Type Composition Confounding by Performing Differential Expression and Correlation Within Macrophages Only
* **WHY:** Splitting all 39,080 cells across 11 cell types into "High" vs. "Low" TrMG score groups compares macrophages/epithelial cells against lymphocytes, recovering lineage markers (*CTSS*, *S100A11*, *TUBB*) rather than genes that regulate tryptophan metabolism.
* **WHAT:** Restrict the High-vs.-Low TrMG differential expression and correlation analysis strictly *within* the macrophage subpopulation (and separately within epithelial cells and fibroblasts), and also directly analyze the 48 curated TrMGs themselves (e.g., *IDO1*, *KYNU*, *KMO*, *TDO2*, *AFMID*, *TPH1*) between UC and HC macrophages at the donor-pseudobulk level.
* **HOW:** Subset the Seurat object to macrophages only (`subset(seurat_obj, idents == "Macrophages")`); compute donor-level pseudobulk expression (`AggregateExpression` by sample ID across the 13 UC and 14 HC patients); test which of the 48 TrMGs are significantly upregulated in UC vs. HC macrophages using `DESeq2` at the patient level ($N = 27$); and identify genes that correlate with TrMG activity specifically within macrophages after controlling for donor batch.

#### 2. Reframe *CTSS*, *S100A11*, and *TUBB* Accurately and Reconcile Contradictions Between Abstract and Results
* **WHY:** Calling *CTSS*, *S100A11*, and *TUBB* *"key tryptophan metabolism-related genes"* and *"key regulators of tryptophan metabolism"* in the Abstract is biologically inaccurate, and claiming in the Abstract that *TUBB* is predominantly expressed in macrophages contradicts Figure 5a–c and line 176.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Revise the Abstract, Results, and Discussion to state accurately that *CTSS*, *S100A11*, and *TUBB* are general inflammatory/myeloid and cytoskeletal transcripts co-upregulated in high-TrMG cell compartments in UC rather than metabolic enzymes or direct regulators of tryptophan catabolism, and correct line 58 of the Abstract to match line 176 (*"CTSS was predominantly expressed in macrophages, S100A11 in macrophages, fibroblasts, and neutrophils, and TUBB in B cells and fibroblasts"*).

#### 3. Correct Template Artifacts, Wrong GEO Accession ID, Seurat Filtering Description, and Corrupted Abbreviations
* **WHY:** The manuscript contains several severe technical and editorial errors that indicate unedited template reuse:
  1. Line 124 (Statistical analysis) states: *"Subtype-specific overall survival (OS) was estimated and compared employing the Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test."* Ulcerative colitis in this study has no survival data or Kaplan-Meier analysis.
  2. Line 89 lists the bulk training cohort as `GSE887466`. No such GEO accession exists; the correct accession for the UC mucosal biopsy dataset is `GSE87466` (which is an Affymetrix microarray cohort, not "Bulk RNAseq").
  3. Line 89 states: *"We also focused on genes that were expressed at levels between 200 and 7000 and were active in at least three cells."* In Seurat, `200` to `7000` is the per-cell `nFeature_RNA` threshold (number of detected genes per cell), not the expression level of a gene.
  4. Lines 226–257 (Abbreviations) omit all abbreviation terms, printing only a disconnected list of definitions (`Area Under the Curve cell-level`, `The Gradient Boosting Machine`, etc.).
* **WHAT & HOW:** Delete the erroneous Kaplan-Meier overall survival sentence from line 124; correct `GSE887466` to `GSE87466` (and label `GSE87466` and `GSE53306` accurately as microarray datasets, stating their exact UC and HC sample sizes); rewrite line 89 to state *"cells expressing between 200 and 7,000 genes (`200 < nFeature_RNA < 7000`) and genes detected in at least 3 cells"*; restore the missing acronym column in the Abbreviations section; and deposit the complete R analysis script on GitHub or Zenodo.
