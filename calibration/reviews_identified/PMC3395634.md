### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Murrell et al. introduce the Mixed Effects Model of Evolution (MEME), a codon-based phylogenetic likelihood framework designed to detect individual coding sites undergoing episodic diversifying selection across a subset of branches without requiring *a priori* branch partitioning. At each codon site, MEME treats the site-specific synonymous rate ($\alpha$), background non-synonymous rate ($\beta^- \le \alpha$), foreground non-synonymous rate ($\beta^+$, unrestricted under the alternative and constrained to $\beta^+ \le \alpha$ under the null), and branch mixture weight ($p^+$) as fixed effects, while treating the selective category assignment on each branch as an independent discrete random effect marginalized analytically via Felsenstein's pruning algorithm over mixture transition matrices. Using parametric bootstrap simulations to calibrate the boundary likelihood ratio test (LRT) statistic as a $0.33\chi^2_0 + 0.30\chi^2_1 + 0.37\chi^2_2$ mixture under a strict neutral worst-case null ($\omega = 1$), the authors benchmark MEME against the Fixed Effects Likelihood (FEL) method across simulated phylogenies (including TreeBase topologies and three empirical tree scaffolds across varying $\omega^-$, $\omega^+$, and $q^+$ regimes) and 16 empirical protein-coding alignments. They show that MEME controls Type I error under strict neutrality, matches FEL in power when selection is pervasive, and detects nearly four times as many positively selected sites as FEL across the 16 empirical datasets (237 vs. 63 sites at $p \le 0.05$), including 56 sites that FEL classifies as negatively selected because strong background purifying selection masks lineage-specific adaptive bursts.

This is a rigorous methodological and empirical study that resolves a major pathology of constant-$\omega$ site models—namely, that adding taxa or sampling conserved clades dilutes the signal of transient adaptive evolution. The mathematical derivation of the marginalized branch-mixture pruning algorithm is exact under branch independence, the simulation benchmarks are symmetric and well-calibrated, and the authors provide a remarkably candid assessment of the limits of empirical Bayes (EB) branch-site attribution, formulating a "selection inference uncertainty principle" after showing that neutral background branches yield high false-discovery rates when attempting to pinpoint single branch-site events. The primary limitations are: (1) fixing relative branch lengths and nucleotide substitution parameters from a global single-$\omega$ codon model in Step 1 without propagating topology or branch-length uncertainty; (2) assuming instantaneous single-nucleotide codon changes and branch-to-branch independence, which leaves the model potentially susceptible to multi-nucleotide mutations or unmodeled synonymous rate variation across branches; and (3) reporting unadjusted $p \le 0.05$ discovery counts in Table 2 as the primary headline metric while relegating false discovery rate ($q$-value) bounds and software/data links to the supplement.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **MEME achieves exact marginalization over $2^B$ branch selective configurations in $O(B)$ pruning time and maintains calibrated Type I error under the strict neutral null: [Well Supported].**  
  By assuming conditional independence of selective regime assignments across branches, Equation (1) validly factors the sum over $2^B$ branch configurations into branch-wise mixture transition matrices $P_b(t_b) = (1 - p^+)e^{Q(\beta^-)t_b} + p^+ e^{Q(\beta^+)t_b}$ (Equation 2). Parametric bootstrap calibration under the worst-case neutral boundary ($\beta^- = \beta^+ = \alpha, p^+ \text{ free}$) establishes the $0.33:0.30:0.37$ mixture of $\chi^2_0, \chi^2_1, \chi^2_2$, and null simulations across $N = 8$ to $256$ taxa and TreeBase trees confirm nominal-to-conservative false positive rates ($\le 0.05$) at moderate divergences.

* **MEME substantially outperforms constant-$\omega$ site methods (FEL) when diversifying selection is restricted to a small fraction of lineages ($q^+ = 0.10\text{--}0.25$) while retaining comparable power under pervasive selection: [Well Supported].**  
  Table 1 and Table S3 provide symmetric comparisons across 27 parameter combinations ($\omega^- \in \{0, 0.2, 0.4\}$, $q^+ \in \{0.1, 0.25, 0.5\}$, $\omega^+ \in \{4, 12, 36\}$) on three empirical tree topologies (JEV *env*, vertebrate rhodopsin, and camelid VHH). When $q^+ = 0.1$, FEL has $<5\%$ power across all regimes because the tree-wide average $\omega$ remains below 1, whereas MEME achieves up to $99\%$ power depending on tree depth and $\omega^+$.

* **Purifying selection on background lineages masks episodic positive selection in empirical alignments, explaining why constant-$\omega$ models lose power as more sequences are sampled: [Well Supported].**  
  Across 16 empirical datasets (Table 2), MEME identifies 56 sites where FEL infers significant purifying selection ($M^+F^-$), such as vertebrate rhodopsin codon 210 ($\Delta \log L = 21.1, p = 10^{-7}$ over FEL), and resolves the sampling-subset paradox previously reported in vertebrate rhodopsin and H3N2 hemagglutinin (Table S20), detecting 1, 2, and 4 sites across nested squirrelfish, fish, and full vertebrate rhodopsin alignments where constant-$\omega$ models collapsed to zero detections on the full tree.

* **Empirical Bayes identification of specific positively selected branches at an individual site is statistically fragile and unsuitable for formal inference: [Well Supported].**  
  Rather than overselling their branch-level EB metric ($\text{EBF}_b \ge 20$), the authors perform targeted simulations (Figures S2–S3) demonstrating that while a selected branch in a strongly conserved background ($\omega^- = 0$) is detected ~45% of the time, under a neutral background ($\omega^- = 1$) at least one neutral branch is falsely flagged at 48.2% of positively selected sites because inference at a single site along a single branch relies on a sample size of one realization of the Markov process.

* **Natural selection is predominantly episodic across coding genes and the number of positively selected sites is nearly fourfold higher than constant-$\omega$ estimates (237 vs. 63 sites across 16 genes): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the qualitative conclusion that episodic selection is much more common than pervasive selection is strongly backed by the 2-d.o.f. LRT comparing MEME directly to FEL (rightmost column of Table 2), the specific headline counts (186 newly detected sites across 16 datasets) are tallied at an unadjusted nominal threshold of $p \le 0.05$ across 4,845 tested codons. Although the neutral null is conservative for sites with $\omega^- < 1$, a fraction of nominal $p \le 0.05$ hits across thousands of codons will reflect Type I errors or unmodeled evolutionary processes (such as multi-nucleotide substitutions or branch-specific synonymous rate variation, since $\alpha$ is fixed across branches at a site).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report False Discovery Rate ($q$-value) Adjusted Site Counts Directly Alongside Nominal $p \le 0.05$ Counts in Table 2
* **WHY:** Table 2 aggregates 4,845 site-level hypothesis tests across 16 genes at an unadjusted threshold of $p \le 0.05$. Even under a slightly conservative null, testing hundreds of codons per gene without multiple-testing adjustment in the main summary table inflates the apparent number of newly discovered adaptive sites and creates a disconnect with the Discussion, which rightly warns readers to use Benjamini-Hochberg $q$-values.
* **WHAT:** Add columns or parenthetical counts to Table 2 reporting the number of MEME and FEL sites surviving $q \le 0.10$ (or $q \le 0.05$) for each of the 16 datasets.
* **HOW:** Since $q$-value upper bounds are already computed in Supplementary Tables S4–S19, extract the per-gene counts of sites with $q \le 0.10$ for both MEME and FEL and display them in Table 2 (e.g., `M+F0: 17 (12)`), updating the Discussion's fourfold comparison to state both the nominal $p \le 0.05$ ratio and the FDR-controlled ratio.

#### 2. Quantify Sensitivity to Multi-Nucleotide Substitutions and Branch-Level Synonymous Rate Variation
* **WHY:** MEME models $\alpha$ (the synonymous substitution rate) as a site-specific fixed effect shared across all branches ($t_b$ scaled by $\alpha$), while allowing $\beta$ to jump to $\beta^+$ on a subset of branches. If a single branch experiences a simultaneous double/triple nucleotide mutation within a codon (a multi-hit event, which Equation 1 sets to instantaneous rate 0) or a localized synonymous rate anomaly, the model is forced to accommodate the concentrated non-synonymous changes on that short branch by inflating $\beta^+$ and $p^+$, potentially generating false positives even when $dN/dS \le 1$.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate MEME's false positive rate under simulations that include a realistic fraction of multi-nucleotide substitutions (or branch-to-branch $\alpha$ variation) and flag empirical $M^+F^-$ sites driven by single-branch multi-hit codon changes.
* **HOW:** Using the existing vertebrate rhodopsin and JEV *env* simulation trees, simulate neutral alignments where 1–5% of substitutions are instantaneous doublet/triplet nucleotide changes (or where $\alpha_b$ varies across branches), record the resulting FPR at $p \le 0.05$, and inspect the ancestral state reconstructions of the 56 empirical $M^+F^-$ sites to report how many rely on a single branch carrying $\ge 2$ nucleotide changes within the same codon.

#### 3. Assess the Impact of Fixing Step-1 Global Codon Branch Lengths on Site-Level Inference
* **WHY:** Step 1 estimates relative branch lengths $\hat{t}_b$ under a single alignment-wide $\omega$ codon model and holds them fixed during Step 2 and Step 3. On trees with highly heterogeneous selection or saturation along specific lineages, fixing $\hat{t}_b$ ignores estimation variance and forces site-level $\alpha$ and $\beta^+$ parameters to absorb branch-length misspecification.
* **WHAT:** Perform a sensitivity check on one or two representative alignments (e.g., vertebrate rhodopsin and Camelid VHH) comparing point-fixed $\hat{t}_b$ against bootstrap/posterior branch-length ensembles.
* **HOW:** Re-run MEME on 50 non-parametric or parametric bootstrap trees (or sample branch lengths from the Step 1 asymptotic covariance matrix) for the rhodopsin dataset and report the concordance of site-level $p$-values (particularly for borderline sites and key functional sites 96, 183, 195, 210, and 273).

#### 4. Provide Explicit Code, Software Version, and Alignment Accession Details in the Main Text
* **WHY:** Reproducibility requires direct access to the exact HyPhy batch files, alignment files, and phylogenetic topologies analyzed in Table 2 without hunting through prose descriptions in Supplementary Text S1.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Add a dedicated *Software and Data Availability* subsection at the end of the Methods stating the exact version of HyPhy and the Datamonkey web server implementing `MEME.bf`, and provide a direct repository URL or supplementary archive containing all 16 sequence alignments and rooted/unrooted Newick trees used in Table 2. Fix minor typographical artifacts in the manuscript text, including the stray character in the Figure 2 caption (`black –Ê no information`) and the misspelling `Mamallian -globin` in Table 2.
