### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

#### Executive Summary
This software platform article presents the architectural and modeling advances in BEAST 2.5 over the four years following the initial BEAST 2.0 release. The authors synthesize a broad ecosystem of modular packages (Table 1) spanning reversible-jump site model averaging (`bModelTest`), polymorphism-aware models (`PoMo`), flexible local clocks (`FLC`), unstructured and structured phylodynamic birth-death and coalescent frameworks (`BDSKY`, `SA`, `EpiInf`, `PhyDyn`, `BDMM`, `BASTA`, `MASCOT`, `SCOTTI`, `BadTrIP`), multispecies coalescent and fossilized birth-death models (`StarBEAST 2`, `STACEY`, `SNAPP`, `CladeAge`), reticulate evolution (`Bacter`, `SpeciesNetwork`, `DENIM`, `AIM`), and marginal likelihood / model adequacy estimators (`NS`, `TreeModelAdequacy`, `MASTER`). Four worked empirical analyses are presented with full XML configurations, alignments, and post-processing scripts archived on Zenodo and GitHub: (1) `bModelTest` on 36 mammalian mitochondrial sequences (906 bp), (2) a sampled-ancestor `bdsky` analysis of 811 West African Ebola virus coding genomes (2013–2015), (3) an Isolation-with-Migration (`AIM`) analysis of 100 nuclear loci across six East African cichlid species (*Neolamprologus savoryi* complex), and (4) posterior predictive model adequacy (`TreeModelAdequacy`) comparing stochastic birth-death SIR and deterministic coalescent SIR models on 100 North American H1N1 influenza genomes (2009).

The manuscript is exceptionally well-organized, mathematically explicit about joint posterior factorizations (Equations 1–3) and model approximations (e.g., contrasting exact structured coalescent MCMC in `MultiTypeTree` vs. lineage-independent state tracking in `BASTA` vs. marginal coalescent approximations in `MASCOT`), and exemplary in its data/code reproducibility (all four case studies have persistent Zenodo DOIs or GitHub repositories). Minor limitations include the unaddressed impact of fixing the root height to a point estimate (9.2 Mya) in the cichlid `AIM` vignette, unmodelled spatial structure in the unstructured `bdsky` Ebola analysis during late-stage localized transmission, and the reliance on informal validation standards across third-party packages.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

#### Detailed Rubric Evaluation

* **Claim 1 — Reversible-jump MCMC in `bModelTest` integrates over nucleotide substitution model uncertainty and identifies a 95% credible set of eight models on mammalian mtDNA: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 2 and the accompanying Zenodo archive (`10.5281/zenodo.1475369`) demonstrate transdimensional sampling across reversible 6-rate partitions, equal/unequal base frequencies, $+\Gamma$, and $+I$ parameters on the 36-species mammalian dataset of Yang and Yoder (2003), avoiding arbitrary single-model conditioning.

* **Claim 2 — Sampled-ancestor birth-death skyline (`bdsky` + `SA`) inference on 811 West African Ebola genomes recovers $R_e(t)$ trajectories and an epidemic origin ($t_0$) concordant with the December 26, 2013 index case and WHO surveillance estimates: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 3 (`10.5281/zenodo.1476124`) estimates $R_e$ across 20 intervals and monthly sampling proportions $p = \psi / \delta$ while allowing removal probability $r < 1$ for post-sampling transmission. Importantly, the authors explicitly diagnose two deviations between inferred sampling proportion $p$ and empirical case-based proportions: overestimation during peak transmission (unrepresented transmission chains) and underestimation in the final two months due to isolated local chains violating the unstructured panmictic assumption of `bdsky`.

* **Claim 3 — The `AIM` (Approximate Isolation with Migration) package in `StarBEAST 2` jointly infers species tree topologies and directional gene flow (Bayes factor > 10) across 100 nuclear loci in *Neolamprologus* cichlids: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Figure 5 summarizes ranked species topologies and migration arrows supported at $\text{BF} > 10$. However, the analysis fixes the species tree root height to a single point value of 9.2 Mya (taken from Meyer et al., 2017) rather than placing a calibration prior density to propagate dating uncertainty, and assumes that backward-in-time gene flow rates are inversely proportional to species coexistence duration with an exponential prior centered at 0.05 without reporting prior sensitivity checks on the resulting Bayes factors.

* **Claim 4 — Posterior predictive simulations in `TreeModelAdequacy` (`TMA` + `MASTER`) show that a stochastic birth-death SIR model captures empirical root height on 2009 North American H1N1 influenza better than a deterministic coalescent SIR model: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Figure 6 (`10.5281/zenodo.1473852`) illustrates how stochastic early-epidemic dynamics yield a wider posterior predictive root-height distribution that overlaps the empirical tree height, whereas the deterministic coalescent SIR model underpredicts root height. However, the comparison conflates two distinct modeling axes simultaneously (stochastic vs. deterministic dynamics *and* birth-death sampling vs. coalescent conditioning on sampling times) using only a single test statistic (root height) without reporting formal two-sided posterior predictive $p$-values.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### Major Issues, Minor Issues & Concrete Recommendations

#### 1. Decouple Stochasticity from Coalescent vs. Birth-Death Conditioning in the H1N1 Adequacy Vignette (Major Issue)
* **WHY:** In Figure 6 ("Model selection and model adequacy"), the authors compare a *stochastic birth-death SIR* model [28] against a *deterministic coalescent SIR* model [27] and conclude that *"stochasticity may play an important role in the early stages of the pandemic."* Because the `phylodynamics` package [27] also implements a *stochastic coalescent SIR* model (explicitly listed in Table 1), comparing birth-death stochastic SIR against coalescent deterministic SIR confounds the tree-prior sampling assumption (birth-death vs. coalescent) with population trajectory stochasticity.
* **WHAT:** Report the posterior predictive root-height distribution (and explicit posterior predictive $p$-values) for the stochastic coalescent SIR model alongside the two existing models, or explicitly caveat that the discrepancy in Figure 6 reflects both birth-death vs. coalescent conditioning and stochastic vs. deterministic dynamics.
* **HOW:** Run `TreeModelAdequacy` on the stochastic coalescent SIR XML configuration from Popinga et al. [27] on the same 100 H1N1 sequences and add the numerical posterior predictive $p$-values to the Figure 6 caption.

#### 2. Discuss Root-Height Fixing and Migration Prior Sensitivity in the `AIM` Cichlid Analysis (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** Fixing the cichlid species tree height to exactly 9.2 Mya (`Fig 5` text) suppresses clock/height uncertainty, and Bayes factors for migration indicators in parameter-rich IM models can be sensitive to the exponential rate-scaler prior (mean 0.05) and the assumption that migration rate scales inversely with coexistence time.
* **WHAT:** Clarify why a point root height was fixed rather than a normal/lognormal prior, and state how the $\text{BF} > 10$ migration arrows behave under alternative rate-scaler priors.
* **HOW:** Add 2–3 sentences in the "Isolation with migration" subsection explaining that fixing the root height was done to calibrate absolute time units, and report the posterior inclusion probabilities corresponding to $\text{BF} > 10$.
