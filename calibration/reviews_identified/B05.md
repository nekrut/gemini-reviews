### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript combines experimental evolution, scarless allelic exchange, biochemical assays, and synthetic mutagenesis in *Methylobacterium extorquens* AM1 to determine how bacterial populations recover from severe fitness defects caused by synonymous recoding of the essential formaldehyde-activating enzyme gene (*fae*). Evolving 8–10 replicate populations (across two effective population size regimes) of six recoded *fae* variants (carrying 46–150 synonymous substitutions across 186 codons) on methylamine for 60–250 generations, the authors find rapid, highly parallel fitness recovery driven by single point mutations localized exclusively to the 5' upstream region or the first 14 codons of *fae* (Fig. 1, Fig. 2). Four of the nine evolved coding mutations are synonymous, and chromosomal reconstruction ("snp" vs. "anc" and "bkg" controls) confirms that these single mutations account for virtually the entire fitness gain (from non-viable to ~75% of wild-type growth rate in AF and AC backgrounds, and >20% gains in others; Fig. 3A, Supplementary Fig. S4). By testing an N-terminal 13-residue FLAG-tag insert (Fig. 4), systematically computing minimum folding energy (MFE) and anti-Shine–Dalgarno (anti-SD) affinity for all possible point mutations in the first 50 coding nucleotides, and experimentally assaying 37 engineered point mutants and 12 cross-allele swaps on inducible plasmids (Figs. 5–6), the authors show that neither predicted 5' MFE nor internal anti-SD affinity predicts mutational fitness effects across allelic contexts.

The experimental design is rigorous: evolving small and large populations in two independent laboratories on different continents rules out cross-contamination as the source of nucleotide-level parallelism, and bidirectional allelic exchange cleanly isolates the causal effect of each evolved SNP from genomic background mutations. The primary methodological limitations are: (1) the 37 engineered point mutants and 12 cross-allele swaps (Figs. 5–6) were assayed on anhydrotetracycline-inducible multicopy plasmids (`pLC291`) rather than at the native chromosomal locus, which alters transcription initiation dynamics and 5' UTR sequence context relative to the evolved chromosomal alleles; (2) for strains AF and AC, transcript, protein, and enzyme activity assays (Fig. 3B–D) had to be performed during a 2-hour methylamine induction pulse in succinate-grown cultures rather than steady-state growth on methylamine, complicating comparisons of small protein increases against large steady-state growth rate rescues; and (3) whole-genome sequencing was not performed on the seven evolved populations (six AR and one AC) that adapted without *fae*-associated mutations. These limitations are transparently discussed or can be addressed through targeted analytical refinements.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Single point mutations—including four synonymous substitutions—in the 5' N-terminal coding region of *fae* repeatedly fix under strong positive selection and rescue severe fitness defects: [Well Supported].**  
  Independent evolution across 8–10 replicate lines per ancestral allele in two separate laboratories, sequencing of founding colonies, and scarless chromosomal allelic replacement in both directions ("snp" in ancestral background and "anc" in evolved background; Fig. 3A, Supplementary Fig. S4A) demonstrate that single synonymous and nonsynonymous point mutations in the first 14 codons confer 20% to >70% growth rate rescues.

* **Appending a frequent-codon N-terminal 13-residue insert rescues the fitness defect across all recoded *fae* variants: [Well Supported].**  
  In Fig. 4, adding the 13-residue N-terminal tag equalizes 5' sequence properties and restores near-WT growth across all recoded alleles on cumate-inducible plasmids (`pHC115`), localizing the primary expression bottleneck of the recoded alleles to the 5' translation initiation / early elongation window.

* **Standard computational predictors of 5' mRNA folding (MFE) and internal Shine–Dalgarno affinity fail to predict the fitness effects of engineered N-terminal point mutations: [Well Supported].**  
  Testing 37 engineered mutations spanning the extremes of predicted $\Delta\text{MFE}$ and $\Delta\text{anti-SD}$ affinity across WT, AF, VA, and AC alleles reveals no correlation between predicted biophysical changes and growth rate ($P > 0.05$, Fig. 5E–J, Supplementary Fig. S9), and this null result holds across multiple temperature settings, dangling-end parameters, and full 16S rRNA tail definitions (Supplementary Figs. S11–S13).

* **Beneficial effects of evolved N-terminal mutations are shaped by intragenic epistasis within the *fae* allele: [Moderately Supported].**  
  When 12 evolved coding mutations were swapped into alternate recoded *fae* alleles (Fig. 6), most failed to match the fitness of the natively evolved mutations (and none of the four AF/VA mutations rescued AC). However, two cross-swaps (*eAC1* in AF and *eAC3* in VA) conferred significantly *higher* fitness than the mutations that actually fixed in AF and VA during experimental evolution, and these assays were performed on `pLC291` plasmids rather than the chromosome. This indicates that while strong intragenic sign epistasis exists (particularly in AC), mutational accessibility or plasmid-versus-chromosomal context also shapes which alleles were observed in evolution.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Account for Plasmid 5' UTR Sequence Context in the MFE and Anti-SD Calculations for Engineered Mutants
* **WHY:** In Fig. 5, MFE calculations use a 100 bp window ($-50$ to $+50$ bp relative to the *fae* start site) and anti-SD calculations use $-5$ to $+50$ bp. While the evolved mutations arose at the native chromosomal *fae* locus under the native upstream intergenic sequence, the 37 engineered mutations in Fig. 5E–J and Fig. 6 were expressed from the anhydrotetracycline-inducible plasmid `pLC291`, which has a different plasmid-derived 5' UTR sequence upstream of the start codon. Base-pairing between the plasmid 5' UTR ($-50$ to $-1$ bp) and the first 50 coding nucleotides can substantially alter the true MFE of the expressed transcript compared to the native chromosomal $-50$ to $+50$ bp window.
* **WHAT:** Recompute the predicted MFE (and ribosome binding site accessibility) of the 37 engineered variants and evolved controls in Fig. 5E–J using the actual `pLC291` plasmid 5' UTR sequence from the transcription start site to $+50$ bp.
* **HOW:** Extract the exact transcribed 5' leader sequence of `pLC291` upstream of the *fae* ATG, run `RNAfold` (and optionally a translation initiation rate calculator such as the Salis RBS Calculator cited in the text, Espah Borujeni et al. 2014) on the chimeric `pLC291`-leader + *fae*(+1 to +50) sequences, and test whether accounting for the plasmid 5' UTR improves the correlation with growth rate in Fig. 5I–J.

#### 2. Quantify Mutation-Rate Biases (Transition vs. Transversion) to Explain Why Accessible Higher-Fitness Variants Did Not Fix in AF and VA
* **WHY:** In Fig. 6, moving *eAC1* into AF and *eAC3* into VA conferred significantly higher growth rates than the mutations that repeatedly fixed in AF (*eAF1*, fixed 8/8 times) and VA (*eVA1–3*). If *eAC1* and *eAC3* represent transversions or low-rate mutational contexts whereas *eAF1* (G$\to$A at bp 40) is a high-rate transition (such as CpG deamination), differences in spontaneous mutation supply ($N_e \mu$) combined with clonal dynamics can explain why a slightly less fit mutation fixed 8/8 times in AF.
* **WHAT:** Compare the nucleotide substitution spectrum (transition vs. transversion, GC$\to$AT bias) of the evolved mutations in Fig. 2A against the higher-fitness engineered/cross-swapped mutations in Figs. 5–6.
* **HOW:** Tabulate the exact nucleotide changes for all 37 engineered/cross-swapped mutations alongside the 15 evolved mutations, classify them by transition/transversion status, and compute whether weighting by the known *M. extorquens* transition/transversion bias explains the fixation of *eAF1* over *eAC1* in AF and *eVA1–3* over *eAC3* in VA.

#### 3. Perform a Power and Detection-Limit Analysis for the Protein and Activity Measurements in Strains AF and AC
* **WHY:** In Fig. 3B–D, several evolved SNP reconstructions in strains AC and AF show massive growth rate rescues (from 0 to ~0.12 $\text{h}^{-1}$) without statistically significant increases in mRNA, FAE protein, or enzyme activity after Benjamini–Hochberg correction ($n = 3\text{--}6$ biological replicates). Because ancestral AF and AC had zero detectable protein (below the Western blot limit of detection), comparing "snp" against a censored zero baseline with $n=3$ replicates has low statistical power under a two-sample $t$-test.
* **WHAT:** Report the Western blot and enzyme assay lower limits of detection (LOD) and quantify the minimum fold-increase in FAE protein supported by the data for *eAF* and *eAC* alleles.
* **HOW:** Use the calibration curve or background noise SD of the anti-FLAG Western blots and NADPH enzyme assays to define the quantitative LOD, and apply a one-sided test against the LOD (or non-parametric/censored regression across all alleles in Fig. 3E–F) to show that the Michaelis–Menten-like saturation curve relating FAE abundance to growth rate explains how a small increase above the detection threshold yields a large fitness jump.
