### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Murrell et al. introduce BUSTED (Branch-Site Unrestricted Statistical Test for Episodic Diversification), a random-effects codon substitution likelihood ratio test implemented in HyPhy and Datamonkey designed to test whether a gene has experienced episodic positive selection ($\omega > 1$) at any subset of sites along either the entire phylogeny or an a priori specified subset of foreground branches. Building on the BS-REL framework, BUSTED models independent three-bin discrete $\omega$ distributions ($\omega_1 \le \omega_2 \le 1 \le \omega_3$, with weights $p_1, p_2, p_3$) on foreground and background branch partitions, testing the null hypothesis $\omega_3^{\text{FG}} = 1$ using a conservative $\chi^2_2$ asymptotic reference distribution. The authors also define a zero-cost exploratory site-level Evidence Ratio ($\text{ER}_s = 2 \log [P(D_s \mid M) / P(D_s \mid M_s)]$) by zeroing out the $\omega_3 > 1$ contribution at site $s$ without re-optimizing global parameters. They benchmark BUSTED across 5,500 simulated alignments from Lu and Guindon (2014) (11 scenarios $\times$ 500 replicates), parametric simulations based on sperm lysin, an empirical HIV-1 reverse transcriptase (RT) drug-resistance dataset (evaluating site-level recovery against Stanford HIVdb), and 10,779 vertebrate gene alignments from Selectome v06 (identifying episodic selection in 24.87% of genes at $P \le 0.05$).

This is a well-formulated, statistically disciplined methodological contribution. Its strengths include modeling unconstrained background positive selection ($\omega_3^{\text{BG}} \ge 1$) so that episodic diversification on background branches does not induce false positives on the foreground partition, explicitly acknowledging the degenerate boundary mixture distribution ($\chi^2_0, \chi^2_1, \chi^2_2$) of the likelihood ratio test when $p_3 \to 0$ or $\omega_2 \to 1$, and calibrating power comparisons against the anticonservative `fitmodel` covarion test using empirical Type I error thresholds (Fig. 2B–C). The primary limitations are: (1) assuming a constant synonymous substitution rate ($dS = 1$) across sites and branches and disallowing instantaneous multi-nucleotide mutations ($\delta(i,j) > 1$), both of which are known in empirical screens like Selectome to inflate $\omega_3$ when $p_3$ is small; (2) evaluating the exploratory site-level $\text{ER}_s$ metric on the HIV-1 RT dataset using an ad hoc $\chi^2_1$ ($P < 0.01$, $\text{ER}_s > 6.635$) cutoff after stating in Methods that $\text{ER}_s$ does not obey asymptotic $\chi^2$ properties; and (3) a minor discrepancy in how the HIV-1 RT True Positive / All Positive counts (13/15 for BUSTED) are tallied from Table 2.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **BUSTED controls gene-wide Type I error rates below nominal $\alpha$ levels while outperforming the Nielsen–Yang branch-site test and calibrated covarion models across diverse simulation regimes:** **[Well Supported].**  
  Across the four null simulation sets in Table 1 ($p_s = p_b = 0$, $N \in \{16, 32, 64\}$) and the sperm lysin null simulations (Fig. 2B), BUSTED maintains empirical false positive rates between 0.006 and 0.020 at $\alpha = 0.05$, whereas `fitmodel` reaches 0.150 (Table 1, 64XX) and 0.230 (Fig. 2B). When foreground branches are specified (`BUSTED FG`), power exceeds the Nielsen–Yang test (`NYbs`) in 4 of 5 power scenarios (e.g., 0.930 vs. 0.754 in `32 B XU`).

* **Allowing unconstrained positive selection on background branches prevents false positives when testing a specific foreground partition:** **[Well Supported].**  
  Unlike fixed-background branch-site formulations that restrict background branches to $\omega \le 1$, BUSTED estimates a separate 3-rate distribution ($\omega_1 \le \omega_2 \le 1 \le \omega_3$) on background branches, ensuring that likelihood gains from constraining $\omega_3^{\text{FG}} = 1$ strictly reflect foreground diversification.

* **Site-level Evidence Ratios ($\text{ER}_s$) recover known HIV-1 RT drug resistance mutations with high precision (13/15 true positives) comparable to specialized directional selection models (MEDS, EDEPS):** **[Moderately Supported].**  
  In Table 2, applying $\text{ER}_s > 6.63$ ($\chi^2_1$ $P < 0.01$) identifies 15 codons in HIV-1 RT, 12 of which are annotated as NRTI/NNRTI resistance or accessory sites in Table 2 (sites 64, 65, 69, 75, 103, 151, 181, 184, 188, 190, 215, 219, 228 — which is actually 13 sites with annotations, and 2 unannotated sites 200 and 245). However, because $\text{ER}_s$ is computed by clamping $\omega_3^{\text{FG}} = 1$ at site $s$ while holding global $p_3^{\text{FG}}$ and $\omega_3^{\text{FG}}$ fixed at their gene-wide MLEs, its scale depends directly on the gene-wide MLE of $\omega_3^{\text{FG}}$ and $p_3^{\text{FG}}$. Using a fixed universal threshold of $6.635$ without conditioning on the gene-wide alternative model parameters or controlling for multiple testing across all codons in RT overstates the calibration of $\text{ER}_s$ as a standalone site-caller.

* **Roughly 24.87% (2,681 / 10,779) of vertebrate genes in Selectome v06 show genuine evidence of episodic diversifying selection:** **[Moderately Supported].**  
  While the screen demonstrates BUSTED's scalability and expected positive correlation with alignment length (Fig. 3A), the model assumes homogeneous synonymous rates across sites and strictly single-nucleotide instantaneous changes ($\delta(i,j) = 1$, Eq. 1). In large comparative genomic databases like Selectome, alignment errors round indels, unmodeled synonymous rate variation (SRV), and multi-nucleotide substitutions frequently get absorbed into a small weight ($p_3 \ll 0.05$) with a huge $\omega_3$ ($\omega_3 \ge 40$, as seen in the saturation plateau of Fig. 3D), inflating empirical detection rates.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Characterize the Null Distribution and Scaling Behavior of Site-Level Evidence Ratios ($\text{ER}_s$)
* **WHY:** In Section "Evidence of Selection at Individual Sites" (line 85), the authors state that $\text{ER}_s = 2 \log[P(D_s \mid M) / P(D_s \mid M_s)]$ cannot be expected to obey asymptotic $\chi^2$ properties. Yet in the HIV-1 RT analysis (line 158), they threshold $\text{ER}_s$ using a $\chi^2_1$ cutoff at $P < 0.01$ ($\text{ER}_s > 6.635$). Note also that $M_s$ sets $\omega_3 = 1$ at site $s$ rather than removing the 3rd rate class entirely, meaning that under the gene-wide alternative model $M$, $P(D_s \mid M) = (1 - p_3) L_s(\omega_1, \omega_2) + p_3 L_s(\omega_3)$, so $\text{ER}_s$ can even be negative at conserved sites where $L_s(\omega_3) < L_s(1)$.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the empirical false positive and true positive rates of the $\text{ER}_s > 6.635$ rule across the Lu and Guindon (2014) simulated alignments where the true positively selected sites are known, and clarify whether $\text{ER}_s$ should only be inspected after the gene-wide LRT rejects the null at $P \le 0.05$.
* **HOW:** Extract $\text{ER}_s$ from the already-executed HyPhy runs for simulation sets `16 B XU`, `32 B XU`, and `64 D XW`, plot an ROC curve of site-level classification using $\text{ER}_s$, and state explicitly in the text that site-level $\text{ER}_s$ filtering is gated on a significant gene-wide BUSTED $P$-value.

#### 2. Quantify the Sensitivity of the Selectome Screen to Boundary $\omega_3$ Outliers ($p_3 \to 0, \omega_3 \ge 40$)
* **WHY:** Figure 3D shows that the Selectome detection rate approaches 100% as $\hat{\omega}_3$ approaches the numerical cap ($\approx 40$). In empirical alignments, a single misaligned codon or multi-nucleotide substitution on a short branch forces $\hat{\omega}_3 \to \infty$ with $\hat{p}_3 \approx 1 / (L \cdot B)$, producing a significant LRT driven by one outlier branch-site cell.
* **WHAT:** Report how many of the 2,681 significant Selectome alignments rely on a vanishingly small weight parameter ($\hat{p}_3 < 0.005$ or supported by only a single site with $\text{ER}_s > 10$).
* **HOW:** Query the 2,681 positive Selectome JSON/HyPhy output files to tabulate the joint distribution of $(\hat{p}_3, \hat{\omega}_3)$ and the number of sites per gene with $\text{ER}_s > 6.635$, reporting the fraction of hits supported by $\ge 2$ sites.

#### 3. Clarify the Exact Mixture Weights for the Conservative $\chi^2_2$ Reference Distribution
* **WHY:** In line 80, the authors state that the asymptotic null distribution is an "analytically intractable mixture of $\chi^2_0, \chi^2_1$, and $\chi^2_2$" and adopt $\chi^2_2$ conservatively. However, when $\hat{\omega}_2 < 1$ strictly in the interior of the parameter space, testing $\omega_3 = 1$ on the boundary $\omega_3 \ge 1$ with nuisance parameter $p_3 \in [0, 1]$ is a standard Davies/Self-Liang boundary problem where $\chi^2_2$ is an upper bound.
* **WHAT:** Compare the conservative $\chi^2_2$ $P$-values against a 20:45:35 mixture of $\chi^2_0 : \chi^2_1 : \chi^2_2$ (as used in related BS-REL / aBSREL implementations) on the Table 1 null simulations (`16 XX`, `32 XX`, `64 XX`).
* **HOW:** Re-evaluate the empirical Type I error rate of the stored LRT statistics from Table 1 under both pure $\chi^2_2$ and the $\chi^2_0 : \chi^2_1 : \chi^2_2$ mixture so readers can see the exact conservatism gap (currently Type I error is 0.006–0.012 at nominal $\alpha = 0.05$).
