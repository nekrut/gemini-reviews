### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Bloom and Neher develop a phylogenetic mutation-counting framework that leverages $\sim 7$ million publicly available SARS-CoV-2 sequences on the UShER mutation-annotated tree (and validates on $\sim 14$ million GISAID sequences) to estimate the amino acid fitness effects of single-nucleotide mutations across all SARS-CoV-2 proteins. By tallying independent branch occurrences of each point mutation within Nextstrain clades ($\ge 10^4$ sequences and $\ge 5,000$ four-fold degenerate mutations) after stringent branch- and site-level artifact filtering, and comparing actual counts ($n_{\text{actual}}$) against clade-specific neutral expected counts ($n_{\text{expected}}$) calibrated from four-fold degenerate synonymous sites, the authors estimate mutational fitness effects as $\Delta f = \ln\left(\frac{n_{\text{actual}} + P}{n_{\text{expected}} + P}\right)$ (with pseudocount $P = 0.5$) and solve a weighted least-squares loss across clades to infer site-specific amino acid fitness profiles. They also provide an analytical population-genetic derivation showing that at a global sampling fraction of $\epsilon \sim 10^{-3}$, the ratio of actual to expected branch occurrences scales inversely with the selection cost $s$ for deleterious mutations with $s \gtrsim \text{a few percent}$. The resulting estimates correlate strongly with experimental deep mutational scanning (DMS) of Spike ($r = 0.66$, approaching the $r = 0.72$ correlation between independent Spike DMS platforms) and $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$ ($r = 0.48–0.53$), outperform site-level FEL $dN/dS$ and machine-learning/epistatic predictors (EVE, DCA, EpiScores), demonstrate strong purifying selection across structural and non-structural proteins alongside near-neutrality of stop codons in accessory proteins ORF6, ORF7a, and ORF8, and capture epistatic shifts in Spike across variants (such as S373P entrenchment in Omicron). Complete code (`snakemake`), data tables, and interactive `altair` visualizations for all proteins are openly available on GitHub.

This is an exemplary quantitative evolutionary biology contribution. The mathematical model is transparent and well-suited to ultra-dense viral phylogenies, the quality-control filters directly address known UShER/GISAID consensus and reversion artifacts, the analytical derivations in the Methods and Appendix ground the heuristic log-ratio estimator in branching-process theory, and the claims are scrupulously bounded by explicit discussion of caveats. Only minor improvements—incorporating local nucleotide sequence context into the expected mutation model, reporting confidence intervals around individual mutation estimates, and fixing missing mathematical symbols in the typeset Methods—are needed.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Comparing observed independent branch counts to clade-specific four-fold degenerate expectations accurately estimates mutational fitness costs ($s \gtrsim \text{a few percent}$) across the SARS-CoV-2 proteome: [Well Supported].**  
  Counting independent mutation occurrences along UShER branches rather than tip frequencies eliminates phylogenetic hitchhiking and founder effects, while per-clade calibration at four-fold degenerate sites accounts for evolutionary shifts in the 12-component SARS-CoV-2 mutation spectrum (such as reduced $G \to U$ transversions in Omicron). The analytical branching-process derivation (Section 4.10) proves why dense but incomplete sampling ($\epsilon \approx 10^{-3}$) makes branch occurrence counts sensitive to transmission fitness costs of a few percent per generation, and replication across the 14-million-sequence GISAID tree (`Supplementary Fig. S1`) and independent geographic subsets (USA vs. England, $r = 0.87$; Figure 2B) confirms high precision.

* **Phylogenetic mutation-effect estimates correlate with Spike and $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$ deep mutational scanning nearly as well as independent DMS experiments correlate with one another, and outperform $dN/dS$, DCA, EVE, and EpiScores: [Well Supported].**  
  Across mutations with $\ge 20$ expected counts, the sequence-based estimates achieve $r = 0.66$ against both full-spike pseudovirus DMS and RBD yeast-display DMS (compared to $r = 0.72$ between the two DMS platforms; Figure 4A), and correctly identify biological constraints missed by yeast $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$ assays (such as polyprotein truncation at Q306\* and inter-subunit interface residue K61N; Figure 4B). Unlike site-level FEL $dN/dS$ (`Supplementary Fig. S5`), which confuses convergent adaptive sweeps (e.g., Spike N501Y) with low functional constraint, the actual-vs.-expected count ratio resolves residue-specific amino acid preferences.

* **Structural and non-structural proteins are under strong purifying selection, whereas accessory proteins (except ORF3a) tolerate amino acid and premature stop-codon mutations with minimal fitness cost: [Well Supported].**  
  The gene-stratified distributions (Figure 3B and `Supplementary Fig. S3–S4`) clearly separate essential replication/structural proteins (where nonsense mutations have median $\Delta f \approx -4$) from accessory genes ORF6, ORF7a, and ORF8 (where nonsense and missense distributions overlap synonymous mutations near $\Delta f \approx 0$), aligning with independent reverse-genetics deletion experiments and natural human transmission of ORF8-knockout lineages.

* **Cross-clade divergence in mutational fitness estimates is concentrated in Spike and reflects genuine epistatic shifts and changing immune pressure rather than statistical noise: [Well Supported].**  
  In Figure 2C–D, increasing the minimum expected-count threshold drives cross-geography (USA vs. England) and non-spike cross-clade correlations toward $r > 0.9$, whereas cross-clade correlations for Spike plateau at a lower level that decays monotonically with pairwise Spike sequence divergence, driven by RBD antibody-escape sites (e.g., K444N, G446S) and epistatically entrenched substitutions (S373P; `Supplementary Fig. S8`).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Quantify the Impact of Local Trinucleotide Sequence Context on Expected Mutation Counts
* **WHY:** As noted in the Discussion (line 146), expected counts are currently computed solely from the 12 single-nucleotide substitution rates ($x \to y$) per clade, assuming uniform rates across all four-fold degenerate sites with parental base $x$. However, APOBEC-driven $C \to U$ deamination in SARS-CoV-2 is strongly elevated in `5'-[U|A]C` dinucleotide contexts; ignoring local flanking context causes neutral $C \to U$ mutations in `UC` motifs to have actual counts above the clade mean and those in `GC/CC` motifs to fall below the clade mean.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate a context-aware expected count model (conditioning on the $5'$ and $3'$ flanking bases at four-fold degenerate sites, or at least the $5'$ base for $C \to U$ mutations) and compare its synonymous residual variance and DMS correlation against the 12-rate baseline model.
* **HOW:** Stratify the ~2,000–4,000 four-fold degenerate sites per clade by their $5'$ nucleotide ($4 \times 12 = 48$ rates) in `expected_counts.py`, re-compute $\Delta f$, and check whether the spread of synonymous mutation effects around zero in Figure 3A tightens.

#### 2. Report Poisson / Negative-Binomial Credible Intervals Alongside Point Estimates of $\Delta f$
* **WHY:** Currently, sampling uncertainty is handled by filtering out mutations with $n_{\text{expected}} < 10$ (or $20$) and tracking $N_x$ counts, but a strongly deleterious mutation with $n_{\text{expected}} = 50$ and $n_{\text{actual}} = 0$ ($\Delta f = \ln(0.5/50.5) = -4.62$) has much greater relative variance in its numerator than a neutral mutation with $n_{\text{actual}} = 50$.
* **WHAT:** Provide analytical 95% confidence/credible intervals for each mutation's $\Delta f$ in `aamut_fitness_all.csv` and in the interactive `altair` tooltips.
* **HOW:** Model $n_{\text{actual}} \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda)$ (or Negative Binomial with overdispersion estimated from four-fold degenerate sites) with a Gamma(0.5, 0) Jeffreys prior, compute the exact 2.5th and 97.5th posterior percentiles of $\ln(\lambda / n_{\text{expected}})$, and add `delta_f_ci_lower` and `delta_f_ci_upper` columns to the output CSVs.
