### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

McHugh et al. report the baseline outcomes of `BARCODE1` (NCT03857477), a prospective, single-arm UK primary-care screening study evaluating a 130-SNP germline polygenic risk score (PRS) to target multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) and transperineal prostate biopsy in European-ancestry individuals assigned male at birth aged 55–69 years. Of 40,292 invited individuals across 69 GP practices, 8,953 (22.2%) expressed interest, 6,393 passed genotyping quality control, and 745 (11.7%) fell at or above the 90th PRS centile (calibrated against the `ProtecT` reference cohort). Of those 745, 468 (62.8%) completed PSA testing, mpMRI (467/468), and 12-core transperineal biopsy irrespective of PSA level or MRI outcome. Prostate cancer (PrCa) was diagnosed in 187/468 participants (40.0% PPV): 84 (44.9%) were NCCN Very Low/Low risk (Gleason 6), 63 (33.7%) were Intermediate Favorable, and 40 (21.4%) were Intermediate Unfavorable/High/Very High risk (total 103/187 [55.1%] Gleason $\ge 3+4$ clinically significant PrCa [csPrCa]). Because 118/187 cancers had $\text{PSA} \le 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$ and 125/186 had negative MRI ($\text{PI-RADS} \le 2$), the standard combined diagnostic triage ($\text{PSA} > 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$ plus $\text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$) would have detected only 30/186 cancers (28/102 csPrCa), missing 74/102 (72.5%, or 73/103 [70.9%] including the 1 patient unable to tolerate MRI) of Gleason $\ge 7$ cases while avoiding 82/84 (97.6%) of Gleason 6 diagnoses. The trial protocol and statistical analysis plan are linked at `NEJM.org`.

This prospective trial provides valuable empirical data because performing systematic transperineal biopsy on all 468 high-PRS participants regardless of PSA or PI-RADS score eliminates the verification bias that plagues standard PSA- or MRI-triggered biopsy studies. At the same time, the single-arm design (biopsying only men with $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile, with no control arm of men with $\text{PRS} < 90\text{th}$ centile undergoing systematic biopsy regardless of PSA/MRI) means the study cannot separate the true PRS enrichment effect from the high baseline prevalence of latent/histological Gleason 6 and low-volume Gleason $3+4$ prostate cancer uncovered whenever 55–73-year-old men undergo routine 12-core biopsy. Additionally, there are internal discrepancies in how the "standard UK diagnostic pathway" is defined and applied across the Abstract, Results, Table 2, and Discussion (yielding conflicting "missed cancer" percentages of 70.9% vs. 42.5% and "avoided insignificant cancer" percentages of 97.6% vs. 26.7%), as well as a major misstatement of the ERSPC trial mortality reduction in the Introduction ("30 percentage points" instead of a ~20–30% relative risk reduction). Clarifying the single-arm counterfactual baseline and reconciling the numerical discrepancies across sections will substantially improve the paper.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Biopsying men aged 55–69 (median 64 at biopsy) with $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile yields a 40.0% overall PrCa detection rate (187/468) and 22.0% csPrCa rate (103/468 Gleason $\ge 3+4$), with low biopsy morbidity: [Well Supported].**  
  Because all 468 biopsied men underwent uniform PSA, mpMRI (467/468), and 12-core transperineal biopsy (plus targeted cores for $\text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$), the within-arm detection rates, positive predictive values (`Table 1`, `Table 2`), and low complication rates (0.2% sepsis, 0.4% UTI, 0.2% catheterization; `Table S12`) are directly observed without PSA/MRI verification bias. However, because `BARCODE1` did not biopsy a random control sample of men with $\text{PRS} < 90\text{th}$ centile, comparing the 40.0% detection rate to historical PSA-triggered trials like ERSPC (where men had elevated PSA and underwent older transrectal sextant biopsies) does not isolate the marginal lift of PRS over unselective systematic biopsy in 64-year-old men.

* **70.9% (73/103) of intermediate/high-risk cancers (or 42.5% [17/40] of Intermediate Unfavorable/High/Very High risk cancers) would have been missed by the standard UK diagnostic pathway, while 26.7% (27) of clinically insignificant cancers would have been avoided: [Fragile].**  
  There is an unresolved internal contradiction in how the "standard UK diagnostic pathway" is defined and calculated across the manuscript:
  1. In `Table 2` ("PSA + MRI" column, requiring *both* $\text{PSA} > 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$ *and* $\text{MRI PI-RADS} \ge 3$), 30 cancers are detected: 2 Low/Very Low (missing 82/84 = 97.6%), 6 Intermediate Favorable (missing 57/63 = 90.5%), 14 Intermediate Unfavorable (missing 14/28 = 50.0%), and 8 High/Very High (missing 3/11 evaluated = 27.3%; 1 High/Very High patient could not tolerate MRI). Summing the three $\ge \text{Intermediate}$ rows gives $6 + 14 + 8 = 28$ detected and $57 + 14 + 3 = 74$ missed out of 102 MRI-evaluated csPrCa (or $103 - 30 = 73$ if subtracting total detected from 103, which improperly includes the 2 Low-risk cases in the 30!). Indeed, $73 / 103 = 70.9\%$ (reported in the Abstract) appears to have been calculated by taking $(103 \text{ csPrCa} - 30 \text{ total cancers of all grades detected by PSA+MRI}) / 103 = 73/103$, conflating total cancers ($N=30$, which includes 2 Low/Very Low risk cancers) with csPrCa ($N=28$)!
  2. In the "Overdiagnosis" section (line 333) and Discussion (line 345), the authors state: "Had we screened the 187 participants... with the standard UK PrCa screening method, we would have avoided detecting 27 (26.7%) of participants with clinically insignificant cancer." Yet in `Table 2`, there are **84** Low/Very Low (Gleason 6) cancers, of which **82** (97.6%) were missed (avoided) by `PSA > 3 + MRI`, **67** (79.8%) by `PSA > 3` alone, and **68** (81.0%) by `MRI` alone. Neither the numerator (`27`) nor the denominator (`27 / 0.267 = 101`?) matches the 84 Gleason 6 cancers in `Table 2`. Furthermore, in the Discussion (line 345), the UK NICE pathway is defined with *age-specific* PSA thresholds ($>3.5\,\mu\text{g/L}$ for 50–60 yrs; $>4.5\,\mu\text{g/L}$ for 60–70 yrs), whereas `Table 2` uses a flat $\text{PSA} > 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$.

* **Systematic biopsy of MRI-negative ($\text{PI-RADS} \le 2$), low-PSA ($\le 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$) men in the top PRS decile detects clinically significant disease with acceptable overdiagnosis (20.8%): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Among the 370 men with negative MRI ($\text{PI-RADS} \le 2$), biopsy detected 125 cancers, of which 68 (54.4%) were Gleason 6 and 57 (45.6%) were Gleason $\ge 7$ (`Table 1`). However, as `Table 2` and `Figure 2` show, the vast majority of the Gleason $\ge 7$ cancers missed by `PSA > 3 + MRI` were **Intermediate Favorable** (57 of 74 missed csPrCa, i.e., Gleason $3+4$ with $\le 10\%$ pattern 4 and low PSA), which frequently qualify for active surveillance rather than immediate radical treatment. Moreover, the overdiagnosis model (`Methods`, line 228; `Tables S8–S10`) relies on literature mean sojourn time (MST) parameters derived from PSA-screened cohorts; because MRI-invisible, low-PSA ($\text{median } 1.8\,\mu\text{g/L}$) microscopic tumors discovered by random systematic biopsy have a longer lead time before clinical presentation than PSA-elevated or MRI-visible lesions, applying standard PSA-based MSTs underestimates the true overdiagnosis rate in the MRI-negative/low-PSA subgroup.

* **Within the top decile ($\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile), PSA and PI-RADS strongly predict csPrCa ($\text{AUC} = 0.78$), whereas further PRS centile stratification (90th vs. 99th) adds minimal discrimination: [Well Supported].**  
  Once restricted to the top 10% tail of the polygenic distribution (`Table 1`: median PRS centile 95 in non-cancer vs. 95 in any cancer and 96 in csPrCa), variation in PRS is narrow, whereas PSA ($\text{median } 1.4 \text{ vs. } 3.1\,\mu\text{g/L}$) and $\text{PI-RADS} \ge 4$ ($4.3\% \text{ vs. } 35.3\%$) provide strong discrimination (`AUC = 0.78`, `Table S7`).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Fix the Arithmetic Error in the Abstract's Headline "73 (70.9%) Missed" Claim and Reconcile the "27 (26.7%)" Insignificant Cancer Count
* **WHY:** In `Table 2`, under `PSA (>3) + MRI`, 30 *total* cancers are detected: 2 Low/Very Low, 6 Intermediate Favorable, 14 Intermediate Unfavorable, and 8 High/Very High. Thus, only $6 + 14 + 8 = 28$ Intermediate/High-risk cancers are detected, and $57 + 14 + 3 = 74$ out of 102 MRI-evaluated Intermediate/High-risk cancers are missed ($74/102 = 72.5\%$, or $75/103 = 72.8\%$ if counting the 1 High-risk patient who could not tolerate MRI). Subtracting 30 *total* cancers (which includes 2 Low-risk Gleason 6 tumors) from 103 *Intermediate/High-risk* cancers to get $103 - 30 = 73$ ($70.9\%$) in the Abstract is a category-mixing arithmetic error. Likewise, the statement on lines 333 and 345 that standard screening "would have avoided detecting 27 (26.7%) of participants with clinically insignificant cancer" contradicts `Table 2`, where 82 of 84 (97.6%) Low/Very Low risk cancers are avoided by `PSA (>3) + MRI`.
* **WHAT:** Correct the numerator and denominator of missed Intermediate/High-risk cancers in the Abstract and main text, and explicitly define the numerator (`27`) and denominator (`101`?) behind the `26.7%` figure on lines 333 and 345.
* **HOW:**
  1. In the Abstract (lines 178 & 183), update `73 (70.9%)` to `74 of 102 MRI-evaluated (72.5%) [or 75 of 103 total (72.8%)]` intermediate/high-risk cancers missed by requiring both $\text{PSA} > 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$ and $\text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$.
  2. In "Overdiagnosis" (line 333) and "Discussion" (line 345), clarify whether the `27 (26.7%)` figure refers to age-specific NICE PSA thresholds + MRI (and why the denominator is 101 instead of the 84 Gleason 6 cancers in `Table 2`), and report the corresponding number from `Table 2` (82 of 84 [97.6%] Gleason 6 cancers avoided).

#### 2. Stratify the Overdiagnosis and Benefit-Harm Analysis by MRI/PSA Status and NCCN Subclass
* **WHY:** Performing transperineal biopsy on the 370 men with negative MRI ($\text{PI-RADS} \le 2$) detected 68 Gleason 6 cancers and 57 Gleason $\ge 7$ cancers, but most of those 57 were low-volume Intermediate Favorable (Gleason $3+4$ with $\le 10\%$ pattern 4). Applying the same mean sojourn time (MST) distribution to MRI-invisible, $\text{PSA} < 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$ micro-foci as to PSA-elevated/MRI-visible tumors understates overdiagnosis in the biopsy-all strategy.
* **WHAT:** Report a 2×2 benefit-harm decision table comparing three biopsy triggers among men with $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile: (A) Biopsy all, (B) Biopsy if $\text{PSA} > 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$ *or* $\text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$, and (C) Biopsy only if $\text{PSA} > 3.0\,\mu\text{g/L}$ *and* $\text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$, broken down by biopsies performed, Gleason 6 detected, Intermediate Favorable detected, and Intermediate Unfavorable/High/Very High detected.
* **HOW:** Using the data already in `Results` (line 297 notes that 100/187 cancers had *either* $\text{PSA} > 3.0$ *or* $\text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$), add a column for `PSA (>3) OR MRI (PI-RADS >=3)` to `Table 2`, and run a sensitivity analysis in `Table S8` increasing the MST of MRI-negative/$\text{PSA} \le 3$ screen-detected cancers by 1.5–2.0×.

#### 3. Correct the Misstatement of ERSPC Mortality Reduction and Other Editorial Typos
* **WHY:** Line 192 states that "the ERSPC showed that after 22 years, mortality was approximately 30 percentage points higher in the control group compared with men who were offered screening through biennial PSA testing." Prostate cancer cumulative mortality in ERSPC is ~1–2%, not >30%; the ~20–30% difference in ERSPC is a **relative risk reduction (percent)**, not "percentage points".
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Introduction (line 192):** Change "approximately 30 percentage points higher" to "approximately 20 to 30% higher (in relative terms)".
  2. **Methods (line 236):** Clarify why $745 / 6,393 = 11.7\%$ of genotyped BARCODE1 participants exceeded the `ProtecT` 90th centile cutoff (e.g., whether self-selection of men with a 20.9% family history rate shifted the PRS distribution upward by $+1.7\%$ relative to `ProtecT`, and report the exact $p$-value of a binomial test against $10.0\%$: $p = 1.2 \times 10^{-5}$).
  3. **Results (line 295):** "negative results (PI-RADS < 2) on MRI" $\rightarrow$ change `< 2` to `≤ 2` (since PI-RADS 1 and 2 are negative, totaling $7 + 1 + 238 + 124 = 370$ participants in `Table 1`).
  4. **Results (lines 313 & 315):** Remove the stray characters in "PI-RADS score re were" (`re`) and "PI-RADS score m." (`m`).
