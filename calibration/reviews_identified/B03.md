### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

#### Executive Summary
This manuscript presents version 7 of the MAFFT multiple sequence alignment (MSA) suite, focusing on algorithms for extending existing alignments (`--add`, `--addfragments`, `--addprofile`), automatic reverse-complement strand detection (`--adjustdirection`), multithreaded execution (`--thread`), and structure-seeded alignments combining ASH and MAFFT (`--seed`). The authors evaluate these capabilities across three empirical test cases: (1) aligning ~5,000 non-overlapping fungal ITS1 and ITS2 pyrosequencing reads onto ~300 full-length 18S–ITS1–5.8S–ITS2–28S sequences, (2) scaling fragment placement on the Gutell CRW `16S.B.ALL` dataset (up to 13,822 backbone sequences and 138,210 fragments), and (3) resolving active-site aspartic acid homology across three highly divergent PIN-domain structures (`3v33`, `2qip`, `1taq`).

The paper is notable for its candor regarding failure modes: the authors explicitly demonstrate where standard progressive (`FFT-NS-2`), iterative (`L-INS-i`), and profile-alignment (`mafft-profile`) strategies fail on non-overlapping fragments due to non-additive guide-tree distances, and acknowledge that default BLOSUM62/200PAM scoring matrices can underperform on closely related sequences. The primary limitations are the absence of external baseline aligners (e.g., PaPaRa, PAGAN, HMMER `hmmalign`, or SEPP) in Table 2, the single-example anecdotal nature of the PIN-domain structural alignment (Test Case 3), and the use of an unarchived personal-communication dataset for Test Case 1.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

#### Detailed Rubric Evaluation

* **Claim 1 — The `--addfragments` option accurately aligns non-overlapping fragmentary sequences (fungal ITS1/ITS2 and bacterial 16S rRNA) to a full-length backbone alignment while avoiding the catastrophic misalignment of `mafft-profile` and `L-INS-i`: [Well Supported].**  
  In Table 2 (`16S.B.ALL`), `--multipair --addfragments` and `--6merpair --addfragments` achieve 0.9969 and 0.9949 SP-score accuracy against the reference CRW alignment (vs. 0.2779 for sequential profile alignment), scaling to 138,210 fragments in 5.91 hours (10 threads). In Test Case 1 (Figure 2), the authors clearly explain the mathematical mechanism: non-overlapping ITS1 and ITS2 fragments violate distance additivity ($d(\text{ITS1}, \text{full}) \approx 0$ and $d(\text{ITS2}, \text{full}) \approx 0$, whereas $d(\text{ITS1}, \text{ITS2})$ is large), which corrupts standard guide trees and pairwise consistency scores unless fragment-to-fragment relationships are ignored.

* **Claim 2 — Parallelization via `--thread` provides high wall-clock efficiency for `--addfragments` and deterministic concordance for progressive alignment, though iterative refinement (`-i`) becomes non-deterministic across threads: [Well Supported].**  
  Table 2 documents a 8.8-fold wall-clock speedup on 10 cores for `--multipair --addfragments` (6.67 CPU days to 18.3 hours) and a 6.2-fold speedup for `--6merpair --addfragments` (3.76 CPU hours to 36.2 minutes). The authors transparently note that multithreaded iterative refinement is non-deterministic across runs and cite Katoh and Toh (2010) for accuracy equivalence.

* **Claim 3 — Integrating ASH structural equivalence matrices ($e_{ij} = \exp(-d_{ij}^2 / d_0^2)$ with $d_0 = 4\text{ \AA}$, threshold $\ge 0.5$) via `--seed` resolves active-site misalignments in divergent proteins: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Figure 4 convincingly illustrates that `MAFFT-L-INS-i` misaligns the central $\text{Mg}^{2+}$-coordinating aspartic acid across human regnase-1 (`3v33`), *Vibrio parahaemolyticus* VPA0982 (`2qip`), and *Thermus aquaticus* Taq polymerase (`1taq`), whereas seeding with ASH pairwise structural alignments restores the catalytic triad. However, this is demonstrated on a single 3-sequence example without quantitative benchmarking across standard structural alignment suites (e.g., BAliBASE, Mattbench, or HOMSTRAD) or comparison against 3DCoffee and PROMALS3D.

* **Claim 4 — Default scoring matrices (BLOSUM62 and 200PAM) work well across general divergence regimes, although JTT 1PAM with weaker gap penalties improves accuracy on simulated closely related sequences: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In the "Parameters" section (lines 125–128), the authors mention that switching from BLOSUM62 to JTT 1PAM improved INDELiBLE simulation benchmark scores on closely related sequences, but provide no numerical table or figure quantifying the magnitude of this improvement or the exact gap penalty values tested.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### Major Issues, Minor Issues & Concrete Recommendations

#### 1. Include External Fragment-Alignment Baselines in Table 2 (Major Issue)
* **WHY:** Table 2 compares five internal MAFFT options on the `16S.B.ALL` dataset from Mirarab et al. (2012), but omits the external tools discussed in the text (e.g., PaPaRa, PAGAN, SEPP, or `hmmalign`) on the exact same hardware and CRW reference split.
* **WHAT:** Add accuracy and runtime rows for at least one established fragment-insertion baseline (such as HMMER `hmmalign` or SEPP as reported by Mirarab et al., 2012) to Table 2.
* **HOW:** Report the published SP-error/accuracy of SEPP/HMMER on `16S.B.ALL` Case 1 alongside the MAFFT v7 `--addfragments` numbers so readers can directly gauge relative accuracy and runtime.

#### 2. Archive the Fungal ITS Benchmark Dataset and Quantify ITS Alignment Accuracy (Major Issue)
* **WHY:** Test Case 1 (Figure 2) relies on an environmental 454 pyrosequencing dataset attributed to "Chen W, personal communication" (line 71) and evaluates alignment quality purely by visual inspection in Jalview (Figure 2A–E).
* **WHAT:** Deposit the anonymized ITS1/ITS2 + full-length rDNA FASTA files on the MAFFT benchmark website or Dryad/Zenodo, and report a quantitative accuracy metric (e.g., fraction of ITS1 and ITS2 reads placed wholly within the respective canonical ITS1 and ITS2 column intervals of the 300 full-length sequences).
* **HOW:** Compute the column-range overlap for the 5,000 fragments against the 5.8S rRNA boundary in the 300 full-length sequences for `FFT-NS-2`, `L-INS-i`, `mafft-profile`, and `--addfragments`, and provide a URL to the input FASTA files.

#### 3. Quantify the Sensitivity to ASH Equivalence Thresholds and Scoring Matrices (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** In Test Case 3 (line 152), an ASH residue equivalence cutoff of $e_{ij} \ge 0.5$ ($d_0 = 4\text{ \AA}$) is chosen without justification, and in the Parameters section (line 127), the INDELiBLE JTT 1PAM improvement is stated qualitatively without numbers.
* **WHAT:** State the sensitivity of the `--seed` alignment to the $e_{ij}$ cutoff (e.g., 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) and report the exact SP/TC scores for BLOSUM62 vs. JTT 1PAM on the INDELiBLE benchmark.
* **HOW:** Add a brief parenthetical or supplementary note listing the exact INDELiBLE accuracy values and `--op` / `--ep` gap parameters.
