### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Obermeyer et al. introduce PyR0, a hierarchical Bayesian multinomial logistic regression framework implemented in Pyro using stochastic variational inference (SVI) to estimate the relative growth rates of 3,000 refined SARS-CoV-2 genetic clusters (derived from 1,544 PANGO lineages) across 1,560 geographic regions in 14-day windows across 6,466,300 GISAID genomes collected through 20 January 2022. By modeling cluster log-relative fitness ($\Delta \log R$, scaled assuming a fixed 5.5-day generation time) as a linear-additive combination of 2,904 common nonsynonymous amino acid substitutions, PyR0 pools statistical evidence across convergent emergences of the same mutation in distinct backgrounds. The authors validate the model through spatial cross-validation, leave-one-lineage-out mutation-only predictions ($R = 0.983$ after adjusting for parent fitness), temporal backtesting of Variant of Concern (VoC) emergence, lentiviral pseudovirus single-cycle infectivity assays in HEK293T-ACE2-TMPRSS2 cells, and correlation of RBD mutational fitness contributions against deep mutational scanning (DMS) antibody-escape scores. They report that BA.2 exhibits an 8.9-fold (95% CI: 8.6–9.2) relative fitness increase over the ancestral Wuhan (A) lineage, identify mutational hotspots in S, N (residues 44–180 and the SR-rich linker), and ORF1ab (nsp4, nsp6, nsp12–14), and show that late-2021 fitness jumps were overwhelmingly driven by RBD immune-escape mutations rather than intrinsic cell infectivity.

PyR0 represents a major computational advance in scaling multinomial growth rate regression to millions of viral genomes while exploiting homoplasy/convergent evolution to regularize early lineage fitness estimates. However, three methodological and biological issues temper several quantitative claims: (1) translating logistic growth rate differences ($\Delta r$) into fold-changes in basic or effective reproduction number ($R / R_A = 8.9$ for BA.2) under a fixed 5.5-day generation time conflates intrinsic transmissibility, immune evasion in a partially immune population, and shortened serial intervals; (2) mean-field or low-rank SVI in a colinear 75-million-parameter space systematically underestimates marginal posterior variance, inflating $z$-score significance rankings ($\text{mean}/\text{SD}$) for tightly linked constellation mutations (such as the Omicron BA.1/BA.2 trunk mutations dominating Table 1); and (3) it is unclear whether the historical backtests (lines 22–23, figs. S8–S10) censored sequences by GISAID `submission_date` rather than `collection_date`, which is essential to avoid lookahead bias from reporting delays. These issues can be resolved through computational sensitivity analyses and more precise epidemiological framing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Hierarchical multinomial logistic regression sharing strength across convergent mutations accelerates early detection of emerging lineages relative to count-only models:** **[Well Supported].**  
  Leave-one-out lineage cross-validation (fig. S17) and temporal accumulation comparisons against unregularized binomial and mutation-free multinomial models (figs. S11–S14) confirm that mutational priors stabilize growth rate estimates when a new lineage has fewer than 100 observations.

* **Late-2021 SARS-CoV-2 fitness increases were primarily driven by Spike RBD immune escape rather than increased intrinsic single-cycle infectivity:** **[Well Supported].**  
  Pseudovirus infectivity assays in HEK293T-ACE2-TMPRSS2 cells (Fig. 3A) show that top-ranked RBD substitutions on a D614G background do not systematically increase target-cell entry, whereas cluster-level RBD $\Delta \log R$ correlates strongly with DMS-derived polyclonal antibody escape scores (Fig. 3B) and spikes sharply upon Omicron emergence in November 2021 (Fig. 3C–D).

* **Individual top-ranked substitutions in Table 1 (e.g., S:S371L, S:L981F, ORF1a:K856R) are independently associated with ~1.04-fold fitness increases based on posterior mean/SD rankings:** **[Fragile].**  
  Inspecting Table 1 reveals that nearly every top-20 substitution (H655Y, T95I, P3395H, N764K, K856R, S371L, E:T9I, Q954H, ORF9b:P10S, L981F, N:P13L, G339D, S375F, N679K, S373P, M:Q19E, D796Y, N969K, T547K) is a defining mutation of the Omicron BA.1/BA.2 stem, and several (ORF1a:K856R, S:L981F, S:S371L, S:T547K) emerged in only 2 to 3 PANGO lineages. Because Omicron entered with ~30 co-occurring substitutions and experienced massive exponential growth, linear regression cannot uniquely partition the trunk growth effect among co-segregating Omicron-specific sites without strong prior shrinkage, and variational inference underestimates the resulting posterior covariance, yielding nearly identical point estimates (1.038–1.041) and artificially narrow standard deviations across the entire haplotype block.

* **Omicron BA.2 has an 8.9-fold (95% CI: 8.6–9.2) higher relative fitness ($R / R_A$) than the ancestral Wuhan A lineage:** **[Overstated].**  
  In a multinomial logistic growth model, the slope estimates the selection coefficient $\Delta r$ (difference in exponential growth rates per unit time) between co-circulating lineages within a region-time bin. Exponentiating $\Delta r \cdot \tau$ (with a fixed generation time $\tau = 5.5$ days) and chaining these ratios across two years of shifting population immunity (vaccination and prior infection) and shortening generation times (~3.0–3.5 days for Omicron) does not estimate a time-invariant ratio of reproduction numbers $R / R_A$. Furthermore, the authors note in line 18 (fig. S3) that the PANGO lineage refinement process itself introduces an upward secular drift in inferred fitness over time.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Disentangle Haplotype Colinearity and Variational Variance Underestimation in Table 1
* **WHY:** In Table 1, 19 of the 20 highest-ranked substitutions belong to the Omicron BA.1/BA.2 defining constellation, and 15 of them have identical or near-identical estimated fold increases ($1.039 - 1.040$) despite appearing in only 2–5 PANGO lineages (e.g., `ORF1a:K856R` in 2 lineages, `S:L981F` in 2 lineages). When $K$ mutations co-occur almost exclusively on the same high-fitness trunk, an $L_2$ or Laplace-regularized linear model splits the trunk effect roughly equally ($\Delta \log R_{\text{trunk}} / K$), and variational approximations that neglect posterior off-diagonal covariance across the 2,904 mutations overstate marginal significance ($|\mu_i| / \sigma_i$).
* **WHAT:** Filter or stratify Table 1 by requiring a minimum number of independent phylogenetic emergences (e.g., $\ge 10$ independent lineages or homoplasic branches) before declaring single-residue significance, and report the posterior correlation matrix among the top 30 mutations.
* **HOW:** Using the fitted Pyro guide distribution, extract the variational covariance (or run Laplace approximation / HMC on the collapsed 3,000-cluster $\times$ 2,904-mutation design matrix, which is only $3000 \times 2904$ at the cluster-regression level once cluster growth rates are summarized) to compute exact marginal credible intervals that account for colinearity among Omicron stem mutations.

#### 2. Audit and Enforce Submission-Date Filtering in Historical Backtesting
* **WHY:** Lines 22–23 claim that PyR0 would have identified BA.2 by mid-December 2021 (from 76 sequences) and B.1.1.7 by late November 2020. In GISAID, sequences collected in mid-December are frequently deposited weeks later; furthermore, defining the 3,000 clusters on the full January 2022 dataset before running historical backtests introduces lookahead leakage in the clustering step.
* **WHAT:** Verify whether figs. S8–S10 filtered GISAID records strictly by `submission_date` (deposition date) rather than `collection_date`, and whether the 3,000 sub-lineage clusters were re-derived de novo at each historical cutoff date.
* **HOW:** Re-run the November 2020, May 2021, and December 2021 backtests censoring all sequences with `submission_date` after the evaluation timestamp and running the hierarchical clustering step exclusively on the sequences available on that date. Report the resulting detection dates alongside the collection-date curves.

#### 3. Correct the $R / R_A$ Scaling for Generation Time Contraction and Lineage Splitting Bias
* **WHY:** Converting growth rate differences $\Delta r$ into $R / R_A = \exp(\Delta r \cdot \tau)$ assumes (i) a constant generation time $\tau = 5.5$ days across all variants from 2019 to 2022 and (ii) no cumulative secular drift from the 3,000-cluster splitting algorithm (acknowledged in line 18 and fig. S3).
* **WHAT:** Report the raw selection coefficient $\Delta r$ ($\text{day}^{-1}$) as the primary metric in Fig. 1 and Table 1, subtract the null simulation lineage-splitting drift (fig. S3), and provide a sensitivity band for $R / R_A$ using variant-appropriate generation times (e.g., $\tau = 3.5$ days for Omicron vs. $5.5$ days for Wuhan-Hu-1).
* **HOW:** Re-scale the inset table of Fig. 1 across $\tau \in [3.0, 5.5]$ days and show the drift-adjusted relative growth rate alongside the unadjusted estimate.
