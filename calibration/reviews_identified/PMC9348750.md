### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Worobey et al. test whether the Huanan Seafood Wholesale Market in Wuhan was the geographic epicenter of the early COVID-19 pandemic by integrating extracted residential coordinates of 155 December 2019 COVID-19 cases from the 2021 WHO-China mission report, 737 January–February 2020 geotagged Weibo COVID-19 help-seeker records, age-matched WorldPop population density baselines, 2013–2014 Sina Visitor System mobility check-ins, photographic/survey records of live mammal sales through November 2019, and 585 China CDC environmental swabs collected inside the market starting January 1, 2020. Using kernel density estimation (KDE), median and center-point Haversine distance permutation tests, spatial relative risk (`sparr`) tolerance contours, and binomial generalized linear modeling (GLM), the authors show that December 2019 cases—including 120 cases with no reported epidemiological link to the market—were tightly centered on the Huanan market (center-point 0.91 km away for unlinked cases, $p = 0.006$; median distance 4.00 km vs. 16.11 km null, $p < 0.001$), that unlinked cases lived closer to the market than market-linked workers ($p = 0.029$), that earliest lineage A and B cases were both geographically associated with the market, and that SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental swabs inside the market clustered significantly in the southwest section where live SARS-CoV-2-susceptible mammals (including raccoon dogs, hog badgers, and red foxes) were sold ($p = 0.004$). Data and analysis code are publicly archived on Zenodo.

This is a rigorous, well-controlled spatial and forensic epidemiological investigation that directly tests and refutes key ascertainment-bias objections (such as showing that up to 68% of unlinked cases nearest the market can be dropped without losing statistical significance, and that coordinate extraction noise of $\sim 50$ m has no impact even when Perturbed by 1,000 m). The primary methodological caveats are: (1) the intra-market binomial GLM treats stalls as independent observations without explicitly modeling spatial autocorrelation or heterogeneous swab counts/surface types per stall; (2) the city-wide venue traffic comparison relies on Sina social media check-in data from 2013–2014, which suffers from demographic and temporal mismatch with late-2019 wholesale market patronage; and (3) the lineage A proximity test relies on $N = 2$ cases with an imputed hotel distance. These limitations can be addressed through spatial regression sensitivity analyses and tempered framing of the 2013–2014 mobility data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **December 2019 COVID-19 cases—especially the 120 cases without direct market links—were geographically centered on the Huanan market and resided closer to it than market-linked cases ($4.00$ km vs. $5.74$ km, $p = 0.029$): [Well Supported].**  
  The spatial centering of December 2019 cases within the top 1% KDE contour around the Huanan market (Figure 1B–C), the comparison against both age-matched WorldPop distributions and January–February 2020 Weibo cases ($p < 10^{-5}$; Figure 2C), and the dropout sensitivity analyses (retaining $\alpha = 0.05$ significance after removing 36–81 of the 120 unlinked cases closest to the market; fig. S12) provide robust support. The finding that unlinked cases lived significantly closer to the market than linked cases (mostly market vendors commuting from across Wuhan) is a strong internal validation of neighborhood community transmission radiating outward from the market rather than hospital ascertainment bias.

* **SARS-CoV-2-positive environmental samples inside the Huanan market are spatially clustered in the southwestern wildlife section and independently predicted by proximity to live-mammal vendors ($p = 0.004$) and human cases ($p = 0.014$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The `sparr` relative risk analysis accounts for the heterogeneous spatial distribution of sampled stalls (Figure 4B–D) and remains robust across three sensitivity scenarios (table S10 and fig. S18). However, the binomial GLM (table S9) models the proportion of positive swabs per business as conditionally independent given distance to live-mammal vendors and human cases, without including a spatial random field (e.g., Matérn Gaussian process) to account for contiguous drainage/sewerage flow and shared corridor aerosols, or adjusting for differences in the types of surfaces swabbed across stalls.

* **Both early SARS-CoV-2 lineages A and B were geographically associated with the Huanan market ($p = 0.017$ for 11 lineage B cases; $p \le 0.001$ for 2 lineage A cases): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The 11 geolocated lineage B cases show a clear median and center-point proximity to the market ($p = 0.017$ and $p = 0.026$), and the subsequent detection of lineage A in a Huanan market environmental swab corroborates market presence of both lineages. However, the statistical test for lineage A residential/stay proximity ($p \le 0.001$) is based on only $N = 2$ individuals, where the second patient's hotel coordinates were unknown ("stayed in a hotel near the market") and conservatively set to $\le 2.31$ km to match the first patient's distance. With $N = 2$ and one imputed coordinate, the combined $p \le 0.001$ estimate is fragile to the exact definition of "near the market."

* **The Huanan market accounted for only 0.12% of market visits and 0.04% of visits to $>400$ high-risk superspreader locations in Wuhan based on Sina Visitor System check-ins: [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 3 and Methods (line 235), intra-urban human foot traffic is quantified using 2013–2014 Sina Visitor System social media check-ins (5–6 years before the outbreak). Social media check-ins on Sina Weibo in 2013–2014 were heavily skewed toward young adults checking into shopping malls, universities, nightlife, and tourist landmarks, whereas wholesale food/wet markets are frequented by older residents and commercial buyers who rarely "check in" on social media, and urban infrastructure in Wuhan changed substantially between 2014 and 2019. While it is qualitatively indisputable that hundreds of venues in a city of 11 million people have high human contact rates, the exact percentages ($0.12\%$ and $0.04\%$) carry substantial demographic and temporal measurement bias.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Incorporate Spatial Autocorrelation and Overdispersion Controls in the Intra-Market Environmental GLM
* **WHY:** In a standard binomial GLM (`glm(cbind(pos, neg) ~ dist_mammal + dist_case, family = binomial)`), multiple swabs taken from the same stall or adjacent stalls sharing floor drains and ventilation violate binomial independence, which can anti-conservatively narrow standard errors and $p$-values ($p = 0.004$ and $p = 0.014$).
* **WHAT:** Fit a quasibinomial / beta-binomial GLM and a spatially correlated generalized linear mixed model (GLMM) with a Matérn or exponential spatial covariance structure across stall centroids.
* **HOW:** Re-estimate the Table S9 regression in R using `glmmTMB` or `spaMM` (`fitme(cbind(pos, total - pos) ~ dist_mammal + dist_human + Matern(1 | x + y), family = binomial)`), and also report a binary stall-level logistic regression (coding each stall simply as $\ge 1$ positive sample vs. $0$ positive samples, controlling for `log(total_samples_taken)` as an offset/covariate) to confirm that multi-swab clustering within single stalls does not drive the $p = 0.004$ result.

#### 2. Conduct a Sensitivity Analysis on the Imputed Hotel Distance for the Second Lineage A Case
* **WHY:** The second December 2019 lineage A patient stayed at an unspecified "hotel near the market," which the text sets at $\le 2.31$ km to compute a joint probability of $p \le 0.001$. If "near the market" in the original clinical history meant within the broader Jianghan/Jiangan commercial district (e.g., up to 4–5 km), the joint $p$-value would change.
* **WHAT:** Plot the joint $p$-value for the two lineage A cases as a continuous function of the assumed maximum radius $R \in [0.5\text{ km}, 10\text{ km}]$ for the second patient's hotel location.
* **HOW:** Using the empirical age-matched WorldPop cumulative distribution function $F(d)$ of distances to the Huanan market, compute $P(D_1 \le 2.31\text{ km}) \times P(D_2 \le R)$ (and the order-statistic test for the center-point of the two cases) across $R = 0.5, 1.0, 2.31, 4.0,$ and $6.0$ km, and report the threshold radius $R_{\max}$ at which the two-case proximity remains significant at $\alpha = 0.05$.

#### 3. Caveat the 2013–2014 Sina Visitor Check-In Proxy and Compare Against Population-Weighted Catchment Areas
* **WHY:** Sina Visitor check-ins from 2013–2014 under-represent older shoppers and wholesale workers at wet markets and predate Wuhan's 2019 subway and commercial layout by 5–6 years.
* **WHAT:** Add an explicit caveat in the Results section (line 156) and Figure 3 caption noting the 2013–2014 timeframe and age/activity bias of social media check-ins, and supplement the check-in rank with a purely demographic metric (e.g., commercial floor area or surrounding 1-km residential population density across the 70 markets).
* **HOW:** Compute the WorldPop residential population (all ages and aged $\ge 60$) within a 1-km and 2-km buffer around each of the $>70$ markets in Figure 3, and report where the Huanan market ranks in local residential catchment density relative to other Wuhan markets.
