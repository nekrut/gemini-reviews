**Authors:** Desingu PA, Nagarajan K.

**Overall Score:** **2.5 / 10.0** (Fundamentally Invalid)

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Desingu and Nagarajan present a brief descriptive commentary summarizing the global prevalence and mutation profiles of SARS-CoV-2 Omicron lineages BA.4, BA.5, and BA.5.1 as of mid-June 2022 using screenshots and summary percentages retrieved from the `outbreak.info` and `outbreakindia.com` web dashboards. The authors note that BA.4 and BA.5 reached global sequence proportions of 8% and 11%, respectively, by 10 June 2022, that most submitted BA.4 and BA.5 genomes originated from South Africa, the United States, the United Kingdom, and Europe, and that BA.4 and BA.5 share an identical Spike amino acid sequence differing from BA.2 at `DEL69/70`, `L452R`, `F486V`, and the `R493Q` reversion while diverging from each other at four non-Spike positions (`ORF1a`, `ORF6`, `ORF7b`, and `N`). They conclude that BA.4 is driving the June 2022 increase in COVID-19 cases in India and that BA.2 infection- or vaccine-induced antibodies can be expected to protect against BA.4 and BA.5. No original sequence alignments, statistical models, or archived dataset snapshots (e.g., GISAID EPI_SET identifiers) are provided.

This correspondence lacks quantitative rigor and contains multiple factual errors in SARS-CoV-2 genomics, epidemiology, and immunology. Specifically: (1) raw shares of global GISAID submissions (`69.31%` of BA.4 sequences from South Africa, the US, and the UK) are misinterpreted as geographic disease burden without normalizing for vast international disparities in sequencing volume; (2) the claim that BA.4 caused the June 2022 case surge in India is asserted without reporting Indian sequence counts, variant proportions, or competing `BA.2` sublineages (`BA.2.38`, `BA.2.75`); (3) the text misidentifies canonical BA.4/BA.5 mutations (reporting a non-existent `ORF7b-D61L` mutation in the 43-amino-acid ORF7b protein instead of `ORF7b:L11F` and `ORF6:D61L`, and erroneously claiming `L452R` was present in ancestral Omicron `B.1.1.529`); and (4) the conclusion that BA.2 antibodies will protect against BA.4/BA.5 because their Spike proteins are "almost identical" directly contradicts both the preceding sentences noting 4- to 20-fold neutralization escape via `L452R`/`F486V` and the empirical observation that BA.4/BA.5 rapidly displaced BA.2 worldwide. A complete rewrite grounded in denominator-normalized surveillance data, verified mutation coordinates, and formal logistic growth modeling would be required to make this analysis scientifically valid.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **BA.4 and BA.5 share an identical Spike protein (differing from BA.2 by DEL69/70, L452R, F486V, and R493Q) and diverge at four non-Spike loci (`OFR1a-DEL141/143`, `ORF7b-D61L`, `N-P151S`, and `ORF6-D3N`): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  While BA.4 and BA.5 do share the same Spike sequence, the manuscript's description of the specific mutations contains several factual errors:
  1. SARS-CoV-2 ORF7b is only 43 amino acids long and cannot harbor a `D61L` substitution (`ORF7b-D61L`). In reality, BA.4 carries **`ORF7b:L11F`**, while **`ORF6:D61L`** is the ancestral BA.2/BA.4 substitution in ORF6 that is absent (reverted) in BA.5. Moreover, Figure 1 does not even plot ORF7b (Figure 1L is labeled `ORF7a`).
  2. The text states that *"mutations such as DEL69/70 and L452R are already present in the Omicron lineage B.1.1.529"*. `L452R` was a hallmark mutation of Delta (`B.1.617.2`) and was *not* present in ancestral Omicron `B.1.1.529` (`BA.1`, `BA.2`, or `BA.3`).
  3. The text states there is *"one amino acid difference in each protein"* between BA.4 and BA.5 (`ORF1a`, `ORF6`, `ORF7b`, `N`), whereas `ORF1a:del141/143` (`NSP1:del141-143`) is a 3-amino-acid deletion (`KSF`).

* **Over 69% of BA.4 sequences and 80% of BA.5/BA.5.1 sequences are found in the US, UK, South Africa, and Europe: [Overstated].**  
  Reporting the fraction of all GISAID BA.4 or BA.5 sequences uploaded by a given country (`31.24%` South Africa, `27.05%` US, `11.02%` UK) reflects national genomic sequencing throughput and GISAID upload volume rather than true global prevalence distribution. Without normalizing by total sequences submitted per country over the same time window, cross-country shares of GISAID counts are confounded by ascertainment bias.

* **The BA.4 lineage is causing the May–June 2022 increase in daily COVID-19 cases in India: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The authors observe that reported daily cases in India rose from 2,338 on 30 May 2022 to 13,216 on 17 June 2022 while BA.4 prevalence ticked upward in Supporting Information Figure 2C, and conclude that *"the BA.4 lineage is causing the increasing number of COVID-19 cases in India."* The manuscript provides zero quantitative data on the actual proportion or sample size of BA.4 sequences in India during this 18-day window (during which `BA.2` sublineages such as `BA.2.38` and `BA.2.75` remained dominant in India while BA.4 remained a minor fraction) and performs no statistical test or variant-specific incidence decomposition.

* **Because the Spike protein is almost identical between BA.2 and BA.4/BA.5, antibodies from BA.2 infection or BA.2-based vaccines can be expected to protect against and control BA.4/BA.5: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This claim contradicts the empirical evidence presented in the very same paragraph and in Figure 1D: (i) the Spike RBD substitutions `L452R` and `F486V` sit directly in class 1–3 neutralizing antibody epitopes and confer the 4- to 20-fold neutralization resistance relative to BA.2 cited two sentences earlier; and (ii) BA.4 and BA.5 rapidly replaced BA.2 across populations that had just experienced massive BA.2 waves.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct the Genomic Mutation Coordinates and Lineage Comparisons
* **WHY:** Multiple amino acid coordinates, protein lengths, and ancestral Omicron states are misstated in the text and Figure 1.
* **WHAT:** Audit and correct all Spike and non-Spike mutation tables against the official Pango lineage definitions and reference genome `Wuhan-Hu-1` (`MN908947.3`).
* **HOW:** (i) Replace `ORF7b-D61L` with **`ORF7b:L11F`** (BA.4-specific) and clarify that **`ORF6:D61L`** is present in BA.2 and BA.4 but absent in BA.5; (ii) add an `ORF7b` panel to Figure 1 (currently missing, as panel L plots `ORF7a`); (iii) remove the incorrect statement that `L452R` was present in ancestral `B.1.1.529`; (iv) describe `ORF1a:del141-143` and `Spike:del69-70` accurately as 3-residue and 2-residue deletions rather than single amino acid changes; and (v) correct the emergence date of BA.4 and BA.5 in the opening sentence from *"April–May 2022"* to **December 2021–January 2022** (consistent with reference 1, Tegally et al.).

#### 2. Normalize Country-Level Sequence Counts by Total National Sequencing Volume
* **WHY:** Dividing a country's BA.4 sequence count by the global BA.4 sequence count (`Figure 1A–C`) conflates viral spread with sequencing capacity in the US, UK, and Europe.
* **WHAT:** Report within-country lineage prevalence ($n_{\text{BA.4}, c} / N_{\text{total}, c}$) and logistic growth rates per country over defined calendar windows.
* **HOW:** For each country shown in Figure 1A–C, extract both the numerator (weekly BA.4, BA.5, and BA.5.1 sequence counts by collection date) and denominator (total weekly SARS-CoV-2 sequences collected in that country with $\ge 50$ sequences/week), and plot the within-country relative frequency trajectories with 95% binomial confidence intervals.

#### 3. Quantify Variant Proportions and Estimate Variant-Attributed Case Incidence Before Making Causal Claims for India
* **WHY:** Concluding that BA.4 caused India's June 2022 case rise without reporting the fraction of Indian sequences belonging to BA.4 versus BA.5 and emerging BA.2 sublineages (`BA.2.38`, `BA.2.75`) is unsupported.
* **WHAT:** Decompose daily Indian case counts by multiplying daily cases by the smoothed GISAID/INSACOG lineage proportions in India from May to June 2022.
* **HOW:** Tabulate the weekly counts and percentages of `BA.2` (and its major sublineages `BA.2.38`, `BA.2.75`), `BA.4`, and `BA.5` in India between 1 May and 17 June 2022, fit a multinomial logistic regression to estimate the relative growth rate advantage per week, and revise the causal claim in paragraph 4 to reflect whichever lineage(s) actually accounted for the majority of new infections in India.

#### 4. Remove Contradictory Speculation Regarding BA.2 Cross-Protection
* **WHY:** Asserting that BA.2 antibodies will control BA.4/BA.5 because their Spike proteins differ at "only four amino acid levels" ignores the functional impact of RBD mutations `L452R` and `F486V` and contradicts the paper's own citation of 4–20$\times$ neutralization escape.
* **WHAT:** Align the immunological discussion with published live-virus and pseudovirus neutralization data from BA.2 convalescent sera against BA.4/BA.5.
* **HOW:** Revise the final three sentences of paragraph 3 to state that although BA.4/BA.5 evolved from a BA.2-like backbone, the acquisition of `L452R` and `F486V` in the RBD substantially reduces neutralization by both post-vaccination BA.1 and BA.2 convalescent sera, enabling BA.4 and BA.5 to outcompete BA.2.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Inconsistent query dates, a mismatched country code in the Figure 1 caption, and gene-name typos impair reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Mismatched country code in Figure 1D–E caption (line 31):** The caption states *"Prevalence of different Omicron lineages in South Africa over the periods. The data were obtained from `https://outbreak.info/location-reports?loc=IND&pango`."* The parameter `loc=IND` queries **India**, not **South Africa** (`loc=ZAF`). Correct the URL to `loc=ZAF` (and verify that the plotted panels in Figure 1D–E actually correspond to South Africa rather than India).
  2. **Inconsistent cutoff dates across text and Figure 1:** Paragraph 2 cites `June 16th, 2022` for BA.4 country counts, `June 6th, 2022` for BA.5 and BA.5.1 country counts, `June 10th, 2022` for global prevalence percentages, and `June 18th, 2022` in the Figure 1F–O caption, while the Figure 1A–C caption states `as of June 16th, 2022` for all three panels. Re-extract all panels using a single uniform collection/submission cutoff date and provide a GISAID `EPI_SET` identifier.
  3. **Gene name typos (`OFR1a`):** In paragraph 2 (line 25), `ORF1a` is misspelled twice as `OFR1a` (`"in proteins such as OFR1a"` and `"OFR1a-DEL141/143"`). Change both to `ORF1a`.
