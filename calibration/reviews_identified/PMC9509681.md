**Manuscript Title:** The Relevance of Host Gut Microbiome Signature Alterations on de novo Fatty Acids Synthesis in Patients with Multi-Drug Resistant Tuberculosis  
**Authors:** Jichan Shi, Gexin Gao, Zhijie Yu, Kaihuai Wu, Youquan Huang, Lian-Peng Wu, Zhengxing Wu, Xinchun Ye, Chaochao Qiu, Xiangao Jiang  
**Journal / Preprint:** Infection and Drug Resistance (2022) | DOI: 10.2147/idr.s372122

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Shi et al. investigate gut microbiome taxonomic composition and the expression of fatty acid metabolism genes (*ACC1*, *FASN*, and *CPT1A*, normalized to *GAPDH*) in 62 tuberculosis (TB) patients—stratified into rifampicin-resistant TB (RR-TB, $n=23$), multi-drug resistant TB (MDR-TB, $n=28$), and pre-extensively/extensively drug-resistant TB (pre-XDR-TB, $n=11$)—compared against 5 healthy controls (CK) recruited at Wenzhou Central Hospital. Stool samples were physically pooled by group ("300 mg of faeces from patients from the same group was mixed") prior to DNA extraction and sequencing, while blood samples were assayed by qRT-PCR for *CPT1A*, *FASN*, and *ACC1* mRNA levels. The authors report shifted α-diversity and phylum/order/family distributions across the TB subgroups alongside elevated *ACC1*/*GAPDH* and *FASN*/*GAPDH* expression following the gradient $\text{MDR-TB} > \text{pre-XDR-TB} > \text{RR-TB} > \text{CK}$, concluding that antimicrobial-induced gut dysbiosis (specifically depletion of *Bacteroidetes*/*Bacteroidales*/*Bacteroidaceae*) drives enhanced host *de novo* fatty acid synthesis and progression to advanced TB. No sequencing reads, accession numbers, or analysis scripts are publicly deposited.

Despite addressing a clinically relevant question regarding gut microbiome alterations during second-line anti-TB therapy, the study suffers from fatal experimental design, statistical, and reporting flaws that invalidate its quantitative conclusions. First, physically mixing 300 mg of stool across patients within each clinical group before DNA extraction collapses inter-individual biological variation into a single composite pool per group ($N_{\text{eff}} = 1$), rendering the error bars and $p$-values in Figure 2 technical pseudoreplication and contradicting the claim in Figure 1 that α-diversity was measured for "each subject." Second, no correlation or regression analysis between microbial taxa and blood mRNA expression is ever computed, leaving the central claim that *Bacteroidetes* abundance correlates with and drives *de novo* fatty acid synthesis completely unsupported. Third, the manuscript is pervaded by internal contradictions in cohort metadata (copying the healthy control group's age range and mean BMI onto the 62 TB patients in the Results text), inconsistent sampling timelines across sections, severe confounding between disease resistance class and antibiotic exposure, and confusion between blood leukocyte mRNA, serum protein, and faecal measurements. Resolving these defects is not possible through computational re-analysis alone; it requires sequencing the unpooled individual patient stool specimens, performing subject-level statistical modeling, and correcting the demographic and molecular records.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Gut microbiome α-diversity and taxonomic profiles differ significantly across RR-TB, MDR-TB, pre-XDR-TB, and healthy control groups: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Both the Abstract and the Methods ("For analysis, 300 mg of faeces collected from the same group of individuals was mixed and analyzed via DNA extraction, high-throughput sequencing, and bioinformatics. ... The extracted DNA was divided into four test tubes") state that stool specimens were physically pooled within each group prior to DNA extraction. Pooling destroys subject-level variance ($N = 1$ biological pool per group). Consequently, the "means ± SD of three independent experiments" and `$*p < 0.05$` reported in Figure 2 reflect technical replicates of a single pooled extraction rather than biological variance across the 23 RR-TB, 28 MDR-TB, 11 pre-XDR-TB, and 5 control individuals. Furthermore, a control group of $n = 5$ is severely underpowered and imbalanced relative to $n = 62$ cases.

* **Enhanced *de novo* fatty acid synthesis in MDR-TB patients is correlated with and caused by antimicrobial-induced depletion of *Bacteroidetes*, *Bacteroidales*, and *Bacteroidaceae*: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Although the Abstract, Results (p. 259), and Discussion (p. 270) assert that *Bacteroidetes*, *Bacteroidales*, and *Bacteroidaceae* display "the most important correlations at a phylum, order, and family level" with *de novo* fatty acid synthesis, the manuscript contains zero correlation coefficients ($r$ or $\rho$), regression models, or $p$-values linking microbiome abundances to *ACC1*, *FASN*, or *CPT1A* expression. Even in principle, subject-level correlation could not be computed if the stool samples were pooled by group. In addition, the Discussion (p. 270) erroneously states that "The proportions [of *ACC1* to *GAPDH* and *FASN* to *GAPDH*] in the faeces of the patient groups varied and were higher than in the CK group," whereas Methods (p. 171) states that RNA was extracted from whole blood samples, not faeces.

* **Blood mRNA ratios of *ACC1*/*GAPDH* and *FASN*/*GAPDH* follow the gradient $\text{MDR-TB} > \text{pre-XDR-TB} > \text{RR-TB} > \text{healthy controls}$, while *CPT1A*/*GAPDH* is elevated in RR-TB: [Fragile].**  
  While qRT-PCR of blood RNA is a standard assay, the methodology and statistical analysis are improperly specified: (1) Methods states that expression was calculated via the $\Delta\text{Ct}$ method normalized to *GAPDH*, yet the text and Figure 6 describe the metric as "proportions of *ACC1* to *GAPDH*" rather than relative fold-change ($2^{-\Delta\Delta\text{Ct}}$ or $2^{-\Delta\text{Ct}}$); (2) Figure 6 reports "means ± SD of three independent experiments" without clarifying whether $N$ represents the 62 individual patients or 3 technical PCR replicates of pooled blood; (3) the Statistical Analysis section mentions only a "one-way variance A test [ANOVA]" in SPSS 13.0 with no post-hoc multiple-comparison correction; and (4) whole-blood leukocyte mRNA levels of *FASN* and *ACC1* do not directly measure hepatic or systemic *de novo* fatty acid synthesis or "the profiles and compositions of FASN in serum" (Discussion, p. 268).

* **Observed microbiome and metabolic shifts are driven by multi-drug anti-TB therapy and/or MDR-TB disease progression: [Overstated].**  
  The study design cannot separate the effect of drug-resistance category from antibiotic regimen, treatment duration, or baseline malnutrition. First, the Study Design (p. 154) lists "current (in the past six months) use of metabolic agents, probiotics, or antibiotics" as an exclusion criterion while simultaneously enrolling 62 active TB patients undergoing multi-drug antibiotic therapy. Second, sampling timelines are mutually contradictory across the text: p. 149 states samples were collected "at the start of anti-TB treatment and three months after the start of treatment," p. 159 states stool was collected "at two and eight weeks," and Figures 1–6 display only a single cross-sectional value per group without indicating which timepoint is plotted. Third, Table 1 and Table 2 show pronounced baseline nutritional differences between TB patients (albumin $37.61 \pm 0.38\text{ g/L}$, hemoglobin $131.5 \pm 23.54\text{ g/L}$) and healthy controls (albumin $46.9 \pm 1.78\text{ g/L}$, hemoglobin $157.5 \pm 7.45\text{ g/L}$) that directly confound lipid metabolism and gut microbiota composition.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Sequence Individual Unpooled Stool Samples and Perform Subject-Level Statistical Inference
* **WHY:** Physical pooling of stool across patients within each clinical group eliminates biological replication ($N = 1$ per group), invalidating ANOVA tests on α-diversity and preventing any patient-level correlation analysis between gut taxa and blood gene expression.
* **WHAT:** Extract DNA and perform 16S rRNA (or metagenomic) sequencing independently on the archived $-80^\circ\text{C}$ stool aliquots for each of the 62 TB patients and an appropriately sized, age-, sex-, and BMI-matched healthy control cohort.
* **HOW:** Process individual patient FASTQ files through a modern, reproducible amplicon pipeline (e.g., QIIME 2 / DADA2 against SILVA/Greengenes2 rather than legacy RDP v2.10.1), compute α-diversity (Shannon, Simpson, Chao1) per individual, and test group differences using Kruskal-Wallis or linear models adjusted for age, sex, BMI, and serum albumin, with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction.

#### 2. Compute Formal Subject-Level Correlations Between Taxa and Host Gene Expression
* **WHY:** The manuscript repeatedly claims that *Bacteroidetes*, *Bacteroidales*, and *Bacteroidaceae* correlate with enhanced *de novo* fatty acid synthesis without presenting a single correlation statistic or scatter plot.
* **WHAT:** Replace speculative prose assertions with formal non-parametric correlation and multivariable regression analyses linking individual patient taxon abundances to blood qRT-PCR measurements.
* **HOW:** Using subject-level relative abundances (or centered log-ratio transformed counts) and individual patient $2^{-\Delta\text{Ct}}$ values for *ACC1*, *FASN*, and *CPT1A*, compute Spearman rank correlations ($\rho$) and FDR-adjusted $q$-values across all tested phyla, orders, and families, and display the resulting correlation matrix or scatter plots as a new figure.

#### 3. Resolve Contradictory Sampling Timelines and Separate Baseline Disease from Antibiotic Treatment Effects
* **WHY:** The Methods section gives three incompatible collection schedules (baseline vs. 3 months on p. 149; before vs. after experiment on p. 154; 2 weeks vs. 8 weeks on p. 159), while the Results present only one timepoint per group and conflate pre-existing MDR-TB status with antibiotic-induced dysbiosis.
* **WHAT:** Clarify the exact longitudinal sampling schedule, specify which timepoint is shown in every figure, and analyze paired pre-treatment (baseline) and on-treatment samples separately.
* **HOW:** Tabulate the exact antimicrobial regimen and treatment duration for each of the RR-TB, MDR-TB, and pre-XDR-TB groups; plot paired baseline ($T_0$) and follow-up ($T_1$) microbiome and qRT-PCR profiles; and use a linear mixed-effects model with subject random effects (`Outcome ~ Group * Timepoint + Age + Sex + BMI + ALB + (1|Patient_ID)`) to separate baseline resistance-group differences from longitudinal antibiotic effects.

#### 4. Correct Demographic Contradictions, Sequencing Methods Descriptions, and Biological Misstatements
* **WHY:** Severe numerical and technical inconsistencies between the text and tables undermine basic data integrity:
  1. In Results (p. 203), the authors write that the 62 TB patients were "aged between 36 and 59 years... The mean BMI of these patients was $24.12\text{ kg/m}^2$." However, in Table 2, $36\text{--}59\text{ years}$ and $\text{BMI } 24.12 \pm 3.14$ are the exact values for the 5 healthy controls, whereas Table 1 lists the 62 TB patients' average age as $51.1$ and BMI as $22.42 \pm 3.99$.
  2. Tables 1 and 2 carry the footnote `"Data are represented as means ± SD of three independent experiments"` for human demographic counts (gender) and clinical blood chemistry.
  3. The sequencing methods description (p. 164: *"DNA fragments were connected into complete DNA molecules. After the primer was hydrolyzed, the primer on the lead chain became a completely new chain to realize bridge amplification"*) omits the sequencing platform (e.g., Illumina MiSeq), target 16S rRNA hypervariable region (e.g., V3–V4), primer sequences, and read depth.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Audit the original clinical records to fix the age and BMI numbers on p. 203; remove the `"three independent experiments"` footnote from Tables 1 and 2; rewrite the sequencing methods to state the exact target amplicon, primers, sequencing instrument, and read counts per sample; correct `"GAPDH"` vs `"GADPH"` in Table 3 and `"proportions in the faeces"` on p. 270 to `"relative mRNA expression in peripheral blood"`; and deposit raw FASTQ files in a public repository (e.g., NCBI SRA or CNCB GSA) with accession numbers in the Data Sharing Statement.
