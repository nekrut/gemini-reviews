### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Gihawi et al. perform a forensic computational audit of a high-profile cancer microbiome study (Poore et al., *Nature* 2020) that had reported near-perfect machine-learning classification of 33 cancer types across 17,625 The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) samples using microbial read signatures. By re-analyzing Poore et al.'s full 17,625-sample × 1,993-genus raw and Voom-SNM normalized count matrices alongside raw unmapped sequencing reads downloaded from 1,255 TCGA samples across bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), and breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA), the authors uncover two independent, fatal errors. First, relying on legacy TCGA BAM unmapped flags left 1.39 to 3.30 million unaligned human reads per sample (14.5%–51% of "unmapped" reads mapping to the complete T2T-CHM13 human reference), and matching these reads against a 2016 Kraken database that lacked the human genome and included thousands of human-contaminated draft bacterial assemblies inflated bacterial read counts by 2 to 4 orders of magnitude across 92.9%–99.4% of non-zero entries. Second, the supervised Voom-SNM normalization step injected cancer-type-specific numerical tags into features with zero raw reads, enabling Gihawi et al. to train Poore et al.'s exact machine-learning pipeline on an information-free $12,803 \times 66$ matrix of 100% raw zeros and achieve near-perfect tumor classification (median sensitivity 0.94, specificity 0.90, NPV 1.00, matching or exceeding the original AUCs in 14 of 32 cancer types). All re-analyzed count tables, database accessions, and scripts are publicly released on GitHub.

This manuscript is a landmark forensic re-analysis that combines definitive negative controls, mechanistic read-level tracing, and full transparency. Both the draft-genome human-read misclassification experiment (demonstrating that 98.1% and 98.9% of reads assigned to *Streptococcus* and *Waddlia* in sample `s2707` align to CHM13 human DNA) and the all-zero raw matrix classification experiment (Figure 6) provide irrefutable proof that the reported pan-cancer microbial signatures were computational artifacts. Only minor technical discrepancies exist within the manuscript itself: a numerical mismatch between Table 1 and the text regarding the percentage of initially unmapped reads in BRCA (0.5% in Table 1 vs. 2.7% in the prose), an unexplained drop from 277 downloaded BLCA WGS samples in Table 1 to 156 re-analyzed BLCA WGS samples in Table 2, and stopping short of pinpointing the exact algebraic mechanism inside the Voom-SNM script that generated the target-label leakage.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Relying on initially unmapped reads from legacy TCGA BAM files leaves 1.4–3.3 million human reads per sample that were misclassified as bacteria due to human-contaminated draft genomes and omission of a human reference in Kraken: [Well Supported].**  
  Re-aligning unmapped BAM reads from 1,255 BLCA, HNSC, and BRCA samples against T2T-CHM13 recovers 519 million to 981 million human reads per cohort. When Gihawi et al. re-classify the 728 WGS samples against a curated RefSeq complete-genome KrakenUniq database that includes GRCh38 and UniVec, 92.9% to 99.4% of Poore et al.'s sample-genus entries ($\ge 10$ or $\ge 100$ reads) drop by more than 10-fold (Table 2), and top reported genera (*Streptococcus*, *Mycobacterium*, *Staphylococcus*) fall by factors of 900 to 67,000 (Figure 1). The causal mechanism is sealed by reconstructing a 2016 GenBank draft database for *Streptococcus* and *Waddlia* on sample `s2707`, proving that 98.1%–98.9% of the resulting 1.43 million and 197,811 "bacterial" reads map directly to CHM13 human DNA.

* **Supervised Voom-SNM normalization introduced artificial tumor-type tags into empty features, allowing machine-learning classifiers to achieve near-perfect accuracy on a matrix of 100% zeros: [Well Supported].**  
  Inspecting individual top-weighted features (*Hepandensovirus* in ACC, *Thiorhodospira* in KICH, *Mulikevirus* in HNSC; Figures 2–5) shows that raw counts of zero were transformed into disjoint, cohort-specific constants (e.g., `3.078874655` across 71/79 ACC tumors when 17,624/17,625 TCGA samples had zero reads). Extracting a $12,803 \times 66$ primary tumor matrix containing exclusively zeros in the raw data, populating it with Poore et al.'s Voom-SNM values, and re-running their exact gradient-boosting classifier reproduces median sensitivity of 0.94 and specificity of 0.90 across all 32 cancer types (Figure 6). Because a matrix of all zeros has strictly zero mutual information with cancer type, this experiment proves beyond doubt that class labels leaked into the feature matrix during normalization.

* **Top-weighted microbial genera in the original classifiers included ecologically implausible extremophiles and non-human taxa with zero or near-zero true counts in human tumors: [Well Supported].**  
  Tables S3–S4 and the re-analysis of downstream studies (lines 249–252) confirm that high-weight features such as hydrothermal vent archaea (*Methanothermus*), soda-lake extremophiles (*Halonatronum*, *Salinarchaeum*), and plant/soil bacteria had zero or near-zero read counts once human-read cross-mapping and Voom-SNM leakage were removed.

* **Downstream studies that trained prognostic or subtype models on Poore et al.'s raw or Voom-SNM matrices are similarly compromised: [Well Supported].**  
  Because both the raw count matrix (93%–99% inflated by human reads matching draft bacterial contigs) and the Voom-SNM normalized matrix (artificially tagged by tumor/sample metadata) are corrupted at the source, any secondary study downloading those matrices inherits the same human-DNA and normalization artifacts.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reconcile Sample Counts and Unmapped Read Percentages Between Table 1, Table 2, and the Main Text
* **WHY:** There are two internal numerical discrepancies between Table 1 and the text/Table 2:
  1. In Table 1 (line 89), BRCA has `324,824,097,837` total reads and `1,532,210,153 (0.5%)` initially unmapped reads ($1.532 / 324.824 = 0.47\%$). However, in the text immediately below (line 99), the authors write: *"In the original data sets, the proportion of reads that originally did not map to the human genome was 2.4%, 1.4%, and 2.7%, respectively"* (`2.7%` instead of `0.5%`).
  2. In Table 1 (line 87), the number of downloaded BLCA WGS samples is listed as `277` (plus `406` RNA-seq = `683`), whereas lines 113, 153 (Table 2), and 267 state that the BLCA WGS re-analysis comprised `156` WGS samples (`129` primary tumor and `27` solid-tissue normal).
* **WHAT & HOW:** Check the BRCA unmapped read denominator to correct either `0.5%` in Table 1 or `2.7%` on line 99; and add a brief clause in Materials and Methods (line 267) explaining the filtering criteria that reduced the 277 downloaded BLCA WGS files in Table 1 to the 156 primary tumor and solid-tissue normal WGS samples analyzed in Figures 1 and Table 2 (e.g., exclusion of blood-derived normal or replicate BAM aliquots).

#### 2. Pinpoint the Exact Algebraic Mechanism of Target Leakage in the Voom-SNM Pipeline
* **WHY:** On line 238, the authors state: *"Note that we do not know precisely where Poore et al. went wrong in applying the normalization code, but because we have the original read count data and the resulting normalized data, we know that the transformation created the artificial signals."* Identifying the exact mechanism in `voom()` and `snm()` is valuable for preventing similar supervised normalization errors in the community.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Inspect Poore et al.'s public R normalization script: in `voom()`, adding a pseudocount (e.g., `0.5`) before dividing by sample library size $L_i$ converts a raw count of `0` into $\log_2(0.5 / L_i)$, which varies systematically across TCGA sequencing centers and cancer cohorts; then, in Supervised Normalization of Microarrays (`snm()`), passing tissue/disease status as the biological variable `bio.var` while adjusting for `adj.var` (sequencing center/platform, which in TCGA is nearly collinear with cancer type) causes `snm` to add the estimated `bio.var` conditional mean shift onto every feature—including all-zero features—before returning the normalized residuals. Adding a 1-paragraph explanation of this `voom` pseudocount + `snm` `bio.var`/`adj.var` collinearity mechanism to the Discussion will make the methodological lesson complete.
