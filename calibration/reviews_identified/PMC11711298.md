### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study evaluates genome-wide association mapping (GWAS) and genomic prediction (GP) across 195 hexaploid oat (*Avena sativa* L.) accessions phenotyped for seven agronomic traits (plant height [PH], cob/panicle length [CL], flag leaf length [FLL], flag leaf width [FLW], spikelets per panicle [SPP], stem diameter [SD], and tiller number [TN]) across two locations in Sichuan, China over two growing seasons (2021–2023). Using 77,835 Super-GBS SNP markers (filtered at MAF $> 1\%$, heterozygosity $< 20\%$, missingness $< 70\%$, and imputed with Beagle v4.1), the authors characterize population structure ($K = 2, F_{ST} = 0.24$), estimate SNP heritability via LDAK-Thin ($h^2 = 0.55\text{--}0.76$), identify 94 Bonferroni-significant trait-associated SNPs (TASs) across four GAPIT3 models, and compare GP accuracy between GBLUP and six machine learning (ML) models (Lasso, Ridge, ElasticNet, KRR, SVR-linear, and SVR-poly) using 500 replications of 5-fold cross-validation (CV). Finally, they evaluate GP accuracy using subsets of the top 100 to 5,000 GWAS-ranked SNPs. Raw sequencing reads are deposited in CNGBdb (`CNP0005721`), though phenotypic tables, imputed genotype matrices, and analysis scripts are not publicly archived.

While multi-environment phenotyping and genotyping of 195 diverse oat accessions provide a useful empirical dataset for oat breeding, the study's core computational and statistical conclusions are invalidated by three severe methodological errors: (1) **test-set data leakage in GWAS-assisted GP**, where GWAS marker selection and phenotype BLUP estimation were performed across all 195 accessions *prior* to splitting into 80%/20% training and test folds, artificially inflating prediction accuracy for top GWAS subsets; (2) **an algebraic contradiction between GBLUP and Ridge Regression**, which are mathematically equivalent L2-penalized linear models yet are reported to have vastly different accuracies ("GBLUP performed poorly in predicting all traits except FLW" while Ridge was top-performing); and (3) **corrupted mathematical equations and squared-error metrics**, where every penalized regression loss function in *Materials and Methods* has the wrong sign on the predictor sum, and the values reported as "MAE" in Section 3.4 are actually the exact squares of the values reported as "MSE" ($14.341^2 = 205.68$, $15.162^2 = 229.88$, $17.311^2 = 299.66$). A complete re-analysis with nested within-fold GWAS selection, corrected error metrics, and properly tuned GBLUP baselines is required.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The 195 oat accessions partition into two strongly differentiated genetic subpopulations ($F_{ST} = 0.24$) with moderate-to-high trait heritabilities ($h^2 = 0.55\text{--}0.76$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Admixture ($K = 2$), PCA (PC1 $= 24.57\%$, PC2 $= 9.68\%$), phylogenetic clustering, and the kinship matrix consistently separate Group 1 ($n = 75$) and Group 2 ($n = 120$) (Figure 1). However, allowing up to 70% missing genotypes per SNP prior to Beagle v4.1 imputation on a small sample of $N = 195$ diverse polyploid lines can introduce substantial imputation artifacts, and LDAK-Thin heritability point estimates are reported without standard errors (Table S3).

* **Three GWAS loci (`chr3A_378078108` and `chr6C_25741715` for FLW; `chr5A_16429536` for FLL) are stably associated across multiple environments: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Applying a Bonferroni threshold ($0.05 / 77,835 = 6.42 \times 10^{-7}$) across four GAPIT3 models (EMMAX, BLINK, FarmCPU, SUPER) identifies 94 TASs, of which three replicate across at least two environments (Figure 3, Table S5). Because $N = 195$ is small for GWAS, individual single-environment hits are prone to Beavis-effect inflation, making the three multi-environment hits the most credible candidates.

* **Using GWAS-derived marker subsets (100–5,000 SNPs) improves genomic prediction accuracy over genome-wide markers, with PH peaking at 100 markers: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  As described in Sections 2.5 and 3.5, the GWAS used to rank and select the top 100–5,000 SNPs was executed on the full cohort of 195 accessions before partitioning into 80% training and 20% validation sets during 5-fold CV. When $p = 77,835 \gg n = 195$, pre-selecting the top 100–5,000 SNPs using the phenotypes of all 195 individuals leaks validation-fold information directly into feature selection, guaranteeing an artificial upward bias in CV accuracy (particularly for small marker subsets such as 100 SNPs).

* **Machine learning models consistently outperform GBLUP in prediction correlation, MSE, and MAE across oat agronomic traits: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, GBLUP ($u \sim N(0, G\sigma_u^2)$) and Ridge Regression (L2-penalized marker regression) are mathematically identical linear predictors up to parameterization ($\lambda = \sigma_e^2 / \sigma_u^2$). Reporting that Ridge Regression achieves the highest accuracy on FLL ($60.87\%$) and high accuracy across traits while GBLUP fails on almost all traits (Figure 4) indicates a software/tuning misconfiguration in the GBLUP implementation rather than an inherent superiority of ML over linear mixed models. Second, the MSE and MAE values reported in Section 3.4 (Line 292) and Table S6 are mathematically impossible: for SPP, PH, CL, and TN, the reported "MSE" values are $14.341, 15.162, 12.965,$ and $17.311$, while the reported "MAE" values are $205.68, 229.88, 168.08,$ and $299.66$—which are the exact squares of the "MSE" numbers ($14.341^2 = 205.66$, $15.162^2 = 229.89$, $12.965^2 = 168.09$, $17.311^2 = 299.67$; similarly for FLL, FLW, and SD, $0.008^2 \approx 0.0001$, $0.112^2 = 0.0125 \approx 0.012$, and $0.105^2 = 0.0110 \approx 0.011$). The authors evidently computed RMSE (or MAE), labeled it "MSE," and squared it to produce "MAE."

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Data Leakage by Nesting GWAS Marker Selection Strictly Inside Each Cross-Validation Training Fold
* **WHY:** Ranking 77,835 SNPs by GWAS $p$-values computed on all 195 accessions before splitting into 80% training ($n = 156$) and 20% test ($n = 39$) sets leaks test-set phenotypes into marker selection, severely inflating prediction accuracy in Figure 5. Similarly, computing multi-environment BLUPs across all 195 genotypes jointly before CV shares information across folds when a kinship matrix or group effect is included.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the GWAS-assisted GP analysis (Section 3.5 and Figure 5) by performing GWAS strictly within each 80% training fold ($n = 156$), selecting the top $k \in \{100, 500, 1000, 3000, 5000\}$ SNPs based solely on the training-fold GWAS $p$-values, fitting the ML model on those $n = 156$ individuals, and predicting the held-out $n = 39$ individuals.
* **HOW:** Within each of the 5 folds in R/Python, subset the genotype and phenotype matrices to `train_idx` ($n = 156$), run a fast linear mixed model GWAS (e.g., EMMAX/GAPIT3) on `train_idx` only, extract the top $k$ SNP columns, train the GP model on `X[train_idx, top_k]`, and evaluate Pearson $r$, RMSE, and MAE on `X[test_idx, top_k]`.

#### 2. Audit the GBLUP Implementation Against Ridge Regression and Evaluate Structure-Stratified CV
* **WHY:** Because GBLUP and Ridge Regression are dual formulations of the same L2-regularized linear model, GBLUP cannot perform drastically worse than Ridge Regression when both use the same centered/scaled marker matrix $Z$ and properly estimated variance components. In addition, because Group 1 and Group 2 have $F_{ST} = 0.24$, random 5-fold CV largely measures between-subpopulation mean differences rather than within-subpopulation genomic breeding value.
* **WHAT:** Debug the GBLUP solver (e.g., using `rrBLUP::mixed.solve` or `sommer::mmer` in R) to verify that it matches Ridge Regression when $G = ZZ^\top / p$, and report within-group and across-group (Group 1 $\leftrightarrow$ Group 2) CV accuracies alongside random 5-fold CV.
* **HOW:** Run `rrBLUP::mixed.solve(y = y_train, Z = X_train)` on the exact same CV splits used for `glmnet(alpha = 0)` (Ridge), verify concordance of predicted values $\hat{y}_{\text{test}}$, and add a stratified CV analysis evaluating accuracy within Group 1 ($n = 75$) and Group 2 ($n = 120$) separately.

#### 3. Re-compute and Correct the Swapped/Squared MSE and MAE Metrics
* **WHY:** In Section 3.4 (Line 292) and Table S6, every reported "MAE" value is the square of the reported "MSE" value (e.g., $\text{MSE} = 17.311$ and $\text{MAE} = 299.66 = 17.311^2$), violating the mathematical definitions of MSE ($\frac{1}{\omega}\sum (f_i - y_i)^2$) and MAE ($\frac{1}{\omega}\sum |f_i - y_i|$).
* **WHAT:** Audit the script that generated Table S6 and Section 3.4, compute true MSE, RMSE ($\sqrt{\text{MSE}}$), and MAE ($\frac{1}{\omega}\sum |f_i - y_i|$) on the validation predictions, and update Section 3.4 and Table S6.
* **HOW:** Verify that for every trait and model, $\text{MAE} \le \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\text{MSE}}$, and report both metrics in consistent phenotypic units in Section 3.4 and Table S6.

#### 4. Fix Mathematical Equations in Section 2.6 and Deposit Genotype/Phenotype Data
* **WHY:** All four penalized regression equations in Section 2.6 contain sign errors in the residual term, the SVR hinge-loss equation omits the response variable $y_i$, and the processed SNP and phenotype matrices are not available for verification.
* **WHAT & HOW:**  
  * **Lines 169, 179, 197, and 205 (Bridge, Ridge, Lasso, and ElasticNet equations):** Change the plus sign inside the squared loss $(y_i + \sum_{j=1}^p x_{ij}\beta_j)^2$ to a minus sign: $(y_i - \sum_{j=1}^p x_{ij}\beta_j)^2$.  
  * **Line 183 (KRR label typo):** Change *"The ElasticNet regression method is:"* preceding the $\hat{\beta}_{\text{KRR}}$ equation to *"The KRR method is:"*.  
  * **Line 205 (ElasticNet penalty parameters):** Distinguish the L1 and L2 regularization weights as $\lambda_1 \sum_{j=1}^p |\beta_j| + \lambda_2 \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j^2$ (or $\lambda [\alpha \|\beta\|_1 + (1-\alpha)\|\beta\|_2^2]$).  
  * **Line 223 (SVR-linear optimization objective):** Correct $\max(0, \beta_0 - X_i^t\beta - \varepsilon)$ to $\max(0, |y_i - (\beta_0 + X_i^t\beta)| - \varepsilon)$ and close the outer parenthesis.  
  * **Line 140 (LDAK-Thin equation):** Replace the division slash in $E[h^2 / j]$ with a conditioning bar $E[h^2 \mid j]$, and note that MAF weighting uses $[p_j(1-p_j)]^{0.75}$ rather than $p_j^{0.75}$.  
  * **Data Availability:** Deposit the imputed 77,835-SNP genotype matrix, the environment-specific and BLUP phenotype tables for the 195 accessions, and the CV scripts on Zenodo or Dryad.
