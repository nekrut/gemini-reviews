### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Turakhia et al. investigate two foundational challenges in pandemic-scale viral phylogenomics: systematic sequencing/bioinformatic errors that masquerade as recurrent mutations (homoplasies) or recombination, and topological instability across phylogenetic trees built from low-diversity SARS-CoV-2 alignments. Analyzing 31 sequential Nextstrain builds (March 23 to April 30, 2020; focusing on the April 19 build of 3,246 variant sites) alongside the April 24 COG-UK phylogeny, the authors introduce automated algorithms to: (1) compute Fitch parsimony scores and identify "extremal" sites whose parsimony recurrence is anomalously high relative to log2 alternate allele count; (2) harmonize misspelled GISAID laboratory metadata and flag lab-associated recurrent variants ($\ge 80\%$ of alternate alleles from a single lab, Fisher's exact $p < 0.01$, parsimony score $\ge 4$); (3) quantify topological divergence using an entropy-weighted matching split distance ($D(T, T')$) and normalized symmetric distance ($S_p(T, T')$); and (4) rapidly untangle large phylogenies via an iterative rank-based node rotation heuristic (`RotTrees`). They identify 16 strongly lab-associated variants that significantly co-localize with ARTIC PCR primer binding sites ($P = 0.028$, permutation test), Nanopore adapter sequences (A24389C/G24390C), or misaligned human rRNA reads (C22802G), validate their topological effects via 81 synthetic error-injection experiments in `IQ-TREE 2`, and show that genuine non-lab-associated recurrent mutations are dominated by APOBEC-like $C \to U$ transitions ($42/50$ sites, $P = 3.66 \times 10^{-7}$) under purifying selection ($P = 0.0553$ for synonymous vs. non-synonymous parsimony scores). All C++/Python tools (`strain_phylogenetics`, `COVID-19-Lab-Specific-Bias-Filter`), regularly updated problematic-site VCFs (`ProblematicSites_SARS-CoV2`), and interactive UCSC SARS-CoV-2 Genome Browser tracks are openly available.

This is an exemplary methodological and forensic study that combines mathematical clarity, biological insight into viral mutation spectra versus sequencing artifacts, controlled simulation benchmarks, and immediately deployable open-source community tools. The conclusions are carefully bounded to match the empirical evidence—explicitly demonstrating that while lab-associated errors distort local lineage tracing and inflate parsimony counts at unlinked sites (in 50 of 54 synthetic error experiments), higher-level clade backbones remain robust across Nextstrain and COG-UK builds. Only minor refinements to the statistical testing of lab associations under phylogenetic clustering and the formal mathematical notation of the "garbage node" split in the Methods are needed.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Apparent recurrent mutations in public SARS-CoV-2 assemblies include systematic lab-specific artifacts caused by primer binding overlap, sequencing adapters, and host read misalignment: [Well Supported].**  
  The 16 flagged lab-associated variants (parsimony $\ge 4$, $>80\%$ single-lab attribution) show a completely different mutational spectrum from genuine viral mutations (only $1/16$ is $C \to U$ vs. $49\%$ genome-wide, $P < 0.0005$, and vs. $84\%$ among non-lab recurrent sites, $P \approx 10^{-7}$), are significantly closer to ARTIC V3 primer binding sites than expected under 10,000 random genome permutations ($P = 0.028$; Figure 3), and have concrete mechanistic explanations (e.g., A24389C/G24390C matching the Oxford Nanopore adapter `CAGCACCTT`, and C22802G tracing to human rRNA read misalignment).

* **Systematic errors distort local phylogenetic relationships and artificially inflate homoplasy counts at other sites across the genome, while leaving deep clade structure largely intact: [Well Supported].**  
  Both the empirical masking experiments (Figures 5–6, 8, 12C) and the 81 synthetic error-addition experiments across Australian and French subsamples (Figure 4) demonstrate that introducing a single recurrent error increases parsimony scores at unlinked genomic sites in 50 of 54 replicates (by 2–44 steps), whereas masking the 16 lab-associated variants eliminates spurious back-mutations (such as at C14408U; Figure 5) and improves the masked alignment log-likelihood by 189 units across 9 of 10 replicate `IQ-TREE 2` runs. At the same time, the authors transparently show via single-site masking controls (Figure 6) and COG-UK tanglegram comparisons ($S_p = 0.12$ across 4,172 tips; Figure 12C) that deep backbone clades remain stable.

* **Genuine, non-lab-associated recurrent mutations are driven primarily by asymmetric $C \to U$ hypermutation in `5'-[U|A]C` contexts rather than positive selection or homopolymer sequencing error: [Well Supported].**  
  Among 50 low-frequency ($<1\%$), bi-allelic, non-lab-associated recurrent sites (parsimony $> 3$), 42 (84%) are $C \to U$ transitions ($P = 3.66 \times 10^{-7}$), which are corroborated across multiple sequencing platforms (81.4%), show no proximity to ARTIC primers ($P = 0.785$), exceed $G \to A$ ($P < 6 \times 10^{-12}$) and $U \to C$ ($P \approx 10^{-10}$) rates on the positive strand, and exhibit higher parsimony recurrence at synonymous sites than non-synonymous sites ($P = 0.0553$; Figure 7B), consistent with host APOBEC-like deamination filtered by purifying selection.

* **The entropy-weighted matching split distance ($D(T, T')$) and `RotTrees` heuristic enable scalable, informative comparisons of clade stability across sequential Nextstrain builds and COG-UK trees: [Well Supported].**  
  The entropy weighting $H(p) = -p \log_2 p - (1-p)\log_2(1-p)$ prioritizes informative internal splits over terminal tip swaps, correlates strongly with classical Robinson-Foulds, Quartet, Path Difference, and Triples metrics (`S3 Text`), and scales to 10,000-tip trees in 20 minutes. Applied to 31 sequential Nextstrain trees (restricted to 468 shared samples), multidimensional scaling and Mantel testing ($r = -0.384, P = 4 \times 10^{-5}$) cleanly capture the temporal drift of clade assignments as sample composition changes.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Account for Phylogenetic Clade Size When Testing Lab-Specific Variant Enrichment
* **WHY:** As noted in the Methods (`Discovery of lab-associated variants`, line 125), applying Fisher's exact test to tip counts treats viral isolates from the same sequencing center as independent and identically distributed. If a single regional reference lab sequences a large local outbreak clade carrying a genuine mutation alongside three singleton occurrences elsewhere (yielding parsimony score 4), the tip-level Fisher's exact test will flag the site with an artificially tiny $p$-value driven by clonal expansion rather than independent recurrent errors.
* **WHAT:** Compute the lab-association proportion and Fisher's exact test across *independent parsimony-inferred mutation events (subclades)* in addition to raw tip counts.
* **HOW:** Using the subclade decomposition already computed by `strain_phylogenetics` (Figure 2B), count the number of independent derived-allele subclades (minimum parsimony origin events) in which $>50\%$ of tips originate from a single lab, and require that a flagged lab be associated with at least 3 or 4 *independent* origin events on the phylogeny rather than $>80\%$ of tips coming predominantly from a single large clade.

#### 2. Reconcile the Formal Mathematical Definition of Matching Split Distance with the "Garbage Node" ($\phi$) Rule
* **WHY:** In the Figure 9B caption (line 41), the text explains that each split in tree $T$ is compared against all splits in $T'$ *"plus a 'garbage node' ($\phi$) containing a null set of leaves... Multiple $T'$ splits can match a $T$ split but the garbage node is given precedence (as is the case in $T$ branch 4)."* However, the formal mathematical definitions in the Methods (`A clade comparison method using branch splits`, lines 137–151) define $c(b, T, T') = \min s(A|B, X|Y)$ strictly over reduced splits $X|Y$ in $T'$ without including the trivial null split $\phi | (A \cup B)$ (for which $s(A|B, \phi | (A \cup B)) = |A|$).
* **WHAT:** Update the equations in the Methods section to explicitly include the null/garbage split $\phi | L$ in the candidate split set of $T'$ so that the mathematical formulas match the algorithm illustrated in Figure 9B–C.
* **HOW:** In the Methods (lines 145–150), define the augmented split set of $T'$ as $\mathcal{S}^*(T') = \mathcal{S}(T') \cup \{ \phi | L, L | \phi \}$, which caps the matching split distance of any clade $A$ at $\min(|A|, |B|)$ when no branch in $T'$ provides a closer match than an empty clade.

#### 3. Provide Sensitivity Curves for the Heuristic Thresholds Used in Lab-Associated and Extremal Site Detection
* **WHY:** The pipeline uses heuristic cutoffs of parsimony score $\ge 4$ and single-lab attribution $\ge 80\%$ (at $p < 0.01$). Readers applying `COVID-19-Lab-Specific-Bias-Filter` to larger datasets need to know how sensitive the flagged site list is to these parameters.
* **WHAT:** Report a brief sensitivity grid showing how many sites are flagged—and what fraction of them are $C \to U$ transitions or overlap ARTIC primers—when varying the parsimony cutoff ($3, 4, 5$) and the single-lab attribution threshold ($60\%, 70\%, 80\%, 90\%$).
* **HOW:** Run `COVID-19-Lab-Specific-Bias-Filter` across the $3 \times 4$ parameter grid on the April 19, 2020 dataset and add a supplementary summary table reporting the total flagged sites, $\% C \to U$, and mean distance to ARTIC primers for each threshold combination.
