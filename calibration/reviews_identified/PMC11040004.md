### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Afonin et al. investigate how extensive nuclear stop-codon reassignment in the trypanosomatid *Blastocrithidia nonstop* (where UAA and UAG are decoded as glutamate by dedicated suppressor $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA}}$ and $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{CUA}}$, and UGA as tryptophan) impacts its kinetoplast (mitochondrial) genome, U-indel RNA editing cascade, and imported translation machinery. Combining deep Illumina sequencing of kDNA, poly(A)-enriched total RNA, kRNA-enriched RNA, and mitochondrial small RNA libraries with `T-Aligner` ORF reconstruction, subcellular Northern blotting, native PAGE Complex I enzymatic assays, and AlphaFold/Swiss-Model structural modeling of the RNA editing substrate-binding complex (RESC), the authors demonstrate that: (1) the *B. nonstop* maxicircle retains the standard protistan mitochondrial genetic code (using UAA and UAG strictly as stop codons across 11 translatable mRNAs); (2) nuclear read-through $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA/CUA}}$ are imported into the mitochondrion rather than excluded; (3) the minicircle repertoire is reduced to 41 classes encoding 42 minicircle guide RNAs (gRNAs) plus 3 maxicircle gRNAs, which suffice to pan-edit *A6*, *COIII*, and *RPS12* without redundancy while lacking gRNAs for all Complex I cryptogenes (*ND3, ND7, ND8, ND9, MURF2, G3, G4*); (4) Complex I NADH dehydrogenase activity is absent, yet Complex I cryptogene transcripts undergo high-level non-productive U-indel editing; and (5) 65 of 71 nuclear-encoded editing/processing proteins are conserved despite harboring frequent $\sim 16$ aa surface-exposed insertions. Raw sequencing reads (NCBI SRA), assembled maxicircle/minicircle/mRNA accessions (OQ909994–OQ910035, OQ911728–OQ911732), and custom `T-Aligner` scripts are publicly available.

This is a thorough, multi-disciplinary study that integrates organellar genomics, gRNA cascade mapping, biochemical fractionation, and structural bioinformatics to resolve a compelling evolutionary question. Its primary limitations are the absence of a randomized sequence null control when attributing 79–86% of non-productive Complex I U-indel reads to off-target binding by the 41 cognate gRNAs under highly permissive alignment thresholds, the lack of formal testing of mitochondrial suppressor tRNA aminoacylation status, and a speculative concluding claim that imported suppressor tRNAs accelerate amino acid divergence in unedited coding regions without causing stop-codon read-through. These issues can be addressed through computational null simulations and targeted textual refinement.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The *B. nonstop* mitochondrial genome is insulated from nuclear UAA/UAG sense recoding, using UAA (8 genes) and UAG (3 genes) exclusively as canonical termination codons across 11 functional mRNAs: [Well Supported].**  
  Polished maxicircle assembly and `T-Aligner` transcript reconstructions confirm that the six unedited genes (*COI, ND1, ND2, ND4, ND5, RPS3*) and five productively edited transcripts (*CYb, COII, COIII, A6, RPS12*) lack internal in-frame UAA/UAG sense codons and terminate at conserved positions using UAA or UAG.

* **Nuclear read-through $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA}}$ and $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{CUA}}$ are imported into the mitochondrion at rates equal to or exceeding canonical $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUC/CUC}}$: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Digitonin fractionation followed by RNase A treatment and Northern blotting across three cell input densities ($2 \times 10^8$, $3 \times 10^8$, and $4 \times 10^8$ cells; Figure 2) shows that the mitochondrial signal percentage $M / (C + M)$ for both suppressor tRNAs (19–37%) consistently exceeds the cytosolic 5.8S rRNA contamination marker (9–15%) and canonical $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUC/CUC}}$ (12–21%). However, the authors do not perform acid-urea PAGE Northern blotting to test whether the mitochondrial pool of $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA/CUA}}$ is aminoacylated (charged with glutamate) in organello, leaving the downstream deactivation mechanism unverified.

* **Loss of functional Complex I in *B. nonstop* results from complete loss of Complex I-specific minicircle gRNAs inside a minimal 41-minicircle repertoire: [Well Supported].**  
  De novo SPAdes assembly across two independent DNA libraries recovers exactly 41 minicircle classes (encoding 42 gRNAs) and 3 maxicircle gRNAs. Forty gRNAs map sequentially with almost zero redundancy onto *A6, COIII*, and *RPS12* (Figure 7), leaving only two orphan gRNAs (classes 40 and 41), which cannot edit the seven Complex I cryptogenes. This genomic and transcriptomic evidence is corroborated by the absence of nuclear Complex I subunits in mass spectrometry data and the absence of the Complex I holoenzyme band in native PAGE NADH dehydrogenase staining (Figure 5).

* **High-level non-productive U-indel editing of Complex I cryptogenes (79–86% of edited reads) is guided by transient non-cognate interactions with the 41 gRNAs of other transcripts: [Fragile].**  
  In the Methods (`Analysis of potential guiding of all editing events`), `findgrna` was run with highly permissive parameters (`--seed_score 20 --seed_length 16 --length 21 --score 25 --gu 17 --mm 4 --anchor 2`), allowing alignments as short as 21 nt containing up to 17 G:U wobble pairs, 4 mismatches, and a 2-nt anchor. Because U-indel edited reads and gRNAs are both strongly compositionally biased (U-rich and A/G-rich, respectively), aligning 41 gRNAs against any U-inserted read under a 2-nt anchor and 17 G:U tolerance has a high probability of producing spurious matches by chance. Without a permutation control (e.g., aligning dinucleotide-shuffled gRNAs to the same Complex I reads), the 79–86% explained fraction is not statistically calibrated above background chance alignment.

* **Frequent $\sim 16$ aa insertions in nuclear-encoded RESC and RECC subunits are structurally accommodated on complex surfaces and flexible loops without disrupting RNA editing: [Well Supported].**  
  AlphaFold v2.3.0 and Swiss-Model superpositions onto the *T. brucei* RESC-A and RESC-B cryo-EM structures (PDB 8FN6; Figure 8) convincingly show that insertions map to unresolved terminal tails, solvent-exposed loops, or large internal cavities, preserving core subunit interfaces and active sites.

* **Mitochondrial import of nuclear read-through tRNAs may accelerate coding sequence divergence and terminal truncations in unedited maxicircle genes (*COI, ND1, ND4, ND5*): [Overstated].**  
  In the final paragraph of the Discussion (line 22 of chunk 2), the authors speculate that incomplete inactivation of imported suppressor $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA/CUA}}$ accelerates amino acid substitution rates in *COI* and *ND1*. Functionally active UAA/UAG suppressor tRNAs can only cause translational stop-codon read-through at UAA/UAG triplets; they do not cause missense substitutions at sense codons throughout the interior of genes that contain zero internal UAA/UAG codons. Elevated sequence divergence in *B. nonstop* mitochondrial genes is much more parsimoniously explained by relaxed purifying selection (following Complex I loss) or elevated mitochondrial mutational pressure (potentially linked to the loss of Ku70/Ku80 DNA repair).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Benchmark Non-Cognate gRNA:Read Alignments Against a Shuffled-Sequence Null Model
* **WHY:** Under the relaxed `findgrna` settings (`--length 21 --gu 17 --mm 4 --anchor 2`), G:U wobble pairing allows A and G in a gRNA to pair with U in an edited mRNA read. Consequently, almost any A/G-rich gRNA can align to U-indel-rich reads by chance, making the raw 79–86% match rate uninterpretable without a false-positive baseline.
* **WHAT:** Compute an empirical false-discovery distribution by aligning 1,000 sets of randomized gRNAs against the same Complex I edited reads (*ND3, ND8, ND9, G3, G4*) and against the rare U-indel reads mapping to unedited genes (*COI, ND1*).
* **HOW:** Generate 1,000 synthetic 41-gRNA repertoires by dinucleotide-shuffling each of the 41 *B. nonstop* gRNAs (preserving exact length and dinucleotide composition), run `findgrna` with the exact same parameters (`--seed_score 20 --seed_length 16 --length 21 --score 25 --gu 17 --mm 4 --anchor 2`), and report the empirical $p$-value and net percentage of Complex I editing events explained above the shuffled null expectation.

#### 2. Quantify Statistical Significance of Mitochondrial Suppressor tRNA Enrichment and Codon Usage Shifts
* **WHY:** Figure 2 presents three biological/input replicates ($2\times 10^8$, $3\times 10^8$, $4\times 10^8$ cells) of Northern blot densitometry as raw percentages without confidence intervals or hypothesis tests comparing suppressor tRNAs against the 5.8S rRNA cytosolic contamination baseline, and Figure 3 flags codon usage differences visually with arrows rather than statistical tests.
* **WHAT:** Report formal paired statistical tests for the Northern blot fractionation ratios and contingency tests for species-specific mitochondrial codon usage.
* **HOW:** Apply a paired one-sided $t$-test (or mixed-effects beta regression across replicates 1–3) testing whether $M/(C+M)$ for $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUA}}$ and $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{CUA}}$ exceeds the paired 5.8S rRNA contamination fraction and canonical $\text{tRNA}^{\text{Glu}}_{\text{UUC/CUC}}$. In Figure 3, perform Fisher's exact tests (with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across codon families) comparing *B. nonstop* synonymous codon counts across the 11 translatable mRNAs against the pooled counts of comparator trypanosomatids.

#### 3. Test Evolutionary Rate Acceleration ($dN/dS$) in Maxicircle Genes Relative to Complex I Loss
* **WHY:** The manuscript observes "coding sequence erosion" and elevated amino acid divergence in *B. nonstop* maxicircle genes (Supplementary Figure S1) and attributes it speculatively to suppressor tRNAs in the Discussion, without testing whether divergence is driven by relaxed purifying selection on respiratory subunits or an elevated synonymous mutation rate.
* **WHAT:** Estimate branch-specific $\omega = dN/dS$ and synonymous substitution rates ($dS$) for *COI, CYb, A6, RPS12*, and the unedited *ND* genes (*ND1, ND2, ND4, ND5*) across the trypanosomatid phylogeny.
* **HOW:** Align the 11 translatable maxicircle genes across *B. nonstop*, *O.ctovani* / *Obscuromonas*, *L. pyrrhocoris*, *L. tarentolae*, *P. serpens*, and *T. brucei*, fit branch models in `IQ-TREE` or `HyPhy` (`aBSREL` / `RELAX`), and compare whether relaxation of purifying selection is restricted to Complex I *ND* genes (which are non-functional pseudogenes at the complex level despite retaining intact ORFs) or also affects Complex III/IV/V subunits (*CYb, COI-III, A6*).

#### 4. Revise the Speculative Mechanism Linking Suppressor tRNAs to Amino Acid Substitution Rates
* **WHY:** In the penultimate paragraph of the Discussion, proposing that imported UAA/UAG suppressor tRNAs accelerate amino acid substitutions in *COI* and *ND1* is biochemically ungrounded, as UAA/UAG tRNAs do not decode sense codons and *ND1/COI* contain no internal UAA/UAG codons.
* **WHAT:** Replace this speculation with the biologically plausible explanation that loss of functional Complex I eliminates purifying selection on maxicircle-encoded *ND1, ND2, ND4,* and *ND5* (explaining their N-terminal truncations, internal indels, and amino acid divergence), while general mitochondrial mutational pressure shapes *COI*.
* **HOW:** Revise the penultimate paragraph of the Discussion (page/chunk 2, line 22) to distinguish between pseudo-genization/relaxed selection on unedited Complex I subunits (*ND1/2/4/5*) and possible C-terminal extension tolerance if occasional stop-codon read-through occurs.
