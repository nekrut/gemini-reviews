### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Williams et al. develop a mathematical population-genetics framework that links somatic $dN/dS$ ratios to the underlying clone size distribution (site frequency spectrum, SFS) to infer the selective advantage (stem-cell self-renewal bias $\Delta$) and stem-cell loss/replacement rate ($r\lambda$) of somatic mutations in normal human tissues. Modelling epithelial homeostasis as a birth–death process of population asymmetry where mutant stem cells divide symmetrically with bias $\Delta$, they derive an analytical expression for "interval $dN/dS$" ($\text{i-}dN/dS$), which integrates expected non-synonymous and synonymous clone size distributions over cumulative clone area intervals $[A_{\min}, A_{\max}]$. Applying this estimator (via `dndscv` and `SSB-dNdS`) to published targeted deep-sequencing datasets from normal human esophagus and normal skin, they estimate patient-level stem-cell turnover rates of $r\lambda = 1.2–5.0\text{ yr}^{-1}$ and construct the somatic distribution of fitness effects (DFE), finding mostly small-effect variants ($\Delta \approx 0.001–0.03$) alongside strong positive selection on `TP53` ($\Delta = 0.057$ for missense, $0.094$ for nonsense in esophagus) and `NOTCH1` ($\Delta = 0.029$ for missense, $0.072$ for nonsense). They further corroborate these inferences by fitting theoretical SFS decay laws across donor ages in Stan (`brms`) and checking for passenger hitchhiking across spatially resolved tissue patches. All simulation (`StemCellModels.jl`) and analysis code (`dnds-clonesize`) are publicly archived with a Snakemake workflow and Singularity container.

This study makes a substantial theoretical and practical contribution by showing that somatic $dN/dS$ is inherently a function of clone frequency and by uniting inter-gene substitution ratios with intra-tissue clone size dynamics. The primary methodological limitations are: (1) the maximum-likelihood fitting procedure treats cumulative $\text{i-}dN/dS$ evaluations across a grid of upper thresholds $f_{\max}$ as independent Gaussian observations when computing Fisher-information confidence intervals, which ignores strong positive autocorrelation across nested intervals and yields unrealistically narrow confidence intervals; (2) filtering gene-level fits by $R^2 > 0.6$ systematically excludes neutral or weakly selected genes ($\Delta \approx 0$, where the theoretical curve is flat and $R^2 \approx 0$) from the DFE; and (3) several equations in the Materials and Methods contain typographical errors. These issues can be resolved directly within the authors' existing codebase by using bootstrap confidence intervals over mutations, removing the $R^2 > 0.6$ filter when estimating the near-neutral DFE bulk, and correcting the equation typos.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Somatic $dN/dS$ is frequency-dependent and its trajectory across clone sizes encodes the stem-cell self-renewal bias $\Delta$ and turnover rate $r\lambda$: [Well Supported].**  
  Equations 1–6 and stochastic birth–death simulations (Figure 2a–c, Figure 2—figure supplement 1) rigorously demonstrate that positively selected variants ($\Delta > 0$) enrich at larger clone sizes according to $g(A) \propto A^{-1} e^{-\rho A / N_\Delta(t)}$, whereas neutral synonymous mutations follow $N_{\Delta=0}(t) = 1 + r\lambda t$ and yield $\text{i-}dN/dS \approx 1$ across all clone areas (Figure 2d).

* **Normal human esophagus and skin exhibit a right-skewed DFE led by large-effect mutations in `TP53` and `NOTCH1`: [Well Supported].**  
  Across donors and two independent $dN/dS$ estimators (`dndscv` and `SSB-dNdS`), `TP53` and `NOTCH1` consistently exhibit the largest self-renewal biases ($\Delta \approx 1\%–9\%$), nonsense mutations confer a 4- to 5-fold stronger proliferative bias than missense mutations, and age-stratified SFS fits (Figure 5b–e, $\log_{10}\text{BF} > 44$ over pure power-law or exponential models) independently confirm faster characteristic clone-size growth for non-synonymous variants in `TP53` and `NOTCH1`.

* **Point estimates of gene-level $\Delta$ within individual patients are determined to within $\pm 0.0001–0.003$ (95% CI): [Overstated].**  
  In the Results, several per-patient, per-gene 95% confidence intervals are extraordinarily narrow—for example, in skin patient `PD18003`, $\Delta = 0.0149$ ($95\%\text{ CI}: 0.0148–0.0150$) for `NOTCH1` missense mutations and $\Delta = 0.0054$ ($95\%\text{ CI}: 0.0051–0.0058$) for `TP53` missense mutations, and in esophagus, $\Delta = 0.094$ ($95\%\text{ CI}: 0.091–0.097$) for `TP53` nonsense mutations. This apparent precision is an artifact of the negative log-likelihood in the "Model fitting" section, which sums squared residuals over the grid of upper integration bounds $f_{\max}$ as if they were independent observations and derives 95% CIs from the Fisher information matrix. Because $\text{i-}dN/dS(f_{\max})$ is a cumulative integral over overlapping nested intervals $[f_{\min}, f_{\max}]$ computed from the same finite set of mutations ($n \ge 8$), consecutive residuals are highly autocorrelated, and increasing the number of evaluation points $f_{\max}$ artificially inflates the Fisher information.

* **Excluding gene fits with $R^2 \le 0.6$ provides an unbiased empirical DFE across genes (Figures 3c–d and 4f): [Moderately Supported].**  
  For a truly neutral or nearly neutral gene ($\Delta \to 0$), the theoretical expectation is a horizontal line $\hat{y}(f_{\max}) = 1$. Because the coefficient of determination $R^2 = 1 - \sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 / \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2$ approaches $0$ (or becomes negative) whenever the true slope is near zero and deviations around $\bar{y}$ are due to sampling noise, restricting downstream DFE histograms to fits with $R^2 > 0.6$ selectively discards genes with small $\Delta$, biasing the empirical DFE away from zero.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Grid-Based Fisher Information Confidence Intervals with Non-Parametric Mutation Bootstrapping
* **WHY:** Evaluating $\text{i-}dN/dS$ on a dense grid of upper cutoffs $f_{\max}$ does not create new independent data points; treating the cumulative grid residuals as independent Gaussian observations in $\text{NLL}(\theta)$ underestimates parameter variance by a factor roughly proportional to the grid density.
* **WHAT:** Compute 95% confidence intervals for $\Delta$ and $r\lambda$ by bootstrapping the underlying mutations (or tissue biopsies) rather than inverting the grid-level Fisher information matrix.
* **HOW:** For each patient and gene (with $n \ge 8$ mutations), draw $B = 1,000$ bootstrap resamples of the observed mutations (or resample the ~90 tissue patches per donor with replacement), recompute the `dndscv` $\text{i-}dN/dS(f_{\max})$ trajectory and least-squares/MLE fit $(\hat{\Delta}^*, \widehat{r\lambda}^*)$ on each bootstrap replicate, and report the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles as the 95% confidence intervals in the text and Figures 2–4.

#### 2. Remove the $R^2 > 0.6$ Selection Filter When Constructing the Empirical DFE
* **WHY:** Conditioning inclusion in Figure 3a–d and Figure 4f on $R^2 > 0.6$ truncates the lower tail of the DFE because flat trajectories ($\Delta \approx 0$) have near-zero explained variance $R^2$ even when the model fits the data with small residual root-mean-square error (RMSE).
* **WHAT:** Replace the $R^2 > 0.6$ filter with a sample-size threshold ($n \ge 8$ mutations) and an absolute goodness-of-fit metric (such as normalized RMSE or parametric bootstrap $p$-value) that does not penalize flat ($\Delta \approx 0$) curves.
* **HOW:** Re-generate Figures 3a–d and 4f including all gene-patient combinations with $\ge 8$ mutations (or filtering on $\text{RMSE} < \tau$ rather than $R^2 > 0.6$) and compare the resulting DFE shape and mean $\Delta$ against the $R^2$-filtered version.

#### 3. Account for Spatial Truncation of Large Clones Across Finite $2\text{ mm}^2$ Biopsies
* **WHY:** In the esophagus dataset, clone area was approximated by summing VAF $\times 2\text{ mm}^2$ across adjacent biopsies when a mutation spanned multiple punches, whereas in the skin dataset biopsies were not contiguous. When clones approach or exceed the biopsy area ($2\text{ mm}^2$), edge truncation distorts the upper tail of the observed clone size distribution.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the sensitivity of inferred $\Delta$ and $r\lambda$ to the upper area cutoff $A_{\max}$ and biopsy boundary truncation.
* **HOW:** Using the spatial 2D lattice or birth–death simulation in `StemCellModels.jl`, simulate circular clones growing across a $2\text{ mm}^2$ biopsy window, apply the exact VAF censoring/summing rule used in the esophagus and skin datasets, and verify whether edge truncation biases $\hat{\Delta}$ at high $\Delta$ values ($\Delta > 0.05$).

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Several equations in the Materials and Methods contain typographical errors that conflict with the actual code implementation.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Typo in the denominator of the closed-form $\text{i-}dN/dS$ equation (Materials and methods, line 302):** The denominator currently reads $\left[\text{Ei}\left(-\frac{\rho A_{\max}}{N(t)}\right) - \text{Ei}\left(-\frac{\rho A_{\max}}{N(t)}\right) + \dots\right]$, where the first two terms cancel identically to zero because $A_{\max}$ appears in both. Change the second term in the denominator to $\text{Ei}\left(-\frac{\rho A_{\min}}{N(t)}\right)$.
  2. **Sign and limit convention in the definition of $\text{Ei}(x)$ (line 306):** Line 306 defines $\text{Ei}(x) = -\int_x^\infty \frac{e^{-n}}{n} dn$ and evaluates $\text{Ei}(-\rho A / N)$ at negative arguments. Standard exponential integral notation is $\text{Ei}(x) = -\int_{-x}^\infty \frac{e^{-t}}{t} dt = \int_{-\infty}^x \frac{e^t}{t} dt$ for $x < 0$ (or $E_1(z) = \int_z^\infty \frac{e^{-t}}{t} dt$ for $z > 0$). Also check the signs of the Euler–Maclaurin boundary terms $+\frac{1}{2}(\dots)$ relative to $\text{Ei}(-\rho A_{\max}/N) - \text{Ei}(-\rho A_{\min}/N)$ so that the numerator and denominator have consistent positive signs.
  3. **Corrupted parameter definitions, priors, and competing model equations in "Fitting site frequency spectra" (lines 364–391):**
     - Line 364 writes *"With the parameters $\alpha = \frac{\mu n_0}{r\lambda \rho}$, and $\alpha = \mu n_0$ to be estimated"*; change the second definition to $\beta = \log(N(t)/\rho)$ (matching line 232).
     - Lines 368–375 display $\beta = \log(N(t))$ and $A \sim \text{Normal}(5,2)$ instead of the prior distributions on $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (since $A$ is the observed clone area, not a parameter!). Replace with the exact `brms` priors used for $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
     - Lines 384 and 390 list the two competing SFS models identically as $C = \frac{\alpha}{A}$ and $C = \frac{\alpha}{A}$. Change the second equation (line 390) to the exponential model $C = \alpha e^{-A/\beta}$ (matching line 244).
  4. **Misplaced reference for esophageal stem-cell density (Results, line 170):** Line 170 cites `(Eyre-Walker and Keightley, 2007)` for *"assuming 5000 stem cells per $\text{mm}^2$ tissue"*. Replace with `(Hall et al., 2019)` (as cited in line 306) or `(Martincorena et al., 2018)`.
