### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Viana et al. report the discovery, genomic characterization, phylodynamics, evolutionary selection profile, and early epidemic growth advantage of the SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variant (B.1.1.529; sublineages BA.1, BA.2, and BA.3) in South Africa and Botswana in November–December 2021. Combining national COVID-19 case surveillance, TaqPath S-gene target failure (SGTF) screening, and multi-platform whole-genome sequencing (Illumina COVIDSeq, Oxford Nanopore Midnight, and Ion Torrent Genexus) with time-calibrated Bayesian coalescent and birth-death skyline (`BDSKY`) phylodynamics ($n = 553$ southern African BA.1 genomes), recombination screening (`3SEQ`, `GARD`, `RDP5`), codon-based selection analyses (`BUSTED`, `RELAX`, `MEME`, `FADE`, `FEL`, `BGM` via HyPhy/RASCL), and multinomial logistic growth modeling ($n = 805$ Gauteng sequences), the authors estimate a BA.1 TMRCA of 9 October 2021 (95% HPD: 30 September–20 October), an effective reproduction number $R_e = 2.79$ (95% HPD: 2.60–2.97; Gauteng $R_e = 3.61$–$3.86$), and a daily growth advantage of $0.24\text{ d}^{-1}$ (95% CI: 0.16–0.33; 5.4-fold weekly increase) over Delta. Consensus genomes are deposited in GISAID (Supplementary Table 1) and raw reads in NCBI SRA (PRJNA784038).

This is a rigorous, well-executed real-time genomic epidemiology study that transparently documents sequencing artifacts (such as Ion Torrent homopolymer frameshifts and RBD amplicon primer dropouts across K417N–G446S), conducts extensive sensitivity analyses across clock rates, infectious periods, and sampling epochs in BEAST/BEAST2, and explicitly bounds the non-identifiability between intrinsic transmissibility ($\tau$) and immune evasion ($\epsilon$) across levels of prior population immunity ($\Omega$). Minor methodological caveats include: (1) fixing the tree topology to the coalescent MCC tree during the `BDSKY` MCMC runs rather than jointly integrating over topological uncertainty in the low-diversity early BA.1 expansion, (2) running selection analyses (`MEME`/`BUSTED`) on very small BA.2 ($n \approx 3$) and BA.3 ($n \approx 2.5$) alignments and interpreting single early sub-clade polymorphisms (e.g., R346K, L452R, A701V) within BA.1 without ruling out residual amplicon/reference-fill artifacts, and (3) a minor mismatch between text and count in the `BUSTED` gene list ("11 genes or ORFs... In all ten genes"). These points can be clarified via brief methodological notes and text corrections.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Omicron (BA.1) emerged from the B.1.1 clade with a TMRCA in early-to-mid October 2021 and expanded rapidly in southern Africa ($R_e \approx 2.8$ overall, $3.6$–$3.9$ in Gauteng; doubling time 2.8–5.1 days): [Well Supported].**  
  The phylogenetic placement and TMRCA estimates (9 October 2021 under the exponential coalescent model, 95% HPD: 30 Sep–20 Oct; 10–11 October under `BDSKY`) are consistent across BEAST v1.10.4 coalescent and BEAST2 v2.5.2 3-epoch and 4-epoch birth-death skyline models (`Extended Data Tables 1, 4, 5`). Furthermore, the authors perform systematic sensitivity analyses varying the molecular clock rate ($0.3 \times 10^{-3}$ to $1.2 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s.s.y.}$) and mean infectious duration (7, 10, and 14 days), demonstrating that TMRCA and $R_e$ inferences are stable. One technical limitation noted in Methods is that for the full South Africa + Botswana `BDSKY` analysis ($n = 552$), the tree topology was held fixed to the coalescent MCC starting tree, which understates posterior variance on internal node heights in a star-like early outbreak phylogeny.

* **Omicron achieved a daily growth advantage of $\rho = 0.24\text{ d}^{-1}$ (95% CI: 0.16–0.33) over Delta in Gauteng, driven substantially by partial evasion of high population immunity ($\Omega \ge 0.6$): [Well Supported].**  
  The growth rate advantage is corroborated by three independent streams: genomic surveillance proportions in Gauteng ($n = 805$, Fig. 4a), rapid nationwide replacement of S-gene positive tests by TaqPath SGTF (~90% by late November, Fig. 1c), and resurgent case incidence across South Africa and Botswana (Fig. 1, Extended Data Figs. 1–2). The mechanistic decomposition $\rho = (1+\tau)\beta(S + \epsilon(1-S)) - \beta S$ (Fig. 4b–e) is appropriately framed: rather than claiming point identifiability of intrinsic transmissibility ($\tau$) versus immune escape ($\epsilon$), the authors propagate uncertainty over 10,000 Monte Carlo draws of $\rho$, generation time $D \sim \mathcal{N}(5.2, 0.8^2)$, and baseline Delta $R_w \in [0.78, 0.85]$ across $\Omega \in \{0.4, 0.6, 0.8\}$, explicitly acknowledging potential upward bias from targeted early SGTF sequencing.

* **Adaptive evolution (positive selection) drove the divergence of the ancestral Omicron branch and ongoing diversification within BA.1: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Gene-wide `BUSTED` ($P < 0.0001$ on Spike and 7 other ORFs; $\omega > 5$ on $\le 6\%$ of branch-site combinations) and `RELAX` ($K = 2.1, P < 0.0001$ on Spike) provide strong statistical evidence for episodic positive selection along the stem branch separating BA.1 from B.1.1. However, two caveats apply to the finer-scale selection claims: first, for BA.2 and BA.3, alignments were downsampled to a median of only 3 and 2.5 unique sequences per gene, where asymptotic likelihood ratio approximations for branch-site models have high variance; second, for the non-lineage-defining sites reported as positively selected *within* BA.1 after its MRCA (such as L452R in Delta and A701V in Beta), early Omicron sequencing during the Delta-to-Omicron transition experienced occasional Delta/Omicron co-infections, laboratory cross-contamination, or reference-fill pipelines, so calling convergent positive selection within early December 2021 BA.1 warrants caution.

* **Weak/inconclusive evidence for recombination within the Spike NTD among BA.1, BA.2, and BA.3, and no evidence that the MRCA of BA.1/BA.2/BA.3 was a recombinant of other circulating lineages: [Well Supported].**  
  The authors test recombination across a stratified global panel ($n = 221$) plus BA.1/BA.2/BA.3 representatives using `3SEQ` ($P = 0.03$), `GARD` ($\Delta\text{c-AIC} = 20$), and `RDP5` (`GENECONV` $P = 0.027$; `RDP` $P = 0.006$) and inspect region-specific RAxML phylogenies bounded by breakpoints 21690 and 22198 (`Extended Data Fig. 6`). They accurately conclude that bootstrap support in the short 509-nt NTD segment is too weak to resolve donor/recipient polarity and explicitly note the power limits of recombination detectors when <5 contiguous polymorphic sites are exchanged or when serial overlapping recombination occurs.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Allow Joint Topological Sampling in the `BDSKY` Phylodynamic Analysis
* **WHY:** In the Methods section, the authors state that for the full South Africa and Botswana `BDSKY` MCMC analysis ($n = 552$), "the maximum clade credibility tree from the exponential growth coalescent model was used as the starting tree. We kept the tree topology fixed, estimating only internal node heights." In a rapidly expanding early outbreak with low sequence divergence (many polytomies), fixing a single resolved point-estimate topology artificially narrows the 95% HPD intervals of $R_e$ and the doubling time.
* **WHAT:** Verify that the `BDSKY` $R_e$ and doubling-time 95% HPD intervals hold when tree topology operators (subtree slide, narrow/wide exchange, Wilson-Balding) are active during the BEAST2 MCMC run.
* **HOW:** Re-run the 4-epoch `BDSKY` XML configuration on the $n = 552$ alignment (or on the $n = 277$ Gauteng alignment if computational mixing is slow) with standard BEAST2 tree-topology operators enabled, and compare the resulting posterior 95% HPDs for $R_e$ and TMRCA against the fixed-topology values in `Extended Data Table 5`.

#### 2. Quantify Sensitivity of the Multinomial Growth Advantage ($\rho$) to Targeted SGTF Enrichment
* **WHY:** As noted in the Methods ("SARS-CoV-2 sampling") and Limitations, routine surveillance in mid-November 2021 was supplemented with targeted sequencing of TaqPath SGTF specimens in Tshwane (Gauteng), which enriches for BA.1 relative to Delta during the initial take-off phase and can bias the multinomial logistic regression slope upward.
* **WHAT:** Compare the genome-based growth advantage estimate ($\rho = 0.24\text{ d}^{-1}$ on $n = 805$ GISAID sequences) against an estimate restricted to randomly sampled routine surveillance sequences and against the unbiased population-level TaqPath SGTF proportion trajectory.
* **HOW:** Fit the same logistic growth model (`nnet::multinom`) to (a) the subset of Gauteng sequences flagged as routine random surveillance (excluding targeted SGTF/outbreak-enriched batches) and (b) the daily/weekly National Health Laboratory Service (NHLS) and Lancet Laboratories TaqPath SGTF counts in Gauteng (shown in Fig. 1c), and report both growth-rate estimates alongside $\rho = 0.24\text{ d}^{-1}$.

#### 3. Verify Raw Read Support for Intra-BA.1 Convergent Sites (R346K, L452R, A701V)
* **WHY:** Early BA.1 sequences deposited in GISAID before 20 December 2021 occasionally contained Delta-characteristic alleles (such as L452R) due to Delta/Omicron co-infection, index hopping on multiplexed plates, or bioinformatic consensus calling in low-depth amplicons.
* **WHAT:** Confirm that the `MEME`/`FADE` positive selection signals at Spike codons 346, 452, and 701 within BA.1 reflect genuine de novo mutations rather than co-infection or sequencing artifacts.
* **HOW:** Inspect the raw `.bam` read alignments (available under BioProject `PRJNA784038`) for the specific southern African BA.1 isolates carrying L452R, R346K, and A701V to verify high read depth, absence of heterozygosity at Delta-defining sites outside the RBD, and phylogenetic clustering of those tips.

* **WHY:** A few minor numerical inconsistencies appear between sentences in the main text.
* **WHAT & HOW:**

In all **ten** genes, this selection was strong..." Change "In all ten genes" to "In all 11 genes" (or clarify which 10 of the 11 genes had $\omega > 5$).
  2. **Breakpoint vs. alignment length in `Extended Data Fig. 6` caption (line 86):** The main text (line 52) gives the NTD recombination segment as genome coordinates `21690–22198` ($509\text{ nt}$), whereas the caption to `Extended Data Fig. 6` refers to "The a 897nt region 2 segment (encoding the N-terminal domain of spike)". Reconcile the coordinate range (`21690–22198`) with the stated segment length ($509\text{ nt}$ vs. $897\text{ nt}$) and remove the stray article in "The a 897nt".
