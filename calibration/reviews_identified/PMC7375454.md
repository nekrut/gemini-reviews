# Review of PMC7375454

**Manuscript Title:** SARS-CoV-2 genomic variations associated with mortality rate of COVID-19.  
**Authors:** Yukari Toyoshima, Kensaku Nemoto, Shinji Matsumoto, Yusuke Nakamura,uma Kiyotani  
**Journal / Preprint:** Journal of Human Genetics (2020) | DOI: 10.1038/s10038-020-0808-9  
**Overall Score:** **3.1 / 10.0** (Flawed / Overstated)  
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.7/3.5 | Claims: 0.7/2.5 | Biology: 0.7/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.0/2.0

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Toyoshima et al. analyze 12,343 SARS-CoV-2 genome sequences from GISAID (as of May 7, 2020) across 28 countries (each with $>30$ sequences) and 17 US states to test whether viral amino acid variant frequencies, national BCG vaccination policies, and population-level HLA allele frequencies correlate with country-level COVID-19 case fatality rates (CFR, computed as cumulative deaths divided by cumulative confirmed cases from Worldometer on May 7, 2020 for countries and July 3, 2020 for US states). Using hierarchical clustering on 16 common mutations and univariate Pearson correlations, they report that Spike D614G and ORF1ab P4715L (which are in near-complete linkage disequilibrium, $r^2 = 0.98, D' = 1.00$) positively correlate with national CFR ($r = 0.43, P = 0.022$ and $r = 0.41, P = 0.029$), that BCG-vaccinating countries have lower CFR ($4.1\%$ vs. $8.1\%, P = 0.031$), and that national frequencies of HLA-A\*11:01 ($r = -0.61, P = 0.0031$) and HLA-B\*54:01 ($r = -0.60, P = 0.017$) negatively correlate with CFR before losing significance when adjusted for S 614G frequency. Summary tables are included in the supplement, though custom analysis scripts are not deposited.

While cataloging global clade frequencies and linkage disequilibrium between S D614G and ORF1ab P4715L is valid descriptively, the study's core epidemiological and immunological conclusions rest on severe ecological fallacy, unadjusted spatio-temporal confounding, uncalibrated multiple hypothesis testing, and a direct contradiction between the authors' in silico HLA binding predictions and their proposed immune escape narrative. Because the G clade (D614G / P4715L) founded the early European and East Coast US epidemics—where overwhelmed hospitals, older demographics, and restricted diagnostic testing inflated crude CFRs during March–May 2020—correlating aggregate country-level variant prevalence with crude CFR confounds viral genotype with regional testing denominators and epidemic wave timing. Resolving these flaws requires patient-level clinical outcome data (or at minimum, multivariable hierarchical models adjusting for testing rates, age structure, and epidemic phase) and reframing the manuscript away from causal claims about viral lethality and BCG/HLA protection.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **ORF1ab P4715L and Spike D614G are in strong linkage disequilibrium ($r^2 = 0.98, D' = 1.00$) and distinguish Asian sequences (Cluster 1) from European and American sequences (Clusters 2 and 3) in early 2020: [Well Supported].**  
  The haplotype analysis (`Haploview`) and hierarchical clustering accurately reflect the well-established phylogenetic structure of early 2020 SARS-CoV-2, where the B.1 lineage carrying both C14408T (ORF1ab P4715L) and A23403G (S D614G) expanded rapidly in Europe and North America while ancestral lineages predominated in early Asian sampling.

* **Higher country- and state-level frequencies of S 614G and ORF1ab 4715L are associated with higher COVID-19 fatality rates ($r = 0.43, P = 0.022$ across 28 countries; $r = 0.49, P = 0.047$ across 17 US states): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This ecological correlation suffers from four compounding flaws:
  1. *Denominator ascertainment bias:* Crude CFR (confirmed deaths / confirmed cases on May 7, 2020) varies by an order of magnitude across countries primarily due to per-capita testing capacity and testing criteria (e.g., hospital-only testing in Western Europe in March–April 2020 vs. broad community contact tracing in South Korea, Singapore, and Australia).
  2. *Spatio-temporal pseudoreplication:* Countries are not independent trials; neighboring European countries shared both the B.1 (614G/4715L) founder event and synchronous healthcare saturation.
  3. *Lack of multiple-testing correction:* Screening 16 common mutations (selected from 131 variants $>10\%$ frequency) across multiple outcomes yields nominal $P = 0.022$ and $P = 0.029$, which do not survive Benjamini-Hochberg or Bonferroni correction ($0.05 / 16 = 0.0031$).
  4. *Temporal data mismatch in the US state analysis:* In the Methods (lines 25 and 35), viral mutation frequencies were calculated from GISAID sequences up to May 7, 2020, whereas US state case and death counts were extracted nearly two months later on July 3, 2020, violating temporal alignment.

* **Routine national BCG vaccination protects populations from SARS-CoV-2 infection and mortality, while S 614G / ORF1ab 4715L variants may escape BCG-potentiated innate immunity: [Overstated].**  
  Comparing crude CFR and cases per million between 11 BCG+ and 17 BCG- countries ($4.1\%$ vs. $8.1\%, P = 0.031$) without controlling for population age structure, per-capita testing, GDP/healthcare reporting, or geographic region conflates East Asian/developing-nation BCG policies with early border controls and younger demographics. Moreover, the claim that "ORF1ab 4715L-type and S protein 614G-type SARS-CoV-2 variants may escape from the [BCG innate] immune response" is based on a non-significant correlation in $N = 11$ countries ($r = 0.54, P = 0.090$; Fig. 4b) and is biologically incoherent, as trained innate immunity from BCG is non-specific and does not recognize single amino acid point substitutions in viral RdRp or Spike.

* **Population frequencies of HLA-A\*11:01, HLA-A\*02:06, and HLA-B\*54:01 explain international differences in COVID-19 susceptibility and mortality via altered binding to P4715L and D614G epitopes: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, HLA-A\*11:01 and HLA-B\*54:01 are high-frequency East Asian alleles; correlating their national frequencies across $N = 15–21$ countries with May 2020 COVID-19 CFR simply rediscovers the geographic contrast between East Asia (low early CFR, high HLA-A\*11:01 / B\*54:01, low 614G) and Europe (high early CFR, low HLA-A\*11:01 / B\*54:01, high 614G). The authors' own multiple regression confirms that when adjusted for S 614G frequency, all three HLA correlations vanish ($P = 0.13, 0.73,$ and $0.45$). Second, the epitope binding predictions in the Discussion (line 62) directly contradict a viral escape hypothesis: the mutant 4715L peptides are predicted to bind *more strongly* (11, 12, and 23 nM) to HLA-B\*07:02, HLA-B\*54:01, and HLA-A\*11:01 than the wild-type P4715 peptides (44, 41, and 45 nM), and S D614G does not change binding affinity to HLA-A\*02:06. Higher affinity of the mutant peptide would enhance, not evade, CD8+ T-cell presentation.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Univariate Ecological Correlations with Multivariable Regression Controlling for Testing, Age, and Geography
* **WHY:** Unadjusted country-level Pearson correlations between crude CFR and viral clade frequency confound viral genetics with testing intensity, demographic age structure, and continent-level founder effects.
* **WHAT:** Fit a multivariable regression model (and spatial/region-stratified mixed model) predicting CFR and mortality per million while controlling for tests per thousand population, median age (or % population $\ge 65$), days since epidemic onset, and continental region.
* **HOW:** Extract country-level tests per million and % population aged $\ge 65$ from Worldometer / UN demographic tables for the exact same date (May 7, 2020), and run a generalized linear model (`glm` with quasibinomial link for CFR or negative binomial for deaths) in R including S 614G frequency, BCG status, % aged $\ge 65$, log(tests per million), and geographic region as covariates. Report the partial regression coefficients and adjusted $p$-values.

#### 2. Synchronize Collection Dates for US State Viral Sequences and Epidemiological Outcomes
* **WHY:** Pairing GISAID viral frequencies from May 7, 2020 with US state cumulative deaths from July 3, 2020 introduces a 57-day mismatch during which both clade frequencies and case ascertainment shifted substantially across Western, Central, and Eastern US states.
* **WHAT:** Align the epidemiological cutoff date for the 17 US states to May 7, 2020 (or update the GISAID sequence dataset to July 3, 2020) and adjust for state-level testing rates and epidemic onset dates.
* **HOW:** Re-compute Figure 3a–c using archived May 7, 2020 state-level case and death counts (e.g., from the COVID Tracking Project or NYT/JHU state repositories) matched to the May 7, 2020 GISAID cutoff.

#### 3. Apply Formal Multiple-Testing Corrections Across All Screened Mutations and HLA Alleles
* **WHY:** Testing 16 (or 131) viral mutations and multiple HLA alleles across several epidemiological metrics without family-wise or false-discovery rate control guarantees false-positive findings at nominal $\alpha = 0.05$.
* **WHAT:** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across all evaluated mutation-fatality and HLA-fatality correlation tests.
* **HOW:** Use `p.adjust(p_values, method = "BH")` in R across the full set of tested mutations and candidate HLA alleles and report both raw $P$ and FDR $Q$ values in the main text and Figures 2–5.

#### 4. Reconcile the Contradiction Between Predicted HLA Epitope Binding Affinities and Virulence Claims
* **WHY:** The manuscript suggests that S 614G and ORF1ab 4715L increase mortality and escape host immunity, yet the authors' own NetMHCpan predictions show that 4715L *improves* MHC Class I binding affinity 2- to 4-fold (from 41–45 nM down to 11–23 nM) and D614G has no effect on binding affinity.
* **WHAT:** Remove the unsupported claim that 614G/4715L escape BCG innate immunity or HLA presentation, and explicitly state in the Results and Abstract that HLA associations with CFR are non-significant after adjusting for geographic clade distribution ($P = 0.13–0.73$).
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract (lines 7–8), Results (lines 51–53), and Discussion (line 62) to state that: (i) HLA allele frequency correlations with CFR are confounded by the East-West geographic gradient of the D614G clade and are not statistically significant in multivariable analysis; and (ii) 4715L does not reduce predicted HLA Class I binding.
