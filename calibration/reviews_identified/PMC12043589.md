# Critical Peer Review: Comprehensive transcriptomic analysis integrating bulk and single-cell RNA-seq with machine learning to identify and validate mitochondrial unfolded protein response biomarkers in patients with ischemic stroke (PMC12043589)

**Overall Score:** **3.3 / 10.0** (Flawed / Overstated)
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.8/3.5 | Claims: 0.7/2.5 | Biology: 0.7/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.1/2.0

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis
Zhang et al. apply differential expression analysis, single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (`ssGSEA`), Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (`WGCNA`), LASSO, Random Forest (`RF`), single-cell RNA-seq (`scRNA-seq`), and reverse-transcription quantitative PCR (`RT-qPCR`) to nominate `MCEMP1`, `CACNA1E`, and `CLEC4D` as "mitochondrial unfolded protein response (`UPRmt`) biomarkers" for ischemic stroke (`IS`). The manuscript suffers from fundamental biological and statistical flaws across multiple analytical pillars:
1. **Conceptual and biological circularity in defining "UPRmt-related genes" (`UPRmt-RGs`):** None of the three nominated genes (`CLEC4D`, `MCEMP1`, `CACNA1E`) encode mitochondrial proteins or participate in the mitochondrial unfolded protein response (`HSPD1`, `HSPE1`, `LONP1`, `CLPP`, `ATF5`, `SATB1`, etc.). Instead, `CLEC4D` (Dectin-3/MCL) is a plasma-membrane C-type lectin pattern-recognition receptor, `MCEMP1` is a mast cell/myeloid transmembrane protein, and `CACNA1E` is a voltage-gated $\text{Ca}_\text{v}2.3$ plasma-membrane calcium channel subunit. They were labeled `UPRmt-RGs` solely because they fell into the 1,243-gene WGCNA "turquoise module" (`r = -0.58`) that correlated negatively with `UPRmt` `ssGSEA` scores in bulk whole blood (`GSE58294`)—an artifact of post-stroke peripheral neutrophilia diluting lymphocyte/mitochondrial RNA in unfractionated blood.
2. **Severe data leakage and overfitting in machine learning and nomogram construction:** All feature discovery (`limma` DEGs, `WGCNA` module selection, `LASSO` $\lambda = 0.017$, and `RF` top-8 intersection) and the final 3-gene clinical nomogram (`AUC = 0.989`, calibration, and Decision Curve Analysis) were trained and evaluated on the *exact same* 92 samples of `GSE58294` (69 IS, 23 controls) without nested cross-validation or an independent hold-out test set for the composite model.
3. **Failure of experimental RT-qPCR validation for two of the three claimed biomarkers:** In the authors' own clinical cohort ($n = 21$ IS patients, $n = 26$ controls; `Figure 5`), two of the three biomarkers (`CACNA1E` and `MCEMP1`) showed **no statistically significant difference** (`ns`, $P > 0.05$; Section 3.4), directly contradicting the Abstract's claim that "`MCEMP1`, `CACNA1E`, and `CLEC4D` were identified as biomarkers and subsequently validated by RT-qPCR."

## 2. Granular Claims & Evidence Audit

* **Claim 1:** *"Subsequently, MCEMP1, CACNA1E, and CLEC4D were identified as biomarkers and subsequently validated by RT-qPCR. A nomogram was then established based on these biomarkers, demonstrating robust predictive utility for IS."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Methodologically Flawed]**
  * **Critique:** This core claim fails on both experimental and statistical grounds:
    1. **Direct contradiction with Figure 5 / Section 3.4:** In the human peripheral blood `RT-qPCR` validation cohort (21 IS vs. 26 controls), Section 3.4 explicitly admits that while `CLEC4D` was significantly elevated ($P < 0.05$), *"Noticeably, the expression levels of CACNA1E and MCEMP1 were higher in the IS group compared to the control group, although the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05) (Figure 5)."* Retaining `CACNA1E` and `MCEMP1` as "validated by RT-qPCR" in the Abstract and combining them into the clinical nomogram after they failed independent clinical validation is invalid.
    2. **Complete in-sample circularity in the nomogram (`Figure 4D–G`):** The 17 candidate genes were selected using all 92 samples of `GSE58294` via `limma` (`|log2FC| > 1.5`) and `WGCNA`. `LASSO` and `RF` were then fit to those same 92 samples to pick 3 genes, and the logistic nomogram (`rms` package) was fit and evaluated (`AUC = 0.989`) on those exact same 92 samples without any out-of-sample testing of the multivariable linear predictor on `GSE16561`. Furthermore, `GSE58294` comprises 69 cardioembolic stroke blood samples drawn serially at *3, 5, and 24 hours* post-stroke from the same 23 patients ($23 \times 3 = 69$ samples) vs. 23 controls; treating 3 longitudinal draws per patient as 69 independent patients violates sample independence and inflates degrees of freedom and `AUC`.

* **Claim 2:** *"We identified 42 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and 17 differential UPRmt-RGs... Ultimately, three biomarkers (MCEMP1, CACNA1E, and CLEC4D) were identified, which were involved in signaling pathways such as inflammatory response and TNFα signaling via NF-κB."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Overstated]**
  * **Critique:** Calling `MCEMP1`, `CACNA1E`, and `CLEC4D` "mitochondrial unfolded protein response biomarkers" (`differential UPRmt-RGs`) is a classic bioinformatic reification fallacy. The authors started with 35 curated `UPRmt` genes from the literature (`Supplementary Table S1`), computed an `ssGSEA` score in bulk whole blood (`GSE58294`), found that `UPRmt` scores were *lower* in IS than controls (`Figure 1B`), and then selected the 1,243-gene WGCNA "turquoise module" because it correlated negatively ($r = -0.58$) with the `UPRmt` score (`Figure 1E–F`). In acute ischemic stroke, peripheral blood undergoes massive neutrophil demargination and lymphopenia within hours (`Figure 6A–B`). Because neutrophils have very low mitochondrial density compared to lymphocytes, bulk blood `UPRmt` transcript scores drop as neutrophil fraction rises, causing every canonical neutrophil/myeloid surface marker (`CLEC4D`, `MCEMP1`, `S100A8/A9`, `MMP9`) to anti-correlate with `UPRmt` `ssGSEA` scores and cluster into the turquoise module. None of the 17 intersected genes are actual `UPRmt` components; they are general markers of post-stroke peripheral neutrophilia.

* **Claim 3:** *"Single-cell profiling revealed elevated CLEC4D expression in neutrophils and macrophages from IS mice, and virtual knockout analysis implicated its role in immune and inflammatory regulation."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Fragile]**
  * **Critique:** The single-cell analysis switches organisms from human peripheral blood (`GSE58294`, `GSE16561`) to mouse middle cerebral artery occlusion (`MCAO`) models (`GSE225948`: mouse peripheral blood, $n = 4$ MCAO vs. $n = 4$ sham; `GSE154396`: mouse brain CD45+ immune cells, $n = 4$ MCAO vs. $n = 2$ sham). First, differential expression ($P < 0.0001$, `Figure 7E`, `Figure 9A`) is computed via cell-level Wilcoxon tests across thousands of individual cells (`n = 11,488` blood cells; `n = 12,359` brain cells) rather than donor-level pseudobulk across the $n = 4$ vs. $n = 2$ biological replicates (pseudoreplication). Second, Section 2.10 states that cell-cell communication in mouse datasets `GSE225948` and `GSE154396` was analyzed using `CellPhoneDB` (`v1.6.1`), which is a human receptor-ligand database, without detailing ortholog mapping or filtering. Third, `scTenifoldKnk` "virtual knockout" is purely an in silico perturbation of a single-cell gene regulatory network covariance matrix (`100` manifold dimensions), not an experimental perturbation, so its output (`146` differentially projected genes) simply reflects genes co-expressed with `Clec4d` in activated mouse neutrophils.

* **Claim 4:** *"In addition, the immune infiltration of 63 immune cell types in the IS and control samples of GSE58294 was evaluated through the xCell algorithm... Among the 32 differentially infiltrated immune cells, 16 were up-regulated in IS, including astrocytes, macrophages, M1 macrophages, and monocytes, whereas 16 were down-regulated, including B cells, CD4+ memory T cells, CD4+ T cells, and CD8+ T cells."*
  * **Evidence & Audit:** **[Methodologically Flawed]**
  * **Critique:** `GSE58294` is a **peripheral whole-blood** microarray dataset, yet the authors ran `xCell` with all 64 default signatures without tissue-appropriate filtering and report significant "infiltration" of **astrocytes, chondrocytes, osteoblasts, and neurons** in peripheral blood (`Figure 6A–B`, Section 3.6, line 231). Reporting that "astrocytes" are significantly upregulated immune cells infiltrating peripheral blood (`Figure 6B`) demonstrates uncritical execution of deconvolution software without biological quality control.

## 3. Concrete Recommendations for Action

1. **Remove the misleading "UPRmt biomarker" label and account for cell-type composition confounding:**
   * Acknowledge explicitly that `CLEC4D`, `MCEMP1`, and `CACNA1E` are myeloid/membrane genes rather than mitochondrial `UPRmt` components, and re-run the bulk differential expression and `ssGSEA` analyses in `GSE58294` and `GSE16561` while adjusting for estimated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratios (`NLR`) to test whether `UPRmt` genes are intrinsically dysregulated per cell or merely diluted by post-stroke neutrophilia.
2. **Account for repeated measures in `GSE58294` and evaluate multivariable models out-of-sample:**
   * Model patient ID as a random effect (or stratify by 3 h, 5 h, and 24 h timepoints so each patient appears once per analysis) in `GSE58294`, freeze the multivariable logistic/nomogram coefficients on `GSE58294`, and report the external validation `AUC`, calibration slope, and Brier score on `GSE16561`.
3. **Revise claims to match the negative `RT-qPCR` results and remove non-hematopoietic `xCell` signatures:**
   * Remove `CACNA1E` and `MCEMP1` from the set of "RT-qPCR validated biomarkers" (since both had $P > 0.05$ in `Figure 5`), correct the `CACNAIE` typographical error in Section 3.2 (line 179), restrict `xCell` deconvolution of peripheral blood to hematopoietic cell types, and perform donor-level pseudobulk testing for the mouse `scRNA-seq` datasets (`GSE225948` and `GSE154396`).
