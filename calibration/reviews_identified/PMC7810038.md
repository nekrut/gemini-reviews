### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the timing and geographic origins of the earliest sustained SARS-CoV-2 community transmission networks in Washington state (United States) and Northern Italy (Europe). Combining forward-in-time epidemic and sequence evolution simulations (FAVITES) with Bayesian generalized linear model (GLM) phylogeographic inference that integrates air traffic covariates and unsampled diversity assigned from regional incidence records, the authors test whether the earliest detected introductions—WA1 in Washington (sampled 19 January 2020, arriving 15 January) and BavPat1 in Bavaria, Germany—directly founded the subsequent regional epidemic clades. Across 1,000 forward simulations parameterized with a 4.7-day median doubling time and a substitution rate of $0.8 \times 10^{-3}$ substitutions/site/year, the observed phylogenetic pattern (zero basal sibling lineages with fewer than two mutations separating WA1 from the C17747T/A17858G outbreak clade among 294 early genomes, and at most one basal sibling WA-S566 in the expanded 1,000-genome dataset) occurs with $P < 0.001$ and $P = 0.010$, respectively. Similarly, the absence of basal polytomies connecting BavPat1 to the Northern Italian B.1 outbreak is rejected under a direct German-origin model ($P = 0.004$). The authors also identify an amplicon sequencing artifact at site 17747 (primer `nCov2019_58_RIGHT` in the ARTIC protocol) that produced spurious ancestral C17747 states in 16 of 27 British Columbia genomes. Bayesian phylogeography places the founding introduction of the Washington outbreak clade around 1 February 2020 (95% HPD: 14 January to 15 February) and the Hubei-to-Italy introduction around 28 January 2020 (95% HPD: 20 January to 6 February).

This is a methodologically strong study that pairs forward coalescent/epidemic hypothesis testing with travel-informed Bayesian phylogeography and careful bioinformatic forensic verification of primer-induced reference bias. Its primary limitations are: (1) a fixed substitution rate ($0.8 \times 10^{-3}$ substitutions/site/year) in the baseline forward simulations rather than drawing from an empirical clock prior distribution; (2) moderate phylogenetic uncertainty in the placement of the late-sampled WA-S566 and identical New York sequences ($\text{pp} = 0.67$), where the inclusion of unsampled Hubei/Zhejiang taxa shifts the inferred introduction date by 6 days compared to the time-inhomogeneous model without unsampled taxa; and (3) policy claims in the abstract and discussion attributing the extinction of WA1 and Bavarian introductions specifically to public health interventions rather than stochastic extinction at low initial copy numbers. These limitations can be addressed through sensitivity analyses over overdispersion ($k$) in the transmission offspring distribution and tempered causal phrasing regarding containment efficacy.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The Washington state A.1 outbreak clade did not descend directly from the mid-January WA1 introduction, but resulted from an independent introduction around late January or early February 2020:** **[Well Supported].**  
  Forward simulations under FAVITES show that a mid-January introduction establishing sustained transmission would leave ancestral or single-step intermediate lineages in 95.5% to 100% of simulated 294-tip trees ($P < 0.001$ for sibling lineages with $<2$ mutations) and 99.0% of 1,000-tip trees ($P = 0.010$ when accounting for WA-S566). Sensitivity analyses doubling the mutation rate ($1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ subs/site/year) or slowing the doubling time (5.6 days) confirm that the absence of basal diversity is incompatible with continuous local transmission from 15 January.

* **Apparent ancestral C17747 states in British Columbia and several U.S. states represent ARTIC primer trimming/incorporation artifacts rather than basal evolutionary intermediates:** **[Well Supported].**  
  The authors demonstrate that six British Columbia C17747 genomes share between one and four derived mutations at other genomic positions with locally circulating C17747T/A17858G viruses (Fig. 3). Explaining this pattern biologically would require multiple independent homoplasies or reversions across a 29.9 kb genome at very low divergence, whereas read-level misincorporation of the overlapping `nCov2019_58_RIGHT` primer provides a parsimonious technical mechanism.

* **The Northern Italian B.1 outbreak originated via an independent introduction from China around 28 January 2020 rather than descending from the January Bavarian (BavPat1) cluster:** **[Well Supported].**  
  Forward simulations of the Bavarian-origin hypothesis reject the absence of basal polytomies ($P = 0.004$), and Bayesian phylogeography integrating air passenger flow and unsampled Italian/Hubei diversity supports independent introductions from China ($\text{pp} = 0.84$) over a Germany-to-Italy transition ($\text{pp} = 0.16$).

* **Early introductions into Germany and Washington state were extinguished specifically because of vigorous public health interventions and contact tracing:** **[Moderately Supported].**  
  While epidemiological follow-up of WA1 and the Bavarian cluster was rapid and no onward descendants were detected in the long-term clades, SARS-CoV-2 transmission exhibits substantial individual-level overdispersion (superspreading, negative binomial dispersion $k \approx 0.1$). Under high overdispersion, the majority of single-index introductions go stochastically extinct even in the absence of control measures. The genomic data prove that WA1 and BavPat1 failed to establish the dominant clades, but cannot by themselves partition credit between contact tracing and stochastic fadeout.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Explicitly Quantify the Role of Transmission Overdispersion ($k$) in Stochastic Extinction vs. Public Health Containment
* **WHY:** In the Abstract and Discussion, the failure of WA1 (and other early introductions) to establish sustained transmission is attributed to public health interventions. However, forward simulations conditioned on producing an epidemic of size comparable to the mid-March Washington outbreak filter out simulated trajectories that die out stochastically, while unconditioned single introductions under a negative binomial offspring distribution ($k \approx 0.1 - 0.2$) frequently go extinct without intervention.
* **WHAT:** Report the unconditional stochastic extinction probability of a single introduction in the FAVITES contact network model alongside the conditioned topological probabilities, and temper the causal attribution in the Abstract.
* **HOW:** Using the existing FAVITES contact network parameterization (table S1 and fig. S2), compute the fraction of simulated index cases that go extinct within 61 days in the absence of intervention, and revise lines 8–9 to state that early introductions failed to establish sustained transmission "likely due to a combination of rapid public health follow-up and stochastic fadeout."

#### 2. Reconcile the Time-Homogeneous Unsampled-Taxa Model with Time-Inhomogeneous Travel Restrictions
* **WHY:** In lines 35–37, the authors note that the time-homogeneous GLM phylogeography with unsampled taxa estimates a 1 February 2020 introduction for the WA outbreak clade (and produces an implausible US-to-Malaysia transition in the MCC tree), whereas the time-inhomogeneous model without unsampled taxa estimates 26 January 2020. Because Hubei lockdown and U.S. travel restrictions occurred precisely during late January / early February, assuming time-homogeneous air flow for unsampled Hubei lineages biases the temporal placement of Markov jumps toward early February.
* **WHAT:** Combine the time-inhomogeneous GLM transition matrix (reflecting the post-23 January Hubei quarantine and 2 February U.S. travel suspension) with the unsampled-taxa dataset, rather than evaluating them only in separate runs.
* **HOW:** In the BEAST XML specification used for Fig. 4 and fig. S4, apply the epoch-based time-inhomogeneous GLM indicators to the full alignment including the unsampled Hubei/Zhejiang tip priors, and report the resulting joint 95% HPD for the Markov jump into Washington state.

#### 3. Propagate Evolutionary Clock Uncertainty in the FAVITES Forward Simulations
* **WHY:** The baseline FAVITES topological rejection tests fix the substitution rate to point values ($0.8 \times 10^{-3}$ or $1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ substitutions/site/year). Over a short 61-day window, Poisson mutational variance is sensitive to rate heterogeneity across lineages.
* **WHAT:** Draw the simulation clock rate for each of the 1,000 FAVITES replicates from the posterior clock rate distribution estimated in the BEAST analysis rather than holding it fixed.
* **HOW:** Sample $\mu_i \sim \text{Gamma}$ (or lognormal matching the empirical BEAST posterior) for each FAVITES run $i \in \{1, \dots, 1000\}$ and recompute the topological summary statistics in Fig. 2B–E.
