## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis

MacLean et al. investigate the evolutionary forces acting on SARS-CoV-2 during the first nine months of the human pandemic (133,741 GISAID genomes collected through October 12, 2020) and across the ancestral Sarbecovirus ("nCoV") lineage in bats and pangolins (68 Sarbecovirus genomes partitioned into 19–20 nonrecombinant regions, NRRs). Using codon-based phylogenetic selection models in HyPhy—specifically `SLAC`, `FEL`, `MEME`, `aBSREL`, and an upgraded `BUSTED[S]` framework incorporating a three-rate Hidden Markov Model (HMM) for spatial autocorrelation in synonymous rates plus multinucleotide substitutions—alongside corrected synonymous dinucleotide usage (`SDUc`), multivariate Ornstein–Uhlenbeck trait shift detection (`PhylogeneticEM`), and two-parameter local-clock Bayesian phylogenetics (`BEAST`), the authors establish three principal findings: (1) early human-to-human transmission of SARS-CoV-2 through October 2020 was characterized by weak purifying selection ($dN/dS \approx 0.45\text{--}0.89$ on internal branches), extreme low genetic diversity, and minimal adaptive evolution (only 52 codons reaching nominal `MEME` $P \le 0.05$ across Spike and RdRp); (2) the ancestral nCoV clade in bats evolved under overwhelming purifying selection ($3,388$ negatively selected sites via `FEL` and $8,184$ invariant codons), punctuated by episodic positive selection (`MEME` $P \le 0.05$ at 85 codons; `aBSREL` across 9/20 NRRs) heavily enriched on the deepest branches ancestral to the nCoV clade and depleted on the terminal branch leading to SARS-CoV-2; and (3) a pronounced drop in CpG dinucleotide representation (`SDUc`) and an associated elevation in substitution rate occurred on the ancestral branch leading to the nCoV clade rather than immediately preceding the emergence of SARS-CoV-2.

The methodological implementation of recombination-aware selection scanning (`BUSTED[S]` with HMM synonymous rate smoothing and multi-hit handling, internal-branch filtering in `MEME`/`FEL` to remove terminal sequencing errors and transient deleterious variants, and `SDUc` frame-specific dinucleotide permutation nulls) is rigorous and well-suited to coronavirus evolution. The primary limitations lie in: (1) over-interpreting sparse taxon sampling in the nCoV bat clade as evidence against host shifts or intermediate hosts, where long unbroken internal branches conflate ancient evolutionary time with specific host-switching events; (2) forcing ultrametricity on non-ultrametric heterochronous MCC trees by extending terminal branches to apply the scalar Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model in `PhylogeneticEM`, which distorts branch-length variance when inferring CpG shifts; (3) an underpowered statistical test in Figure 4B ($t_{2.07} = 3.03, P = 0.045$, one-tailed $t$-test on $n = 3$ codon frame positions) used to argue that the `RmYN02` Spike recombination event reflects co-infection in a shared bat host species; and (4) reporting nominal $P \le 0.05$ site counts for `MEME` and `FEL` without false discovery rate (FDR) correction across $\sim 9,700$ codons.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Evaluation

* **Claim 1:** During the first nine months of human circulation (through October 2020), SARS-CoV-2 exhibited limited adaptive evolution and weak purifying selection on internal branches, with most high-frequency nonsynonymous variants (outside Spike D614G and RdRp P323L) reflecting founder effects and neutral drift rather than widespread positive selection. **[Well Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Separating terminal branches from internal branches in `SLAC`, `FEL`, and `MEME` across 133,741 genomes (and longitudinal sub-epochs in Spike and RdRp, Figure 1A–E) demonstrates that terminal branches have higher $dN/dS$ ($0.68\text{--}0.99$) than internal branches ($0.45\text{--}0.67$) due to incomplete purifying selection and sequencing errors on tips. Tracking statistical power via internal tree length shows that until total internal branch length reached $\sim 0.15\text{--}0.50$ substitutions/site, `MEME` and `FEL` had near-zero power, and even by October 2020 only 52 codons across Spike and RdRp reached nominal $P \le 0.05$.

* **Claim 2:** Evolution across the Sarbecovirus nCoV clade was dominated by strong purifying selection ($\sim 84\%$ of codons conserved and $35\%$ statistically significant under `FEL`), while episodic positive selection (`MEME` at 85 sites, $0.87\%$ of codons; `BUSTED[S]` and `aBSREL` on 9/20 NRRs) occurred primarily on the deeper ancestral branches of the nCoV lineage in bats rather than on the terminal branches leading to human SARS-CoV-2 or `RmYN02`. **[Well Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The authors partition the alignment into 19–20 nonrecombinant regions (NRRs) based on Boni et al. (2020) and apply `BUSTED[S]` augmented with a 3-rate HMM for spatial synonymous rate variation (accounting for coronavirus RNA secondary structures and overlapping reading frames) and multinucleotide substitutions. Only 5 positive selection events map to the terminal branches of SARS-CoV-2 and `RmYN02` combined (an event density of $3.9$ per unit branch length), compared to $28.0$ events per unit branch length on internal nCoV branches ($P = 0.005$, Fisher's exact test, S2 Table).

* **Claim 3:** The 85 positively selected codons in the nCoV clade are non-randomly clustered across the genome and significantly enriched in B-cell and T-cell epitopes in Orf1ab, Spike, and Nucleocapsid (N). **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The spatial permutation test confirming clustering of the 85 `MEME` sites ($P < 0.0001$) is sound. However, the candidate 85 sites were selected at a nominal threshold of `MEME` $P \le 0.05$ across $\sim 9,744$ codons without multiple-testing correction; under a well-calibrated null, up to $\sim 487 \times f_{\text{variable}}$ false positives could arise at nominal $\alpha = 0.05$ (though conservative at low divergence). Moreover, comparing bat-lineage selected sites against *human* SARS-CoV-1/SARS-CoV-2 adaptive immune epitopes (IEDB) is an indirect proxy for bat adaptive immune pressure, especially in N where only 5 selected sites drive the enrichment.

* **Claim 4:** A major evolutionary shift toward stronger CpG dinucleotide suppression (`SDUc`) and an elevated evolutionary substitution rate occurred on the ancestral branch leading to the entire nCoV clade ($\sim \text{year } 1467$ in NRR2), rather than during a recent host switch into humans or intermediate hosts. **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The `SDUc` permutation framework (accounting for amino acid sequence and mononucleotide composition across all three codon reading frames: `pos1`, `pos2`, and `bridge`, Figure 3B) convincingly shows that all members of the nCoV clade share lower CpG representation than sister non-nCoV Sarbecoviruses, and that the recombinant non-nCoV Spike fragment of `RmYN02` retains high CpG levels matching non-nCoV viruses. However, two methodological caveats weaken the phylogenetic dating and shift modeling:
    1. *Artificial ultrametric extension in `PhylogeneticEM`:* The scalar Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) model in `PhylogeneticEM` requires an ultrametric tree, so the authors forced the heterochronous MCC trees to be ultrametric *"by extending all the external edges of the trees to match the most recently sampled tip."* Extending tip branches alters the terminal variance-covariance structure of the phylogenetic OU process.
    2. *Local clock identifiability:* In the two-parameter local clock BEAST analysis (Figure 3A, S3 Text), the authors note that applying the local clock only to the stem branch of the nCoV clade versus the entire nCoV clade alters substitution rates and root date estimates (yielding median nCoV tMRCA of 1467 with wide 95% HPD in NRR2), and they state explicitly that they *"cannot formally distinguish which model is a better fit."*

* **Claim 5:** Recombination breakpoints and `SDUc` differences between the nCoV and non-nCoV regions of `RmYN02` Spike prove that the ancestral nCoV lineage evolved solely in *Rhinolophus* bats via co-infection and rule out any evolutionary requirement for an intermediate pangolin or non-bat host in the emergence of SARS-CoV-2. **[Overstated]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** First, the statistical test in Figure 4B comparing the absolute `SDUc` differences between SARS-CoV-2 and `RmYN02` in the non-nCoV versus nCoV regions of Spike is an unpaired one-tailed $t$-test across only $n = 3$ non-independent codon frame positions (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge`) from the same locus ($t_{2.07} = 3.03, P = 0.0450$), which has virtually no statistical degrees of freedom ($\text{df} = 2.07$) and would not even be significant under a two-tailed test ($P = 0.090$). Second, because the branch between the `RmYN02`/SARS-CoV-2 ancestor ($\sim 1976$) and the emergence of SARS-CoV-2 in 2019 spans over four decades of unsampled evolution, absence of detected positive selection or CpG shifts on that single unbroken terminal branch cannot distinguish direct bat-to-human spillover from brief passage through an intermediate mammalian host over months or a few years.

## 3. Technical, Statistical, and Biological Critique

### 3.1 Major Methodological and Statistical Issues

1. **Unadjusted Nominal Significance ($P \le 0.05$) in Genome-Wide Site-Level Selection Scans (`MEME` and `FEL`):**
   Across both the 133,741 human SARS-CoV-2 dataset (Figure 1C, E) and the 69-taxon Sarbecovirus nCoV alignment (~9,744 codons across 19–20 NRRs, Figure 2A), codons are classified as positively or negatively selected using an unadjusted threshold of $P \le 0.05$ (`MEME` $P \le 0.05$ yields 85 sites in the nCoV clade; `FEL` $P \le 0.05$ yields 3,388 negatively selected sites). While `MEME` and `FEL` likelihood ratio tests are conservative at invariant or near-invariant sites, testing $\sim 1,560$ variable codons in the nCoV clade at nominal $\alpha = 0.05$ without false discovery rate (FDR) control means that a substantial fraction of the 85 reported positively selected sites may represent false positives. Providing $q$-values or empirical null calibration for the 85 sites would clarify how many individual site calls survive multiplicity adjustment.

2. **Ad Hoc Ultrametric Transformation for Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Shift Inference (`PhylogeneticEM`):**
   In the Methods section ("Identifying shifts in CpG content"), the authors apply a scalar Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process via `PhylogeneticEM` to $\ln(\text{SDUc})$ across the three frame positions (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge`), stating: *"The trees were forced to be ultrametric, as required for the scalar OU model, by extending all the external edges of the trees to match the most recently sampled tip."* Under an OU process, extending terminal branches changes the expected stationary covariance $V_{ij} \propto e^{-\alpha d_{ij}}(1 - e^{-2\alpha t_{ij}})$ for earlier-sampled tips relative to internal branches. Moreover, because the two-parameter local clock BEAST tree already scales branch lengths in calendar time using tip sampling dates (spanning only a few years/decades relative to a 550-year root), any large departure from ultrametricity either reflects tip-date differences (which are minor on a 550-year tree) or substitution-rate scaling (if a phylogram rather than a chronogram was passed to `PhylogeneticEM`).

3. **Underpowered One-Tailed $t$-Test on $n = 3$ Codon Frame Positions (Figure 4B):**
   In Figure 4B, to test whether the `SDUc` divergence between Wuhan-Hu-1 and `RmYN02` is larger in the recombinant non-nCoV Spike fragment than in the non-recombinant nCoV Spike fragment, the authors treat the 3 reading frame positions (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge`) within the same sequence segment as independent observations and run an unpaired one-tailed Welch $t$-test ($t_{2.07} = 3.03, P = 0.0450$). Because `bridge` shares the 3rd codon nucleotide with `pos2` and the 1st codon nucleotide with `pos1`, the three frame positions are structurally dependent, $n = 3$ is far too small to verify normality, and a two-tailed test yields $P = 0.090$. Instead of a 3-point $t$-test, the hypothesis should be tested directly via sequence-level codon permutation of the difference statistic $\Delta |\text{SDUc}_{\text{SARS-CoV-2}} - \text{SDUc}_{\text{RmYN02}}|$ between the two regions.

### 3.2 Minor Issues and Reproducibility Strengths

1. **Typographical Error in Methods:**
   In the BEAST Methods section ("Bayesian evolutionary analysis of NRR1 and NRR2"), "NRR2" is misspelled once as "NNR2" (*"We partitioned the coding regions of NRR1 and NNR2 by codon position..."*).
2. **Exemplary Computational Transparency:**
   The authors provide interactive Observable notebooks (`https://observablehq.com/@spond/sars-cov-2-october-2020`, `https://observablehq.com/@spond/ncov-clade-selection`, `https://observablehq.com/@spond/sars-cov-2-selected-sites`), full HyPhy batchfiles (`BUSTED[S]` with HMM synonymous rates and multi-hit support), BEAST XML configurations (S1 Data), and complete GISAID/GenBank accession tables (S4 and S5 Tables).
