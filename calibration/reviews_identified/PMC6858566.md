### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Aykut et al. investigate the role of the fungal mycobiome in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDA) using spontaneous (`KC`) and orthotopic (`FC1242` KPC) mouse models alongside human fecal ($n=18$) and pancreatic tissue specimens ($n=13$ PDA, $n=5$ normal). Using 18S/ITS1 amplicon sequencing, qPCR, and 28S rRNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), the authors report a ~3,000-fold increase in intrapancreatic fungal DNA in PDA compared to normal pancreas, distinct alpha and beta diversity between gut and tumor mycobiomes, and marked enrichment of the genus *Malassezia*. They further show that oral antifungal treatment (Amphotericin B or Fluconazole) slows tumor progression in specific-pathogen-free (SPF) mice—but not in germ-free, mannose-binding lectin–deficient (`MBL-null`), or complement `C3-/-` mice—and that oral repopulation with *Malassezia globosa* (but not *Candida*, *Saccharomyces*, or *Aspergillus*) accelerates tumor growth via the host MBL–C3–tumor C3aR complement cascade. Raw ITS sequencing reads are not linked to a public repository accession (e.g., NCBI SRA or ENA) in the manuscript text.

While the murine genetic epistasis experiments (`MBL-null`, `C3-/-`, and tumor `C3aR` shRNA knockdown) provide a coherent immunological demonstration that extra-tumoral lectin/complement activation promotes orthotopic KPC tumor growth, the study's foundational mycobiome sequencing claims suffer from severe low-biomass and bioinformatic artifacts. Specifically, the Bioinformatics section reveals that 93.57% of quality-filtered ITS reads (`1,856,993` of `1,989,618`) failed to align to fungi, leaving **only 636 total fungal reads across all human pancreatic tissue samples combined** (an average of ~35 reads per tissue sample distributed across 311 operational taxonomic units [OTUs]). Computing unrarefied alpha-diversity (`Observed OTUs`, `Chao1`, `ACE`) and Bray–Curtis beta-diversity ordinances between fecal samples (~853 fungal reads/sample) and human tissue samples (~35 reads/sample) without computational contaminant subtraction for *Malassezia* (the dominant human skin and reagent contaminant) invalidates the human diversity comparisons. Furthermore, control mouse data are reused across figures without statistical adjustment, and TCGA survival analyses rely on small, unevenly sliced sub-cohorts. Re-analyzing the ITS data with rarefaction, explicit reagent-control subtraction (`decontam`), and full-cohort Cox regression on TCGA data is essential to establish whether a resident intratumoral mycobiome exists above background contamination.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Human PDA tumors harbor a distinct, diverse mycobiome enriched for *Malassezia* compared to gut and normal pancreas: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  According to the Bioinformatics and Statistical analyses section, after removing 93.57% non-fungal reads, all human pancreatic tissue samples combined ($n=13$ PDA and $n=5$ healthy controls in Figure 2) yielded a total of only **636 fungal reads assigned to 311 OTUs** (~35 reads per sample; ~2.0 reads per OTU across the entire human tissue dataset), compared to 15,349 reads across 18 human fecal samples (~853 reads per sample). In Figure 2C–E, comparing richness (`Observed OTUs`, `Chao1`, `ACE`), evenness (`Shannon`, `Simpson`), and Bray–Curtis PCoA between 853-read fecal libraries and 35-read tissue libraries without rarefaction is mathematically invalid, as richness and Bray–Curtis dissimilarity are dominated by sequencing depth at 35 reads/sample. Moreover, *Malassezia* is a ubiquitous human skin commensal and laboratory reagent contaminant in low-biomass sequencing; although negative controls were sequenced, no computational decontamination or blank-subtraction step is reported, and undefined "outlier" samples were manually excluded.

* **Fungi rapidly translocate from the gut lumen into the pancreas within 30 minutes of oral gavage: [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 1E and Methods, mice were orally gavaged with $10^8$ CFU of GFP-labeled *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* and sacrificed 30 minutes later, with translocation quantified solely as "GFP+ foci by flow cytometry" in dissociated pancreata. Detecting intact non-motile yeast migration from the stomach through the duodenum and pancreatic duct into the parenchyma within 30 minutes of oral gavage by flow cytometry alone—without colony-forming unit (CFU) recovery on agar plates, sorting of GFP+ events, or microscopic verification of intact yeast inside pancreatic ducts—cannot exclude autofluorescent cellular debris or procedural cross-contamination during harvest.

* **Oral antifungal ablation protects against PDA and *Malassezia globosa* repopulation accelerates oncogenesis in mice: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Oral Amphotericin B and Fluconazole reduced tumor weight in orthotopic KPC and spontaneous KC models (Figure 3A–C, Extended Data Figure 3A), and Amphotericin B had no effect on orthotopic tumors in germ-free mice (Extended Data Figure 3B), arguing against direct drug cytotoxicity on tumor cells. However, in Figure 3D (comparing 5 repopulation arms: *M. globosa*, *S. cerevisiae*, *Candida* sp., *Aspergillus* sp., and vehicle) and Figure 3C (4 treatment arms), groups are compared using unadjusted pairwise two-tailed Student's $t$-tests rather than one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's or Tukey's post-hoc correction.

* **Host MBL and C3 complement activation and tumor cell C3aR signaling drive PDA growth in mice and predict human PDA survival: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The mouse genetic experiments—showing reduced tumor growth in `MBL-null` and `C3-/-` hosts (Figure 4A–E), rescue by intratumoral recombinant C3a (Figure 4D), protection via tumor cell `C3aR` shRNA knockdown (Figure 4F–G), and loss of Amphotericin B or *M. globosa* effects in `MBL-null` and `C3-/-` mice (Figure 4H, Extended Data Figure 4B–C)—are well-controlled, with the caveat that Figure 4A reuses the $n=11$ *vehicle-gavaged* `KC` control mice from Figure 3A as the control group for unmanipulated `KC;MBL-null` mice. By contrast, the human TCGA survival claims in Extended Data Figure 4A and 4D are fragile: `MBL` (`MBL2`) is synthesized almost exclusively in the liver rather than pancreatic tumor tissue (consistent with the authors' own finding that *extra-tumoral* host MBL deletion is protective), and the TCGA Kaplan–Meier curves use unexplained subset sample sizes ($n=83$ split into `16, 24, 26, 17` for `MBL`; only $n=33$ split into `18` vs. `15` for `C3` out of ~178 TCGA PAAD patients).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Perform Rigorous Blank Subtraction (`decontam`) and Depth Normalization on the ITS Dataset
* **WHY:** With 93.57% off-target non-fungal reads and only 636 total fungal reads across all human pancreatic tissue specimens (~35 reads/sample), alpha- and beta-diversity metrics in Figure 2 are confounded by library size, and skin/reagent *Malassezia* contamination can dominate ultra-low-biomass tissue profiles.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the exact read counts and taxonomic composition of all sequenced negative (reagent-only) controls, apply formal statistical decontamination, and rarefy (or apply library-size-aware models) before computing diversity indices.
* **HOW:** (i) Report a supplementary table listing the raw read count, quality-filtered read count, and fungal-aligned read count for every mouse sample, human sample, and negative reagent control, and explicitly list which samples were removed as "outliers" and why; (ii) run the `R` package `decontam` (combined frequency and prevalence method using PicoGreen DNA concentrations and sequenced negative controls) to flag and remove reagent/skin contaminant OTUs; and (iii) repeat the alpha- and beta-diversity comparisons in Figures 1F–L and 2A–E after rarefying to a uniform read depth (or report which human tissue samples fall below a minimum threshold of 500 fungal reads).

#### 2. Apply Proper Multiple-Testing Corrections and Separate Concurrent Controls in Animal Experiments
* **WHY:** Figure 4A reuses the $n=11$ vehicle-gavaged `KC` control group from Figure 3A to compare against `KC;MBL-null` mice without adjusting for shared controls or gavage handling, and multi-arm experiments (Figures 3C, 3D, 4G, 4H) rely on unadjusted pairwise Student's $t$-tests.
* **WHAT:** Replace unadjusted pairwise $t$-tests in multi-group experiments with one-way or two-way ANOVA followed by Dunnett's or Holm–Šídák post-hoc tests, and account for shared control groups.
* **HOW:** Re-calculate $p$-values for Figure 3C (4 groups: $2 \times 2$ factorial ANOVA testing `Amphotericin B × Gemcitabine`), Figure 3D (5 groups: one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's test against `Vehicle`), Figure 4G (3 groups: one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's test of `shC3aR1` and `shC3aR2` against `Scrambled`), and Figures 3A/4A (one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's correction for comparing both `Amphotericin B` and `KC;MBL-null` against the shared $n=11$ `KC` control group, while noting the gavage difference).

#### 3. Re-Analyze TCGA Survival Across the Full PAAD Cohort Using Continuous Cox Regression
* **WHY:** Extended Data Figure 4A ($n=83$: groups of 16, 24, 26, and 17) and Extended Data Figure 4D ($n=33$: 18 high vs. 15 low) include less than half and less than one-fifth of the TCGA pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) cohort, respectively, creating post-hoc threshold and subset selection bias. Moreover, `MBL2` is a liver-expressed gene that is undetectable in most bulk pancreatic tumors.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate `MBL2`, `C3`, and `C3AR1` expression across the complete unfiltered TCGA PAAD cohort.
* **HOW:** For all primary PDA tumors in TCGA-PAAD ($n \approx 177$), report the fraction of samples with zero vs. non-zero `MBL2` read counts, plot Kaplan–Meier curves using exact median or quartile splits across the entire cohort, and report multivariable Cox proportional hazards ratios (with 95% CIs and $p$-values) treating $\log_2(\text{TPM}+1)$ of `MBL2`, `C3`, and `C3AR1` as continuous predictors adjusted for age, sex, and tumor stage.

#### 4. Deposit Raw Amplicon Sequencing Data and Metadata in a Public Nucleotide Archive
* **WHY:** Without access to the raw FASTQ files for the 166 samples and the negative/mock controls, independent verification of the 636 human tissue fungal reads and *Malassezia* OTU assignments is impossible.
* **WHAT:** Deposit all demultiplexed FASTQ files (including reagent blanks and mock communities) and sample metadata in NCBI SRA or ENA.
* **HOW:** Upload the 16,647,630 raw paired-end MiSeq reads and QIIME mapping file to NCBI SRA and add the BioProject accession number to a Data Availability statement.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Several numerical, anatomical/genetic, and unit errors appear in the Methods section.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Read count mean discrepancy (Bioinformatics and Statistical analyses, line 56):** The text states *"The 1,989,618 quality reads (Mean 8575; n=166) were then processed with QIIME."* However, $1,989,618 / 166 = 11,985.7$ (whereas $1,989,618 / 8,575 = 232$). Reconcile the total quality read count, mean per sample, and total sample count $n$.
  2. **Sum of fungal reads across sample types (line 56):** The text states *"A total of 127,646 sequence reads were clustered into 1899 OTUs (86,640 reads) for longitudinal mouse fecal samples; 390 OTUs (25,021 reads) for mouse tissue samples; 2980 OTUs (15,349 reads) for human fecal samples; and 311 OTUs (636 reads) for human tissue samples."* Note that $1,989,618 - 1,856,993 \text{ (non-fungal)} = 132,625$ (not $127,646$). Clarify whether the remaining $4,979$ reads were removed as chimeras or singletons.
  3. **Misdescription of the ITS1 locus (Fungal DNA extraction and sequencing, line 51):** The text refers to *"the ITS1 region of the 18S rRNA gene"*. The internal transcribed spacer 1 (`ITS1`) is the non-coding spacer region located *between* the 18S and 5.8S rRNA genes (amplified by `ITS1F` in 18S and `ITS2` in 5.8S), not part of the 18S gene itself. Correct this description throughout the text and figure captions (`18S ITS sequencing` $\to$ `ITS1 rRNA sequencing`).
  4. **Puromycin concentration typo (C3aR Knockdown, line 26):** The text states *"selection with puromycin (2 μg/ul) for 10 days"*. `2 μg/μl` equals `2,000 μg/ml`; correct the unit to `2 μg/ml`.
