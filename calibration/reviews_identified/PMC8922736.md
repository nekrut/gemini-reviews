### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Li et al. benchmark six differential expression analysis methods—three parametric (`DESeq2`, `edgeR`, `limma-voom`) and three non-parametric (`NOISeq`, `dearseq`, and the classical two-sample Wilcoxon rank-sum test)—across 13 human population-level bulk RNA-seq datasets ($N = 100$ to $1,376$), comprising one anti-PD-1 melanoma immunotherapy cohort (GSE91061; 51 pre-nivolumab and 58 on-nivolumab samples), six GTEx tissue-pair datasets, and six TCGA tumor-versus-normal datasets. Using 1,000 sample-label permutations per dataset, the authors demonstrate that `DESeq2` and `edgeR` suffer from severe false discovery rate (FDR) inflation in large cohorts: on the anti-PD-1 dataset at a nominal 5% FDR, `DESeq2` and `edgeR` call more differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in 84.88% and 78.89% of permuted null datasets than in the unpermuted data, with 15.3% and 60.8% of their original DEG calls recurring in $\ge 50\%$ of label permutations and yielding spurious immune Gene Ontology (GO) enrichments. Using 600 semi-synthetic datasets with down-sampling from $n=2$ to $100$ per condition, they show that the Wilcoxon rank-sum test on TMM-normalized counts-per-million (CPM) consistently controls the empirical FDR and achieves superior or comparable power once per-condition sample size exceeds $n=8$. All datasets are publicly available from GEO, GTEx, and GDC Xena Hub, and software versions are documented in the Methods.

The sample-label permutation analysis provides a clear, practical demonstration that extreme count outliers in population-scale human RNA-seq violate negative binomial mean models, causing parametric tests to report repeatable false-positive DEGs and misleading functional enrichments. However, the study's secondary methodological components have notable flaws: (i) the semi-synthetic benchmark permutes *raw unnormalized counts* independently gene-by-gene across samples with unequal library sizes while leaving true DEGs unpermuted, which decouples counts from sample sequencing depth; (ii) the negative binomial goodness-of-fit test uses `vcd::goodfit` on `round(normalized_counts)`, violating both count-scaling properties and $\chi^2$ minimum bin-count requirements; (iii) `DESeq2` was run without the `filterByExpr` pre-filtering applied to `edgeR`, `limma-voom`, `dearseq`, and `Wilcoxon`; and (iv) paired datasets (patient-matched pre/on-nivolumab biopsies, same-donor GTEx tissues, and TCGA matched tumor-normal pairs) are analyzed as unpaired two-sample studies. These issues can be resolved through computational re-analysis using library-size-preserving semi-synthetic simulations, symmetric pre-filtering, and paired/covariate-adjusted comparisons.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **DESeq2 and edgeR exhibit severe FDR inflation on large human population RNA-seq datasets due to extreme count outliers: [Well Supported].**  
  The 1,000-permutation negative-control experiments (Figure 1A–D, Additional file 1: Figures S5–S17) permute the condition label vector $C$ across intact sample columns, preserving each sample's library size and gene–gene correlation structure. Because the same outlier-harboring genes are repeatedly flagged as DEGs in $\ge 50\%$ of random label permutations and produce large estimated $\log_2$ fold changes, this directly proves that parametric mean tests are sensitive to single-sample or few-sample expression spikes in human cohorts.

* **Spurious DEGs generated by outlier sensitivity under null permutations can produce biologically plausible but false GO term enrichments: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 1E shows that genes called in $\ge 10\%$ of permuted anti-PD-1 datasets are significantly enriched for immune-related biological processes (`clusterProfiler` BH-adjusted $p < 0.05$), demonstrating how hyper-variable immune transcripts in tumor biopsies can mislead biological interpretation when parametric assumptions fail.

* **The Wilcoxon rank-sum test controls the FDR across sample sizes and achieves higher power than parametric and asymptotic competitors when $n \ge 8$ per condition: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While the rank-based invariance of the Wilcoxon test naturally protects against outlier-driven false positives in the column-permutation experiments, the power and FDR comparisons in Figure 2 rely on a semi-synthetic generation scheme that permutes raw integer counts $(X_i, Y_i)$ independently for each non-DEG gene across samples while keeping $k$ true DEGs unpermuted. Because raw counts scale with sample sequencing depth, permuting raw counts across samples with different library sizes (prior to running TMM or DESeq2 size-factor estimation) distorts the relationship between permuted counts and sample normalization factors, disadvantaging count models that condition on sample library size.

* **Poor goodness-of-fit of the negative binomial distribution explains why specific genes are repeatedly misidentified in permuted datasets: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While visual inspection of the outlier counts (Additional file 1: Figure S5) confirms extreme heavy tails, the formal goodness-of-fit metric in Figure 1F (`vcd::goodfit(round(normalized_counts), type = "nbinomial", par = list(size = 1/dispersion))`) is statistically misspecified: `DESeq2` and `edgeR` model raw counts $K_{ij} \sim \text{NB}(s_j q_i, \alpha_i)$ rather than rounded normalized counts (dividing by $s_j \neq 1$ alters the NB variance function), and Pearson's $\chi^2$ test in `goodfit` is invalid on sparse discrete count tables where almost all observed count bins have expected frequencies $< 1$.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Preserve Sample Library Sizes in the Semi-Synthetic Power and FDR Benchmark
* **WHY:** In the semi-synthetic benchmark (Methods, "The generation of permuted and semi-synthetic data"), permuting raw read counts $(X_i, Y_i)$ independently for each non-DEG gene $i$ moves raw counts from high-depth samples into low-depth samples while the $k$ unpermuted true DEGs retain their original sample library sizes. When TMM or `DESeq2` subsequently divides column $j$ by a sample normalization factor $s_j$, the independently permuted raw counts are divided by the wrong sample's sequencing depth, artificially inflating dispersion and mean shifts for parametric count models.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the semi-synthetic benchmark in Figure 2 using library-size-adjusted count resampling (or quantile-adjusted conditional maximum likelihood / Poisson thinning) so that every gene in sample $j$ respects sample $j$'s sequencing depth.
* **HOW:** Before applying gene-wise permutation $\sigma_i$ to non-DEG genes: (i) convert raw counts to TMM-normalized proportions $p_{ij} = X_{ij} / L_j^*$, (ii) permute the normalized expression profiles $\sigma_i(p_i)$ across samples for non-DEGs while keeping $p_i$ fixed for true DEGs, and (iii) regenerate integer counts for sample $j$ by multinomial or Poisson sampling with mean $L_j^* p'_{ij}$ (or evaluate `limma-voom` and `Wilcoxon` directly on the normalized matrix). Compare the resulting FDR and power curves against Figure 2.

#### 2. Apply Symmetric Gene Pre-Filtering (`filterByExpr`) and Outlier-Handling Options Across All Methods
* **WHY:** In Methods ("The identification of DEGs"), `filterByExpr` was applied before `edgeR`, `limma-voom`, `dearseq`, and `Wilcoxon`, but omitted for `DESeq2`. Furthermore, `edgeR::glmQLFit` includes an option `robust = TRUE` specifically designed to downweight extreme count outliers, and `DESeq2` has `minReplicatesForReplace` and Cook's distance parameters that govern outlier handling in large cohorts.
* **WHAT:** Standardize the input gene set across all six methods and test whether built-in robust modes (`edgeR` with `robust = TRUE`, `limma` with `robust = TRUE`) mitigate FDR inflation.
* **HOW:** Apply `edgeR::filterByExpr` identically before running `DESeq2`, and add `edgeR (robust = TRUE)` and `limma-voom (robust = TRUE)` to Figure 1A–C and Figure 2A–B so readers can see whether robust quasi-likelihood/empirical Bayes estimation corrects the outlier vulnerability or whether rank testing remains necessary.

#### 3. Replace `vcd::goodfit` on Rounded Normalized Counts with a Validated Count Model Residual Diagnostic
* **WHY:** Applying `vcd::goodfit(round(normalized_counts), ...)` (Methods, line 109) tests an invalid probability model (since $K_{ij}/s_j$ is not Negative Binomial when $s_j \neq 1$) and relies on an unbinned asymptotic $\chi^2$ test where most discrete count cells have expected counts $\ll 1$.
* **WHAT:** Quantify negative binomial model violation directly on the fitted raw count distribution using randomized quantile residuals (Dunn–Smyth residuals) or parametric bootstrap goodness-of-fit.
* **HOW:** For each gene $i$ and condition, compute Dunn–Smyth randomized quantile residuals $r_{ij}$ from the fitted NB distribution $\text{NB}(\hat{\mu}_{ij} = \hat{s}_j \hat{q}_i, \hat{\alpha}_i)$ on raw counts $K_{ij}$ (e.g., via `statmod::qresiduals` or `DHARMa`), and test normality/tail-heaviness of $r_{ij}$ (or maximum absolute studentized residual) between the $\ge 20\%$ and $\le 0.1\%$ permuted-DEG groups in Figure 1F.

#### 4. Evaluate Paired/Longitudinal Designs and Covariate Adjustment in Population Cohorts
* **WHY:** Several benchmark datasets contain paired measurements from the same individuals (GSE91061 pre- vs. on-nivolumab patient biopsies; GTEx left ventricle vs. atrial appendage from the same donors; TCGA tumor vs. matched adjacent normal tissue). Analyzing paired samples with an unpaired two-sample Wilcoxon test or unpaired `DESeq2`/`edgeR` ignores donor-level correlation, and population human studies routinely require adjusting for sex, age, and batch.
* **WHAT:** Demonstrate how rank-based testing performs when accounting for donor pairing (Wilcoxon signed-rank test) and covariates (probabilistic index models or rank-based regression).
* **HOW:** On the matched donor subset of GSE91061 and GTEx heart tissues, run within-donor label permutations (flipping pre/on therapy or tissue labels within each donor) comparing paired `DESeq2` (`~ patient + condition`), paired `edgeR`, paired `limma-voom`, and the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test (`wilcox.test(..., paired = TRUE)`), and include an example script for covariate-adjusted rank testing (`pim` or proportional odds ordinal regression).

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Duplicated paragraphs in the Methods section and missing code links hinder readability and direct reproduction.
* **WHAT & HOW:**

Delete the duplicated text blocks.
