### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

#### Executive Summary
This manuscript introduces `BOLT-LMM`, a scalable Bayesian linear mixed model (LMM) algorithm for genome-wide association studies (GWAS) that reduces time complexity from $O(M N^2)$ or $O(M^2 N)$ to a small number of $O(M N)$ conjugate-gradient and cyclic-coordinate-descent variational iterations (~$O(M N^{1.5})$ empirical scaling) and reduces memory footprint to $\approx M N / 4$ bytes (raw 2-bit genotypes in RAM without forming or factoring an $N \times N$ genetic relationship matrix). The method proceeds in four steps: (1a) Monte Carlo REML variance component estimation via conjugate gradient iteration, (1b) leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) infinitesimal retrospective mixed-model association testing (`BOLT-LMM-inf`, Equation 8) calibrated against exact prospective statistics at 30 random SNPs, (2a) 5-fold cross-validation hyperparameter selection over an 18-point grid of two-component Gaussian spike-and-slab mixture priors ($f_2 \in \{0.5, 0.3, 0.1\}$, $p \in \{0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01\}$) fitted via factored variational Bayes / iterative conditional expectation (ICE), and (2b) retrospective association testing against LOCO Gaussian-mixture phenotypic residuals calibrated via LD Score regression intercepts. Across simulated WTCCC2 mosaic cohorts up to $N = 480,000$ ($M = 300,000$) and empirical analyses of nine quantitative traits in the Women's Genome Health Study (WGHS; $N = 23,294$, $M = 324,488$), `BOLT-LMM` completes analyses where all prior methods (`GCTA-LOCO`, `FaST-LMM`, `FaST-LMM-Select`, `GEMMA`, `EMMAX`) fail above $N = 7,500$–$30,000$, matches `GCTA-LOCO` at $R^2 > 0.999$ under the infinitesimal model, and achieves up to a 25% increase in mean $\chi^2$ at standardized causal SNPs in simulations and up to a 10% gain (vs. PCA) and 9% gain (vs. `BOLT-LMM-inf`) on WGHS lipid traits (ApoB) while controlling false positives on null chromosomes and at the *LCT* (lactase) locus.

This is a landmark statistical genetics paper that combines deep numerical linear algebra (matrix-free Monte Carlo REML and conjugate gradients) with a principled hybrid Bayesian-frequentist testing framework (variational spike-and-slab LOCO conditioning coupled to LD Score-calibrated retrospective score statistics). Every modeling approximation—such as estimating $\sigma_g^2$ once genome-wide rather than per left-out chromosome (<0.5% calibration difference, Supplementary Table 4), using 30 random SNPs for $c_{\text{inf}}$ (<1% error, Supplementary Table 14), and comparing variational Bayes against exact Gibbs MCMC ($N = 5,211$, Supplementary Table 6)—is explicitly audited and validated.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

#### Detailed Rubric Evaluation

* **Claim 1 — Matrix-free conjugate gradient iteration and Monte Carlo REML enable `BOLT-LMM` to scale to $N = 480,000$ individuals and $M = 300,000$ SNPs in $\approx O(M N^{1.5})$ time and $\approx M N / 4$ bytes of RAM, while `BOLT-LMM-inf` reproduces exact prospective `GCTA-LOCO` statistics with $R^2 > 0.999$: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 1, Supplementary Table 1, and Supplementary Table 7 substantiate both the scaling and numerical equivalence. Operating directly on 2-bit packed genotypes requires ~36 GB for $N=480,000, M=300,000$, whereas $O(N^2)$ GRM storage alone requires ~1.8 TB in double precision.

* **Claim 2 — Modeling non-infinitesimal genetic architectures via a two-Gaussian mixture prior and variational Bayes (`BOLT-LMM`) increases association power as a function of $h_g^2 N / M_{\text{causal}}$, yielding up to a 25% increase in mean $\chi^2$ in WTCCC2 simulations and matching exact MCMC Gibbs sampling: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 2, Supplementary Figure 5, and Supplementary Table 6a,b demonstrate that the power gain over `BOLT-LMM-inf` and `GCTA-LOCO` scales with effective sample size per causal locus ($h_g^2 N / M_{\text{causal}}$) toward the theoretical asymptote $1 / (1 - h_g^2)$, while variational Bayes yields test statistics indistinguishable from MCMC at $N = 5,211$.

* **Claim 3 — On nine WGHS quantitative traits ($N = 23,294$), `BOLT-LMM` increases out-of-sample cross-validation prediction $R^2$ across all 9 traits ($p < 0.0002$) and increases mean $\chi^2$ at known GWAS loci by up to 10% over PCA and 9% over `BOLT-LMM-inf`: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 3 and Supplementary Tables 8–10 report both direct $\chi^2$ gains at known loci (statistically significant at $p < 0.05$ for 6 of 9 traits due to finite numbers of known loci, 19–180 per trait, with jackknife standard errors of 0.6–2.2%) and cross-validation $R^2$ gains (significant at $p < 0.0002$ for all 9 traits), confirming that results are not driven by single outlier loci via flat averages across replicating loci.

* **Claim 4 — Leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) residualization, a non-zero spike variance ($f_2 \ge 0.1$), and LD Score regression calibration control Type I error and eliminate population stratification (including the *LCT*–height false positive): [Well Supported].**  
  Supplementary Figure 4b, Supplementary Tables 3–5, and Supplementary Tables 11–12 show well-calibrated null $\chi^2$ means (~1.00) on non-causal chromosome halves in ancestry-stratified WTCCC2 simulations (where unadjusted regression is inflated by 35%) and complete elimination of the spurious *LCT* association with height in WGHS. Furthermore, the Discussion (line 57) transparently delineates eight scenarios where `BOLT-LMM` has limitations (e.g., ascertained case-control studies of rare diseases with prevalence <5%, strong family structure, very rare variants, and non-human LD regimes where $x_{\text{test}}' V^{-1} x_{\text{test}}$ is non-constant).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### Major Issues, Minor Issues & Concrete Recommendations

#### 1. Clarify the Behavior of the Retrospective Denominator Approximation Across Minor Allele Frequency Bins and High-Relatedness Samples (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** Equations (8) and (11) replace the SNP-specific prospective denominator $x_{\text{test}}' V_{\text{LOCO}}^{-1} x_{\text{test}}$ with a global calibration constant $c_{\text{inf}}$ (or $c$). While true for common variants in unrelated cohorts where normalized $x_{\text{test}}$ vectors have similar projections onto the eigenvectors of $K$, markers with strong subpopulation differentiation ($F_{ST}$) or rare alleles clustered in cryptically related families can have smaller $x_{\text{test}}' V_{\text{LOCO}}^{-1} x_{\text{test}}$ than random SNPs.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the variance of the ratio $(x_{\text{test}}' V_{\text{LOCO}}^{-1} y)^2 / (c_{\text{inf}} \chi_{\text{LMM-LOCO}}^2)$ stratified by MAF and $F_{ST}$ in the WTCCC2 and WGHS cohorts.
* **HOW:** Add a supplementary plot or note showing the distribution of $x_{\text{test}}' V_{\text{LOCO}}^{-1} x_{\text{test}}$ across MAF deciles (0.01 to 0.50) and across top-PC loading deciles to show precisely where the constant-denominator approximation holds within <1%.

#### 2. Editorial and Typographical Fixes in Equations and Text (Minor Issue)
* **WHY:** Two minor typographical errors in the Online Methods equations and text should be corrected for mathematical precision.
* **WHAT & HOW:**

  2. Line 133 (`Step 2a`): Fix typo *"Gaussian mixture model association analyis"* to *"Gaussian mixture model association analysis"*.
  3. Line 137 (`Step 2a`): In the parameter grid $p \in \{0.5, 0.2.0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01\}$, replace the period between `0.2` and `0.1` with a comma: $p \in \{0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01\}$.
