### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates whether the T cell response to anti-PD-1 checkpoint blockade in human skin cancers is driven by reinvigoration of pre-existing tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) or recruitment of novel T cell clonotypes. The authors generate paired 5' droplet scRNA-seq and single-cell TCR-seq profiles across 79,046 cells from site-matched serial tumor biopsies before and after anti-PD-1 therapy in 11 patients with advanced basal cell carcinoma (BCC; 53,030 total cells, 33,106 TILs, 28,371 with paired TCRs) and 4 patients with cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (SCC; 26,016 TILs), complemented by whole-exome sequencing (WES), bulk TCR$\beta$ sequencing of tumors (8/11 BCC patients) and peripheral blood (5 patients, 10 samples), and IHC. Clustering CD8+ TILs reveals that the largest clonally expanded populations co-express chronic activation, exhaustion (*PDCD1*, *HAVCR2*, *TIGIT*), tissue-residency (*ITGAE*/CD103, *CXCR6*), and tumor-reactivity (*ENTPD1*/CD39) markers (Fig. 2). By tracking exact TCR$\beta$ clonotypes and GLIPH specificity groups across timepoints, the authors report that pre-existing exhausted CD8+ clones rarely transition to non-exhausted effector states or expand post-therapy; instead, 84% of post-treatment exhausted CD8+ clones per patient (55% of pooled clones with $\ge 5$ cells in BCC, and 50% of expanded exhausted clones in SCC) consist of novel clonotypes undetected in the pre-treatment biopsy, 11.8% of which were already detectable in pre-treatment peripheral blood (Fig. 4). All scRNA/TCR-seq (GEO `GSE123814`), WES (SRA `PRJNA533341`), and bulk TCR-seq (`ImmuneACCESS`) datasets are publicly deposited, while custom analysis scripts are only noted as "available upon request."

Pairing longitudinal single-cell transcriptomics with single-cell and deep bulk TCR sequencing in site-matched human biopsies before and after PD-1 blockade is a powerful design that directly links clonotype dynamics to cell states in patients. However, the central inference of "clonal replacement" (that pre-existing exhausted clones are replaced by newly recruited clones rather than persisting or expanding) is sensitive to a statistical detection-threshold asymmetry ("regression to the mean" / zero-truncation bias) and spatial intra-tumoral sampling variance. Specifically, defining a clone as "novel" requires 0 cells (or 0 bulk reads) in the pre-treatment biopsy and $\ge 5$ cells (or significant binomial expansion) in the post-treatment biopsy. Because pre-treatment tumors have much lower overall CD3+ T cell infiltration (Fig. 1b) and lower sample TCR template counts in several patients (bulk TCR templates range from 554 to 99,264; Methods, line 91), clones present at low frequency pre-treatment fall below the sampling floor and are classified as "novel" when overall T cell infiltration increases post-treatment. Furthermore, the control against spatial biopsy heterogeneity rests on a single patient (`su001`, Extended Data Fig. 8d), and clinical response stratification (responders vs. non-responders) is not integrated into the clonal replacement tests. These analytical limitations can be addressed by downsampling/power-matching pre- and post-treatment repertoires and modeling spatial binomial sampling uncertainty.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Clonally expanded CD8+ TILs in BCC and SCC are enriched for an exhausted/chronically activated transcriptional phenotype co-expressing tumor-reactivity markers CD39 (*ENTPD1*) and CD103 (*ITGAE*): [Well Supported].**  
  Across 28,371 TCR-paired TILs in BCC and 26,016 TILs in SCC, Gini clonality indices, clone size distributions, and module scores consistently show that the largest clones reside in the exhausted and exhausted/activated clusters co-expressing *PDCD1*, *HAVCR2*, *TIGIT*, *ENTPD1*, and *ITGAE* (Fig. 2d–g, Extended Data Fig. 10b–d), and FACS sorting of CD8+CD39+ T cells in sample `su010-S` confirms enrichment of the top unsupervised exhausted clonotypes (Extended Data Fig. 10d).

* **T cells sharing an identical TCR clonotype or GLIPH specificity motif exhibit restricted phenotypic states and minimal transition from exhausted to non-exhausted effector states post-PD-1 blockade: [Well Supported].**  
  Permutation tests confirm that cells within a clone or GLIPH paratope group share cluster identities significantly more often than size-matched random T cells (Fig. 3a–c, Extended Data Fig. 6a–d), and longitudinal tracking of persistent clones shows that pre-treatment exhausted clones remain locked in the exhausted state post-treatment (Fig. 3d–e).

* **Post-treatment exhausted CD8+ TILs are predominantly derived from novel clonotypes ("clonal replacement") rather than expansion of pre-existing TILs: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While 84% of post-treatment exhausted clones per patient (and 55% of clones with $\ge 5$ cells across patients) were undetected in pre-treatment scTCR-seq—and bulk TCR-seq confirms novel expanded clones—part of the contrast between exhausted CD8+ clones and memory/effector CD8+ clones (55% vs. 20% novel; Fig. 4b–c) is confounded by asymmetric sampling depth and clone-size conditioning. In several patients, pre-treatment biopsies yielded far fewer total T cells and bulk TCR templates (down to 554 templates) than post-treatment biopsies (Fig. 1b). If a pre-existing clone is present at 0.05% frequency in a pre-treatment sample of 500 T cells, the Poisson probability of observing 0 cells pre-treatment is $\exp(-0.25) = 78\%$; if that clone expands or is sampled in a larger post-treatment collection of 3,000 T cells to reach $\ge 5$ cells, it is labeled "novel." Moreover, the spatial sampling control (two pre-treatment and two post-treatment biopsies) was performed in only $N=1$ patient (`su001`, Extended Data Fig. 8d).

* **Novel tumor-infiltrating exhausted T cell clones are recruited from peripheral blood and drive a new wave of neoepitope immunoediting: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Detecting 11.8% of novel exhausted TIL clonotypes in pre-treatment peripheral blood (Fig. 4g) demonstrates that a subset of post-treatment TIL clones circulated systemically before therapy. However, 88.2% of novel exhausted TIL clones were *not* detected in pre-treatment blood despite deep bulk sequencing (mean 113,528 templates), and the WES neoepitope loss analysis (Fig. 1c, Extended Data Fig. 1a–c) does not functionally link any specific novel TCR clonotype to any lost tumor neoepitope or correlate clonal replacement magnitude with clinical RECIST response.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Asymmetric Pre- vs. Post-Treatment Sampling Depth Using Rarefaction and Zero-Truncated Binomial Models
* **WHY:** Total T cell recovery in scTCR-seq and template counts in bulk TCR-seq (ranging over 170-fold from 554 to 99,264 templates; Methods, line 91) vary substantially between pre- and post-treatment biopsies. When pre-treatment sampling depth is lower than post-treatment depth, rare pre-existing clones frequently yield zero counts pre-treatment by chance and are misclassified as "novel" upon post-treatment expansion. Furthermore, conditioning on clones that expanded post-treatment (or have $\ge 5$ cells post-treatment) introduces selection bias ("winner's curse") favoring clones that had low/zero random counts at baseline.
* **WHAT:** Recompute the fraction of "novel" expanded clones after downsampling (rarefying) pre- and post-treatment scTCR-seq and bulk TCR-seq libraries to matched template depths within each patient, and run a time-reversal symmetry check.
* **HOW:**
  1. For each patient, randomly subsample the larger timepoint (typically post-treatment) down to the exact number of TCR-assigned cells (for scTCR-seq) or productive TCR templates (for bulk TCR-seq) of the smaller timepoint across 1,000 bootstrap iterations, and recalculate the novel exhausted clone fractions in Fig. 4b, c, f, and k.
  2. Perform the symmetric time-reversed calculation: take all exhausted clones with $\ge 5$ cells in the *pre-treatment* biopsy and calculate what fraction are "completely lost" (0 cells) in the *post-treatment* biopsy compared to memory clones. If spatial heterogeneity or sampling noise drives part of the turnover, pre-treatment clones will show symmetric "clonal disappearance."

#### 2. Stratify Clonal Replacement and Neoepitope Loss by Clinical Response (RECIST) and Concurrent Vismodegib Treatment
* **WHY:** In the Methods ("Human subjects", line 61), the authors note that response was assessed by RECIST v1.1 and that "a subset of patients received ongoing treatment with 150 mg vismodegib daily (Supplementary Table 1)." However, the main text analyses (Figs. 1–4) pool all 11 BCC and 4 SCC patients without testing whether clonal replacement of exhausted CD8+ T cells or WES mutational/neoepitope clearance differs between clinical responders and non-responders, or between anti-PD-1 monotherapy and concurrent Hedgehog-inhibitor (vismodegib) therapy (which is known to remodel the BCC microenvironment and induce T cell infiltration).
* **WHAT:** Stratify the clonal replacement metrics (Fig. 4d, f) and neoepitope loss (Fig. 1c) by clinical response status and vismodegib co-treatment.
* **HOW:** Add clinical response (CR/PR/SD/PD) and vismodegib status annotations to Fig. 1c, Fig. 4d, and Extended Data Fig. 8c, and report non-parametric comparisons (Wilcoxon rank-sum test) of novel exhausted clone expansion between responders and non-responders and between monotherapy vs. vismodegib-treated patients.

#### 3. Eliminate Circularity in the Activation and Exhaustion Gene Signature Definitions
* **WHY:** In Fig. 2f–g, the authors define the 50-gene "activation" and "exhaustion" signatures by computing Pearson correlations of all variable genes with *IFNG* and *HAVCR2* across the exact same CD8+ T cells that are subsequently scored and plotted, and then highlight that high-exhaustion-score cells express *ENTPD1* (CD39) and *ITGAE* (CD103). If *ENTPD1* and *ITGAE* are among the top 50 genes correlated with *HAVCR2* in the dataset, they are baked into the exhaustion module score itself, making the correlation between the exhaustion score and CD39/CD103 expression partly circular.
* **WHAT:** Exclude *ENTPD1*, *ITGAE*, and *KLRG1* from the 50-gene *HAVCR2* exhaustion module before testing their association with exhaustion score in Fig. 2f, or use an externally defined exhaustion gene set (analogous to how the *TCF7*+ signature was taken externally from Im et al. 2016).
* **HOW:** Re-score CD8+ T cells in Fig. 2f using the external bulk CD8+ TIL exhaustion signature from Simoni et al. 2018 (`GSE113590`, already cited in Methods line 193) or leave-one-out module scoring where the plotted marker gene is omitted from the signature calculation.

#### 4. Deposit Custom Analysis Scripts in a Public Repository and Fix Minor Editorial Typos
* **WHY:** While raw and processed sequencing data are exemplary in their public deposition across GEO, SRA, and ImmuneACCESS, the Code Availability statement reads "All custom code used in this work is available upon request" (line 205), which falls short of modern computational reproducibility standards for complex custom GLIPH modifications, clone-matching pipelines, and HoneyBADGER scCNV workflows.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Deposit the R/Python scripts used for Seurat heat-shock/cell-cycle regression, custom GLIPH motif clustering, scTCR-to-bulk-TCR clonotype matching, and figure generation in a public GitHub/Zenodo repository.
  2. Correct minor typos in the text: in Methods line 103, change `Propidium iodine` to `Propidium iodide`; in Methods line 187, change `diffusion psuedotime` to `diffusion pseudotime`; in Extended Data Fig. 7a legend, change `without no clonotype overlap` to `with no clonotype overlap`; and in Extended Data Fig. 10j legend, remove the stray XML tag `</ Figure_Caption >`.
