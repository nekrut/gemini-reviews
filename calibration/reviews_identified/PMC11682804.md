### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Damdinjav et al. report a serologic surveillance study investigating potential avian influenza A(H5N1) spillover into unvaccinated, clinically healthy horses in Mongolia between July 2021 and October 2023. The authors collected 2,160 serum samples from 24 herds (10 horses per herd sampled 3 times per year across 9 timepoints; 14 herds in the wetland-rich Ugiinuur area of Arkangai Province and 10 herds in dry steppe/Gobi-adjacent areas of Bulgan and Uvurkhangai Provinces). Samples were screened sequentially using a commercial influenza A nucleoprotein (NP) ELISA ($n = 997/2,160$ positive), a competitive H5 hemagglutinin ELISA on the NP-positive subset ($9$ positive, $8$ doubtful, $980$ negative), and live-virus microneutralization (VN) assays using clade 2.3.4.4b A/chicken/England/053052/2021 on the 17 H5-reactive/doubtful sera. Two samples (one from Bulgan in October 2021 and one from Arkangai in October 2022) neutralized the H5N1 virus at a titer of 1:20, whereas 13 control sera from horses experimentally inoculated with equine influenza virus (EIV) tested negative across both H5 assays. No raw serologic data tables or repository links are provided, and the published text contains unresolved editorial queries regarding missing appendix citations.

While documenting potential equid exposure to H5 avian influenza viruses is timely given the host-range expansion of clade 2.3.4.4b, the manuscript's central epidemiologic conclusions outstrip its empirical evidence. Finding only 2 samples with borderline 1:20 neutralization titers out of 2,160 total sera (0.09%)—one of which originated in the dry control region with low wild-bird density—does not support the conclusion that *"spillover events are likely frequent."* Furthermore, the statistical and epidemiologic presentation omits the longitudinal repeated-measures structure of the 24-herd cohort (failing to report whether the same animals were sampled across the 9 visits or whether the 17 ELISA-reactive and 2 VN-positive samples showed longitudinal seroconversion/seroreversion), lacks neuraminidase (N1) subtype confirmation or cross-reactivity controls against low-pathogenicity avian H5Nx strains circulating in wild waterfowl, and leaves broken `(Appendix)` cross-references in the text. These issues can be substantially remedied by presenting the full herd- and animal-level longitudinal serologic data, computing design-adjusted seroprevalence confidence intervals, and tempering the claim of frequent spillover.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Two equine serum samples from Mongolia exhibited low-titer (1:20) neutralizing activity against clade 2.3.4.4b H5N1 without cross-reactivity from equine influenza virus (EIV) antibodies: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The sequential three-tier testing cascade (NP ELISA $\rightarrow$ H5 competitive ELISA $\rightarrow$ live-virus neutralization) and the negative results across 13 experimental EIV antisera rule out simple cross-reactivity with endemic H3N8 or H7N7 EIV. However, both neutralizing samples achieved only a borderline titer of 1:20, and no hemagglutination inhibition (HI), western blot, or N1-specific assay was performed to rule out cross-neutralization from antibodies elicited by low-pathogenicity H5Nx avian influenza viruses (e.g., H5N2, H5N3, H5N8) or non-specific serum inhibitors surviving RDE treatment.

* **The seropositive horses represent specific spillover of highly pathogenic Influenza A(H5N1) rather than other avian H5 subtypes: [Fragile].**  
  The screening competitive ELISA targets the H5 hemagglutinin broadly, and microneutralization at a 1:20 dilution against an H5N1 test strain is primarily driven by anti-H5 HA antibodies. Without viral RNA detection, N1-specific serology, or comparative neutralization titers against sympatric low-pathogenicity H5Nx strains from Mongolian waterfowl, attributing a 1:20 H5 neutralization titer exclusively to H5N1 is unconfirmed.

* **H5N1 spillover events into horses are "likely frequent": [Overstated].**  
  Across 2,160 serum samples collected over 2.3 years, only 9 (0.42%) were positive by H5 ELISA and only 2 (0.09%) showed neutralizing activity at 1:20. Moreover, one of the two VN-positive horses was sampled in the Bulgan dry area, which was specifically chosen as a low-exposure region with low wild-bird density, yielding an identical crude count (1/1,260 vs. 1/900) between high-wetland and dry regions. Describing a 0.09% borderline seroreactivity rate as evidence that *"spillover events are likely frequent"* contradicts the quantitative results.

* **High baseline IAV nucleoprotein seropositivity (997/2,160; 46.2%) reflects widespread prior IAV exposure in unvaccinated Mongolian horses: [Well Supported].**  
  Nearly half of all serum samples tested positive for anti-NP antibodies despite all horses being unvaccinated, which is consistent with endemic H3N8 EIV circulation in Mongolian herds and provides a large denominator showing that virtually all IAV seropositivity in these herds (980/997 NP-positive samples) is non-H5.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report the Longitudinal Cohort Structure and Animal-Level Serologic Trajectories
* **WHY:** Collecting 10 horses from 24 herds 3 times per year over 9 sampling occasions ($24 \times 10 \times 9 = 2,160$) induces strong clustering by herd and—if the same horses were resampled—within-animal longitudinal correlation. Treating 2,160 sera as independent samples obscures how many distinct horses were actually tested and whether the 9 H5-ELISA-positive and 2 VN-positive samples represent transient single-timepoint titers or sustained seroconversions in tracked individuals.
* **WHAT:** Clarify whether sampling was longitudinal (repeated sampling of the same 240 horses) or cross-sectional within herds, and provide a summary table of all 17 H5-ELISA positive/doubtful samples.
* **HOW:** Add a table listing for each of the 17 H5-reactive/doubtful samples: Horse ID, Herd ID, Province/ecosystem (Arkangai wetland vs. Bulgan/Uvurkhangai dry), Sampling date, horse age/sex, NP ELISA S/N ratio, H5 competitive ELISA inhibition percentage, VN titer, and the corresponding test results for the same horse/herd at preceding and subsequent sampling timepoints.

#### 2. Compute Cluster-Adjusted Seroprevalence Estimates and Exact Confidence Intervals
* **WHY:** The manuscript reports raw counts ($997$, $9$, $8$, $2$) without proportions, confidence intervals, or ecological comparisons between the 14 wetland herds ($n = 1,260$ samples) and 10 dry-area herds ($n = 900$ samples).
* **WHAT:** Report herd-clustered seroprevalence estimates with 95% confidence intervals for NP ELISA, H5 ELISA, and H5N1 VN across both ecological zones.
* **HOW:** Using the existing herd and location metadata, calculate exact Clopper-Pearson or cluster-robust (generalized estimating equation / random-effects binomial) 95% confidence intervals for NP seropositivity and H5 VN seropositivity overall and stratified by wetland vs. dry province, explicitly noting that the difference in VN positivity between Arkangai (1/1,260; 0.08%) and Bulgan/Uvurkhangai (1/900; 0.11%) is statistically indistinguishable ($p = 1.0$, Fisher's exact test).

#### 3. Temper Claims Regarding Spillover Frequency and Subtype Specificity
* **WHY:** A 0.09% detection rate (2/2,160) at the minimum 1:20 dilution threshold without N1 subtype confirmation does not support the claim that H5N1 spillovers into horses are frequent.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Revise the Discussion sentence on line 80 from *"Our findings show that horses are susceptible to infection by H5N1 viruses and that spillover events are likely frequent"* to *"Our findings provide serologic evidence that sporadic spillover of avian H5 influenza viruses into horses can occur, although confirmed neutralizing reactivity was rare (2/2,160 samples; 0.09%) and observed at low titer (1:20)."* Explicitly note the caveat that H5 seroreactivity could also reflect exposure to low-pathogenicity avian H5Nx subtypes.

#### 4. Resolve Missing Appendix Citations and Provide Raw Assay Data
* **WHY:** Lines 76 and 99–103 contain unresolved production queries (`"No appendix matches the in-text citation '(Appendix)'. Please supply the missing appendix or delete the citation"`), leaving the experimental EIV cross-reactivity controls and microneutralization protocol undocumented in the main text.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Supply the missing Appendix file (detailing the 13 experimental EIV control sera, RDE treatment, ELISA cutoffs, and BSL-3 live-virus neutralization starting dilutions and readout criteria) and deposit a de-identified CSV of the 2,160 sample metadata and ELISA/VN readouts.
