### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents the reference genome assembly (`gasAcu1.0`, 463 Mb, 9.0x Sanger coverage, N50 scaffold size 10.8 Mb anchored to linkage groups) for the threespine stickleback (*Gasterosteus aculeatus*) alongside whole-genome resequencing of 20 additional individuals (average 2.3x Illumina coverage per individual, 48x combined) sampled across global marine and freshwater population pairs. Using a self-organizing map/Hidden Markov Model (SOM/HMM) phylogenetic clustering approach and a pairwise nucleotide divergence cluster separation score (CSS) across 2,500 bp sliding windows, the authors identify 242 genomic regions (0.5% of the genome; 147 shared between methods) exhibiting parallel marine-freshwater divergence, including the canonical *EDA* locus and three large chromosomal inversions (on chromosomes I, XI, and XXI) validated via marine BAC paired-end anomalies. By intersecting the top 64 strictest parallel divergence windows with Ensembl gene annotations and common-garden microarray expression data from Little Campbell River (marine) and Fish Trap Creek (freshwater) tissues, the authors estimate that at least 41% of parallel adaptive loci map entirely to non-coding regions and only 17% contain consistent non-synonymous ecotype substitutions, concluding that regulatory changes predominate over protein-coding changes during repeated stickleback adaptation. The reference genome and sequence data are publicly archived, though custom source code for the SOM/HMM, CSS, and data-driven clustering (DDC) algorithms is only described algorithmically in the supplement rather than deposited in a public code repository.

As a foundational genomic resource and genome-wide scan for parallel ecological divergence, the reference assembly, BAC-validated inversion breakpoints, and recovery of shared standing variation islands are technically strong. However, the evolutionary inferences regarding the exact proportion of regulatory versus coding changes (83% vs. 17%) and the local hybrid-zone reuse fraction (35.3%) carry methodological caveats driven by the low per-individual sequencing depth (2.3x) and window-based classification rules. At 2.3x coverage per individual, requiring a "perfect allele-ecotype association" across 21 fish is sensitive to stochastic allele dropout at heterozygous sites and missing data, which can differentially filter single-nucleotide coding variants versus multi-SNP regulatory haplotypes. Furthermore, classifying an entire 43% intermediate category (regions containing both coding and non-coding sequence without a fixed amino acid substitution) as purely regulatory overlooks synonymous selection, splicing alterations, and uncalled coding indels or copy-number variants. These limitations can be addressed through genotype-likelihood uncertainty propagation and sensitivity testing across allele-association thresholds without generating new sequencing data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **High-contiguity reference genome assembly (`gasAcu1.0`) and recovery of 147–242 globally shared marine-freshwater divergent loci: [Well Supported].**  
  The 9.0x Sanger assembly achieves high contiguity (83.2 kb contig N50, 10.8 Mb scaffold N50, 86.9% anchored to linkage groups). Despite low individual resequencing coverage (2.3x), pooling information across 2,500 bp windows in CSS and local trees in SOM/HMM aggregates sufficient SNP density to recover the positive-control 16 kb *EDA* haplotype breakpoints (Fig. 2) and validate 91% (10/11) of tested novel regions via independent Sequenom genotyping across external populations.

* **Identification of three marine-freshwater chromosomal inversions on chrI, chrXI, and chrXXI: [Well Supported].**  
  The elevated CSS plateaus (442 kb, 412 kb, and 1,700 kb) and sharp DDC transitions are orthogonally confirmed by physical mapping of anomalous paired-end reads from a marine large-insert (~220 kb) BAC library against the freshwater reference assembly (Fig. 5a–b), identification of flanking inverted repeats, and allele frequency shifts across hybrid zones (Supplementary Table 10).

* **Regulatory changes predominate over protein-coding changes (83% vs. 17%) across the 64 strictest parallel adaptive regions: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While 41% of the 64 top regions fall entirely in non-coding sequence and divergent regions are enriched near genes differentially expressed in common-garden fish ($P < 7.1 \times 10^{-8}$, Fig. 6b), assigning the entire 43% mixed coding/non-coding category to regulatory evolution because they lack SNPs with "perfect allele-ecotype association" causing amino acid changes is fragile at 2.3x coverage. At 2.3x depth, individual genotypes have high missingness and heterozygote undercalling; a causal non-synonymous SNP that is missed in one or two low-coverage individuals or segregates at 90% rather than 100% frequency is excluded from the 17% coding bin, whereas a wider regulatoryLD block has more candidate SNPs available to pass the strict association filter by chance.

* **Globally shared parallel regions account for 35.3% of the top 0.1% differentiated windows across the River Tyne hybrid zone: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The River Tyne comparison is based on whole-genome sequencing of a single marine and a single freshwater individual from opposite ends of the river (Fig. 4a–b). Estimating the genome-wide distribution of local differentiation from $N=1$ diploid individual per ecotype conflates true ecotypic fixation with individual-level sampling variance and local background linkage disequilibrium, and the pair is not independent of the 21-fish discovery panel (which already includes the River Tyne pair).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Propagate Low-Coverage Genotype Uncertainty in the Coding vs. Regulatory Classification
* **WHY:** At 2.3x average Illumina coverage per fish, calling discrete genotypes or requiring "perfect allele-ecotype association" across the 21 individuals suffers from high false-negative rates for single-site non-synonymous variants due to coverage dropout, biasing the 64-region breakdown against single-SNP coding changes relative to multi-SNP non-coding haplotypes.
* **WHAT:** Re-evaluate the coding versus non-coding classification of the 147 consensus regions (and the 64 strictest regions) using probabilistic genotype likelihoods and relaxed ecotype-association thresholds (e.g., $\ge 80\%$ and $\ge 90\%$ allele frequency difference) rather than strict 100% concordance.
* **HOW:** Compute site-level marine vs. freshwater allele frequency posteriors directly from read pileups in the 21 BAM files (accounting for binomial sampling variance at 2.3x depth), re-tabulate the proportion of regions in Fig. 6a containing high-probability non-synonymous variants, splice-site variants, or small indels across a grid of frequency thresholds, and report 95% bootstrap confidence intervals on the coding (17%), mixed (43%), and non-coding (41%) fractions.

#### 2. Control for Local Recombination Rate and Background Selection in CSS and SOM/HMM Outlier Scans
* **WHY:** Both CSS (based on pairwise $\pi$ matrices) and SOM/HMM identify regions of reduced within-ecotype diversity relative to between-ecotype divergence. In regions of low recombination (such as centromeres or inversions), background selection and local hitchhiking reduce within-population $\pi$ and inflate relative divergence metrics even without parallel adaptive allele reuse.
* **WHAT:** Stratify the 242 divergent regions and the gene-density / conserved non-coding element (CNS) enrichment tests (Supplementary Figs. 8–9) by local genetic-to-physical map ratio (cM/Mb) derived from the F2 linkage cross used to anchor the reference assembly.
* **HOW:** Using the F2 intercross marker positions and physical scaffold coordinates, compute local recombination rates (cM/Mb) in 1 Mb windows; test whether CSS scores and recovered outlier regions correlate with low recombination outside the three inversions; and re-run the permutation tests for gene density ($P < 4.5 \times 10^{-13}$) and CNS enrichment ($P < 1.9 \times 10^{-11}$) matched on local recombination rate and GC content.

#### 3. Eliminate Circularity in the River Tyne Hybrid-Zone Parallel Reuse Estimate
* **WHY:** The two River Tyne individuals sequenced to define the "top 0.1% divergent windows" in the local hybrid zone (Fig. 4b) are part of the 21-individual global discovery cohort used to calculate the global CSS scores, introducing positive covariance between the single-pair divergence metric and the global CSS metric.
* **WHAT:** Recompute the global CSS distribution leaving out the two River Tyne individuals ($N=19$ fish) before evaluating the overlap with the River Tyne pair's top 0.1% divergent windows.
* **HOW:** Re-run the 2,500 bp sliding-window CSS pipeline on the 19×19 pairwise divergence matrices excluding the Tyne marine and freshwater fish, and recalculate the percentage of the Tyne top 0.1% divergent windows that exceed the 5% and 2% FDR global CSS thresholds.

#### 4. Temper Mechanistic Claims on the Mixed Coding/Non-Coding Regions and Single-Pair Microarray Data
* **WHY:** In the Abstract and Main Text, the 43% of top regions that span both coding and non-coding sequences without a fixed amino acid substitution are treated as "likely contribut[ing] to adaptive divergence by regulatory alterations," and expression differences between a single population pair (LITC marine vs. FTC freshwater) are used to support parallel regulatory evolution.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly qualify that the 43% mixed category may harbor uncalled coding indels, copy-number variants, or synonymous variants affecting mRNA stability/translation, and note that a single population pair microarray cannot distinguish parallel *cis*-regulatory divergence from lineage-specific *trans*-acting changes.
* **HOW:** Revise the text in "Proportion of regulatory & coding change" and the Discussion to present 41% (purely non-coding) as the strict lower bound and 83% (non-coding + mixed) as the upper bound for regulatory predominance, and clarify that the LITC vs. FTC expression enrichment reflects one Pacific pair rather than replicated multi-basin expression profiling.

#### 5. Deposit Executable Scripts for the SOM/HMM, CSS, and DDC Pipelines
* **WHY:** While the sequencing reads and genome assembly are deposited in public archives, the three core statistical algorithms introduced in this paper (SOM/HMM, CSS, and Bayesian DDC) are only described in prose in the Supplementary Information, limiting exact computational reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Package the source scripts used to compute the 21×21 window distance matrices, CSS permutation FDRs, SOM/HMM tree transitions, and DDC cluster assignments, and deposit them in a permanent public archive or supplementary code bundle alongside the exact coordinates of all 242 divergent regions. Also correct the double period typo in the Methods Summary ("...possible chromosome rearrangements.. Sequenom iPlex...").
