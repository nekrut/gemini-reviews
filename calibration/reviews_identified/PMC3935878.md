### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces BAMM (Bayesian Analysis of Macroevolutionary Mixtures), a reversible-jump Markov Chain Monte Carlo (rjMCMC) framework designed to infer the number, phylogenetic locations, and parameters of macroevolutionary regime shifts across time-calibrated trees of extant species. Under a compound Poisson process, lineages transition between distinct evolutionary regimes characterized by exponentially time-varying speciation rates ($\lambda(t) = \lambda_{0,i}\exp(z_i t_i)$) and constant extinction rates ($\mu_i$). The author evaluates BAMM across 3,000 simulated phylogenies spanning six scenarios (500 replicates each of a single-process constant-rate birth-death model, a two-process exponential-change model, and two- to five-process linear diversity-dependent models), benchmarks its performance against the stepwise AICc method MEDUSA, and applies it to an empirical phylogeny of 87 extant cetacean species. Both the C++ source code (`www.bamm-project.org`) and the simulated tree datasets (Dryad `doi:10.5061/dryad.hn1vn`) are publicly archived.

BAMM represents a substantial methodological advance over piecewise constant-rate diversification models by jointly sampling regime partitions and within-regime temporal rate trajectories. The simulation benchmarks and prior sensitivity analyses ($\gamma \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1, 5, 10\}$) are thorough, and the manuscript candidly reports the upward bias and low explained variance ($r^2 = 0.10\text{--}0.59$) of extinction rate estimates. However, three methodological and framing issues temper the conclusions: (1) the differential equation for the extinction probability $E(t)$ (Equations 11–12) ignores the possibility of unobserved regime transitions along extinct side-branches and in root-survival conditioning; (2) the simulation benchmark against MEDUSA omits multi-process constant-rate birth-death scenarios where MEDUSA's within-regime model is well-specified; and (3) the title and text frame exponential time-variation ($\lambda(t)$) as direct detection of diversity-dependence without testing whether the model can distinguish diversity-dependent regulation from abiotic temporal slowdowns. All three issues can be addressed through targeted mathematical clarification, additional constant-rate multi-shift simulations, and more precise framing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **BAMM accurately recovers the number of evolutionary regimes and branch-specific speciation rates under time-varying and diversity-dependent simulations: [Well Supported].**  
  Across 2,500 multi-process simulations (Figures 4 and 6), BAMM recovers the true regime count or underfits by at most one regime in the majority of replicates while avoiding overfitting (>95% of MAP models have $\le k_{\text{true}}$ regimes under $\gamma = 1$). Branch-specific speciation rate OLS slopes cluster near 1.0 (mean $> 0.85$ across all scenarios, reaching 0.95 in DD5) with mean relative rate differences near 1.0.

* **MEDUSA severely underestimates regime numbers and branch-specific speciation rates when within-regime rates vary through time: [Well Supported].**  
  When applied to the same 2,500 time-varying and diversity-dependent trees (Figures 7 and 8), MEDUSA recovers the true number of regimes in only 40.2% of DD2 replicates (vs. 90% for BAMM) and $<5\%$ of DD5 replicates (vs. 38.4% for BAMM), with modal regression slopes of 0.0 for branch-specific speciation rates. However, because no multi-regime constant-rate ($z_i = 0$) scenario was included, the comparison only evaluates MEDUSA under model misspecification.

* **BAMM automatically detects diversity-dependence on phylogenetic trees: [Overstated].**  
  BAMM does not model lineage diversity $n_t$ or carrying capacity $K$ (Equation 14); instead, it fits an exponential function of elapsed time since the regime shift, $\lambda(t_i) = \lambda_{0,i}\exp(z_i t_i)$ (Equation 1). While negative $z_i$ approximates the temporal deceleration produced by linear diversity-dependence during clade expansion, the model cannot distinguish diversity-dependent regulation from extrinsic, environmentally driven declines in speciation rate, nor does it model clade-wide diversity dependence once a subclade reaches equilibrium or interacts ecologically with parent lineages.

* **The cetacean radiation is governed by two macroevolutionary regimes with a major rate shift leading to the Delphinidae: [Moderately Supported].**  
  BAMM finds a posterior probability of $0.975$ for a rate shift on one of the branches leading to crown Delphinidae (excluding *Orcinus orca*), with a posterior odds ratio of 44.6 favoring $M_1$ (two processes) over $M_0$ (one process) and consistent marginal speciation rates across priors $\gamma \in [0.1, 10]$ (Figures 9 and 10). Support is moderated by the fixed tree topology and branch lengths, as well as the omitted effect of downstream rate shifts on root-survival conditioning and $E(t)$ propagation.

* **Branch-specific extinction rates can be estimated across multi-process phylogenies using BAMM: [Fragile].**  
  As the author transparently documents in Table 1 and Figure 5B, extinction estimates exhibit substantial upward bias (proportional error $1.27\text{--}1.85$) and explain very little variance in the true generating rates across diversity-dependent scenarios ($r^2 = 0.10\text{--}0.16$), confirming that molecular phylogenies of extant taxa carry minimal information about regime-specific $\mu_i$.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Address Unobserved Regime Transitions in the Extinction Probability $E(t)$ and Root Conditioning
* **WHY:** Equations 10–13 compute $E(t)$ and $D(t)$ along each branch segment using only the local regime parameters $(\lambda_i, \mu_i)$, passing $E_0$ continuously across mapped shift locations $\xi_i$. Under a compound Poisson process with transition rate $\Lambda$, any lineage that sprouts from an observed branch and goes extinct before the present could itself experience a transition to a new regime $\Phi'$ prior to extinction. Ignoring unobserved transitions in $dE/dt$ and conditioning root survival solely on the root regime parameters $(\lambda_0, \mu_0)$ introduces a mathematical inconsistency between the generative compound Poisson process and the likelihood function.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state that Equations 10–13 represent a conditional approximation that assumes zero transitions occur on extinct side-lineages, and evaluate whether passing $E(t)$ across large shifts in $\lambda$ and $\mu$ induces likelihood pathologies.
* **HOW:** Add a subsection in *Materials and Methods* clarifying that the likelihood conditions on no regime shifts along unobserved extinct lineages (valid when per-branch transition probability $\Lambda / T \ll 1$), and check how root-survival conditioning ($1 - E_{\text{root}}(t_{\text{root}}))^2$ behaves when a major shift with high $\lambda_i$ occurs near the base of the tree.

#### 2. Add a Symmetric Multi-Regime Constant-Rate Birth-Death Benchmark Against MEDUSA
* **WHY:** All five multi-process simulation scenarios (exp2, DD2–DD5) include strong temporal rate variation within regimes, guaranteeing that MEDUSA is evaluated exclusively under model misspecification. A symmetric benchmark requires testing whether BAMM also outperforms or matches MEDUSA when MEDUSA's assumption of piecewise constant rates ($z_i = 0$) holds.
* **WHAT:** Simulate 500 trees under a multi-process constant-rate birth-death model (e.g., 2 to 4 regimes with $z_i = 0$) and compare regime recovery and branch-rate accuracy between full BAMM ($z_i$ free), constrained BAMM ($z_i = 0$), and MEDUSA.
* **HOW:** Use the existing C++ simulation pipeline to generate 500 trees with 2 and 3 constant-rate shifts ($\lambda \sim \text{Uniform}(0.05, 0.40)$, $\mu / \lambda \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 0.99)$, $z = 0$), run BAMM and Geiger's `medusa`, and add a summary panel to Figures 4, 6, 7, and 8.

#### 3. Quantify Discretization Step-Size Sensitivity ($\Delta t = 1.0$)
* **WHY:** The piecewise-constant discretization of $\lambda(t)$ uses a fixed step size of $\Delta t = 1.0$ time units (Line 190). While $\Delta t = 1.0$ represents $1\%$ of tree depth on the simulated trees ($T = 100$), on empirical trees with different time scales (e.g., total depth of $5\text{--}35$ Ma in cetaceans) or rapid exponential decay ($|z| \approx 0.10$), a fixed 1.0-unit step may be too coarse and bias likelihood ratios during rjMCMC updates of $\xi_i$ and $z_i$.
* **WHAT:** Benchmark the likelihood calculation and posterior inference across finer discretization intervals ($\Delta t \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0\}$) or scale $\Delta t$ relative to total tree depth.
* **HOW:** Re-compute log-likelihoods on a subset of 50 exp2 trees and the cetacean phylogeny with $\Delta t = 0.1$ versus $\Delta t = 1.0$, report the maximum absolute log-likelihood discrepancy, and set default step sizes in BAMM as a fraction of root age (e.g., $T_{\text{root}} / 200$).

#### 4. Temper Claims Regarding Direct Detection of Diversity-Dependence
* **WHY:** Because Equation 1 models speciation strictly as a function of time elapsed since $\xi_i$ rather than contemporaneous lineage count $n_t$, BAMM detects temporal rate deceleration ($z_i < 0$), which is necessary but not sufficient evidence for diversity-dependent regulation.
* **WHAT:** Reframe claims in the Title, Abstract, and Discussion to distinguish between directly modeling time-varying diversification rates and approximating the temporal signature of diversity-dependence.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract and Discussion to state that BAMM detects *time-varying diversification regimes* that can capture the macroevolutionary signature of diversity-dependent slowdowns, while noting that distinguishing biotic diversity-dependence from abiotic temporal rate shifts requires explicit lineage-dependent likelihoods or paleontological data.
