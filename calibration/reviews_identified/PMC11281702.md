### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This retrospective surveillance study screens nasopharyngeal/oropharyngeal swabs and serum samples from 546 cats (376 from veterinary hospitals/households and 170 from animal shelters across 10 provinces in South Korea, collected between April 2021 and March 2022) for SARS-CoV-2 using RT-qPCR (`ORF3a` and `N` genes) and a multi-species indirect `N`-protein ELISA. Overall, 19 of 546 cats (3.48%) were seropositive (4.26% in households vs. 1.76% in shelters) and 1 household cat (0.18%, Case #17, `JBNU-cat`, sampled in February 2022 from a COVID-19-positive owner's household in Jeonbuk) tested positive for viral RNA (`Ct = 25.32` for `ORF3a`, `25.18` for `N`). The authors isolated infectious virus (`JBNU-cat`) in Vero E6 cells, confirmed replication over three passages via RT-qPCR, $\text{TCID}_{50}$, and immunofluorescence, sequenced the whole genome (`29,841` nt, $14,783\times$ depth; GISAID `EPI_ISL_16577826`), and placed the isolate within Omicron sublineage BA.1.1 via maximum likelihood phylogenetic analysis alongside 121 human genomes from South Korea.

The isolation and full-genome sequencing of an infectious Omicron BA.1.1 virus from a symptomatic household cat owned by a COVID-19 patient provide solid empirical evidence of anthroponotic (human-to-cat) transmission during South Korea's early-2022 Omicron wave. However, the manuscript suffers from several methodological omissions, internal contradictions, and over-interpretations: (1) the Discussion states that *all* 546 cats exhibited COVID-19-like clinical signs (fever $> 39.5\ ^\circ\text{C}$ or cough), which contradicts the Methods claim that cats were "randomly selected" and precludes testing for associations between serostatus and symptoms; (2) whole-genome sequencing was performed on the Vero E6 cell-culture isolate rather than the primary clinical swab, and the Discussion speculates about "selective pressures exerted by the feline host environment" without distinguishing standard Omicron BA.1.1 lineage mutations (or Vero E6 passage adaptations) from unique feline substitutions; (3) Table 2 presents Plaque Reduction Neutralization Test (PRNT) titers for all 19 cats, but PRNT methodology is completely absent from *Materials and Methods*; and (4) Table 2 lists three animal-shelter cats (Cases #7, #13, #14) as having a "COVID-19 Patient Owner = Yes." Addressing these discrepancies and distinguishing lineage-defining from isolate-specific mutations will substantially strengthen the paper.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Infectious SARS-CoV-2 Omicron BA.1.1 (`JBNU-cat`) was isolated from a symptomatic household cat living with a COVID-19-positive owner in South Korea: [Well Supported].**  
  Case #17 yielded clear RT-qPCR amplification (`Ct = 25.32` and `25.18`), produced cytopathic effects and Spike immunofluorescence across serial passages in Vero E6 cells (Figure 1, Table 3), and clustered within human Omicron BA.1.1 sequences from South Korea collected during February 2022 (Figure 2, `EPI_ISL_16577826`). While whole-genome sequencing of the owner's specimen was not available to establish a direct 1-to-1 transmission pair, the epidemiological and phylogenetic evidence for human-to-cat spillover is strong.

* **Overall SARS-CoV-2 seroprevalence in the sampled feline cohort was 3.48% (19/546), with active viral shedding in 0.18% (1/546): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The regional testing totals (Table 1) and serology/neutralization summary (Table 2) document 19 seropositive cats. However, because Section 2.1 states that cats were "randomly selected" while Line 228 states that "Of the 546 cats included in the study, all exhibited at least one COVID-19-related clinical sign, such as fever (body temperature above 39.5 °C) and cough," the sample represents a clinically symptomatic veterinary convenience cohort rather than a random population sample. In addition, five of the ten provinces have zero animal-shelter samples (`0` in Table 1), and Table 2 reports raw `OD450` values rather than the normalized `S/P (%)` values required by the IDvet cutoff criteria ($\text{S/P} > 60\%$).

* **There is no correlation between SARS-CoV-2 serological status and clinical presentation in cats: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  On Line 228, the authors conclude that because all 546 cats had clinical signs (fever or cough) and only 19 (3.48%) were seropositive, there is *"no direct correlation between serological status and clinical presentation."* If 100% of enrolled cats were required to have clinical signs (zero asymptomatic control cats), clinical presentation is a constant in the dataset; a $2 \times 2$ association between symptoms and seropositivity cannot be statistically evaluated when the asymptomatic group has $N = 0$.

* **Multiple Spike mutations in the `JBNU-cat` isolate suggest selective pressures and adaptation within the feline host environment: [Overstated].**  
  In Section 3.2 (Line 203) and the Discussion (Line 222), the authors compare `JBNU-cat` against Wuhan-Hu-1, Delta, and Omicron reference sequences (Figure 3) and suggest that the observed Spike mutations reflect feline host adaptation. However, Figure 3 merely displays the canonical NTD and RBD mutations shared by human Omicron BA.1.1 relative to Wuhan-Hu-1 and Delta! The authors do not identify a single private mutation unique to `JBNU-cat` relative to contemporaneous Korean human BA.1.1 sequences, and because sequencing was performed after propagation in Vero E6 cells (Section 2.5) without sequencing the primary swab, any private mutation would also have to be vetted against Vero E6 cell-culture adaptation.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Distinguish Human BA.1.1 Lineage-Defining Mutations from Any Private Mutations in `JBNU-cat`
* **WHY:** Comparing `JBNU-cat` to Wuhan-Hu-1 and Delta in Figure 3 conflates the ~30 Spike mutations already fixed in the infecting human Omicron BA.1.1 lineage with de novo mutations arising in the cat. Furthermore, sequencing Vero E6 passage supernatant without comparing against the unpassaged swab cannot rule out Vero E6 cell-culture artifacts.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly list all nucleotide and amino acid differences between `hCoV-19/South Korea/JBNU-cat/2022` (`EPI_ISL_16577826`) and the consensus sequence of its closest South Korean human BA.1.1 relatives in Figure 2, and revise the Discussion to remove claims of feline-specific Spike adaptation unless private mutations confirmed in the primary swab are demonstrated.
* **HOW:** Align `EPI_ISL_16577826` against the closest human BA.1.1 genome in the 121-sequence alignment, tabulate any private SNPs/amino acid substitutions across the 29,841-nt genome, and state clearly in Section 3.2 and the Discussion whether `JBNU-cat`'s Spike sequence is identical to circulating human BA.1.1 strains.

#### 2. Add the Missing PRNT Methodology and Report Calibrated ELISA `S/P (%)` Ratios in Table 2
* **WHY:** Table 2 reports `PRNT Neutralization Titer` values (`1/24`, `1/33`, `1/45`, `>1/5`) for all 19 seropositive cats, but *Materials and Methods* contains no mention of PRNT. In addition, Section 2.3 defines ELISA positivity as `S/P > 60%`, whereas Table 2 lists raw `OD450` values (where Case #17 has `OD450 = 0.5824`, the lowest in the table).
* **WHAT:** Add a dedicated subsection in *Materials and Methods* detailing the PRNT protocol, and add a column to Table 2 reporting the calculated `S/P (%)` value for each of the 19 cats alongside `OD450`.
* **HOW:** In Section 2.3, specify the challenge virus strain used for PRNT (e.g., ancestral Wuhan, Delta, or `JBNU-cat` BA.1.1), cell line (Vero E6), serial dilution series, incubation time, and neutralization endpoint ($\text{PRNT}_{50}$ or $\text{PRNT}_{90}$), and explain what `>1/5` denotes for Cat #8 (e.g., $<1/10$ or $>1/5$). In Table 2, list `ODNC`, `ODPC`, and `S/P (%)` for all 19 samples, explaining why Case #17 (sampled during acute RNA shedding) had a lower `OD450` (`0.5824`) consistent with early seroconversion.

#### 3. Reconcile the Sampling Design (Random Selection vs. Symptomatic Veterinary Cases) and Shelter Owner Metadata
* **WHY:** Stating in Section 2.1 that 546 cats were "randomly selected" contradicts Line 228 ("all exhibited at least one COVID-19-related clinical sign"). Similarly, Table 2 lists Cases #7, #13, and #14 as `Source: Animal shelter` while simultaneously listing `COVID-19 Patient Owner: Yes`, which is self-contradictory unless those cats were surrendered by COVID-19 patients.
* **WHAT:** Clarify the exact inclusion criteria in Section 2.1 and the Discussion, and explain how owner COVID-19 status was known for the three animal-shelter cats in Table 2.
* **HOW:** Revise Section 2.1 to state that the 546 cats were sampled from cats *presenting with respiratory signs or fever* at veterinary hospitals and shelters (or report the true number of asymptomatic vs. symptomatic cats among the 546 if asymptomatic cats were actually included), and add a footnote to Table 2 explaining why shelter cats #7, #13, and #14 are recorded as having a COVID-19-positive owner (e.g., temporary quarantine shelter for pets of hospitalized COVID-19 patients).

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Inconsistent target gene names between the text and figures and unexplained qPCR copy-number conversions reduce technical clarity.
* **WHAT & HOW:**  
  * **Section 2.2 (Line 73) vs. Figure 1A (Line 89):** Section 2.2 and Table 2 state that the qPCR assay targets the `ORF3a` and `N` genes, whereas Figure 1A and its caption state *"The copy number of N and RdRp gene of JBNU-cat measured by qRT-PCR."* Clarify whether a separate `N`/`RdRp` qPCR kit was used for the Vero E6 passage experiments in Figure 1A and Table 3.  
  * **Table 3 (Lines 184–190):** Specify the standard curve equation used to convert `Cq` to `Copy Number (log10)` per mL (or per reaction), and replace `1.285` $\log_{10}$ copies for the unamplified `Negative` control (`Cq = 40`) with `Not Detected (ND)`.  
  * **Table 1 (Lines 116–134):** Replace bare `0` entries for Animal Shelters in Seoul, Gangwon, Chungbuk, Gyeongnam, and Jeju with `Not sampled (—)` to distinguish unsampled strata from `0/N (0.00%)`.  
  * **Table 2, Row 14 (Line 163):** Fix the typo `"Unkown"` to `"Unknown"`.
