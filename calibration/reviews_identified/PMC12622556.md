### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Young et al. present `CellPhenoX`, an explainable machine learning workflow aimed at linking single-cell omics profiles to sample-level clinical outcomes and covariate interactions. The pipeline constructs a cell-level nearest-neighbor graph across all pooled samples, computes a Neighborhood Abundance Matrix (NAM), decomposes the NAM using PCA or Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) followed by Harmony batch correction, trains a Random Forest (or XGBoost) classifier to predict the clinical phenotype $Y$ from cell-level latent embeddings $X_i$, covariates $\gamma$, and interaction terms $X_i \cdot \delta_i$, and aggregates Fast TreeSHAP values across features to produce a cell-specific "Interpretable Score" ($\psi_m = \sum_i \phi_{m,i}$). The authors evaluate `CellPhenoX` on custom Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) simulated single-cell datasets ($N = 30$ subjects, 10 clusters, with differential abundance or sex-by-disease interaction) against `CNA` and `MiloR`, and apply it to three public clinical datasets: COVID-19 PBMC CITE-seq proteomics (healthy $n=24$, mild $n=26$, moderate $n=32$), ulcerative colitis (UC) mucosal fibroblasts (18 paired inflamed vs. non-inflamed biopsies; 14,822 cells), and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) T cells following anti-PD1 therapy (8 clonally expanded vs. 10 non-expanded patients; 24,021 cells).

While the goal of detecting nonlinear covariate-by-cell-state interactions in clinical single-cell cohorts is well-motivated, `CellPhenoX` suffers from a foundational statistical and machine learning flaw: **cell-level pseudoreplication and train/test data leakage across cells from the same patient/donor**. Because clinical outcomes ($Y$: disease status, severity, or treatment expansion) and demographic covariates ($\gamma, \delta$: age, sex, smoking, days from onset) are *patient-level* attributes shared identically by all 500–2,000 cells originating from the same donor, training a cell-level Random Forest ($M$ cells as rows) using stratified $k$-fold cross-validation across cells places cells from the same donor (with identical age, sex, and sample-level NAM profiles) into both training and test folds, and feeds shared graph embeddings (NAM + PCA/NMF + Harmony computed on the full dataset before splitting) into the classifier. Furthermore, the mathematical exposition in the Experimental Section contains multiple dimension mismatches and algebraic errors (e.g., transposing the $N \times M$ NAM definition, summing SHAP values into $\psi_m$ when standard SHAP efficiency guarantees $\sum_i \phi_{m,i} = \hat{f}(x_m) - E[\hat{f}(X)]$, and a broken differential abundance formula in Equation 1). Correcting the method requires strictly donor-grouped cross-validation (splitting by patient ID, constructing the kNN graph and NAM within training folds, and holding out entire patients) and fixing the mathematical definitions.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **`CellPhenoX` provides an unbiased, generalizable classification and SHAP attribution framework for clinical outcomes from single-cell data: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Three severe leakage and pseudoreplication errors invalidate the cross-validation and hold-out performance estimates (e.g., the reported $\text{AUROC} = 0.80, \text{AUPRC} = 0.86$ in UC fibroblasts):
  1. **Cell-level vs. donor-level splitting with patient-level covariates:** In "Classification Model Training and Prediction", the feature matrix has $M$ rows (individual cells), and includes patient-level covariates $\gamma$ (age, sex, smoking status, days from onset) and interaction terms $X_i \cdot \delta_i$. The data are split into training, testing, and validation sets using a "stratified $k$-fold strategy to maintain the relative class proportions in each fold" across cells rather than grouping by donor/sample ID (`GroupKFold`). When thousands of cells from the same patient share the exact same tuple of `(age, sex, days_from_onset, smoking_status)` and the exact same clinical label $Y$, a decision tree can trivially memorize patient-level covariate combinations to achieves near-perfect accuracy on held-out cells from those same patients. Even without covariates, cells from the same biopsy share donor-specific transcriptional signatures.
  2. **Full-dataset graph, NAM, PCA/NMF, and Harmony before CV:** The nearest-neighbor graph $A$, the random-walk Neighborhood Abundance Matrix $Q$, the PCA/NMF decomposition, and Harmony batch correction are all computed once across all $M$ cells and $N$ samples *before* cross-validation. Because the NAM entry $Q_{n,m}$ explicitly measures the fraction of cells from sample $n$ landing in neighborhood $m$ across the entire dataset, test-set cells and test-set samples directly shape the training features.
  3. **Redundancy of the "Interpretable Score" ($\psi_m = \sum_i \phi_{m,i}$) with model predictions:** By the fundamental efficiency (local accuracy) property of Shapley values, the sum of SHAP values across all features $i$ for any instance $m$ equals the model prediction minus the expected baseline prediction: $\sum_i \phi_{m,i} = \hat{f}(\beta_m) - E[\hat{f}(\beta)]$. For binary classification, $\psi_m$ is simply a constant shift of the Random Forest predicted probability (or log-odds). Thus, the claim in Figure 2D that "our Interpretable Score more effectively separates cell types A and J... compared to the random forest model's predicted probabilities" is mathematically impossible unless either (a) covariate SHAP values were excluded from the sum, or (b) `ψ_m` was averaged over CV repeats differently than predicted probabilities.

* **`CellPhenoX` outperforms `CNA` and `MiloR` in simulation benchmarks, including rare cell types and sex-by-disease interactions: [Fragile].**  
  First, the custom simulator (Equations 1–6) does not simulate single-cell count data (e.g., negative binomial or Poisson-lognormal counts with library-size variation and dropout); it generates standard normal vectors $X \in \mathbb{R}^n$ directly as linear additive combinations of Gaussian factor means ($S_{\text{cell type}}, S_{\text{disease}}, S_{\text{sex}}, S_{\text{disease}} \circ S_{\text{sex}}$; Equations 2–6). Injecting a direct cell-level Gaussian shift $w_{\text{disease}} S_{\text{disease}}$ into every cell from a diseased subject means that disease status is directly encoded as a continuous offset on every gene/feature, rather than solely as a change in cluster cell-count proportions. Second, in the `CNA` benchmark ("Benchmarking Methods and Performance Evaluation—CNA"), the authors state: "Specifically, the sample was controlled as covariate from the simulation disease-control single-cell data". Controlling for `sample` (donor ID) as a fixed covariate when testing for a sample-level outcome (`disease` vs. `control`) makes the design matrix collinear (since disease status is nested within sample/subject), crippling `CNA` by regressing out the subject-level disease signal!

* **`CellPhenoX` identifies biologically meaningful myeloid activation in moderate COVID-19, inflammatory WNT2B+ fibroblast transitions in Ulcerative Colitis, and exhausted/cytotoxic T-cell expansion in anti-PD1-treated TNBC: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The biological cell types and marker genes highlighted in the three empirical datasets—CD14+/CD16+/proliferating monocytes expressing `SIGLEC1`, `CD33`, `C5AR1`, `CR1`, `IL1B`, and `CXCL10` in moderate COVID-19 (Figure 4); inflammatory fibroblasts expressing `CCL19`, `CCL8`, `COL6A3`, and `IGFBP5` vs. `WNT2B+` fibroblasts in paired inflamed UC biopsies (Figure 5A–G); and clonally expanded T cells expressing `LAG3`, `CXCL13`, `IFNG`, `GZMB`, and `CTLA4` in anti-PD1 TNBC (Figure 5H–K)—are biologically plausible and match the known findings reported by the original studies of those datasets (Stephenson et al. 2021; Smillie et al. 2019; Bassez et al. 2021). However, the statistical $p$-values reported for the `CellPhenoX` Interpretable Score comparisons (e.g., $p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ across 14,822 fibroblasts from $n=18$ patients in Figure 5C, and Wilcoxon $p$-value across 24,021 T cells from $n=18$ patients in Figure 5I) treat individual cells as independent observations ($N = 14,822$ or $24,021$) rather than testing at the patient/biopsy level ($N = 18$), inflating statistical significance by orders of magnitude.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Enforce Donor-Level Splitting (`GroupKFold`) and Prevent Patient-Covariate Memorization
* **WHY:** Splitting at the cell level (`StratifiedKFold`) puts thousands of cells from the same patient into both training and testing sets. When patient-level covariates (age, sex, days from onset, smoking) are included as cell-level features, tree models memorize the exact covariate fingerprint of each of the 18–82 patients.
* **WHAT:** Partition all cross-validation and hold-out splits strictly at the **patient/donor level** (`StratifiedGroupKFold` grouped by `donor_id`), and compute the cell-specific Interpretable Score by summing SHAP values *only* over the cellular latent embeddings ($X_i$ and $X_i \cdot \delta_i$), excluding the main-effect patient covariates ($\gamma$).
* **HOW:**
  1. Replace `StratifiedKFold` with `sklearn.model_selection.StratifiedGroupKFold(groups=donor_id)` in the outer and inner CV loops so that all cells from a given donor reside entirely in `train` or entirely in `test`.
  2. Fit PCA/NMF on the training-set NAM and project test-set cells into the learned basis (without running Harmony across the combined train+test labels).
  3. Define the cell-specific Interpretable Score explicitly as $\psi_m = \sum_{i \in \text{cellular}} (\phi_{m, X_i} + \phi_{m, X_i \cdot \delta_i})$, omitting $\phi_{m, \gamma}$ so that $\psi_m$ reflects cell-state contributions rather than patient age/sex main effects.

#### 2. Fix the `CNA` Baseline Covariate Specification and Simulator Generative Process
* **WHY:** Passing `sample` (donor ID) as a nuisance covariate to `CNA` when testing a sample-level outcome (`disease` vs. `control`) is collinear with the outcome and artificially destroys `CNA`'s power. Additionally, adding a cell-level `w_disease * S_disease` shift in the GMM simulator alters per-cell expression conditional on disease even within non-expanded clusters, whereas `MiloR` and `CNA` are designed to test differential *abundance* of transcriptional states across samples.
* **WHAT:** Re-run `CNA` without `sample` as a covariate (passing `batch` or `sex` instead, as intended by `CNA`), and benchmark on simulation datasets where disease affects only cluster cell-count proportions ($p_j$) without injecting a global `S_disease` offset into every cell's coordinates.
* **HOW:** In the R script running `cna::association.Seurat`, remove `covs = "sample"` (since `sampleid` is already specified as the sample grouping variable in `CNA`) and update Figure S3.

#### 3. Perform Patient-Level (Pseudobulk / Mixed-Effects) Hypothesis Testing in Empirical Datasets
* **WHY:** Testing differences in $\psi_m$ or correlating gene expression with $\psi_m$ across $M = 14,822$ or $24,021$ individual cells (Figure 4E,F and Figure 5C,F,G,I) treats cells from the same biopsy as independent samples, yielding $p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ even for tiny effects. Similarly, the UC dataset consists of *paired* inflamed and non-inflamed biopsies from the *same* 18 patients.
* **WHAT:** Aggregate $\psi_m$ and gene expression to the donor/sample level (or include a random intercept for `donor_id` in a linear mixed-effects model) when computing $p$-values and FDRs in Figures 4 and 5.
* **HOW:** For Figure 5C and 5I, compute the mean $\psi_m$ per patient/biopsy ($n = 18$ paired biopsies in UC; $n = 8$ vs. $10$ patients in TNBC) and apply a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test (for UC) and an unpaired two-sample Wilcoxon rank-sum test (for TNBC) at the sample level ($N = 18$).

#### 4. Correct Mathematical Equations and Dimension Definitions in the Experimental Section
* **WHY:** Equations (1), (4), (8)–(10), (13), and (14) in the Experimental Section contain algebraic typos, unclosed brackets, and matrix dimension mismatches that hinder reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Fix NAM dimensions in Equations (8)–(9) (line 12 of Experimental Section):** $R_{n,m}$ is defined as an $N \text{ (samples)} \times M \text{ (cells)}$ matrix normalized so each row $n$ sums to 1 ($Q_{n,m}$). Decomposition via PCA/NMF to obtain cell embeddings $X_i$ of length $M$ (number of cells, used in line 19) requires operating on the transpose $Q^\top$ ($M \times N$), where each row is a cell/neighborhood $m$ and each column is a sample $n$. Explicitly write $Q^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$.
  2. **Fix Equation (1) (line 99):** $\text{diff}_j = N \cdot p_j \cdot (1+f) - p_j - N \cdot p_j \cdot f$ algebraically simplifies to $p_j(N - 1)$, independent of the fold-change $f$! Rewrite this equation to match the actual code used to compute the disease vs. control cell counts for cluster $j$.
  3. **Fix Equation (4) (line 101):** The normalization constraint is missing `= 1`: change $\sum_f w_f^2 + w_{\text{noise}}^2$ to $\sum_f w_f^2 + w_{\text{noise}}^2 = 1$.
  4. **Fix Equation (10) (line 19):** $\beta = X_i + \gamma + X_i * \delta_i$ uses R formula notation (`+` and `*`) inside a mathematical equation defining a set of predictors; write $\beta = \{X_1, \dots, X_k, \gamma, X_1 \odot \delta_1, \dots, X_k \odot \delta_k\}$.
  5. **Fix Equation (13) (line 33):** Close the missing square bracket `[f(S \cup \{i\}) - f(S)]`, add cardinality bars $|S|!(|\beta| - |S| - 1)! / |\beta|!$, and fix the text immediately below ("In Equation (7)...") to read "In Equation (13)...".
  6. **Fix Equation (14) (line 35):** The right-hand side $\frac{1}{b}\sum_{i=1}^b S_n^{(i)}$ is missing the feature column index $j$; change $S_n^{(i)}$ to $S_{n,j}^{(i)}$.
  7. **Provide the `CellPhenoX` software repository URL:** The manuscript provides download links for the three public datasets (SCP259, E-MTAB-10026, and a personal Google Drive link for TNBC), but never states the GitHub/CRAN/PyPI URL for the `CellPhenoX` package itself. Add a dedicated Data and Code Availability statement with the permanent repository URL and archival DOI.
