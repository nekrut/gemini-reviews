### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Wen et al. investigate the shared genetic architecture between chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD; 58,550 cases and 937,358 controls of European ancestry) and seven autoimmune diseases (ADs: Crohn's disease [CD], ulcerative colitis [UC], inflammatory bowel disease [IBD], multiple sclerosis [MS], primary sclerosing cholangitis [PSC], rheumatoid arthritis [RA], and systemic lupus erythematosus [SLE]) using publicly available GWAS summary statistics. They estimate global genetic correlations via linkage disequilibrium score regression (LDSC) and high-definition likelihood (HDL), carry forward five trait pairs (COPD paired with MS, RA, PSC, CD, and SLE) into SNP-level pleiotropy discovery using PLACO and CPASSOC, annotate independent genomic risk loci in FUMA, perform Bayesian colocalization (`coloc`, $\text{PP.H4} \ge 0.70$), and conduct gene-level prioritization using MAGMA, Metascape, summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR/HEIDI), and FUSION transcriptome-wide association studies (TWAS) across GTEx v8 tissues. The authors report 57 shared pleiotropic loci (22 colocalized) and 162 pleiotropic genes (125 unique), highlighting the 17q12 (*ORMDL3*, *GSDMB*, *ZPBP2*, *PGAP3*) and 16p11.2 (*MAPK3*) regions as convergent immunogenetic hotspots.

While the study integrates a broad suite of complementary post-GWAS summary-statistic tools to catalog candidate overlapping loci between COPD and autoimmune disorders, several methodological and technical weaknesses undermine the reliability of its quantitative claims. First, the selection of the "five autoimmune diseases" from the seven tested in Table 1 relies on unadjusted nominal $p$-values ($p = 0.024$ for CD and $p = 0.042$ for SLE in HDL, which fail multiple-testing correction across 7 traits and were null in LDSC) and retains PSC despite its *negative* genetic correlation ($r_g = -0.205$) contradicting the clinical comorbidity hypothesis. Second, the authors apply PLACO and CPASSOC without documenting any decorrelation adjustment for sample overlap across the large European consortium and biobank control cohorts, nor do they report the LDSC intercepts promised in the Methods. Third, applying single-causal-variant `coloc` and independent gene-level SMR/TWAS interpretation across extreme long-range LD blocks (such as the 17q12 asthma/autoimmune inversion hotspot and the extended MHC) conflates horizontal LD co-regulation of neighboring genes (*ORMDL3*, *GSDMB*, *ZPBP2*, *PGAP3*) with independent causal mechanisms. Finally, the manuscript contains multiple numerical discrepancies between the main text and Table 1, conflates Bonferroni and FDR terminology, and lacks a dedicated repository containing the study's own executed code. All of these issues can be resolved through computational re-analysis, explicit overlap adjustment, and editorial correction.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **COPD shares significant positive global genetic correlations with rheumatoid arthritis ($r_g = 0.206, p = 8.73 \times 10^{-8}$) and multiple sclerosis ($r_g = 0.125, p = 1.22 \times 10^{-4}$): [Well Supported].**  
  Both LDSC and HDL yield concordant, statistically robust positive genetic correlations for COPD–RA and COPD–MS that easily survive Bonferroni correction across all seven tested autoimmune traits ($\alpha = 0.05 / 7 = 0.0071$).

* **COPD exhibits significant global genetic correlations with Crohn's disease ($r_g = 0.114, p = 0.024$) and SLE ($r_g = 0.113, p = 0.042$) and a shared comorbidity architecture with PSC ($r_g = -0.205, p = 8.86 \times 10^{-4}$): [Overstated].**  
  In LDSC, neither CD ($r_g = 0.056, p = 0.137$) nor SLE ($r_g = 0.099, p = 0.072$) is statistically significant. In HDL, CD ($p = 0.024$) and SLE ($p = 0.042$) are only nominally significant before multiple-testing correction and do not survive Bonferroni or FDR adjustment across the 7 tested ADs. Conversely, PSC could not be analyzed by HDL at all (`/` in Table 1) and exhibits a *negative* genetic correlation with COPD ($r_g = -0.205$) in LDSC—meaning genetic liability to PSC is associated with *lower* genetic liability to COPD, opposite to the positive comorbidity narrative framed in the Introduction and Discussion.

* **PLACO and COLOC identify 57 independent pleiotropic loci and 22 colocalized causal signals shared between COPD and the five ADs: [Fragile].**  
  Two methodological omissions affect these counts: (1) PLACO requires that $Z$-scores from the two traits be asymptotically uncorrelated under the null or explicitly decorrelated using the estimated sample-overlap correlation matrix. Because the COPD meta-analysis ($N \approx 996\text{k}$, incorporating UK Biobank and major European cohorts) shares control individuals with European autoimmune GWAS consortia, unadjusted sample overlap inflates Type I error in both PLACO and CPASSOC. (2) Standard `coloc` (v5.2.1) assumes at most one causal variant per trait per locus; in complex multi-signal regions with dense LD (such as 17q12 and 16p11.2, or if the extended MHC on 6p21 was not excluded), violating the single-causal-variant assumption distorts $\text{PP.H4}$.

* **Multiple adjacent genes at 17q12 (*ORMDL3*, *GSDMB*, *ZPBP2*, *PGAP3*) and 16p11.2 (*MAPK3*) independently drive shared COPD–AD susceptibility via SMR and TWAS: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While 17q12 and 16p11.2 are genuine, well-established pleiotropic risk regions for obstructive airway disease and autoimmunity, variants in the 17q12 block exist in tight linkage disequilibrium and act as simultaneous cis-eQTLs for *ORMDL3*, *GSDMB*, *ZPBP2*, and *PGAP3* in blood and lung. Standard marginal SMR and FUSION TWAS test one gene at a time and cannot distinguish which of these co-regulated neighboring transcripts is the true causal mediator versus a passenger of shared enhancer/LD tagging.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct for Sample Overlap in PLACO/CPASSOC and Report Bivariate LDSC Intercepts
* **WHY:** Although Methods (line 34) states that *"we examined the LDSC intercept to assess the presence of residual population stratification,"* neither the univariate intercepts nor the bivariate cross-trait LDSC intercepts (which quantify sample overlap between the COPD and AD GWAS datasets) are reported in Table 1, and no decorrelation step is described prior to running PLACO.
* **WHAT:** Report all univariate and bivariate LDSC intercepts in Table 1, and apply PLACO's decorrelation procedure (`cor.pearson` / `VarZ` adjustment) prior to computing pleiotropic $p$-values.
* **HOW:** Extract the bivariate LDSC intercept for each of the 7 COPD–AD pairs; compute the empirical correlation matrix $R$ of $Z$-scores across null SNPs ($p > 10^{-4}$); pass the decorrelated $Z$-scores into `PLACO()` and `CPASSOC`; and update the count of genome-wide significant SNPs ($p < 5 \times 10^{-8}$) and FUMA loci in the Results and Tables S2–S6.

#### 2. Apply Formal Multiple-Testing Correction to Global Genetic Correlations and Clarify MHC Exclusion
* **WHY:** Table 1 highlights unadjusted $p < 0.05$ values in bold, leading to the inclusion of CD ($p = 0.024$) and SLE ($p = 0.042$) despite their failure to pass LDSC or multiple-testing correction across 7 traits. In addition, the Methods do not state whether the extended major histocompatibility complex (MHC, chr6: 25–34 Mb) was excluded during LDSC, HDL, PLACO, and MAGMA analyses.
* **WHAT:** Add Benjamini-Hochberg FDR $q$-values to Table 1, temper claims regarding CD and SLE global correlations, and explicitly document how the MHC region was handled at every stage of the pipeline.
* **HOW:** Compute $q$-values across the 7 LDSC tests and 6 HDL tests in Table 1; label CD and SLE as having "nominal, non-FDR-significant" global correlations; and state in Methods whether chr6:25–34 Mb was masked in LDSC/HDL/MAGMA and how many of the 57 FUMA loci fall within the MHC.

#### 3. Perform Conditional / Fine-Mapped TWAS and Multi-Causal Colocalization at 17q12 and 16p11.2
* **WHY:** At 17q12, *ORMDL3*, *GSDMB*, *ZPBP2*, and *PGAP3* share overlapping cis-eQTLs in tight LD, so marginal SMR and FUSION TWAS flag all four genes together.
* **WHAT:** Run joint/conditional TWAS analysis across co-localized genes within the same locus to identify which transcript(s) explain the local GWAS signal conditionally.
* **HOW:** Use FUSION's built-in post-processing script (`FUSION.post_process.R --locus_win 500000`) on the 17q12 and 16p11.2 loci to compute joint and conditional TWAS $p$-values for *ORMDL3*, *GSDMB*, *ZPBP2*, and *PGAP3*, reporting which gene(s) remain conditionally significant after accounting for correlated predicted expression.

#### 4. Resolve Internal Numerical Contradictions, Statistical Terminology Errors, and Code Availability
* **WHY:** Multiple discrepancies exist between the prose and tables:
  1. Line 72 states COPD–RA LDSC is `rg = 0.205, P = 5.74×10−8`, whereas Table 1 lists `rg = 0.206 (0.038), P = 8.73×10−8`.
  2. Line 80 states COPD–MS HDL has `P = 1.23×10−5` and COPD–CD HDL has `P = 0.025`, whereas Table 1 lists `P = 1.23×10−4` and `P = 0.024`.
  3. Line 44 states *"A Bonferroni-adjusted significance threshold was applied to account for multiple testing (Pfdr<0.05)"*, contradicting itself by naming Bonferroni while writing $P_{\text{fdr}} < 0.05$.
  4. Line 98 states *"we identified 162 pleiotropic genes, of which 125 were unique (Table S9). Notably, 88.6% (140/158) of these genes were located within pleiotropic loci"*, introducing an unexplained denominator of `158`.
  5. Line 34 contains a missing verb (*"we further HDL analysis"*) and line 44 misses a preposition (*"using MAGMA the FUMA platform"*).
  6. Line 64 links only to an external third-party repository (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`) rather than code for the present COPD study.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Audit the R outputs to reconcile all numbers between lines 72–80, line 98, and Table 1; clarify whether Bonferroni ($0.05 / N_{\text{genes}}$) or Benjamini-Hochberg FDR ($q < 0.05$) was used in MAGMA; fix the grammatical typos on lines 34 and 44; and deposit the exact R scripts and harmonized summary-statistic configuration files used for this study in a dedicated public repository (e.g., Zenodo/GitHub).
