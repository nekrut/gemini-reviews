### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Turchin et al. test for polygenic adaptation on human adult height across Europe by combining per-SNP height association statistics from the GIANT consortium meta-analysis with Northern, Central, and Southern European allele frequency data from the MIGen and POPRES cohorts. Focusing first on 139 directly genotyped (or high-LD proxy) genome-wide significant height SNPs in MIGen ($N = 257$ Northern European-ancestry US individuals vs. $254$ Spanish individuals), they find that height-increasing alleles are elevated in Northern relative to Southern Europeans (85/139 SNPs, sign test $p = 0.011$; mean allele frequency difference $\Delta\text{AF} = 0.012$, $t$-test $p = 4.3 \times 10^{-4}$; permutation $p = 0.0056$ against 10,000 frequency-matched SNP sets). Extending the analysis to ~1,400 independent SNPs ($r^2 < 0.1$, $\ge 1\text{ Mb}$ apart) selected by comparing GIANT effect sizes against a within-sibship regression in the Framingham Heart Study (FHS; 4,819 individuals across 1,761 sibships), the authors apply a likelihood ratio test (LRT) comparing genetic drift alone against drift plus additive directional selection ($w = s\beta$), reporting LRT $p$-values of $10^{-15}$ to $10^{-35}$ and selection coefficients of $s \sim 10^{-3}\text{--}10^{-5}$ per allele per generation.

This study pioneers an influential framework for testing polygenic adaptation by aggregating directional allele frequency shifts across GWAS loci, and its finding at the 139 genome-wide significant loci is carefully benchmarked against frequency-matched genomic controls, HapMap ascertainment simulations, and Orr's phenotypic ascertainment bound. However, the extension to ~1,400 sub-genome-wide-significant SNPs—which drives the headline $p < 10^{-15}$ selection claim in the Abstract and Table 1—rests on a flawed statistical validation against residual population stratification. Specifically, the authors conclude that the top ~1,400 GIANT SNPs are unconfounded by stratification because a paired $t$-test comparing GIANT effect sizes to noisy within-sibship FHS estimates fails to reject the null ($p = 0.36$), and they then use the *GIANT* effect signs (not the family-based signs) to polarize those ~1,400 SNPs in Table 1. Furthermore, Figure 1a reveals that even randomly chosen frequency-matched control SNPs exhibit a positive Northern-minus-Southern shift ($\Delta\text{AF} = 0.0035$) when polarized by GIANT's predicted height-increasing allele, directly indicating residual North-South stratification in the genome-wide GIANT summary statistics. Re-running the Table 1 drift-versus-selection LRT strictly polarizing by the family-based FHS effect signs and correcting an algebraic formatting error in the selection equation are doable steps needed to put the broader polygenic claims on solid footing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **At the 139 genome-wide significant GIANT loci, height-increasing alleles have higher average frequency in Northern than in Southern Europeans: [Well Supported].**  
  At the 139 stringently associated loci, the signal of association with height is large enough that sign errors from residual stratification are minimal. The elevation in Northern vs. Southern Europeans ($\Delta\text{AF} = 0.012$, $t$-test $p = 4.3 \times 10^{-4}$) remains statistically significant ($p = 0.0056$) when compared against the empirical distribution of 10,000 sets of LD-pruned, allele-frequency-matched random SNPs (Figure 1a), telling against simple genetic drift at the top loci.

* **The top ~1,400 independent GIANT SNPs are unconfounded by population stratification because their effect sizes do not differ significantly from within-sibship Framingham Heart Study estimates ($p = 0.36$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Concluding that GIANT effect estimates at ~1,400 sub-significant SNPs are free of stratification because a paired $t$-test against a small within-sibship regression ($1,761$ FHS sibships) yields $p = 0.36$ (MIGen) and $p = 0.89$ (POPRES) conflates a lack of statistical power to detect a difference with proof of equivalence. A within-sibship regression in 4,819 individuals has vastly larger standard errors per SNP than the GIANT meta-analysis ($N > 130,000$), so the paired difference is dominated by FHS sampling variance. Crucially, the authors yourself note (line 75; Figure 1a) that the 10,000 randomly drawn frequency-matched control SNP sets show a systematic positive shift ($\Delta\text{AF} = 0.0035$) when polarized by GIANT height-increasing alleles, which is direct empirical evidence that sub-significant GIANT effect signs are correlated with the North-South European allele frequency gradient.

* **Allele frequency differences across ~1,400 height-associated SNPs reject neutral genetic drift at $p < 10^{-15}$ (up to $p = 3 \times 10^{-35}$ in Table 1) in favor of widespread weak selection ($s \sim 10^{-3}\text{--}10^{-5}$): [Overstated].**  
  In Table 1, every $t$-test and LRT $p$-value ($9.6 \times 10^{-16}$ in MIGen; $3.0 \times 10^{-35}$ in POPRES UK vs. Italy) is computed on the ~1,400 SNPs polarized by their **GIANT** association signs and $\beta$ values rather than the stratification-immune FHS within-sibship signs. If even a small fraction of those ~1,400 sub-significant SNPs achieved low $p$-values in GIANT partly because they had higher allele frequencies in taller Northern European cohorts, polarizing by the GIANT sign in MIGen and POPRES circularly selects SNPs with $\text{AF}_{\text{North}} > \text{AF}_{\text{South}}$ and inflates the LRT statistic by many orders of magnitude. In addition, the seven rows of Table 1 are not independent comparisons: five of the POPRES comparisons reuse the same UK ($N = 208$), Swiss-French ($N = 208$), Italian ($N = 208$), and Portuguese ($N = 125$) samples pairwise.

* **Within-sibship regression in FHS confirms a true polygenic association signal for Northern-predominant alleles among top GIANT-ranked SNPs: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Figure 2b shows that the running average of within-sibship regression coefficients for Northern-predominant alleles in FHS exceeds 1,000 within-sibship phenotype permutations ($p < 10^{-3}$) across the top-ranked SNPs. While this confirms that Northern-predominant alleles among top GIANT hits genuinely predict taller stature within families, its statistical significance ($p \sim 10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$ by permutation) is over ten orders of magnitude more modest than the $p < 10^{-15}$ to $10^{-35}$ headline claim derived from GIANT-polarized SNPs in Table 1.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Polarize the Table 1 Drift-vs.-Selection LRT Using Strictly Stratification-Immune Within-Sibship Effect Estimates
* **WHY:** Polarizing the ~1,400 sub-significant SNPs by GIANT effect signs leaks any residual North-South European stratification from GIANT directly into the Northern-minus-Southern allele frequency difference test, producing circular inflation of the Table 1 $p$-values ($10^{-15}$ to $10^{-35}$).
* **WHAT:** Re-compute Table 1 (both the mean allele frequency difference $t$-tests and the drift-vs.-selection LRTs) under two clean, non-circular designs: (a) restricted strictly to the 139 (MIGen) and 109 (POPRES) genome-wide significant loci, and (b) across the ~1,400 SNPs polarized and weighted exclusively by the FHS within-sibship regression coefficients $\hat{\beta}_{\text{sib}}$.
* **HOW:** Using the existing FHS within-sibship regression output and MIGen/POPRES allele frequencies, replace $\beta_{\text{GIANT}}$ with $\hat{\beta}_{\text{sib}}$ in the selection model $w = s\hat{\beta}_{\text{sib}}$, re-run the LRT optimization in R, and report these stratification-free LRT $p$-values alongside the 139-SNP LRT $p$-values in Table 1 and the Abstract.

#### 2. Subtract the Empirical Background GIANT Stratification Baseline from Sub-Significant SNP Comparisons
* **WHY:** In Figure 1a, the 10,000 randomly drawn frequency-matched SNP sets exhibit a mean Northern-minus-Southern frequency difference of $+0.0035$ (purple vertical line) rather than the theoretical null of $0$ (blue vertical line) when polarized by GIANT effect direction. In Table 1, however, the one-sample $t$-tests and drift LRTs for the ~1,400 SNPs test against a theoretical null mean of $0.0000$ rather than the empirical genome-wide GIANT background shift of $+0.0035$.
* **WHAT:** Benchmark the ~1,400-SNP mean frequency difference and LRT against the empirical background distribution of frequency-matched control SNPs polarized by GIANT, rather than a zero-mean theoretical normal distribution.
* **HOW:** Compare the observed $\Delta\text{AF} = 0.0079$ (MIGen US vs. Spain in Table 1) against the null distribution of $\Delta\text{AF}$ across 10,000 sets of 1,437 frequency-matched SNPs (centered around $+0.0035$), and modify the null drift model in the LRT to include an empirical stratification intercept $\mu_0$ estimated from the genome-wide background (`ΔAF ~ N(μ_0, p(1-p)(2F_ST + 1/N_1 + 1/N_2))`).

#### 3. Account for Covariance Across Overlapping Pairwise Population Comparisons in POPRES
* **WHY:** Table 1 presents five pairwise POPRES comparisons (UK vs. Italy, UK vs. Portugal, UK vs. Switzerland, Switzerland vs. Italy, Switzerland vs. Portugal) formed from just four population samples, creating strong mathematical dependence across rows (e.g., $\Delta\text{AF}_{\text{UK-Italy}} \equiv \Delta\text{AF}_{\text{UK-Switz}} + \Delta\text{AF}_{\text{Switz-Italy}}$).
* **WHAT:** Replace or supplement the overlapping pairwise $t$-tests and LRTs with a single joint multi-population test across the latitudinal gradient.
* **HOW:** Model the allele frequencies across all POPRES populations jointly using a generalized linear or normal drift-covariance model (with covariance matrix $\Sigma = p(1-p)\mathbf{F} + \text{diag}(p(1-p)/N_i)$) testing a single latitudinal selection slope against drift.

#### 4. Fix Mathematical Formatting and Typographical Errors in the Selection Equation and Text
* **WHY:** The displayed equation for expected allele frequency change under selection (lines 91 and 146) is corrupted in typesetting, and several grammatical/spelling errors appear in the main text.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. In both the main text (line 91) and Online Methods (line 146), the equation is printed as `ΔAF_Sel ≈ T × ( w p^2 + w p + p 1 + 2 w p - p )`, missing the fraction bar between numerator and denominator. Rewrite it clearly as:
     $$\Delta\text{AF}_{\text{Sel}} \approx T \left( \frac{w p^2 + w p + p}{1 + 2 w p} - p \right) = \frac{T w p (1 - p)}{1 + 2 w p}$$
  2. Correct the truncated caption at the end of Table 1 (line 67 ends mid-sentence: *"likely to span the actual (unknown) value of"* $\rightarrow$ *"value of $T$."*).
  3. Fix `"we must also considered confounding"` (line 77 $\rightarrow$ `"we must also consider confounding"`) and `"Kolmagoroff-Smirnov"` (line 99 $\rightarrow$ `"Kolmogorov-Smirnov"`).
  4. Provide a public repository link (e.g., GitHub or institutional archive) containing the R scripts and the list of 139 and ~1,400 SNPs with their MIGen, POPRES, GIANT, and FHS summary values.
