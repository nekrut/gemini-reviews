### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Tang et al. examine the evolutionary divergence between SARS-CoV-2 (`NC_045512`) and related bat and pangolin sarbecoviruses (RaTG13, Guangdong and Guangxi pangolin CoVs, ZC45, ZXC21, BM48-31, and human SARS-CoV) across nine conserved open reading frames (ORFs), and perform population genetic analyses on 103 early public SARS-CoV-2 genomes. Using PAML (`YN00` and `CODEML`), they report a genome-wide synonymous divergence of $dS = 0.17$ between SARS-CoV-2 and RaTG13 (and $dS = 0.411$ across the Spike RBD motif between SARS-CoV-2 and Guangdong Pangolin-CoV, dating divergence to 19.2–53.7 years ago) and infer positive selection across sarbecoviruses via a concatenated CODEML M7 vs. M8 test ($p < 10^{-10}$). Within the 103 human SARS-CoV-2 genomes, they identify strong linkage disequilibrium ($r^2 = 0.954$, $\text{LOD} = 50.13$) between positions 8,782 (`orf1ab` T8517C) and 28,144 (`ORF8` C251T / S84L), defining two major clades designated "L" (~70%, carrying CT) and "S" (~30%, carrying TC, which matches animal outgroups), report a higher derived mutational load in the L lineage ($p < 0.0001$), and screen 12 SRA sequencing datasets for intrahost "heteroplasmy."

The inter-species synonymous divergence analysis ($dS = 0.17$ vs. RaTG13) is a useful quantitative demonstration that 96% overall nucleotide identity understates neutral evolutionary distance due to strong purifying selection on nonsynonymous sites ($\omega = 0.044$). However, the manuscript's downstream evolutionary and population-genetic analyses contain major methodological flaws: (1) running CODEML site models (M7/M8) on a single concatenated phylogeny across sarbecoviruses ignores well-known recombination breakpoints in Spike, generating artifactual positive selection signals where gene trees conflict with the species tree; (2) polarizing human SARS-CoV-2 variants against a highly divergent outgroup ($dS = 0.17\text{--}0.47$) and comparing derived mutation counts between L and S lineages via an i.i.d. Wilcoxon rank-sum test ($p < 0.0001$) is both phylogenetically pseudoreplicated and circularly biased because the two clade-defining SNPs themselves add +2 "derived" mutations to every L genome; (3) interpreting the 70% vs. 30% sample proportion in an unrepresentative convenience sample of 103 genomes (heavily enriched for Wuhan market L-clade cases) as evidence of differential selective pressure is confounded by founder and ascertainment effects; and (4) the intrahost "heteroplasmy" calls in Table 2 rely on raw `bcftools mpileup` counts as low as 2 alternate reads without sequencing-error or contamination controls. These issues require re-analyzing non-recombinant segments separately, applying phylogenetic comparative tests rather than i.i.d. rank-sum tests, and imposing rigorous variant-calling filters.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Neutral synonymous divergence between SARS-CoV-2 and RaTG13 ($dS = 0.17$) is roughly fourfold higher than overall nucleotide divergence (~4%) due to strong purifying selection on nonsynonymous sites ($\omega = 0.044$): [Well Supported].**  
  Separating nonsynonymous ($dN = 0.008$) and synonymous ($dS = 0.17$) substitutions across 28,734 aligned coding nucleotides (Table 1) validly demonstrates that 87.6–95.6% of nonsynonymous mutations are purged by purifying selection, making raw nucleotide identity a misleading metric of divergence time. (For more distantly related viruses in Table 1 where $dS > 1.0$, however, synonymous sites approach saturation and `YN00` estimates carry substantial variance.)

* **Shared ACE2-contact residues in the Spike RBD between SARS-CoV-2 and Guangdong Pangolin-CoV arose by convergent mutation and positive selection (CODEML M8 vs. M7, $p < 10^{-10}$) rather than recombination: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To test for positive selection, the authors concatenated 9 ORFs across all sarbecoviruses onto a single neighbor-joining tree (Figure 1A) and ran CODEML M7 vs. M8. Coronaviruses undergo frequent homologous recombination, and specifically in the RBD region, Guangdong Pangolin-CoV is the sister lineage to SARS-CoV-2, whereas in the rest of the genome RaTG13 is the sister lineage. Fitting CODEML site models on a single concatenated tree when the RBD has a discordant recombinant topology violates the fundamental model assumption of a single shared genealogy, forcing CODEML to explain topology-mismatched amino acid sharing via inflated $\omega > 1$ and producing false-positive BEB sites in the RBD (such as 493Q). Furthermore, a regional synonymous divergence of $dS = 0.411$ between SARS-CoV-2 and GD Pangolin-CoV across 24 codons (484–507) only rules out recombination within the last few decades with *that exact sampled pangolin strain*; it does not rule out historical recombination with an unsampled lineage prior to 20–50 years ago.

* **Early SARS-CoV-2 genomes form two well-defined lineages ("L" at ~70% and "S" at ~30%) separated by tightly linked SNPs at positions 8,782 and 28,144: [Well Supported].**  
  The linkage disequilibrium analysis ($r^2 = 0.954$, $\text{LOD} = 50.13$ across 103 genomes; Figure 3), haplotype network (Figure 4A), and maximum-likelihood phylogeny (Figure 5) clearly establish that early pandemic sequences partition into two basal clades defined by C8782T and T28144C.

* **The L lineage has accumulated a significantly higher mutational load than the S lineage ($p < 0.0001$) and the two lineages likely experienced different selective pressures: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This conclusion suffers from three compounding errors:
  1. *Circular outgroup polarization:* Because the authors polarize mutations against RaTG13 and GD Pangolin-CoV (where the S haplotype TC matches the outgroup), the two lineage-defining sites (8,782 and 28,144) are counted as "derived" in all 72 L genomes and "ancestral" in all 29 S genomes. In early SARS-CoV-2 genomes that differ from the root by only 0–5 mutations total, automatically adding +2 derived mutations to every L genome guarantees a highly significant difference even if the evolutionary rate is identical.
  2. *Phylogenetic pseudoreplication:* Applying a two-sample Wilcoxon rank-sum test across 103 tip genomes treats phylogenetically related viral isolates sharing internal branches as independent observations, inflating Type I error.
  3. *Ascertainment bias:* The 70% L vs. 30% S frequency in the 103 GISAID genomes reflects heavy early sampling of the initial Wuhan outbreak (where the L clade was the local founder) rather than a global selective sweep or transmission advantage.

* **Five patients in SRA deep-sequencing datasets and patient `USA_2020/01/21.a` harbor intrahost SARS-CoV-2 heteroplasmy / L+S co-infection: [Fragile].**  
  In Table 2, the authors call intrahost variants using default `bcftools mpileup` without filtering for minimum sequencing depth, minor allele frequency threshold, strand bias, or primer dimers. As a result, 10 of the 18 rows in Table 2 rest on **2 to 5 alternate reads** at low total depth (e.g., `SRR11092063` position 29,441 with 6 reference and 2 alternate reads; `SRR11092059` position 7,042 with 5 reference and 3 alternate reads), which cannot be distinguished from RT-PCR or Illumina sequencing error. Likewise, inferring L+S co-infection in `EPI_ISL_404253` solely from IUPAC `Y` ambiguity characters in a consensus FASTA sequence without examining raw read depth or laboratory contamination is unsubstantiated.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct the Mutational Load Comparison for Outgroup Polarization Circularity and Phylogenetic Non-Independence
* **WHY:** Counting the two lineage-defining SNPs (8,782 and 28,144) as derived mutations in L and testing all 103 non-independent tips with a Wilcoxon rank-sum test creates a circular statistical artifact.
* **WHAT:** Recompute the mutational load comparison excluding positions 8,782 and 28,144 (measuring tip-to-clade-MRCA branch lengths within L and within S separately) and account for phylogenetic structure and sampling dates.
* **HOW:** For each of the 72 L genomes and 29 S genomes, count the number of substitutions relative to the respective L-clade consensus (`CT`) and S-clade consensus (`TC`), regress tip divergence against collection date (root-to-tip regression) for each clade to account for differences in sampling dates, and compare the evolutionary rates and mean tip-to-clade-root distances using a phylogenetic permutation or bootstrap test.

#### 2. Partition Recombinant Regions Before Running CODEML Selection Models
* **WHY:** Applying CODEML M7/M8 to a concatenated alignment whose Spike RBD has a discordant phylogenetic history relative to `orf1ab` violates the model's single-tree assumption and produces false-positive selection signals.
* **WHAT:** Detect recombination breakpoints across the sarbecovirus alignment and run gene-tree-specific CODEML analyses on non-recombinant partitions.
* **HOW:** Run GARD or RDP4 on the sarbecovirus alignment (or partition the Spike RBD separately from the rest of the genome), infer a separate maximum-likelihood tree for the RBD (where GD Pangolin-CoV groups with SARS-CoV-2) and for the non-recombinant backbone, and re-run CODEML M7 vs. M8 and branch-site tests on each partition using its own concordant topology.

#### 3. Apply Stringent Variant-Calling Filters to the SRA Intrahost Analysis (Table 2)
* **WHY:** Calling intrahost single-nucleotide variants (iSNVs) from 2–5 reads at total depths of 8–20x (Table 2) conflates amplification/sequencing artifacts with biological co-infection or quasispecies diversity.
* **WHAT:** Re-filter the 12 SRA alignments using standard viral iSNV quality thresholds.
* **HOW:** Require total read depth $\ge 100\text{x}$, alternate allele depth $\ge 10$ reads, minor allele frequency $\ge 5\%$, base Phred quality $\ge 30$, and forward/reverse strand balance before calling an intrahost variant in Table 2, and remove all low-coverage calls that fail these criteria.

#### 4. Fix Author List Metadata, Citations, and Textual Inconsistencies
* **WHY:** Several technical and editorial inconsistencies appear in the manuscript:
  1. Line 130 states that the 12 SRA libraries are listed in `Table S2`, whereas line 92 states `Table S3` (and `Table S2` is the codon usage bias table).
  2. In the divergence time formula on line 41 ($t = dS / (u \times 2 \times 2.29)$), plugging in $dS = 0.411$ and $u = 1.67\text{--}4.67 \times 10^{-3}$ yields $0.411 / (2 \times 2.29 \times 4.67 \times 10^{-3}) = 19.2\text{ years}$ and $0.411 / (2 \times 2.29 \times 1.67 \times 10^{-3}) = 53.7\text{ years}$, which implicitly assumes the elevated Spike $dS$ ratio ($2.29$) applies equally to both lineages on top of a whole-genome SARS-CoV clock without propagating the huge standard deviation ($\pm 1.45$) of that ratio.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Correct the `Table S2` vs. `Table S3` cross-reference on line 130, propagate the uncertainty of the Spike rate multiplier ($2.29 \pm 1.45$) into the RBD divergence time interval, and deposit the exact alignment files, PAML control files, and RAxML trees in a public repository.
