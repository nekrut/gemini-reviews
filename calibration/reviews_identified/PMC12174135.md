### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Hong et al. construct an 8-gene prognostic risk score (`MLRS`: `BIRC5`, `TPX2`, `AGER`, `CLIC5`, `EMCN`, `MAMDC2`, `SEMA3G`, `TEK`) for clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) using TCGA-KIRC ($n = 529$) as a discovery cohort and E-MTAB-1980 ($n = 101$), ICGC ($n = 91$), and GSE22541 as validation cohorts. The authors intersect WGCNA co-expression modules with TCGA tumor-vs-normal `limma` differentially expressed genes ($|\log_2 \text{FC}| > 1.0$, unadjusted $P < 0.05$) and univariate Cox regressions ($P < 0.05$) to obtain 25 candidate genes, evaluate 101 combinations of 10 survival machine-learning algorithms, and select a `CoxBoost + Ridge` model based on the highest average C-index across the validation datasets. They then correlate `MLRS` with clinical stage, GSEA/GSVA pathways, CIBERSORT/ssGSEA immune deconvolution, `TIDE` and four non-renal immunotherapy cohorts (IMvigor210 urothelial cancer, GSE78220 and GSE91061 melanoma, GSE135222 NSCLC), `oncoPredict` drug sensitivity, scRNA-seq (`GSE210042`, $n = 7$ ccRCC tumors, $30,663$ cells), and in vitro CCK-8 proliferation assays in 786-O, Caki-1, and HUVEC cells following `EMCN` overexpression or siRNA knockdown.

Despite combining multi-cohort bulk transcriptomics, single-cell analysis, and wet-lab CCK-8 experiments, this manuscript is undermined by direct self-contradictions on its central findings, validation-set selection leakage, and basic arithmetic failures: (1) **the Abstract directly contradicts the Results on the two primary claims of the paper**—the Abstract states `"the high-MLRS group had a favorable outcome in four independent datasets"` and `"EMCN knockdown inhibited cell proliferation"`, whereas Results Section 3.2 (line 107) and Section 3.3 (line 120) report that the **high-MLRS group had poor clinical outcomes** (and low-MLRS had favorable outcomes), and Results Section 3.8 (Part 2, line 3) reports that **`EMCN` overexpression inhibited proliferation whereas `EMCN` knockdown in HUVECs *enhanced* ccRCC proliferation**; (2) **Section 3.5 (line 136) and Discussion (Part 2, line 14) contradict each other by 180 degrees on the tumor immune microenvironment**, with line 136 stating that $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells, $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells, B cells, and M1 macrophages are significantly greater in **high-MLRS** (`"indicating immune activation status"`) and four sentences later declaring high-MLRS to be `"cold tumors"`, while Discussion line 14 claims those exact same four cell types (`CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells (TIMER method), B cells (CIBERSORT method), and M1 macrophages (Quantieq method)`) characterize the **low-MLRS** group; (3) the final `CoxBoost + Ridge` model was chosen from 101 candidates based on the **highest average C-index across the validation datasets** (Section 2.3, line 37; Discussion Part 2, line 10), which constitutes direct test-set leakage that invalidates the external validation claims and the comparison against 101 published signatures; and (4) basic gene counts in Section 3.1 fail to add up (`"a total of 122 genes from those two modules (turquoise: 22 and blue: 12)"`, where $22 + 12 = 34 \ne 122$), and no custom analysis code is made publicly available.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The high-MLRS group has a favorable clinical outcome across four independent datasets (Abstract) / The high-MLRS group has a poor clinical outcome (Results Section 3.2):** **[Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The manuscript cannot agree with itself on the sign of its own prognostic signature. Abstract line 7 states: `"the high-MLRS group had a favorable outcome in four independent datasets. Furthermore, the low-MLRS group displayed a greater possibility of responding to immunotherapy"`. Yet Results Section 3.2 (line 107) states: `"patients in the TCGA (P < 0.0001), E-MTAB-1980 (P = 0.00013), ICGC (P = 0.04), and GSE22541 (P < 0.0001) datasets suffered poor clinical outcomes in the high-MLRS group than in the low-MLRS group (Figure 2B)"`. Furthermore, choosing the winning model out of 101 algorithm combinations by maximizing the average C-index across the external validation cohorts (Section 2.3, line 37) leaks the validation data into model selection.

* **EMCN knockdown inhibits ccRCC cell proliferation (Abstract) / EMCN overexpression inhibits and EMCN knockdown promotes ccRCC cell proliferation (Results Section 3.8):** **[Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Abstract line 7 claims `"EMCN was downregulated in ccRCC, and further functional experiments demonstrated that EMCN knockdown inhibited cell proliferation."` Biologically, if a tumor suppressor (`EMCN`) is downregulated in cancer, knocking it down further should *promote*—not inhibit—proliferation. Indeed, Results Section 3.8 (Part 2, line 3) reports that **overexpression** of `EMCN` in 786-O and Caki-1 cells inhibited proliferation (Fig. 8N), whereas **knockdown** of `EMCN` (`si-EMCN#1/#2`) in co-cultured HUVECs **significantly enhanced** ccRCC proliferation (Fig. 8P). The Abstract states the exact opposite of the experimental results.

* **Low-MLRS tumors represent immune-infiltrated "hot" tumors whereas high-MLRS tumors represent immune-depleted "cold" tumors:** **[Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Results Section 3.5 (line 136), the authors write: `"the infiltration levels of immune cells, such as CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells (TIMER method), B cells (CIBERSORT method), and M1 macrophages (Quantieq method), were significantly greater in patients with high MLRS than in those with low MLRS (Figure 5A), indicating immune activation status."` Three sentences later in the same paragraph, they conclude: `"These findings strongly implied that ccRCC patients with low MLRS values were significantly more inclined to be reclassified as having 'hot tumors', whereas those with high MLRS values were more likely to have 'cold tumors'."` Then, in the Discussion (Part 2, line 14), they invert the finding entirely, writing: `"In contrast, ccRCC patients in the low-risk group had a highly infiltrated immune microenvironment characterized by the presence of CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells (TIMER method), B cells (CIBERSORT method), and M1 macrophages (Quantieq method)"`. Moreover, biologically, human ccRCC is well established to be an atypical solid tumor in which higher $\text{CD8}^+$ T-cell infiltration paired with Treg/TGF-$\beta$ exhaustion correlates with *worse* survival; forcing a generic "hot = good prognosis / cold = bad prognosis" template onto ccRCC leads the authors to contradict their own Fig. 5A data.

* **MLRS predicts immunotherapy benefit in ccRCC patients:** **[Overstated].**  
  None of the four immunotherapy cohorts evaluated in Section 3.6 are ccRCC cohorts: IMvigor210 is urothelial (bladder) carcinoma (although line 147 erroneously refers to them as `"patients with ccRCC after 3 months of treatment"`), GSE78220 and GSE91061 are melanoma cohorts, and GSE135222 is non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Extrapolating an endothelial-enriched kidney signature (`EMCN`, `TEK`, `SEMA3G`, `CLIC5`) to melanoma and lung cancer cohorts without testing a single ccRCC anti-PD-1/PD-L1 cohort (such as CheckMate-009/010/025 or JAVELIN Renal 101) does not establish predictive utility for ccRCC immunotherapy.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Validation-Set Leakage in the 101-Model Selection Pipeline
* **WHY:** In Section 2.3 (line 37) and Discussion (Part 2, line 10), the authors state that the `CoxBoost + Ridge` model was selected from 101 models because it achieved the highest average C-index across the validation datasets. Using the validation cohorts to pick the best model among 101 candidates violates the independence of the validation sets and biases the comparison against the 101 published signatures (which were not tuned on those validation sets).
* **WHAT:** Select the winning machine-learning algorithm and its hyperparameters exclusively using the 10-fold cross-validation C-index *within the TCGA discovery cohort*, lock the model weights, and evaluate the locked model once on E-MTAB-1980, ICGC, and GSE22541.
* **HOW:** Rank the 101 algorithm combinations by mean 10-fold CV C-index inside TCGA ($n = 529$), select the top model without inspecting E-MTAB-1980, ICGC, or GSE22541, and report the resulting unbiased external C-indices in Fig. 2A.

#### 2. Reconcile the Direct Self-Contradictions in the Abstract, Results, and Discussion
* **WHY:** The Abstract states that high-MLRS has a favorable outcome and `EMCN` knockdown inhibits proliferation, directly contradicting Results Sections 3.2 and 3.8; similarly, Results Section 3.5 and Discussion Section 4 state opposite directions for $\text{CD4}^+/\text{CD8}^+$ T-cell, B-cell, and M1 macrophage infiltration between low- and high-MLRS groups.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Abstract (line 7):** Change `"the high-MLRS group had a favorable outcome in four independent datasets"` to `"the low-MLRS group had a favorable outcome (whereas the high-MLRS group had poor survival) in four datasets"`, and change `"EMCN knockdown inhibited cell proliferation"` to `"EMCN overexpression in ccRCC cells inhibited proliferation, whereas EMCN knockdown in co-cultured endothelial cells promoted ccRCC proliferation."`
  * **Section 3.5 (line 136) vs. Discussion (Part 2, line 14):** Audit Figure 5A to verify whether `TIMER CD4+/CD8+`, `CIBERSORT B cells`, and `quanTIseq M1 macrophages` are higher in high-MLRS or low-MLRS, and rewrite both paragraphs so they report the exact same direction and accurately reflect ccRCC's exhausted-immune-infiltrated high-risk phenotype rather than mislabeling an immune-infiltrated group as "cold".

#### 3. Validate Immunotherapy Prediction on Actual ccRCC Checkpoint Blockade Cohorts
* **WHY:** Section 3.6 tests `MLRS` in IMvigor210 (urothelial cancer, misdescribed in line 147 as `"patients with ccRCC"`), GSE78220 (melanoma), GSE91061 (melanoma), and GSE135222 (NSCLC). Because 5 of the 8 `MLRS` genes (`EMCN`, `TEK`, `CLIC5`, `SEMA3G`, `AGER`) are renal glomerular/endothelial vascular markers (which are protective in ccRCC and drive vascular normalization), testing them in melanoma or bladder cancer does not validate ccRCC immunotherapy response.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate `MLRS` in publicly available transcriptomic cohorts of actual ccRCC patients treated with nivolumab (anti-PD-1) or atezolizumab/avelumab (e.g., Braun et al. 2020 CheckMate-009/010/025 cohort).
* **HOW:** Compute the locked 8-gene `CoxBoost + Ridge` score on the CheckMate-025 RNA-seq cohort, comparing OS, PFS, and objective response rate (CR/PR vs. SD/PD) in the nivolumab arm versus the everolimus control arm (testing the `MLRS` $\times$ treatment interaction term).

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Arithmetic errors, misreported assay durations, and multiple typographical errors undermine technical credibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Section 3.1 (line 98):** Fix the arithmetic contradiction `"a total of 122 genes from those two modules (turquoise: 22 and blue: 12)"` ($22 + 12 = 34$, not $122$). Report the true module counts that sum to 122.
  * **Section 2.9 (line 73) vs. Figure 8P caption (Part 2, line 1):** Methods line 73 states `"After 48 h of co-culture, ccRCC cell proliferation was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8"`, whereas the Figure 8P caption states `"after 4-day co-culture with HUVECs"`. Reconcile whether the Transwell co-culture lasted 48 hours or 4 days.
  * **Section 2.3 (line 35) & Section 3.1 (line 102):** Apply Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate adjustment ($q < 0.05$) rather than unadjusted $P < 0.05$ when screening 1,895 DEGs and univariate Cox regressions.
  * **Typos throughout text:** Abstract line 7 `"used to contract MLRS"` -> `"construct"`; Line 61 `"CTPR (https://portals.broadinstitude.org/ctrp)"` -> `"CTRP (https://portals.broadinstitute.org/ctrp)"`; Line 100 `"bule"` -> `"blue"`; Line 136/14 `"Quantieq"` -> `"quanTIseq"`; Line 138 `"CIBESORT"` -> `"CIBERSORT"`; Line 147 `"IMigor210"` -> `"IMvigor210"`; Line 160/158 `"FTG-b"` -> `"TGF-β"`; Line 167 `"subclusters (EC0-5)"` -> `"five subclusters (EC0-4)"`; Part 2 line 12 `"The role of AGER role"`, `"suggseted"`, and sentence fragment `"Despite limited data on ccRCC."`
