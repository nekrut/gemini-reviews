### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study evaluates the survivorship and genotype frequency distribution of the *CCR5*-$\Delta 32$ deletion (`rs333`) across 409,693 British-ancestry participants in the UK Biobank (395,704 with complete genotype and age data; 13,831 recorded deaths through February 16, 2016). Using an Affymetrix Axiom array probe (`rs62625034`) whose target sequence spans the 32-bp deletion of `rs333`, the authors estimate yearly mortality rates from age 41 to 78 via left-truncated, right-censored survival analysis (Kaplan–Meier/product-limit survival curves, log-rank test, and Cox proportional hazards model) and test for departures from Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) at enrollment. They report that $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ homozygotes exhibit a ~21% increase in all-cause mortality rate prior to age 76 (log-rank $Z = 2.37$, one-tailed $P = 0.0089$; Cox HR $= 1.214$, 95% CI $[1.034, 1.426]$) and a significant deficiency of $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ homozygotes at recruitment compared to 5,932 MAF-matched SNPs (empirical one-tailed $P = 0.0034$). Analytical scripts and a project notebook are publicly available on GitHub (`https://github.com/AprilWei001/CCR5-delta32`).

Direct estimation of human viability selection in biobank cohorts is an important methodological direction, and the authors provide transparent code and notebook documentation. However, the study's two core lines of evidence are compromised by a critical genotyping probe vulnerability and statistical fragility: (1) the proxy marker `rs62625034` is a SNP probe physically located *inside* the 32-bp `rs333` deletion, resulting in a 3.4% missing-genotype rate (13,979 British-ancestry individuals missing genotypes: $409,693 - 395,714$) and making the HWE deficiency of $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ homozygotes highly susceptible to null-allele/deletion hybridization failure or heterozygous miscalling; (2) although the authors identify a clean flanking tag SNP (`rs113010081`, $r^2 = 0.93\text{--}0.94$ with `rs333`) and UK Biobank imputed `rs333` calls exist, they omit the HWE inbreeding coefficient $F$ and exact survival statistics for `rs113010081` ("not shown"); and (3) the survival effect relies on one-tailed $P$-values ($P_{\text{one-tailed}} = 0.0089$, i.e., $P_{\text{two-tailed}} = 0.0178$) evaluated at a specific age truncation (age 76). Re-running the exact HWE and Cox survival analyses on the flanking SNP `rs113010081`, imputed `rs333`, and whole-exome/whole-genome sequencing calls is essential to determine whether the HWE deficit and mortality signal are biological or probe-induced.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Homozygosity for *CCR5*-$\Delta 32$ increases all-cause mortality by 21% in the UK Biobank cohort: [Fragile].**  
  The survival difference between $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ and the other two genotypes at age 76 reaches $Z = 2.37$ (one-tailed $P = 0.0089$, two-tailed $P = 0.0178$; Cox 95% CI $[3.4\%, 42.6\%]$). This result is fragile for three reasons: (a) it is only modestly significant under a one-tailed test and sensitive to the age-76 truncation cutoff (in Figure 1a, the $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ survival curve flattens from age 76 to 78 while $+/+$ and $\Delta 32/+$ continue to decline); (b) 13,979 British-ancestry participants have missing `rs62625034` genotypes, and differential probe call rates by sample quality or recruitment batch could correlate with follow-up time or mortality; and (c) the survival curve and $P$-value for the clean flanking marker `rs113010081` are asserted to be "similar" on Line 59 but are not shown.

* **A significant deficiency of $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ individuals at recruitment ($F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} < 0$) independently confirms pre-enrollment mortality selection against homozygotes: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The Affymetrix probe for `rs62625034` (`3:46414975`) lies entirely within the 32-bp deletion of `rs333` (`3:46414947–46414978`). On $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ chromosomes, the target sequence is absent, so genotype calling depends on background/off-target hybridization intensity. Probes overlapping deletions or copy-number variants routinely suffer from elevated no-call rates in homozygotes (consistent with the 13,979 missing genotypes in the British subset) or misclustering of homozygotes as heterozygotes, which directly manufactures a negative inbreeding coefficient ($F < 0$, deficiency of called homozygotes). Moreover, in the Figure 1b caption (Line 36), the authors report $F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} = -0.19$ (a 19% deficiency of homozygotes), which is biologically incompatible with a 21% elevation in cumulative pre-enrollment mortality (since baseline cumulative mortality by mean recruitment age 56.5 in the UK is $<6\%$, predicting at most a $\sim 1\text{--}3\%$ deficit of homozygotes, as shown on the y-axis of Extended Data Figure 1b).

* **Deviations from HWE across age groups correlate with survival-predicted HWE deviations ($\rho = 0.67, P = 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$): [Fragile].**  
  In Extended Data Figure 1a, the 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for the observed HWE ratio at every single recruitment age span across $1.0$ (no deviation) and overlap substantially with one another. Furthermore, the sample sizes reported in the caption for each age dot range from $15,191$ to $100,117$ with a mean of $65,479$ across ~25 age points—summing to $>1.6\text{ million}$, which exceeds the total sample size of $395,704$ by fourfold! This indicates that the dots in Extended Data Figure 1a are computed over overlapping sliding age windows rather than independent single-year age cohorts, violating the independence assumption of Spearman's rank correlation and inflating $P = 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$.

* **The *CCR5*-$\Delta 32$ allele is highly pleiotropic across UK Biobank phenotypes compared to MAF-matched SNPs (empirical $P = 0.0128$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Comparing the number of transcriptome/phenome-wide associations against 5,932 MAF-matched variants (Line 42) is a reasonable empirical control, though LD structure in the chemokine receptor gene cluster (*CCR5*–*CCR2*–*CCR3* on 3p21.31) and gene density are not matched in the background SNP set.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Validate HWE and Survival Analyses Using Flanking Tag SNP `rs113010081` and Imputed `rs333`
* **WHY:** Because the `rs62625034` probe hybridizes inside the 32-bp deletion of `rs333` and fails to call genotypes in 13,979 British-ancestry individuals (3.4% missingness), any preferential dropout or cluster shift of $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ homozygotes will directly create a spurious deficit of homozygotes ($F < 0$) and could bias survival estimates if hybridization intensity covaries with DNA quality or recruitment center.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the exact HWE test ($F$), age-stratified HWE correlation, log-rank test, and Cox proportional hazards model using (a) the directly genotyped flanking SNP `rs113010081` (which lies outside the deletion and has $r^2 = 0.93\text{--}0.94$ with `rs333`) and (b) the UK Biobank imputed genotype calls for `rs333` (`3:46414947_TACAGTCAGTATCAATTCTGGAAGAATTTCCAG_T`).
* **HOW:** Using the authors' existing UK Biobank basket and GitHub pipeline, extract genotypes for `rs113010081` and imputed `rs333` (filtering at INFO $> 0.9$), tabulate the $3 \times 3$ genotype concordance matrix against `rs62625034` (including the 13,979 missing samples), and report side-by-side in Table 1: missingness rate, $F$, log-rank two-tailed $P$, and Cox HR (95% CI) for all three markers.

#### 2. Match Control SNPs on Call Rate / Missingness and Local LD when Testing $F$
* **WHY:** In Figure 1b, $F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32}$ (computed on $N = 395,714$ samples, 96.6% call rate) is compared against 5,932 MAF-matched SNPs whose mean sample size is $405,428$ (99.0% call rate). Array probes with high missingness ($>3\%$) systematically exhibit technical HWE distortions due to poor cluster separation.
* **WHAT:** Restrict or stratify the empirical null distribution of $F$ to array probes matched not only on MAF ($\pm 0.0025$) but also on genotype missingness rate ($\sim 3\text{--}4\%$) and probe hybridization quality, or evaluate $F$ using only high-call-rate flanking/imputed markers.
* **HOW:** Compute the missingness rate for all 5,932 MAF-matched SNPs in the UK Biobank British subset, plot $F$ versus missingness rate to check for technical correlation, and re-calculate the empirical $P$-value for both `rs62625034` and `rs113010081`.

#### 3. Eliminate Sliding-Window Autocorrelation in Extended Data Figure 1 and Report Two-Tailed Survival Tests Across Full Follow-Up
* **WHY:** In Extended Data Figure 1a, the per-dot sample sizes average $65,479$ across ~25 age points (summing to $>4\times$ the cohort size of $395,704$), showing that overlapping age windows were used. Computing Spearman's correlation ($\rho = 0.67, P = 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$) across overlapping sliding windows against a monotonic cumulative survival curve violates independence and severely inflates significance. Similarly, reporting one-tailed $P$-values truncated at age 76 rather than two-tailed $P$-values across the full cohort follow-up overstates statistical support.
* **WHAT:** Compute the age-specific HWE deviation on strictly non-overlapping 1-year age cohorts (weighted by inverse variance) or assess significance by permuting genotype labels across individuals before computing the sliding windows, and report two-tailed $P$-values for the Cox proportional hazards model across all uncensored ages (41–78) without post-hoc age truncation.
* **HOW:** In R, run `coxph(Surv(age_recruit, age_exit, death) ~ I(geno == 2) + sex + PC1 + ... + PC10)` across the entire follow-up range without dropping ages 77–78 (as the Cox partial likelihood naturally handles small risk sets at older ages without requiring binned $h_{76} = h_{77}$ assumptions) and report the two-tailed Wald and likelihood-ratio $P$-values. For Extended Data Figure 1a, compute the null distribution of Spearman's $\rho$ across 10,000 permutations of age-at-recruitment across individuals.

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** The survival probability formula in *Materials and Methods* is mathematically incorrect as written, and the value of $F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32}$ in the Figure 1b caption contradicts the figure axis by a factor of 10.
* **WHAT & HOW:**  
  * **Line 64 (Survival probability equation error):** The text states *"The death rate per year is then calculated as $h_i = O_i / N_i$, and the probability of surviving to age $i + 1$ is $S_i = \prod_{n=1}^{n=i} h_n$."* Multiplying death rates $h_n$ yields zero; correct the equation to $S_i = \prod_{n=41}^{i} (1 - h_n)$.  
  * **Line 36 (Figure 1b caption $F$ value discrepancy):** The caption states *"The black arrow points to the observed $F$ of $\Delta 32$ ($F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} = -0.19$)"*, whereas a 19% deficit would correspond to $\sim 4,500$ rather than $\sim 5,500$ homozygotes (Line 28). Check whether $-0.19$ is a decimal typo for $-0.019$ and make the caption, main text, and Figure 1b x-axis consistent.  
  * **Line 97 (Extended Data Figure 1 caption):** Fix the truncated sentence *"The observed and predicted values are coefficient $\rho = 0.67, P = 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$)."* and explicitly state the sliding-window width used to generate each point.
