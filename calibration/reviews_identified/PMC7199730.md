### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Van Dorp et al. analyze 7,666 filtered SARS-CoV-2 genome assemblies downloaded from GISAID as of April 19, 2020 to estimate the time to the most recent common ancestor (tMRCA), characterize global phylogeographic structure, and identify recurrent mutations (homoplasies) across the viral genome. Using root-to-tip regression in `BactDating` ($R^2 = 0.20, p < 0.001$) and strict-clock temporal calibration in `TreeDater` with 50 parametric bootstrap replicates, the authors estimate a substitution rate of $\sim 6 \times 10^{-4}$ substitutions per site per year (95% CI: $4 \times 10^{-4} - 7 \times 10^{-4}$) and a tMRCA between October 6 and December 11, 2019. Applying Fitch parsimony via `HomoplasyFinder` on an `MPBoot` maximum parsimony tree, they identify 1,042 unmasked homoplastic sites and apply heuristic quality filters ($\ge 0.1\%$ isolate frequency, nearest-neighbor clustering metric $p_{nn} > 0.1$, presence across multiple originating and submitting laboratories, and zero ambiguous bases within $\pm 5$ bp) to retain 198 high-confidence recurrent sites, validating a subset against 348 samples with raw reads in the NCBI Short Read Archive (SRA). Code for homoplasy filtering and SRA variant calling, as well as an interactive Shiny visualization app, are publicly provided.

This is a well-executed early genomic surveillance study that recognizes and actively mitigates GISAID assembly artifacts through multi-lab filtering and raw-read cross-validation. The primary methodological limitations are the reliance on a single point-estimate Maximum Parsimony tree topology in a low-diversity regime (average pairwise distance of 9.6 SNPs)—where arbitrary polytomy resolution inflates inferred parsimony step counts—and the absence of a formal neutral mutational null model when interpreting the 80% non-synonymous fraction among recurrent mutations as evidence of positive host adaptation. These issues can be resolved through computational re-analysis across bootstrap tree distributions and codon-aware neutral simulations without generating new data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **All circulating human SARS-CoV-2 sequences share a common ancestor between October 6 and December 11, 2019 ($95\%$ CI), ruling out cryptic widespread human circulation months prior to late 2019: [Well Supported].**  
  The temporal signal across the 7,666 genomes is statistically significant ($R^2 = 0.20, p < 0.001$ across 1,000 date permutations), and the `TreeDater` strict-clock estimate concurs with independent Bayesian (`BEAST`) analyses summarized in Table 1. One technical caveat in Section 2.3 is that the 50 parametric bootstrap replicates were generated on the *unmasked* alignment while keeping the tree topology fixed, which understates topological uncertainty and re-introduces noisy terminal sites that were masked during tree inference.

* **Viral diversity within well-sampled countries (UK, USA, Iceland, Australia) recapitulates global phylogenetic diversity, indicating numerous independent introductions early in the pandemic: [Well Supported].**  
  The phylogenetic distribution of tips across major clades (Figures 1c and 2) clearly demonstrates that local epidemics in Europe and North America were seeded repeatedly from the global pool rather than arising from single point introductions, while the authors appropriately note that geographic clustering on the tree largely reflects temporal waves of sequencing effort.

* **198 filtered sites (notably positions 11,083 in Nsp6, 13,402 in Nsp11, 16,887 in Nsp13, and 21,575 in Spike) represent robust recurrent mutations validated against sequencing errors: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The multi-criteria filtering pipeline and the 348-sample SRA raw-read comparison (where 9 of 10 filtered SRA homoplasies matched the GISAID calls) eliminate singleton errors and lab-specific primers/contaminants. However, `HomoplasyFinder` was run on a single binary tree produced by `MPBoot`. Because 7,666 genomes differing by an average of 9.6 SNPs contain extensive multifurcations (polytomies), arbitrary binary resolution of zero-length branches forces shared derived alleles onto separate sister clades, artificially inflating both the Fitch minimum change count and the nearest-neighbor clustering statistic $p_{nn}$.

* **Nearly 80% of filtered recurrent mutations are non-synonymous, pointing to ongoing positive adaptation of SARS-CoV-2 to the human host: [Overstated].**  
  In Section 3.3 and the Abstract, the observation that 232 of 290 amino acid changes at the 198 homoplastic sites are non-synonymous ($\sim 80\%$) is framed as a signature of ongoing adaptive evolution. Under a random nucleotide substitution model across the SARS-CoV-2 coding genome, approximately $75\%$ to $78\%$ of all possible point mutations are non-synonymous simply due to the structure of the genetic code. Furthermore, strong host-driven mutational biases (such as APOBEC-mediated $C \to U$ deamination or reactive oxygen species $G \to U$ transversions) repeatedly hit susceptible sequence motifs, generating recurrent non-synonymous mutations under neutral or slightly deleterious drift before purifying selection can purge them. Without testing against a context-dependent neutral substitution model (e.g., $dN/dS$ or mutation-spectrum-informed permutation), an 80% non-synonymous fraction does not demonstrate positive selection.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Account for Polytomies and Topological Uncertainty in Homoplasy Counting
* **WHY:** Running Fitch parsimony (`HomoplasyFinder`) on a single fully resolved `MPBoot` bifurcating tree in a low-divergence alignment (9.6 pairwise SNPs across 29.9 kb) creates spurious homoplasies whenever sequences sharing a mutation are split across arbitrarily resolved zero-length internal branches.
* **WHAT:** Collapse near-zero-length branches into hard polytomies or evaluate minimum parsimony changes across the 1,000 `MPBoot` bootstrap trees and the RAxML Maximum Likelihood tree.
* **HOW:** Before passing the tree to `HomoplasyFinder`, collapse all internal branches with length $< 1/29903$ (fewer than 0.5 expected substitutions) or bootstrap support $< 50\%$ using `ape::di2multi` in R, and report the median and 95% interval of inferred state changes per site across 100 bootstrap topologies.

#### 2. Benchmark the Non-Synonymous Homoplasy Fraction Against a Context-Aware Neutral Null
* **WHY:** Comparing the observed 80% non-synonymous fraction (232/290) implicitly against 50% rather than the genetic-code baseline ($\sim 76\%$) conflates the baseline coding opportunity for non-synonymous changes and directional host $C \to U$ hypermutation with positive selection.
* **WHAT:** Compute the expected proportion of non-synonymous changes and hydrophobicity/charge shifts under the empirical 12-rate nucleotide substitution spectrum of SARS-CoV-2.
* **HOW:** Tally the empirical 12-component transition/transversion matrix from synonymous singletons in the 7,666-genome alignment, simulate 10,000 random recurrent mutation sets weighted by trinucleotide/dinucleotide context across the reference ORFs, and compute an exact empirical $p$-value for whether 232/290 non-synonymous changes exceeds neutral expectations.

#### 3. Align Masking Protocols Between Point Estimation and Parametric Bootstrapping in `TreeDater`
* **WHY:** Section 2.2 masks the first 130 bp, last 50 bp, and four artifact-prone sites (18529, 29849, 29851, 29853) prior to RAxML tree inference, but Section 2.3 states that parametric bootstrapping in `TreeDater` was performed on the *unmasked* alignment. Including noisy terminal sites in branch-length resampling distorts the Poisson variance of tip-to-root distances.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the 50 `TreeDater` parametric bootstrap replicates strictly on the masked alignment of length 29,719 bp.
* **HOW:** Apply the identical site mask from the Augur pipeline before Poisson branch-length resampling in `TreeDater`, and report the updated substitution rate in standard units of substitutions per site per year.

#### 4. Temper Adaptive Framing in the Abstract and Highlights
* **WHY:** While the Discussion appropriately acknowledges that most mutations are neutral or deleterious and that recurrent mutations can arise neutrally, the Abstract and Highlights emphasize "ongoing adaptation" and "convergent evolution" based primarily on raw parsimony counts and the 80% non-synonymous fraction.
* **WHAT:** Reframe the 198 recurrent sites in the Abstract and Highlights as a quality-filtered catalog of mutational hotspots and candidate sites requiring functional or selection testing, rather than direct evidence of host adaptation.
* **HOW:** Revise Highlight 4 and the Abstract conclusion to state that recurrent mutations reflect a combination of sequence-specific mutational hotspots (such as host RNA editing) and putative selection candidates, noting that the 80% non-synonymous rate is close to the neutral expectation for early coding diversity.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Several numerical denominators and units in the Results section are internally inconsistent or dimensionally incorrect.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Section 3.1 (line 175):** Correct the substitution rate units from `"~6 × 10−4 nucleotides/genome/year"` to `"~6 × 10−4 substitutions per site per year"` ($6 \times 10^{-4}$ substitutions per site per year equals $\sim 18$ substitutions per genome per year).
  2. **Section 3.3 (line 203):** Reconcile the inconsistent denominators in the amino acid property breakdown: the text states that 232 of 290 changes are non-synonymous, but immediately writes `"53 of the remaining 101 non-synonymous mutations involved neutral hydrophobicity changes"`, `"79 of the remaining 101 non-synonymous mutations involved neutral changes"`, and `"87 of the remaining 110 non-synonymous mutations involved neutral charge changes"`. Explicitly define how the subsets of 101 and 110 unique non-synonymous substitutions were derived from the 232 total non-synonymous changes (e.g., collapsing multiple alleles at the same site or restricting to specific ORFs).
  3. **Highlights (line 61):** Fix the typo `"COVID-2 pandemic"` to `"COVID-19 pandemic"`.
  4. **Table 1 (line 122):** Check the point estimate for Duchene et al. strict clock (`1.23 × 10−4`), which falls outside its own reported 95% HPD (`5.63 × 10−4, 1.98 × 10−3`) and should be `1.23 × 10−3`.
