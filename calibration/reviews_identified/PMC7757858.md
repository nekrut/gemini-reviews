### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Nejman et al. characterize the low-biomass bacterial microbiome across 1,526 human tumor and normal/normal-adjacent tissue (NAT) samples spanning seven solid tumor types (breast, lung, ovary, pancreas, melanoma, bone, and glioblastoma) alongside 811 negative controls (437 DNA extraction blanks, 206 PCR no-template controls, and 168 tissue-free paraffin block margins). Using a multiplexed 5-region (5R) 16S rDNA amplicon sequencing assay reconstructed via `SMURF` and filtered through six sequential contamination controls, combined with 16S qPCR, immunohistochemistry (anti-LPS and anti-LTA), 16S rRNA FISH across $>400$ additional tumor cores, correlative light and electron microscopy (CLEM), ex vivo fluorescent D-alanine metabolic labeling, and aerobic/anaerobic culturomics of five fresh breast tumors, the authors demonstrate that viable, metabolically active bacteria reside intracellularly within both cancer cells and CD45+ immune cells and that breast tumors harbor a particularly rich microbiome (mean 16.4 species per tumor). Sequencing data (BioProject PRJNA624822) and analysis/filtering code (Zenodo) are publicly available.

This is a landmark experimental and bioinformatic effort that sets a high bar for physical and negative-control validation in low-biomass tissue microbiome research. However, three methodological issues temper the downstream statistical claims regarding tumor-type specificity and metabolic pathway enrichment: (1) Filter 6 of the contamination pipeline retains a species in a tissue type only if it is significantly enriched in that tissue type across medical centers, which introduces selection circularity when subsequently evaluating beta-diversity clustering (Figure 4A, F) on tissue-filtered hit lists; (2) metabolic pathway claims rely entirely on `PICRUSt2` 16S phylogenetic imputation rather than shotgun metagenomics, yielding significant pathway hits (e.g., 49 pathways in smoker lungs at $Q < 0.25$) even when zero individual bacterial taxa pass multiple-testing correction; and (3) clinical subgroup comparisons use a permissive false discovery rate ($Q < 0.25$) without adjusting for patient-level confounders. These issues can be addressed through non-circular beta-diversity re-analysis and stricter statistical thresholds.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Viable, metabolically active bacteria are physically present inside human tumor and immune cells, often in a cell-wall-deficient (L-form-like) perinuclear state: [Well Supported].**  
  The convergence of orthogonal experimental modalities—16S qPCR above the 99th percentile of extraction/paraffin controls, spatial co-localization of anti-LPS IHC and 16S rRNA FISH (with complement probe negative controls), CLEM imaging of intracellular perinuclear bacteria in four breast tumors (Figure 2E), intracellular incorporation of fluorescent D-alanine into peptidoglycan in four live breast tumor slice cultures (Figure 3G), and recovery of $>1,000$ colonies per tumor across four of five fresh breast tumors versus 5 total colonies across 350 PBS control plates—provides strong evidence of genuine intracellular bacterial colonization.

* **Breast tumors harbor a richer and more diverse bacterial microbiome than the other six solid tumor types and normal breast tissue: [Well Supported].**  
  Across rarefied subsampling (Figure 3C–F), breast tumors consistently show higher genus/species richness (mean 16.4 species vs. $<9$ in other tumors, $P < 10^{-17}$) and progressive enrichment from healthy breast tissue to NAT to breast tumor.

* **Each tumor type possesses a distinct species-level microbial composition that separates tumor types in Jaccard PCoA and heatmaps: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While order-level phylotype differences (such as Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes in colorectal tumors vs. Proteobacteria in pancreatic tumors; Figure 4B) are biologically plausible, the species-level beta-diversity separation in Figure 4A and 4F is partly inflated by circular conditioning in the filtering pipeline. Specifically, Filter 6 ("excluded bacteria that were not significantly enriched in a specific tumor type across multiple medical centers", line 246) uses the tumor-type label to decide whether a species is zeroed out or retained as a "hit" in that tumor type. Figure 4F then computes Jaccard PCoA where "Only bacteria that passed all filters for the specific tissue type were considered" (line 263). Zeroing out non-enriched taxa on a per-tumor-type basis before computing pairwise Jaccard similarity mathematically forces samples of the same tumor type to share a tissue-specific support set and cluster together.

* **Intratumor bacterial metabolic pathways (`PICRUSt2`) specifically adapt to tumor microenvironments, such as cigarette-smoke chemical degradation and plant-metabolite synthesis in smokers' lung tumors and mycothiol synthesis in ER+ breast tumors: [Fragile].**  
  These functional claims rest entirely on `PICRUSt2` predictions imputed from partial 16S rDNA sequences rather than shotgun metagenomic or metatranscriptomic measurement of actual metabolic genes. In the smoker ($n=100$) vs. never-smoker ($n=43$) lung tumor comparison, *none* of the bacterial taxa differed significantly after multiple-testing correction (Figure 5C; line 282), yet 49 `MetaCyc` pathways were called significant at a permissive FDR of $Q < 0.25$ using an unadjusted two-sample proportion $z$-test (Figure 5B). Because `PICRUSt2` assigns shared reference pathways to broad Proteobacterial/Actinobacterial clades, minor non-significant shifts across dozens of Proteobacterial species aggregate into nominally significant pathway proportions without proving that those accessory catabolic operons (often plasmid-borne or strain-specific) are actually present or expressed in vivo.

* **Specific intratumor bacterial taxa correlate with breast cancer receptor subtypes (ER/PR/HER2) and melanoma response to immune checkpoint inhibitors ($n = 29$ responders vs. $n = 48$ non-responders): [Fragile].**  
  Subtype and ICI response comparisons (Figures 4E and 5F) rely on univariate proportion/binomial tests at a lenient FDR cutoff ($Q < 0.25$) without controlling for medical center of origin, patient age, sex, prior systemic therapy, or antibiotic exposure, leaving these associations vulnerable to clinical and batch confounding.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Tissue-Specific Filtering Circularity When Evaluating Beta-Diversity Across Tumor Types
* **WHY:** Applying Filter 6 separately within each tumor type (retaining only species enriched in that tumor type) and zeroing out all non-passing species in that tumor type prior to Jaccard similarity and PCoA (Figure 4A, 4F) leaks the class label into the feature matrix and artificially inflates between-tumor separation.
* **WHAT:** Re-compute the Jaccard similarity matrix (Figure 4A), PCoA (Figure 4F), and PERMANOVA $R^2 / P$-values using a single, unified post-decontamination feature matrix across all samples where Filters 1–5 are applied globally without zeroing out species on a per-tumor-type basis via Filter 6.
* **HOW:** Construct a global species presence/absence matrix retaining all species that pass laboratory and paraffin contamination Filters 1–5 (or the union of all species passing Filter 6 in at least one tissue, evaluated across *all* samples without setting non-hit tissues to zero), and report the resulting within- vs. between-tumor Jaccard similarities and PCoA ordination.

#### 2. Calibrate `PICRUSt2` Pathway Enrichment Using Taxon-Label Permutation Nulls and Stricter FDR Control
* **WHY:** Because hundreds of `MetaCyc` pathways are co-inherited across the 16S reference phylogeny, testing 287 correlated pathways with independent two-sample proportion $z$-tests at $Q < 0.25$ produces inflated false positives when underlying taxonomic shifts are statistically null (as in Figure 5C).
* **WHAT:** Evaluate pathway enrichment in smoker vs. never-smoker NSCLC and ER+ vs. ER- breast cancer using sample-label permutations and a standard FDR threshold ($Q < 0.05$).
* **HOW:** Permute smoker/never-smoker (and ER+/ER-) labels 10,000 times across patients stratified by medical center, re-compute the pathway proportion $z$-statistics in each permutation to account for pathway correlation structure, and report which pathways survive at family-wise or permutation-calibrated FDR $Q < 0.05$. Explicitly state in the Abstract and Results that metabolic functions were * computationally imputed from 16S taxonomy* rather than directly sequenced.

#### 3. Adjust Clinical Subgroup Comparisons for Medical Center and Patient Covariates
* **WHY:** Breast cancer receptor subtypes and melanoma ICI responder cohorts were collected across multiple hospitals; univariate $z$-tests and binomial tests do not account for center-level baseline microbiome differences or patient covariates (age, stage, prior treatment).
* **WHAT:** Re-analyze differential taxon prevalence in Figure 4E (breast subtypes), Figure 4G (tumor vs. NAT), and Figure 5F (melanoma ICI response) using multivariable logistic regression or Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel tests stratified by medical center.
* **HOW:** For each taxon, fit `glm(presence ~ subtype + center + age, family = binomial)` in R (and a paired conditional logistic regression or McNemar's test for matched patient tumor-NAT pairs in Figure 4G), and report results at both $Q < 0.05$ and $Q < 0.25$.

#### 4. Clarify Taxonomic Resolution Limits of `SMURF` 5R Reconstruction
* **WHY:** Augmenting Greengenes with RDP species labels for 380,000 reference sequences allows `SMURF` to report "species-level" assignments, yet 28% of cultured breast tumor colonies (105 of 374 sequenced isolates; line 250) could not be assigned to a species even with whole-genome sequencing, and many hits in Figure 4D–E are labeled "unknown species (US)".
* **WHAT:** Quantify the posterior ambiguity of `SMURF` species assignments (number of indistinguishable reference species per reconstructed 5R cluster) and discuss why 40.5% of cultured breast tumor species were filtered out by the 6-filter pipeline.
* **HOW:** Add a supplementary table or histogram reporting the distribution of `SMURF` ambiguity set sizes across the 9,190 called species, and detail which of the six filters eliminated the 15 cultured bacterial species that were detected in 16S reads but failed filtering.
