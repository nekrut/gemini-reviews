# Review of PMC7196762

**Manuscript Title:** Phylogenetic network analysis of SARS-CoV-2 genomes.  
**Authors:** Peter Forster, Lucy Forster, Colin Renfrew, Michael Forster  
**Journal / Preprint:** Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (2020) | DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2004999117  
**Overall Score:** **3.2 / 10.0** (Flawed / Overstated)  
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.8/3.5 | Claims: 0.6/2.5 | Biology: 0.7/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.1/2.0

---

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Forster et al. apply a median-joining phylogenetic network algorithm (`Network 5011CS`, with $\epsilon = 0$ and $\epsilon = 10$ followed by Steiner tree reduction) to 160 complete human SARS-CoV-2 genomes (comprising 100 distinct haplotypes truncated to positions 56–29,797) downloaded from GISAID as of March 4, 2020. Using the bat coronavirus BatCoV RaTG13 (96.2% nucleotide identity) as an outgroup to root the network, the authors define three clusters—A, B, and C—designating cluster A as the ancestral human viral type. From the geographic distribution of these 160 sequences, they conclude that types A and C predominate in Europeans and Americans, whereas type B is predominant in East Asia and could not spread outside East Asia without further mutation due to founder effects or host immunological/environmental resistance. Sequences are accessible via GISAID and network files are hosted on the Fluxus Technology website.

While applying haplotype visualization to early pandemic surveillance data is well-intentioned, the study's core evolutionary and epidemiological conclusions are undermined by three fundamental methodological flaws: (1) rooting an intraspecific network of 160 nearly identical human viral genomes (tree length of 229 mutations across 29,742 nt) with a distantly related bat virus differing at $\sim 1,150$ nucleotide positions, where homoplasy and transition bias along the outgroup branch render single-site parsimony rooting statistically unidentifiable; (2) treating a convenience sample of 160 early GISAID sequences as an unbiased census of global viral prevalence and host immunology; and (3) using an unweighted, clock-free parsimony heuristic (`median-joining`) that ignores collection dates and substitution rate heterogeneity. Re-Evaluating these data requires removing the distant outgroup in favor of tip-dated phylodynamic rooting and stripping unsupported claims regarding immunological resistance and ancestral geographic origins.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Cluster A is the ancestral human SARS-CoV-2 genome type based on outgroup rooting with BatCoV RaTG13: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  BatCoV RaTG13 differs from human SARS-CoV-2 at roughly 3.8% of nucleotides ($\sim 1,150$ substitutions), whereas the entire 160-genome human SARS-CoV-2 network spans a Steiner tree length of 229 mutations ($\sim 0.08\%$ per-site divergence). Under unweighted parsimony, the placement of the outgroup root between human clusters A and B (which are separated by only two mutations, at positions 8,782 and 28,144) depends entirely on whether RaTG13 happens to match the nucleotide state of cluster A or B at those two sites. Along a branch of $\sim 1,150$ substitutions—particularly given intense $C \to U$ and $G \to A$ transition biases in betacoronaviruses—the probability of back-mutation or parallel substitution at those two diagnostic sites is high, and this outgroup rooting directly contradicts the temporal evidence that the earliest December 2019 Wuhan genomes belong to cluster B.

* **Ancestral type B could not spread outside East Asia without mutating into derived B types, pointing to immunological or environmental resistance outside Asia: [Overstated].**  
  This claim extrapolates a sweeping biological hypothesis (population-specific immunological or environmental resistance to a viral haplotype differing by a couple of point mutations) from 160 non-randomly sampled genomes collected prior to March 4, 2020. Early GISAID depositions were heavily skewed toward specific travel cohorts, contact-traced clusters, and countries with active sequencing programs. Observing derived mutations in exported lineages is the standard expectation of serial founder bottlenecks coupled with ongoing viral replication ($\sim 2$ mutations per month), requiring no invocation of host immunological or environmental resistance.

* **Character-based median-joining networks are the "method of choice" for reconstructing SARS-CoV-2 evolutionary paths under selection and parallel evolution: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Median-joining networks (Bandelt et al. 1999) were designed as distance-free parsimony heuristics for intraspecific recombination-free markers without a molecular clock. They do not parameterize nucleotide substitution biases (e.g., $C \to U$ hypermutation), rate variation across sites, or sampling dates, and they treat every character state conflict—including recurrent sequencing artifacts or hypermutable sites like position 11,083—as an unweighted reticulation. Maximum likelihood and Bayesian phylodynamic models that incorporate tip dates and explicit substitution models are strictly superior for inferring viral ancestor-descendant relationships.

* **The phylogenetic network faithfully traces documented infection routes and can identify undocumented infection sources for quarantine: [Fragile].**  
  Because SARS-CoV-2 evolves more slowly ($\sim 1$ substitution every 11–15 days) than its serial interval ($\sim 4–6$ days), identical viral genomes are shared across many unlinked transmission chains, and travel history correlation in a handful of early cases does not validate directionality of transmission from sequence topology alone, especially when the root of the network is inverted.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Outgroup Rooting on BatCoV RaTG13 with Tip-Dated Molecular Clock Rooting
* **WHY:** A 3.8% divergent outgroup ($\sim 1,150$ mutations) experiences substantial multiple hits and transition saturation at hypermutable sites, making parsimony attachment to an ingroup differing by $1–10$ SNPs statistically invalid and reversing the true temporal polarity between clusters A and B.
* **WHAT:** Exclude BatCoV RaTG13 from the human intraspecific alignment and root the human SARS-CoV-2 phylogeny using sample collection dates.
* **HOW:** Use maximum likelihood tip-dating (`TreeTime` or `LSD2` / `IQ-TREE`) or Bayesian skyline coalescent dating (`BEAST`) on the 160 human genomes using their exact GISAID collection dates, and report the posterior/bootstrap probability of the root haplotype (comparing Wuhan-Hu-1 / cluster B against cluster A).

#### 2. Quantify Uncertainty in Outgroup Attachment via Substitution Simulations
* **WHY:** If an outgroup comparison is retained for discussion, the probability of homoplasy at the diagnostic sites separating clusters A and B (positions 8,782 and 28,144) must be formally calculated.
* **WHAT:** Compute the likelihood of parallel or back-mutations at positions 8,782 (synonymous C/T) and 28,144 (T/C) along the branch connecting RaTG13 to the human SARS-CoV-2 MRCA.
* **HOW:** Fit an asymmetric GTR+$\Gamma$ model to the Sarbecovirus alignment in `IQ-TREE` or `RAxML-NG`, perform ancestral state reconstruction at the human MRCA node, and report the marginal posterior probabilities of states C and T at positions 8,782 and 28,144.

#### 3. Account for Ascertainment Bias and Serial Founder Drift Before Invoking Host Resistance
* **WHY:** With $N = 160$ sequences unevenly distributed across 82 laboratories, geographic differences in clade frequencies reflect contact-tracing clusters and travel restrictions rather than biological resistance.
* **WHAT:** Test whether the geographic distribution of A, B, and C haplotypes deviates from a neutral spatial import-export branching process given the actual number of sequenced introductions per country.
* **HOW:** Collapse epidemiologically linked household/travel cluster samples into single index introductions, and perform a permutation test of clade frequency across regions conditioned on sampling date. Remove all claims of "immunological or environmental resistance against this type outside Asia" from the Abstract and Discussion.

#### 4. Mask Known Hypermutable and Artifactual Sites Prior to Network Construction
* **WHY:** Figure 1 notes that network reticulations are "mainly caused by recurrent mutations at np11083." Position 11,083 is a notorious homoplastic site prone to both recurrent mutation and assembly error in poly-U/A tracts, which creates artificial loops in median-joining networks.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the network and phylogenetic analyses after masking position 11,083 and terminal alignment regions prone to primer/sequencing error.
* **HOW:** Mask position 11,083 (and verify sensitivity to coding 24-nt deletions as single events) and compare the resulting tree topology and Steiner length against a standard Maximum Likelihood tree inferred under GTR+$\Gamma$.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Essential methodological details and quantitative network statistics are missing from the main text.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Materials and Methods (line 16):** Specify the exact nucleotide positions and codon changes that define clusters A, B, and C in the main text rather than leaving them solely to poster figures.
  2. **Abstract (line 7):** Remove the promotional and unsubstantiated statement that character-based phylogenetic networks are "the method of choice for reconstructing their evolutionary paths and their ancestral genome in the human host."
  3. **Data Availability (line 21):** Provide standard, open-format alignment (FASTA) and tree/network files (Newick/Nexus) in a permanent public repository (e.g., Zenodo or GitHub) rather than requiring proprietary/shareware software (`Network5011CS` on Fluxus Technology) to inspect the data.
