# Peer Review: Genome-Wide Association Studies in a Large Korean Cohort Identify Quantitative Trait Loci for 36 Traits and Illuminate Their Genetic Architectures (PMC12120081)

**Overall Score:** **7.8 / 10.0** (Strong / Sound)
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 2.5/3.5 | Claims: 2.0/2.5 | Biology: 1.6/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.7/2.0

## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis

Jee et al. report a genome-wide association study (GWAS) of 36 quantitative cardiometabolic, anthropometric, hematological, hepatic, renal, and endocrine traits in up to 153,950 Korean participants from the Korean Cancer Prevention Study-II (KCPS2) Biobank. Applying linear mixed models (SAIGE v1.1.9) to genotypes imputed against the 1000 Genomes Project Phase 3 reference panel, the authors identify 2,962 independent trait-associated loci ($P < 5 \times 10^{-8}$), including 301 lead variants not previously reported in the GWAS Catalog, Open Targets Genetics, Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study (KoGES), or Taiwan Biobank (TWB). The authors subsequently perform fixed-effect and random-effect meta-analyses across 21 shared traits combining KCPS2 with KoGES ($N \approx 72,000$), BioBank Japan (BBJ, $N \approx 179,000$), TWB ($N \approx 102,000$), and the UK Biobank (UKB, $N \approx 420,000$), identifying 12,224 locus-trait associations (4,588 newly reaching significance in the combined meta-analysis). They further compare polygenicity ($\pi$), SNP-based heritability ($h^2_{SNP}$), and selection parameter ($S$) across East Asian (EAS) and European (EUR) biobanks using SBayesS, estimate within- and cross-ancestry genetic correlations ($r_g$) via LDSC and Popcorn, and conduct statistical fine-mapping (SuSiE) and colocalization (`coloc.susie`) across the pleiotropic `ALDH2` locus on chromosome 12.

This study expands the catalog of East Asian quantitative trait associations and provides open access to both the summary statistics and analysis code on Zenodo. However, several methodological weaknesses temper the downstream comparative architecture and fine-mapping conclusions: (1) combining two distinct genotyping platforms (Illumina GSA v2 and Affymetrix Korean-Chip v1) by merging post-imputation dosage datasets imputed solely against the small 1000 Genomes Phase 3 reference panel ($N = 2,504$ total; $\sim 504$ EAS) limits imputation accuracy for low-frequency Korean variants and introduces platform-specific imputation artifacts; (2) the cross-biobank SBayesS comparisons of polygenicity ($\pi$) and selection ($S$) are confounded by large sample size differences ($N \approx 72\text{K}$ to $420\text{K}$), model misspecification in KoGES (where linear mixed model summary statistics were passed to SBayesS), and LD reference mismatch in TWB (where a Korean KCPS2 LD reference was applied to Taiwanese summary statistics, generating an impossible $h^2_{SNP} > 1.0$ on chromosome 2 for bilirubin); and (3) SuSiE fine-mapping of the `ALDH2` region on the merged two-chip KCPS2 dataset produces multi-signal artifacts (such as seven separate single-SNP credible sets with $\text{PIP} = 1.0$ for alcohol intake) driven by differential imputation/genotyping coverage between the two arrays.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Evaluation

* **Claim 1:** GWAS of 36 quantitative traits in 153,950 KCPS2 participants identifies 2,962 independent trait-associated loci, of which 301 represent associations not previously reported in Open Targets Genetics, the GWAS Catalog, KoGES, or TWB, including an East Asian-enriched missense variant in *CD36* (`rs75326924`, p.Pro90Ser) associated with lower thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH). **[Well Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The primary SAIGE v1.1.9 linear mixed model analysis accounts for cryptic relatedness and population structure (with 10 PCs, age, sex, and array batch covariates). LD clumping ($r^2 < 0.1$ within 500 kb windows merging within 250 kb) and systematic cross-checking against major repositories support the 2,962 loci and 301 novel signals. Notably, the *CD36* missense variant `rs75326924` (p.Pro90Ser) associated with TSH in KCPS2 ($\text{MAF} = 0.008$, $\beta = -0.151$, $P = 3.21 \times 10^{-11}$) replicates in BBJ alongside an independent EAS loss-of-function variant (`rs56381858`, p.Gln74Ter) that also reduces TSH levels ($\beta = -0.152$, $P = 2.66 \times 10^{-11}$), providing an allelic series linking *CD36* function to TSH variation.

* **Claim 2:** Cross-biobank meta-analysis of 21 traits across four East Asian cohorts (KCPS2, KoGES, BBJ, TWB) and UKB identifies 12,224 trait-associated loci, including 4,588 loci that were not genome-wide significant in any individual cohort prior to meta-analysis, with effect sizes showing high directional concordance between fixed-effects (METAL) and random-effects (MR-MEGA / Han–Eskin RE2) models. **[Well Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The meta-analysis design (Figure 1, Table 1, Supplementary Data 6–7) and sensitivity comparison between inverse-variance weighted fixed-effects (METAL) and random-effects (RE2 / MR-MEGA accounting for ancestry principal components) demonstrate that $93.9\%$ of METAL loci remain genome-wide significant under MR-MEGA, confirming that the vast majority of discovered loci are driven by consistent additive effects rather than extreme inter-cohort heterogeneity.

* **Claim 3:** Cross-biobank genetic correlation analyses (LDSC within EAS and Popcorn between EAS and EUR) reveal lower cross-ancestry genetic correlations ($r_g$ between EAS and UKB) than within-EAS correlations, whereas within-Korea correlations (KCPS2 vs. KoGES) exceed $r_g = 0.87$ across all shared traits. **[Well Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Figure 3 and Supplementary Data 8 show consistent patterns: mean $r_g = 0.903$ between KCPS2 and KoGES, $r_g = 0.822\text{--}0.838$ between Korean and Japanese/Taiwanese biobanks, and lower cross-ancestry $r_g$ with UKB ($0.722\text{--}0.800$), particularly for lifestyle and anthropometric traits such as alcohol consumption ($r_g = 0.435$) and BMI ($r_g = 0.692$).

* **Claim 4:** Bayesian genetic architecture estimation via SBayesS demonstrates widespread negative selection ($S < 0$) across all 21 traits in all five biobanks, while showing significant inter-biobank differences in polygenicity ($\pi$) and SNP heritability ($h^2_{SNP}$). **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** While the qualitative finding that $S < 0$ (negative relationship between minor allele frequency and squared effect size) holds across traits, the quantitative comparisons of $\pi$, $h^2_{SNP}$, and $S$ across the five biobanks (Figure 4, Supplementary Data 10) suffer from three methodological confounders:
    1. *Sample size confounding on $\pi$:* As shown in Figure 4b, estimated polygenicity $\pi$ increases monotonically with sample size from TWB/KoGES ($N \approx 72\text{K}\text{--}102\text{K}$) to KCPS2/BBJ ($N \approx 154\text{K}\text{--}179\text{K}$) and UKB ($N \approx 420\text{K}$). At smaller sample sizes, SBayesS lacks power to distinguish small-effect polygenic variants from zero, shrinking them into the point-mass at zero and downward-biasing $\pi$ while upward-biasing the apparent per-SNP variance of included SNPs.
    2. *LD reference mismatch in TWB:* Because the authors lacked individual-level TWB genotypes, they ran SBayesS on Taiwanese summary statistics using the Korean KCPS2 LD matrix ($N = 50,000$). For total bilirubin, this LD mismatch caused SBayesS divergence on chromosome 2 around *UGT1A1*, yielding an impossible chromosome-2 heritability exceeding $1.0$ and total $h^2_{SNP} = 1.12$, forcing the authors to manually exclude chromosome 2 post hoc.
    3. *Model mismatch in KoGES:* The authors note that SBayesS requires standard linear regression summary statistics, so they re-ran PLINK2 linear regression on unrelated samples in KCPS2 ($N = 109,968$) and UKB ($N = 361,194$), but for KoGES they fed SAIGE linear mixed model summary statistics directly into SBayesS, which alters the effective sample size and residual variance scaling.

* **Claim 5:** Statistical fine-mapping (SuSiE) and multi-trait colocalization (`coloc.susie`) of the chromosome 12 `ALDH2` region (111–113 Mb) in KCPS2 resolves causal architecture across 26 traits, identifying up to 9 credible sets for γ-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT) and 7 single-variant credible sets ($\text{PIP} = 1$) for alcohol consumption, while grouping traits into shared pleiotropic clusters around `rs671`. **[Fragile]**
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The KCPS2 dataset was constructed by imputing two different genotyping arrays (64,812 individuals on Illumina GSA v2 and 89,138 on Affymetrix Korean-Chip v1) separately and merging post-imputation variants with INFO $\ge 0.8$. In a region under extreme selection and long-range LD like `ALDH2` (`12:111000000–113000000`, where the functional missense variant `rs671` has $Z$-scores $> 50$ for alcohol intake and GGT), summary-statistic fine-mapping with SuSiE ($L = 10$) is notoriously sensitive to tiny discrepancies in sample size or imputation accuracy between variants across merged arrays. Finding 7 distinct single-variant credible sets each with $\text{PIP} = 1.00$ for alcohol consumption in a 2 Mb window around `rs671`—where `rs671` itself is placed in the 3rd credible set and all 7 sets collapse into `rs671` when restricting $L = 1$—is a hallmark of summary-statistic/LD mismatch and array-merging artifacts rather than seven independent causal variants each with posterior probability $1.0$.

## 3. Technical, Statistical, and Biological Critique

### 3.1 Major Methodological and Statistical Issues

1. **Post-Imputation Merging of Two Different Genotyping Arrays Imputed Against 1000 Genomes Phase 3:**
   In KCPS2, 64,812 samples were genotyped on the Illumina Global Screening Array (GSA v2) and 89,138 samples on the Affymetrix Korean-Chip v1. Rather than imputing against a large population-specific Korean or East Asian whole-genome sequencing reference panel, both cohorts were phased with SHAPEIT4 and imputed with IMPUTE5 against the 1000 Genomes Project Phase 3 panel (which contains only $\sim 504$ East Asian individuals and no large Korean subset). Afterward, the two imputed datasets were merged by taking the intersection of variants with $\text{INFO} \ge 0.8$ ($5,551,935$ SNPs). Because GSA v2 and Korean-Chip v1 have different backbone probe sets, variants directly genotyped on one chip but imputed at $\text{INFO} \approx 0.80\text{--}0.85$ on the other exhibit differential effective sample sizes and error variances. While the authors included a binary chip indicator covariate in SAIGE to control for mean phenotype shifts between batches, a linear mean covariate does not correct for differential genotype dosage attenuation (errors-in-variables dilution) between the two chips. In regions with massive marginal effects like `ALDH2` (`rs671`), differential dosage attenuation across variants between the two chips breaks the local correlation structure between $Z$-scores and the composite in-sample LD matrix $\mathbf{R}$, driving false-positive multi-signal splits in `susie_rss`.

2. **Cross-Ancestry LD Mismatch and Sample Size Confounding in SBayesS:**
   Summary-based Bayesian regression methods such as SBayesS (`GCTB`) model the joint distribution of GWAS marginal effects $\hat{\mathbf{b}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{R}\sigma^2_e / N)$. When $\mathbf{R}$ is estimated from Korean individuals (KCPS2, $N = 50,000$) and applied to Taiwanese summary statistics (TWB, predominantly Southern Han Chinese ancestry), sub-continental LD differences at loci with large effects (such as *UGT1A1* on chromosome 2 for bilirubin) cause the MCMC sampler to fail to converge or inflate genetic variance past the phenotypic bound ($h^2_{SNP} = 1.12$). Even on chromosomes where $h^2_{SNP}$ does not exceed $1.0$, subtle LD mismatch between Korean and Southern Han Chinese populations inflates residual polygenic variance and distorts the inferred selection parameter $S$. Moreover, comparing raw posterior means of polygenicity $\pi$ across cohorts ranging nearly 6-fold in sample size ($72\text{K}$ vs. $420\text{K}$) without down-sampling or matching statistical power conflates true genetic architecture differences with discovery power.

3. **Lack of Multiple-Trait Correction in Genome-Wide Discovery Claims:**
   The authors define genome-wide significance at the standard single-trait threshold ($P < 5 \times 10^{-8}$) across 36 traits in KCPS2 and 21 traits in the multi-biobank meta-analysis, without applying a study-wide significance threshold (e.g., $P < 5 \times 10^{-8} / 36 \approx 1.39 \times 10^{-9}$) when claiming novel loci. For borderline novel variants near the $5 \times 10^{-8}$ threshold (especially low-frequency imputed variants with $\text{MAF} \approx 0.01\text{--}0.02$), reporting study-wide significance alongside nominal genome-wide significance would strengthen confidence in the 301 novel KCPS2 associations.

### 3.2 Minor Issues and Textual Inconsistencies

1. **Sample Size Reporting in Methods vs. Results:**
   In the Methods section ("Korean Cancer Prevention Study-II (KCPS2) Biobank"), the text states: *"Specifically, 78,260 participants were genotyped using the Illumina Global Screening Array (GSA) v2.0 and 90,245 using the Affymetrix Korean-chip v1.0"* (which sums to 168,505 pre-QC samples), and then states two sentences later: *"After quality control (QC), 153,950 participants remained for analysis (64,812 from GSA-chip and 89,138 from Korean-chip)."* Clearly labeling the first pair of numbers as pre-QC counts avoids confusion.
2. **Typographical Error in the Taiwan Biobank (TWB) Methods Description:**
   Under the heading **Taiwan Biobank (TWB)** in the Methods section, the first sentence reads: *"We extracted 23 quantitative traits from BBJ that matched those analyzed in KCPS2..."* where "BBJ" is a copy-paste typographical error for "TWB".
3. **Data and Code Availability:**
   Summary statistics (`10.5281/zenodo.15132424`) and analysis scripts (`10.5281/zenodo.15110489`) are deposited on Zenodo with clear versioning of software tools (`SAIGE v1.1.9`, `PLINK2`, `METAL`, `MR-MEGA`, `GCTB v2.02`, `LDSC v1.0.1`, `Popcorn v1.0`, `susieR v0.12.35`, `coloc v5.2.3`).
