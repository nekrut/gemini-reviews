**Manuscript Title:** Could the D614G substitution in the SARS-CoV-2 spike (S) protein be associated with higher COVID-19 mortality?  
**Authors:** Eaaswarkhanth M, Al Madhoun A, Al-Mulla F.  
**Journal / Preprint:** Int J Infect Dis (2020) | DOI: 10.1016/j.ijid.2020.05.071  
**Overall Score:** **2.5 / 10.0** (Fundamentally Invalid)  
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.4/3.5 | Claims: 0.8/2.5 | Biology: 0.6/2.0 | Reproducibility: 0.7/2.0

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

In this short communication, Eaaswarkhanth, Al Madhoun, and Al-Mulla hypothesize that the SARS-CoV-2 Spike D614G substitution (clade G) increases COVID-19 case fatality and explains cross-national disparities in COVID-19 mortality per million population as of 2 May 2020. The authors juxtapose screenshots from the public Nextstrain web interface (`nextstrain.org/ncov`, showing 4,246 GISAID sequences stratified into 614D and 614G clades and pie charts on a map of Europe) with a horizontal bar chart of crude national deaths per million population taken from Worldometer (`worldometers.info/coronavirus/`) for selected countries (Belgium, Spain, Italy, France, United Kingdom, Netherlands, Switzerland, Sweden, Ireland, Portugal, Germany, and Kuwait) (Fig. 1). They further display a static structural rendering of the Spike trimer (Fig. 2) and speculate that D614G mimics the open conformation to expose the S1/S2 cleavage site to FURIN or TMPRSS2, accelerating cleavage and increasing clinical virulence.

While the authors frame their piece with a question mark in the title and acknowledge that their evidence is circumstantial, the manuscript commits textbook methodological fallacies across almost every analytical pillar: (1) it contains **zero statistical tests**—no correlation coefficient, regression model, $P$-value, confidence interval, or contingency table is computed anywhere in the paper; (2) it relies entirely on an unadjusted ecological comparison between country-level Nextstrain clade pie charts and crude national deaths per million population (deaths divided by total census population, not even infected cases), confounding viral genotype with epidemic wave timing, total attack rate, population age structure, and testing/reporting criteria; (3) the Nextstrain pie charts in Fig. 1B directly contradict the authors' own text regarding Germany (the map shows Germany's sampled genomes are predominantly 614G orange, identical in proportion to Switzerland and the Netherlands, yet line 47 claims Germany and Kuwait "constitute most strains with the wild-type 614D"); and (4) the structural claim in Fig. 2 is a visual assertion without molecular dynamics, free-energy calculations, or protease cleavage assays. Without patient-level clinical outcome metadata linked to viral sequences and multivariable adjustment for age and epidemic phase, this ecological juxtaposition cannot support any inference regarding D614G and mortality.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Countries with a high prevalence of the SARS-CoV-2 S-D614G clade (Belgium, Spain, Italy, France, Netherlands, Switzerland) exhibit higher COVID-19 mortality per million population than countries dominated by wild-type 614D (Germany, Kuwait):** **[Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the metric plotted on the x-axis of Fig. 1B is *deaths per million total population* from Worldometer on 2 May 2020, which measures cumulative national epidemic attack rate and time since epidemic seeding rather than infection fatality rate (IFR) or case fatality rate (CFR). Second, no formal statistical test of association is performed. Third, visual inspection of the authors' own Nextstrain map inset in Fig. 1B shows that Germany's pie chart is majority 614G (orange), directly contradicting the statement in line 47 that "Germany and Kuwait, with a lower death toll, constitute most strains with the wild-type 614D at S."

* **The S-D614G substitution may increase clinical virulence and disease severity in infected individuals:** **[Overstated].**  
  An ecological comparison of national population mortality rates and GISAID sequence proportions suffers from severe ecological fallacy and phylogenetic founder confounding: clade G (carrying D614G alongside `ORF1b:P314L` / `nsp12:P323L`) founded the initial Northern Italian and broader Western European epidemic waves in late January / February 2020, where older demographics and overwhelmed hospitals drove early mortality before diagnostic testing was widespread. Inferring intrinsic viral virulence without individual-level patient outcome records adjusted for age, sex, comorbidities, and hospital load is invalid.

* **D614G induces conformational modifications that mimic the open RBD state and expose the cleavage domain to FURIN or TMPRSS2 to speed up proteolytic cleavage:** **[Fragile].**  
  Figure 2 simply highlights residue 614 in white on a static 3D surface rendering of the Spike protein without specifying the PDB accession code, modeling software, or energetics of the D-to-G substitution. No quantitative biophysical metric ($\Delta \Delta G$, hydrogen-bond disruption between protomers, or cleavage kinetics) is computed or cited to establish a mechanistic link to clinical lethality.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Unadjusted Country-Level Ecological Comparisons with Patient-Level Multivariable Regression
* **WHY:** Comparing national deaths per million inhabitants (`worldometers.info`) against country-level GISAID clade proportions confounds viral genotype with national epidemic onset date, cumulative incidence, testing denominator, and demographic age structure. Furthermore, "deaths per million population" is a function of how many people were infected in a country, not the probability of death given infection with 614G vs. 614D.
* **WHAT:** Test the association between D614G genotype and clinical severity at the individual patient level using GISAID patient metadata fields (`Patient status`, `Age`, `Gender`, `Collection date`, `Location`), or at minimum adjust ecological regressions for time since 10th death, diagnostic testing rate, and median population age.
* **HOW:** Extract all GISAID sequences up to May 2020 that include annotated clinical outcome metadata (e.g., hospitalized/deceased vs. asymptomatic/mild outpatient) and fit a mixed-effects logistic regression:
  $$\text{logit}(P(\text{Fatal/Severe}_{ij})) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \mathbb{I}(\text{614G}_{ij}) + \beta_2 \text{Age}_{ij} + \beta_3 \text{Sex}_{ij} + \beta_4 t_{\text{collection},ij} + u_{\text{country } j}$$
  reporting the adjusted odds ratio $\exp(\beta_1)$, 95% confidence interval, and $P$-value.

#### 2. Correct the Factual Contradiction Between the Text and Figure 1B Regarding Germany's 614G Frequency
* **WHY:** Line 47 states that `"Germany and Kuwait, with a lower death toll, constitute most strains with the wild-type 614D at S (Figure 1B)."` However, in the Nextstrain map of Europe shown in Figure 1B, the pie chart over Germany is predominantly orange (`614G`, $>65\%$), nearly identical to the pie charts over the Netherlands and Switzerland.
* **WHAT:** Tabulate the exact counts and percentages of 614D and 614G genomes in GISAID for every country plotted in Fig. 1B as of 2 May 2020, and correct the erroneous description of Germany.
* **HOW:** Download the GISAID metadata table behind the 4,246-sequence Nextstrain build, count $n_{614D}$ and $n_{614G}$ per country, provide this data as Table 1, and remove the false claim that most German strains in the May 2020 Nextstrain build were 614D.

#### 3. Account for Linkage Disequilibrium Across the Clade G Haplotype
* **WHY:** The D614G substitution in clade G co-segregates in near-complete linkage disequilibrium with three other defining mutations: `C241T` (5' UTR), `C3037T` (synonymous), and `C14408T` (`ORF1b:P314L` / `nsp12:P323L` in the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase). Attributing clade-level differences exclusively to `S:D614G` ignores linked mutations in the viral replication machinery.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state in the text that clade G is defined by a linked four-mutation haplotype including `nsp12:P323L`, and cannot be decoupled into an isolated `S:D614G` effect by clade-level tree coloring alone.
* **HOW:** Add a paragraph noting the linked `C241T` / `C3037T` / `C14408T` / `A23403G` haplotype and explaining why functional validation requires isogenic reverse-genetics or pseudovirus comparisons rather than clade-level epidemiology.

#### 4. Provide Reproducible Structural and Data Provenance Details
* **WHY:** Figure 1 is a composite of screenshots from two external websites (`nextstrain.org` and `worldometers.info`) with no underlying data table or GISAID acknowledgment table, and Figure 2 provides no PDB ID or structural modeling method.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * Specify the exact PDB accession number (e.g., `6VSB` or `6VYB`) and molecular graphics software used to generate Figure 2, and measure the inter-protomer distance between D614 and the neighboring protomer (e.g., T859 in S2) rather than relying on a qualitative surface cartoon.
  * Provide a supplementary CSV table listing the country-level sequence counts ($N$, $n_{\text{614D}}$, $n_{\text{614G}}$), confirmed cases, confirmed deaths, CFR, and deaths per million as of 2 May 2020.
