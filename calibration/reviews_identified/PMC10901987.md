# Review of PMC10901987

**Manuscript Title:** Explainable artificial intelligence for microbiome data analysis in colorectal cancer biomarker identification.  
**Authors:** Novielli P, Romano D, Magarelli M, Bitonto PD, Diacono D, Chiatante A, Lopalco G, Sabella D, Venerito V, Filannino P, Bellotti R, De Angelis M, Iannone F, Tangaro S.  
**Journal / Preprint:** Front Microbiol (2024) | DOI: 10.3389/fmicb.2024.1348974  
**Overall Score:** **4.6 / 10.0** (Flawed / Overstated)  
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 1.3/3.5 | Claims: 1.1/2.5 | Biology: 1.0/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.2/2.0

---

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Novielli et al. apply machine learning (Random Forest, XGBoost, and Support Vector Machine) and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) to 16S rRNA (V4 region) fecal microbiome genus-level profiles pooled from three published colorectal cancer (CRC) case-control cohorts (Zackular et al. 2014, $n=60$; Zeller et al. 2014, $n=91$; Baxter et al. 2016, $n=291$; total $N=442$: 191 CRC and 251 controls). After removing genera present in $<10\%$ of samples (reducing 462 features to 164), applying a centered log-ratio (CLR) transformation, and appending four demographic covariates (age, gender, BMI, and country), the authors evaluate models over 20 repetitions of 5-fold stratified cross-validation with nested 3-fold random hyperparameter search. Random Forest achieves the highest area under the Precision-Recall curve ($\text{AUPRC} = 0.668 \pm 0.016$, $\text{AUC ROC} = 0.699 \pm 0.011$, accuracy $= 0.673 \pm 0.015$, precision $= 0.729 \pm 0.038$, F1 $= 0.507 \pm 0.030$). SHAP analysis highlights known CRC-associated oral/gut genera (*Fusobacterium*, *Porphyromonas*, *Peptostreptococcus*, *Parvimonas*) alongside age, gender, and BMI among the top 20 predictors. Data and code are linked to Zenodo and a public GitHub repository (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`).

While the study uses nested cross-validation and open-source data/code, its core methodological execution and claims suffer from three major flaws: (1) there is no demographic-only or microbiome-only baseline model, making it impossible to tell how much of the modest $\text{AUC ROC} = 0.699$ is simply driven by the highly significant demographic imbalances between CRC cases and controls in age ($p \le 0.0001$), gender ($p < 0.01$), and BMI ($p \le 0.01$) rather than by the microbiome; (2) prevalence filtering ($<10\%$) was performed on the pooled 442-sample dataset prior to cross-validation, and pooling three independent studies without leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) validation conflates cohort/study effects with disease signal; and (3) the narrative repeatedly overstates a weak classifier ($\text{AUC ROC} = 0.699$, $\text{Recall} \approx 0.39$) as a "highly dependable prediction of CRC outcomes" and claims the "identification of new disease biomarkers" when the top features are well-known CRC-associated genera already established in the original 2014–2016 source papers. All of these issues can and should be resolved through straightforward computational re-analysis on the existing dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The Random Forest model provides a "highly dependable prediction of CRC outcomes" ($\text{AUC ROC} = 0.699$, $\text{AUPRC} = 0.668$, $\text{F1} = 0.507$): [Overstated].**  
  In Section 5 (Discussion), the authors describe their pipeline as "offering a highly dependable prediction of CRC outcomes." Quantitatively, Table 3 reports $\text{Accuracy} = 0.673 \pm 0.015$, $\text{Precision} = 0.729 \pm 0.038$, and $\text{F1} = 0.507 \pm 0.030$ for the RF model on a dataset where the majority (control) class alone accounts for $251/442 = 56.8\%$ of samples. From $\text{F1} = 2 \cdot \text{Prec} \cdot \text{Rec} / (\text{Prec} + \text{Rec}) = 0.507$ and $\text{Prec} = 0.729$, the implied sensitivity (Recall)—which the authors define in Section 3.3 as one of their core evaluation metrics but omit from Table 3—is $\text{Recall} \approx 0.389$. A screening classifier that misses over 60% of colorectal cancer cases ($\text{Recall} = 38.9\%$) and achieves an $\text{AUC ROC}$ of $0.699$ is far from a dependable diagnostic tool.

* **Gut microbiome features meaningfully drive CRC classification above and beyond demographic confounders (Age, Sex, BMI): [Fragile].**  
  Figure 1 and Table 2 show that CRC cases and controls differ strongly in Age ($p \le 0.0001$), Gender ($60.0\%$ male in CRC vs. $40.2\%$ male in controls, $p < 0.01$), and BMI ($p \le 0.01$). The authors include Age, Gender, BMI, and Country as input features alongside the 164 CLR-transformed bacterial genera, and Figure 4B shows that Age, Gender, and BMI all rank among the top 20 SHAP predictors. Because the authors never train a baseline classifier using *only* demographic variables (Age + Gender + BMI + Country) or *only* microbiome variables (the 164 genera), nor do they evaluate microbiome performance on age/sex/BMI-matched strata, it is unknown how much incremental predictive power ($\Delta \text{AUC ROC}$ or $\Delta \text{AUPRC}$) the 164 microbial genera add over a simple logistic regression on Age, Sex, and BMI.

* **SHAP analysis identifies *Fusobacterium*, *Porphyromonas*, *Peptostreptococcus*, and *Parvimonas* as positively associated with CRC and *Lachnospira* / *Eubacterium* as negatively associated: [Well Supported].**  
  The directional associations of oral pathobionts (*Fusobacterium*, *Porphyromonas*, *Peptostreptococcus*, *Parvimonas*) with CRC and butyrate-producing Clostridiales/Lachnospiraceae with healthy controls are consistent across RF, XGBoost, and SVM SHAP rankings (Table 4; Figure 4B; Figure 5) and replicate the original findings of Zackular et al. (2014), Zeller et al. (2014), and Baxter et al. (2016). However, the Conclusion's statement that this "suggest[s] the identification of new disease biomarkers" is inaccurate, as these exact genera were the primary biomarkers reported by the three source studies a decade ago. Furthermore, in Section 5 (line 317), the authors inexplicably write that "a high concentration of bacteria from the Lachnospiraceae family is associated with a lower likelihood of CRC. This spurious association has been observed in previous works..."—using the word "spurious" where they appear to mean "protective" or "consistent."

* **The preprocessing, normalization, and cross-validation pipeline is free of leakage and properly tuned for compositional data: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Several technical flaws affect the modeling pipeline:
  1. **Pre-CV feature filtering and zero-replacement unspecified:** Filtering out genera with $<10\%$ prevalence across all 442 samples was executed before splitting the data into cross-validation folds (Section 3.1 & 4.1). In addition, the Centered Log-Ratio (CLR) transformation ($\text{clr}(x_i) = \ln x_i - \frac{1}{D}\sum_{j=1}^D \ln x_j$) is mathematically undefined when any count $x_i = 0$ (which affects up to 90% of samples for retained genera), yet the manuscript never specifies how zeros were imputed or what pseudocount was added prior to taking logarithms.
  2. **SVM feature scaling and hyperparameter grid:** Unlike tree ensembles, RBF-kernel SVMs are highly sensitive to feature scale. Combining unscaled Age (in years, ~50–80), BMI (~20–35), binary Gender, categorical Country, and CLR-transformed taxa without within-fold $z$-score standardization (`StandardScaler`), while tuning only $\gamma \in \{0.001, 0.01, 1\}$ without tuning the regularization cost $C$, handicaps the SVM baseline.
  3. **Incorrect justification for AUPRC:** In Section 3.3 (line 206), the authors state that AUPRC is chosen for "an imbalanced dataset, where the number of positive cases is greater than the number of negative cases." In their dataset, positive cases (CRC, $n=191$) are *fewer* than negative cases (controls, $n=251$), and AUPRC is specifically sensitive to the minority positive class when positives are rare, not when positives outnumber negatives.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Add Ablation Baselines Separating Demographic Confounders from Microbiome Signal
* **WHY:** Because CRC patients in the pooled cohort are significantly older, more often male, and have higher BMI than controls (Figure 1, Table 2), a model combining demographics and microbiome features cannot demonstrate the diagnostic value of the gut microbiota without comparing against a demographics-only baseline.
* **WHAT:** Train and evaluate identical RF, XGBoost, and SVM pipelines on three feature sets: (1) **Demographics only** (Age, Gender, BMI, Country), (2) **Microbiome only** (the 164 CLR-transformed genera), and (3) **Combined** (Demographics + Microbiome).
* **HOW:** Run the existing 20-repeated 5-fold nested CV script separately on the 4 metadata columns and on the 164 taxonomic columns, add the resulting rows to Table 3 (including the missing Recall column), and perform a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test across CV folds to test whether adding the 164 microbial genera significantly improves $\text{AUC ROC}$ and $\text{AUPRC}$ over demographics alone.

#### 2. Perform Leave-One-Dataset-Out (LODO) External Validation Across the Three Studies
* **WHY:** Pooling 442 samples from three studies (Baxter, Zackular, Zeller) during 5-fold cross-validation mixes DNA extraction protocols, geographic cohorts, and sequencing batches across training and test sets. Moreover, Table 2 shows that "Country" was included as a feature, where France ($41\text{ CRC} / 50\text{ Control}$) corresponds entirely to Zeller et al. (2014).
* **WHAT:** Evaluate cross-study generalizability by training on two cohorts and testing on the held-out third cohort (Leave-One-Dataset-Out validation).
* **HOW:** Partition the 442 samples by source study (`Baxter`, `Zackular`, `Zeller`), fit the prevalence filter ($\ge 10\%$), zero-imputation/CLR transformation, and hyperparameter tuning strictly within the training studies, and report test-set $\text{AUC ROC}$, $\text{AUPRC}$, Precision, Recall, and F1 on each held-out study.

#### 3. Move Feature Filtering Inside Cross-Validation, Document Zero Handling in CLR, and Standardize SVM Inputs
* **WHY:** Applying the 10% prevalence filter on the full dataset before CV leaks feature-selection information, omitting the zero-replacement rule makes the CLR step irreproducible, and running SVM on unscaled features with a 3-point grid is an unfair baseline comparison.
* **WHAT:** Encapsulate prevalence filtering, zero replacement + CLR transformation, one-hot encoding of `Country`, and `StandardScaler` inside a `scikit-learn` `Pipeline` fit strictly on each training fold, and include `C` in the SVM hyperparameter grid.
* **HOW:**
  1. Explicitly state in Section 3.1 the pseudocount or multiplicative replacement method used to handle zero counts before computing $\text{clr}(x)$.
  2. Use `sklearn.pipeline.Pipeline` so that the $<10\%$ prevalence filter and `StandardScaler` (for SVM) are computed only on the 4 training folds in each split.
  3. Expand the SVM grid in Section 3.2.3 to include `C` $\in \{0.1, 1, 10, 100\}$ alongside `gamma`, and note that with only 15–45 total hyperparameter combinations in RF/XGBoost/SVM, `GridSearchCV` should be used instead of `RandomizedSearchCV`.

#### 4. Temper Diagnostic Claims and Fix Mathematical/Editorial Errors
* **WHY:** Claiming "highly dependable prediction of CRC outcomes" and "new disease biomarkers" is unsupported by an $\text{AUC ROC}$ of $0.699$ (Recall $\approx 0.39$) and rediscovery of well-known CRC taxa, and several equations and text passages contain typographical errors.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Add Recall to Table 3 and temper text:** Add the `Recall` column to Table 3 (since Recall is defined in Section 3.3 and required to interpret Precision and F1), and revise Section 5 ("offering a highly dependable prediction of CRC outcomes") and Section 6 ("suggesting the identification of new disease biomarkers") to state that the model achieves *moderate* classification performance ($\text{AUC ROC} = 0.70$) and *confirms previously established* CRC-associated genera (*Fusobacterium*, *Parvimonas*, *Porphyromonas*, *Peptostreptococcus*).
  2. **Fix Equation (1) notation (Section 3.4, line 215–219):** Replace the slash notation `/F/!` and `/S/!` with standard cardinality bars $|F|!$ and $|S|!$, write $F \cup \{j\}$ instead of $F \cup j$, and fix the definition on line 219 from "$S - (F \subseteq \{j\})$" to "$S \setminus (F \cup \{j\})$".
  3. **Fix AUPRC explanation (Section 3.3, line 206):** Change "where the number of positive cases is greater than the number of negative cases" to "where the classes are imbalanced and performance on the minority positive (CRC) class is prioritized."
  4. **Fix wording in Discussion (Section 5, line 317):** Replace "This spurious association has been observed in previous works" with "This protective (inverse) association has been observed in previous works."
