### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Rambaut et al. propose a dynamic, hierarchical phylogenetic nomenclature system (implemented in the `pangolin` software tool and `cov-lineages.org`) to classify global genetic diversity of SARS-CoV-2 and track actively transmitting viral lineages. Applying their framework to 27,767 near-complete SARS-CoV-2 genomes ($\ge 29,000$ bp, $\ge 95\%$ reference coverage) downloaded from GISAID on 18 May 2020 and aligned with MAFFT for maximum-likelihood phylogenetic inference in IQ-TREE 2 under a $\text{GTR}+\Gamma$ model, the authors partition the pandemic root into two major lineages (A and B, distinguished at nucleotide positions 8782 and 28144) and define descendant sub-lineages using four operational criteria: (a) $\ge 1$ shared nucleotide difference from the ancestor, (b) $\ge 5$ genomes with $>95\%$ coverage, (c) $\ge 1$ shared internal nucleotide change indicating onward transmission, and (d) $>70\%$ ultrafast bootstrap support, alongside epidemiological evidence of geographic spread. To prevent unbounded label length, hierarchies are capped at three numeric sublevels (e.g., `A.1.1.1`) before aliasing to a new alphabetical prefix (`C.1`), and lineages are dynamically categorized as `active` ($<1$ month since last observation), `unobserved` (1–3 months), or `inactive` ($>3$ months). Source code, curated trees, and GISAID sequence acknowledgements are publicly available.

This manuscript provides a practical, well-timed, and biologically grounded classification framework that avoids premature claims of phenotypic differences while accommodating coronavirus recombination and rapid lineage turnover. Its primary limitations are minor mathematical and operational inconsistencies in the formal decision rules—specifically, requiring $>70\%$ bootstrap support for branches defined by a single nucleotide substitution (where theoretical multinomial bootstrap support is $\sim 63\%$, forcing ad hoc manual overrides), ambiguous phrasing between criteria (a) and (c), unaddressed submission lags in the 1-month activity window, and arithmetic contradictions in the main text's lineage counts. All of these issues can be resolved directly through textual clarification and minor refinements to the rule definitions.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **A capped hierarchical aliasing system with activity states (`active`, `unobserved`, `inactive`) keeps SARS-CoV-2 lineage nomenclature tractable at pandemic scale: [Well Supported].**  
  Restricting numeric depth to three sublevels before assigning a new letter prefix and retiring unobserved lineages after 1–3 months bounds both label complexity and the number of active identifiers in circulation while preserving full phylogenetic ancestry via lookup tables.

* **Early SARS-CoV-2 diversity partitions at the root into lineage A (sharing ancestral nucleotides 8782 and 28144 with bat sarbecoviruses RaTG13 and RmYN02) and lineage B (including Wuhan-Hu-1): [Well Supported].**  
  The evolutionary rationale for rooting the nomenclature at lineages A and B is clearly supported by outgroup comparison at positions 8782 (ORF1ab) and 28144 (ORF8), and the authors appropriately note that earlier sampling of lineage B sequences (26 December 2019 vs. 5 January 2020 for lineage A) reflects ascertainment timing rather than evolutionary polarity.

* **New descendant lineages can be consistently delineated by requiring both $\ge 1$ defining nucleotide substitution and $>70\%$ bootstrap support: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In a 29,903-nt alignment with low sequence divergence, a clade defined by a single uncontradicted nucleotide substitution (criterion a) is resampled in $1 - (1 - 1/L)^L \approx 1 - e^{-1} \approx 63.2\%$ of standard bootstrap replicates. Consequently, a single-mutation branch systematically falls below the $>70\%$ bootstrap cutoff (criterion d), explaining why the authors had to manually relax the bootstrap threshold for major epidemiological clades such as B.1 and B.2. In addition, ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot) values in IQ-TREE 2 behave differently from standard non-parametric bootstrap proportions (where UFBoot $\ge 95\%$ corresponds to roughly $p \ge 0.95$) and suffer from polytomy-induced instability in low-diversity SARS-CoV-2 trees.

* **Application of the naming rules to the 18 May 2020 GISAID snapshot yields 81 lineages across A, B, and B.1: [Fragile].**  
  While the tree topology and major lineage summaries in Table 1 are clear, the lineage totals reported in the text are internally inconsistent: the text states that "81 viral lineages" were identified overall, yet the immediately following sentences report 6 lineages from A, 2 sub-lineages of A.1, 16 lineages from B, "$>70$ sub-lineages" of B.1, and 6 sub-lineages of B.2, which sum to $>100$ lineages.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reconcile the Single-Substitution Rule (Criterion a) with the Bootstrap Cutoff (Criterion d)
* **WHY:** Because a single-SNP synapomorphy across a ~30 kb genome has a theoretical bootstrap inclusion probability of $1 - e^{-1} \approx 63.2\%$, requiring $>70\%$ bootstrap support (criterion d) contradicts criterion (a) whenever a lineage is defined by a single mutation, necessitating subjective manual exceptions.
* **WHAT:** Replace or calibrate the $>70\%$ UFBoot requirement with a criterion appropriate for low-divergence viral phylogenies.
* **HOW:** Clarify in the "Lineage naming rules" section whether criterion (d) uses standard non-parametric bootstrap or IQ-TREE 2 ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot), note that branches supported by a single homoplasy-free nucleotide change have a theoretical bootstrap ceiling of ~63%, and formalize the rule as requiring *either* $\ge 1$ low-homoplasy apomorphic mutation with high site concordance factor ($\text{sCF}$) *or* UFBoot $>70\%$ when multiple substitutions or homoplasies are present.

#### 2. Disambiguate Criterion (a) vs. Criterion (c) in the Lineage Definition
* **WHY:** Criterion (a) states that a new lineage *"exhibits one or more shared nucleotide differences from the ancestral lineage"*, while criterion (c) states that *"genomes within the lineage exhibit at least one shared nucleotide change among them"*. As written, "genomes within the lineage exhibit at least one shared nucleotide change among them" can be read as synonymous with sharing a mutation relative to the ancestor (criterion a), rather than requiring an *internal* sub-branch shared by a subset of members within the proposed lineage.
* **WHAT:** Rewrite criterion (c) so its topological meaning is unambiguous.
* **HOW:** Revise criterion (c) to read: *"at least a subset ($\ge 2$) of genomes within the candidate lineage share at least one additional internal nucleotide substitution derived after the lineage-defining node, demonstrating internal branching and ongoing transmission rather than a single point-source polytomy."*

#### 3. Specify Collection Date vs. Submission Date and Account for Surveillance Bias in Activity Labels
* **WHY:** Defining lineages as `active` ($<1$ month), `unobserved` (1–3 months), or `inactive` ($>3$ months) without specifying whether the timestamp is sample collection date or GISAID deposition date creates ambiguity, as sequencing and upload delays frequently exceed 30 days. Similarly, requiring $\ge 5$ genomes with $>95\%$ coverage means lineage designation and activity status are strongly confounded by national sequencing volume.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly define the reference date for activity status and report the activity classification for the lineages in Table 1.
* **HOW:** State in "Principles of a dynamic nomenclature system" that activity windows are calculated from the most recent *sample collection date* relative to the database freeze date (with a caveat for typical reporting lags in under-sampled regions), and add an `Activity Status (as of 18 May 2020)` column to Table 1 (noting, for example, that A.1 [last sampled Mar-25] and B.4 [last sampled Apr-14] cross the 1-month `unobserved` threshold relative to the 18 May freeze).

#### 4. Clarify Paraphyletic vs. Monophyletic Genome Counts in Table 1
* **WHY:** In Table 1, the genome counts across the 11 listed major lineages sum to 19,307 out of 27,767 total genomes, and it is unclear whether the count for a parent lineage (e.g., `B.1`: 7,438 genomes) includes or excludes genomes assigned to its child lineages (e.g., `B.1.1`: 6,286 genomes).
* **WHAT:** State explicitly in the Table 1 caption whether genome counts and date ranges are cumulative (clade-wide) or restricted to sequences assigned directly to that basal lineage label.
* **HOW:** Add a footnote to Table 1 specifying both the direct (basal) assignment count and the total clade count (including all descendant sub-lineages) for A, B, B.1, and B.2.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** The lineage count summary in the main text contains an arithmetic contradiction and a lineage label typo.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Lineage count contradiction (Lineage naming rules, paragraph 5):** The text states *"Using this scheme we identified 81 viral lineages"* but then lists 2 root lineages (A, B) + 6 from A + 2 from A.1 + 16 from B + $>70$ from B.1 + 6 from B.2 ($>102$ total). Reconcile the total number of lineages (81 vs. $>102$) with the exact count in the 18 May 2020 release on `cov-lineages.org`.
  2. **Typo in A.1 sub-lineage label (Lineage naming rules, paragraph 5):** The text states *"We identified six lineages derived from lineage A (denoted A.1-A.6) and two descendant sub-lineages of A.1 (A.1.1 and A.3)."* `A.3` is a first-level descendant of `A` (listed in Table 1), not a sub-lineage of `A.1` (which should be `A.1.2` or `A.1.3`). Correct the label of the second A.1 sub-lineage.
