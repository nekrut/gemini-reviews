### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Lippert et al. evaluate whether whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data can be linked to physical and demographic profiles in an ethnically diverse convenience cohort of 1,061 adults from San Diego sequenced at $>30\times$ depth. Using 10-fold cross-validation (CV), the authors build ridge regression and shrinkage models to predict 3D facial shape and texture principal components (PCs), 100-dimensional voice embeddings and pitch, somatic age (from telomere repeat read fraction and mosaic X/Y chromosome loss), height, weight, BMI, and continuous RGB eye and skin color, alongside inverse models predicting age, sex, and genetic ancestry from facial images and voice recordings. They then train a maximum-entropy-weighted similarity metric over the joint genome–phenotype embeddings to evaluate candidate ranking ("select at $N$," $s_N$) and 1-to-1 bipartite graph assignment ("match at $N$," $m_N$). In small pools of size $N=10$, the full model achieves $s_{10}=74\%$ and $m_{10}=83\%$ across the mixed cohort, dropping to $s_{10}=44\%–50\%$ and $m_{10}=47\%–57\%$ when stratified into African American or European subsets. Genomic data are placed under managed access, and no source code or trained model weights are publicly deposited.

The manuscript provides a sobering empirical demonstration that polygenic prediction of complex biometric traits beyond sex and ancestry remains very weak ($R_{CV}^2 = 0.06–0.08$ for height SNPs alone; zero genome-wide significant hits for 36 facial landmark distances; zero gain from known craniofacial SNPs in Figure 3; and only 3 of 100 voice identity dimensions exceeding $R_{CV}^2 = 0.10$). However, the title, abstract, and policy framing substantially overstate individualized "trait prediction" from WGS by conflating individual-level biometric prediction with coarse demographic filtering (sex, continental and sub-continental admixture proportions, pigmentation, and somatic blood-derived age) in tiny lineups ($N=10$) under closed-world bipartite matching. These limitations can be addressed through computational re-analysis by adding a strict demographic/ancestry/pigmentation baseline, controlling for fine-scale admixture and relatedness within self-reported ethnic strata, and reframing the claims around demographic/ancestry linkage rather than individualized craniofacial or voice reconstruction.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Known craniofacial SNPs and polygenic biometric predictors explain minimal phenotypic variance beyond sex, age, and genomic ancestry: [Well Supported].**  
  The internal cross-validation experiments in Figures 3 and 5B demonstrate that previously published facial SNPs add no predictive power over sex and 1,000 genomic PCs, a GWAS of 36 facial landmark distances yields no significant loci after multiple-testing correction, height SNPs alone explain only $R_{CV}^2 = 0.06$ (males) and $0.08$ (females), and 97 of 100 voice identity-vector dimensions have $R_{CV}^2 \le 0.10$.

* **Continuous RGB eye and skin pigmentation and biological sex are accurately predicted from WGS data in a multi-ethnic cohort: [Well Supported].**  
  Combining three genomic PCs with known pigmentation loci yields cross-validated $R_{CV}^2$ values of 0.77–0.82 across RGB channels for eye and skin color (Figure 5C–D), reflecting both major-effect pigmentation alleles and between-population allele frequency differences across the cohort's ancestry groups.

* **Somatic telomere length and sex-chromosome mosaicism in blood WGS predict chronological age with $R_{CV}^2 = 0.44$: [Fragile].**  
  While combining telomere repeat read fraction and X/Y copy-number loss yields $R_{CV}^2 = 0.44$ ($\text{MAE} = 8.0$ years) in the 1,061-person cohort (Figure 5A), the authors note that telomere length alone gave $R_{CV}^2 = 0.29$ here compared to $R^2 = 0.05$ in a 1,960-female replication cohort sequenced on the exact same pipeline. A nearly 6-fold discrepancy in variance explained by telomere read counts between cohorts on the same platform strongly suggests technical batch confounding (e.g., correlation between participant age and sequencing batch, library preparation lot, or DNA extraction conditions) that was not controlled for.

* **Trait prediction from WGS enables reidentification of $>8$ of 10 individuals in a mixed cohort and 5 of 10 within European or African American cohorts: [Overstated].**  
  First, the headline figures in the Abstract refer to $m_{10}$ (83% overall, 47% AFR, 57% EUR) under a closed-world 1-to-1 bipartite matching formulation where the algorithm knows a priori that the 10 genomes correspond to the exact 10 phenotypic profiles in the pool, allowing elimination of candidates by mutual exclusion. Second, a pool size of $N=10$ (where random guessing achieves 10%, and binary sex plus coarse age and skin tone immediately narrow candidates to 1–2 individuals) does not reflect realistic database reidentification ($N \sim 10^3–10^6$). Third, the "3D face" embedding in Materials and Methods explicitly bundles image-derived predictions of sex, age, skin color, and 5-way ancestry (`AFR, EUR, EAS, AMR, CSA`) alongside facial PCs. Even within the self-reported `AFR` ($n=569$) and `EUR` ($n=273$) strata, Figure 1B shows substantial continuous variation in continental admixture proportions, meaning that within-stratum matching is still heavily driven by sex, somatic age, continuous admixture proportions, and eye/skin shade rather than individualized facial morphology.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Isolate Individualized Biometric Prediction from Demographic, Admixture, and Pigmentation Matching
* **WHY:** Because the "3D face" and "Full" embeddings incorporate sex, somatic age, continuous 5-way ancestry proportions, and RGB skin/eye color, the manuscript never tests whether predicted 3D craniofacial geometry, voice identity vectors, or polygenic height/weight/BMI provide any statistically significant reidentification lift beyond basic demographic and pigmentation variables.
* **WHAT:** Add a strict baseline model to the $s_N$ and $m_N$ analyses that uses only sex, predicted somatic age, continuous genomic ancestry proportions (`AFR, EUR, EAS, AMR, CSA`), and RGB eye/skin color, and test the marginal gain of adding 3D facial shape residuals, voice identity vectors, and anthropometric traits.
* **HOW:** Residualize the 3D facial shape PCs and 100 voice identity-vector dimensions on sex, predicted age, and the 5 admixture proportions within each training fold. Re-compute $s_{10}$ and $m_{10}$ across all strata (`All`, `m`, `f`, `AFR`, `EUR`, and `sex × ethnicity`) for: (i) `Demographics + Admixture + Pigmentation only`, (ii) `Residualized 3D Face Shape only`, and (iii) `Full Model`, reporting paired bootstrap 95% confidence intervals for the difference in accuracy.

#### 2. Prevent Leakage and Over-Parameterization in the 1,000-PC and Two-Stage CV Pipeline
* **WHY:** The cohort contains $N=1,061$ individuals (~955 per 9/10 training fold), yet the ridge regression models include $K=1,000$ genomic PCs—meaning $K$ exceeds the rank of the training genetic relatedness matrix—and Materials and Methods does not specify whether PCA and the maximum-entropy similarity weights $w_d$ (Equation 1) were fit using nested out-of-fold predictions. If $w_d$ is trained on the same 9 folds used to fit the ridge regressions, in-sample shrinkage residuals $|\psi_P(p)_d - \phi_P(g)_d|$ will be underestimated relative to test folds. Furthermore, cryptic relatedness in a local convenience cohort can inflate cross-validated matching if relatives span train and test splits.
* **WHAT:** Audit and enforce strict nested cross-validation, filter cryptic relatives, and justify the number of genomic PCs.
* **HOW:** (i) Compute pairwise KING kinship coefficients across all 1,061 genomes and either exclude one member of any pair with $\text{kinship} > 0.044$ (3rd-degree relatives) or assign entire families to the same CV fold; (ii) compute facial PCA, genomic PCA (e.g., top 20–50 PCs rather than 1,000), and ridge regression weights strictly on the training folds; and (iii) learn the maximum-entropy weights $w_d$ using out-of-fold inner-CV predictions so that $\phi_P(g)$ has identical error variance during weight learning and evaluation.

#### 3. Investigate Batch Confounding in Telomere and Mosaic Sex-Chromosome Age Prediction
* **WHY:** Telomere read fraction explained $R_{CV}^2 = 0.29$ of age variance in the 1,061-person study cohort versus $R^2 = 0.05$ in 1,960 females sequenced on the same pipeline. In short-read WGS, telomere repeat counts and sex-chromosome depth ratios are highly sensitive to DNA extraction kit lots, shearing, PCR-free vs. PCR library protocols, and sequencing flowcell batches.
* **WHAT:** Test and adjust for sequencing batch and library preparation covariates in the somatic age model.
* **HOW:** Regress estimated telomere length and X/Y coverage ratios on sequencing run ID, instrument ID, library preparation date, and mean autosome coverage depth. Re-evaluate $R_{CV}^2$ for age prediction after batch adjustment and when training the model on the 1,960-female cohort and testing on the 1,061-person cohort.

#### 4. Evaluate Open-World Scaling ($s_N$ for $N \ge 1,000$) and Predicted-Covariate Facial Accuracy
* **WHY:** Figures 3 and 4 evaluate facial shape $R_{CV}^2$ using *observed* age and BMI (`Sex + Age + BMI + Anc + SNPs`), whereas the actual reidentification pipeline must use *genomically predicted* age ($R_{CV}^2=0.44$) and BMI ($R_{CV}^2=0.17$). In addition, reporting $m_{10}$ and $s_{10}$ obscures how rapidly identification degrades at realistic database scales.
* **WHAT:** Show per-pixel facial $R_{CV}^2$ using genomically predicted covariates, and report $s_N$ across the full cohort ($N=1,061$) and simulated larger pools.
* **HOW:** Add a violin column to Figure 3 showing `Sex + Pred_Age + Pred_BMI + Anc + SNPs`. In the main text and Table 2, report top-1 and top-10 identification accuracy ($s_N$) when searching against the entire test fold ($N \approx 106$) and across the full cohort ($N=1,061$ via out-of-fold embeddings), alongside the stratified `sex × ethnicity` numbers currently relegated to SI Appendix Figure S31.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Equation 1 has a sign/definition mismatch, and key performance metrics and code are missing from the main text.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Sign error in Equation 1 (Materials and Methods):** Equation 1 defines "similarity" as $\delta_P(\psi_P(p), \phi_P(g)) = \sum_{d=1}^D w_d |\psi_P(p)_d - \phi_P(g)_d|$. Because $|\psi_P(p)_d - \phi_P(g)_d|$ is an $L_1$ distance (non-negative and increasing with mismatch), $\delta_P$ is a *dissimilarity* unless negated ($\delta_P = -\sum_{d=1}^D w_d |\psi_P(p)_d - \phi_P(g)_d|$) or constrained to $w_d \le 0$. Explicitly state the sign convention and provide the exact maximum-entropy likelihood objective used to optimize $w_d$.
  2. **Public code and model specification:** Deposit the scripts implementing the maximum-entropy metric learner, bipartite matching ($m_N$), and ridge regression pipelines in a public repository (GitHub/Zenodo).
