# Review of PMC7395502

**Manuscript Title:** BCG vaccine protection from severe coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19).  
**Authors:** Luis E Escobar, Alvaro Molina-Cruz, Carolina Barillas-Mury  
**Journal / Preprint:** Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (2020) | DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2008410117  
**Overall Score:** **4.8 / 10.0** (Flawed / Overstated)  
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 1.2/3.5 | Claims: 1.2/2.5 | Biology: 1.0/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.4/2.0

---

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Escobar et al. perform a cross-national ecological analysis of COVID-19 mortality (collected through April 22, 2020) against national Bacillus Calmette–Guérin (BCG) vaccination policies (current, interrupted, or never implemented) and historical vaccination coverage (1980–2018). Attempting to mitigate country-level confounding, the authors filter the global dataset down to 22 "socially similar" countries ($\ge 1$ death/million, $\ge 15\%$ population aged $\ge 65$, $>60\%$ urban, $<300$ inhabitants/$\text{km}^2$, and Human Development Index $> 0.7$), compare 5 US states against 5 Mexican and Brazilian states over the first 25 days after each state's first reported death, compare former East and West German states, and regress first-month COVID-19 mortality against a composite "BCG index" across a subset of European countries ($r^2 = 0.88, P = 8 \times 10^{-7}$). Raw country and state datasets are provided in `Dataset S1`.

Unlike cruder ecological studies, the authors recognize that HDI, urbanization, and age structure strongly confound global COVID-19 mortality ($r^2 = 0.49$ for HDI and density alone) and include explicit caveats in the Abstract and Discussion that coarse ecological signals cannot establish causality without randomized clinical trials. Nevertheless, the study's statistical inferences and title ("BCG vaccine protection from severe coronavirus disease 2019") are compromised by four methodological flaws: (1) post-hoc exclusion of the United Kingdom and France—two high-mortality Western European nations with decades of universal BCG vaccination—from the flagship European regression; (2) severe longitudinal pseudoreplication in the Americas analysis ($t[237] = 14.274$ across only 10 states); (3) unadjusted spatial autocorrelation and calendar-time lockdown confounding between Western and Eastern Europe; and (4) selective highlighting of the 19–37% of filtered comparisons that remained nominally significant without multiple-testing correction. Re-analyzing the data with proper degrees of freedom, retaining all countries meeting the pre-specified inclusion criteria (including the UK and France), and controlling for calendar date of epidemic onset and non-pharmaceutical interventions is required.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Human Development Index (HDI), urbanization, and proportion of population aged $\ge 65$ are strongly and positively correlated with early reported COVID-19 mortality globally, eliminating 63–81% of crude BCG associations upon filtering: [Well Supported].**  
  In the Refined Analysis (line 86 and `SI Appendix, Fig. S1, S4`), the authors demonstrate that international travel connectivity, older demographics, and higher HDI/urbanization drive early pandemic mortality, and candidly report that filtering to 22 socially similar countries reduces the proportion of nominally significant BCG linear models from 46% to 19% (and $t$-tests from 92% to 37%).

* **In socially similar European countries, the BCG index explains 88% of the variance in first-month COVID-19 mortality ($r^2 = 0.88, P = 8 \times 10^{-7}$; Fig. 4E), with each 10% increase in BCG index linked to a 10.4% mortality reduction: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To achieve this headline correlation (featured in the Abstract and Figure 4D–E), the authors excluded both the United Kingdom and France after inspecting their high COVID-19 mortality, rationalizing that the UK vaccinated 12- to 13-year-olds and France vaccinated infants and school-age children rather than exclusively newborns (line 110). Because the paper's own East vs. West Germany analysis (line 61 and Fig. 3B) argues that vaccinating 15-year-old teenagers in East Germany *conferred* protection, excluding the UK and France for vaccinating children/adolescents is internally inconsistent post-hoc data dredging that removes two major counterexamples to the hypothesis. Furthermore, within the remaining small subset of European countries, the BCG index is strongly colinear with the West-to-East geographic gradient: Eastern European countries recorded their first COVID-19 deaths later in March 2020 and enacted national lockdowns *before* widespread community transmission occurred, whereas Italy, Belgium, and the Netherlands experienced weeks of unmitigated exponential growth prior to their first deaths and lockdowns.

* **US states without BCG vaccination had significantly higher 25-day mortality than main entry-point states in Mexico and Brazil ($t[237] = 14.274, P < 0.001, r^2 = 0.84$; Fig. 3A): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This comparison evaluates only $N = 5$ US states (NY, IL, LA, AL, FL) against $N = 5$ Latin American states (Pernambuco, Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, Mexico State, Mexico City). A two-group comparison of 10 states has at most $10 - 2 = 8$ degrees of freedom. Reporting $t[237] = 14.274$ indicates that the authors pooled 25 serial daily cumulative mortality values per state ($\sim 250$ daily points) as independent observations in a pooled $t$-test/regression, committing severe longitudinal pseudoreplication that inflates the test statistic and degrees of freedom by an order of magnitude. It also ignores vast differences in age structure ($\ge 65$ population share in Brazil/Mexico vs. Florida/US) and diagnostic death registration in April 2020.

* **Lower COVID-19 mortality in former East German states (14.2 per million) compared to West German states (40.5 per million; $t[11] = -2.592, P = 0.025$; Fig. 3D) reflects broader BCG vaccination coverage of older cohorts in East Germany: [Fragile].**  
  As the authors partially note in the Discussion (line 127), West Germany has nearly double the population density of East Germany ($282/\text{km}^2$ vs. $154/\text{km}^2$), far greater international air connectivity, and direct proximity to early Alpine/Austrian/Italian superspreading hubs that seeded Western German outbreaks in February 2020. When Germany enacted a nationwide lockdown in mid-March 2020, eastern German states were at a much earlier epidemic stage on the calendar timeline; comparing cumulative mortality on April 22, 2020 without adjusting for calendar date of introduction or state-level population density confounds BCG history with spatial epidemic seeding.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Restore Excluded European Countries (UK and France) and Control for Calendar Date of Lockdowns
* **WHY:** Dropping the UK and France from Figure 4D–E based on childhood vaccination age contradicts the East Germany teenager vaccination argument and artificially inflates $r^2$ to 0.88 by removing high-mortality, BCG-vaccinated Western European nations. Moreover, "30 days since first death" does not control for calendar-time spillover of lockdowns from early-hit Italy/Spain to later-hit Eastern Europe.
* **WHAT:** Re-compute the European BCG index regressions (Figure 4D and 4E) including the UK, France, and all European countries meeting the objective demographic filters, and add calendar date of first death (or days between first death and national lockdown) and population density as covariates.
* **HOW:** Calculate the BCG index for the UK and France using the exact formula in Figure 4C, re-run the linear regression in R (`lm(deaths_30d ~ BCG_index + date_first_death + pop_density)`), and report the unedited $r^2$ and $P$-value in the Abstract and Figure 4.

#### 2. Correct Longitudinal Pseudoreplication in the Americas and US State Analyses
* **WHY:** Treating 25 daily cumulative mortality counts across 10 states as 250 independent observations ($t[237] = 14.274$) violates independence of repeated measures and invalidates the $p$-value. Similarly, plotting 50 US states alongside sovereign countries in Figures 1B and 2A treats sub-national units of a single country as 50 independent national policies.
* **WHAT:** Analyze each state as a single observational unit ($N = 10$ for Figure 3A) or fit a linear mixed-effects model with a random intercept and slope for state, and keep country-level analyses strictly at the national level ($N = 1$ for the US).
* **HOW:** For Figure 3A, run a two-sample Welch $t$-test (or Wilcoxon rank-sum test) strictly on the single day-25 mortality value per state ($N_1 = 5, N_2 = 5$, $\text{df} \le 8$), or fit `lme4::lmer(mortality ~ day * BCG_group + (1 + day | state))` in R, and update the reported $t$-statistic, degrees of freedom, and $P$-value.

#### 3. Apply Multiple-Testing Correction Across the Grid of Exploratory Models in Tables S2–S4
* **WHY:** `SI Appendix, Tables S2–S4` evaluate dozens of combinations of dependent variables (mean, median, max deaths at 21 d, 30 d, 0.1/million, 1/million, midpoint, full period) and independent variables. In the refined analysis, 63% to 81% of tests are null ($P > 0.05$), meaning that several of the remaining 19–37% significant tests are expected false positives under multiple testing.
* **WHAT:** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) adjustment across all tests in `Tables S2–S4` and report the proportion of refined models that survive at $Q < 0.05$.
* **HOW:** Pass the vector of all refined-analysis $p$-values across `Tables S2–S4` to `p.adjust(..., method = "BH")` in R, add an FDR column to `Tables S2–S4`, and report the FDR-adjusted results in the Filtered Analysis section (line 86).

#### 4. Temper the Title to Reflect an Ecological Association Rather Than Proven Clinical Protection
* **WHY:** While the body text carefully notes that clinical trials are required to establish causality, the title *"BCG vaccine protection from severe coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)"* states a causal protective effect as an established fact.
* **WHAT:** Revise the title to accurately describe an ecological correlation exploration.
* **HOW:** Change the title to *"Ecological association between national BCG vaccination policy and early COVID-19 mortality"* and update the Abstract to report the European correlation with the UK and France included.
