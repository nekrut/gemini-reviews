### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Neher analyzes 12 million SARS-CoV-2 genomes from GISAID (accessed 25 July 2022; EPI_SET_220812zf) and open INSDC/Nextstrain repositories to compare the rates of synonymous and non-synonymous evolution *within* 18 major Nextstrain clades (plus 3 composite clades) against the *inter-clade* ("backbone") evolutionary rate across the first 2.5 years of the pandemic. Using Nextclade quality filtering, manual curation of clade-founder genotypes, outlier pruning ($>2\sigma + 3$ mutations), and calendar-week weighted linear regressions (weighted by $N_{\text{week}}^{1/4}$) corroborated by an analytical supercritical branching-process Poisson mutation accumulation model (Equation 1), the study finds that: (1) synonymous substitution rates are stable both within clades ($\sim 5$–$8$ changes/genome/year) and between clade founders ($7.3$ changes/genome/year); (2) inter-clade non-synonymous evolution ($\sim 25$–$26$ amino acid changes/genome/year) is roughly 3- to 4-fold faster than intra-clade non-synonymous evolution; (3) intra-clade non-synonymous evolution decelerated from $12$–$16$ changes/year in early-2020 clades (19A, 19B) to $3$–$9$ changes/year in 2021–2022 clades (primarily driven by ORF1ab rather than Spike); and (4) counting minor non-singleton intra-lineage polymorphisms across Pango lineages normalized by baseline mutation spectra yields a high-resolution map of purifying selection across codon positions (1st/2nd vs. 3rd) and ORFs (`ORF1b` and `M` strongly constrained; `ORF3a`, `ORF6`, `ORF7a`, `ORF7b`, and `ORF8` weakly constrained). All Snakemake workflows, tabular outputs, and scripts are publicly available at `[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`.

This is a thoughtful, transparent, and quantitatively grounded study that replaces black-box phylogenetic subsampling with scalable, mathematically interpretable summary statistics across millions of genomes, while candidly discussing potential confounders (founder definition conditioning via composite clades `19B+`, `19B++`, `20A+`; early subclade sweeps violating Poisson independence; and cryptic diversity in Omicron `21L`/`22A`/`22B`). The primary methodological limitation is that `Table 1` and `Figure 3D` report point estimates of the weekly weighted root-to-tip regression slopes without formal uncertainty intervals (e.g., phylogenetic block-bootstrap or independent basal-sublineage bootstrap confidence intervals) to account for shared ancestry within clades, and the text contains a few minor LaTeX-to-XML conversion omissions and a unit typo in the Abstract ("or per year and codon"). Adding clade-level bootstrap confidence intervals and fixing the text omissions will make this work even stronger.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Synonymous evolutionary rates are invariant within and between SARS-CoV-2 clades ($\sim 6$–$7$ changes/genome/year), whereas inter-clade non-synonymous evolution ($\sim 25$–$26$ AA changes/year) is 3- to 4-fold faster than within-clade evolution: [Well Supported].**  
  Across all 18 non-composite Nextstrain clades (`Table 1`, `Figure 3C,D`), within-clade synonymous rates cluster tightly between $4.85$ and $8.39$ changes/year, matching the inter-clade founder regression slope of $7.3$ synonymous changes/year. In contrast, the inter-clade founder regression for amino acid substitutions is $\sim 25$–$26\text{ yr}^{-1}$ versus $2.81$–$12.79\text{ yr}^{-1}$ within clades (`Figure 3B,D`). Because synonymous mutations serve as an internal neutral control exposed to the exact same epidemiological, sampling, and sequencing error processes as non-synonymous mutations on the same genomes, the selective acceleration of non-synonymous changes along variant-defining stem branches is robustly established.

* **Within-clade non-synonymous rates were higher in early pandemic clades (19A, 19B: $\sim 12$–$13$ AA changes/year) than in later clades ($\sim 3$–$9$ AA changes/year), driven primarily by `ORF1ab` rather than `Spike`: [Moderately Supported].**  
  `Table 1`, `Figure 4`, and `Supplementary Figure S1` show that clades `19B` ($12.37\text{ yr}^{-1}$) and `19A` ($12.79\text{ yr}^{-1}$) had higher non-synonymous slopes than post-mid-2020 clades ($2.81$–$10.05\text{ yr}^{-1}$). The author carefully tests whether clade-splitting conditioning biases these slopes by analyzing composite clades (`19B+`, `19B++`, `20A+`) and notes in the Discussion that "Maybe it is just chance, after all it is only clades 19A and 19B where the rate is substantially higher." Because shared ancestry among sequences within a clade violates the independence of weekly bins (especially in early 2020 when global sequencing was sparse and dominated by a few regional transmission chains), and because `Table 1` does not report standard errors or bootstrap confidence intervals around the slopes, the statistical significance of the `19A`/`19B` vs. `20A`–`21J` difference remains somewhat uncertain.

* **Genome-wide mutational tolerance inferred from non-singleton intra-Pango-lineage variation demonstrates strong purifying selection in `ORF1b`, `M`, `S`, `E`, and `N` (~50% of 1st/2nd codon positions strongly constrained) and relaxed constraint in accessory genes (`ORF3a`, `ORF6`, `ORF7a`, `ORF7b`, `ORF8`): [Well Supported].**  
  By requiring mutations to appear in $\ge 2$ sequences within a Pango lineage (filtering out terminal singleton sequencing errors) and normalizing by the 12-rate nucleotide mutation spectrum away from the ancestral base (`Supplementary Fig. S2`), the mutational tolerance proxy cleanly separates 3rd codon positions from 1st/2nd codon positions (`Figure 5`), captures known conserved RNA structures (the `ORF1b` ribosomal frameshift pseudoknot and the central region of `E` around codon 35; `Figure 6`), and demonstrates near-neutral tolerance at 1st/2nd positions in `ORF6`, `ORF7a`, `ORF7b`, and `ORF8`.

* **Supercritical branching-process Poisson dynamics (Equation 1) accurately describe early intra-clade genotype frequency trajectories when evolution is quasi-neutral: [Well Supported].**  
  The analytical solution $m_i(t) \propto \frac{(ut)^i}{i!} e^{(\alpha - u)t}$ provides a clean, mechanistic baseline for the sequential replacement of the 0-mutation founder by 1-, 2-, and 3-mutation genotypes (`Figure 2F`), and deviations from this Poisson hierarchy (`Supplementary Fig. S21` for `21K` / `S:R346K`) diagnostic of intra-clade selective sweeps or basal demography are transparently documented.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Add Polytomy-Cluster / Sublineage Bootstrap Confidence Intervals to `Table 1` and `Figure 3D`
* **WHY:** As noted on line 58, sequences within a clade share phylogenetic ancestry along basal lineages, so standard OLS/WLS regression standard errors across calendar weeks are anti-conservative, and `Table 1` currently reports point estimates with no uncertainty bounds.
* **WHAT:** Compute 95% confidence intervals for the overall, amino-acid, and synonymous rate estimates in `Table 1` and `Figure 3D` by bootstrapping independent basal sublineages (or Pango sublineages / geographic regions) within each Nextstrain clade.
* **HOW:** Within each Nextstrain clade in the `SC2_variant_rates` Snakemake pipeline, group sequences by their basal polytomy descendant branch (or by country/Pango sublineage), resample these clusters with replacement 1,000 times, recompute the $N_{\text{week}}^{1/4}$-weighted regression slope on the first 6 months of each bootstrap replicate, and report the 2.5th–97.5th percentile intervals in `Table 1` and as error bars in `Figure 3D` and `Figure 4`.

#### 2. Quantify Sensitivity of Founder-Filtered Regressions to Reversion Exclusion and Window Length
* **WHY:** Filtering out any sequence that lacks the full set of clade-defining mutations (line 46) removes amplicon-dropout artifacts, but also censors genuine reversions to Wuhan-Hu-1 (which occur at a non-negligible rate in heavily mutated clades like Omicron `21K`/`21L`/`22A`/`22B` and bias divergence upward slightly if excluded, or downward if included). Furthermore, restricting regressions to a fixed 6-month window spans different epidemiological phases across clades.
* **HOW:** Add a supplementary sensitivity table comparing the `Table 1` slopes under (a) 4-month vs. 6-month vs. 8-month windows, and (b) allowing up to 1 or 2 missing founder mutations in sequences that otherwise carry derived clade-specific markers and have Nextclade QC score $= 0$.
