### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Poore et al. re-examine unmapped non-human sequencing reads across 18,116 whole-genome (WGS, $n = 4,831$) and whole-transcriptome (RNA-Seq, $n = 13,285$) samples from 10,481 patients spanning 33 cancer types in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), alongside a new 169-sample plasma cell-free DNA (cfDNA) cohort from UC San Diego (69 HIV-negative non-cancer controls, 59 prostate cancers, 25 lung cancers, and 16 melanomas). Using `Kraken` (31-mer matching against 59,974 microbial genomes from `RepoPhlan`) and `SHOGUN` (Bowtie2 alignment against the 10,575-genome `Web of Life` database), followed by `decontam` filtering, `Voom` log-cpm transformation, Supervised Normalization (`SNM`), and stochastic gradient boosting machine (`GBM`) classifiers, the authors report that tissue and whole-blood microbial profiles accurately discriminate almost all 33 TCGA cancer types (one-cancer-type-versus-all-others and tumor-versus-normal), including Stage Ia–IIc blood samples and tumors lacking somatic alterations on commercial ctDNA panels, and that plasma cfDNA microbial signatures distinguish healthy controls from prostate, lung, and melanoma patients. Summarized genus-level tables, ENA/Qiita raw plasma reads, and GitHub processing scripts (`biocore/tcga`) are provided.

While the scale of the computational effort and the recovery of known viral/bacterial positives (such as *Alphapapillomavirus* in CESC/HNSCC, *Orthohepadnavirus* in HBV-positive LIHC, *Lymphocryptovirus* in EBV-subtype STAD, and *Fusobacterium* in GI tumors; Figure 2) are notable, the manuscript's flagship machine-learning diagnostic claims across 33 cancer types and blood/plasma samples are invalidated by pervasive data leakage, supervised normalization artifacts, batch-target colinearity, and database contamination. Specifically: (1) `Voom` and Supervised Normalization (`SNM`) were applied to the **entire combined dataset** prior to train/test splitting or leave-one-out cross-validation; (2) in the plasma validation study, the target variable itself (`disease type`) was passed as the *supervised biological variable of interest* to `SNM` across all 169 samples before cross-validation; (3) in TCGA, regressing out `sequencing center` ($n = 8$) and `tissue source site` ($n = 191$, which is nearly 1-to-1 specific to cancer type) via `SNM` on sparse zero-inflated matrices imprints hospital/cancer-type-specific continuous offsets onto zero counts that `GBM` trees trivially exploit to predict cancer type even after discarding 92.3% of reads; and (4) the plasma validation cohorts were collected under separate clinical IRB protocols with extreme demographic imbalance (e.g., 100% male in prostate cancer vs. younger mixed-sex controls). Resolving these issues requires eliminating `SNM`, performing all normalization strictly inside cross-validation folds, evaluating classifiers on held-out hospital sites (`tissue source site` / `sequencing center`), and purging human/vector sequence contamination from the reference microbial database.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Known tumor-associated viruses (HPV in CESC/HNSCC, HBV in LIHC, EBV in STAD) and mucosal pathobionts (*Fusobacterium* in GI cancers) are enriched in their expected TCGA primary tumor tissues relative to blood and solid-tissue negative controls: [Well Supported].**  
  The targeted non-parametric comparisons in Figure 2b–g use internal clinical metadata (HPV status, clinical hepatitis history, STAD molecular subtype) and matched blood/normal tissue controls to confirm genuine biological enrichment of established oncoviruses and gastrointestinal *Fusobacterium*. However, even within LIHC, the manuscript notes (chunk 1, line 47) that the #1 feature ranked above HBV for predicting liver cancer was *Microcystis*—a freshwater photosynthetic cyanobacterium that does not colonize human organs—pointing to unaddressed k-mer/reference-database false positives in low-biomass read assignment.

* **Normalized microbial profiles in TCGA tissues and whole blood accurately discriminate among all 33 cancer types, including Stage Ia–IIc blood samples and ctDNA-mutation-negative patients: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This core claim rests on a leaky, confounded normalization and machine-learning pipeline (`Methods: Quantitative measurement and normalization of TCGA technical variation` and `TCGA ML methods`, chunk 1 lines 106 and 116):
  1. *Pre-split normalization leakage:* `Voom` (with TMM or quantile normalization) and `SNM` were fitted on the full 17,625-sample dataset *before* partitioning into 70% training and 30% test sets. Even in the split-half validation (`Extended Data Fig. 3a`), `Voom-SNM` was applied across all cancer types within each half using hospital and center covariates that encode cancer identity.
  2. *Artificial signal injection by `SNM` on sparse count data:* As the authors state on line 106, `tissue source site` ($n = 191$ hospital collection centers) and `sequencing center` ($n = 8$) are heavily or completely confounded with `disease type` (cancer type) in TCGA. When `SNM` fits linear models across log-cpm values of 1,993 genera (the vast majority of which are zero in any given blood or tumor sample) to subtract `sequencing center` ($n=8$), `tissue source site` ($n=191$), `platform` ($n=6$), and `strategy` ($n=2$) while preserving `sample type`, every zero entry in the raw count matrix is shifted to a non-zero residual value uniquely determined by that sample's `(tissue source site, sequencing center, platform)` tuple. Because `tissue source site` and `sequencing center` identify the TCGA study (cancer type), gradient boosted decision trees (`GBM`) simply split on these hospital-specific baseline shifts to achieve near-perfect one-versus-all cancer classification. This explains why discarding 92.3% of all microbial reads (`most stringent filtering`) barely alters the AUROC of predicting 33 cancer types in blood and tissue (`Extended Data Fig. 6g–l`, Figure 3a): the classifier is reading the `SNM` batch-correction footprint rather than true microbial presence.

* **Plasma-derived cell-free microbial DNA (`mbDNA`) accurately discriminates between healthy controls ($n = 69$) and patients with prostate ($n = 59$), lung ($n = 25$), and melanoma ($n = 16$) cancers in an independent validation cohort: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The plasma validation analysis (`Methods: Plasma microbiome data normalization, permutation testing, and ML`, chunk 2 lines 53–55) suffers from two fatal flaws:
  1. *Direct supervised target leakage into `SNM` before Leave-One-Out (LOO) cross-validation:* Line 53 states: *"we incorporated age and sex as technical factors in the Voom-SNM while holding disease type as the biological variable of interest... ML on the Voom-SNM normalized plasma microbiome samples was done... with leave-one-out (LOO) ML."* Running Supervised Normalization (`SNM`) on all 169 samples together with `disease type` (healthy vs. PC vs. LC vs. SKCM) specified as the target biological variable to be preserved/maximized—and only *afterward* running LOO or 70/30 bootstrap cross-validation on the `SNM`-transformed matrix—leaks the ground-truth diagnosis of every test sample directly into its normalized features prior to classification.
  2. *Cohort collection and demographic confounding:* As documented in `Clinical cohort selection and IRB protocols numbers` (chunk 2, line 27) and `Extended Data Fig. 9d–e`, the four groups were not prospectively collected under a unified protocol: all 59 prostate cancer plasma samples came from one investigator (`R.M.`, IRB 131550), all lung and melanoma samples from a second (`S.P.P.`, IRB 150348), and all 69 HIV-negative controls from a third (`R.H.`, five separate HIV/neurocognitive IRBs). Furthermore, the prostate cancer cohort is 100% male and significantly older than the mixed-sex healthy control cohort (`Extended Data Fig. 9d–e`). Regressing out `age` and `sex` while preserving `disease type` in `SNM` when one disease class (`PC`) is 100% male deterministically shifts all features of the prostate cancer class relative to controls, fabricating separability even under random noise.

* **`Kraken` genus-level taxonomic assignments on unmapped TCGA reads have a validated false-positive rate of only 1.09%: [Overstated].**  
  In the Methods (chunk 1, line 96), the 1.09% false-positive rate was computed simply by re-aligning `Kraken`-positive reads against the *exact same* 59,974-genome `RepoPhlan` database using `BWA` without human host depletion against a complete human reference (such as T2T-CHM13 or pangenome decoys) or filtering draft bacterial assemblies for embedded human/adapter contigs. If a draft bacterial genome in `RepoPhlan` contains a contaminant human repeat or cloning vector contig, both `Kraken` and `BWA` will map human/vector reads to that bacterial genus (yielding 98.9% agreement between `Kraken` and `BWA` while still being 100% false-positive for microbial presence).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Supervised Normalization (`SNM`) and Prevent All Pre-Split Data Leakage
* **WHY:** Applying `Voom-SNM` to the combined dataset prior to train/test splitting or LOO cross-validation—especially when passing `disease type` as the supervised biological variable in the plasma cohort, or regressing out `tissue source site` ($n = 191$) in TCGA—injects class-specific and hospital-specific numerical signatures into zero counts and invalidates all downstream AUROC/AUPR metrics.
* **WHAT:** Completely remove `SNM` from the machine-learning pipeline. Evaluate classifiers on raw relative abundances, simple within-sample Centered Log-Ratio (CLR) transformations, or binary presence/absence matrices where any cross-sample scaling is fit strictly on the training fold and applied blindly to the held-out test fold.
* **HOW:** Modify the R training scripts in `[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]` so that the input to `caret::train` / `gbm` is the un-normalized count table (or sample-wise CLR transformation $\ln(x_{ij} + 0.5) - \frac{1}{G}\sum_g \ln(x_{ig} + 0.5)$, which operates independently on each sample $i$ and cannot leak information across samples or encode hospital batch offsets onto zeros). Re-compute Figures 1f–h, 3, and 4 without `Voom-SNM`.

#### 2. Evaluate TCGA Classifiers Using Leave-One-Sequencing-Center-Out and Leave-One-Hospital-Out Cross-Validation
* **WHY:** Random stratified 70%/30% splits place samples from the same `tissue source site` (hospital) and `sequencing center` into both training and test sets, allowing `GBM` models to classify cancer types by recognizing hospital- or center-specific reagent/contaminant profiles rather than tumor biology.
* **WHAT:** Benchmark all TCGA one-cancer-type-versus-all-others and tumor-versus-normal classifiers using group-disjoint cross-validation where entire `tissue source sites` (or entire `sequencing centers`) are held out as the test set.
* **HOW:** Use `caret::groupKFold(metadata$tissue_source_site, k = 5)` in R so that no hospital `tissue source site` appears in both the training and test partitions. For multi-center comparisons, train models exclusively on samples sequenced at Harvard Medical School (`HMS`) and test them directly on samples sequenced at The Broad Institute or Baylor College of Medicine without re-normalizing the test center using its disease labels.

#### 3. Restrict Plasma Validation Comparisons to Age- and Sex-Matched Subsets Without `SNM`
* **WHY:** In the 169-sample plasma dataset, comparing 100% male, older prostate cancer patients against younger, mixed-sex healthy controls after adjusting for `age` and `sex` inside a `disease type`-supervised `SNM` step confounds demographic differences and biobank tube protocols with cancer status.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate the plasma `GBM` classifiers on raw/CLR-transformed counts (without `SNM`) restricted to male-only healthy controls age-matched to the prostate cancer cohort (and age/sex-matched controls for lung cancer and melanoma).
* **HOW:** Subset the 69 healthy controls to males within the age range of the prostate cancer cohort, apply sample-wise CLR transformation (with no access to `disease type`, `age`, or `sex`), run nested LOO cross-validation, and report the unconfounded AUROC and AUPR.

#### 4. Screen Reference Microbial Databases for Human Repeats, Low-Complexity k-mers, and Vector Contamination
* **WHY:** Legacy 2016 `RepoPhlan` draft microbial assemblies contain unmasked human DNA contaminants, phage/plasmid vectors, and low-complexity sequences. Unmapped human reads from TCGA BAM files (which were aligned to older human builds like GRCh37/hg19 lacking centromeric/telomeric repeats represented in T2T-CHM13) readily map to these contaminated microbial contigs, generating biologically implausible hits such as freshwater cyanobacteria (*Microcystis*) in liver tumors.
* **WHAT:** Re-filter human reads against the complete Telomere-to-Telomere human reference (`T2T-CHM13v2.0`) plus human pangenome decoys before taxonomic classification, and mask human-homologous regions and low-complexity sequences (`dustmasker`) in the microbial reference database.
* **HOW:** Map all candidate microbial reads in the plasma cohort and the 4 benchmark TCGA cohorts (`CESC, STAD, LUAD, OV`) against `T2T-CHM13v2.0` + `GRCh38` using `Bowtie2 --very-sensitive-local`, discard any read aligning to human sequence, and require a minimum breadth of genome coverage (or distinct genomic loci per taxon) before calling a microbial genus present.
