### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

Pekar et al. investigate the evolutionary root and early zoonotic transmission dynamics of SARS-CoV-2 by analyzing 787 complete early-pandemic viral genomes (lineages A and B) and 20 putative transitional haplotypes (C/C or T/T at nucleotides 8782 and 28144) collected through 14 February 2020. First, through forensic examination of sequencing read depth, laboratory clustering, and shared rare mutations, they demonstrate that early C/C and T/T genomes are artifacts of low sequencing coverage ($\le 10\text{x}$), contamination, or downstream convergent evolution rather than surviving transitional human lineages. Second, they reconstruct a 15-segment non-recombinant sarbecovirus progenitor (`recCA`), show that 82.6% (19/23) of early mutations toward `recCA` are C-to-T transitions driven by host mutational bias, and fit a non-reversible random-effects substitution model in BEAST v1.10.5 across multiple rooting priors (`unconstrained`, `no market`, `recCA`, and fixed haplotypes). Third, by combining phylodynamic posterior haplotype probabilities with forward stochastic epidemic and coalescent simulations (FAVITES-COVID-Lite on a 5-million-node contact network) via rejection sampling conditioned on earliest case onset/hospitalization dates, they report strong support for at least two separate zoonotic introductions into humans ($\text{BF} = 61.6$ with `recCA`; $\text{BF} = 60.0$ unconstrained), dating the lineage B primary infection to ~18 November 2019 (95% HPD: 23 October–8 December) and the lineage A primary infection to ~25 November 2019 (95% HPD: 29 October–14 December) with minimal cryptic pre-December circulation. Complete BEAST XMLs, simulation outputs, `recCA` reconstructions, and analysis code are publicly archived on Zenodo and Dryad.

This is a methodologically sophisticated and transparently executed study that integrates forensic sequence auditing, non-reversible substitution modeling, and forward epidemiological simulation to resolve the apparent conflict between outgroup similarity (which favors lineage A) and molecular clock divergence (which favors lineage B). The forensic refutation of putative transitional C/C and T/T genomes and the rejection of triple-revertant roots (such as WA1 and 20SF012) are compelling. The primary methodological caveats are: (1) the two-introduction Bayes factor ($\text{BF} \approx 60$) combines haplotype posteriors from a single-tree coalescent BEAST run with topological summary-statistic frequencies from forward simulations defined by sharp heuristic thresholds (basal polytomies of $\ge 20$ lineages, clade sizes strictly within 30–70%, and zero sampled intermediates along a 2-mutation branch) without explicitly modeling differential early case ascertainment around the Huanan market; and (2) the two introductions are modeled as independent samples from a static ancestral haplotype rather than jointly modeling coalescent and mutational divergence within the putative animal reservoir prior to spillover. These limitations can be addressed through targeted simulation sensitivity analyses and joint reservoir-to-human tree modeling.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Putative transitional C/C and T/T genomes sampled before mid-February 2020 reflect sequencing coverage artifacts, contamination, or downstream convergent evolution rather than a human evolutionary bridge between lineages A and B: [Well Supported].**  
  The authors audit all 20 intermediate genomes from the pre-14 February 2020 dataset (Table S1, Figs. S1–S5), confirming with originating laboratories and raw read inspection that 16 had $\le 10\text{x}$ coverage or ambiguous base calls at positions 8782/28144 or shared rare derived mutations with co-sequenced lineage A/B samples, while later C/C and T/T genomes nest deeply within established clades (e.g., Diamond Princess cruise ship) via recurrent C-to-T mutation.

* **Highly revertant early genomes (such as A.1/WA1 and A+C29095T/20SF012) arose via C-to-T mutational bias and are strongly rejected as the SARS-CoV-2 MRCA ($\text{BF} > 100$): [Well Supported].**  
  Across the early phylogeny, 19 of 23 reversions toward the reconstructed progenitor (`recCA`) are C-to-T substitutions, occurring nearly three times more frequently at the 381 `recCA`-divergent sites (6.04%) than background sites (2.17%). Under the non-reversible random-effects substitution model, both WA1-like and 20SF012-like ancestral haplotypes have posterior probabilities $\le 0.20\%$ ($\text{BF} > 100\text{--}1000$ rejection) across all rooting strategies (Table 1).

* **Lineages A and B resulted from at least two separate zoonotic introductions into humans ($\text{BF} \approx 60\text{--}61.6$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While a single introduction struggles to reconcile the molecular clock (which favors root B, 80.85% unconstrained posterior) with sarbecovirus outgroup proximity (which favors root A, 77.28% `recCA` posterior), the exact numerical magnitude of the Bayes factor ($\text{BF} = 61.6$) depends on the topological rejection criteria applied to the FAVITES-COVID-Lite simulations (Figure 2). Specifically, a single introduction from lineage A or B is accepted as matching the empirical phylogeny only if it produces a basal polytomy of $\ge 20$ lineages plus a derived clade of 30–70% of all taxa separated by exactly two mutations with zero sampled intermediates (occurring in $0.5\%$ of homogeneous-ascertainment simulations). In reality, intense targeted genomic sampling of Huanan market cases in late December 2019 selectively oversampled the lineage B superspreading polytomy while undersampling non-hospitalized community transmission, which can distort clade-size ratios and reduce the probability of sampling a low-prevalence 1-mutation intermediate in a single-introduction scenario.

* **The primary human infection of lineage B occurred in mid-November 2019 (18 November; 95% HPD: 23 October–8 December), followed within days to weeks by lineage A (25 November; 95% HPD: 29 October–14 December), with minimal cryptic pre-December transmission: [Well Supported].**  
  Coupling the phylodynamic tMRCA posteriors with epidemic rejection sampling anchored by earliest symptom onset and hospitalization dates demonstrates that under realistic SARS-CoV-2 doubling times (~3.5 days), cumulative human infections prior to December 2019 were small (median 3 at tMRCA, 99% of simulations $\le 33$ infections; median 0 hospitalizations before 1 December), aligning independently with negative retrospective serosurveys of Wuhan blood donors and influenza-like illness cohorts.

* **Approximately eight (95% HPD: 2–23) total zoonotic introductions occurred to establish lineages A and B: [Fragile].**  
  This estimate treats each spillover event as an independent and identically distributed Bernoulli trial with a constant extinction probability ($p_{\text{ext}} = 0.778$) derived from a scale-free network simulation without accounting for superspreading heterogeneity at the animal-human interface (e.g., high-density market stalls vs. sporadic contact) or time-varying force of infection from the animal reservoir.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Model Spatially/Epidemiologically Biased Case Ascertainment (Market Superspreading vs. Community Sampling) in the Topological Bayes Factor
* **WHY:** The FAVITES-COVID-Lite simulations assume uniform random ascertainment of infections across the 5-million-node network when calculating that only $0.1\%$ (C/C root) and $0.5\%$ (A or B root) of single-introduction epidemics produce two large polytomies of 30–70% taxon share separated by two mutations with no sampled intermediates. However, early Wuhan sequencing heavily targeted hospitalized Huanan market patients and clusters, which concentrates sampling on a market-amplified sub-clade (lineage B) while leaving a precursor 1-mutation human transmission chain unsampled if it did not seed a hospital cluster.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate the single-introduction topological probabilities ($P(\text{Fig. 2B})$ and $P(\text{Fig. 2C})$) under clustered/overdispersed case ascertainment where sampling probability is elevated in secondary superspreading clusters.
* **HOW:** Using the existing simulated FAVITES transmission trees on Dryad, implement a two-tier sampling scheme (e.g., high ascertainment probability in one randomly selected early superspreading cluster representing the market, and lower baseline ascertainment in the general network) and recompute the frequency of topologies matching Figure 2B and 2C and the resulting 2-vs-1 introduction Bayes factor in Table S5.

#### 2. Explicitly Model Within-Reservoir Coalescence and Substitution Between the Lineage A and B Progenitors
* **WHY:** In the primary BEAST phylodynamic analyses (Table 1), all 787 human sequences (both A and B) are forced to coalesce into a single tree under a single human coalescent prior, and the resulting root haplotype posteriors are then plugged into a formula that concludes the MRCA was *not* in humans. Conversely, if A and B represent two separate introductions, their connecting branches (carrying C8782T and T28144C) evolved in the animal reservoir under potentially different effective population sizes and evolutionary rates.
* **WHAT:** Run a structured two-deme (or two-subtree) BEAST model in which lineages A and B have independent human exponential-growth coalescent priors and connect only in an ancestral reservoir deme anchored by `recCA`.
* **HOW:** Modify the existing BEAST v1.10.5 XML (already set up to estimate separate tMRCAs for clades A and B in Fig. 3) to place independent exponential skygrowth/coalescent priors on the human A and B subtrees while linking their roots via a two-branch reservoir ancestral model, verifying that the lineage A and B tMRCA posteriors in Figure 3A–B remain unchanged when the joint human coalescent constraint at the root is removed.

#### 3. Quantify Sensitivity of the Two-Introduction Bayes Factor to the 2-Mutation Branch-Length Conditioning
* **WHY:** In Figure 2B–C, the simulation acceptance criteria require that the two clades be separated by *exactly* two mutations along the internal connecting branch simulated on the coalescent tree. Conditioning on an exact mutation count (2 mutations) conflates tree shape probability with the Poisson realization of branch mutations, which is already accounted for in the BEAST phylogenetic likelihood.
* **WHAT:** Separate the probability of the coalescent tree topology (two basal polytomies without sampled basal side-branches on the connecting edge) from the probability of accumulating two substitutions on that edge.
* **HOW:** Re-tabulate the fraction of simulated single-introduction coalescent trees in which an internal branch leading to a clade of 30–70% of tips has waiting time $\Delta t$ long enough to accumulate $\ge 1\text{--}2$ substitutions without leaving sampled intermediate tips, and report the resulting Bayes factor alongside the mutation-conditioned Bayes factor.
