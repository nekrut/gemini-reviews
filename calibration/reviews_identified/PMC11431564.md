### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates the molecular evolution of 26 genes encoding subunits of the three mammalian Structural Maintenance of Chromosomes (SMC) complexes—cohesin (11 genes, including 4 meiosis-specific subunits), condensin I/II (8 genes), and SMC5/6 (7 genes)—across 46 to 63 mammalian species. Using maximum likelihood codon models in PAML (`codeml` M1a vs. M2a and M7 vs. M8 under F3x4 and F61 codon frequency models, plus the Free Ratio branch model) alongside HyPhy site-level methods (SLAC, FEL, and FUBAR), the authors report pervasive purifying selection overall ($d_N/d_S$ ranging from 0.001 to 0.494) but identify statistically significant signatures of site-level positive selection in 7 of the 26 genes: all four meiotic cohesins (`RAD21L1`, `REC8`, `SMC1B`, `STAG3`), the condensin I subunit `NCAPG`, and two SMC5/6 subunits (`SMC5`, `NSMCE4A`). Requiring concordance across at least two site-level methods (BEB, FEL, FUBAR), they map 48 positively selected codons, show that 67% fall within predicted intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs), and report a positive rank correlation between gene-wide $d_N/d_S$ and transcriptional upregulation during female mouse leptotene meiosis. No multiple sequence alignments, phylogenetic trees, or analysis scripts are deposited in a public repository.

The comparative dataset covering all 26 SMC subunits across mammals is a useful resource, and requiring concordance across two codon frequency models (F3x4 and F61) and three site-inference methods helps reduce single-method artifacts. However, the study's main conclusions are weakened by several methodological and statistical flaws: (1) uncalibrated multiple testing across 26 genes and reliance on the over-parameterized PAML Free Ratio model to claim lineage-specific positive selection; (2) pseudoreplication in the codon-level Kruskal–Wallis test (Figure 1b) and an unadjusted two-cluster leverage artifact in the female meiotic expression correlation (Figure 3) that ignores expression breadth; (3) potential confounding of positive selection in IDRs with alignment ambiguity near manually edited gaps and synonymous rate variation; and (4) speculative narratives attributing selection to viral arms races, phase separation modulation, and female meiotic drive without formal comparative or functional tests. These limitations can be resolved through computational re-analysis using branch-site models, proper alignment uncertainty filtering, multiple-testing corrections, and tempered framing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Mitotic cohesin subunits evolve under extreme purifying selection, whereas meiosis-specific cohesins evolve more rapidly across mammals: [Well Supported].**  
  Gene-wide SLAC $d_N/d_S$ estimates (Table 1, Figure 1a) clearly separate mitotic cohesins ($d_N/d_S = 0.001\text{--}0.041$, below the 10th percentile of mammalian genes) from meiotic cohesins ($d_N/d_S = 0.215\text{--}0.494$, with `RAD21L1` above the 98th percentile). However, the Kruskal–Wallis and Nemenyi post-hoc test on site-wise $d_N - d_S$ in Figure 1b treats thousands of codons within the same genes as independent observations, constituting pseudoreplication.

* **Seven SMC complex genes (`RAD21L1`, `REC8`, `SMC1B`, `STAG3`, `NCAPG`, `SMC5`, `NSMCE4A`) experience pervasive site-level positive selection: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Six of the seven genes show large likelihood ratio test statistics ($-2\Delta\ln L \ge 10.11$) across both M1a/M2a and M7/M8 under F3x4 and F61 (Table 2). However, no multiple-testing correction (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across the 26 tested genes) is applied; for `SMC5`, the M1a vs. M2a test under F61 yields $p = 0.019$, which does not survive correction across 26 genes. Furthermore, PAML M2a and M8 assume constant $d_S$ across sites and are vulnerable to false positives in fast-evolving disordered regions when alignments are manually edited near indels.

* **Positively selected sites are enriched in intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) and modulate phase separation: [Fragile].**  
  While 32 of 48 candidate sites map to Metapredict-defined IDRs (Figure 2), IDRs naturally have much lower structural constraint and higher indel rates than folded globular domains. Testing enrichment via a binomial test against total protein length conflates relaxed purifying selection (where neutral and nearly neutral sites accumulate in IDRs) and indel-alignment errors with adaptive evolution. Moreover, the claim that three sites in ParSe-predicted regions of `RAD21L1` and `STAG3` drive adaptation through phase separation is based solely on sequence predictors without biophysical validation or null permutation controls.

* **Evolutionary rates of SMC complex genes correlate specifically with female meiotic expression due to intragenomic centromere/telomere-like conflict: [Overstated].**  
  In Figure 3, the correlation between $d_N/d_S$ and female mouse leptotene $\log_2$ fold-change ($\tau = 0.34, p = 0.015$) is driven by a bimodal split between 22 ubiquitously expressed somatic/mitotic subunits ($\log_2\text{FC} \approx 0$) and the 4 meiosis-specific cohesins ($\log_2\text{FC} > 4$). Because expression breadth across somatic tissues is one of the strongest predictors of mammalian $d_N/d_S$, genes restricted to germ cells naturally exhibit higher $d_N/d_S$ due to relaxed pleiotropic constraint. In addition, the four meiotic cohesins are also strongly upregulated in male meiosis (Figure 3, bottom panels: $\log_2\text{FC} \approx 2\text{--}5$), where they are essential for spermatogenesis (`RAD21L1` knockouts cause male azoospermia while females remain fertile), contradicting a strictly female meiotic-drive explanation.

* **Lineage-specific positive selection across mammalian orders and host–pathogen arms races drive the evolution of condensin and SMC5/6 complexes: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To infer branch-specific positive selection (`dN/dS > 1` in Supplementary Figure S1), the authors use the PAML Free Ratio (FR) model, which estimates an independent $d_N/d_S$ parameter for every branch (~120 parameters per tree). Point estimates of branch $d_N/d_S > 1$ from the FR model without branch-specific LRTs or synonymous substitution floors are statistically uncalibrated and dominated by small-$d_S$ sampling noise on short internal and terminal branches.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Free Ratio Point Estimates with Calibrated Branch-Site Tests and Apply FDR Correction
* **WHY:** Estimating a separate $\omega$ parameter on every branch in the PAML Free Ratio model produces noisy point estimates (including $\omega \to \infty$ when branch $d_S = 0$) and does not test whether $\omega > 1$ is statistically significant on any specific lineage. Likewise, performing 104 site-model LRTs (26 genes $\times$ 4 comparisons) without multiple-testing correction inflates the family-wise error rate.
* **WHAT:** Apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across all 26 genes for the site-model LRTs in Table 2, and replace the Free Ratio lineage analysis (Section 3.2 and Supplementary Figure S1) with HyPhy's `aBSREL` (adaptive Branch-Site Random Effects Likelihood) or PAML's branch-site model A vs. null model A1.
* **HOW:** Using the existing phyML trees and MSAs in the local HyPhy suite, run `hyphy absrel --alignment <gene.fas> --tree <gene.nwk>` for all 26 genes and report only branches with Holm–Bonferroni corrected $p < 0.05$. Apply `p.adjust(p_values, method = "BH")` in R across the 26 gene-level p-values in Table 2 and Supplementary Tables S2–S4.

#### 2. Control for Alignment Uncertainty and Relaxed Constraint in IDR Enrichment Tests
* **WHY:** Manual editing of alignments near small gaps (Section 2.1) introduces subjective bias and frequently places non-homologous codons in the same column within fast-evolving IDRs, inflating $d_N/d_S$ in PAML. Furthermore, testing IDR enrichment against total protein length ignores the baseline concentration of unconstrained codons in IDRs.
* **WHAT:** Replace manual alignment editing with automated alignment masking (e.g., GUIDANCE2 or trimAl) and PAML's `M8a vs. M8` test (1 df), and re-evaluate IDR enrichment relative to the background distribution of unconstrained ($d_N - d_S \ge 0$) codons rather than total protein length.
* **HOW:** Run PRANK or MAFFT followed by GUIDANCE2 masking (score $< 0.93$ masked as `NNN`) on the 7 candidate genes, re-run PAML `M8a` ($\omega_s = 1$ fixed) vs. `M8` ($\omega_s > 1$ free) and HyPhy `BUSTED` (which incorporates synonymous rate variation), and test via Fisher's exact test whether positively selected sites are enriched in IDRs compared to neutral/unconstrained sites ($\text{FEL } p > 0.1$ with $d_N/d_S \ge 0.8$).

#### 3. Account for Somatic Expression Breadth and Gene-Level Non-Independence
* **WHY:** In Figure 1b, running a Kruskal–Wallis test on pooled codons across genes violates independence by treating thousands of sites from the same gene as independent replicates. In Figure 3, correlating $d_N/d_S$ with meiotic fold-change across 26 genes is confounded by tissue specificity (meiotic vs. ubiquitous somatic expression).
* **WHAT:** Re-compute Figure 1b at the gene level ($n = 26$ gene means or medians) or via a linear mixed-effects model with `Gene` as a random intercept, and include somatic tissue expression breadth ($\tau_{\text{tissue}}$ across mammalian tissues) as a covariate when testing the association with female vs. male meiotic upregulation.
* **HOW:** In R, run `kruskal.test(dNdS ~ Group, data = table1_df)` on the 26 gene-level estimates in Table 1, and fit a partial Kendall correlation or multiple linear regression `lm(log(dNdS) ~ female_leptotene_FC + male_leptotene_FC + somatic_breadth)` to test whether female meiotic upregulation predicts $d_N/d_S$ beyond germ-cell restriction.

#### 4. Temper Mechanistic Claims in the Title, Abstract, and Discussion
* **WHY:** Because 19 of 26 SMC subunits (73%) show no evidence of positive selection and all 26 exhibit genome-wide $d_N/d_S \le 0.494$, the title *"Positive Selection Drives the Evolution of the Structural Maintenance of Chromosomes (SMC) Complexes"* overstates the prevalence of adaptive evolution.
* **WHAT:** Revise the Title, Abstract, and Discussion to reflect that SMC complexes evolve primarily under strong purifying selection, with episodic/site-specific positive selection restricted to meiotic cohesins and three condensin/SMC5/6 subunits.
* **HOW:** Change the title to *"Contrasting Selective Pressures Across Mammalian SMC Complexes: Strong Purifying Selection in Mitotic Cohesins and Site-Specific Positive Selection in Meiotic Subunits"* and explicitly frame the viral arms-race and female meiotic-drive hypotheses as testable functional hypotheses rather than demonstrated causes.

#### 5. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Inconsistencies between tables, inaccurate table footnotes, and missing alignment/tree depositions hinder reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**  
  * **Data Availability (Line 259):** Deposit the 26 unedited and final codon alignments and phyML Newick trees in Zenodo or Dryad rather than stating *"available upon reasonable request."*  
  * **Table 1 vs. Table 2 (`STAG3` sample size):** Reconcile the number of species for `STAG3`, which is reported as `63` in Table 1 (Line 77) but `62` in Table 2 (Line 173).  
  * **Table 2 Footnote b (Line 189):** Footnote b states *"p-value of rejecting the neutral models (M8a and M7) in favor of the positive selection model (M8)"*, whereas the table reports `M1 vs. M2` and `M7 vs. M8`. Correct the footnote to reference `M1a` and `M7`.  
  * **Table 2 Site Ordering and Gene Symbol (Lines 164–186):** Standardize `RAD21L*` in Table 2 to `RAD21L1*` (matching Table 1), sort the positively selected codon lists in ascending numerical order (`...411, 433, 477` for `RAD21L1`; `152, 168, 178, 191, 199, 244, 253...` for `REC8`), and add the missing space in `33, 38, 542, 797` for `SMC5`.
