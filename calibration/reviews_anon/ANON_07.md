### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript contrasts molecular evolutionary regimes across (i) 133,741 QC-filtered human SARS-CoV-2 genomes sampled through 12 October 2020 and (ii) 69 *Sarbecovirus* genomes partitioned into 19–20 non-recombinant regions (NRRs) to determine whether SARS-CoV-2's transmissibility in humans arose from post-spillover human adaptation or ancestral evolution in bats. Using codon models that account for terminal-branch sequencing artifacts, site-to-site and spatially autocorrelated synonymous rate variation (`BUSTED[S]` with a 3-rate HMM), multi-nucleotide substitutions (`BUSTED[S]+MH`), branch-site diversifying selection (`aBSREL`), site-level episodic/pervasive selection (`MEME`, `FEL`, `SLAC`), base-composition-corrected synonymous dinucleotide usage ($\text{SDU}_c$), BEAST local molecular clocks, and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (`PhylogeneticEM`) trait-shift detection, the authors show that human SARS-CoV-2 evolution up to October 2020 was dominated by weak purifying selection ($dN/dS \approx 0.4\text{--}0.9$) and sparse episodic selection (52 codons in S and RdRp, including S:D614G and RdRp:P323L), whereas the ancestral branch leading to the bat "nCoV" clade experienced both episodic positive selection and a marked downward shift in CpG content. Further, they show that the close bat relative RmYN02 (divergence from SARS-CoV-2 dated to ~1976) is a natural recombinant between the low-CpG nCoV lineage and a high-CpG non-nCoV sarbecovirus across Spike, proving co-infection in horseshoe bats. Live monitoring (`covid19.datamonkey.org`), accession tables, and BEAST XML files (S1 Data) are provided.

This is a methodologically sophisticated comparative evolutionary analysis that directly addresses critical confounding factors—including recombination breakpoints, terminal-branch sequencing/lab errors, spatial synonymous rate variation, and multi-hit substitutions—that have misled simpler coronavirus selection studies. The primary limitations are: (1) a tendency in the Abstract and Discussion to interpret the *absence* of statistically significant `aBSREL` branch-level selection on the long, unbroken terminal branch leading to SARS-CoV-2 (~44 years of evolution since 1976) as positive evidence that *no* adaptive host-specific changes occurred in an unsampled intermediate host or early human phase, despite low statistical power of branch-site $dN/dS$ tests to detect a handful of critical functional replacements against a background of strong purifying selection ($dN/dS \approx 0.04$); (2) forcing a non-ultrametric time tree to be ultrametric by artificially extending heterochronous tip branches before running `PhylogeneticEM` scalar OU trait-shift inference; and (3) a circular two-step procedure where the `PhylogeneticEM` CpG shift on the MCC tree is used to motivate a branch-specific local clock in BEAST, and the resulting MCC tree is used to run `PhylogeneticEM`. Addressing these methodological caveats and calibrating the power claims on the terminal SARS-CoV-2 branch will make an already strong paper exemplary.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Early human evolution of SARS-CoV-2 (December 2019–October 2020) was characterized by weak purifying selection ($dN/dS \approx 0.4\text{--}0.9$) and limited episodic positive selection on internal branches: [Well Supported].**  
  By excluding terminal branches (which are enriched for sequencing errors, laboratory chimeras, and unfixed deleterious mutations) and tracking internal-branch `MEME`, `FEL`, and `MG94xREV` estimates longitudinally across 133,741 genomes (Fig. 1A–E), the authors provide a well-controlled quantification of selective pressures during the first 11 months of the pandemic.

* **Episodic positive selection and a lineage-wide shift toward CpG depletion occurred at the base of the bat nCoV clade, and RmYN02 demonstrates recombination between low- and high-CpG sarbecovirus lineages within bats: [Well Supported].**  
  Partitioning the 69 *Sarbecovirus* genomes into non-recombinant regions and applying `BUSTED[S]` with a 3-class HMM for synonymous rate variation plus multi-nucleotide substitutions (`S3 Table`) rigorously controls against false-positive selection signals. Furthermore, the RDP5 breakpoint analysis (positions 21,248–24,058 in RmYN02) and frame-specific $\text{SDU}_c$ comparison across the recombinant vs. non-recombinant regions of RmYN02 Spike (Fig. 4A–B) provide compelling evidence that high- and low-CpG sarbecoviruses co-infect the same *Rhinolophus* bat hosts.

* **Lack of `aBSREL` branch-level positive selection on the terminal branch leading to SARS-CoV-2 indicates that adaptations happened in bats long ago, rather than in an unsampled intermediate host or early human phase: [Overstated].**  
  In the Discussion (line 73), the authors write: *"That we fail to find evidence of diversifying selection on the terminal branch leading up to the emergence of SARS-CoV-2 in humans (Fig 2B), indicates that the adaptations that created a generalist capable of efficient replication in humans and other mammals, probably did not occur in the unsampled SARS-CoV-2 lineage."* This conflates failing to reject the null in an `aBSREL` branch-site test with evidence for the absence of adaptation (Pillar 4):
  1. *Low power of `aBSREL` over decades of background purifying selection:* The terminal branch leading to SARS-CoV-2 in NRR2 spans from ~1976 to 2019 (~43 years, or since ~1969/1970 for RaTG13 in other regions), over which genome-wide $dN/dS \approx 0.04$ (Results line 30). If an ancestor underwent 40 years of strong purifying selection in bats followed by 3–10 adaptive amino acid substitutions in an intermediate host or early human transmission chain over a few months, those few substitutions are diluted across >40 years of synonymous divergence on the same unsplit terminal branch, giving `aBSREL` near-zero statistical power to detect an $\omega > 1$ rate class on that branch.
  2. *Spike RBD exclusion/recombination:* In Spike (particularly the variable loop / RBD where key hACE2 contact residues reside), recombination fragments the tree into short alignments where branch-site LRTs have limited power per segment.

* **The paired/unpaired $t$-test in Fig. 4B ($t_{2.07} = 3.03, p = 0.0450$) and the `PhylogeneticEM` scalar OU test on artificially extended ultrametric trees: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Two minor statistical modeling issues should be noted (Pillar 3 & 4):
  1. In Fig. 4B (line 66), the authors compare the absolute difference in $\text{SDU}_c$ between SARS-CoV-2 and RmYN02 across the 3 codon frame positions (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge`) in the non-nCoV ($n = 3$) vs. nCoV ($n = 3$) Spike regions using an *unpaired* one-tailed Welch $t$-test ($t_{2.07} = 3.03, p = 0.045$). Because the exact same 3 frame positions (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge`) are measured in both regions, the observations are matched by frame position, and with only $n = 3$ summary values per group, a permutation test or bootstrap over codons within the non-nCoV vs. nCoV Spike regions is far more appropriate than a $t$-test on 3 frame averages.
  2. In Methods ("Identifying shifts in CpG content", page 2 line 6), `PhylogeneticEM` requires an ultrametric tree for the scalar OU process, so the authors forced the heterochronous BEAST tree (containing tips sampled from 2003 to 2020) to be ultrametric *"by extending all the external edges of the trees to match the most recently sampled tip."* Extending terminal branches by up to 17 years distorts terminal evolutionary variance under an OU process; contemporary implementations (e.g., `l1ou` or `bayou` or non-ultrametric diagonal OU in `PhylogeneticEM` / `PCMBase`) handle non-ultrametric fossil/heterochronous tips natively without distorting tip branch lengths.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Quantify the Statistical Power of `aBSREL` on the Terminal Branch Leading to SARS-CoV-2
* **WHY:** Concluding from a negative `aBSREL` test on the terminal SARS-CoV-2 branch (Fig. 2B, line 73) that adaptive changes "probably did not occur in the unsampled SARS-CoV-2 lineage" ignores the dilution of brief adaptive episodes across a ~40-year terminal branch dominated by purifying selection ($dN/dS \approx 0.04$).
* **WHAT:** Perform a brief parametric simulation or analytical calculation showing the minimum number of positively selected codon substitutions (or minimum proportion of sites $p_+$ and $\omega_+$) required on the terminal branch of NRR2 / Spike for `aBSREL` to achieve 80% power at $p \le 0.05$, and temper the text in line 73 accordingly.
* **HOW:** Using HyPhy, simulate alignments along the empirical NRR2 and Spike segment trees where the terminal branch leading to SARS-CoV-2 has background $\omega_0 = 0.04$ across $1 - p_+$ of sites and $\omega_+ \in \{5, 10, 20\}$ across $p_+ \in \{0.005, 0.01, 0.02\}$ of sites (corresponding to 2–10 adaptive substitutions along the terminal branch). Report the `aBSREL` detection rate and explicitly state in the Discussion that while massive lineage-wide remodeling occurred at the base of the nCoV clade, a small number of punctual adaptive substitutions during recent spillover cannot be ruled out by `aBSREL`.

#### 2. Replace the $n=3$ Frame-Position $t$-Test in Figure 4B with a Codon-Level Bootstrap or Permutation Test
* **WHY:** Applying a Welch $t$-test to $n = 3$ summary numbers (`pos1`, `pos2`, `bridge` $\text{SDU}_c$ differences, yielding $\text{df} = 2.07, p = 0.0450$; Fig. 4B caption) has virtually no degrees of freedom and treats frame positions as independent draws rather than testing the underlying codon alignment.
* **WHAT:** Compute a bootstrap 95% confidence interval and permutation $p$-value by resampling codons (or amino acid pairs) within the non-nCoV and nCoV Spike regions.
* **HOW:** Using the `seqinr` / R script already used for $\text{SDU}_c$ (Eq. 1), resample the informative amino acids/pairs $i = 1 \dots k$ with replacement 1,000 times in both the non-nCoV (nt 1–2,810 of S) and nCoV (nt 2,811–3,822 of S) segments of Wuhan-Hu-1 and RmYN02, and compute the exact two-sided bootstrap $p$-value for $|\Delta\text{SDU}_c(\text{non-nCoV})| > |\Delta\text{SDU}_c(\text{nCoV})|$.

#### 3. Validate the CpG Shift Without Artificial Terminal-Branch Extension
* **WHY:** Artificially extending external branches of pre-2020 tips to make the BEAST MCC tree ultrametric for `PhylogeneticEM`'s scalar OU model (Methods, page 2 line 6) inflates the expected trait variance on older tips (such as 2003–2005 SARS-CoV-1 sequences) relative to their actual evolutionary time.
* **WHAT:** Verify that the inferred CpG shift at the base of the nCoV clade holds under a phylogenetic model that natively accepts non-ultrametric time trees (or Brownian motion / OU with true tip heights).
* **HOW:** Run the R package `l1ou` (`estimate_shift_configuration` on the un-extended BEAST MCC tree, or `PCMBase` / `phylolm` with `model = "OUfixedRoot"`) using the exact BEAST branch lengths, and confirm that the lasso-selected shift remains on the ancestral branch leading to the nCoV clade.

#### 4. Clarify Model Selection Between the Branch-Only Local Clock and Clade-Wide Local Clock in BEAST
* **WHY:** In Figure 3A, the caption states *"using different local clocks for the nCoV clade (red branches) and the rest of the phylogeny"* and colors the entire nCoV clade red, whereas the Methods (page 2 line 1) states *"We focused on the branch-only local clock model... Fig 3A presents the phylogeny for NRR2"*. This creates confusion over whether Figure 3A displays the *branch-only* local clock or the *clade-wide* local clock.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Reconcile the Figure 3A branch coloring and caption with the Methods description: if Figure 3A shows the clade-wide local clock, state so in the Methods; if it shows the branch-only local clock, color only the stem branch leading to the nCoV clade in red (or use two distinct shades for the stem branch vs. crown clade) and report the log marginal likelihoods (via stepping-stone / path sampling in BEAST) comparing the strict clock, stem-branch local clock, and clade-wide local clock.
