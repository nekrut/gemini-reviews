### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces a phylogeny-based mutational fitness estimation framework that leverages the ~7-million publicly available SARS-CoV-2 sequences in the UShER mutation-annotated tree (as of 11 May 2023, replicated on the ~14-million sequence GISAID tree) to estimate the fitness effects of individual single-nucleotide and amino acid mutations across all SARS-CoV-2 proteins. By counting independent mutational occurrences along branches within Nextstrain clades ($\ge 10^4$ sequences and $\ge 5,000$ four-fold degenerate mutations) after filtering error-prone sites, hyper-mutated branches ($>4$ mutations), and reference-reversion artifacts, the authors compute the expected neutral count of each nucleotide substitution $x \rightarrow y$ from four-fold degenerate sites in the clade founder and define the mutational fitness effect as $\Delta f = \ln\left(\frac{n_{\text{actual}} + P}{n_{\text{expected}} + P}\right)$ with pseudocount $P = 0.5$. They validate these estimates across viral clades (Delta, BA.1, BA.2, BA.5) and geographic subsets (USA vs. England), demonstrate strong correlation with deep mutational scanning (DMS) experiments for Spike ($r = 0.66$, nearly matching the $r = 0.72$ inter-experiment correlation) and $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$ ($r = 0.48\text{--}0.55$), show that structural and nonstructural proteins experience strong purifying selection while most accessory proteins (ORF6, ORF7a, ORF7b, ORF8, ORF10) tolerate stop codons and missense mutations, and provide an open-source Snakemake pipeline (`github.com/jbloomlab/SARS2-mut-fitness`) and interactive Altair visualizations.

This is an exemplary computational and evolutionary biology paper: the core mathematical insight (that at ~0.1% global sampling intensity, the count of independent branch occurrences in a densely sampled phylogeny scales inversely with the selection coefficient $s$ for deleterious mutations with $s \gg \epsilon$) is derived from branching-process theory in Section 4.10, quality-control filters directly address known UShER/consensus-calling artifacts, limitations are stated transparently in the Discussion, and reproducibility is outstanding. Two minor methodological refinements would further strengthen the study: (1) local nucleotide context (specifically $5'$ and $3'$ flanking bases, such as APOBEC-targeted $\text{TC}\underline{\text{N}}$ motifs for $\text{C}\rightarrow\text{T}$ transitions and RNA structure) systematically modulates four-fold degenerate mutation rates by 2- to 5-fold around the clade-wide $x \rightarrow y$ mean, contributing floor variance to $\Delta f$; and (2) for deleterious mutations where $n_{\text{actual}} = 0$, the fixed pseudocount $P = 0.5$ makes the lower bound of $\Delta f = \ln(0.5 / (n_{\text{expected}} + 0.5))$ strictly a function of the mutation spectrum rate $n_{\text{expected}}$ (which varies 60-fold from $\sim 9$ for $\text{T}\rightarrow\text{G}$ to $\sim 565$ for $\text{C}\rightarrow\text{T}$), creating a mutation-rate-dependent floor in Fig. 2A ("regression slope deviates from $x=y$") and Fig. 3A. Addressing these two points will sharpen the quantitative interpretation of strong deleterious effects.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Comparing actual branch-level mutation counts to clade-specific four-fold degenerate expectations accurately estimates mutational fitness effects for $s$ exceeding the sampling intensity ($\sim 10^{-3}\text{--}10^{-2}$): [Well Supported].**  
  The theoretical derivation in Section 4.10 (showing that expected lineage size scales as $1/s$ and detection probability scales as $\epsilon / s$ when $s \gg \epsilon$), combined with the clean separation of synonymous ($\Delta f \approx 0$), missense, and nonsense ($\Delta f \ll 0$) distributions across essential genes (Fig. 1B, Fig. 3A) and concordance between the 7M public tree and 14M GISAID tree (Fig. S1), firmly establishes the validity of the estimator.

* **Sequence-based fitness estimates correlate with Spike ($r = 0.66$) and $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$ ($r = 0.48\text{--}0.55$) deep mutational scanning and outperform site-level $dN/dS$ (`FEL`), DCA, EVE, and EpiScores: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 4 and Supplementary Figs. S5–S6 benchmark the estimates against five independent DMS datasets across two proteins under matched temporal windows (31 January 2022 for `FEL` $dN/dS$) and thoughtfully dissect specific biological discrepancies (e.g., $\text{M}^{\text{pro}}$ Q306* stop codon and K61N polyprotein-interface residue tolerated in isolated yeast assays but lethal in natural virus; Spike P527L C-terminal yeast-display boundary artifact).

* **Structural and nonstructural proteins are under strong purifying selection, whereas accessory proteins other than ORF3a (e.g., ORF7a, ORF8) experience minimal purifying selection against missense and nonsense mutations: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 3B and Supplementary Figs. S3–S4 show that stop-codon and missense mutations in ORF7a and ORF8 have median $\Delta f$ near zero, consistent with viable clinical isolates carrying ORF8 deletions/truncations and experimental reverse-genetics knockouts.

* **Cross-clade divergence in Spike mutational fitness effects reflects genuine epistatic shifts and changing immune selection rather than statistical noise: [Well Supported].**  
  By contrasting cross-clade comparisons (Delta vs. BA.1/BA.2/BA.5) against cross-geography controls (USA vs. England) across increasing expected-count thresholds (Fig. 2C–D), the authors cleanly separate finite-count Poisson variance from true biological shifts in Spike.

* **Dynamic range truncation and sample-size slope bias caused by the fixed $P = 0.5$ pseudocount across mutation types with 60-fold different expected counts: [Moderately Supported].**  
  For a lethal mutation ($n_{\text{actual}} = 0$), $\Delta f = \ln\left(\frac{0.5}{n_{\text{expected}} + 0.5}\right)$. Because $n_{\text{expected}}$ is $\sim 565$ for $\text{C}\rightarrow\text{T}$ but only $\sim 9\text{--}15$ for $\text{T}\rightarrow\text{G}$, a lethal $\text{C}\rightarrow\text{T}$ stop or missense mutation receives $\Delta f \approx \ln(0.5/565.5) = -7.03$, whereas an equally lethal $\text{T}\rightarrow\text{G}$ mutation receives $\Delta f \approx \ln(0.5/10.5) = -3.04$. This explains two phenomena in the paper: (i) why the stop-codon histogram in Fig. 3A spans a wide bimodal/broad range from $-2$ to $-7$, and (ii) why in Fig. 2A (Delta vs. BA.5), where one clade has more total sequences than the other, the OLS regression line deviates substantially from the $x = y$ identity line (since larger $n_{\text{expected}}$ stretches the negative tail of $\Delta f$ for strongly deleterious mutations).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Quantify or Adjust for Trinucleotide Context ($5'/3'$ Flanking Bases) in Expected Mutation Counts
* **WHY:** Currently, $n_{\text{expected}}(x \rightarrow y)$ is computed as the mean count across all four-fold degenerate sites with parental base $x$ in each clade (Section 4.4). However, in SARS-CoV-2, $\text{C}\rightarrow\text{T}$ transitions driven by host APOBEC3 deaminases occur at substantially higher rates in $5'\text{-U}\underline{\text{C}}\text{-}3'$ contexts than in $5'\text{-G}\underline{\text{C}}\text{-}3'$ or $5'\text{-C}\underline{\text{C}}\text{-}3'$ contexts. Using a context-independent rate underestimates $n_{\text{expected}}$ at $\text{U}\underline{\text{C}}$ sites (making neutral mutations appear mildly beneficial, $\Delta f > 0$) and overestimates $n_{\text{expected}}$ at non-$\text{U}\underline{\text{C}}$ sites.
* **WHAT:** Test a trinucleotide-context (or $5'$-dinucleotide-context) stratification of four-fold degenerate sites when computing $n_{\text{expected}}$, or quantify how much of the variance in synonymous $\Delta f$ around 0 (Fig. 3A) is explained by local flanking context.
* **HOW:** Since position 2 of a codon with a four-fold degenerate position 3 is fixed by the amino acid, stratify four-fold degenerate counts by the preceding base $b_{i-1} \in \{\text{A, C, G, U}\}$ (and following base $b_{i+1}$, which has 4,000+ total counts for major transitions like $\text{C}\rightarrow\text{T}$ and $\text{G}\rightarrow\text{A}$). Compare the width (IQR) of the synonymous $\Delta f$ distribution in Fig. 3A and the DMS correlations in Fig. 4 before and after $5'/3'$ context correction.

#### 2. Provide Confidence Intervals for $\Delta f$ and Address Pseudocount Censoring in Cross-Clade Regressions
* **WHY:** A mutation with $n_{\text{actual}} = 0$ and $n_{\text{expected}} = 10$ gives $\Delta f = -3.04$, whereas $n_{\text{actual}} = 0$ and $n_{\text{expected}} = 500$ gives $\Delta f = -6.91$, even though both observations are left-censored at zero actual counts ($n_{\text{actual}} = 0$). In Fig. 2A (Delta vs. BA.5), this difference in sample-size ceiling/floor pulls the ordinary least-squares slope away from $x = y$.
* **WHAT:** Report Poisson confidence intervals (or credible intervals under a Gamma-Poisson conjugate prior) alongside point estimates in `aa_fitness.csv`, and note how left-censoring at $n_{\text{actual}} = 0$ affects the dynamic range across clades of different sizes.
* **HOW:** Under $n_{\text{actual}} \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda n_{\text{expected}})$ with a Jeffreys prior $\text{Gamma}(0.5, 0)$ (which corresponds exactly to the authors' $P = 0.5$ pseudocount point estimate!), compute the 95% posterior credible interval for $\Delta f = \ln \lambda$ from the quantiles of $\text{Gamma}(n_{\text{actual}} + 0.5,\ n_{\text{expected}})$. In Fig. 2A, additionally report the correlation restricting to non-zero counts ($n_{\text{actual}} \ge 1$) or subsampling the larger clade's expected counts to match the smaller clade so the $x = y$ slope is preserved.
