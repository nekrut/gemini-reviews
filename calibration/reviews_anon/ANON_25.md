### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript experimentally tests whether site-specific thermodynamic amino acid preferences remain conserved over billion-year evolutionary timescales or are erased by epistatic coevolution ("evolutionary Stokes shifts"). The authors leverage resurrected Precambrian proteins—specifically thioredoxins corresponding to the Last Bacterial Common Ancestor (LBCA, $\sim 4$ Ga; $55\%$ sequence identity to *Escherichia coli*), LPBCA ($\sim 2.5$ Ga), and LGPCA ($\sim 1.5$ Ga), as well as Precambrian class A $\beta$-lactamases (PNCA, GNCA, GPBCA, ENCA; down to $37\%$ identity with *E. coli* TEM-1). Using differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), they compare the unfolding free energy ($\Delta\Delta G$) and denaturation temperature ($\Delta T_m$) impacts of 21 chemically conservative mutations ($\text{I}\leftrightarrow\text{V}$, $\text{E}\leftrightarrow\text{D}$) between *E. coli* and LBCA thioredoxin ($r = 0.89$, slope $= 1.03 \pm 0.21$, $P = 9 \times 10^{-8}$), alongside non-conservative exchanges at position 90 in thioredoxins ($\text{K}\leftrightarrow\text{L}$) and position 182 in $\beta$-lactamases ($\text{M}\leftrightarrow\text{T}$, a known clinical global suppressor in TEM-1). They further solve the X-ray crystal structure of the LPBCA thioredoxin L90K mutant (deposited in PDB under accession `4ulx`, compared against `2H6X`, `4BA7`, and `2yj7`) to reveal a local side-chain burial/exposure structural switch, and perform 23-replicate 15-day batch competition assays in *E. coli* to show a stationary-phase fitness advantage of wild-type K90 over K90L.

The experimental execution—combining multi-concentration and scan-rate-dependent DSC thermodynamic characterization, high-resolution X-ray crystallography (`4ulx`), and 23-replicate clonal competition assays—is thorough and biophysically insightful. However, the evolutionary generalization that site-specific amino acid preferences are broadly conserved across protein evolution is overstated relative to the restricted mutation sample: 21 of the 23 tested positions are chemically conservative methyl/methylene changes ($\text{I}\leftrightarrow\text{V}$, $\text{E}\leftrightarrow\text{D}$) selected from a prior single-site study, while the two non-conservative mutations ($\text{K90L}$ and $\text{M182T}$) were specifically chosen as known outliers/global suppressors. Furthermore, the ancestral sequences were inferred using site-homogeneous phylogenetic substitution models that are known to bias ancestral reconstructions toward consensus/thermally stabilizing residues (such as helix-capping T182 and hydrophobic core L90), creating a potential circularity when testing whether ancestral states are thermodynamically preferred. These issues can be addressed by explicitly bounding claims to the tested mutation classes and performing sensitivity checks across alternative posterior ancestral sequences.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Thermodynamic effects ($\Delta\Delta G$ and $\Delta T_m$) of 21 conservative $\text{I}\leftrightarrow\text{V}$ and $\text{E}\leftrightarrow\text{D}$ mutations correlate strongly between extant *E. coli* and resurrected LBCA thioredoxins ($r = 0.89$, $P = 9 \times 10^{-8}$): [Well Supported].**  
  The DSC measurements are carefully controlled for concentration dependence and scan-rate hysteresis (Supplementary Tables S1–S3), and reporting both Schellman-approximated $\Delta\Delta G$ and raw $\Delta T_m$ confirms that preference rankings agree at 18 of 21 sites (with the 3 discrepancies at positions 4, 60, and 61 involving near-zero $\Delta\Delta G$). Figure 3B also transparently documents a modest systematic shift toward greater destabilization in the extant background, consistent with a partial evolutionary Stokes shift that does not invert the sign of $\Delta\Delta G$.

* **A conserved local structural switch accommodates the non-conservative $\text{L90}\leftrightarrow\text{K90}$ exchange in thioredoxins while maintaining an energetic preference for leucine: [Well Supported].**  
  The new crystal structure of LPBCA thioredoxin L90K (PDB `4ulx`, compared with wild-type LPBCA `2yj7` and extant `2H6X` / LBCA `4BA7` in Figure 10) directly demonstrates that mutating buried L90 to K90 triggers a local backbone rearrangement that exposes the positively charged lysine side chain to solvent, matching the conformation observed in modern *E. coli* thioredoxin while explaining why L90 remains thermodynamically favored by up to $\Delta T_m \approx 15^\circ\text{C}$.

* **Site-specific amino acid energetic preferences are generally conserved throughout evolutionary history across dissimilar amino acids: [Overstated].**  
  Generalizing from 21 conservative $\text{I}\leftrightarrow\text{V}$ and $\text{E}\leftrightarrow\text{D}$ replacements (which alter side-chain volume by a single $-\text{CH}_2-$ or $-\text{CH}_3$ group and are governed by first-order secondary-structure propensities or local cavity packing) plus just two hand-picked non-conservative positions (position 90 in thioredoxin and the known global suppressor M182T in TEM-1 $\beta$-lactamase) to all evolutionary replacements is not statistically justified. Moreover, for the 7 red points in Figure 3A where the ancestral and extant residues actually diverged, sign-flipping the ancestral $\Delta\Delta G$ into the "*E. coli* direction" induces a mathematical correlation whenever mutations away from the wild-type residue in either background are predominantly destabilizing or when intrinsic secondary-structure propensities ($\text{D}\to\text{E}$ in $\alpha$-helices) dominate over tertiary context.

* **The clinical global suppressor mutation M182T in TEM-1 $\beta$-lactamase represents a reversion to an ancestral, helix-capping Ncap threonine state: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The DSC profiles across PNCA, GNCA, GPBCA, *B. licheniformis*, and *E. coli* TEM-1 (Figure 6) and the structural analysis of the 183–195 helix Ncap hydrogen bond (Figure 9) provide a clear biophysical mechanism for why T182 is stabilizing. However, because maximum-likelihood and Bayesian ancestral sequence reconstructions under site-homogeneous models exhibit a well-documented "consensus bias" toward the most frequent, canonically stabilizing residue in an alignment (threonine is the consensus Ncap residue across modern $\beta$-lactamases), the inference that T182 is strictly ancestral rather than a homoplastic consensus state requires robustness verification against ancestral reconstruction uncertainty.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Separate Intrinsic Secondary-Structure Propensity from Tertiary Context Conservation in the 21-Site Regression
* **WHY:** Six of the ten $\text{E}\leftrightarrow\text{D}$ mutations lie in $\alpha$-helices where glutamate is intrinsically favored over aspartate by $\sim 0.5$–$1.0\text{ kcal/mol}$ even in isolated peptides, and sign-flipping the 7 diverged sites into the "*E. coli* direction" mixes directional Stokes shifts with conserved intrinsic propensities.
* **WHAT:** Decompose the correlation in Figure 3A into (i) the 14 unchanged sites versus the 7 diverged sites, and (ii) residual $\Delta\Delta G$ after subtracting standard intrinsic secondary-structure propensities (e.g., Pace–Scholtz helix and Kim–Berg sheet scales).
* **HOW:** Compute the partial Pearson and Spearman correlations between $\Delta\Delta G_{\text{LBCA}}$ and $\Delta\Delta G_{\text{E. coli}}$ after regressing out secondary-structure propensity, and report separate slopes and $95\%$ confidence intervals for the 14 conserved-state sites (blue) and 7 diverged-state sites (red).

#### 2. Quantify the Magnitude of the Evolutionary Stokes Shift at Diverged Versus Unchanged Sites
* **WHY:** Figure 3B shows $\Delta\Delta\Delta G = \Delta\Delta G_{\text{E. coli}} - \Delta\Delta G_{\text{LBCA}}$, which is predominantly negative, indicating evolutionary accommodation of the extant residue. Testing whether this shift is significantly larger at the 7 diverged sites (red) or at sites with lower $6\text{ \AA}$ neighborhood sequence identity (Figure 2C) directly tests the Stokes-shift hypothesis.
* **WHAT:** Perform a formal statistical test comparing $|\Delta\Delta\Delta G|$ (and $|\Delta\Delta T_m|$) between the 7 diverged sites and the 14 unchanged sites, and regress $\Delta\Delta\Delta G$ against the fraction of identical residues within the $6\text{ \AA}$ sphere (Figure 2C).
* **HOW:** Use a two-sample Wilcoxon rank-sum test and ordinary least squares regression of $\Delta\Delta\Delta G$ on local $6\text{ \AA}$ sequence divergence using the existing data in Supplementary Table S1 and Figure 2C.

#### 3. Bound Claims on Non-Conservative Replacements and Address Consensus Bias in Ancestral Reconstructions
* **WHY:** Only two non-conservative exchanges ($\text{K90L}$ in thioredoxin and $\text{M182T}$ in $\beta$-lactamase) were experimentally assayed, and ancestral sequence reconstruction is known to favor consensus stabilizing states.
* **WHAT:** Temper the Abstract and Conclusions so that preference conservation across highly dissimilar amino acids is presented as a proof-of-principle demonstrated at two structurally rationalized sites rather than a universal rule, and report the posterior probabilities of L90 (in LBCA/LPBCA) and T182 (in PNCA/GNCA/GPBCA/ENCA) alongside the frequency of L90 and T182 in the extant alignments.
* **HOW:** Add a column or paragraph reporting the marginal posterior probabilities of the reconstructed states at positions 90 and 182 and the extant alignment frequencies, and revise the Abstract to specify that systematic multi-site correlation was established for conservative ($\text{I}/\text{V}$, $\text{E}/\text{D}$) replacements while two non-conservative switches were dissected mechanistically.
