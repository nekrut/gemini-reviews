### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript conducts a genome-wide association study (GWAS) and genomic prediction (GP) analysis of salt tolerance across 563 soybean (*Glycine max*) germplasm accessions from the USDA GRIN collection (150 salt-tolerant scored as `1` and 413 salt-susceptible scored as `9`), genotyped at 34,181 filtered SNPs from the SoySNP50K array on SoyBase. Using GAPIT 3 (`GLM`, `MLM`, `FarmCPU`, and `BLINK`), the authors report 10 SNPs exceeding the Bonferroni threshold ($\text{LOD} = -\log_{10} P > 5.83$) across chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 7, and 16, and list 11 candidate genes located within 4–5 kb of 7 of those SNPs. They then evaluate genomic prediction accuracy (Pearson's $r$ across 100 random splits) using GAPIT BLUP methods (`maBLUP`, `gBLUP`, `sBLUP`) on all 34,181 SNPs as well as five linear/Bayesian models (`rrBLUP`, `BA`, `BB`, `BL`, `BRR`) across eight random SNP subsets (`r10` to `r10000`, $r = 0.19\text{–}0.31$) and the 10 GWAS-selected SNPs (`m10`, $r = 0.36\text{–}0.40$). The underlying phenotypic and genotypic datasets are publicly accessible via USDA GRIN and SoyBase.

While leveraging public germplasm repositories to identify salt-tolerance markers in soybean is a practical breeding objective, the study's main conclusions are undermined by three major methodological flaws. First, the genomic prediction experiment for the 10-SNP GWAS set (`m10`, Table 2 and Figure 4) suffers from severe target leakage: the 10 SNPs were selected by running GWAS on the entire 563-accession panel before partitioning the same 563 accessions into training and validation sets, artificially inflating prediction accuracy ($r = 0.38$) relative to random SNP sets via the "winner's curse." Second, although four GWAS models were run to control false positives, 8 of the 10 reported SNPs were detected *exclusively* by the naive Generalized Linear Model (`GLM`, which lacks a kinship matrix to control for familial relatedness) and failed to reach significance in `MLM`, `FarmCPU`, or `BLINK` (Table 1). Third, a strictly binary phenotype (`1` vs. `9`) is analyzed throughout using Gaussian linear models, $t$-tests, and Pearson correlations without binary classification metrics, and the candidate gene search is restricted to an arbitrary $\pm 4\text{–}5\text{ kb}$ window despite soybean linkage disequilibrium (LD) blocks spanning $>100\text{ kb}$. Re-running GWAS feature selection strictly inside each cross-validation training fold, prioritizing kinship-controlled multi-locus associations, and fixing numerous numerical and text-table discrepancies are required to make the study sound.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The 10-SNP GWAS marker set (`m10`) achieves superior genomic prediction accuracy ($r = 0.36\text{–}0.40$) compared to up to 10,000 genome-wide SNPs ($r = 0.28\text{–}0.31$) (Abstract, Results lines 186–194, Table 2, Figure 4): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The 10 SNPs in `m10` were identified by performing GWAS across all 563 accessions (line 71). Evaluating those same 10 SNPs via 100 random cross-validation splits of the same 563 accessions leaks the validation set phenotypes into the feature-selection step. In high-dimensional data ($p = 34,181 \gg n = 563$), pre-selecting the top 10 markers correlated with the outcome across the whole dataset guarantees an upward-biased cross-validation correlation ($r = 0.38$) that will not generalize to independent breeding cohorts. Furthermore, the manuscript never specifies the training-to-validation split ratio used in the 100 iterations (line 83), and the "reference" GP values ($r = 0.46, 0.60, 0.44$ for `maBLUP`, `gBLUP`, `sBLUP`; lines 184, 218) appear to reflect in-sample ("for itself") fits rather than out-of-sample cross-validation.

* **Ten SNPs across chromosomes 1, 2, 3, 7, and 16 are robustly associated with soybean salt tolerance across multiple GWAS models (Results lines 110–118, Table 1, Figure 3): [Overstated].**  
  Table 1 shows that **zero** SNPs reached significance in `MLM` or `BLINK`, and 8 of the 10 reported SNPs (all three on Chr 1, all three on Chr 2, `Gm03_43213208_ss715586397`, and `Gm07_41776639_ss715598058`) were detected solely by `GLM`. Because `GLM` only includes fixed subpopulation covariates ($Q_1\text{–}Q_4$) without a polygenic kinship matrix ($K$), it suffers from substantial $p$-value inflation in structured soybean panels (visible in the `GLM` QQ plot in Figure 3B). Only two SNPs—`Gm03_43220331_ss715586399` ($\text{LOD} = 6.03$ in `FarmCPU` and $6.11$ in `GLM`) and `Gm16_7722217_ss715625494` ($\text{LOD} = 5.95$ in `FarmCPU`)—were supported by a model that accounts for confounding polygenic background.

* **Eleven genes within 4–5 kb of the significant SNPs represent the primary candidate genes for salt tolerance (Results lines 173–179, Table 1): [Fragile].**  
  In self-pollinating cultivated soybean (*Glycine max*), genome-wide LD typically decays over 100–500 kb in euchromatic regions. Restricting the candidate gene search window to $\pm 4\text{–}5\text{ kb}$ around tag SNPs on a medium-density array (34,181 SNPs, ~1 SNP per 28 kb) is biologically arbitrary and misses causal genes in strong LD with the tag SNPs. Moreover, no expression data, haplotype analysis, or functional annotation connecting these 11 genes to known soybean ion-homeostasis loci (such as `GmSALT3`/`GmCHX1` on chromosome 3) is provided.

* **Phylogenetic and population structure analyses show that the 150 salt-tolerant accessions span diverse genetic backgrounds while clustering by geographic origin (Results lines 199–203, Figures 2, 5): [Moderately Supported].**  
  PCA, kinship, and neighbor-joining/maximum-likelihood trees support four major clusters ($Q_1\text{–}Q_4$) and geographic grouping among the top six source countries. However, the text contains arithmetic and methodological discrepancies: line 203 states that six countries contributed 104 tolerant germplasms ($46 + 31 + 12 + 7 + 6 + 5$), which actually sums to **107**, and the Methods (line 66) state that MEGA 7 trees were built from 10,000 random SNPs whereas the Figure 5 caption (line 209) states 6,000 random SNPs.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Data Leakage in Genomic Prediction by Nesting GWAS Inside Cross-Validation Folds
* **WHY:** Selecting `m10` on the full 563-accession dataset before cross-validation leaks validation phenotypes into marker selection, invalidating the claim that 10 GWAS markers outperform 10,000 genome-wide markers.
* **WHAT:** Perform GWAS marker selection strictly within each training split during the 100 cross-validation iterations, and explicitly report the training/validation split ratio (e.g., 80% training / 20% validation).
* **HOW:** In each of the 100 random iterations (splitting the 563 lines into e.g. $n_{\text{train}} = 450$ and $n_{\text{val}} = 113$), run GAPIT GWAS on the 450 training lines only, select the top 10 SNPs from that training-set GWAS, train `rrBLUP`, `BA`, `BB`, `BL`, and `BRR` on the 450 training lines using those 10 SNPs, and predict the held-out 113 validation lines. Update Table 2 and Figure 4 with the leak-free out-of-sample $r$-values, and also report the area under the ROC curve (AUC) given that the observed phenotype is binary (`1` vs. `9`).

#### 2. Distinguish Kinship-Controlled Multi-Locus Hits from Unadjusted `GLM` Associations and Calibrate the LD Window
* **WHY:** Eight of the 10 SNPs in Table 1 only pass the threshold in `GLM` (no kinship matrix) and fail `MLM`, `FarmCPU`, and `BLINK`, while a 4–5 kb window is narrower than the average 28 kb inter-SNP spacing of the SoySNP50K chip.
* **WHAT:** Prioritize the two `FarmCPU`-validated loci (`Gm03_43220331_ss715586399` and `Gm16_7722217_ss715625494`) as high-confidence associations, frame the 8 `GLM`-only loci as suggestive/tentative, and define candidate gene windows using empirical local LD decay ($r^2 > 0.2$ or $D'$ blocks).
* **HOW:** Compute pairwise LD ($r^2$) around each significant SNP using PLINK or GAPIT 3, expand Supplementary Table 2 to list genes within the local LD block (or within the standard $\pm 100\text{ kb}$ euchromatic window in soybean), and check the physical distance and LD ($r^2$) between the chromosome 3 locus (`43.21–43.22 Mb`) and the known soybean salt-tolerance gene `GmCHX1`/`GmSALT3`.

#### 3. Reconcile Contradictions Across Abstract, Methods, Results, Tables, and Conclusion
* **WHY:** Multiple sections contradict one another on which GWAS models were run, which chromosomes harbor the 11 candidate genes, how many SNPs are on chromosome 1, and how many accessions come from the six countries.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Abstract (line 16) vs. Methods/Results (lines 71, 110):** The Abstract states that the four GWAS models were *"MLM, MLMM, FarmCPU, and BLINK"*, whereas the Methods, Results, and Figure 3 use *"GLM, MLM, FarmCPU, and BLINK."* Change *"MLMM"* to *"GLM"* in the Abstract (and remove the duplicated phrase *"Employing four distinct models—we performed a genome-wide association study (GWAS) using four models... we conducted a Genome-Wide Association Study (GWAS)"* in line 16).
  2. **Abstract (line 21) vs. Table 1 / Results (line 179):** The Abstract states that the 11 candidate genes are on *"chromosomes 1, 3, and 16"*, omitting **chromosome 2** (`Glyma.02G195400`). Update line 21 to *"chromosomes 1, 2, 3, and 16."* Also standardize the window size description across lines 21, 76, and 175 (*"5 kb"*) versus Table 1 lines 127 and 142 (*"0–4 kb"* / *"less than 4 kb"*).
  3. **Conclusion (line 223):** Change *"four SNP markers on chromosome 1 (Gm01_4306329_ss715579436, Gm01_4312808_ss715579441, and Gm01_4336306_ss715579451)"* to *"three SNP markers on chromosome 1"*, so the chromosomal counts ($3 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 1$) sum to 10 rather than 11.
  4. **Results (line 203) vs. Figure 5 Caption (line 209):** The country breakdown *"Japan (46), China (31), India (12), Suriname (7), Indonesia (6), and Nepal (5)"* sums to **107**, not **104**. Verify which country counts or total (`104` vs. `107`) are correct and update line 203 and the Figure 5 caption accordingly. Also reconcile line 66 (*"10,000 randomly selected SNPs in MEGA 7"*) with the Figure 5 caption (*"6000 randomly selected SNPs"*).
  5. **Results (lines 118, 177, 179) Table Cross-References:** Change all citations of `(Table 2)` in lines 118, 177, and 179 to `(Table 1)`, as Table 2 contains genomic prediction $r$-values rather than GWAS SNP statistics or candidate genes.
  6. **Methods (line 81) vs. Results (line 184):** `cBLUP` is listed in the Methods as one of the four GAPIT GP models, but its $r$-value is omitted from the Results (line 184) and Discussion (line 218). Report the `cBLUP` $r$-value alongside `maBLUP`, `gBLUP`, and `sBLUP`.
