# Forensic Peer Review: `ANON_19`

## 1. Executive Assessment & Calibration

**Overall Score:** **3.8 / 10.0** (Flawed / Overstated)
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.9/3.5 | Claims: 0.7/2.5 | Biology: 0.9/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.3/2.0

**Synthesis:** This manuscript examines neutral synonymous divergence ($d_S$) between SARS-CoV-2, bat coronavirus RaTG13, and Guangdong/Guangxi pangolin coronaviruses, and analyzes 103 early human SARS-CoV-2 genomes to propose that the virus has evolved into "two major types (designated L and S)" defined by two tightly linked SNPs (`T8782C` and `C28144T`, $r^2 = 0.954$) that purportedly experience distinct selective pressures and transmission rates. While the genome-wide $d_S$ calculations between SARS-CoV-2, RaTG13 ($d_S = 0.17$), and pangolin sarbecoviruses ($d_S = 0.469$–$0.722$) are straightforward and informative, the manuscript's three main evolutionary claims are methodologically flawed: (1) it attempts to refute RBD recombination between ancestral bat and pangolin lineages using $d_S = 0.411$ over a tiny 24-codon window while running single-tree PAML `CODEML` across a recombinant alignment; (2) it misinterprets the basal bifurcation of a non-recombining coalescent genealogy (`8782`/`28144`) and spatiotemporal sampling ascertainment in Wuhan as evidence of two functionally distinct viral types with different evolutionary rates; and (3) it claims rampant intra-host "heteroplasmy" from raw SRA read alignments with as few as 2 alternate reads and duplicate sample accessions.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Audit

* **Claim 1:** "Genome-wide synonymous divergence ($d_S$) between SARS-CoV-2 and RaTG13 is $17\%$ ($0.168$–$0.17$), corresponding to roughly 19.8–55.4 years of divergence under coronavirus molecular clock priors, and $46.9\%$ ($0.469$) between SARS-CoV-2 and GD Pangolin-CoV." — **[Well Supported]**
  * **Audit:** Using `yn00` and `codeml` in PAML across concatenated coding sequences (Table 1), the authors properly separate synonymous substitutions ($d_S$) from nonsynonymous substitutions ($d_N$), showing that strong purifying selection ($d_N/d_S = 0.044$ genome-wide) compresses overall nucleotide divergence ($\sim 4\%$) relative to neutral synonymous divergence ($16.8\%$). The wide molecular-clock interval reflects genuine rate uncertainty in bat sarbecoviruses.

* **Claim 2:** "The high amino acid identity at six critical RBD ACE2-contact residues between SARS-CoV-2 and GD Pangolin-CoV is caused by elevated mutation rate and convergent natural selection rather than recombination, because $d_S = 0.411$ in the 24-codon motif (`484–507`) implies 19.2–53.7 years of divergence." — **[Methodologically Flawed]**
  * **Audit:** This argument rests on two methodological fallacies. First, estimating $d_S = 0.411$ over a 24-codon window (72 nucleotides, containing only $\sim 16$–$18$ synonymous sites) carries an enormous standard error that cannot statistically distinguish regional divergence depths without a formal confidence interval or breakpoint test. Second, and more fundamentally, showing that the RBD of SARS-CoV-2 and the RBD of the 2019 GD Pangolin-CoV isolate diverged 20–50 years ago does **not** refute recombination; rather, ancestral recombination between the RaTG13/SARS-CoV-2 progenitor lineage and an ancestral pangolin-related sarbecovirus lineage decades ago predicts that the RBD will have a radically different tree topology (clustering SARS-CoV-2 with GD Pangolin-CoV instead of RaTG13) while still accumulating decades of synonymous substitutions since the ancestral recombination event. Indeed, Table 1 shows that in Spike, $d_S$ between SARS-CoV-2 and RaTG13 jumps to $0.321$ (nearly double the genome-wide average of $0.168$), whereas $d_S$ between SARS-CoV-2 and GD Pangolin-CoV drops from $0.751$ in `orf1ab` to $0.354$ in `S`—the classic signature of historical recombination. Furthermore, running PAML `CODEML` site models (`M7` vs. `M8`) assuming a single tree topology across a recombinant Spike alignment violates the fundamental assumption of phylogenetic codon models and generates false-positive Bayes Empirical Bayes (BEB) selection calls.

* **Claim 3:** "Population genetic analysis of 103 SARS-CoV-2 genomes reveals two major viral types (L, $\sim 70\%$, and S, $\sim 30\%$) in near-complete linkage across `T8782C` and `C28144T` ($r^2 = 0.954, \text{LOD} = 50.13$); the derived L type is more prevalent in early Wuhan, carries a significantly higher derived mutation load ($P < 0.0001$), and is likely more aggressive/transmissible due to natural selection." — **[Methodologically Flawed]**
  * **Audit:** In a non-recombining, clonally expanding viral outbreak, all non-recurrent mutations that arise on the same internal branch of the coalescent tree are automatically in complete linkage disequilibrium ($r^2 = 1.0$) unless disrupted by sequencing errors or homoplasy. Reporting a `Haploview` linkage disequilibrium test ($\text{LOD} = 50.13$) across a non-recombining tree as if the viral genomes were independent recombining diploids is statistically invalid. Any two mutations occurring near the root of a star-like coalescent tree partition the sample into an ancestral paraphyletic grade ("S") and a derived clade ("L"). Moreover, the comparison of L vs. S frequencies in Wuhan (26 L vs. 1 S among 27 Wuhan genomes, compared to 45 L vs. 28 S outside Wuhan, Fisher's exact $P = 0.0004$) and the Mann-Whitney test of derived allele counts ($P < 0.0001$, Figure 4) are severely confounded by **spatiotemporal ascertainment and non-independence**: the 27 Wuhan genomes were heavily sampled from hospitalized cases linked to the initial Wuhan cluster in late December 2019 and early January 2020 (where the L subclade founded the market/hospital cluster), whereas non-Wuhan genomes were sampled across later January 2020 travel exports. Comparing mutation counts across two clades of a tree using an independent-samples Mann-Whitney $U$ test violates phylogenetic non-independence (pseudoreplication).

* **Claim 4:** "Five of 12 patients analyzed by raw next-generation sequencing reads show multi-site intra-host heteroplasmy, including co-occurrence of L and S alleles (`positions 8782 and 28144`) in `SRR11092057`/`SRR11092062`." — **[Methodologically Flawed]**
  * **Audit:** Inspection of Table 2 reveals two severe defects:
    1. **Duplicate sample accession treated as two patients:** Table 2 lists both `SRR11092057` and `SRR11092062` as mapping to the exact same patient sample (`WH_2019/12/30.e`, GISAID accession `EPI_ISL_402124`). Thus, only 11 unique GISAID specimens were examined, and the two "independent" runs showing `8782`/`28144` minor alleles come from the same specimen (`EPI_ISL_402124`).
    2. **Unfiltered low-depth sequencing errors and cross-contamination:** In Table 2, the authors call "heteroplasmy" using `bcftools mpileup` at positions supported by only **2 alternate reads** out of 8 to 21 total reads (e.g., position `29441` in `SRR11092057` has `6 Ref / 2 Alt`; position `4965` in `SRR10903402` has `6 Ref / 3 Alt`; position `23457` has `15 Ref / 2 Alt`; position `1822` has `19 Ref / 2 Alt`). In amplicon or metagenomic Illumina sequencing of clinical swabs sequenced in the same laboratory batch as other Wuhan samples, 2 alternate reads without PCR duplicate removal, strand-bias filtering, or negative-control subtraction cannot distinguish true intra-host co-infection from index hopping, cross-sample contamination, or RT-PCR/sequencing error.

## 3. Concrete Suggestions for Improvement

1. **Test for Recombination Explicitly Before Running `CODEML` (`Claim 2`):**
   * Run a sliding-window phylogenetic topology and breakpoint analysis (`RDP4`, `GARD`, or sliding-window `dS` and maximum-likelihood trees across `orf1ab`, `S` NTD, `S` RBD, and `orf3a–N`) prior to fitting `CODEML` M7/M8. Fit codon selection models separately on non-recombinant partitions using each partition's local tree topology rather than a single concatenated genome tree.

2. **Remove Epidemiological Claims of Differential "Aggressiveness" and "Transmission Rate" Between L and S (`Claim 3`):**
   * Reframe "L and S" simply as two basal phylogenetic clades separated by two root-proximal substitutions (`C8782T` and `T28144C`) on a non-recombining viral tree.
   * Replace the independent-samples Mann-Whitney $U$ test on per-genome derived mutation counts (Figure 4) with a tip-dated Bayesian molecular clock (`BEAST`) or root-to-tip regression that accounts for exact sampling date (`collection_date`) and shared phylogenetic ancestry.

3. **Apply Strict Variant-Calling Filters and Deduplicate Patient Specimens in Table 2 (`Claim 4`):**
   * Merge or clarify `SRR11092057` and `SRR11092062` in Table 2 so that `EPI_ISL_402124` (`WH_2019/12/30.e`) is not double-counted as two independent patients.
   * Require standard intra-host single-nucleotide variant (iSNV) calling criteria (e.g., minimum total depth $\ge 100\times$, minimum alternate allele frequency $\ge 3$–$5\%$, minimum $\ge 10$ unique deduplicated alternate reads, and Phred base quality $\ge 30$ with forward/reverse strand balance) before claiming biological heteroplasmy.
