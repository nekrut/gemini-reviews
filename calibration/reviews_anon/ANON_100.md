### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This pilot study evaluates the effect of ex vivo bisphenol A (BPA) exposure on fecal microbiota from 28 Spanish children aged 5–11 years—15 normal-weight (`NW`, median BMI $15.43$) and 13 with obesity (`OB`, median BMI $23.47$). Fecal slurries (1:10 w/v in PBS) were incubated with 50 ppm BPA for 10 days at $37^\circ\text{C}$ under anaerobic conditions, yielding four comparison groups: unexposed baseline (`NW`, `OB`) and 10-day BPA-exposed (`NW10`, `OB10`). Using V3–V4 16S rRNA sequencing (`DADA2`/`QIIME2` on 46 passing samples; 393 ASVs) alongside anaerobic culturomics (pre-enrichment in sheep blood and bovine rumen fluid for 1, 15, and 30 days followed by MALDI-TOF and 16S Sanger identification of 655 isolates across 56 species), the authors report that 10-day BPA exposure reduces alpha diversity, depletes *Bacteroidota* (specifically *Bacteroides*), and enriches *Actinomycetota* (*Bifidobacterium*, *Collinsella*) and endospore-forming *Clostridia*/*Bacilli*. They further compare baseline taxon ratios in 150 children (`NW` $n = 93$, `OB` $n = 57$) from the `OBEMIRISK` cohort (`PRJNA979040`), proposing *Bacteroidota*/*Actinomycetota* and *Bacteroides*/*Bifidobacterium* ratios as biomarkers of BPA- and obesity-associated dysbiosis.

Combining 16S amplicon sequencing with anaerobic culturomics is a useful strategy for distinguishing DNA persistence from viable bacterial survival, and raw sequencing reads are deposited in NCBI SRA (`PRJNA979040`). However, the study's core experimental design and statistical analysis suffer from three major flaws that confound the conclusions. First, the unexposed groups (`NW` and `OB`) represent unincubated Day-0 baseline stool ("before treatment," lines 33 and 109), whereas `NW10` and `OB10` were incubated for 10 days at $37^\circ\text{C}$ in nutrient-free PBS plus BPA (and an unspecified BPA solvent) without a time-matched Day-10 vehicle-incubated control (`PBS + solvent` for 10 days at $37^\circ\text{C}$ without BPA). Consequently, the massive die-off of vegetative Gram-negative anaerobes (*Bacteroides*) and relative survival of hardy Gram-positive *Actinomycetota* and endospore-forming *Clostridia*/*Bacilli* cannot be separated from the effect of 10-day ex vivo starvation in PBS at $37^\circ\text{C}$. Second, the 15 `NW` and 13 `OB` donors were explicitly pre-selected at baseline to have non-overlapping *Bacillota*/*Bacteroidota* ratios (`NW < 0.8–4.5` and `OB > 4.5–120`, line 33), making the subsequent claim that `OB` has depleted *Bacteroidota* and therefore resembles `Bacteroidota`-depleted `NW10`/`OB10` samples circular. Third, the statistical tests ignore the paired repeated-measures structure (`NW` vs. `NW10` and `OB` vs. `OB10` originate from the same children). Addressing these issues requires clarifying or running a Day-10 vehicle control, accounting for subject-level pairing in linear mixed models, and explicitly acknowledging the baseline ratio pre-selection.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Ten-day ex vivo exposure to 50 ppm BPA specifically depletes *Bacteroidota* (*Bacteroides*) and promotes *Actinomycetota* (*Bifidobacterium*, *Collinsella*) and spore-forming *Clostridia*/*Bacilli*: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  According to Section 2.1 (lines 33, 57–60) and Figure A1, fecal samples were homogenized 1:10 (w/v) in nutrient-free PBS and exposed to 50 ppm BPA for 10 days at $37^\circ\text{C}$ (`NW10`, `OB10`) and compared against the unexposed samples (`NW`, `OB`, described as "before treatment" in line 33 and "before and after ex vivo BPA exposure" in Figure 1 caption, line 109). Because there is no Day-10 vehicle control (fecal slurry incubated in PBS + vehicle solvent for 10 days at $37^\circ\text{C}$ without BPA), the design completely confounds BPA toxicity with 10 days of nutrient starvation in PBS at $37^\circ\text{C}$ (as well as the unspecified organic solvent required to dissolve 50 ppm BPA, since BPA's aqueous solubility requires ethanol or DMSO). Vegetative *Bacteroides* lyse rapidly during prolonged nutrient-free incubation in PBS, whereas endospore-forming *Clostridia*/*Bacilli* and thick-walled *Bifidobacterium*/*Collinsella* persist, which also explains why `NW10` and `OB10` become statistically indistinguishable (`PERMANOVA R^2 = 0.050, p = 0.237`, Table A1).

* **Microbiota of non-exposed children with obesity (`OB`) resemble BPA-exposed communities (`NW10`, `OB10`), demonstrating that BPA induces an obesity-like dysbiosis: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Section 2.1 (line 33), the authors state: *"Microbiota 16S rRNA were revised so that we could choose different Bacillota / Bacteroidota ratios (NW < 0.8–4.5 and OB > 4.5–120)."* Because the 13 `OB` samples were deliberately selected to have extreme *Bacillota*/*Bacteroidota* ratios ($>4.5\text{--}120$, i.e., very low *Bacteroidota*) and the 15 `NW` samples were selected to have low ratios ($<0.8\text{--}4.5$, i.e., high *Bacteroidota*), the observation that `OB` has lower *Bacteroidota* than `NW` (and therefore smaller Bray–Curtis distance to the *Bacteroidota*-depleted `NW10`/`OB10` groups in Table A1 and Figure 1b) is a direct, circular artifact of the baseline sample selection rule. Paradoxically, in the full 150-child cohort (`OBEMIRISK`, Section 3.5, line 185 and Figure 7j), the *Bacteroidota*/*Bacillota* ratio showed *no significant difference* between obese and normal-weight children, confirming that the pilot samples were artificially extreme.

* **`ANCOM-BC2` and `MaAsLin2` identify statistically significant taxon shifts across the four study groups (`NW`, `OB`, `NW10`, `OB10`): [Fragile].**  
  `NW10` and `OB10` are paired post-incubation aliquots derived from the same 28 fecal specimens as `NW` and `OB`. However, in Section 2.4 (lines 82–86) and Section 3.2.2 (line 125), Mann–Whitney U tests, PERMANOVA, and `MaAsLin2` (run with "default parameters" comparing `OB`, `NW10`, and `OB10` against `NW` as a fixed reference) treat all 46 sequenced samples as independent observations without a random intercept for donor (`(1|SubjectID)`) or stratified permutations in `vegan::adonis2(..., strata = SubjectID)`.

* **In the 150-child `OBEMIRISK` cohort, *Bifidobacterium* and *Actinomycetota* are elevated and *Bacteroides* is reduced in children with obesity, validating the *Bacteroides*/*Bifidobacterium* and *Bacteroidota*/*Actinomycetota* ratios: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The cross-sectional differences in the larger 150-child cohort (`NW` $n = 93$, `OB` $n = 57$; Figure 7) are supported by adequate sample size, though the 28 pilot samples appear to be a subset of this same cohort (`López et al. 2024 [15]`, line 31 and line 88) rather than an independent external cohort, and dietary/urinary BPA levels were not measured in these 150 children to link their in vivo microbiota ratios to actual BPA exposure.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Clarify the Baseline vs. Day-10 Incubation Design and Vehicle Solvent Controls
* **WHY:** If `NW` and `OB` were sequenced at Day 0 while `NW10` and `OB10` were sequenced after 10 days of anaerobic incubation at $37^\circ\text{C}$ in PBS + BPA solvent, it is impossible to know how much of the *Bacteroides* loss and *Clostridia*/*Bifidobacterium* enrichment was caused by 10 days of PBS starvation or the solvent (e.g., ethanol/DMSO) rather than BPA.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state in Section 2.1 the exact solvent and final v/v concentration used to dissolve 50 ppm BPA, clarify whether `NW` and `OB` underwent 10-day vehicle incubation at $37^\circ\text{C}$ or were frozen Day-0 aliquots, and if no Day-10 PBS/vehicle control was sequenced, highlight this prominently as a major limitation in the Abstract, Results, and Discussion.
* **HOW:** Update Section 2.1 (lines 57–60) and Figure A1 with the exact vehicle composition and incubation timeline for `NW` and `OB`. If stored fecal aliquots remain for a subset of donors (e.g., $n = 5$), run a 10-day $37^\circ\text{C}$ anaerobic incubation in PBS + vehicle solvent alongside PBS + 50 ppm BPA to quantify the vehicle-adjusted BPA effect by qPCR or 16S sequencing.

#### 2. Account for Donor Pairing in `MaAsLin2`, `ANCOM-BC2`, PERMANOVA, and Diversity Tests
* **WHY:** Because `NW` and `NW10` (and `OB` and `OB10`) are repeated measurements from the same children, treating them as independent groups violates the independence assumption of the Mann–Whitney U test, unstratified PERMANOVA, and fixed-effects `MaAsLin2`.
* **WHAT:** Re-run the statistical comparisons incorporating donor ID as a random effect or pairing variable.
* **HOW:** In `MaAsLin2`, set `random_effects = c("SubjectID")` and `fixed_effects = c("BMI_Group", "BPA_Exposure")`; in `ANCOM-BC2`, set `rand_formula = "(1 | SubjectID)"`; for within-group BPA comparisons (`NW` vs. `NW10` and `OB` vs. `OB10`), apply the paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the complete pairs; and for PERMANOVA of BPA exposure, constrain permutations within subjects using `strata = SubjectID`.

#### 3. Acknowledge the Circularity of Pre-Selecting Pilot Samples by *Bacillota*/*Bacteroidota* Ratio
* **WHY:** Selecting the 15 `NW` and 13 `OB` pilot samples specifically so that `NW` had *Bacillota*/*Bacteroidota* $< 0.8\text{--}4.5$ and `OB` had $> 4.5\text{--}120$ (line 33) guarantees by construction that `OB` has depleted *Bacteroidota* at baseline, which artificially inflates the similarity between `OB` and `NW10`/`OB10` and contradicts the null *Bacteroidota*/*Bacillota* difference in the full 150-child cohort (Figure 7j).
* **WHAT:** Remove claims that the pilot study independently discovered baseline *Bacteroidota* depletion in `OB`, and state clearly in Sections 3.2 and 3.5 that the 28 pilot samples were a stratified extreme-ratio subset drawn from the 150-child `OBEMIRISK` cohort.
* **HOW:** In Section 3.5 (line 88 and line 171), state whether the 28 pilot children are included within the 150 `OBEMIRISK` samples; if so, re-compute the Figure 7 Mann–Whitney tests on the remaining $150 - 28 = 122$ non-overlapping children so the validation set is strictly independent of the pilot subset.
