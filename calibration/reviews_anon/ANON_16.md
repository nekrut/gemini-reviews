### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript characterizes microbial reads across 18,116 whole-genome sequencing (WGS; $n = 4,831$) and RNA-seq ($n = 13,285$) samples spanning 33 cancer types from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), alongside a validation cohort of 169 cell-free plasma samples (69 HIV-negative non-cancer controls, 59 prostate cancer, 25 lung cancer, and 16 melanoma patients) sequenced by shallow shotgun metagenomics. Unmapped reads extracted from TCGA BAM files were classified at the genus level ($n = 1,993$ genera) using Kraken against a 2016 RepoPhlAn database of 59,974 microbial genomes (and on a subset of 13,517 samples using SHOGUN against the 10,575-genome Web of Life database), filtered through four *in silico* decontamination tiers (`decontam` plus a reagent blacklist), transformed via `Voom` and Supervised Normalization of Microarrays (`SNM`), and used to train stochastic gradient boosting (GBM) classifiers. The authors report high classification accuracy across 33 cancer types in primary tumor tissue and whole blood—including in stage Ia–IIc cancers and tumors lacking Guardant360 or FoundationOne Liquid genomic alterations—as well as high accuracy distinguishing cancer patients from healthy controls in the UCSD plasma cohort. Pre-processed raw and Voom-SNM normalized genus matrices, metadata, plasma sequencing reads (ENA/Qiita), and analysis code are publicly available.

While the study confirms expected, well-known oncoviruses and mucosal pathobionts in specific primary tissues (such as *Alphapapillomavirus* in cervical and head-and-neck cancers, *Orthohepadnavirus* in hepatitis B-positive liver cancer, *Lymphocryptovirus* in EBV-positive stomach cancer, and *Fusobacterium* in gastrointestinal tumors; Figure 2), its primary pan-cancer and liquid-biopsy classification conclusions are invalidated by two severe methodological flaws. First, extracting unmapped reads from legacy TCGA BAM files without uniform re-alignment to a complete human reference (and vector decoys) and classifying them with Kraken against a 59,974-genome draft database that omits the human reference genome causes millions of residual human reads per sample to match human-contaminated draft bacterial contigs; the authors' BWA benchmark (reporting a "1.09% false positive rate") is circular because it aligns Kraken-positive reads back against the exact same uncurated 59,974-genome microbial database without a human reference. Second, the `Voom-SNM` normalization pipeline is applied globally across the dataset *prior* to train/test splitting and cross-validation, using target labels (`sample type` in TCGA and `disease type` in the plasma cohort and TCGA blood simulations) as the supervised biological variable while regressing out 191 `tissue source site` covariates that are confounded with cancer type on pseudocount-imputed zeros. Resolving these flaws requires re-filtering all unmapped reads against complete human references (including T2T-CHM13 and human pangenome/vector decoys), restricting microbial databases to decontaminated complete genomes that include a competitive human reference, and eliminating global supervised `Voom-SNM` normalization prior to machine learning.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Known tumor viruses (HPV, HBV, EBV) and gastrointestinal *Fusobacterium* are enriched in their expected primary tumor types relative to matched normal tissues and blood: [Well Supported].**  
  The targeted hypothesis tests in Figure 2b–g demonstrate significant enrichment of *Fusobacterium* in gastrointestinal tumors ($p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$), *Alphapapillomavirus* in clinically HPV-positive CESC and HNSC tumors ($p \le 3 \times 10^{-9}$), *Orthohepadnavirus* in HBV-positive LIHC ($p \le 2.8 \times 10^{-7}$), and *Lymphocryptovirus* in EBV-positive STAD ($p \le 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$), with blood-derived normals showing no enrichment. These high-biomass viral and mucosal signals reflect genuine biological colonization/infection in primary tissues.

* **Kraken genus-level assignments in TCGA have a validated false-positive rate of only 1.09%: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  To estimate Kraken's false-positive rate, the authors took reads classified as microbial by Kraken in four cancer types and aligned them with BWA against the **exact same 59,974-genome microbial database** (which lacked the human reference genome and included thousands of draft microbial assemblies), concluding that because 98.91% of Kraken reads aligned with BWA to that database, the false-positive rate was 1.09% (Results, page 2; Methods, page 7; Table S3). This validation is circular: when unmapped human reads match human DNA sequences contaminating draft microbial genomes in the 59,974-genome database via 31-mers, gapped alignment (BWA) maps those same human reads to the same contaminated microbial contigs. Testing alignment consistency against the same contaminated database without competitive alignment to a complete human reference does not measure the true false-positive rate of human-to-microbial misclassification.

* **Distinctive microbial signatures accurately discriminate all 33 TCGA cancer types in tissue and blood, including stage Ia–IIc and ctDNA-mutation-negative tumors: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The pan-cancer classifiers (Figures 1f–h and 3a–d) are trained on data transformed by `Voom` and `SNM` across the full cohort before 70%/30% train/test splitting. In `limma::voom`, every zero read count ($y_{gi} = 0$) is replaced by $\log_2((0 + 0.5)/(L_i + 1) \times 10^6)$, a deterministic function of sample $i$'s total microbial library size $L_i$. Subsequently, `SNM` fits a linear model preserving `sample type` ($n = 7$, directly leaking the target label for Tumor vs. Normal classifiers in Figure 1g) while subtracting batch effects for `sequencing center` ($n = 8$), `platform` ($n = 6$), `strategy` ($n = 2$), `FFPE status`, and **`tissue source site` ($n = 191$)** (Methods, page 8). Because TCGA tissue source sites (hospitals/clinics) are almost strictly nested within individual cancer types, subtracting fitted `tissue source site` and `center` offsets from library-size-scaled pseudocounts imprints a unique, deterministic numerical tag for each cancer type and sample type onto every zero-count taxon in the matrix. Even in the "most stringent filtering" dataset (discarding 92.3% of reads), sparse taxa consisting almost entirely of zeros retain these batch-regression tags, allowing GBM decision trees to separate cancer types with near-perfect accuracy in the absence of biological signal.

* **Cell-free microbial DNA in plasma accurately discriminates healthy controls from prostate, lung, and melanoma patients in the external validation cohort: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the UCSD plasma cohort ($n = 169$; Figure 4) and the TCGA blood power simulations (Extended Data Figure 9a), the authors explicitly passed **`disease type` as the supervised biological variable of interest** into `Voom-SNM` across all samples *prior* to performing the 500 bootstrap 70%/30% splits and Leave-One-Out (LOO) cross-validation (Methods, pages 10 and 12). Using the prediction target (`disease type`) to supervise feature normalization across the entire dataset before cross-validation leaks the test sample's class label directly into its normalized features. Furthermore, the three clinical groups in the plasma cohort were sourced from three separate biobanks under different IRB protocols (prostate cancer from one investigator, IRB 131550; lung/melanoma from a second investigator, IRB 150348; and younger HIV-negative controls from a third group, Extended Data Figure 9d–e), completely confounding disease status with pre-analytical blood collection, storage history, and demographic structure.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Global Supervised Voom-SNM Normalization and Perform Strict Out-of-Fold Preprocessing
* **WHY:** Applying `Voom-SNM` across the full dataset before train/test splitting—and passing `sample type` (in TCGA) or `disease type` (in the plasma cohort and TCGA blood simulations) as the supervised target variable while subtracting 191 cancer-confounded `tissue source site` offsets from pseudocount zeros—leaks class and collection-site metadata into the feature matrix.
* **WHAT:** Remove `Voom-SNM` entirely from the machine learning pipeline, and evaluate classifiers directly on raw (or simple within-sample relative abundance / centered log-ratio) count matrices where zeros remain strictly zero.
* **HOW:** Re-run the GBM classifiers in Figures 1f–h, 3a–d, and 4b–k on raw unnormalized genus counts (or row-wise relative abundances $y_{gi}/L_i$ where $y_{gi}=0 \mapsto 0$), with any feature scaling or unsupervised batch adjustment fitted strictly inside the 70% training split (or $N-1$ LOO training fold) without passing `sample type`, `disease type`, or `tissue source site` to the normalizer. As a mandatory negative control, run the exact normalization and ML pipeline on a submatrix of genera whose raw counts are all zeros, and on label-permuted data where labels are permuted *before* normalization, verifying that AUROC equals $0.50$.

#### 2. Re-Filter Unmapped Reads Against Complete Human Assemblies and Include a Human Reference in Kraken
* **WHY:** Relying on pre-existing unmapped flags in legacy TCGA BAM files leaves millions of unaligned human reads per sample, and classifying those reads against a 2016 microbial database containing draft genomes and no human reference causes pervasive false-positive bacterial calls.
* **WHAT:** Perform uniform two-pass host depletion against GRCh38, the complete T2T-CHM13 assembly, and UniVec vector sequences, and rebuild the Kraken/Bowtie2 reference index to include the human genome alongside exclusively finished/curated microbial genomes.
* **HOW:** Align all initially unmapped reads from TCGA and the plasma cohort against GRCh38 + T2T-CHM13 + PhiX + UniVec prior to taxonomic classification. Re-run taxonomic classification using a database restricted to complete RefSeq microbial genomes plus the human reference genome (so k-mers shared between human repeats and microbial genomes are assigned to the root/human rather than bacteria). Replace the circular BWA benchmark in Table S3 with an alignment of Kraken-assigned microbial reads against T2T-CHM13 and NCBI `nt` to measure the true rate of human read misclassification.

#### 3. Control for Biobank/Collection-Site Confounding and Demographic Mismatch in the Plasma Cohort
* **WHY:** In the UCSD validation cohort ($n = 169$), healthy controls ($n = 69$), prostate cancer patients ($n = 59$, 100% male), and lung/melanoma patients ($n = 41$) were obtained from three separate biobanks with distinct age and sex distributions (Extended Data Figure 9d–e). Regressing out age and sex while preserving `disease type` in `Voom-SNM` across the whole cohort creates artificial group separation.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate plasma classification without `Voom-SNM` on age- and sex-matched subsets, and test whether models trained on TCGA blood can predict UCSD plasma samples without retraining.
* **HOW:** Construct a propensity-matched subset of the UCSD plasma cohort matching healthy controls and cancer patients on age and sex (e.g., restricting prostate cancer vs. control comparisons to age-matched males on raw/relative counts without SNM). Additionally, evaluate external generalizability by applying the TCGA blood models directly to the UCSD plasma samples without re-fitting supervised normalization on the UCSD labels.

#### 4. Correct the SourceTracker2 Input Pipeline
* **WHY:** SourceTracker2 is a Bayesian Dirichlet-multinomial model that requires discrete integer read counts, where zeros represent the absence of reads. Feeding `Voom-SNM` normalized values (where zero read counts are mapped to positive continuous numbers and shifted by batch regression) into SourceTracker2 violates the multinomial likelihood model, and creates an asymmetry because the HMP2 source samples were not transformed with `Voom-SNM`.
* **WHAT:** Run SourceTracker2 on raw, decontaminated integer read counts for both HMP2 sources and TCGA COAD/SKCM sinks.
* **HOW:** Re-compute Figure 2a and Extended Data Figures 5b and 6c–f using the raw genus count matrices (after removing human-aligned reads and `decontam`-flagged genera) rarefied to a common read depth, without applying `Voom-SNM`.
