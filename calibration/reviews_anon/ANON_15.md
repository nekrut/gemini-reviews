### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript performs a forensic bioinformatic and statistical re-analysis of a prominent pan-cancer microbiome study (Poore et al., 2020) that analyzed 17,625 TCGA samples across 33 cancer types and 1,993 microbial genera. By downloading and re-analyzing raw unmapped reads from 1,255 TCGA samples across bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA, $n = 683$), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC, $n = 334$), and breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA, $n = 238$), as well as auditing the full $17,625 \times 1,993$ raw and Voom-SNM normalized count matrices, the authors uncover two independent, fatal flaws in the original study: (1) incomplete host-read removal (relying on pre-existing GRCh37/GRCh38 BAM flags) left an average of $1.4\text{–}3.3$ million human reads per sample, which subsequently matched human DNA sequences contaminating thousands of draft bacterial genomes in the 2016 Kraken database (which lacked a human reference genome), inflating bacterial read counts by $10$- to $67,000$-fold across $>92\text{–}99\%$ of non-zero sample/genus entries; and (2) the Voom-SNM normalization pipeline assigned tumor-type-specific and sample-type-specific numerical constants to genera whose raw read counts were entirely zero, creating artificial labels that gradient-boosting classifiers exploited. The authors prove this second flaw by extracting an information-free $12,803 \times 66$ submatrix of all-zero raw counts, substituting the published Voom-SNM normalized values, and reproducing near-perfect pan-cancer classification accuracy across 32 cancer types (median sensitivity $0.94$, specificity $0.90$, NPV $1.0$). All re-estimated count matrices, database accession lists, the 384 GB curated KrakenUniq index, and replication scripts are publicly available on GitHub and AWS.

This is a rigorous, decisive methodological audit whose core conclusions are incontrovertible. The controlled ablation on sample `s2707` (`TCGA-DK-A1AB`) and the zero-matrix machine learning replication (Figure 6) provide textbook demonstrations of how database contamination and improper normalization create synthetic predictive signals in low-biomass metagenomics. The manuscript can be further strengthened by addressing a few minor numerical inconsistencies between Table 1 and the main text, clarifying why blood-derived normal WGS samples were excluded from the BLCA re-analysis ($n = 156$ of $277$ WGS samples) while retained in HNSC and BRCA, and briefly explaining the exact algebraic mechanism by which `Voom` ($\log_2\text{CPM}$ pseudocount scaling) and `SNM` (`tissue_source_site` and batch regression) imprint sample metadata onto zero counts.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Unmapped reads in TCGA BAM files contain millions of residual human reads per sample that were misclassified as bacterial due to contaminated draft genomes and the omission of a human reference in the Kraken database: [Well Supported].**  
  Re-aligning initially unmapped reads from 1,255 BLCA, HNSC, and BRCA samples against the complete T2T-CHM13 human reference genome identified $981.5$ million ($19.5\%$), $519.2$ million ($14.5\%$), and $785.9$ million ($51.0\%$) additional human reads, respectively. In the deep-dive experiment on BLCA tumor sample `s2707`, mapping unaligned reads against 2016 GenBank draft genomes for *Streptococcus* ($10,270$ genomes) and *Waddlia* ($4$ genomes) without a human decoy reproduced massive false-positive counts ($1,434,287$ and $197,811$ read pairs), of which $98.1\%$ and $98.9\%$ aligned directly to CHM13 human DNA; simply adding CHM13 to the Kraken database reduced these false positives by $132$-fold and $>1,100$-fold, respectively.

* **More than 92–99% of non-zero microbial read counts ($\ge 10$ or $\ge 100$ reads) reported by Poore et al. in BLCA, HNSC, and BRCA are inflated by at least an order of magnitude: [Well Supported].**  
  Using two-pass human filtering (GRCh37/38 followed by CHM13) and classifying remaining reads with KrakenUniq against a curated database of finished bacterial/archaeal/viral genomes, EuPathDB, UniVec, and GRCh38 (Table 2), the authors show that for entries with $\ge 10$ reads in Poore et al., $98.5\%$ (BLCA), $92.9\%$ (HNSC), and $97.9\%$ (BRCA) were at least $10\times$ too high, and fewer than $0.2\text{–}1.0\%$ of entries were within $50\%$ of the re-estimated counts. Top reported genera such as *Streptococcus* ($560,000$ reads/sample) and *Mycobacterium* ($411,000$ reads/sample) in BLCA dropped to $36$ and $6$ reads/sample, respectively (Figure 1).

* **Voom-SNM normalization artificially tagged zero-count microbial features with cancer-type-specific and sample-type-specific constants, enabling near-perfect machine-learning classification from information-free (all-zero) raw data: [Well Supported].**  
  Inspecting individual high-importance features (*Hepandensovirus* in ACC, *Thiorhodospira* in KICH, *Nitrospira* in LUSC, and *Mulikevirus* in HNSC; Figures 2–5) demonstrates that taxa with raw counts of $0$ across virtually all samples were transformed into disjoint numerical clusters separating tumor types or separating tumor from normal tissue. Training Poore et al.'s gradient-boosting pipeline on a $12,803 \times 66$ submatrix where every single raw read count was $0$ yielded a median sensitivity of $0.94$ and median specificity of $0.90$ across 32 cancer types, matching or exceeding the original published AUCs in 14 of 32 cancer types (Figure 6).

* **Follow-up studies that trained prognostic or diagnostic models on the published Poore et al. raw or Voom-SNM matrices are invalidated by these upstream artifacts: [Well Supported].**  
  The authors document over a dozen downstream studies (references 19–30) that directly ingested the Voom-SNM normalized matrix or contaminated raw count matrix, resulting in prognostic signatures driven by deep-sea hydrothermal vent archaea (*Methanothermus*) and extremophiles (*Halonatronum*, *Salinarchaeum*) that have zero genuine reads in human breast tumors.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Explain the Exact Algebraic Mechanism of the Voom-SNM Zero-Count Tagging Artifact
* **WHY:** On page 9, the authors state: *"Note that we do not know precisely where Poore et al. went wrong in applying the normalization code, but because we have the original read count data and the resulting normalized data, we know that the transformation created the artificial signals that we describe here."* Pinpointing the exact mathematical step inside `Voom` and `SNM` that turns a matrix of zeros into group-specific tags will help the bioinformatics community avoid repeating this error.
* **WHAT:** Add a brief paragraph in the Results or Discussion explaining how `limma::voom` pseudocount log-library-size scaling interacts with `snm` covariate subtraction on sparse count matrices.
* **HOW:** Note that `limma::voom` transforms a raw count $y_{gi} = 0$ into $\log_2\text{CPM}_{gi} = \log_2\!\left(\frac{0 + 0.5}{L_i + 1}\times 10^6\right) = 18.93 - \log_2(L_i + 1)$, which replaces every zero with a deterministic function of sample $i$'s total microbial library size $L_i$ (or TMM-scaled library size). When `snm` subsequently fits a supervised linear model preserving `sample_type` (tumor vs. normal vs. blood) while subtracting batch covariates (`sequencing_center`, `platform`, `experimental_strategy`, and the 191 `tissue_source_site` levels, which are heavily confounded with cancer type and sample type in TCGA), the fitted covariate shifts for each center, tissue source site, and sample type are subtracted from $- \log_2(L_i + 1)$, directly stamping every zero-count cell with a linear combination of the sample's metadata design row.

#### 2. Reconcile Numerical Discrepancies Between Table 1 and the Main Text
* **WHY:** There are two numerical mismatches between Table 1 and the first paragraph of the Results ("Filtered 'non-human' reads contained millions of human reads") that could confuse readers checking the arithmetic.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Page 3 (Table 1 vs. Line 55):** Table 1 reports `Initially unmapped reads` for BRCA as `1,532,210,153 (0.5%)` out of `324,824,097,837` total reads ($1.53 \times 10^9 / 3.25 \times 10^{11} = 0.47\%$). However, the text immediately below Table 1 states: *"In the original data sets, the proportion of reads that originally did not map to the human genome was 2.4%, 1.4%, and **2.7%**, respectively."* Correct `2.7%` in the text to `0.5%` (or clarify if `2.7%` referred to a different subset).
  * **Page 3 (Line 55):** The text states that $981,451,972$ human reads across the BLCA dataset *"equates to an average number of human reads per sample of 1.39 million, 1.55 million, and 3.3 million in the BLCA, HNSC, and BRCA data sets, respectively."* Dividing $981,451,972$ by the $683$ downloaded BLCA samples in Table 1 yields **$1.44\text{ million}$** reads per sample ($1,436,972$), whereas $519,222,095 / 334 = 1.55\text{ million}$ and $785,947,157 / 238 = 3.30\text{ million}$. Update `1.39 million` to `1.44 million` (or state the exact denominator of samples used if $>683$).

#### 3. Clarify the Exclusion of 121 Blood-Derived Normal WGS Samples in the BLCA Re-analysis
* **WHY:** Table 1 lists $277$ downloaded WGS samples for BLCA, $334$ WGS samples for HNSC, and $238$ WGS samples for BRCA. However, the KrakenUniq re-analysis in Figure 1 and Table 2 analyzes all $334$ HNSC WGS samples (including $140$ blood-derived normals) and all $238$ BRCA WGS samples (including $106$ blood-derived normals), but only **$156$** of the $277$ BLCA WGS samples ($129$ primary tumors and $27$ solid-tissue normals, omitting the remaining $121$ WGS samples).
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state in the Results and Materials and Methods why the remaining $121$ BLCA WGS samples (presumably blood-derived normals) were excluded from Table 2 and Figure 1, or include all $277$ BLCA WGS samples for symmetry with HNSC and BRCA.
* **HOW:** Add a parenthetical explanation in Materials and Methods (Page 11, Paragraph 1) noting that the remaining $121$ BLCA WGS samples were blood-derived normals and either report their re-analyzed counts alongside the $156$ tissue samples or explain why BLCA was restricted to solid tissue while HNSC and BRCA included blood-derived normals.

#### 4. Add a Nuanced Qualification Regarding Genuine Mucosal/Viral Taxa in Raw Unnormalized Counts
* **WHY:** While the pan-cancer ML classifiers and >95% of raw read counts in Poore et al. are invalid, the Importance section states broadly that *"The original report of a cancer microbiome and more than a dozen follow-up studies are, therefore, likely to be invalid."* In the authors' own curated re-analysis of HNSC (Table S5), genuine oral commensals/pathobionts such as *Fusobacterium* and *Prevotella* still exhibit hundreds to thousands of true bacterial reads in mucosal head-and-neck tumors.
* **WHAT:** Add one sentence in the Discussion distinguishing between the invalidated pan-cancer ML classifiers/inflated draft-genome counts and the genuine presence of expected mucosal bacteria and tumor viruses in unsterile mucosal primary tumors (e.g., oral/GI tracts) visible in Tables S2, S5, and S8–S10.
* **HOW:** Note in the Discussion that while the re-analyzed tables (Tables S8–S10) confirm expected mucosal taxa in non-sterile sites like HNSC at much lower read depths, these genuine local signals neither support a universal pan-cancer microbiome across sterile internal organs nor rescue any classifiers built on the corrupted Voom-SNM data.
