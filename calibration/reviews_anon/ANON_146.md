### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript re-analyzes a previously published integrated colorectal cancer (CRC) single-cell RNA-seq atlas (306 samples—204 tumor core and 102 adjacent normal—comprising 448,255 cells after QC from Chu et al.) alongside two public bulk transcriptomic cohorts (TCGA-COAD, $n = 351$; GSE17536, $n = 177$) to construct a fibroblast-derived prognostic signature. Using `Seurat` (v4.0) and `CellChat`, the authors report that fibroblasts and endothelial cells exhibit the highest intercellular interaction counts and weights in CRC tumors, identify 435 fibroblast-specific marker (FSM) genes ($\log\text{FC} \ge 2$, $\text{min.pct} \ge 0.25$, $\text{FDR} < 0.05$), filter them to 23 univariate Cox survival genes in TCGA-COAD, and evaluate 101 machine-learning algorithm combinations (`Mime1`) to select a 7-gene Lasso + forward stepwise Cox Fibroblast-Related Signature (FRS: *CSRP2*, *DBN1*, *FSTL3*, *GPX3*, *PAM*, *RGS16*, and *CXCL14*). The 7-gene FRS yields C-indices of 0.65 in TCGA-COAD and 0.63 in GSE17536, is combined with clinical variables into a nomogram (C-index = 0.81), and is correlated with bulk immune deconvolution (`CIBERSORT`, `ESTIMATE`, `TIDE`) and imputed drug sensitivity (`DSigDB`, `oncoPredict`). All analyses rely on public datasets, but custom scripts and exact model weights are only available "by contacting the corresponding author."

While integrating single-cell cell-type markers with bulk survival modeling is a popular bioinformatic workflow, this study suffers from multiple methodological flaws, circular stromal confounding, and internal inconsistencies that undermine its substantive conclusions: (1) validation set leakage during model selection—the authors explicitly rank and select the optimal machine-learning model based on its C-index in the external validation cohort GSE17536 (Methods line 33, Results line 89, Figure 4A), invalidating GSE17536 as an independent test set; (2) severe confounding by bulk tumor purity—because the 7 FRS genes are fibroblast-specific markers ($\log\text{FC} \ge 2$ in fibroblasts), their bulk RNA-seq expression predominantly measures the stromal fraction of the biopsy, making the downstream findings that "high-FRS tumors have higher `ESTIMATE` stromal scores, lower tumor purity, and higher `TIDE` CAF-driven T-cell exclusion scores" (Figure 7A, 7D–E) mathematically tautological; and (3) misapplication of `oncoPredict` (which is trained on epithelial cancer cell lines devoid of fibroblasts) to predict stromal-gene-driven chemotherapy sensitivity, compounded by an implausible DSigDB odds ratio of $109,648$ for estradiol in Table 1. Re-analysis with strict training/test separation and purity adjustment is necessary.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Single-cell intercellular communication analysis (`CellChat`) identifies fibroblasts and endothelial cells as the dominant signaling hubs in CRC tumor cores compared to adjacent normal mucosa: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The `CellChat` ligand-receptor network comparison across 204 tumor and 102 normal samples (Figure 3) shows elevated interaction counts and weights for fibroblasts. However, `CellChat` interaction counts are heavily biased toward extracellular matrix (ECM)–integrin and collagen–receptor pairs, which are curated at high multiplicity in the database and naturally dominate stromal cells unless normalized for pathway size and cell-type capture proportions across the 15 pooled cohorts.

* **The 7-gene machine-learning Fibroblast-Related Signature (FRS) is an independently validated, robust predictor of overall survival in CRC (C-index = 0.65 in TCGA-COAD, 0.63 in GSE17536): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, in both the Methods (line 33: *"Top-performing combinations were selected based on their validation set C-index"*) and Results (line 89: *"All models were ranked based on their C-indices in the validation set"*), the external cohort GSE17536 was directly used to choose the model architecture and feature subset among 101 candidates, constituting test-set leakage. Second, a C-index of 0.63–0.65 represents modest discrimination. Third, there is an internal contradiction in the Results (line 89) versus Figure 4 caption (line 95): the text claims the 1-, 3-, and 5-year ROC curves measure *"progression-free survival"*, whereas the Figure 4 caption and Methods state they measure *"overall survival (OS)"*.

* **Combining FRS with clinical variables in a multivariate Cox nomogram achieves high prognostic accuracy (C-index = 0.81) and outperforms clinical staging alone: [Overstated].**  
  In Figure 5A, the univariate ROC curves in GSE17536 show that Stage alone has a lower single-timepoint AUC only if evaluated crudely, while the nomogram's jump to C-index = 0.81 is overwhelmingly driven by TNM Stage and Age rather than the FRS (which alone has C-index = 0.63). Crucially, the manuscript never reports the C-index of the baseline clinical model (`Age + Gender + Grade + Stage` *without* FRS) to demonstrate whether adding FRS yields a statistically significant $\Delta\text{C-index}$ or net reclassification improvement over TNM staging.

* **High-FRS patients exhibit an immunosuppressive microenvironment with elevated stromal/immune infiltration, impaired adaptive immunity, and resistance to immunotherapy (`TIDE` exclusion/dysfunction): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  This conclusion is an artifact of circular bulk-tissue confounding. The 7 FRS genes were selected specifically because they are highly expressed in fibroblasts ($\log\text{FC} \ge 2$). In bulk tumor RNA-seq (TCGA-COAD), a high FRS score simply indicates a biopsy with a high proportion of cancer-associated fibroblasts (low tumor purity), which is confirmed by the `ESTIMATE` heatmap in Figure 7A showing high StromalScore and low TumorPurity in the high-risk group. Similarly, the `TIDE` T-cell exclusion score is computed directly from the correlation of bulk gene expression with a reference CAF signature (which shares core matrix/stromal genes with the 435 FSMs). Correlating one fibroblast signature with two other stromal/CAF signatures in unpurified bulk RNA-seq is circular.

* **Low-risk FRS patients have heightened sensitivity to camptothecin and irinotecan, whereas high-risk patients are more sensitive to paclitaxel (`DSigDB` and `oncoPredict`): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  `oncoPredict` imputes drug IC50 values in bulk patient tumors using ridge regression trained on GDSC/CTRP *in vitro* 2D epithelial cancer cell lines, which contain zero fibroblasts. Applying a cell-line drug response model to a patient risk score driven by stromal fibroblast abundance conflates stromal contamination in bulk biopsies with intrinsic tumor-cell drug sensitivity. Moreover, in Table 1 (`DSigDB`), the top hit "Estradiol" is reported with an impossible `Odds ratio = 109,648` and `Combined score = 1,173,816` because all 7 out of 7 query genes matched a broad estrogen-responsive gene set (producing a zero denominator in the $2 \times 2$ contingency table odds ratio that was replaced by a software pseudocount artifact).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Validation Set Leakage and Publish the Explicit Linear Risk Score Equation
* **WHY:** Selecting the `Lasso+StepCox[forward]` model after inspecting its performance on GSE17536 violates the independence of the validation cohort. Furthermore, the manuscript never states the exact regression coefficients ($\beta_i$) for the 7 genes (*CSRP2*, *DBN1*, *FSTL3*, *GPX3*, *PAM*, *RGS16*, *CXCL14*), making it impossible for any reader or clinician to compute the FRS on a new patient.
* **WHAT:** Perform all model selection (10-fold CV or LOOCV C-index ranking and parsimony selection) strictly within the TCGA-COAD training cohort ($n = 351$), lock the 7-gene Cox regression coefficients, and evaluate GSE17536 ($n = 177$) once as a blinded external test set.
* **HOW:** Report the explicit linear predictor formula in the Results section:
  $$\text{FRS} = \sum_{i=1}^{7} \beta_i \cdot \text{Expr}_i$$
  with each gene's numerical $\beta_i$ coefficient to 4 decimal places, and re-plot Figure 4A ranked strictly by the TCGA-COAD cross-validation C-index rather than the GSE17536 validation C-index.

#### 2. Benchmark the Nomogram Against a Clinical-Only Baseline and Adjust for Bulk Tumor Purity
* **WHY:** Because the 7 FRS genes are fibroblast markers, bulk FRS is strongly anti-correlated with `ESTIMATE` tumor purity (Figure 7A) and positively correlated with TNM stage (Figure 5C–D). Without comparing `Clinical + FRS` against `Clinical Only` and adjusting for tumor purity or total fibroblast fraction (`MCP-counter`/`EPIC` CAF score), it is unclear whether FRS captures qualitative fibroblast reprogramming or merely total stromal content and advanced stage.
* **WHAT:** Report the C-index and likelihood-ratio test $p$-value comparing `Cox(Stage + Age + Gender + Grade)` vs. `Cox(Stage + Age + Gender + Grade + FRS + ESTIMATE_Purity)` in both TCGA-COAD and GSE17536.
* **HOW:** Using the `survival` and `compareC` packages in R, fit the nested Cox models with and without FRS (and with `ESTIMATE` StromalScore/Purity as covariates) and add a comparison table showing the exact C-index (with 95% CI) of `Clinical Only`, `FRS Only`, and `Clinical + FRS`.

#### 3. Correct the Circularity in `TIDE` / `ESTIMATE` Immune Analyses and `DSigDB` Odds Ratio Artifacts
* **WHY:** The `TIDE` exclusion score uses a reference CAF gene expression profile as one of its three primary pillars, making its positive correlation with a fibroblast marker signature in bulk RNA-seq tautological. Similarly, the `109,648` odds ratio for Estradiol in Table 1 is a division-by-zero pseudocount artifact from Enrichr/DSigDB when 100% of a 7-gene query overlaps a large database term.
* **WHAT:** Evaluate immune infiltration associations after partialing out bulk stromal fraction (`ESTIMATE` StromalScore) or directly within the single-cell dataset (`n = 204` tumor cores) by correlating patient-level pseudobulk fibroblast FRS expression (computed specifically inside the scRNA-seq fibroblast cluster) with actual scRNA-seq T-cell, NK-cell, and macrophage proportions. For Table 1, apply Haldane-Anscombe correction (or Fisher's exact test odds ratio with 95% CI) and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjustment across all tested DSigDB compounds.
* **HOW:** In the 204 scRNA-seq tumor core samples, compute each patient's mean FRS score strictly within annotated fibroblasts (avoiding bulk stromal abundance confounding) and correlate it with the patient's empirical scRNA-seq NK, macrophage, and CD8+ T-cell fractions. In Table 1, replace the raw unadjusted $p$-values and infinite/pseudocount ORs with exact Fisher test ORs and FDR $q$-values.

#### 4. Resolve Statistical and Textual Contradictions Between Methods, Results, and Figures
* **WHY:** Multiple inconsistencies appear between the text and figures:
  1. **Results (line 89) vs. Figure 4 caption (line 95):** Line 89 states the ROC curves predict *"progression-free survival"*, whereas Figure 4D–F and the Methods state *"overall survival (OS)"*.
  2. **Methods (line 53):** States *"the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied for paired group comparisons"*—the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (Mann-Whitney $U$) is for *unpaired* independent groups, whereas the Wilcoxon signed-rank test is for paired data (and high- vs. low-risk patient subgroups are unpaired).
  3. **Methods (line 38) vs. Results (line 111):** The PPI enrichment analysis on 7 genes plus STRING neighbors (`FRSR genes`) reports top GO terms at the root of the ontology (`cellular process GO:0009987`, `cellular anatomical entity GO:0110165`, `binding GO:0005488`), which are generic level-1/level-2 ontology categories shared by >80% of all human proteins and provide no biological specificity.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Change *"progression-free survival"* on line 89 to *"overall survival"*, change *"paired group comparisons"* on line 53 to *"unpaired two-group comparisons"*, and deposit the complete R analysis scripts and Seurat/Cox intermediate objects on GitHub or Figshare/Zenodo rather than requiring email requests.
