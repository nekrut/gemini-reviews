# Forensic Peer Review: `ANON_20`

## 1. Executive Assessment & Calibration

**Overall Score:** **9.1 / 10.0** (Exemplary)
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 3.2/3.5 | Claims: 2.3/2.5 | Biology: 1.9/2.0 | Reproducibility: 1.7/2.0

**Synthesis:** This manuscript develops a rigorous mathematical and statistical genetics framework for detecting polygenic adaptation across multiple populations by combining GWAS effect-size estimates with population-level allele frequencies under a neutral multivariate normal (MVN) genetic drift model. By decomposing the quadratic overdispersion statistic $Q_X$ into an $F_{ST}$-like single-locus differentiation component and an LD-like cross-locus allelic covariance component—and validating the framework via explicit perturbation simulations on the Human Genome Diversity Panel (HGDP)—the authors demonstrate that cross-locus directional covariance drives virtually all statistical power to detect polygenic selection. The biological interpretation and discussion of confounding factors (uncorrected GWAS stratification, cross-population LD decay, background selection, and countergradient G$\times$E variation) are exemplary. Only minor technical issues require attention: (1) clarifying the dependence of unweighted Cholesky-whitened Pearson/Spearman environmental correlations on population ordering and the dropped reference population (unlike the invariant quadratic form $Q_X$), and (2) restoring dozens of inline mathematical symbols and bolded statistically significant cells in Tables 2 and 3 that were dropped during manuscript text rendering.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Audit

* **Claim 1:** "Under neutral genetic drift, the vector of population mean genetic values $\vec{Z} = 2\sum_{l=1}^L \alpha_l \vec{p}_l$ follows a multivariate normal distribution $\vec{Z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu\vec{1}, 2V_A\mathbf{F})$, and the whitened quadratic form $Q_X = \vec{X}^T\vec{X} = \frac{(\vec{Z} - \mu)^T \mathbf{F}^{-1} (\vec{Z} - \mu)}{2V_A}$ follows a $\chi^2_{M-1}$ distribution when $\mathbf{F}$ and $V_A$ are estimated from matched background markers." — **[Well Supported]**
  * **Audit:** The derivation from centered allele frequencies, the projection onto $M-1$ independent contrasts via $\mathbf{T}$ to remove the linear dependency induced by sample mean centering ($\mu = \bar{Z}$), and the validation of the $\chi^2_{M-1}$ null distribution using resampled sets of SNPs matched on GWAS ascertainment MAF, imputation status, and background selection $B$-values (Figure 1) are mathematically sound and empirically well calibrated.

* **Claim 2:** "Decomposing $Q_X$ into an $F_{ST}$-like variance component and an LD-like cross-locus covariance component (Equation 12) demonstrates that virtually all power to detect polygenic adaptation arises from cross-locus covariance rather than single-locus $F_{ST}$ outliers, and power depends on total variance explained rather than the number of causal loci $L$." — **[Well Supported]**
  * **Audit:** Equations (12)–(14) and the simulation experiments in Figure 2A–D provide a decisive theoretical and empirical demonstration. When $L$ loci explain a fixed proportion of additive variance, increasing $L$ dilutes the per-locus frequency shift $\delta_l \propto L^{-1/2}$ so the $F_{ST}$-like sum of squares remains indistinguishable from neutral drift, whereas the $L(L-1)$ cross-locus covariance terms accumulate coherently when allele frequency shifts align with trait effect signs $\alpha_l$.

* **Claim 3:** "After Cholesky whitening ($\vec{X} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{T}\vec{Z}$ and $\vec{Y} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{T}\vec{E}$), the linear environmental regression statistics ($\hat{\beta}, r^2$) and Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$) are invariant to which of the $M$ populations is dropped in the $(M-1) \times M$ contrast matrix $\mathbf{T}$." — **[Moderately Supported]**
  * **Audit:** On page 5 (`Linear model handshake`) and page 13 (`Dropping One Population`), the manuscript states that the choice of which population is dropped in $\mathbf{T}$ is completely arbitrary and has no impact on $Q_X$ or the linear environmental framework. While this invariance holds strictly for the quadratic form $Q_X = (\mathbf{T}\vec{Z})^T (\mathbf{T}\mathbf{F}\mathbf{T}^T)^{-1} (\mathbf{T}\vec{Z})$ and for the generalized least squares (GLS) regression estimator $\hat{\beta}_{\text{GLS}} = (\vec{Y}^T \vec{Y})^{-1} \vec{Y}^T \vec{X}$ (since $\vec{Y}^T\vec{X} = (\mathbf{T}\vec{E})^T (\mathbf{T}\mathbf{F}\mathbf{T}^T)^{-1} (\mathbf{T}\vec{Z})$ is invariant to the choice of full-rank contrast basis $\mathbf{T}$ provided $\mathbf{T}$ spans the orthogonal complement of the null space of $\mathbf{F}$), two subtleties should be clarified:
    1. **Spearman rank correlation $\rho(\vec{X}, \vec{Y})$ is NOT invariant to population ordering or the dropped population:** Because $\mathbf{C}$ is a lower-triangular Cholesky factor of $\mathbf{T}\mathbf{F}\mathbf{T}^T$, the individual entries of $\vec{X} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{T}\vec{Z}$ represent sequential Gram-Schmidt conditional residuals depending on the ordering of the $M-1$ populations. Permuting the order of populations or changing which population is dropped rotates $\vec{X}$ and $\vec{Y}$ by an orthogonal matrix $\mathbf{Q}$, which preserves inner products ($\vec{X}^T\vec{X}, \vec{Y}^T\vec{X}, r^2, \hat{\beta}$) but changes the individual component values and therefore changes Spearman's rank correlation $\rho(\vec{X}, \vec{Y})$.
    2. **Centering weight vector:** Using the unweighted sample mean $\mu = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M Z_m$ in $\mathbf{T}$ rather than the GLS precision-weighted ancestral mean $\hat{\mu}_{\text{GLS}} = \frac{\vec{1}^T \mathbf{F}^{-1} \vec{Z}}{\vec{1}^T \mathbf{F}^{-1} \vec{1}}$ slightly affects the projection if $\mathbf{F}$ has unequal row sums, though asLong as the exact same matrix $\mathbf{T}$ is applied everywhere, $\mathbf{T}\mathbf{F}\mathbf{T}^T$ remains exact for $\mathbf{T}\vec{Z}$.

* **Claim 4:** "Empirical application across 52 HGDP populations and 6 human traits identifies strong global and regional polygenic adaptation signals for height ($Q_X$, European conditional divergence) and skin pigmentation ($Q_X$, latitude correlation, and conditional divergence in Africa, the Middle East, and Central Asia), alongside localized signals for BMI and IBD." — **[Well Supported]**
  * **Audit:** The empirical signals for height (driven almost entirely by the cross-locus LD component rather than the $F_{ST}$ component, Figure 3B) and skin pigmentation (driven by large-effect loci showing both $F_{ST}$ and LD components, Figure 4B) are thoroughly cross-checked against matched null SNP distributions and conditional MVN outlier expectations (Equation 18). Furthermore, the authors' discussion of how subtle uncorrected European GWAS stratification (e.g., North-South stratification in GIANT) can mimic polygenic selection across target populations is remarkably prescient and balanced.

## 3. Concrete Suggestions for Improvement

1. **Clarify Basis/Ordering Dependence of Spearman's $\rho$ on Cholesky-Whitened Vectors (`Claim 3`):**
   * Note in the Methods (`Alternative Env Framework` and `Dropping One Population`) that while the inner-product statistics $Q_X$, $\hat{\beta} = (\vec{Y}^T\vec{Y})^{-1}\vec{Y}^T\vec{X}$, and Pearson $r^2 = \frac{(\vec{Y}^T\vec{X})^2}{(\vec{Y}^T\vec{Y})(\vec{X}^T\vec{X})}$ are invariant to orthogonal rotations of the whitened space (and thus invariant to population ordering in the Cholesky factorization), the individual coordinate values of $\vec{X} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{T}\vec{Z}$ and $\vec{Y} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{T}\vec{E}$—and therefore Spearman's rank correlation $\rho(\vec{X}, \vec{Y})$ and the scatterplots of $X_k$ vs. $Y_k$—depend on the sequential ordering of populations in the Cholesky decomposition. Alternatively, use the symmetric inverse square-root whitening matrix $(\mathbf{T}\mathbf{F}\mathbf{T}^T)^{-1/2}$ via eigendecomposition so that whitening does not depend on an arbitrary sequential population ordering.

2. **Restore Missing Mathematical Symbols and Bolded Significant Cells in Tables 2 and 3:**
   * During XML/LaTeX-to-text rendering of the manuscript, every bolded (statistically significant) cell in **Table 2** and **Table 3** and dozens of inline mathematical symbols were stripped out (leaving blank entries in the tables and missing variables in the prose):
     * **Table 2 (page 7):** The statistically significant entries for `Height` (`WINPC2` and `Q_X` $p$-values), `Skin Pigmentation` (`Latitude`, `SUMPC1`, `WINPC1`, and `Q_X` $p$-values), `Crohn's Disease` (`SUMPC2` $p$-values), and `Ulcerative Colitis` (`Latitude` and `SUMPC2` $p$-values) are blank in the rendered table and must be restored.
     * **Table 3 (page 8):** The statistically significant regional $Q_X$ $p$-values for `Height` (`Europe`), `Skin Pigmentation` (`Middle East`, `Central Asia`, `Africa`), `BMI` (`East Asia`, `Americas`), `Type 2 Diabetes` (`Europe`, `East Asia`), and `Ulcerative Colitis` (`Europe`) are blank in the rendered table and must be restored.
     * **Inline mathematical symbols (pages 3–14):** Restore dropped inline variables such as $2V_A\mathbf{F}$ (page 3), $Q_X$ (pages 5–11), $Z$-scores (`French`, `Basque`, `San`, `Mandenka`, `Yoruba`, `Mozabite`, `Sardinian` on pages 6–10), and index limits in the Methods (pages 11–14).

3. **Provide False Discovery Rate ($q$-value) Annotations Alongside Raw $p$-values in Tables 2 and 3 (`Claim 4`):**
   * Because Table 2 evaluates 6 traits across 5 environmental axes plus global $Q_X$ (36 tests) and Table 3 evaluates 6 traits across 7 geographic regions (42 tests), report Benjamini-Hochberg FDR $q$-values alongside the unadjusted $p$-values so readers can immediately distinguish signals that survive family-wise/FDR correction (e.g., global $Q_X$ for height and skin pigmentation, latitude for skin pigmentation, and regional outliers) from nominal $0.01 < p < 0.05$ exploratory signals.
