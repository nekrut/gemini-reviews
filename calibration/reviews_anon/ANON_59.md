### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript analyzes the evolutionary rate heterogeneity and genome-wide selective constraints of SARS-CoV-2 across ~12 million sequences deposited in GISAID (`EPI_SET_220812zf`) and INSDC over the first 2.5 years of the pandemic (up to July 2022). Using `Nextclade` to partition sequences into 18 major Nextstrain clades (plus 3 composite clades `19B+`, `19B++`, and `20A+`) that circulated for at least 6 months, the author filters out recombinants, incomplete/back-filled genomes lacking clade-defining founder mutations, low-quality assemblies ($\text{QC} > 30$), and temporal outliers ($>2\sigma + 3$ mutations). Using weekly binned weighted linear regressions ($N_{\text{seq}}^{1/4}$ weighting) and an analytical supercritical branching-process model with Poisson mutation accumulation (Equation 1), the study separates within-clade evolutionary rates from the inter-clade ("backbone") evolutionary rate connecting clade founders. The synonymous substitution rate is shown to be nearly invariant both within clades ($\sim 5\text{–}8$ changes/genome/year) and between clades ($7.3$ changes/genome/year, or $7.5 \times 10^{-4}$ per year and codon). In contrast, the inter-clade non-synonymous rate ($\sim 26$ amino acid changes/year, or $2.7 \times 10^{-3}$ per year and codon) is roughly three- to four-fold higher than the post-2020 within-clade non-synonymous rate ($3\text{–}9$ changes/year), which itself declined from higher initial rates in late 2019/early 2020 clades (`19A`, `19B`: $\sim 10\text{–}15$ changes/year). Finally, by aggregating non-singleton within-Pango-lineage rare mutations (`unlabeledSubstitutions` and `reversionSubstitutions`) normalized by nucleotide mutation spectra, the author constructs a genome-wide map of short-term mutational tolerance, showing strong purifying constraint in `ORF1ab`, `S`, `M`, and `E` (~50% of 1st/2nd codon positions more constrained than the bottom 10% of 3rd positions) and relaxed constraint in accessory genes (`ORF3a`, `ORF6`, `ORF7a`, `ORF7b`, `ORF8`). A complete Snakemake workflow and processed rate/tolerance tables are publicly available on GitHub (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`) alongside open Nextstrain/INSDC files.

This is a rigorous, transparent, and well-calibrated study that resolves how saltatory adaptive bursts and within-lineage evolution jointly shape the SARS-CoV-2 molecular clock without requiring massive phylogenetic downsampling. The author proactively tests for conditioning artifacts using composite clades (`19B+`, `19B++`, `20A+`) and candidly discusses the limitations of root-to-tip regressions when basal polytomies or selective sweeps violate branching-process independence (e.g., in Omicron `21L`, `22A`, `22B`). The only notable methodological limitation is the absence of uncertainty intervals (e.g., block-bootstrap or window-sensitivity bounds) around the point estimates in Table 1 and Figures 3D/4, alongside minor table/text formatting items noted below.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Synonymous evolutionary rates are stable across time and consistent between within-clade ($\sim 6$ changes/year) and inter-clade ($7.3$ changes/year) scales, whereas inter-clade non-synonymous evolution ($\sim 26$ changes/year) is strongly accelerated relative to within-clade evolution (Results lines 63–103, Table 1, Figures 3–4): [Well Supported].**  
  The invariance of synonymous rates across 18 clades and between clade founders acts as an internal negative control that rules out changes in viral polymerase fidelity, generation time, or global sequencing error rates as drivers of the non-synonymous rate disparity. The 3- to 4-fold excess of amino acid substitutions along the inter-clade backbone directly demonstrates that variant emergence is driven by positive selection rather than neutral drift.

* **Within-clade non-synonymous rates were higher in the earliest pandemic clades (`19A`, `19B`, `20C`: $9.9\text{–}12.8$ aa changes/year) than in clades circulating in 2021–2022 ($3\text{–}9$ aa changes/year), driven primarily by `ORF1ab` rather than `Spike` (Results line 103, Discussion lines 141–143, Figure 4, Supplementary Figure S1): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Because all clades are analyzed over a matched 6-month window, incomplete purifying selection affects early and late clades symmetrically and cannot easily explain the 2-fold drop in within-clade non-synonymous rates. However, as the author appropriately notes in the Discussion (line 143), the elevated initial rate is concentrated in `19A` and `19B` (while later clades such as `20H [Beta]` at $9.51$/yr and `21H [Mu]` at $10.05$/yr overlap with `20C` at $9.92$/yr), and Table 1 provides no confidence intervals or standard errors on individual clade slopes to formally test the temporal trend.

* **A supercritical branching process with Poisson mutation accumulation accurately describes early intra-clade diversification when a single founder expands without strong sublineage sweeps (Results lines 45–62, Equation 1, Figure 2F): [Well Supported].**  
  The analytical derivation of the genotype class dynamics $m_i(t) = e^{(\alpha - u)t} \frac{(ut)^i}{i!}$ matches the empirical progression of 0-, 1-, 2-, and 3-mutation genotypes in well-sampled clades such as `20C` (Figure 2F), and the author clearly delineates the regimes where this approximation breaks down (early stochastic subclade dominance or within-clade selective sweeps such as `S:R346K` in `21K`).

* **Short-term mutational tolerance across Pango lineages reveals strong purifying selection at 1st/2nd codon positions in structural/replicase genes (`ORF1b`, `M`, `E`, `S`, `N`) and minimal constraint in accessory ORFs (`ORF3a`, `ORF6`, `ORF7a`, `ORF7b`, `ORF8`) (Results lines 116–134, Figures 5–6): [Well Supported].**  
  Normalizing the fraction of Pango lineages carrying non-singleton rare mutations by the empirical nucleotide mutation spectrum cleanly separates codon positions 1 and 2 from position 3, recapitulates known RNA structural conservation (such as the `ORF1b` ribosomal frameshift site and codon 35 of `E`), and aligns with independent deep mutational scanning and comparative coronavirus constraint maps.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Quantify Estimation Uncertainty and Sensitivity Bounds for Clade Evolutionary Rates in Table 1 and Figures 3D/4
* **WHY:** As noted in line 43, ordinary least-squares standard errors from root-to-tip regressions underestimate uncertainty because sequences share phylogenetic ancestry along basal sublineages. However, reporting point estimates alone in Table 1 and Figures 3D/4 leaves readers unable to gauge the precision of individual clade slopes (for example, whether `21H` at $10.05$ aa/yr or `22A` at $2.81$ aa/yr have wide or narrow uncertainty).
* **WHAT:** Add empirical uncertainty intervals to Table 1 and error bars to Figures 3D and 4 using subclade/country block-bootstrapping and window-length sensitivity analysis.
* **HOW:** Within each Nextstrain clade in the Snakemake workflow, group sequences by their immediate basal sub-lineage (e.g., by distinct 1-mutation descendant branches off the clade founder or by country-month strata), perform 200 block-bootstrap resamples of those semi-independent clusters, and recompute the weighted linear regression slope. Additionally, evaluate the slopes across 4-, 6-, and 8-month cutoffs and with $N^{1/2}$ vs. $N^{1/4}$ bin weights, and report the resulting 95% block-bootstrap/sensitivity intervals in Table 1 and Figure 4.

#### 2. Assess the Impact of Strict Founder-Mutation Filtering on Apparent Rates in Highly Diverged Omicron Clades
* **WHY:** In line 31, any sequence lacking the *complete* set of clade-defining founder mutations relative to Wuhan-Hu-1 is excluded to guard against reference back-filling of amplicon dropouts. While a clade with 6–9 founder mutations (`20A`–`20C`) rarely suffers true reversions or single-site dropouts, Omicron clades (`21K`, `21L`, `22A`, `22B`) carry 56 to 71 founder mutations (Table 1). Requiring 100% presence of all ~68 founder mutations systematically discards sequences that acquire genuine secondary reversions (or have a single N/dropout at any of the 68 sites) as time progresses, which may artificially depress the apparent within-clade rate in `21L` ($9.24$/yr) and `22A` ($7.71$/yr).
* **WHAT:** Perform a sensitivity check on `21K`, `21L`, `22A`, and `22B` allowing 1–2 missing/reverted clade-defining sites (while requiring high overall sequence coverage) to quantify how much of the low Omicron rate estimate is driven by strict founder-site conditioning.
* **HOW:** Modify the founder filter in the Snakemake script to allow up to 2 reversions at clade-defining sites (excluding known amplicon-dropout-prone primer regions) for clades with $>30$ founder mutations, and report in Section "Results" (line 113) whether the `21L` and `22A` rate estimates increase under relaxed founder matching.
