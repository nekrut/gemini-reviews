### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript assembles genome-wide ancient DNA (aDNA) capture data (`1240k` panel targeting 1,237,207 SNPs, including 47,384 candidate functional variants) from 230 West Eurasian individuals dated between 6500 and 1000 BCE—including 163 newly reported datasets (83 newly analyzed individuals, 67 upgraded from 390k to 1240k capture, and 13 shotgun samples enriched via capture)—to investigate Holocene population transformations and perform a direct genome-wide scan for natural selection across time. Key demographic findings include the first genome-wide profile of 26 Anatolian Neolithic individuals (primarily extracted from the petrous pyramid at Barcın), establishing them as the direct source of Early European Farmers (EEF) with 7%–11% additional Western Hunter-Gatherer (WHG) admixture acquired during European expansion ($|Z| > 6$), and a 37-sample Samara steppe transect (5600–300 BCE) revealing two distinct waves of Southern/farming gene flow into steppe pastoralists. By modeling allele frequencies across four present-day European 1000 Genomes cohorts (CEU, GBR, IBS, TSI) as linear mixtures of three ancestral sources (Hunter-Gatherers, Early Farmers, and Steppe Ancestry) and applying a genomic-control-corrected ($\lambda = 1.38$) likelihood-ratio test across 1,084,781 autosomal SNPs, the authors identify 12 genome-wide significant selection loci ($p < 5 \times 10^{-8}$ with $\ge 3$ significant SNPs within 1 Mb) governing diet (*LCT*, *FADS1/2*, *DHCR7*), pigmentation (*SLC45A2*, *GRM5*, *HERC2/OCA2*), and immunity (*TLR1/6/10*, *MHC*, *SLC22A4*, *ATXN2/SH2B3*), alongside polygenic selection on standing height ($Q_X$ test, $p = 0.002$). Sequence alignments are deposited in ENA (PRJEB11450), genotype datasets on the lab website, and selection scan code on GitHub (`mathii/europe_selection`).

This is a landmark palaeogenomic study that transitions ancient DNA from descriptive demographic history to quantitative evolutionary genetics, supported by a well-formulated read-count binomial likelihood model and explicit power and misspecification simulations. Two main methodological caveats warrant attention: (1) the single-locus selection scan experiences substantial genome-wide test-statistic inflation ($\lambda = 1.38$, rising to $\lambda = 2.07$ in the MHC), and dividing all $\chi^2_4$ statistics by a single scalar $\lambda$ assumes uniform drift variance across the allele-frequency spectrum and across the four modern target populations, which is approximate when IBS and TSI have boundary mixture weights of $0$ for HG; and (2) the polygenic height selection analysis ($Q_X$, $p = 0.002$, pairwise $p = 0.003$ to $0.042$) relies on 169 GWAS-significant height SNPs whose effect sizes were estimated in modern European meta-analyses and is vulnerable to subtle residual population stratification in the discovery GWAS as well as differential capture/imputation bias across low-coverage ancient groups. Both caveats can be addressed through targeted robustness analyses.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Anatolian Neolithic farmers were the primary genetic source of Europe's first farmers (EEF), who subsequently absorbed 7%–11% Western Hunter-Gatherer (WHG) ancestry during their expansion across Europe: [Well Supported].**  
  Supported by petrous-derived genome-wide data from 26 Anatolian Neolithic individuals clustering tightly with German (LBK), Hungarian, and Iberian Early Neolithic farmers in PCA and ADMIXTURE, low pairwise $F_{ST}$ ($0.005\text{–}0.016$), shared Y-chromosome haplogroup G2a (47%), and highly significant $f_4$ and $f_3$ statistics ($|Z| = 6.8\text{ to }11.9$, Extended Data Table 2).

* **Eurasian steppe populations in the Samara transect experienced at least two distinct episodes of external admixture—an Eneolithic/Early Bronze Age dilution of EHG by Armenian-like Near Eastern ancestry to form Yamnaya/Poltavka, followed by a Late Bronze Age influx of ~17% EEF/Anatolian-related ancestry in Srubnaya: [Well Supported].**  
  Demonstrated by symmetric $f_4$ and negative $f_3$ tests across the 37-sample Samara time series (Extended Data Table 2, $|Z| = 4.5\text{ to }13.1$) and Y-chromosome turnover from R1b in Yamnaya/Poltavka to R1a-Z93 in Srubnaya.

* **Twelve genomic regions dealing with diet (*LCT*, *FADS1/2*, *DHCR7*), pigmentation (*SLC45A2*, *GRM5*, *HERC2/OCA2*), and immunity (*TLR1/6/10*, *MHC*, *SLC22A4*, *ATXN2/SH2B3*) show genome-wide significant deviations from neutral 3-way ancestral admixture in West Eurasia: [Well Supported].**  
  The read-level genotype likelihood-ratio test properly integrates coverage uncertainty across low-depth aDNA libraries, requires $\ge 3$ SNPs passing $p < 5 \times 10^{-8}$ after $\lambda = 1.38$ genomic control correction within 1 Mb, and is backed by forward Wright-Fisher power and admixture-misspecification simulations (Extended Data Fig. 6). Furthermore, the core top signals (*LCT*, *SLC45A2*, *FADS1*, *TLR1/6/10*, *MHC*) remain genome-wide significant even when restricting the source pools strictly to WHG, Anatolian Neolithic, and Bronze Age Steppe ($\lambda = 1.32$, Extended Data Table 3).

* **Two independent episodes of polygenic selection on height—selection for decreased height in Iberian Neolithic farmers and increased height in Steppe pastoralists—drove the modern European North-South height gradient: [Fragile].**  
  The Berg & Coop $Q_X$ statistic across 169 GIANT-consortium height SNPs yields $p = 0.002$ overall and nominal unadjusted pairwise $p$-values of $p = 0.042$ (AN vs. INC), $p = 0.003$ (CEM vs. INC), and $p = 0.030$ (CEM vs. STP). Because the 169 candidate SNPs are included among the 47,384 "potentially functional" capture baits and carry small effect sizes derived from meta-analyses that were not fully immune to North-South European population structure, even slight systematic stratification in GWAS effect signs correlates with the AN/EEF vs. Steppe axis and can inflate $Q_X$. Moreover, the pairwise $p$-values ($0.030$ and $0.042$) are not corrected for the $\binom{8}{2} = 28$ pairwise comparisons displayed in Figure 4B.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Evaluate Drift-Covariance Null Calibration vs. Scalar Genomic Control ($\lambda = 1.38$) in the Selection Scan
* **WHY:** The genome-wide selection test assumes under $H_0$ that $p_{\text{mod}} = C p_{\text{anc}}$ exactly, attributing all variance between $p_{\text{mod}}$ and $C p_{\text{anc}}$ solely to binomial/read sampling noise in the ancient and modern samples. In reality, genetic drift between the ancient proxy populations and the true admixing populations adds a binomial drift variance proportional to $p(1-p) F_{ST}^{(j)}$ in each modern population $j$, plus covariance across modern populations sharing post-admixture history (e.g., CEU and GBR). Dividing the 4-df LRT statistic by a single scalar $\lambda = 1.38$ only rescales the mean/median inflation without accounting for the correlation among CEU, GBR, IBS, and TSI or boundary effects when $p_{\text{anc}}$ is near 0 or 1.
* **WHAT:** Compare the 4-df scalar-GC-corrected LRT $p$-values against an empirical drift-covariance null model (e.g., modeling $p_{\text{mod}} - C p_{\text{anc}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, p(1-p)\Sigma_{\text{drift}} + \Sigma_{\text{sampling}})$, analogous to `TreeMix`/`f4` covariance) or population-specific genomic control factors.
* **HOW:** Estimate the $4 \times 4$ empirical covariance matrix $\hat{\Sigma}$ of standardized frequency residuals $(p_{\text{mod}} - C \hat{p}_{\text{anc}}) / \sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}$ across the ~1 million neutral autosomal SNPs, compute the Mahalanobis chi-square statistic $T_i = r_i^T (\hat{p}_i(1-\hat{p}_i)\hat{\Sigma} + V_{\text{obs},i})^{-1} r_i$, and report whether all 12 candidate loci retain $p < 5 \times 10^{-8}$.

#### 2. Test the Robustness of the Polygenic Height Selection Signal to GWAS Stratification and Multiple Pairwise Testing
* **WHY:** In Figure 4B, 28 pairwise population comparisons are shown, and the highlighted steppe height selection signal ($p = 0.030$ for STP vs. CEM) and Anatolian-Iberian signal ($p = 0.042$ for AN vs. INC) would not survive Bonferroni or FDR correction across all pairs. Additionally, family-based or within-sibship height effect estimates are needed to verify that the $Q_X$ signal is not driven by uncorrected North-South stratification in the GIANT summary statistics.
* **WHAT:** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction to the pairwise $Q_X$ matrix in Figure 4B, and test whether the directional height differences persist when restricting exclusively to the 1,055,209 neutral array SNPs (for LD/frequency matching) and when using sign-only vs. magnitude-weighted polygenic scores.
* **HOW:** Using the `europe_selection` repository scripts, compute a binomial sign test on the proportion of height-increasing alleles that are at higher frequency in STP vs. CEM and AN vs. INC (which is less sensitive to extreme effect-size weights), and report FDR-adjusted $q$-values alongside the unadjusted pairwise $Z$-scores in Figure 4B.

#### 3. Account for Reference-Allele Mapping Bias in the Read-Level Binomial Likelihood
* **WHY:** In the Methods ("Genome-wide scan for selection", line 174), the heterozygous genotype read likelihood is modeled as $B(R_i, T_i, 0.5)$, assuming an equal $0.5$ probability of observing a reference vs. alternative read in a heterozygote. For short ancient DNA fragments mapped with `bwa` against the human reference genome (`hg19`), reference-allele mapping bias typically shifts the empirical reference read fraction in heterozygotes to $0.51\text{–}0.53$, which can slightly bias maximum-likelihood allele frequency estimates ($\hat{p}_{\text{anc}}$) toward the reference allele in low-coverage ancient groups relative to high-coverage diploid 1000 Genomes calls.
* **WHAT:** Estimate the empirical reference-read proportion $q_{\text{het}}$ at heterozygous sites in the high-coverage ancient individuals (e.g., Loschbour, Stuttgart, and the highest-coverage Barcın petrous samples) and check the sensitivity of the 12 selection peaks when replacing $0.5$ with $q_{\text{het}}$ in the likelihood equation.
* **HOW:** Re-run the numerical MLE optimization on the 12 candidate loci with $B(R_i, T_i, q_{\text{het}})$ for $q_{\text{het}} \in \{0.50, 0.51, 0.52\}$ and confirm that the inferred ancient allele frequency trajectories in Figure 3 and Extended Data Fig. 4B are unchanged.
