### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript applies adaptive laboratory evolution (ALE) to an acid-tolerant thraustochytrid strain, *Aurantiochytrium limacinum* BBF001, to improve cell growth and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) production under combined acidic (pH 4.5) and elevated temperature conditions. Using stepwise thermal escalation from $30^\circ\text{C}$ to $38^\circ\text{C}$ (in $2^\circ\text{C}$ increments every 5 three-day transfers) followed by 30 serial transfer cycles at $34^\circ\text{C}$, the authors isolate an adapted strain, BBF002. In 5-day shake-flask fermentations across $25^\circ\text{C}$, $30^\circ\text{C}$, and $35^\circ\text{C}$ (in triplicate), BBF002 achieves higher dry cell weight (DCW, $23.10\text{ g/L}$) and volumetric DHA titer ($2.59\text{ g/L}$) at $30^\circ\text{C}$ and pH 4.5 than both the native strain (TBRC-BCC55172) and the parental strain BBF001, accompanied by a shift in fatty acid composition toward palmitic acid (C16:0) and lower intracellular DHA content (% DCW). The authors then fit a three-ODE kinetic system—logistic biomass growth, substrate consumption with fixed yield and maintenance, and Luedeking–Piret product formation—to the $30^\circ\text{C}$ fermentation time courses. No raw time-series tables or modeling scripts are deposited in an external repository.

The experimental ALE strain development and fatty acid profiling across temperatures provide useful empirical data for industrial bioprocess design, demonstrating a clear trade-off between thermal biomass accumulation and polyunsaturated fatty acid unsaturation. However, the mathematical and kinetic modeling sections contain several formulation errors, internal parameter contradictions, and non-standard statistical definitions that compromise the quantitative conclusions: (1) the specific growth rate in Section 2.5 (Equation 4) uses a finite-difference midpoint approximation over a 5-day interval (which spans lag, exponential, and stationary phases) rather than the logarithmic definition, producing values ($0.379\text{ d}^{-1}$) that contradict the ODE-fitted $\mu_{\max}$ ($1.617\text{ d}^{-1}$) by more than four-fold; (2) the empirical biomass yield reported for BBF002 at $30^\circ\text{C}$ ($Y_{X/S} = 0.523\text{ g/g}$ from $40\text{ g/L}$ glucose) exceeds both the theoretical stoichiometric yield assumed in the ODE model ($Y_{X/S} = 0.45\text{ g/g}$) and the mass balance of $23.10\text{ g/L}$ DCW unless unmeasured yeast extract/peptone carbon assimilation is accounted for; and (3) the goodness-of-fit $R^2$ definition in Equation 11 is mathematically non-standard and guarantees $R^2 > 0$ even for poor fits. These issues can be resolved through a rigorous mathematical re-analysis of the existing fermentation data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Stepwise ALE at pH 4.5 and $34^\circ\text{C}$ generated an adapted strain (BBF002) with higher biomass ($23.10\text{ g/L}$) and volumetric DHA titer ($2.59\text{ g/L}$) at $30^\circ\text{C}$ than the parental acid-tolerant strain BBF001: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Triplicate shake-flask experiments (Figures 2 and 3) consistently show higher DCW and volumetric DHA titer for BBF002 at $30^\circ\text{C}$. However, there is an internal numerical discrepancy between the Abstract/text (which claims BBF002 biomass and DHA production were higher than BBF001 by `32.95%` and `7.12%` at $30^\circ\text{C}$) and Section 3.4 (line 185), which reports $30^\circ\text{C}$ DHA titers of `2.42–2.59 g/L` for BBF001 and BBF002 ($2.59 / 2.42 = +7.02\%$), while in Section 3.2 (line 167) BBF001 biomass at $30^\circ\text{C}$ is stated to be `22.30–23.70 g/L` (which would make $23.10\text{ g/L}$ nearly identical, whereas $+32.95\%$ implies a BBF001 biomass of $17.37\text{ g/L}$).

* **High-temperature adaptation induces a metabolic trade-off that increases saturated palmitic acid (C16:0) while reducing intracellular DHA percentage in total fatty acids: [Well Supported].**  
  GC-FID FAME profiles across $25^\circ\text{C}$, $30^\circ\text{C}$, and $35^\circ\text{C}$ (Figures 3, 4, and 5) demonstrate that C16:0 rises from 5.83% in the native strain to 36.52%–39.28% in BBF001/BBF002 while the DHA fraction of TFA decreases from 53.19% to ~40%–44%, consistent with biophysical homeoviscous adaptation of membrane lipids under thermal stress.

* **The logistic and Luedeking–Piret kinetic ODE models accurately capture biomass, glucose consumption, and DHA accumulation ($R^2 > 0.87$), showing that BBF002 has higher maintenance energy ($m_S$) and mixed-growth DHA kinetics: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the ODE model fixes the true biomass yield on glucose at $Y_{X/S} = 0.45\text{ g/g}$ (Table 1, line 126), which directly contradicts the empirical yield of $Y_{X/S} = 0.523\text{ g/g}$ reported for BBF002 at $30^\circ\text{C}$ in Section 3.3 (line 174), and the fitted logistic carrying capacity for BBF002 in Table 1 ($C_{Xm} = 19.891\text{ g/L}$) underpredicts the measured 5-day maximum biomass ($23.10\text{ g/L}$) by more than $3.2\text{ g/L}$. Second, in Equation 9 ($dC_S/dt = -(1/Y_{X/S})(dC_X/dt) + m_S C_X$), the maintenance term $+m_S C_X$ has the wrong sign (positive instead of negative; compare Table 1 where parentheses are omitted), and no parameter standard errors or confidence intervals are reported to establish whether $m_S$, $\alpha$, and $\beta$ are identifiable separately from $Y_{X/S}$. Third, Equation 11 defines $R^2 = \text{SSR} / (\text{SSR} + \text{SSE})$ rather than the standard coefficient of determination $1 - \text{SSE}/\text{SST}$, artificially inflating goodness-of-fit values.

* **Temperature variation ($25, 30, \text{and } 35^\circ\text{C}$) had no influence on DHA proportions: [Overstated].**  
  In Section 3.4, line 207 states `"it was found that the effect of temperature variation (25, 30, and 35 °C) had no influence on DHA proportions"`, which directly contradicts line 193 in the exact same section (`"For polyunsaturated fatty acids, the increased culture temperature increased the proportion of DPA but decreased that of DHA in all the strains"`).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Correct the Mathematical Definitions of Specific Growth Rate ($\mu$), Substrate Mass Balance, and $R^2$
* **WHY:** In Section 2.5, Equation 4 calculates the specific growth rate over a 5-day batch window using the linear midpoint formula $\mu = Q_X / [(C_{X,1} + C_{X,2})/2]$, which severely underestimates the exponential growth rate and conflates exponential and stationary phases. Furthermore, in Section 2.6, Equation 9 writes $dC_S/dt = -\frac{1}{Y_{X/S}}\frac{dC_X}{dt} + m_S C_X$ without parentheses around the right-hand sum (implying maintenance *produces* glucose), and Equation 11 defines $R^2 = \sum(C_{\text{cal}} - C_{\text{exp,ave}})^2 / [\sum(C_{\text{cal}} - C_{\text{exp,ave}})^2 + \sum(C_{\text{cal}} - C_{\text{exp}})^2]$, which is not equal to the standard $R^2 = 1 - \text{SSE}/\text{SST}$ for nonlinear models.
* **WHAT:** Replace Equation 4 with the standard exponential-phase regression of $\ln(C_X(t))$, add the missing parentheses (or minus sign) to Equation 9 and Table 1, and recompute goodness of fit using the standard $R^2 = 1 - \sum(C_{\text{exp}} - C_{\text{cal}})^2 / \sum(C_{\text{exp}} - \bar{C}_{\text{exp}})^2$ alongside root-mean-square error (RMSE).
* **HOW:** Write Equation 9 as:
  $$\frac{dC_S}{dt} = -\left(\frac{1}{Y_{X/S}}\frac{dC_X}{dt} + m_S C_X\right)$$
  and report the standard $R^2$ and RMSE separately for each state variable ($C_X$, $C_S$, and $C_P$) in Table 1.

#### 2. Reconcile the Carbon Mass Balance and Uncouple $Y_{X/S}$ from Complex Nitrogen Sources
* **WHY:** The production medium contains $40\text{ g/L}$ glucose plus $10\text{ g/L}$ yeast extract and $10\text{ g/L}$ peptone (Section 2.1). In Section 3.3 (line 174), the authors report for BBF002 at $30^\circ\text{C}$ a glucose consumption rate of $Q_S = 8.595\text{ g/L d}$ over 5 days—which equals $42.98\text{ g/L}$ of consumed glucose (exceeding the nominal $40\text{ g/L}$ initial concentration)—and an empirical yield of $Y_{X/S} = 0.523\text{ g/g}$, while fixing $Y_{X/S} = 0.45\text{ g/g}$ in the kinetic model (Section 2.6). Because cells assimilate carbon from yeast extract and peptone (20 g/L total) and lipid-rich biomass ($33\%$ TFA) has a higher degree of reduction than standard $\text{CH}_{1.79}\text{O}_{0.5}\text{N}_{0.2}$ biomass, fixing $Y_{X/S} = 0.45\text{ g/g}$ forces the maintenance parameter $m_S$ to absorb stoichiometric mismatch.
* **WHAT:** Report the actual measured initial glucose concentration ($C_{S,1}$ at $t = 0$) after autoclaving/inoculation, and either fit $Y_{X/S}$ simultaneously with $m_S$ (reporting profile likelihoods or standard errors) or include an explicit product-formation term $-\frac{1}{Y_{P/S}}\frac{dC_P}{dt}$ in the substrate consumption ODE.
* **HOW:** In Berkeley Madonna (or Python/R nonlinear least-squares), fit the coupled ODE system both with fixed $Y_{X/S}$ and free $Y_{X/S}$, report $\pm \text{SE}$ for all estimated parameters ($\mu_{\max}, C_{Xm}, Y_{X/S}, m_S, \alpha, \beta$) in Table 1, and verify why $C_{Xm} = 19.891\text{ g/L}$ in Table 1 differs from the $23.10\text{ g/L}$ DCW reported in Section 3.3.

#### 3. Resolve Numerical Contradictions Across Sections for BBF001 Biomass and Temperature Trends
* **WHY:** Several quantitative statements in the manuscript contradict each other:
  1. In Section 3.2 (line 167), BBF001 biomass at $30^\circ\text{C}$ is described as `"comparable to that of the parental strain at 22.30–23.70 g/L"`, whereas in the Abstract (line 8) and Section 3.4 (line 209), BBF002 ($23.10\text{ g/L}$) is stated to be `32.95%` higher than BBF001 (which requires BBF001 to be $17.37\text{ g/L}$).
  2. In Section 3.4 (line 209), the text states `"which were 32.95% and 7.12% of the biomass and DHA titers of the parental strain (BBF001)"`—omitting the word `"higher than"` (stating $32.95\%$ *of* the parental strain means a $67\%$ reduction).
  3. In Section 3.4, line 193 states that increased culture temperature decreased the proportion of DHA in all strains, whereas line 207 states that temperature variation ($25, 30, \text{and } 35^\circ\text{C}$) had no influence on DHA proportions.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Cross-check the underlying triplicate spreadsheets for Figure 1A and Figure 2A to clarify the exact DCW of BBF001 at $30^\circ\text{C}$ in both experiments, change `"32.95% and 7.12% of"` on line 209 to `"32.95% and 7.12% higher than"`, and revise line 207 so that it accurately reflects the temperature-dependent decrease in DHA proportion reported on line 193.

#### 4. Replace Duncan's Multiple Range Test with Tukey's HSD and Provide Raw Time-Series Data
* **WHY:** Duncan's multiple range test (Section 2.5, implemented in legacy SPSS 11.5) does not control the family-wise Type I error rate across multi-group comparisons. Additionally, no numerical tables of the time-course data plotted in Figure 6 or the fatty acid percentages in Figures 4–5 are provided in the manuscript or supplement.
* **WHAT:** Re-evaluate one-way ANOVA post-hoc comparisons using Tukey's HSD test and include a supplementary table containing the mean $\pm \text{SD}$ time-series measurements ($t = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\text{ d}$) for DCW, residual glucose, and DHA across all three strains.
* **HOW:** Run Tukey's HSD at $\alpha = 0.05$ for each temperature panel in Figures 1–3, update the significance letters if any change, and append the raw time-series table for Figure 6 to enable independent verification of the ODE fits.
