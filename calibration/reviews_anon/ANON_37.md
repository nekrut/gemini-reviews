### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript reports a retrospective molecular and serological screen of 435 archived clinical specimens (148 oropharyngeal swabs, 141 urine samples, and 146 sera) from 156 measles/rubella-negative rash patients collected in Lombardy, Italy, across three periods: a control cohort (August 2018 – July 2019, $N = 100$), a pre-pandemic cohort (August 2019 – February 2020, $N = 44$), and a pandemic cohort (March 2020 – March 2021, $N = 12$). While all 289 swab and urine samples tested negative by standard CDC Real-Time RT-qPCR, screening with four in-house hemi-nested end-point PCR assays (NsP3, RdRp, Spike A, Spike B) followed by Sanger sequencing yielded positive bands in 11/44 (25%) pre-pandemic cases (earliest September 12, 2019) and 2/12 (16.7%) pandemic cases. Sanger sequences from October 2019 samples carried the three canonical lineage `B.1` / `20A` mutations (`C3037T`, `C14408T`, `A23403G`). Extrapolating backwards by 6 mutations from October 17, 2019, the authors conclude that the SARS-CoV-2 progenitor (`proCoV2`) emerged in late June – late July 2019 and that `B.1`-like viruses were circulating in Italy and Brazil months before the December 2019 Wuhan outbreak. Fourteen partial Sanger sequences (`MZ223385`–`MZ223398`) are deposited in GenBank.

The central conclusions of this manuscript—that SARS-CoV-2 lineage `B.1` circulated in Lombardy at a 25% positivity rate among morbilliform rash patients starting in September–October 2019 and that `proCoV2` originated in June–July 2019—are scientifically unsustainable and rest on classic molecular contamination artifacts, asymmetric diagnostic criteria, and phylodynamic implausibilities: (1) every putative positive sample tested negative by standard diagnostic RT-qPCR and amplified only after two rounds of hemi-nested end-point PCR (typically at only 1 of the 4 targeted loci per patient) in a laboratory that included pandemic SARS-CoV-2 patient RNA (`B.1` background) as a positive control in every amplification round; (2) recovering derived European `B.1` sequences (`C3037T`, `C14408T`, `A23403G`, which sit $\ge 6$ mutations downstream of the Wuhan-Hu-1 / lineage A root) in October 2019 samples without ancestral lineage A/B intermediates is a hallmark of carryover contamination from 2020/2021 clinical isolates or positive controls; (3) none of the 11 pre-pandemic RNA-positive patients had confirmed neutralizing antibodies by PRNT80, and similar rates of unconfirmed ELISA reactivity / partial PRNT reduction in the 2018–2019 control cohort were dismissed as "inconclusive"; and (4) widespread circulation of a transmissible `B.1` strain in Milan and Brescia from September 2019 to February 2020 (5 months) without triggering excess pneumonia hospitalizations or a single positive in the Lombardy influenza/severe respiratory surveillance network until late February 2020 is epidemiologically impossible.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **SARS-CoV-2 RNA was genuinely present in 25% (11/44) of pre-pandemic measles-like rash patients in Lombardy starting September 12, 2019: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Table 3, 100% (13/13) of reported positive cases tested negative (`–`) on the standard CDC diagnostic RT-qPCR assay, and 10 of the 11 pre-pandemic cases amplified in only 1 of the 4 hemi-nested PCR targets (e.g., Patient #1 was negative on RT-qPCR, NsP3, RdRp, and Spike A, and positive only on Spike B in urine; Patient #2 was positive only on Spike B in swab; Patients #3, #4, #6 only on RdRp; Patients #5, #7 only on NsP3). Nested and hemi-nested PCRs involving open tube transfers between first- and second-round amplifications carry an extreme risk of aerosol/amplicon carryover, especially when positive control RNA from a pandemic SARS-CoV-2 patient was amplified alongside test samples in every round (Section 2.2). Independent replication in an external, SARS-CoV-2-free ancient-DNA-grade clean facility or whole-genome high-throughput sequencing from unamplified primary extracts was not performed.

* **Sanger detection of `C3037T`, `C14408T`, and `A23403G` in October 2019 Lombardy samples proves that lineage `B.1` (`20A`) was circulating globally before the Wuhan outbreak and pushes the `proCoV2` tMRCA to late June – late July 2019: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Lineage `B.1` (`C3037T`, `C14408T`, `A23403G`, plus the three upstream $\alpha$ mutations `T8782C`, `T18060C`, `C28144T` that were not even sequenced here) is the exact dominant viral genotype that circulated in Lombardy during 2020 and was handled in the authors' university hospital laboratory. Finding derived 2020 Lombardy `B.1` sequences in October 2019 samples—two months before the ancestral root lineages (`A` and basal `B`) were ever sampled in Wuhan—contradicts thousands of tip-dated phylodynamic genomes worldwide and is diagnostic of laboratory contamination by modern `B.1` template or amplicons. Furthermore, the "unique" mutations cited in Case #5 (`T2987C` and `T3012C`) and double peaks in Case #10 (`23000C/T` and `23222G/A`) are $T \to C$, $C \to T$, and $G \to A$ transitions generated when non-proofreading `DreamTaq` polymerase amplifies near-single-copy cDNA templates over two nested rounds (~70–80 total cycles).

* **Serological testing (ELISA and PRNT80) corroborates pre-pandemic SARS-CoV-2 infections in the RNA-positive patients: [Overstated].**  
  Among the 11 pre-pandemic RNA-positive cases (Table 3), 0/9 tested sera were positive for neutralizing antibodies at the standard $\text{PRNT}_{80} \ge 1:10$ threshold (8 were completely negative `–` and 1 had partial 62% reduction at 1:10), and 5/9 were negative across all three ELISA classes (IgM, IgA, IgG). Conversely, in the negative control cohort collected before September 2019 (October 2018 – September 11, 2019), 12 samples were ELISA-positive (2 IgM, 8 IgA, 2 IgG) and 4 showed 50–59% plaque reduction at 1:10 (Section 3.1, line 161). Dismissing the 12 ELISA-positive / 4 partially neutralizing control samples prior to September 2019 as false-positive cross-reactivity ("inconclusive") while citing non-neutralizing ELISA reactivity in 4 pre-pandemic cases as confirmatory evidence of SARS-CoV-2 infection is an asymmetric, circular interpretation.

* **A strain with an $\alpha\beta$ (`B.1`) mutational signature capable of efficient human-to-human transmission circulated in Northern Italy from September 2019 without causing noticeable excess mortality until March 2020: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Lineage `B.1` (carrying Spike D614G / `A23403G` and RdRp P323L / `C14408T`) is the exact virus that drove the explosive February–April 2020 Lombardy epidemic with an basic reproduction number $R_0 \approx 3$ and a doubling time of ~3–4 days. An introduction of `B.1` into Milan and Brescia in September 2019—infecting 25% of all non-measles rash cases by October 2019—would have produced hundreds of thousands of cases and overwhelmed Lombardy intensive care units by November–December 2019, and would have been readily detected by the Lombardy severe acute respiratory / influenza surveillance network (which tested hundreds of respiratory specimens in autumn–winter 2019/2020 and found zero SARS-CoV-2 positives prior to February 2020, Section 4.2).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Authenticate Putative Pre-Pandemic Detections via Independent Blinded Replication and Unamplified Metagenomic Sequencing
* **WHY:** Two-round hemi-nested end-point PCR in a laboratory that uses pandemic `B.1` SARS-CoV-2 RNA as a positive control cannot rule out low-level template or amplicon contamination when 100% of samples are RT-qPCR negative and yield disconnected single-locus `B.1` fragments.
* **WHAT:** Subject residual primary aliquots of the 11 pre-pandemic specimens (and matched 2018–2019 negative controls) to blinded independent extraction and shotgun metatranscriptomic or hybrid-capture next-generation sequencing (avoiding nested PCR and excluding positive controls) in an independent facility.
* **HOW:** Extract RNA from unopened backup biobank aliquots in a PCR-free clean room alongside blank extraction controls, perform ribodepletion and shotgun Illumina sequencing (or whole-genome tiled capture without nested amplification), and verify whether complete genomes can be assembled, whether they carry genuine pre-`B.1` basal genotypes rather than modern laboratory `B.1` sequences, and whether damage/degradation profiles match archived 2019 urine/swab RNA.

#### 2. Apply Symmetric Diagnostic Thresholds Across the Control (2018–2019) and Pre-Pandemic (2019–2020) Cohorts
* **WHY:** Counting non-neutralizing ELISA reactivity ($\text{PRNT}_{80} < 80\%$ at 1:10) as supportive of SARS-CoV-2 infection in pre-pandemic cases (Table 3) while classifying 12 ELISA-positive / 4 partially neutralizing control sera from 2018–2019 as negative/inconclusive (Section 3.1, Table S1) introduces severe confirmation bias.
* **WHAT:** Report the exact false-positive rate of the Euroimmun IgM/IgA/IgG ELISA and 1:10 partial PRNT reduction in the $N = 98$ pre-August 2019 control sera, and formally compare the seropositivity rate between the control cohort (12/98 = 12.2%) and the pre-pandemic cohort (6/38 = 15.8%, adding the 4 RNA-positive/ELISA-positive and 2 RNA-negative/ELISA-positive cases) using Fisher's exact test.
* **HOW:** Add a contingency table comparing ELISA (IgM, IgA, IgG) and $\text{PRNT}_{80}$ ($\ge 80\%$ vs. $50\text{–}79\%$ reduction) rates across all three cohorts (Control $N = 98$, Pre-pandemic $N = 38$, Pandemic $N = 10$), demonstrating that pre-pandemic serological reactivity does not significantly exceed the 2018–2019 background false-positive rate ($P = 0.58$).

#### 3. Correct Codon Numbering Errors in Table 2 and Section 3.2
* **WHY:** In Section 3.2 (line 176), the two NsP3 mutations in Case #5 (`T2987C` and `T3012C`) are reported as amino acid changes `F908L` and `L916S` "in NsP3", whereas in Table 2 (line 80) the NsP3 amplicon (`2949–3212`) is annotated as amino acids `76–164`. Codons 908 and 916 are the coordinates in the full `ORF1a` polyprotein (where NsP3 begins at ORF1a residue 819), so `F908L` and `L916S` correspond to NsP3 residues 90 and 98, while `F106F` (`C3037T`) in Table 2 is also misnumbered (nucleotide 3037 is ORF1a codon 925 = NsP3 codon 107).
* **WHAT & HOW:** Standardize all amino acid annotations in Table 2 and Section 3.2 to specify either `ORF1a` polyprotein codon numbers (e.g., `ORF1a:F908L`, `ORF1a:L916S`, `ORF1a:F924F`) or mature `NsP3` peptide codon numbers consistently.
