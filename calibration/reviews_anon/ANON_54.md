### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates the molecular evolutionary rates and selective regimes of 26 genes encoding subunits of the three mammalian Structural Maintenance of Chromosomes (SMC) complexes—cohesin (7 mitotic and 4 meiosis-specific subunits), condensin I/II (8 subunits), and SMC5/6 (7 subunits)—across 46 to 63 mammalian species. Using `RevTrans`/MAFFT codon alignments, `phyML` trees, `GARD` recombination screening, `SLAC` gene-wide $d_N/d_S$ and site-wise $d_N - d_S$ estimates, and `PAML` `codeml` site models (M1a vs. M2a and M7 vs. M8 under both F3x4 and F61 codon frequency models), the authors identify pervasive positive selection in 7 of 26 genes (27%): all four meiosis-specific cohesins (`RAD21L1`, `REC8`, `SMC1B`, `STAG3`), condensin I subunit `NCAPG`, and SMC5/6 subunits `SMC5` and `NSMCE4A`. Intersecting site-level predictions from BEB ($\ge 0.90$), FUBAR ($\ge 0.90$), and FEL ($p < 0.10$) yields 48 positively selected codons, ~67% of which fall within Metapredict V2-predicted intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs), including three `ParSe` v2.0 phase-separation (PS)-promoting segments in `RAD21L1` and `STAG3`. Finally, correlating gene-wide $d_N/d_S$ against mouse gonadal RNA-seq fold-changes shows a positive correlation with transcriptional upregulation during female leptotene meiosis. Species lists and likelihood ratio statistics are provided in the article and supplements, though multiple sequence alignments and tree files are available only upon request.

The comparative analysis of all 26 SMC subunits across mammals clearly establishes a sharp contrast between deeply conserved mitotic cohesins ($d_N/d_S = 0.001\text{–}0.041$) and rapidly evolving meiosis-specific cohesins ($d_N/d_S = 0.215\text{–}0.494$), while highlighting biologically plausible host-virus interfaces in `SMC5` and `NSMCE4A`. However, several methodological and interpretive weaknesses temper the mechanistic conclusions: (1) IDRs experience relaxed purifying constraint and elevated indel rates across 99 million years of mammalian evolution, making alignment-driven false positives a risk when using `MAFFT` without alignment uncertainty filtering; (2) the correlation between $d_N/d_S$ and female meiotic fold-change (Figure 3) is confounded by tissue expression breadth (comparing 4 germline-restricted genes against 22 ubiquitously expressed somatic housekeeping genes); (3) branch-level adaptation is inferred by counting unvalidated `codeml` Free-Ratio point estimates ($d_N/d_S > 1$) across ~120 branches; and (4) functional phosphorylation claims rely on degenerate PROSITE short linear motifs without experimental phosphosite validation. These limitations can be addressed through computational controls and targeted reframing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Mitotic cohesins evolve under extreme purifying constraint, whereas meiosis-specific cohesins (`RAD21L1`, `REC8`, `SMC1B`, `STAG3`), `NCAPG`, `SMC5`, and `NSMCE4A` show evidence of positive selection in mammals (Sections 3.1–3.2, Tables 1–2, Figure 1): [Well Supported].**  
  Requiring significance across both M1a/M2a and M7/M8 likelihood ratio tests under two codon frequency parameterizations (F3x4 and F61), verifying the absence of recombination breakpoints with `GARD`, checking $d_S$ saturation, and requiring concordance among at least two of BEB, FUBAR, and FEL provides a conservative framework for gene- and site-level inference. Note, however, that after Bonferroni correction across 26 genes ($\alpha = 0.05/26 = 0.0019$), the M1a vs. M2a F61 test for `SMC5` ($p = 0.019$) and `REC8` ($p = 0.0064$) are only nominally significant.

* **Positively selected sites in meiotic cohesins and `SMC5` cluster in IDRs and drive adaptation by modulating phase separation (Section 3.3, Figure 2): [Fragile].**  
  While binomial tests confirm that selected sites are enriched in Metapredict V2 IDRs for `RAD21L1` ($p = 0.01$), `REC8` ($p = 0.013$), `STAG3` ($p = 1.77 \times 10^{-4}$), and `SMC5` ($p = 0.023$), IDRs are unstructured regions that tolerate high amino acid substitution and indel rates. Aligning divergent mammalian IDRs with `MAFFT` followed by manual gap editing (line 75) can juxtapose non-homologous codons and inflate site-specific $\omega$ estimates. Furthermore, the claim in the Abstract that these sites *"most likely contribute to adaptation through modulation of phase separation"* rests solely on observing one or more selected sites within three `ParSe`-predicted regions in `RAD21L1` and `STAG3`, without testing whether `ParSe` segments are statistically enriched for selected sites relative to non-PS IDRs.

* **Meiotic cohesin evolutionary rates specifically correlate with female (oogenesis) rather than male (spermatogenesis) meiotic expression, supporting female meiotic drive (Section 3.4, Figure 3): [Overstated].**  
  The Kendall rank correlation across the 26 SMC genes in Figure 3 is driven by a bimodal contrast between 22 ubiquitously expressed somatic subunits (which are already expressed in pre-meiotic mitotic cells at E12.5, yielding $\log_2\text{FC} \approx 0$ and low $d_N/d_S$ due to broad somatic pleiotropy) and 4 germline-restricted meiotic cohesins (which are silent in somatic cells and strongly induced at meiosis, yielding high $\log_2\text{FC}$ and higher $d_N/d_S$). In mammalian genomics, germline/tissue-restricted genes systematically exhibit higher $d_N/d_S$ than housekeeping genes due to narrower expression breadth. Moreover, in postnatal male testes at 6 dpp (the reference baseline used for male meiosis), pre-leptotene spermatocytes and somatic Sertoli/Leydig cells differ in baseline transcript composition from E12.5 fetal ovaries, and `RAD21L1`—the fastest-evolving gene ($d_N/d_S = 0.494$)—is functionally essential for *male* fertility (spermatogenesis arrest in knockout mice) rather than female-specific drive.

* **Lineage-specific positive selection across mammalian orders (Free-Ratio model, Section 3.2, Supplementary Figure S1) and viral kinase targeting of CK2 phosphorylation motifs in `NCAPG` (Section 3.3, Discussion): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, using the `PAML` Free-Ratio (FR) model to classify any branch with a point estimate of $d_N/d_S > 1$ as positively selected (line 173, Supplementary Figure S1) is statistically invalid: estimating ~120 independent $\omega$ parameters per gene yields noisy ratio estimates on short mammalian branches where $d_S$ is near zero, without any branch-specific likelihood ratio test or multiple-testing control. Second, scanning sequences with degenerate 3–4 residue PROSITE patterns for PKC (`[ST]-x-[RK]`) and CK2 (`[ST]-x(2)-[DE]`) phosphorylation sites (line 186) has a high false-positive rate in serine/threonine-rich IDRs; no evidence from experimental phosphoproteomics (e.g., PhosphoSitePlus) or permutation enrichment testing is provided to show that `NCAPG` sites 36, 37, and 84 are bona fide in vivo phosphorylation sites.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Alignment Uncertainty in IDRs and Test Enrichment in Phase-Separation Regions
* **WHY:** Unstructured IDRs accumulate insertions and deletions across mammalian orders; `MAFFT` alignments with manual editing near gaps can introduce false-positive codon substitutions, and `ParSe` overlap was not statistically tested.
* **WHAT:** Re-verify positive selection in IDRs after automated alignment uncertainty masking, and perform a formal binomial/Fisher's exact test for selected-site enrichment in `ParSe` PS-promoting regions versus remaining IDR residues.
* **HOW:** Run `GUIDANCE2` (with 100 bootstrap MAFFT/PRANK trees) or `PRANK` codon-aware alignment on the 7 positively selected genes, mask columns with alignment confidence scores $< 0.93$, and re-run `codeml` M7/M8, `FEL`, and `FUBAR` to confirm which of the 48 sites remain significant. Then compute a $2 \times 2$ Fisher's exact test comparing the proportion of selected codons inside `ParSe` blue regions vs. non-`ParSe` gray IDR regions in `RAD21L1` and `STAG3`.

#### 2. Replace Unvalidated Free-Ratio Branch Point Estimates with Calibrated Branch-Site Tests
* **WHY:** Counting branches where the `codeml` Free-Ratio point estimate exceeds $\omega = 1$ (Section 3.2, line 173, Supplementary Figure S1) conflates estimation noise on short branches (small $d_S$) with episodic positive selection.
* **WHAT:** Use a formal branch-site likelihood ratio test with multiple-testing correction to identify mammalian lineages experiencing episodic diversifying selection.
* **HOW:** Since `HYPHY` is already installed and used in the pipeline (line 90), run `aBSREL` (Adaptive Branch-Site Random Effects Likelihood) on the 26 SMC alignments and `phyML` trees. Report only branches that pass the Holm–Bonferroni corrected likelihood ratio test ($p_{\text{corrected}} < 0.05$) in Supplementary Figure S1 and Section 3.2.

#### 3. Decouple Tissue Expression Breadth from Sex-Specific Meiotic Fold-Change and Validate Phosphosites
* **WHY:** The correlation in Figure 3 is driven by comparing 4 germline-specific genes against 22 somatic housekeeping genes, and PROSITE short kinase motifs have high random occurrence rates.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Meiotic Expression Analysis (Section 3.4, Figure 3):** Explicitly report the exact Kendall's $\tau$ and $p$-values for all four panels of Figure 3 in Section 3.4 (line 191). Test whether the correlation with female leptotene $\log_2\text{FC}$ holds after controlling for somatic tissue expression breadth (e.g., partial Kendall correlation controlling for GTEx/ENCODE tissue specificity index $\tau$, or comparing absolute TPM in female vs. male pachytene/leptotene oocytes and spermatocytes rather than fold-change relative to heterogeneous 6 dpp whole testes). Discuss the biological caveat that `RAD21L1` is essential for male spermatogenesis.
  2. **Phosphosite Validation (Section 3.3, line 186):** Cross-reference the 48 positively selected residues (especially `NCAPG` residues 36, 37, and 84) against experimentally verified mammalian phosphorylation sites in **PhosphoSitePlus** (`https://www.phosphosite.org/`), and distinguish experimentally confirmed phosphosites from sequence-only PROSITE predictions.
