## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis
The authors present an integrative computational study of human pancreatic single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) datasets across non-diabetic (ND), type 2 diabetic (T2D), and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) cohorts (`Baron`, `Segerstolpe`, `Enge`, `Ngara`, `Fang`, `Kang`, `Steele`, and `Xin`). The manuscript introduces two XGBoost-based classifiers—`PanSubPred` for seven-lineage pancreatic cell annotation and `PSC-Stat` for distinguishing quiescent (`qPSC`) vs. activated (`aPSC`) pancreatic stellate cells—alongside an intercellular communication analysis (`CellCall`, `CellChat`, `CellPhoneDB`) that identifies a 15-gene T2D ductal signature, and subclustering analyses of beta, acinar, ductal, and alpha cells. While benchmarking `PanSubPred` against four established tools (`caSTLe`, `scID`, `scPred`, and retrained `CellTypist`) and cross-checking ligand-receptor networks across three communication tools are notable strengths, the machine-learning and statistical workflows suffer from critical design flaws: (1) cell-level rather than donor-level train/test splitting in `PanSubPred`, `PSC-Stat`, and the 15-gene T2D ductal classifier, which leaks donor-specific batch effects and treats thousands of cells from a handful of individuals as independent clinical observations; (2) circular evaluation of `PSC-Stat` on external cohorts using filtered `Azimuth` predictions (`score > 0.9`) as surrogate ground truth; (3) parametric $t$-tests on highly skewed `aPSC/qPSC` ratios with only $n = 4$ T2D donors; and (4) an overly stringent $>70\%$ single-cell detection filter in ductal cells that reduces the "communication-driven 15-gene T2D signature" primarily to ubiquitous ribosomal and cytoskeletal housekeeping genes (`RPL3`, `RPS4X`, `RPL13`, `RPL36`, `ACTB`, `ACTG1`).

## 2. Direct Appraisal of Core Claims
* **Claim 1: `PanSubPred` identifies 64 pancreatic lineage markers (38 novel) and achieves cross-dataset annotation accuracy (`AUC > 0.970`) even after excluding canonical hormone/exocrine markers** — `[Moderately Supported]`
  Retraining `caSTLe`, `scID`, `scPred`, and `CellTypist` on the identical `Baron` reference dataset and evaluating them across three external datasets (`Segerstolpe`, `Enge`, `Ngara`, lines 99–101) provides a fair cross-dataset benchmark for major cell-type annotation. However, two methodological issues inflate the internal validation metrics: first, splitting the 6,749 cells of the `Baron` dataset randomly 70%:30% at the cell level (line 82) places cells from the same four human donors into both training and test sets, leaking donor and library-prep effects; second, feature selection (`MIC`, `ANOVA`, `F-score`, `PCA` + Incremental Feature Selection [IFS], line 94) must be nested strictly inside each cross-validation fold rather than computed across the full training set prior to 5-fold CV.
* **Claim 2: `PSC-Stat` accurately classifies quiescent vs. activated stellate cells (`AUC = 0.835–0.992`) and reveals a progressive `aPSC/qPSC` activation cascade from ND (`1.44 ± 1.02`) to T2D (`4.72 ± 4.01`) to PDAC (`18.67 ± 18.70`)** — `[Fragile]`
  This claim is weakened by three compounding methodological flaws:
  1. **Feature-selection leakage and cell-level splitting:** The 17 candidate input genes for `PSC-Stat` were taken from `PanSubPred` (trained on `Baron`) and pre-tested for differential expression between `qPSCs` and `aPSCs` across the dataset (Figure 2A, line 155) before splitting the 400 `Baron` PSCs 70%:30% at the cell level (line 82).
  2. **Circular external validation on high-confidence `Azimuth` labels:** Because the external cohorts (`Fang`, `Kang`, `Steele`) lacked ground-truth `qPSC`/`aPSC` annotations, the authors transferred labels via `Azimuth` and retained only cells with `Azimuth prediction score > 0.9` (line 163). Discarding all ambiguous or intermediate cells (`score <= 0.9`) guarantees artificially high AUCs (`0.835–0.886`) and only measures concordance between two reference-based classifiers on extreme cells rather than true biological validation.
  3. **Underpowered parametric testing on skewed donor ratios:** In Figure 2H–I (lines 161, 165), the donor-level `aPSC/qPSC` ratio comparison includes only $n = 4$ T2D donors against $n = 9$ ND and $n = 14$ PDAC donors, and the standard deviations equal or exceed the means (`4.72 ± 4.01` and `18.67 ± 18.70`), demonstrating severe positive skewness. Applying an unadjusted parametric two-sided Student's $t$-test to $n = 4$ skewed ratios is statistically invalid.
* **Claim 3: T2D reorganizes intercellular communication into ductal-centric hubs (`FGF7-FGFR2/3`, `EFNB3-EPHB2/4/6`, `EFNA5-EPHA2`), yielding a mechanistic 15-gene T2D ductal diagnostic signature (`AUC = 0.846`)** — `[Overstated]`
  While the shift toward ductal-directed ligand-receptor interactions in T2D is consistently observed across `CellCall`, `CellChat`, and `CellPhoneDB` (Figure 3A, Figure S6), the derivation and biological interpretation of the 15-gene ductal classifier (lines 182–184) are flawed:
  1. **Cell-level pseudoreplication of a donor-level phenotype:** In `Fang et al.`, T2D vs. ND status is a property of the **human donor**, not an independent property of each individual ductal cell. Randomly splitting all extracted ductal cells 80%:20% into training and internal test sets (line 82, line 182; Table S9) places hundreds of cells from the exact same donors into both sets, so the internal test ROC curve (`AUC ~ 0.95`, Figure 3I) reflects donor-identity memorization rather than disease classification.
  2. **Artifactual enrichment of ribosomal and cytoskeletal genes via a $>70\%$ dropout filter:** To handle single-cell sparsity, the authors excluded any gene not detected in $>70\%$ of ductal cells (line 182), collapsing 1,729 candidate pathway/regulon genes down to just 78 genes in one dataset (Figure 3E). In droplet/plate scRNA-seq, a $>70\%$ non-zero detection threshold strips away genuine transcription factors and signaling receptors, leaving almost exclusively high-abundance ribosomal subunits (`RPL3`, `RPS4X`, `RPL13`, `RPL36`), cytoplasmic actins (`ACTB`, `ACTG1`), cytokeratins (`KRT19`, `KRT7`), and `S100A6`/`S100A10`. Interpreting ubiquitous ribosomal proteins and `ACTB` as specific downstream effectors of `FGF7`/`EFNB3 -> MYC/ETS1` ductal signaling (line 184) overstates a technical abundance/dropout filter artifact.
* **Claim 4: Integrated subclustering reveals T2D-associated depletion of mature beta cells (`INS+ NKX6-1+`) and expansion of immature (`CD81+ RBP4+`) beta, inflammatory acinar, and secretory ductal subsets** — `[Moderately Supported]`
  The donor-level comparison of beta-cell cluster proportions across $n = 19$ ND and $n = 9$ T2D donors (Figure 4F, Wilcoxon rank-sum test $p < 0.05$ for cluster 0 and cluster 1; note that subsets of donors with zero cells appear to reduce the denominator to `ND n=7, T2D n=7` in cluster 1 and `ND n=13, T2D n=5` in cluster 2) aligns with established islet literature. However, for the acinar and ductal subclusters in Figure 5C and Figure 5F, the manuscript reports only pooled cell percentages (`70.3%`, `70.5%`, `70.4%`, lines 204, 212) without donor-level error bars or statistical testing accounting for inter-donor composition variance.

## 3. Essential Revisions & Methodological Critiques
1. **Replace cell-level random splits with donor-stratified splits and donor-level aggregation:**
   - Re-evaluate `PanSubPred`, `PSC-Stat`, and the 15-gene T2D ductal classifier using **leave-one-donor-out** or **donor-grouped cross-validation** (`GroupKFold` by donor ID), ensuring that no cells from the same individual ever appear in both training and validation/testing sets.
   - For the 15-gene T2D ductal signature, evaluate classification performance at the **donor level** (e.g., pseudobulk ductal expression per donor or mean predicted probability per donor across the discovery and `Segerstolpe` cohorts, reporting exact donor sample sizes $N_{\text{ND}}$ and $N_{\text{T2D}}$ and 95% confidence intervals).
2. **Eliminate the $>70\%$ single-cell detection filter and circular `Azimuth` threshold:**
   - Lower the gene detection threshold in ductal cells from $>70\%$ (line 182) to a standard scRNA-seq threshold ($10\%$–$20\%$ of cells, or use donor-level pseudobulk counts) so that genuine signaling and transcriptional target genes are not replaced by ribosomal (`RPL3`, `RPS4X`, `RPL13`, `RPL36`) and actin (`ACTB`, `ACTG1`) housekeeping genes.
   - In the external validation of `PSC-Stat` (line 163), report AUCs across **all** annotated PSCs without filtering for `Azimuth prediction score > 0.9`, and validate activation scores directly against canonical myofibroblastic/ECM gene sets (`ACTA2`, `COL1A1`, `COL1A2`, `FN1`, `SPARC`) rather than relying solely on `Azimuth` label transfer.
3. **Correct donor-level statistical tests, restore missing Equation 1, and deposit source code:**
   - In Figure 2H–I (`aPSC/qPSC` ratios across ND $n=9$, T2D $n=4$, PDAC $n=14$), replace parametric Student's $t$-tests with non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis and Dunn's/Mann–Whitney tests with multiple-testing correction, and include all donors (including those with 0 cells in a subcluster, assigning $0\%$ rather than dropping them from the denominator) in Figure 4F and Figure 5C, F.
   - Fill in the missing mathematical formulas in Equation 1 (line 110, currently an empty table cell `| | 1 |`), and deposit the full training, benchmarking, and analysis source code for `PanSubPred` and `PSC-Stat` in a public repository (GitHub/Zenodo).
