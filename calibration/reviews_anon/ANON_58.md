### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This surveillance report investigates serologic evidence of avian influenza A(H5) spillover in unvaccinated, clinically healthy horses in Mongolia between July 2021 and October 2023. The authors collected 2,160 serum samples from 24 herds (10 horses per herd, sampled 3 times per year across 9 rounds): 14 herds in the wetland-rich Ugiinuur area of Arkhangai Province (high migratory waterfowl density) and 10 herds in the arid Dashinchilen/Burd soum region of Bulgan and Uvurkhangai Provinces (low wild bird density). Following heat inactivation and receptor-destroying enzyme (RDE) treatment, screening with a commercial influenza A nucleoprotein (NP) ELISA identified 997 NP-positive sera (46.2%). Testing those 997 samples on a competitive H5 hemagglutinin ELISA yielded 9 positive and 8 doubtful samples, while 13 control sera from horses experimentally infected with equine influenza virus (EIV H3N8/H7N7) tested negative. Live-virus neutralization (VN) assays against clade 2.3.4.4b `A/chicken/England/053052/2021` (H5N1) on the 17 H5-ELISA-reactive sera identified 2 positive samples—both with a titer of 1:20—collected from working horses in October 2021 (Bulgan, dry region) and October 2022 (Arkhangai, wetland region). No raw serological data table or appendix protocol is included in the manuscript file.

Monitoring equids for avian influenza spillover is biologically and epidemiologically important given the endemic circulation of H3N8 EIV and the risk of avian–equine reassortment. The three-tier serologic algorithm (NP ELISA $\to$ H5 competitive ELISA $\to$ live-virus VN) with RDE treatment and experimental EIV negative controls is a reasonable screening workflow. However, three limitations weaken the conclusions: (1) claiming that *"spillover events are likely frequent"* (line 34) is not supported by finding only 2 borderline (1:20) VN-positive samples out of 2,160 sera (0.09%), where 15 of 17 H5-ELISA-positive/doubtful samples failed to neutralize the virus; (2) H5 hemagglutinin serology and neutralization against a single H5N1 strain do not test neuraminidase (N1) specificity or rule out exposure to low-pathogenicity avian influenza (LPAI) H5Nx viruses circulating in Eurasian wild birds; and (3) the longitudinal design (sampling 10 horses from 24 herds 3 times per year over 9 rounds) is not leveraged to show whether the two positive horses seroconverted between consecutive sampling rounds or maintained antibodies at subsequent visits, and the cited Appendix detailing the VN protocol is missing (lines 55–57). Addressing these points requires supplying the missing Appendix, reporting the longitudinal titer trajectories of the positive horses, and tempering the frequency and subtype-specificity claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Two of 2,160 equine serum samples (0.09%) exhibit low-titer (1:20) neutralizing antibodies against clade 2.3.4.4b H5 virus without EIV cross-reactivity (Main Text line 30): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Sequential testing by NP ELISA, competitive H5 ELISA, and live-virus VN, alongside 13 experimental EIV sera that tested negative in both H5 ELISA and VN, supports rare H5 seroreactivity. However, 15 of the 17 H5-ELISA-positive/doubtful samples (88.2%) were negative by VN, and the two VN-positive samples both sat at a titer of 1:20 (typically the minimum starting dilution in microneutralization assays). Because the Appendix describing the exact VN assay protocol, starting dilution, positive control titers, and cutoff criteria is missing from the manuscript (lines 55–57), the margin above the limit of detection cannot be verified.

* **Spillover of H5N1 into horses is "likely frequent" (Main Text line 34): [Overstated].**  
  Detecting 2 VN-positive samples at a 1:20 titer out of 2,160 sera collected over 2.5 years (0.09% sample positivity, or 2/997 [0.20%] among IAV NP-seropositive samples) indicates that H5 spillover into Mongolian horses is **rare**, not frequent. Moreover, one of the two VN-positive samples came from the arid Bulgan region with a low density of wild birds rather than the Arkhangai wetlands, showing no statistically significant enrichment in the high-exposure wetland ecosystem (1/1,260 in wetlands vs. 1/900 in dry areas).

* **The seropositive horses were specifically infected with HPAI A(H5N1) (Abstract line 8, Main Text lines 14, 34): [Fragile].**  
  Both the competitive H5 ELISA and the VN assay (which primarily measures HA-mediated neutralization) detect antibodies directed against the H5 hemagglutinin. Without neuraminidase inhibition (NI / ELLA) testing against N1 or comparative HI/VN titration against Eurasian LPAI H5Nx strains (e.g., H5N2, H5N3, H5N8), serology alone cannot distinguish subclinical exposure to LPAI H5Nx from HPAI A(H5N1) clade 2.3.4.4b.

* **Longitudinal sampling across 24 herds (3 times per year from July 2021 to October 2023) captures subclinical equine influenza exposure (Main Text lines 16, 30, 34): [Fragile].**  
  Although the study design collected $24\text{ herds} \times 10\text{ horses} \times 9\text{ time points} = 2,160\text{ sera}$, the manuscript treats the 2,160 samples as an unstructured cross-sectional pool. It never states whether the same individual horses were followed longitudinally across the 9 visits, what proportion of the 240 horses accounted for the 997 NP-positive samples, or whether the two VN-positive horses (sampled in October 2021 and October 2022) tested negative at prior visits and retained NP/H5 antibodies at subsequent visits.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Supply the Missing Appendix and Report Complete Serological Titers and Controls
* **WHY:** Lines 55–57 flag that no Appendix file matches the two in-text `(Appendix)` citations in line 30. Without the Appendix, readers cannot evaluate the VN starting dilution, cell substrate, cytopathic/immunostaining readout, positive H5 control antisera titers, or the subtypes/strains of the 13 experimental EIV control sera.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Provide the complete Appendix text and include a summary table of the 17 H5-ELISA-positive/doubtful samples listing: Sample ID, Herd ID, Province/Ecosystem (Arkhangai wetland vs. Bulgan/Uvurkhangai dry), Sampling Date, NP ELISA S/N ratio, H5 Competitive ELISA inhibition percentage, and exact VN titer against `A/chicken/England/053052/2021` alongside homologous H5 positive control serum and the 13 EIV negative control sera.

#### 2. Report Longitudinal Paired-Sample Trajectories and Herd-Level Seroprevalence
* **WHY:** Because 10 horses in each of 24 herds were sampled 3 times per year over 9 rounds ($n = 2,160$), serial samples from the two VN-positive horses (October 2021 in Bulgan; October 2022 in Arkhangai) should exist in the dataset.
* **WHAT:** Clarify whether the same 10 horses per herd were sampled repeatedly across the 9 time points, and report the pre- and post-detection ELISA and VN results for the two VN-positive horses and their herd-mates.
* **HOW:** Query the project metadata for the two VN-positive horses across all sampling rounds (July 2021 through October 2023) to check for documented seroconversion (e.g., negative in July 2021 $\to$ 1:20 in October 2021) and subsequent antibody persistence or waning. Also report the NP-ELISA seroprevalence stratified by province/ecosystem (wetland vs. dry area) and sampling season.

#### 3. Temper Claims Regarding Spillover Frequency and H5N1 Subtype Specificity
* **WHY:** A 0.09% (2/2,160) VN positivity rate at a 1:20 titer does not support the claim that spillover events are "likely frequent," and H5 HA serology does not confirm the N1 neuraminidase subtype.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. In **line 34**, change *"Our findings show that horses are susceptible to infection by H5N1 viruses and that spillover events are likely frequent"* to *"Our findings provide serologic evidence that horses can be exposed to H5 avian influenza viruses, although confirmed neutralizing seropositivity was rare (2/2,160 samples; 0.09%) and at low titer (1:20)."*
  2. Explicitly acknowledge in the text that H5 ELISA and H5N1 VN assays detect H5 hemagglutinin antibodies and cannot definitively exclude cross-neutralization from Eurasian LPAI H5Nx strains without N1-specific serology or viral RNA detection.
  3. In **line 32**, fix the taxonomic spelling *"Equus hemonius hemonius"* to *"Equus hemionus hemionus"*.
