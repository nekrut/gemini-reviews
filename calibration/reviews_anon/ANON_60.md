### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates whether viral genetic changes or altered in vitro hepatic replication fitness contributed to the increased clinical severity and frequent hepatic involvement observed during the 2016–2017 dengue virus serotype 1 (DENV-1) outbreak in New Caledonia compared to the 2013–2014 outbreak. Starting from 86 RT-qPCR-confirmed clinical serum samples (17 retrospectively biobanked from 2013–2014 and 69 prospectively collected in 2016–2017), the authors recovered 24 near-complete DENV-1 genomes (3 from 2013–2014 and 21 from 2016–2017, supplemented with 2 GenBank genomes from 2014) and isolated 13 viable DENV-1 strains in C6/36 cells (6 from 2013–2014 and 7 from 2016–2017) for replication and cytopathogenicity kinetics in human hepatocarcinoma HuH7 cells. Phylogenetic analysis indicates that the 2016–2017 DENV-1 strains form a monophyletic Genotype I clade distinguished from the 2013–2014 strains by 26 synonymous and 4 non-synonymous substitutions (`NS1 Y886H`, `NS4A V2157A`, `NS5 K2623R`, and `NS5 G2875A`), with no genetic separation between severe ($n = 5$) and non-severe ($n = 15$) 2016–2017 cases. Consensus genome sequences are deposited in GenBank (`MW315172`–`MW315195`), though raw sequencing reads and isolate-level phenotypic data tables are not provided.

While the clinical cohort characterization and whole-genome surveillance data from New Caledonia are useful additions to regional arbovirus genomics, several methodological and interpretive flaws undermine the manuscript's main conclusions. First, the in vitro HuH7 phenotypic comparison ($n = 6$ vs. $n = 7$) yielded no statistically significant differences in infection rate, viral progeny titer, or cell mortality at any time point, yet the Abstract, Results headings, and Discussion repeatedly frame this null result as a "trend towards reduced replicative fitness and cytopathogenicity" and speculate that it contributed to DENV-1 lineage decline in 2018. Second, none of the 13 viral isolates tested in HuH7 cells originated from patients with severe dengue (`S = 0/13` in Table 1, with 4/6 of the 2013–2014 isolates lacking clinical severity classification), precluding a direct test of whether severe-case isolates exhibit enhanced hepatotropism. Third, the claim that the 2016–2017 substitutions modified viral antigenicity rests solely on single-sequence `NetCTL 1.2` and `BepiPred 2.0` epitope predictions without comparing wild-type versus mutant binding/cleavage scores, and the text contains coordinate mismatches between the mutated residues and the reported epitope positions. These issues can be resolved without new wet-lab experiments by recalculating comparative epitope scores, reporting exact test statistics and effect sizes with confidence intervals, correcting internal Table 1 inconsistencies, and reframing null findings accurately.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **2016–2017 New Caledonia DENV-1 strains form a distinct Genotype I clade separated from 2013–2014 strains by 26 synonymous and 4 non-synonymous substitutions, with no viral genetic signature distinguishing severe from non-severe 2016–2017 cases: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Maximum-likelihood phylogenetic reconstruction (`IQ-TREE` with `ModelFinder` and 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates) supports monophyletic clustering of the 21 sequenced 2016–2017 genomes and interspersed placement of the 5 severe and 15 non-severe genomes (Figure 1). However, the 2013–2014 reference set comprises only 3 newly sequenced genomes and 2 public sequences from 2014, all derived from high-viremia samples (median $1.32 \times 10^7$ copies/mL vs. $4.35 \times 10^5$ copies/mL across the 2013–2014 cohort), and 5 severe cases provide limited statistical power to rule out low-frequency viral determinants of severity.

* **DENV-1 isolates from 2016–2017 exhibit a "trend towards reduced replicative fitness and cytopathogenicity" in human hepatic HuH7 cells that may have contributed to DENV-1 replacement in 2018: [Overstated].**  
  Every Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney comparison between 2013–2014 ($n = 6$) and 2016–2017 ($n = 7$) isolates across days 1, 2, and 3 post-infection is statistically non-significant (`ns` in Figure 2A–C; exact $p$-values are not reported). Interpreting non-significant differences in an underpowered sample of 13 isolates as a biologically meaningful fitness decline—and extrapolating HuH7 carcinoma cell kinetics to population-level serotype replacement by DENV-2 in 2018—conflates sampling noise under the null hypothesis with directional evidence. Furthermore, Table 1 ("Analysis 2") shows that 0 of the 13 cultured isolates came from severe dengue patients (`S = 0/6` in 2013–2014; `S = 0/7` in 2016–2017), and 4 of the 6 isolates from 2013–2014 had unknown clinical severity (`N/A = 4`).

* **Three of the four 2016–2017 amino acid substitutions (`NS1 Y886H`, `NS4A V2157A`, `NS5 G2875A`) alter T- and B-cell epitopes and may have favored severe hepatic presentations via immune-mediated liver injury: [Fragile].**  
  This conclusion is based purely on in silico screening with `NetCTL 1.2` (restricted to HLA-A24) and `BepiPred 2.0` without experimental validation or differential scoring of the 2013–2014 versus 2016–2017 sequences. Moreover, the manuscript reports conflicting residue coordinates: the Results section states that the mutations occur at polyprotein positions `886` (`NS1 Y886H`), `2157` (`NS4A V2157A`), `2623` (`NS5 K2623R`), and `2875` (`NS5 G2875A`), but immediately states that *"Residues 887, 2158 and 2871 are located in T cell epitopes as predicted using NetCTL 1.2 server."* Whether the 9-mer peptides spanning positions 886, 2157, and 2875 gain or lose predicted HLA-A*24:02 binding affinity or proteasomal cleavage scores is never shown.

* **Cohort-level comparison of secondary dengue and Zika seroprevalence between the 2013–2014 and 2016–2017 outbreaks: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Table 1 documents a substantially higher prevalence of prior DENV IgG in the 2016–2017 cohort (`24/62 [39%]`) than in the 2013–2014 cohort (`1/17 [5.9%]`), consistent with secondary infection acting as a host risk factor for severe disease (`OR = 2.847, 95% CI [0.88; 9.2]`). However, the comparison is confounded by asymmetric recruitment: 2013–2014 samples were retrospectively drawn from a biobank with 29% missing clinical classification (`5/17 N/A`) and no severe cases, whereas 2016–2017 patients were prospectively enrolled at hospital/referral presentation.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reframe Null In Vitro Findings and Report Exact Statistics and Power Bounds
* **WHY:** Calling non-significant Mann–Whitney tests ($p > 0.05$) in $n = 6$ vs. $n = 7$ isolates a "trend towards reduced replicative fitness and cytopathogenicity" in the Abstract, Results subheadings, and Discussion violates standard statistical reporting and overstates an underpowered null result.
* **WHAT:** Replace the directional subheadings in the Results section and revise the Abstract and Discussion to state directly that replication kinetics, viral progeny titers, and cell death in HuH7 cells did not differ significantly between 2013–2014 and 2016–2017 non-severe DENV-1 isolates. Report exact two-sided Mann–Whitney $U$ statistics, exact $p$-values, and Hodges–Lehmann median difference confidence intervals for all comparisons in Figure 2A–C.
* **HOW:** Extract the isolate-level means (across the 3 technical replicates per isolate) for Day 1, Day 2, and Day 3 `% 2H2+ cells`, Day 2 viral titer (`FFU/mL`), and Day 1–3 `% dead cells`. Compute exact Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney $p$-values and non-parametric 95% confidence intervals for the difference in medians, add a supplementary table containing the isolate-level values for all 13 strains, and perform a brief post-hoc minimum detectable effect size calculation at $\alpha = 0.05$, $80\%$ power for $n_1 = 6, n_2 = 7$.

#### 2. Quantify Differential Epitope Scores and Resolve Polyprotein Coordinate Discrepancies
* **WHY:** Reporting that residues `887`, `2158`, and `2871` lie in predicted epitopes when the actual substitutions are `Y886H`, `V2157A`, and `G2875A` leaves it unclear whether the mutations fall inside the 9-mer epitopes, at MHC anchor positions, or outside the predicted peptides, and does not show whether the substitutions actually change predicted immunogenicity.
* **WHAT:** Reconcile the numbering of the polyprotein positions and explicitly compare the `NetCTL 1.2` (MHC binding, C-terminal cleavage, TAP transport, and combined score) and `BepiPred 2.0` residue scores between the 2013–2014 consensus protein sequence and the 2016–2017 consensus protein sequence across all overlapping peptides spanning the four mutated sites.
* **HOW:** Run both the 2013–2014 and 2016–2017 DENV-1 polyprotein sequences through `NetCTL 1.2` (supertype A24) and `BepiPred 2.0`. Tabulate the start–end coordinates of every predicted 9-mer epitope overlapping positions 886, 2157, 2623, and 2875, showing the wild-type vs. mutant peptide sequence, anchor position status, and $\Delta\text{NetCTL}$ / $\Delta\text{BepiPred}$ scores in Supplementary Table 1.

#### 3. Account for Passage History and Selection Bias in Cultured and Sequenced Subsets
* **WHY:** Only samples with very high viral loads ($\sim 10^7$ copies/mL, roughly 100-fold higher than the cohort median of $2.80 \times 10^5$ copies/mL) yielded whole genomes or C6/36 isolates, and viral stocks underwent "no more than four passages in C6/36" before HuH7 infection, while the positive control was passaged "several times in C6/36 cells." Differential passage number (e.g., P1 vs. P4) can alter mosquito-cell adaptation and mammalian HuH7 infectivity.
* **WHAT:** Document the exact C6/36 passage number for each of the 13 clinical isolates and verify whether passage number differs between the 2013–2014 ($n = 6$) and 2016–2017 ($n = 7$) groups.
* **HOW:** Add a column or footnote to the isolate table listing the GenBank accession (if sequenced), exact C6/36 passage number, patient days post-symptom onset, and clinical classification for each of the 13 isolates tested in Figure 2.

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Multiple numerical contradictions exist between the main text and Table 1, alongside formatting defects in Table 1.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **ZIKV IgG Denominator Mismatch (Results, Paragraph 4 vs. Table 1):** The text states *"Anti-ZIKV IgG were found in 0/17 (0%) and 7/60 (12%) patients from the successive epidemics. Among those anti-ZIKV IgG positive, 5/6 (83.3%) had both anti-ZIKV and DENV IgG."* However, Table 1 reports `ZIKV IgG: 0/6 (0%)` for 2013–2014 (not `0/17`), and `DENV&ZIKV IgG: 5/59 (8.5%)` for 2016–2017. Clarify that only 6/17 samples in 2013–2014 were tested for ZIKV IgG, and explain why the denominator among the 7 ZIKV IgG-positive patients is 6 (`5/6`) rather than 7 (`5/7`).
  2. **Viral Load Lower Quartile of Zero (Results, Paragraph 3 & Table 1):** For the 2016–2017 cohort, median viral load is reported as `2.37 × 10^5 [0 - 1.05 × 10^7]` copies/mL, despite the Methods stating that *"Patients found positive for DENV by qRT-PCR were included in the study."* Explain whether `0` represents samples positive on qualitative serotype RT-qPCR but below the limit of quantification of the standard curve, and report the assay's lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) instead of `0`.
  3. **Duplicate Text Blocks (Results):** Remove the duplicated table caption/legend lines in the Results section (`Demographic data and clinical and biological parameters...` and repeated Figure 1 and Figure 2 captions embedded in the body text).
