### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study evaluates whether deep mutational scanning (DMS)-informed prediction of SARS-CoV-2 receptor-binding domain (RBD) evolution can prospectively enrich for broadly neutralizing antibodies (bnAbs) from early-pandemic repertoires. Profiling 7,018 mAbs (including 1,637 potent autologous neutralizing antibodies with $\text{IC}_{50} < 0.05\,\mu\text{g/mL}$) across seven human exposure cohorts against historical and contemporary variants (`D614G` through `XBB.1.5`, `HK.3`, `JN.1`, and `KP.3`), the authors show that autologous potency poorly predicts breadth against future variants: only 2 of 141 (`1.4%`) potent ancestral (WT)-elicited mAbs retained potency against `XBB.1.5`, and only 1 (`BD55-1205`) neutralized `JN.1` and `KP.3`. By filtering the 141 potent WT-elicited mAbs against 17 single-mutant and 5 combinatorial (`B.1-S1` to `B.1-S5`) pseudoviruses carrying DMS-predicted escape hotspots, the authors narrow the candidate pool to 5 mAbs, capturing both `XBB.1.5`-potent antibodies (`2/5 [40%]`) and the sole `JN.1`/`KP.3`-potent antibody (`BD55-1205`). They then characterize `BD55-1205` (an `IGHV3-66` class 1 antibody) using replication-competent rVSV and authentic `XBB.1.5.6` serial-passage escape selection, surface plasmon resonance (SPR), cryo-EM structures of `BD55-1205` Fab bound to the `XBB.1.5` spike trimer ($3.5\,\text{\AA}$) and RBD ($3.3\,\text{\AA}$), and intravenous mRNA-LNP delivery in human FcRn transgenic mice (`Tg32-SCID` and `Tg32`), achieving peak serum $\text{ID}_{50}$ titers of $\sim 4,500\text{–}5,100$. Source data tables are provided for all main and extended data figures.

The experimental execution—spanning high-throughput neutralization across 7,018 mAbs, orthogonal rVSV and BSL-3 authentic virus escape profiling, high-resolution structural biology, site-directed paratope mutagenesis, and in vivo mRNA-LNP pharmacokinetics—is thorough and convincing. The primary limitations are statistical and methodological nuances in the retrospective enrichment analysis and small-sample mouse statistics: (1) the headline enrichment from `1%` to `40%` (`2/141` to `2/5`) is based on a denominator of 5 antibodies (exact 95% binomial CI: `5.3%–85.3%`) and relies on combinatorial mutant panels (`B.1-S1`–`S5`) whose specific amino acid combinations were assembled retrospectively without a formalized algorithmic rule; (2) `B.1-S1`–`S5` did not include class 1 escape mutations at `L455`/`F456`/`A475`, so 4 of the 5 WT-elicited mAbs passing the filter still failed completely against `HK.3.1`, `JN.1`, and `KP.3` ($\text{IC}_{50} > 10\,\mu\text{g/mL}$, Table 1); and (3) the mouse statistical comparisons ($n = 4$) use unpaired tests on repeated/paired measurements and pool longitudinal time points in ordinary Pearson correlation $t$-tests. These limitations can be addressed through straightforward statistical refinements and textual clarifications.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Autologous neutralization potency in early-pandemic or Omicron-breakthrough cohorts does not reliably predict breadth against subsequent SARS-CoV-2 lineages: [Well Supported].**  
  The empirical screen of 7,018 mAbs (and 1,637 potent autologous NAbs across 7 cohorts) against 8 major variant pseudoviruses (Figure 1a–d, Extended Data Figure 1) provides quantitative evidence that highly potent autologous antibodies suffer steep attrition against subsequent antigenic clusters, including 85% loss of Omicron activity among SARS-CoV-1/SARS-CoV-2 cross-reactive mAbs from the `SARS+WT` cohort.

* **DMS-guided combinatorial pseudovirus screening (`B.1-S1` to `B.1-S5`) increases the discovery rate of `XBB.1.5`-effective bnAbs from 1% to 40% among WT-elicited mAbs: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Passing all five designed combinatorial mutants reduced the 141 potent WT-elicited mAbs to 5 candidates while retaining both `XBB.1.5`-potent mAbs (`BD-505` and `BD55-1205`, $p < 0.01$ by hypergeometric test, Figure 2d–e, Table 1). However, two caveats temper the generality of the `40%` figure: first, `2/5` carries wide sampling uncertainty (exact Clopper–Pearson 95% CI: `[5.3%, 85.3%]`), and for `HK.3.1`, `JN.1`, and `KP.3`, only `1/5` (`20%`) of the filtered WT mAbs remained active because the `B.1-S1`–`S5` designs did not incorporate mutations at class 1 sites `L455`, `F456`, or `A475`. Second, while the mutated sites were identified from WT DMS profiles, the exact amino acid identities and 5-way combinations in `B.1-S1`–`S5` (e.g., `R346T+K417T+K444N+L452R+E484K+F486S` in `B.1-S3`) were selected manually rather than generated by a blinded, pre-registered computational algorithm, leaving potential retrospective lookahead bias in the choice of amino acid substitutions.

* **`BD55-1205` exhibits broad, potent neutralization and a high genetic barrier to viral escape via ACE2 mimicry and extensive contacts with the RBD peptide backbone: [Well Supported].**  
  This conclusion is supported by multiple independent lines of evidence: pseudovirus neutralization across all tested variants including `JN.1`, `KP.3`, and `JN.1+F456L+A475V` (Table 1, Figure 3d); authentic virus neutralization against `WT`, `BA.5.2.1`, `FL.8`, `XBB.1.5.6`, and `JN.3` ($\text{IC}_{50} = 0.007\text{–}0.026\,\mu\text{g/mL}$, Extended Data Figure 6a); serial-passage escape resistance in both `XBB.1.5` rVSV (surviving to passage 6, matching a two-mAb cocktail) and authentic `XBB.1.5.6` virus (12 passages, selecting only `S490Y` with minimal $\text{IC}_{50}$ shift and reduced hACE2 binding); $3.3\,\text{\AA}$ cryo-EM structural resolution demonstrating 12 hydrogen bonds to RBD main-chain atoms across 9 residues (`L455`, `R457`, `K458`, `Q474`, `A475`, `G476`, `S490`, `L492`, `G502`, Figure 4d–g); and reversion mutagenesis of somatic hypermutations (`R31S`, `P53S`, `R102Y`, and `BD55-1205-GLHV`, Extended Data Figure 9d–e).

* **Intravenous delivery of mRNA-LNPs encoding `BD55-1205-LA` in human FcRn transgenic mice produces high, durable serum IgG concentrations and neutralizing titers comparable to clinical benchmark `CHKV-24`: [Well Supported].**  
  In `Tg32-SCID` ($n = 8$ combined across two experiments) and immunocompetent `Tg32` mice, a $0.5\text{ mg/kg}$ intravenous dose yielded peak serum concentrations of $\sim 505\,\mu\text{g/mL}$ at 48 h and geometric mean $\text{ID}_{50}$ titers of `4,496` (`XBB.1.5`), `5,138` (`HK.3.1`), and `4,608` (`JN.1`) (Figure 5b–e). While the pharmacokinetic and ex vivo serum neutralization data are clear, in vivo protective efficacy against lethal or replication-competent viral challenge was not directly tested, and the statistical comparisons in Figure 5e–f have minor methodological flaws (use of unpaired rank-sum tests on paired within-mouse variant titers and pooling of serial longitudinal bleeds in a simple Pearson correlation).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report Exact Binomial Confidence Intervals and Formalize the Combinatorial Mutant Design Rule
* **WHY:** Reporting that the probability of identifying `XBB.1.5` bnAbs increases from `1%` to `40%` (`2/5`) without confidence intervals overstates the precision of a 5-antibody subset, and Figure 2f / Extended Data Figure 2f–g use a normal approximation ($\hat{p} \pm z\sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})/k}$) that is poorly calibrated for small $k$ ($k \le 10$) and proportions near 0 or 1. Furthermore, readers need a clear algorithmic rule explaining how the 5 specific multi-mutant combinations (`B.1-S1` to `B.1-S5`) were constructed without lookahead knowledge of Omicron subvariants.
* **WHAT:** Replace normal-approximation confidence intervals in Figure 2f and Extended Data Figure 2f–g with exact Clopper–Pearson (or Wilson score) 95% confidence intervals, report exact 95% CIs alongside the `2/141 (1.4%)` and `2/5 (40%)` proportions in the text, and document the quantitative criteria used to select both the specific amino acid substitutions and the 5 multi-site combinations in `B.1-S1`–`S5`.
* **HOW:** In R or Python (`scipy.stats.binomtest` / `statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportion_confint(method='wilson')`), recompute the 95% error bars for each rank $k$ in Figure 2f and Extended Data Figure 2f–g. In the Methods (*Pseudovirus design and neutralization assays*), state the exact numeric thresholds for DMS escape score, single-nucleotide codon accessibility, DMS ACE2-binding score, and DMS RBD-expression score that selected the specific mutant residues (e.g., why `R346T`, `K417T`, `K444N`, and `F486S` were chosen over alternative substitutions at those sites).

#### 2. Correct Paired and Longitudinal Statistical Tests in the Mouse mRNA-LNP Experiments
* **WHY:** In Figure 5e, neutralizing titers against `XBB.1.5`, `HK.3.1`, and `JN.1` were measured on the *same* 4 mouse sera ($n = 4$), making the observations paired across variants rather than independent groups; moreover, with $n = 4$, a two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test has a minimum attainable $p$-value of $2/2^4 = 0.125$ (zero power to detect differences at $\alpha = 0.05$). In Figure 5f, pooling repeated longitudinal bleeds from the same 4 mice into an ordinary Pearson correlation $t$-test violates independence and inflates degrees of freedom.
* **WHAT:** Account for within-animal pairing and longitudinal repeated measures in Figure 5e and Figure 5f, and note the sample-size power limitation when interpreting `NS` ($p > 0.05$) in $n = 4$ animals.
* **HOW:**
  1. For Figure 5e, report geometric mean ratios (GMRs) with 95% confidence intervals between variant pairs within the 4 mice (or apply a Friedman test across the 3 variants) and note that similarity is supported by the near-unity GMRs (`4,496` vs. `5,138` vs. `4,608`) rather than by the underpowered non-significant rank-sum $p$-value.
  2. For Figure 5f, compute the correlation on $\log_{10}(\text{hIgG})$ versus $\log_{10}(\text{ID}_{50})$ using a repeated-measures correlation (`rmcorr` package in R) or linear mixed-effects model with mouse ID as a random effect, or report the correlation separately at the 48 h peak time point ($n = 4$).

#### 3. Clarify Bivalent IgG Avidity vs. Monovalent Fab Affinity in SPR Measurements
* **WHY:** In the Results (*BD55-1205 shows a high barrier to escape*) and Extended Data Figure 5c–d, SPR measurements are performed by immobilizing full-length bivalent IgG1 on Protein A chips and flowing monomeric RBD (which correctly measures 1:1 site affinity without avidity), yet the text and figure legend label the resulting constants as "apparent affinity" ranging down to `1 pM` (`XBB.1.5` / `WT`), where dissociation rates approach the instrument's lower detection limit.
* **WHAT:** Clarify why the 1:1 RBD-over-immobilized-IgG measurement is termed "apparent affinity" and report the lower limit of dissociation rate ($k_d$) resolution for the Biacore 8K assay.
* **HOW:** In the *Surface plasmon resonance* Methods section, state the duration of the association and dissociation phases (in seconds) and indicate when $k_d$ reached the instrument floor (e.g., $k_d < 10^{-5}\,\text{s}^{-1}$, corresponding to $K_D \le 1\text{ pM}$) in Extended Data Figure 5c–d.

#### 4. Technical and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Minor inconsistencies exist between the Abstract and Main Text sample counts and between the two Spike mutation lists in the Methods.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **WT Cohort Denominator in Abstract vs. Main Text:** The Abstract states *"A retrospective analysis of 1,103 SARS-CoV-2 wild-type-elicited mAbs shows that this method can increase the probability of identifying effective bnAbs to the XBB.1.5 strain from 1% to 40%"*, whereas Results (*The rational strategy to select bnAbs*) computes the `1%` (`2/141`) to `40%` (`2/5`) enrichment on the `141` potent autologous WT-elicited NAbs ($\text{IC}_{50} < 0.05\,\mu\text{g/mL}$ against D614G, selected from the 1,103 WT mAbs in Figure 1a). Clarify in the Abstract that the 141 potent WT-neutralizing mAbs (out of 1,103 total WT mAbs) formed the baseline screening denominator.
  2. **Missing `R493Q` in Mouse Serum Pseudovirus Spike Mutation List (Methods):** In *Mouse serum pseudovirus neutralization assays*, the mutation lists for `XBB.1.5`, `JN.1`, and `HK.3.1` list `Q498R` (inherited from `BA.2`) but omit the `R493Q` reversion (or list neither `Q493R` nor `R493Q`, unlike the earlier *Pseudovirus design and neutralization assays* section, which defines them relative to `BA.2` with `+R493Q`). Verify that the full-length spike plasmids carried wild-type `Q493` (corresponding to the `R493Q` reversion relative to `BA.1`/`BA.2`).
  3. **Cryo-EM Workflow Cross-Reference Typo (Methods):** In *Cryo-EM sample collection, data acquisition and structure determination*, change *"All dataset processing is shown in Extended Data Fig. 6"* to *"Extended Data Fig. 7"*.
  4. **Fc Modification Notation (Methods):** In *Generation of modified mRNA and LNPs*, define the exact amino acid substitutions for the `LS` modification on `CHKV-24` (`M428L/N434S`) alongside `LA` (`M428L/N434A`) on `BD55-1205`, and note whether the difference between `LA` and `LS` affects human FcRn binding kinetics in `Tg32` mice.
