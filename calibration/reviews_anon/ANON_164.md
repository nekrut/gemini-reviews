## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis

This manuscript presents `scRGCL`, a deep learning framework for single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) cell-type annotation that integrates a protein-protein interaction (PPI) graph representation module (residual GraphSAGE plus a 4-layer CNN), a supervised contrastive learning module (`SupCon`), and a classification module utilizing a "weight freezing" operation. The authors benchmark `scRGCL` across 12 human and mouse scRNA-seq datasets (`Kidney`, `Puram`, `Brain`, `Colon`, `Lung1`, `Lung2`, `Skin`, `Zheng68K`, `HCL`, `Liver1`, `Liver2`, `Pancreas`) against six baseline methods (`scBERT`, `scDeepSort`, `ACTINN`, `Garnett`, `SingleR`, `Scanpy`), reporting higher macro-F1 and accuracy scores.

While applying graph neural networks and contrastive representation learning to cell-type classification is a reasonable methodological direction, a rigorous audit of the mathematical formulations, architectural descriptions, evaluation protocols, and benchmark tables uncovers severe technical and presentation defects:
1. **All Mathematical Equations (Equations 1–10) Are Missing/Blank in the Manuscript:** Every numbered equation block (`(1)` through `(10)`) and nearly all inline mathematical symbols in the Methods section are completely blank (`| | (1) |`), rendering the mathematical exposition broken and unverifiable from the text.
2. **Conceptual Contradictions in the Graph Formulation and Loss Function:** In the "Graph representation module" (line 96), the text states that *"A graph represents a cell, nodes represent genes, and edges represent the relationships... Each gene is embedded as an 8D feature"*, using PPI networks with up to $29,600$ genes and $11,465,584$ edges (Table 2). Yet in Equation 9 (line 185), the authors state that they add the learned edge importance score directly into the minimization loss alongside Cross-Entropy and `SupCon` loss—which either mismatches tensor dimensions (adding an edge vector to a scalar loss) or drives edge weights toward $-\infty$ / $0$ during minimization rather than regularizing sparsity via an $L_1$ penalty.
3. **Mischaracterization of "Weight Freezing" as Sparse Connectivity:** The authors state that weight freezing applies a binary mask only during backward propagation while preserving dense weights during forward propagation, and claim this *"replace[s] the dense connections with sparse connections"* (lines 160–172). Freezing a subset of weights at their random initialization during backprop while multiplying by those nonzero random weights during the forward pass does **not** create sparse connections; it leaves the layer dense with untrained random weights.
4. **Internal Hyperparameter Contradictions and Biased Unassigned-Cell Filtering in Cross-Dataset Generalization:** The Methods contradict themselves on the learning rate (`0.01` on line 195 vs. `0.001` on line 208) and regularization (`Weight freezing` replacing dropout on lines 23 and 160 vs. *"we applied the R-drop regularization technique, set the dropout to 0.3"* on line 208). Furthermore, in the cross-dataset generalization experiment (`HCL` $\to$ `Lung2`, line 366), the authors state that *"If a single cell was identified as having scores for all cell types below the threshold 0.1, we deleted the cell"* before evaluating accuracy—discarding low-confidence test cells inflates test metrics.
5. **Corrupted Benchmark Tables (Tables 1 and 2):** In Table 1 (lines 52 and 55), `Colon` `[19]` and `T` (`Skin`) `[22]` have identical cell counts (`43,817`) and identical cell-type counts (`5`), indicating a copy-paste error. In Table 2 (line 74), the column headers (`Dataset | Tissues | Species | #Genes`) are shifted by one column relative to the data (`Network Name | Species | #Genes | #Edges`).

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Evaluation

* **Claim 1 (Abstract, line 10; Results, lines 224–273; Figures 2–3): "Experimental results on multiple datasets derived from different species and tissues show that scRGCL consistently outperforms current state-of-the-art methods, confirming its superior accuracy, robustness and generalizability."** — `[Moderately Supported]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Across the 9 datasets in Figure 2 and 2 mouse datasets in Figure 3, `scRGCL` achieves competitive F1 and accuracy values (~85–99%). However, the baseline comparisons omit recent fast graph/transformer/linear benchmarks (such as `CellTypist`, `scANVI`, or `SingleCellNet`), and `Scanpy` is not a supervised cell-type annotation algorithm (Scanpy's `tl.ingest` or marker overlap is not specified). In addition, no standard deviations, confidence intervals, or paired statistical significance tests across the 5 cross-validation folds are reported in the main text for the benchmark bar charts.
* **Claim 2 (Introduction, line 23; Methods, lines 160–172): "Different from the traditional dropout strategy, we add weight freezing to avoid overfitting... Weight freezing masks specific parameters instead of directly changing neurons to create sparse connections."** — `[Methodologically Flawed]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** As described on lines 160–172, weight freezing applies a binary mask during the backward pass so that masked weights have zero gradient update, while keeping all weights active during the forward pass. A weight that is not updated during backpropagation remains at its initial (nonzero) random value unless explicitly zeroed in the forward pass. Therefore, dense matrix multiplication still occurs over all weights during forward propagation; this does not create sparse connections, nor do the authors specify the masking rate or selection criterion (random vs. magnitude-based). Moreover, line 208 contradicts this claim by stating *"we applied the R-drop regularization technique, set the dropout to 0.3"*.
* **Claim 3 (Results, line 213–220; Table 3): "To fully grasp the importance of each module in scRGCL, we implemented three variants... GraphSAGE -> GCN, Without SupCon, Weight Freezing -> Drop out... removing or replacing any module leads to a drop in F1 and ACC."** — `[Fragile]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** In Table 3 (line 216), the ablation differences on `Puram` (`5,665` cells) are small (`F1 = 0.9036` for `scRGCL` vs. `0.8923` for `Weight Freezing -> Drop out`, a difference of `0.0113`), and no standard deviations across cross-validation folds or multiple random seeds are provided. Furthermore, if `scRGCL` already uses `dropout = 0.3` and `R-drop` as stated on line 208, the meaning of the `Weight Freezing -> Drop out` ablation variant is ambiguous.
* **Claim 4 (Results, lines 366–378; Figure 7): Cross-dataset generalization from `HCL` to `Lung2` achieves `F1 = 0.8959` and `ACC = 0.9163`, outperforming `scBERT`, `scDeepSort`, and `ACTINN`.** — `[Methodologically Flawed]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** On line 366, the authors state: *"If a single cell was identified as having scores for all cell types below the threshold 0.1, we deleted the cell."* First, deleting difficult/ambiguous test cells that fall below a confidence threshold before computing test accuracy and F1-score introduces survivorship bias unless the exact rejection rate is reported and applied identically across all methods. Second, if the model outputs softmax probabilities over $M$ classes, $\sum_{m=1}^M p_m = 1.0$; thus, having all class probabilities $< 0.1$ is mathematically impossible whenever $M \le 10$, and for $M = 17$ (`Lung2`) it only triggers when the output distribution is nearly uniform (maximum probability $< 0.1$ vs. uniform $1/17 \approx 0.059$). Third, line 366 cites `[18]` for `Lung2`, whereas in Table 1 `[18]` is `Lung1` and `[21]` is `Lung2`.

## 3. Technical & Methodological Critique

### 3.1 Missing Mathematical Equations and Broken Notation
In the manuscript text (lines 96–205), all nine numbered equation blocks—labeled `(1)`, `(2)`, `(3)`, `(4)`, `(5)`, `(7)`, `(8)`, `(9)`, `(10)` (note that `(6)` is skipped in the numbering)—are completely empty (`| | (1) |`), and every inline mathematical variable in the surrounding prose is missing (e.g., line 104: *"where is a learnable parameter vector... is the inner product, and is the Sigmoid function"*). In addition to the missing typesetting, the textual descriptions of these equations contain mathematical inconsistencies:
* **Edge Score in the Loss Function (Equations 1 and 9, lines 100–106 and 185–191):** Line 104 states that an edge importance score in $[0, 1]$ is computed via a sigmoid function, and line 106 states *"The edge score will be added to the final loss function as part of the model optimization."* Line 185 (`(9)`) then states *"we design a joint loss function that consists of three parts: the Cross-Entropy loss, the supervised contrastive loss, and the edge score."* Adding positive edge weights directly to a loss function being minimized does not make sense dimensionally (there are millions of edges in Table 2, whereas the loss is a scalar) unless an $L_1$ norm $\sum_{(u,v) \in E} |e_{uv}|$ is taken, and if it is minimized without a sparsity threshold, it simply penalizes all edges toward zero.
* **Graph Size vs. CNN Architecture (lines 96–134):** If each cell is represented as a graph where every gene is a node embedded in $\mathbb{R}^8$ ($N_{\text{genes}} \times 8$), and a 4-layer 1D/2D CNN with kernel size 3 and stride 2 is applied before a 512-unit fully connected layer (line 130), the authors never specify how variable gene counts ($11,315$ to $30,672$ across datasets in Table 1) are mapped to a fixed input dimension for cross-dataset generalization (`HCL` $\to$ `Lung2`).

### 3.2 Conflicting Hyperparameter Specifications
Two sections of the Methods provide contradictory training hyperparameters:
* **Line 195 ("Classification module"):** *"We use the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.01, and a weight decay of 1e-5."*
* **Line 208 ("Evaluation strategies"):** *"Specifically, we applied the R-drop regularization technique, set the dropout to 0.3, the batch size to 64 and the learning rate to 0.001, and used Adam as the optimizer."*
A $10\times$ difference in learning rate (`0.01` vs. `0.001`) and the unannounced introduction of `R-drop` and `dropout = 0.3` (when weight freezing was supposed to replace dropout) make it impossible to know what configuration was actually used.

### 3.3 Errors in Tables 1 and 2
* **Table 1 (lines 46–61):**
  * Row 4 (`Colon`, `10x`, `[19]`) lists `#Cells = 43,817` and `#Cell Types = 5`.
  * Row 7 (`T` [referred to in the text as `Skin`], `10x`, `[22]`) also lists `#Cells = 43,817` and `#Cell Types = 5`. Having the exact same cell count (`43,817`) and class count (`5`) across two unrelated datasets (`Colon` `[19]` and `Skin` `[22]`) is a tabular duplication error.
* **Table 2 (lines 74–83):**
  * The 4 column headers are `Dataset | Tissues | Species | #Genes`.
  * However, the 4 columns in every row are `PPI Network Name (e.g., BIOGRID) | Species (H. sapiens / M. musculus) | #Genes (29,600) | #Edges (1,073,748)`. The `Tissues` column does not exist in the data rows, shifting `Species`, `#Genes`, and `#Edges` under the wrong headers.

### 3.4 Actionable Recommendations for Revision
1. **Restore All Equations and Mathematical Symbols:** Provide complete, properly typeset mathematical expressions for Equations (1)–(10) and all inline variables, fix the missing Equation (6) numbering, and clarify how the edge scores are aggregated into a scalar regularization term in Equation (9).
2. **Clarify the Weight Freezing Mechanism and Reconcile Hyperparameters:** Explicitly write the forward and backward equations for weight freezing, explain why freezing random initial weights during backpropagation is claimed to yield "sparse connections," and resolve the contradiction between `lr = 0.01` (line 195) vs. `lr = 0.001` with `R-drop` and `dropout = 0.3` (line 208).
3. **Evaluate Cross-Dataset Generalization Without Deleting Test Cells:** Report `HCL` $\to$ `Lung2` accuracy and macro-F1 across all test cells without deleting low-confidence predictions (or report the exact fraction of rejected cells alongside coverage-risk curves for every baseline method).
4. **Fix Tables 1 and 2 and Report Fold Variance:** Correct the duplicated cell count (`43,817`) in Table 1, fix the shifted column headers in Table 2 (`Network | Species | #Genes | #Edges`), and report mean $\pm$ SD across the 5 cross-validation folds in Figures 2–3 and Table 3.
