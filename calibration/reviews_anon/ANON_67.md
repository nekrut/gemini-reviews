### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates the shared genetic architecture between chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD; 58,550 cases and 937,358 controls of European ancestry) and seven autoimmune diseases (ADs: Crohn's disease [CD], ulcerative colitis [UC], inflammatory bowel disease [IBD], multiple sclerosis [MS], primary sclerosing cholangitis [PSC], rheumatoid arthritis [RA], and systemic lupus erythematosus [SLE]) using publicly available GWAS summary statistics. The authors estimate genome-wide genetic correlations using `LDSC` and `HDL`, identify SNP-level cross-trait associations using `PLACO` and `CPASSOC` followed by `FUMA` clumping and `COLOC` Bayesian colocalization ($\text{PP.H4} \ge 0.70$), perform gene-level and pathway enrichment via `MAGMA` and `Metascape`, and prioritize candidate genes using `SMR`/`HEIDI` and `FUSION` TWAS across GTEx v8 tissues (whole blood, EBV-transformed lymphocytes, and lung). They report genetic correlations between COPD and five ADs, 57 pleiotropic loci (22 colocalized, with hotspots at `17q12` and `16p11.2`), 162 pleiotropic gene associations (125 unique genes), and four prioritized genes (`ORMDL3`, `PGAP3`, `MAPK3`, and `GMPPB`). All analyses rely on public GWAS summary statistics and standard open-source software packages.

While the multi-method post-GWAS workflow (`LDSC`, `HDL`, `PLACO`, `CPASSOC`, `COLOC`, `SMR`, and `TWAS`) is systematic and addresses a clinically relevant comorbidity, several methodological, statistical, and biological issues weaken the main conclusions. First, only three of the seven ADs (`MS`, `RA`, and `PSC`—where `PSC` is *negatively* correlated with COPD, $r_g = -0.205$) survive multiple-testing correction in global genetic correlation analysis; `COPD–CD` ($P_{\text{LDSC}} = 0.137, P_{\text{HDL}} = 0.024$) and `COPD–SLE` ($P_{\text{LDSC}} = 0.072, P_{\text{HDL}} = 0.042$) are non-significant in `LDSC` and do not survive Bonferroni or FDR correction across the seven traits in `HDL`, yet are carried forward and described in the Discussion as "strong genetic correlations." Second, applying `MAGMA` directly to `PLACO` composite-null $p$-values violates `MAGMA`'s single-trait $\chi^2$ covariance assumptions, and it is unclear whether the extended MHC region (`chr6:25–34 Mb`) was excluded prior to `LDSC`, `HDL`, and `PLACO`. Third, the manuscript does not account for genetic confounding by cigarette smoking (which positively drives COPD, RA, and CD while negatively correlating with PSC and UC) or asthma–COPD overlap at the canonical childhood asthma locus `17q12` (`ORMDL3`/`GSDMB`), and the therapeutic target section proposes MEK inhibitors (`trametinib`, `cobimetinib`) for `MAPK3` despite the Discussion noting that `MAPK3` deficiency *exacerbates* autoimmune inflammation. Fourth, multiple numerical contradictions exist between the text and Table 1/Table S14. These issues can be resolved through computational reanalysis and textual revisions without generating new data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Significant genome-wide genetic correlations exist between COPD and five of the seven analyzed autoimmune diseases (MS, RA, PSC, CD, and SLE): [Overstated].**  
  In Table 1, only three trait pairs pass a Bonferroni-corrected threshold ($\alpha = 0.05 / 7 = 0.0071$): positive correlations for `COPD–RA` ($r_g = 0.206, P = 8.73 \times 10^{-8}$ in LDSC; $r_g = 0.266, P = 2.13 \times 10^{-6}$ in HDL) and `COPD–MS` ($r_g = 0.125, P = 1.22 \times 10^{-4}$ in LDSC; $r_g = 0.149, P = 1.23 \times 10^{-4}$ in HDL), and a *negative* correlation for `COPD–PSC` ($r_g = -0.205, P = 8.86 \times 10^{-4}$ in LDSC). `COPD–CD` ($r_g = 0.056, P = 0.137$ in LDSC; $r_g = 0.114, P = 0.024$ in HDL) and `COPD–SLE` ($r_g = 0.099, P = 0.072$ in LDSC; $r_g = 0.113, P = 0.042$ in HDL) are null in `LDSC` and only nominally $P < 0.05$ in `HDL` before multiple-testing correction. Calling `COPD–CD` a "strong genetic correlation" in the Discussion overstates a weak, unadjusted nominal signal. Moreover, because smoking heaviness/initiation is a major genetic determinant of COPD and exerts opposite epidemiological effects on RA/CD (risk) versus PSC/UC (protective), unadjusted genetic correlations may partially reflect shared smoking genetics rather than intrinsic immunopathology.

* **Joint cross-trait analysis (`PLACO`, `CPASSOC`, and `COLOC`) identifies 57 shared risk loci (22 with colocalization $\text{PP.H4} \ge 0.70$), including convergence hubs at `17q12` and `16p11.2`: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Replicating 43 of the 57 `PLACO`-identified loci with `CPASSOC` and requiring $\text{PP.H4} \ge 0.70$ in `COLOC` (identifying 22 colocalized loci) provides reasonable statistical support for locus-level sharing at `17q12` (`ORMDL3`/`GSDMB`/`PGAP3`) and `16p11.2` (`MAPK3`). However, the Methods do not state whether the extended MHC region (`chr6:25–34 Mb`) was excluded (where extreme AD $Z$-scores and long-range LD violate `PLACO`'s composite null variance assumptions) or whether `PLACO` $Z$-scores were decorrelated using the bivariate `LDSC` intercept to control for sample overlap between cohorts. In addition, `17q12` (`ORMDL3`/`GSDMB`) is the strongest known locus for childhood and adult asthma, so its prominence in the COPD GWAS may reflect asthma–COPD overlap or diagnostic misclassification rather than pure emphysematous/airway COPD.

* **`MAGMA` gene-based and pathway enrichment analyses identify 162 pleiotropic genes significantly enriched in KEGG pathways such as Th17 cell differentiation and T-cell receptor signaling: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Two issues undermine this claim. First, `MAGMA` computes gene-level test statistics by aggregating SNP-level $p$-values under a single-trait multivariate normal LD model where SNP correlations scale with $r_{ij}$; feeding `PLACO` $p$-values (which test a product of two normal $Z$-scores, $Z_1 Z_2$, governed by a modified Bessel distribution whose covariance scales with $r_{ij}^2$) directly into `MAGMA` leads to miscalibrated gene-level $p$-values. Second, the Discussion (Paragraph 5) explicitly claims that *"Multi-layered analyses using MAGMA, GO, and KEGG revealed that the associated genes are significantly enriched in pathways... with particularly strong enrichment observed in Th17 cell differentiation and T cell receptor signaling"*, directly contradicting the Results section (*Functional Characterization*, Paragraph 1), which states that *"no pathways remained statistically significant after FDR correction (Table S14)"* in the KEGG analysis.

* **`SMR`/`HEIDI`, `TWAS`, and druggability analyses establish `MAPK3` (`ERK1`) as a high-priority druggable target for COPD–AD comorbidity via approved MAPK/ERK inhibitors (`trametinib`, `cobimetinib`): [Overstated].**  
  While `SMR` (with `HEIDI` filtering) and `FUSION` TWAS support cis-regulatory associations at `ORMDL3`, `PGAP3`, `MAPK3`, and `GMPPB`, the therapeutic repurposing interpretation for `MAPK3` is directionally inconsistent with the biology described in the manuscript. First, `trametinib` and `cobimetinib` are allosteric `MEK1/2` (`MAP2K1/2`) inhibitors used in *BRAF*-mutated oncology, not direct `MAPK3` (`ERK1`) inhibitors, and carry substantial pulmonary and gastrointestinal toxicity. Second, as the authors themselves state in the Discussion (Paragraph 4), `MAPK3` acts as a negative regulator of dendritic cell maturation and T-cell overactivation, and *"its deficiency exacerbates autoimmune encephalomyelitis."* Pharmacologically inhibiting a protective kinase whose loss of function worsens autoimmunity would be expected to aggravate rather than ameliorate autoimmune comorbidity.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Apply Multiple-Testing Correction to Table 1 and Control for Genetic Confounding by Smoking and Asthma
* **WHY:** Seven autoimmune traits were tested against COPD in `LDSC` and `HDL` without multiple-testing correction, leading `COPD–CD` ($P = 0.024$) and `COPD–SLE` ($P = 0.042$) to be misclassified as significant. Furthermore, cigarette smoking and asthma have strong genetic overlap with COPD and differential relationships with RA, CD, UC, and PSC.
* **WHAT:** Report Benjamini–Hochberg FDR $q$-values (or Bonferroni-adjusted $p$-values) in Table 1, temper claims regarding `CD` and `SLE` to "nominally associated in HDL only," and perform multivariable `LDSC` (`GenomicSEM` or `mtCOJO`) adjusting the COPD GWAS summary statistics for smoking behavior and asthma.
* **HOW:**
  1. Add an `FDR q-value` column to Table 1 (`p.adjust(p, method = "BH")` across the 7 traits for LDSC and 6 traits for HDL) and report the `LDSC` bivariate intercept and its standard error for each trait pair to quantify sample overlap.
  2. Using publicly available European GWAS summary statistics for smoking initiation / cigarettes per day (GSCAN) and asthma (GBMI / UKB), run multivariable LDSC (`GenomicSEM::ldsc`) or `GCTA-mtCOJO` to test whether the positive `COPD–RA` and `COPD–MS` genetic correlations, the negative `COPD–PSC` correlation, and the `17q12` (`ORMDL3`/`GSDMB`) locus persist after conditioning COPD on smoking and asthma.

#### 2. Replace `MAGMA`-on-`PLACO` With Separate Single-Trait `MAGMA` Intersection and Clarify MHC Handling
* **WHY:** `MAGMA` assumes standard single-trait $p$-value distributions and LD covariance ($r_{ij}$), which are violated by `PLACO`'s $Z_1 Z_2$ product statistic. Additionally, including the extended MHC region (`chr6:25–34 Mb`) in `LDSC`, `HDL`, and `PLACO` can severely bias global correlation and composite-null parameter estimates.
* **WHAT:** Confirm that `chr6:25–34 Mb` was excluded from `LDSC`, `HDL`, and `PLACO` (or re-run with MHC excluded), and validate the 125 unique pleiotropic genes by running `MAGMA` separately on the original COPD GWAS and each individual AD GWAS.
* **HOW:** Run standard `MAGMA v1.08` separately on the COPD summary statistics and on each of the five AD summary statistics using the 1000G EUR LD reference. Define confirmed gene-level pleiotropy by intersecting genes that achieve $P_{\text{FDR}} < 0.05$ in COPD *and* $P_{\text{FDR}} < 0.05$ in the paired AD (or by combining single-trait gene-level $Z$-scores via `PLACO` at the gene level), and compare this list against Table S9.

#### 3. Align Direction of Effect in `SMR`/`TWAS` With Drug Target Repurposing Recommendations
* **WHY:** Proposing `MEK`/`ERK` inhibitors (`trametinib`, `cobimetinib`) for `MAPK3` without checking whether increased or decreased `MAPK3` expression confers disease risk—especially when the Discussion notes that `MAPK3` deficiency worsens autoimmunity—risks recommending a contraindicated mechanism of action.
* **WHAT:** Report the signed `SMR` effect sizes ($\beta_{\text{SMR}}$) and `TWAS` $Z$-scores ($Z_{\text{TWAS}}$) for `ORMDL3`, `PGAP3`, `MAPK3`, and `GMPPB` across COPD and each AD in the main text, and revise the therapeutic assessment to match the direction of genetically predicted expression.
* **HOW:** Inspect Table S11 for the sign of $\beta_{\text{SMR}}$ and $Z_{\text{TWAS}}$ in whole blood, lymphocytes, and lung for COPD vs. each AD. State explicitly whether genetically predicted higher `MAPK3` expression increases or decreases risk of COPD and MS/RA/CD/SLE, and note that cytotoxic oncology MEK1/2 inhibitors (`trametinib`, `cobimetinib`) are not appropriate repurposing candidates if `MAPK3` plays an immunoregulatory protective role in dendritic cells.

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Multiple numerical mismatches between the Results text, Discussion, and Tables reduce reader confidence in the reported statistics.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Table 1 vs. Main Text Mismatches (*Results — Genetic Correlations*):**
     - For `COPD–RA` (LDSC), the text reports `rg = 0.205, P = 5.74×10−8`, whereas Table 1 reports `rg = 0.206 (0.038), P = 8.73×10−8`.
     - For `COPD–MS` (HDL), the text reports `P = 1.23×10−5`, whereas Table 1 reports `P = 1.23×10−4` (for $r_g = 0.149, \text{SE} = 0.034$, $Z = 4.38$, which corresponds to $P = 1.17 \times 10^{-5}$, indicating that `1.23×10−4` in Table 1 was likely copied from the adjacent LDSC column!).
     - For `COPD–CD` (HDL), the text reports `P = 0.025`, whereas Table 1 reports `P = 0.024`. Reconcile all values between the text and Table 1.
  2. **KEGG Enrichment Contradiction (*Results* vs. *Discussion*):** In *Results* (*Functional Characterization*), the authors state that *"no pathways remained statistically significant after FDR correction (Table S14)"* for KEGG, whereas *Discussion* (Paragraph 5) claims that KEGG revealed significant enrichment in *"Th17 cell differentiation and T cell receptor signaling."* Revise the Discussion to state that these KEGG pathways were only nominally enriched ($P_{\text{unadjusted}} < 0.05$) and did not pass FDR correction.
  3. **Gene Count Denominator Discrepancy (*Results — Identification and Functional Characterization of Pleiotropic Genes*):** The text states *"we identified 162 pleiotropic genes, of which 125 were unique (Table S9). Notably, 88.6% (140/158) of these genes were located within pleiotropic loci..."* Explain or correct the `158` denominator so it matches either `162` (total gene–pair hits) or `125` (unique genes).
  4. **SNPs vs. Loci Terminology (*Results — Shared Pleiotropic Genetic Loci*):** Change *"resulting in 2,020 genome-wide significant loci (P < 5×10−8; Table S2)"* to *"2,020 genome-wide significant SNPs"*, since those SNPs were subsequently clumped into the `57` pleiotropic loci.
  5. **Bonferroni vs. FDR Terminology (*Methods — Identification and Functional Enrichment of Pleiotropic Genes*):** Clarify *"A Bonferroni-adjusted significance threshold was applied to account for multiple testing (Pfdr<0.05)"*—specify whether Bonferroni adjustment ($0.05 / N_{\text{genes}}$) or Benjamini–Hochberg FDR ($q < 0.05$) was used.
  6. **Missing Word Typo (*Methods*):** In *Global Genetic Correlation Analysis*, change *"we further HDL analysis"* to *"we further performed HDL analysis"*; in *Identification and Functional Enrichment of Pleiotropic Genes*, change *"using MAGMA the FUMA platform"* to *"using MAGMA on the FUMA platform"*.
