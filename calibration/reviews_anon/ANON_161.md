## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis

This manuscript is a brief (~350-word, four-paragraph) editorial commentary summarizing early reports of highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b spillover into marine mammals (seals in Maine, 2022; sea lions in Peru, 2023), farmed fur mammals (mink in Spain, 2022; foxes and raccoon dogs in Finland, 2023), and domestic ruminants (goat kids in Minnesota and dairy cattle in Texas, Kansas, and Michigan in March 2024). While the topic of mammalian and ruminant H5N1 spillover is timely for veterinary and public health surveillance, the manuscript contains no primary data, no genomic or phylogenetic analyses, no quantitative methods, and no figures or tables. More importantly, even evaluated as a brief commentary, the text exhibits major internal contradictions between its bulleted Highlights and the body text, unsubstantiated claims regarding the direction of human-to-ruminant transmission, a factual error in *Orthomyxoviridae* taxonomy, and an inaccurate CRediT authorship declaration claiming "Data curation" and "Formal analysis" for an article that presents neither data nor analysis.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Evaluation

* **Claim 1 (Highlights 4 & 5): "Cross-species transmission of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus between ruminant animals and humans" and "Highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus RNA was present in milk."** — `[Overstated]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Neither cross-species transmission from ruminants to humans nor the detection of H5N1 viral RNA in milk is mentioned, discussed, or cited anywhere in the four paragraphs of the main text (lines 38–44). Listing two core findings in the article's Highlights that are completely absent from the body of the manuscript is a major editorial failure.
* **Claim 2 (Line 42): "A preliminary investigation into the source of the infection suggests that it was caused by infected wild birds or humans by HPAI virus on farms."** — `[Fragile]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** In discussing the March 2024 infections of goats in Minnesota and dairy cattle in Texas, Kansas, and Michigan, the authors assert that the cattle/goat infections were caused "by infected wild birds *or humans* by HPAI virus on farms" without citing any epidemiological or genomic source for human-to-ruminant introduction. This statement also reverses the transmission direction implied in Highlight 4 without evidence.
* **Claim 3 (Line 40): "Since 2022, HPAI H5N1 viruses of clade 2.3.4.4b have been detected via deep sequencing and evolutionary analysis to cross species boundaries, infecting wild sea mammals and farmed fur mammals... including seals in Maine, USA in 2022 [2], sea lions in Peru in 2023 [3], mink in Spain in 2022 [4], and foxes and arctic raccoon dogs in Finland in 2023 [5]."** — `[Well Supported]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** These four spillover events are accurately attributed to the cited literature (Puryear et al., 2023; Leguia et al., 2023; Agüero et al., 2023; Kareinen et al., 2024), although "arctic raccoon dogs" conflates arctic foxes (*Vulpes lagopus*) and raccoon dogs (*Nyctereutes procyonoides*) from the Finnish outbreak report.
* **Claim 4 (Line 38): "IV contains four genera, designated as *Alphainfluenzavirus*, *Betainfluenzavirus*, *Gammainfluenzavirus* and *Deltainfluenzavirus*. Among them, only one type of species is known, and these are named Influenza A virus (IAV), Influenza B virus (IBV), Influenza C virus (ICV), and Influenza D virus (IDV)."** — `[Methodologically Flawed]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The statement "Among them, only one type of species is known, and these are named..." is taxonomically garbled. Each of the four genera contains a single recognized viral species (*Alphainfluenzavirus influenzae*, *Betainfluenzavirus influenzae*, *Gammainfluenzavirus influenzae*, and *Deltainfluenzavirus influenzae* under current ICTV binomial nomenclature), rather than "only one type of species" across the four genera.

## 3. Technical & Methodological Critique

### 3.1 Absence of Analytical Content and Inaccurate CRediT Declaration
The manuscript consists entirely of four short narrative paragraphs summarizing four published papers (`[2–5]`) and early March 2024 veterinary notifications. No sequence alignments, phylogenetic trees, mutation tables (e.g., PB2 E627K, D701N, or T271A), or epidemiological metrics are included. Despite the complete absence of datasets or quantitative methodology, the CRediT authorship contribution statement (line 49) credits the first author with **"Data curation, Formal analysis"**. Authorship declarations must accurately reflect the work performed; claiming formal analysis and data curation on a 350-word opinion piece without data or analysis is inappropriate.

### 3.2 Disconnect Between Highlights and Main Text
A fundamental requirement of scientific reporting is that summary bullets (Highlights) reflect claims substantiated in the manuscript body. Here:
1. Highlight 4 claims *"Cross-species transmission of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus between ruminant animals and humans"*, and Highlight 5 claims *"Highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus RNA was present in milk"*.
2. The main text (line 42) never mentions milk or human cases contracted from cattle; it only notes that H5N1 was detected in *"tissues of infected goat kids in Minnesota, and varieties of samples from dairy cows on farms in Texas, Kansas, and Michigan"*, and then speculates that the farm animals were infected *"by infected wild birds or humans"*.

### 3.3 Actionable Recommendations for Revision
1. **Reconcile Highlights with the Main Text:** Either substantiate Highlights 4 and 5 in the main text with primary citations documenting viral loads in unpasteurized milk and cattle-to-human transmission (e.g., conjunctivitis in dairy workers), or remove those claims from the Highlights.
2. **Correct the Direction of Spillover and Taxonomic Nomenclature:** Remove the unsupported claim that humans introduced HPAI H5N1 into U.S. dairy cattle/goats on farms (line 42), correct the ICTV taxonomic description of the four *Orthomyxoviridae* influenza genera (line 38), and separate "arctic foxes (*Vulpes lagopus*)" from "raccoon dogs (*Nyctereutes procyonoides*)" when citing Kareinen et al. (line 40).
3. **Correct the CRediT Statement:** Remove "Data curation" and "Formal analysis" from the authorship contribution statement unless an explicit genomic or epidemiological dataset and analysis are added to the paper.
