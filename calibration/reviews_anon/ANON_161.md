## 1. Executive Summary & Synthesis

This manuscript is a brief (~350-word, four-paragraph) editorial commentary summarizing reports of highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b spillover into wild and farmed mammals across Asia, America, and Europe (skunks, foxes, bears, minks, seals, porpoises, sea lions, and dolphins `[2]`) and domestic ruminants in the United States in March 2024 (a goat in Minnesota and dairy cattle originating from five U.S. states: Texas, Kansas, New Mexico, Idaho, and Michigan `[3]`; note that contemporaneous genomic epidemiology of the first wave documented spread from Texas into eight other U.S. states—Idaho, Kansas, Michigan, Minnesota, New Mexico, North Carolina, Ohio, and South Dakota—nine states total). While the topic of mammalian and ruminant H5N1 spillover is timely for veterinary and public health surveillance, the manuscript contains no primary data, no genomic or phylogenetic analyses, no quantitative methods, and no figures or tables. More importantly, even evaluated as a brief commentary, the text exhibits major internal contradictions between its bulleted Highlights and the body text, unsubstantiated claims regarding the direction of human-to-ruminant transmission, a factual error in *Orthomyxoviridae* taxonomy, and an inaccurate CRediT authorship declaration claiming `Data curation` (first and second authors) and `Formal analysis` (third author) for an article that presents neither datasets nor formal analysis.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Evaluation

* **Claim 1 (Highlights 4 & 5): "Cross-species transmission of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus between ruminant animals and humans" and "Highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus RNA was present in milk."** — `[Overstated]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** Neither cross-species transmission from ruminants to humans nor the detection of H5N1 viral RNA in milk is mentioned, discussed, or cited anywhere in the four paragraphs of the main text (lines 38–44; line 42 only notes that *"Sick cows had a mild illness, and produced less milk"*). Listing two core findings in the article's Highlights that are absent from the body of the manuscript (even though documented in the cited external literature, e.g., Burrough et al. `[3]` and Uyeki et al. `[5]`) is a major editorial failure.
* **Claim 2 (Line 42): "Preliminary investigation into the source of these infection suggests that it was caused by infected wild birds or humans by HPAI virus on farms or the farm surroundings [3]."** — `[Fragile]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** In discussing the March 2024 infections of a goat in Minnesota and dairy cattle in five U.S. states (Texas, Kansas, New Mexico, Idaho, and Michigan), the authors assert that the cattle/goat infections were caused "by infected wild birds *or humans* by HPAI virus on farms or the farm surroundings" without citing any epidemiological or genomic evidence for human-to-ruminant introduction (the cited reference `[3]`, Burrough et al., implicates wild birds and cattle movement, not human-to-cattle transmission). This statement also reverses the cattle-to-human transmission direction documented in dairy workers `[5]`.
* **Claim 3 (Line 42): "Highly pathogenic avian influenza A (HPAI H5N1) virus, a subtype of IAVs, was reported in mammals (including skunks, foxes, bears, minks, seals, porpoises, sea lions, and dolphins, etc.) from Asia, America, and Europe [2]."** — `[Well Supported]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The broad host expansion of HPAI H5N1 across terrestrial and marine mammals in Asia, the Americas, and Europe is supported by the cited review literature (Charostad et al., 2023 `[2]`), though the text provides only a single secondary review citation rather than primary outbreak reports.
* **Claim 4 (Line 38): "IV contains four genera, designated as *Alphainfluenzavirus*, *Betainfluenzavirus*, *Gammainfluenzavirus* and *Deltainfluenzavirus*. Among them, only one type of species is known, and these are named Influenza A virus (IAV), Influenza B virus (IBV), Influenza C virus (ICV), and Influenza D virus (IDV)."** — `[Methodologically Flawed]`
  * **Evidence & Audit:** The statement "Among them, only one type of species is known, and these are named..." is taxonomically garbled. Each of the four genera contains a single recognized viral species (*Alphainfluenzavirus influenzae*, *Betainfluenzavirus influenzae*, *Gammainfluenzavirus influenzae*, and *Deltainfluenzavirus influenzae* under current ICTV binomial nomenclature), rather than "only one type of species" across the four genera.

## 3. Technical & Methodological Critique

### 3.1 Absence of Analytical Content and Inaccurate CRediT Declaration
The manuscript consists entirely of four short narrative paragraphs summarizing four published references (`[2–5]`: Charostad et al., Burrough et al., Sreenivasan et al., and Uyeki et al.) and early March 2024 USDA veterinary notifications. No sequence alignments, phylogenetic trees, mutation tables, or epidemiological metrics are included. Despite the complete absence of datasets or quantitative methodology, the CRediT authorship contribution statement (line 49) credits the first and second authors (`Shao-Lun Zhai` and `Sheng-Nan Chen`) with **`Data curation`** and the third author (`Jieshi Yu`) with **`Formal analysis`** (alongside `Conceptualization`, while CRediT roles such as `Investigation`, `Methodology`, or `Resources` are not applicable to a secondary commentary). Authorship declarations must accurately reflect the work performed (`Conceptualization`, `Writing – original draft`, `Writing – review & editing`, `Supervision`, `Funding acquisition`); claiming `Data curation` and `Formal analysis` on a 350-word opinion piece without data or analysis is inappropriate.

### 3.2 Disconnect Between Highlights and Main Text
A fundamental requirement of scientific reporting is that summary bullets (Highlights) reflect claims substantiated in the manuscript body. Here:
1. Highlight 4 claims *"Cross-species transmission of highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus between ruminant animals and humans"*, and Highlight 5 claims *"Highly pathogenic avian influenza A (H5N1) virus RNA was present in milk"*.
2. The main text (line 42) never mentions viral RNA in milk or human cases contracted from cattle; it only notes that H5N1 jumped into *"cows originating from Texas, Kansas, New Mexico, Idaho, and Michigan"* (producing less milk) and *"a goat in Minnesota"*, and then speculates that the farm animals were infected *"by infected wild birds or humans"*.

### 3.3 Actionable Recommendations for Revision
1. **Reconcile Highlights with the Main Text:** Either substantiate Highlights 4 and 5 in the main text by explicitly discussing the findings of Burrough et al. `[3]` (high viral loads and viral RNA in unpasteurized milk) and Uyeki et al. `[5]` (cattle-to-human transmission causing conjunctivitis in a Texas dairy worker, and subsequent spread across nine U.S. states), or remove those claims from the Highlights.
2. **Correct the Direction of Spillover and Taxonomic Nomenclature:** Remove the unsupported claim that humans introduced HPAI H5N1 into U.S. dairy cattle/goats on farms (line 42) and correct the ICTV taxonomic description of the four *Orthomyxoviridae* influenza genera (line 38).
3. **Correct the CRediT Statement:** Remove `Data curation` (assigned to Zhai and Chen) and `Formal analysis` (assigned to Yu) from the authorship contribution statement unless an explicit genomic or epidemiological dataset and formal analysis are added to the paper.
