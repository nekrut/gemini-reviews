# Forensic Peer Review: `ANON_17`

## 1. Executive Assessment & Calibration

**Overall Score:** **2.1 / 10.0** (Fundamentally Invalid)
* **Score Breakdown:** Rigor: 0.4/3.5 | Claims: 0.3/2.5 | Biology: 0.5/2.0 | Reproducibility: 0.9/2.0

> **[Erratum Note (Issue #7)](https://github.com/spond/gemini-reviews/issues/7):** An earlier version of this review contained two minor inaccuracies: (1) in Claim 1, it misstated the LD validation pair in Materials and Methods (`Marker selection and validation`) as imputed `rs333` vs. `rs62625034` ($r^2 = 0.93$), whereas the paper reports $r^2 = 0.93$ between `rs113010081` and `rs333` in 1000 Genomes (`CEU+GBR`) and $r^2 = 0.94$ between `rs113010081` and the `rs62625034` probe in UK Biobank British ancestry (note that later retraction analyses also examined alternative tag SNPs such as `rs113341849` / `rs79815064`); and (2) in the body of Claim 3 and Suggestion 4, it referred to "Pearson correlation ($r = 0.67$)" rather than "Spearman rank correlation of $\rho = 0.67$" (which was already correctly written as $\rho = 0.67$ in the Claim 3 header). The sample size $395,714$ is exact and unchanged. Both text points have been corrected below; the overall score (`2.1 / 10.0`, Fundamentally Invalid) is unchanged.

**Synthesis:** This manuscript uses UK Biobank genotype and mortality records ($n = 409,693$ individuals of British ancestry) to claim that homozygosity for the *CCR5*-$\Delta 32$ deletion is associated with an approximately 21% increase in all-cause mortality before age 76 and a significant Hardy-Weinberg deficiency at enrollment. However, the study's primary findings are invalidated by a critical genotyping probe artifact: rather than analyzing directly sequenced or properly imputed *CCR5*-$\Delta 32$ (`rs333`, a 32-bp deletion spanning GRCh37 `3:46414947–46414978`), the authors rely on an Affymetrix SNP array probe (`rs62625034`, `3:46414975`) whose target sequence physically overlaps the 32-bp deletion, causing elevated genotype call failure ($3.41\%$ missingness, or $13,979$ uncalled individuals out of $409,693$, leaving $395,714$ called individuals) and systematic under-calling of $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ homozygotes. In addition to this technical artifact, the Methods section contains two explicit algebraic errors in the definitions of cumulative survival probability ($S_i = \prod_{n=1}^i h_n$) and the Hardy-Weinberg deviation coefficient ($F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32}$), and the survival claim rests on a fragile, unadjusted one-tailed $P = 0.0089$ truncated at age 76 under a post-hoc recessive grouping.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim Audit

* **Claim 1:** "*CCR5*-$\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ homozygotes show a severe deficiency relative to Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) expectations at recruitment ($F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} = -0.0185$ [reported in Figure 1b caption as $-0.19$], empirical one-tailed $P = 0.0034$ across 5,932 MAF-matched variants), indicating elevated pre-enrollment mortality." — **[Methodologically Flawed]**
  * **Audit:** The manuscript states in Materials and Methods (`Marker selection and validation`) that the directly genotyped SNP `rs62625034` (`3:46414975` in GRCh37) on the UK Biobank Affymetrix array was used to identify $\Delta 32$ (`rs333`), validated by comparing its linkage disequilibrium with the tag SNP `rs113010081` ($r^2 = 0.93$ between `rs113010081` and `rs333` in 1000 Genomes `CEU+GBR`, and $r^2 = 0.94$ between `rs113010081` and the `rs62625034` probe in UK Biobank British ancestry; note that subsequent re-analyses of `rs333` in UK Biobank also evaluated tag SNPs `rs113010081`, `rs113341849`, and `rs79815064`). Crucially, the Affymetrix probe for `rs62625034` (`CCATACAGTCAGTATCAATTCTGGAAGAATTTCCA[G/T]ACATTAAAGATAGTCATCTTGGGGCTGGTCCTGCC`) binds directly across the 32-bp `rs333` deletion (`3:46414947–46414978`). Because the 32-bp deletion removes the left half of the probe binding site, hybridization intensity drops sharply in $\Delta 32$ carriers and homozygotes, resulting in genotype cluster failure and differential missingness. This is directly visible in the manuscript's reported sample sizes: out of $409,693$ British-ancestry participants, `rs62625034` genotypes are available for only $395,714$ individuals, representing $13,979$ missing calls ($3.41\%$ missingness), whereas the mean sample size of the 5,932 control SNPs in Figure 1b is $405,428$ ($1.04\%$ missingness). A $3.41\%$ call failure rate enriched among $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ homozygotes (where only $\sim 5,313$ homozygotes are expected out of $395,714$ at $p = 0.1159$, and $\sim 98$ fewer homozygotes suffice to generate a $-1.85\%$ deficit) completely explains the observed HWE deficiency as a genotyping call-rate artifact rather than biological mortality. Furthermore, the equation for $F$ in Materials and Methods (`Estimation of F`) is non-standard and mis-scaled: the text defines $P_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} = (1 + F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32}) P_{\Delta 32}^2$, which measures relative homozygote excess/deficit $\frac{O - E}{E}$ rather than Wright's inbreeding coefficient $F = \frac{P_{AA} - P_A^2}{P_A(1 - P_A)}$, and Figure 1b caption misstates the value as $F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} = -0.19$ instead of $-0.019$ (a 10-fold error; a value of $-0.19$ would fall off the left edge of the histogram in Figure 1b, where the bin lies at $-0.02$).

* **Claim 2:** "Homozygous $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ individuals have an approximately 21% increase in all-cause mortality rate before age 76 (log-rank one-tailed $P = 0.0089$; Cox proportional hazards HR $= 1.214$, 95% CI $[1.034, 1.426]$, one-tailed $P = 0.0089$)." — **[Fragile]**
  * **Audit:** First, the survival equation in Materials and Methods (`Estimation of survival probability`) is mathematically wrong: the text states that annual death rate is $h_i = O_i / N_i$ and the probability of surviving to age $i + 1$ is $S_i = \prod_{n=1}^{n=i} h_n$. Multiplying annual hazard rates $h_n \approx 10^{-3}$ produces a product of $\sim 10^{-105}$ over 35 years; the correct discrete-time Kaplan-Meier survival product is $S_i = \prod_{n=1}^{i} (1 - h_n)$. Second, the statistical significance of the mortality difference is fragile: it relies on a **one-tailed** $P = 0.0089$ (two-tailed $P = 0.0178$) without correction for multiple hypothesis testing across three genotypes ($\Delta 32/\Delta 32$, $\Delta 32/+$, $+/+$), alternative genetic models (additive, dominant, recessive, 2-d.f. genotypic), or alternative age cutoffs (truncating follow-up at age 76 while excluding partial birth-to-death intervals and ages 77–78). Third, because genotyping call failure at `rs62625034` depends on DNA sample quality and array intensity batch effects—which in turn correlate with recruitment center, recruitment date, and participant frailty—differential genotype missingness ($13,979$ excluded individuals) can confound survival estimates when evaluating a rare recessive genotype ($\sim 1.3\%$ of the cohort) with a modest raw event count.

* **Claim 3:** "The trajectories of observed HWE deviation across recruitment ages and predicted cumulative survival ratios from the mortality data are highly correlated ($\rho = 0.67, P = 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$; Extended Data Figure 1), providing independent joint evidence of deleteriousness." — **[Methodologically Flawed]**
  * **Audit:** In Extended Data Figure 1, the predicted deviation curve (`Extended Data Figure 1b`) is derived from the cumulative survival probability $S_i$, which is a cumulative product over age $i$ and therefore strongly autocorrelated and monotonically trending by mathematical construction whenever any hazard difference exists. Computing a standard Spearman rank correlation of $\rho = 0.67$ ($P = 1.4 \times 10^{-4}$) between age-indexed HWE deviations and a serially accumulated survival curve across age bins violates the independence assumption of the standard correlation test, severely inflating the nominal significance.

## 3. Concrete Suggestions for Improvement

1. **Replace Array Probe `rs62625034` with Direct Sequencing or Validated Copy-Number/Indel Calling (`Claim 1` & `Claim 2`):**
   * Re-evaluate Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and Cox proportional hazards survival using directly sequenced exome/whole-genome sequencing calls of `rs333` (`CCR5`-$\Delta 32$) in the UK Biobank cohort, where the 32-bp deletion is called directly by gapped read alignment rather than an overlapping hybridization probe (`rs62625034`) that suffers from $3.41\%$ missingness and allele dropout.
   * Report the exact Contingency Table of genotype counts ($+/+$, $\Delta 32/+$, $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$, and `Missing`) stratified by assessment center, genotyping array type (UK BiLEVE vs. UK Biobank Axiom), and mortality status.

2. **Perform Sensitivity Analysis for Differential Genotype Missingness (`Claim 1`):**
   * With $13,979$ missing genotypes out of $409,693$ British-ancestry individuals at `rs62625034`, test how many of those missing calls would need to be $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ homozygotes to eliminate the HWE deviation completely. At $P_{\Delta 32} = 0.1159$, expected homozygote frequency is $P_{\Delta 32}^2 = 0.01343$; a relative deficiency of $1.85\%$ across $395,714$ individuals corresponds to only $\sim 98$ missing $\Delta 32/\Delta 32$ calls ($0.70\%$ of the $13,979$ uncalled samples).

3. **Correct the Algebraic Formulas in Materials and Methods (`Claim 1` & `Claim 2`):**
   * In `Estimation of survival probability`, replace $S_i = \prod_{n=1}^{n=i} h_n$ with the survival product $S_i = \prod_{n=1}^{i} (1 - h_n)$.
   * In `Estimation of F` and the Figure 1b caption, distinguish the relative homozygote deviation $\frac{O_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} - E_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32}}{E_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32}} = \frac{P_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32}}{P_{\Delta 32}^2} - 1 \approx -0.0185$ from Wright's inbreeding coefficient $F = \frac{P_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} - P_{\Delta 32}^2}{P_{\Delta 32}(1 - P_{\Delta 32})} \approx -0.0024$, and correct the typo $F_{\Delta 32/\Delta 32} = -0.19$ in the Figure 1b caption to $-0.019$.

4. **Use Standard Two-Tailed Survival Models Without Arbitrary Age Truncation (`Claim 2` & `Claim 3`):**
   * Report two-tailed $P$-values for Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for sex, birth year/recruitment age, assessment center, genotyping array batch, and the top 10–20 genetic principal components across the full follow-up distribution (including ages $>76$ and exact person-days of follow-up without discarding partial years).
   * Remove the non-independent Spearman rank correlation test ($\rho = 0.67$) between cumulative survival curves and age-stratified HWE deviations in Extended Data Figure 1, or compare non-cumulative age-specific increments using a valid time-series or permutation test.
