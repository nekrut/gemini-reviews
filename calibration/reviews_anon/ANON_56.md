### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript reports the baseline results of a prospective, single-arm UK primary-care prostate cancer (PrCa) screening study (`NCT03857477`) evaluating a 130-SNP polygenic risk score (PRS) in individuals assigned male at birth aged 55–69 years of self-reported European ancestry. Of 40,292 individuals invited across 69 primary-care practices, 8,953 (22.2%) expressed interest and 6,393 were genotyped from postal saliva samples. Exactly 745 participants (11.7%) had a $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile (referenced to the ProtecT cohort) and were invited for multiparametric MRI (PI-RADS v2.1) and systematic/targeted transperineal prostate biopsy (mean 12.7 cores) regardless of serum PSA concentration or MRI findings. Among the 468 men (62.8%) who completed MRI and biopsy, PrCa was diagnosed in 187 (40.0%): 84 (44.9%) were NCCN Low/Very Low risk (Gleason Score $\le 6$), 63 (33.7%) were Intermediate Favorable, 28 (15.0%) were Intermediate Unfavorable, and 12 (6.4%) were High/Very High risk. Notably, 63.1% (118/187) of detected cancers occurred in men with $\text{PSA} < 3.0\ \mu\text{g/L}$ and 67.2% (125/186) occurred in men with negative MRI ($\text{PI-RADS} \le 2$). Sojourn-time modeling estimates that 15.6%–20.8% of screen-detected cancers represent overdiagnosis. Study protocols and statistical analysis plans are provided via the journal supplement.

This trial provides valuable empirical data on the yield of universal MRI and transperineal biopsy in men within the top decile of polygenic risk, demonstrating that a substantial fraction of Gleason Score $\ge 7$ tumors in high-PRS men occur below standard PSA ($3.0\ \mu\text{g/L}$) and MRI ($\text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$) triage thresholds. However, three methodological and reporting issues require attention: (1) because the study uses a single-arm design in which no participants with $\text{PRS} < 90\text{th}$ centile received PSA-independent, MRI-independent 12.7-core biopsies, the high cancer yield (40.0%, nearly half of which is Gleason 6) conflates PRS risk enrichment with the known high prevalence of histological prostate cancer discovered whenever unselected men aged 55–73 undergo reflex biopsy regardless of PSA or MRI; (2) the Abstract states that all 103 (55.1%) intermediate- or high-risk cancers "required treatment," whereas 63 of the 103 are NCCN Intermediate Favorable tumors that are routinely managed with active surveillance rather than radical treatment (as acknowledged in Discussion line 186); and (3) the manuscript inconsistently reports the percentage of clinically significant cancers missed by the UK diagnostic pathway as 70.9% in the Abstract (lines 21, 26) versus 42.5% in the Discussion (line 188, which conflates the 40 Intermediate Unfavorable/High-Risk cases from line 140 with all 103 csPrCa cases). These issues can be addressed through textual precision and transparent discussion of the single-arm design.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Universal transperineal biopsy in men with $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile yields a 40.0% prostate cancer detection rate (187/468), with 55.1% (103/187) having Gleason Score $\ge 7$ and low biopsy morbidity (Results, Tables 1–2, S12): [Well Supported].**  
  The prospective clinical workflow, central PI-RADS v2.1 reading, transperineal biopsy protocol, NCCN 2023 risk stratification, and adverse event tracking (0.2% sepsis, 0.4% UTI, 0.2% catheterization) are rigorously documented and directly support the reported detection rates within the biopsied cohort.

* **73 of 103 (70.9%) cancers "requiring treatment" would have been missed by the standard UK diagnostic pathway (Abstract lines 21–26, Results line 140, Table 2, Discussion line 188): [Overstated].**  
  First, classifying all 103 NCCN Intermediate/High/Very High risk cancers in the Abstract (line 21) as *"requiring treatment"* overstates clinical necessity: 63 of those 103 cancers (61.2%) are **Intermediate Favorable** (predominantly Gleason $3+4$ with $\le 10\%$ pattern 4; Figure 2), for which NICE and NCCN guidelines recommend active surveillance rather than immediate radical therapy—a distinction the authors themselves make in Discussion line 186 (*"requiring clinical management in 55.1% and radical treatment in 21.4%"*). Second, the manuscript defines and quantifies the "standard UK pathway" inconsistently across sections:
  - In **Table 2** and **Results line 140**, the "standard criterion" is defined as requiring *both* $\text{PSA} > 3.0\ \mu\text{g/L}$ and $\text{MRI PI-RADS} \ge 3$, which misses $57 + 14 + 3 = 74$ of the 102 MRI-evaluated Gleason $\ge 7$ cancers (reported as $73/103 = 70.9\%$ in the Abstract) and $14 + 3 = 17$ of the 40 Intermediate Unfavorable/High/Very High risk cancers ($17/40 = 42.5\%$).
  - In **Discussion line 188**, the UK pathway is instead defined using NICE age-specific PSA thresholds ($>3.5\ \mu\text{g/L}$ for ages 50–60 and $>4.5\ \mu\text{g/L}$ for ages 60–70) followed by MRI, and the text states that *"42.5% of the csPrCa would have been missed"*—inadvertently substituting the $42.5\%$ ($17/40$) figure for Intermediate Unfavorable/High risk cancers in place of all csPrCa (Gleason $\ge 7$, $n = 103$).

* **PRS-based screening detects a higher proportion of csPrCa and achieves a higher biopsy PPV (40%) than PSA- or MRI-based screening programmes (Discussion lines 190, 213): [Fragile].**  
  Comparing the 40% biopsy detection rate in this study against the 24.1%–35.5% rate in ERSPC (line 190) is asymmetric because all 468 participants here underwent systematic 12.7-core transperineal biopsy *irrespective of PSA or MRI*, whereas PSA/MRI screening trials only biopsy men who cross a PSA or MRI trigger. Historical trials with end-of-study protocol biopsies in unselected men with normal PSA (e.g., PCPT) showed that systematic biopsy detects histological PrCa in 15%–25% of men even without genetic enrichment. Because the study lacked a comparator arm of men with $\text{PRS} < 90\text{th}$ centile undergoing the same PSA- and MRI-blinded 12.7-core transperineal biopsy, the incremental PPV attributable to $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile versus universal systematic biopsy cannot be isolated from baseline data alone.

* **Within the $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile cohort, PSA and PI-RADS strongly predict csPrCa ($\text{AUC} = 0.78$), whereas further PRS stratification (90th vs. 99th centile) and PSA density add minimal discrimination (Results lines 154–160, Table S7): [Well Supported].**  
  Univariable and multivariable logistic regression models across the 468 biopsied men appropriately quantify the discrimination of age, family history, PSA, PSA density, PI-RADS, and within-top-decile PRS centile.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Align the Abstract and Discussion on Treatment vs. Active Surveillance and Reconcile the 70.9% vs. 42.5% Pathway Numbers
* **WHY:** Stating in the Abstract that 103 (55.1%) cancers "required treatment" conflicts with UK NICE practice (where Intermediate Favorable cancers are managed with active surveillance) and with Discussion line 186 (which states 21.4% required radical treatment). Simultaneously, citing 70.9% missed in the Abstract vs. 42.5% missed in Discussion line 188 confuses two different denominators ($n = 103$ Gleason $\ge 7$ vs. $n = 40$ Intermediate Unfavorable/High/Very High risk).
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. In the **Abstract (line 21)**, revise *"103 (55.1%) cancers were of intermediate or high risk and so required treatment"* to *"103 (55.1%) cancers were of intermediate or high risk requiring clinical management (including active surveillance or radical treatment), of which 40 (21.4%) were Intermediate Unfavorable, High, or Very High risk requiring radical treatment."*
  2. In **Table 2 (lines 120–122)**, note that under `PSA + MRI`, the number of missed Gleason $\ge 7$ cancers is $57 + 14 + 3 = 74$ out of 102 MRI-evaluated csPrCa cases ($72.5\%$, or $74/103 = 71.8\%$ including the 1 MRI-intolerant patient). Explain how $73/103$ ($70.9\%$) in the Abstract was derived (presumably counting the 1 MRI-intolerant patient who had $\text{PSA} > 3.0\ \mu\text{g/L}$ as detected).
  3. In **Discussion (line 188)**, clarify that under the $\text{PSA} > 3.0\ \mu\text{g/L} + \text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$ criterion, **70.9% (73/103)** of all Gleason $\ge 7$ cancers and **42.5% (17/40)** of Intermediate Unfavorable/High/Very High risk cancers would have been missed. Also report the exact numbers missed when applying the actual UK NICE age-specific PSA thresholds ($>3.5\ \mu\text{g/L}$ for ages 50–60; $>4.5\ \mu\text{g/L}$ for ages 60–70).

#### 2. Clarify the Calculation of Avoided Clinically Insignificant Cancers (27 / 26.7%)
* **WHY:** In Results line 176 and Discussion line 188, the authors state: *"Had we screened the 187 participants (in whom we diagnosed cancer in this study) with the standard UK PrCa screening method, we would have avoided detecting 27 (26.7%) of participants with clinically insignificant cancer."* However, Table 2 shows there were **84** Low/Very Low risk (Gleason $\le 6$) cancers, of which `PSA + MRI` missed **82** ($97.6\%$), `PSA (>3)` missed **67** ($79.8\%$), and `MRI` missed **68** ($81.0\%$). Neither the numerator (`27`) nor the denominator implied by `26.7%` ($\approx 27/101$) matches the 84 Low/Very Low risk cancers in Table 2.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Explicitly state in Results line 176 and Table S8–S10 what denominator ($N = 101$ or $N = 84$) and which screening rule produced the `27 (26.7%)` figure, and reconcile it with the `Low/Very Low` row of Table 2 ($n = 84$, where 82 of 84 Gleason 6 cancers are avoided by requiring $\text{PSA} > 3.0\ \mu\text{g/L} + \text{PI-RADS} \ge 3$).

#### 3. Contextualize the Single-Arm Design When Comparing Biopsy Yield Against PSA/MRI Trials
* **WHY:** Because all 468 biopsied men had $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile and underwent biopsy regardless of PSA or MRI, the study cannot directly measure the biopsy prevalence of PrCa in men with $\text{PRS} < 90\text{th}$ centile subjected to the same universal transperineal biopsy protocol.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Add a paragraph to the Limitations section explicitly noting that in a single-arm design with universal biopsy, part of the 40.0% cancer detection rate (particularly the 125 cancers found in MRI-negative $\text{PI-RADS} \le 2$ men, 68 of which were Gleason 6) reflects verification via systematic biopsy rather than PRS stratification alone, and compare the observed yield against historical unselected biopsy cohorts (such as the PCPT control arm) alongside the expected $2.9\times$ theoretical enrichment in the top PRS decile (line 60).

#### 4. Technical, Numerical, and Editorial Corrections
* **WHY:** Several factual and typographical errors appear in the text and tables.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Introduction (line 35):** Correct *"mortality was approximately 30 percentage points higher in the control group"* to *"relative prostate-cancer mortality was approximately 20% to 30% lower in the screened group (or ~30% higher relative risk in the control group)."* In ERSPC, the 22-year relative risk reduction is ~20–30%, whereas the absolute mortality difference is <1 percentage point, not 30 percentage points.
  2. **Results (line 138):** Change *"Of the 370 persons with negative results (PI-RADS < 2) on MRI"* to *"(PI-RADS ≤ 2)"*. As shown in Table 1 (lines 102–103), $\text{PI-RADS } 1$ ($7 + 1 = 8$) plus $\text{PI-RADS } 2$ ($238 + 124 = 362$) equals 370.
  3. **Results (line 79):** Clarify the participant accounting for the 745 men with $\text{PRS} \ge 90\text{th}$ centile: $468\text{ (biopsied)} + 177\text{ (personal withdrawal)} + 95\text{ (team withdrawal)} = 740$, plus 5 deaths $= 745$. Reword line 79 so that the 5 deaths are clearly listed outside (rather than *"including"*) the 95 study-team withdrawals.
  4. **Discussion (line 202):** Change *"(comprising 44.4% of detected PrCas)"* to *"44.9%"* ($84/187 = 44.9\%$, matching Table 2 line 119; or $83/187 = 44.4\%$ if referring specifically to the 83 on active surveillance).
  5. **Typos (lines 151, 156, 158):** Fix *"detection PrCa of by biopsy"* to *"detection of PrCa by biopsy"* (line 151); *"PI-RADS score re were"* to *"PI-RADS score were"* (line 156); and *"PI-RADS score m."* to *"PI-RADS score."* (line 158).
