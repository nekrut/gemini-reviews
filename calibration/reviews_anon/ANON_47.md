### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates regulatory divergence (`cis` vs. `trans`) between the house mouse subspecies *Mus musculus musculus* and *M. m. domesticus* and its relationship to asymmetric F1 hybrid male sterility using whole-testis mRNA-seq ($N=3$ biological replicates per genotype; 7.5 Gb mean 100-bp paired-end reads per sample). To avoid confounding from inbreeding depression in the parental lines, the authors generate intrasubspecific outbred parental controls (*M. m. musculus*$^{\text{PWK}} \times \text{M. m. musculus}^{\text{CZII}}$ and *M. m. domesticus*$^{\text{LEWES}} \times \text{M. m. domesticus}^{\text{WSB}}$) and compare their gene expression against reciprocal intersubspecific F1 hybrids between $\text{PWK}$ and $\text{LEWES}$, which produce fertile males in one direction ($\text{LEWES}\ \female \times \text{PWK}\ \male$) and sterile males with reduced testis weight and sperm count in the reciprocal direction ($\text{PWK}\ \female \times \text{LEWES}\ \male$). Across 9,851 testable autosomal genes in fertile hybrids, they classify $24\%$ as `cis`-only, $9\%$ as `trans`-only, and $44\%$ as `cis` + `trans` (including $13\%$ strictly compensatory and $7\%$ `cis` $\times$ `trans`), reporting a highly significant excess of opposing `cis` and `trans` changes ($P < 0.0001$), directional `cis`-regulatory evolution in transcriptional regulation GO categories, X-chromosome overexpression in sterile hybrids, and a strong enrichment of opposing/compensatory `cis`–`trans` changes among the 63 misexpressed and 202 aberrantly expressed autosomal genes unique to sterile hybrids. Raw sequencing reads are deposited in NCBI BioProject under accession `PRJNA286765`; custom analysis scripts are not archived.

The experimental cross design—using intrasubspecific F1 heterozygotes as parental controls and leveraging asymmetric reciprocal F1 sterility to separate hybridization effects from sterility-associated misexpression—is biologically well-conceived. However, the quantitative inference of "widespread compensatory evolution" and its enrichment among misexpressed genes is undermined by two severe statistical flaws in the primary analysis: (1) **shared-term regression / subtraction artifact (the Fraser–Wittkopp–Goncalves artifact)**: because the `trans` effect is estimated as the difference between the parental log-ratio $\log_2(P_{\text{dom}}/P_{\text{mus}})$ and the hybrid allelic log-ratio $\log_2(H_{\text{dom}}/H_{\text{mus}})$ (where $\text{cis} = \log_2(H_{\text{dom}}/H_{\text{mus}})$ and $\text{trans} = P - H$), any sampling variance or measurement error in the hybrid allelic ratio $H$ enters `cis` with a $+1$ sign and `trans` with a $-1$ sign, mechanically creating a strong negative covariance ($\text{Cov}(\hat{\text{cis}}, \hat{\text{trans}}) = -\text{Var}(H) < 0$) and artificially inflating opposing (`cis` $\times$ `trans` and compensatory) classifications even under pure white noise; and (2) pooling reads across the 3 biological replicates and running binomial and Fisher's exact tests on the pooled read counts (Methods line 7) treats individual sequencing reads rather than biological mice as independent units, ignoring biological overdispersion and inflating both `cis` and `trans` false-positive rates. Re-analyzing the existing dataset with cross-replicate splitting (using separate hybrid replicates to estimate `cis` and `trans`) and overdispersion-aware negative binomial models (`DESeq2`/`edgeR`) is essential to salvage the quantitative claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Sterile F1 hybrid males ($\text{PWK}\ \female \times \text{LEWES}\ \male$) exhibit widespread overexpression of the *M. m. musculus* X chromosome compared to fertile reciprocal F1 hybrids and autosomal genes: [Well Supported].**  
  The X-chromosome overexpression specific to the sterile cross direction (Figure 2, Supplemental Tables S3–S4) is large in magnitude, uses the fertile reciprocal F1 hybrid ($\text{LEWES}\ \female \times \text{PWK}\ \male$) as a direct baseline, and is consistent with prior microarray and flow-sorted germ cell studies demonstrating disrupted meiotic sex chromosome inactivation (MSCI).

* **When both `cis` and `trans` regulatory changes occur, they act in opposition much more often than expected under neutrality ($P < 0.0001$), demonstrating widespread compensatory evolution: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Following the standard McManus/Goncalves framework, the authors estimate the `cis` effect from the fertile hybrid allelic ratio $H = \log_2(H_{\text{dom}}/H_{\text{mus}})$ and the `trans` effect from $T = P - H$, where $P = \log_2(P_{\text{dom}}/P_{\text{mus}})$ is the parental subspecies ratio, using the *same* pooled hybrid read counts $H$ in both quantities. Because $H$ appears positively in $\hat{\text{cis}} = H$ and negatively in $\hat{\text{trans}} = P - H$, any random binomial/biological error $\epsilon_H$ in the hybrid allelic counts shifts $\hat{\text{cis}}$ by $+\epsilon_H$ and $\hat{\text{trans}}$ by $-\epsilon_H$. Consequently, under a null model where true `trans` effects are zero or independent of `cis` effects, shared estimation error in $H$ automatically generates a massive excess of opposing `cis` and `trans` signs (and specifically inflates the "compensatory" category where $P \approx 0$ and $|\epsilon_H|$ is large enough to pass the unadjusted binomial test on pooled reads). Compounding this artifact, pooling reads across replicates and applying a binomial test (Methods line 7) treats tens of thousands of RNA-seq reads from 3 mice as independent Bernoulli trials, turning biological variance between replicates into thousands of "statistically significant" allelic imbalances.

* **Genes uniquely misexpressed (63 genes) or aberrantly expressed (202 genes) in sterile F1 hybrids are nonrandomly enriched for opposing / compensatory `cis`–`trans` regulatory changes: [Fragile].**  
  While the biological hypothesis that disrupted `cis`–`trans` co-adaptation underlies Dobzhansky–Muller incompatibilities is plausible, the statistical enrichment reported in Table 4 and Figure 3 is confounded by two factors: (i) the "compensatory" category itself is heavily inflated by overdispersed biological variance in the fertile hybrid counts via the shared-term $H$ artifact described above, and genes with high expression variance or cell-type specificity across testis stages are simultaneously more likely to exhibit noisy allelic ratios in fertile hybrids and fold-change shifts in sterile hybrids; and (ii) whole-testis RNA-seq in sterile hybrids reflects altered germ-cell stage proportions (incomplete meiotic arrest and reduced post-meiotic spermatids, Supplemental Table S16), which shifts bulk transcript abundance for stage-specific genes relative to both parents.

* **Subspecies-level `cis`-regulatory divergence is corroborated across a broader panel of 15 wild-derived inbred mouse lines and shows directional selection in transcriptional regulation GO categories: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Cross-validating the differentially expressed genes against the 7 *M. m. domesticus* and 8 *M. m. musculus* lines from Phifer-Rixey et al. (2014) ($P = 1.75 \times 10^{-16}$, with 57% of overlapping genes falling in `cis` and reinforcing `cis` + `trans` categories) confirms that the major parental expression differences are not merely strain-specific idiosyncrasies of PWK and LEWES. Note, however, that the lowest overlap with Phifer-Rixey et al. occurred precisely in the opposing `cis` $\times$ `trans` and opposing `cis` + `trans` categories (line 63), which is consistent with those categories containing substantial within-experiment estimation noise.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate the Shared-Term Regression Artifact Using Cross-Replicate Splitting
* **WHY:** Estimating $\hat{\text{cis}} = H$ and $\hat{\text{trans}} = P - H$ from the same hybrid data $H$ induces a negative error covariance $\text{Cov}(\hat{\text{cis}}, \hat{\text{trans}}) = -\text{Var}(\epsilon_H)$ that creates spurious opposing `cis` and `trans` changes and invalidates the neutral expectation test in Table 2.
* **WHAT:** Re-estimate `cis` and `trans` divergence and re-run the test for opposing vs. reinforcing changes using independent biological replicates for the two terms (cross-replicate splitting, as established by Fraser et al. / Randolph et al.).
* **HOW:** Split the fertile F1 hybrid biological replicates into two disjoint subsets ($H_1$ and $H_2$; e.g., across the $\binom{3}{1} = 3$ possible 1-vs-2 splits of the 3 replicates, or 2-vs-1). Estimate $\hat{\text{cis}} = H_1$ using subset 1 and $\hat{\text{trans}} = P - H_2$ using subset 2, so that estimation errors $\epsilon_{H_1}$ and $\epsilon_{H_2}$ are statistically independent ($\text{Cov}(\epsilon_{H_1}, \epsilon_{H_2}) = 0$). Recompute Table 2, Table 4, and Figure 3 under cross-replicate splitting.

#### 2. Replace Pooled-Read Binomial and Fisher's Exact Tests with Overdispersion-Aware Replicate Models
* **WHY:** Pooling reads across the 3 biological replicates and running a binomial test on total read counts assumes zero biological variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{bio}} = 0$) among mice of the same genotype. For moderately to highly expressed testis genes (hundreds to thousands of reads), a 5% biological difference in allelic ratio between individual mice produces an extreme binomial $p$-value when reads are pooled.
* **WHAT:** Model allele-specific and parental read counts across the $N=3$ individual biological replicates using a beta-binomial or negative binomial generalized linear model (`DESeq2`, `edgeR`, or `mamba`/`asep`) that estimates biological dispersion across replicates.
* **HOW:** Fit a negative binomial GLM with main effects for `Species/Allele` (*musculus* vs. *domesticus*), `Condition` (Parental vs. F1 Hybrid), and their interaction `Species:Condition` (which directly tests the `trans` effect $P - H$ while accounting for replicate-to-replicate variance). Report the revised proportions of `cis`-only, `trans`-only, and `cis` + `trans` genes.

#### 3. Stratify the Sterile Hybrid Misexpression Analysis by Testis Spermatogenic Cell-Type Markers
* **WHY:** Because sterile $\text{PWK}\ \female \times \text{LEWES}\ \male$ males have reduced testis weights and sperm counts (Supplemental Table S16), whole-testis RNA-seq conflates cell-autonomous regulatory incompatibility with bulk depletion of post-meiotic round/elongating spermatids and relative enrichment of somatic Sertoli/Leydig and pre-meiotic cells.
* **WHAT:** Stratify the 63 misexpressed and 202 aberrantly expressed autosomal genes by their known spermatogenic stage expression profiles (somatic, mitotic spermatogonia, pachytene spermatocytes, post-meiotic spermatids).
* **HOW:** Use published stage-sorted mouse germ-cell expression data (cited in the manuscript as `[AUTHOR et al.] 2013` / Chalmel et al.) to covariate-adjust or stratify Table 4 and Figure 3 by cell-type expression class, verifying that the enrichment of cross-replicate-validated compensatory genes among sterile-hybrid misexpressed genes holds within individual spermatogenic cell stages.
