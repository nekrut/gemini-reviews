### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates whether live bacteria colonize human fetal organs during the second trimester of gestation (12–22 weeks estimated gestational age, EGA) and prime tissue-resident memory T cells *in utero*. The authors profile fetal T cells across gut, lung, liver, skin, thymus, mesenteric lymph nodes (mLN), and blood using suspension mass cytometry (CyTOF); assay bacterial DNA across fetal tissues and negative controls using BactQuant 16S qPCR and high-throughput 16S rRNA sequencing at two facilities; culture bacteria aerobically and anaerobically from 8 fetal donors; visualize cocci-like structures and 16S rRNA in the fetal gut lumen via scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and RNAscope *in situ* hybridization (RNA-ISH; $n = 3\text{–}4$); and perform syngeneic co-cultures of fetal mLN dendritic cells (DCs) and T cells ($n = 4$) pulsed with heat-killed fetal bacterial isolates (*Staphylococcus* and *Lactobacillus*). Raw 16S sequencing data are deposited in GEO (GSE169306), though custom Python and R analysis scripts are not publicly archived.

While the CyTOF characterization of second-trimester human fetal T-cell subsets confirms prior reports of tissue-resident $\text{CD45RO}^+$ and regulatory T cells, the central claims of *in utero* microbial colonization and microbe-specific memory T-cell priming are undermined by severe sampling and contamination confounders, kit-contaminant taxonomic signatures, and non-specific polyclonal T-cell activation assays. Specifically, all fetuses were obtained via prostaglandin-induced vaginal delivery through the birth canal, which directly explains why canonical vaginal and skin taxa (*Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Prevotella*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*) exhibit a decreasing gradient from surface-exposed tissues (placenta, skin) to mucosal tracts open to the exterior (gut, lung) and internal organs (spleen, thymus). Furthermore, several highlighted "fetal-enriched" 16S taxa (*Bradyrhizobium*, *Afipia*, *Brevundimonas*, *Pseudomonas*, *Flavobacterium*) are well-characterized reagent/water contaminants amplified by a 54-cycle nested PCR protocol, and the 55–70% T-cell proliferation observed upon *Staphylococcus* exposure is consistent with superantigen- or innate PAMP-driven polyclonal activation rather than cognate antigen-specific memory recall. Resolving these foundational limitations requires hysteresis-free surgical/cesarean or *in utero* sampling controls, MHC-blocking and superantigen-Swap controls in T-cell assays, and major reframing of the manuscript's causal claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Second-trimester human fetal organs harbor distinct effector memory ($\text{CD45RO}^+$) and regulatory ($\text{CD25}^+\text{FOXP3}^+\text{CD152}^+$) T-cell populations compared to blood and adult tissues: [Well Supported].**  
  The CyTOF profiling across fetal gut ($n=7$), lung ($n=9$), liver ($n=5$), skin ($n=6$), thymus ($n=8$), and mLN ($n=3$) clearly demonstrates tissue-specific clustering, $\text{PD1}^+\text{Ki67}^+$ Treg enrichment, and PMA/ionomycin-inducible TNF and $\text{IFN}\gamma$ production capacity (Figures 1 and S1). However, the existence of $\text{CD45RO}^+$ T cells in the fetal thymus and periphery is a known developmental feature (including homeostatic proliferation in lymphopenic fetal environments and maternal microchimerism) and does not by itself establish microbial priming.

* **16S rRNA sequencing and qPCR demonstrate a true *in utero* microbiome across fetal organs distinct from contamination: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  All fetal specimens were delivered vaginally following medical induction with prostaglandins (STAR Methods). Laboratory PBS, reagent, and operator hand-swab controls never pass through the maternal vaginal canal and therefore cannot control for intrapartum exposure to high-biomass cervicovaginal flora. Crucially, the authors' observation of a "decreasing gradient of microbial diversity from external or surface-exposed fetal organs [placenta, skin] to internal organs [spleen, thymus]" (Figures 2F and 3C) is the exact physical signature expected from intrapartum surface contamination and fetal swallowing/aspiration of vaginal fluid during delivery. In addition, the primary 16S protocol used `McrBC` digestion followed by three rounds of PCR (V2–V6 enrichment, V4–V5 nested PCR, and barcoding PCR totaling 54–72 cycles) and rarefaction to 1,000 reads. Five of the seven "fetal-enriched taxa" reported in Figure 2G (*Bradyrhizobium*, *Afipia*, *Pseudomonas*, *Flavobacterium*, and *Brevundimonas*) are notorious reagent and ultrapure-water contaminants ("kitome") whose differential recovery between tissue extracts and pure PBS is a classic artifact of carrier host DNA altering extraction yield and low-template PCR kinetics.

* **Viable bacteria isolated from freshly harvested fetal organs represent authentic *in utero* colonizers: [Overstated].**  
  The dominant genera cultured from fetal tissues—*Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Enterococcus*, *Prevotella*, and *Finegoldia* (Figure 3D)—are the hallmark commensals of the human vagina, perineum, and skin. Because matched maternal vaginal/cervical swabs were not collected and surgical (non-vaginal) deliveries were not compared, intrapartum colonization during prostaglandin-induced labor cannot be distinguished from *in utero* residency.

* **SEM and 16S rRNA-ISH confirm localized bacterial colonization within the 14-week fetal mid-gut lumen: [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 4A–B, SEM identification of 1–2 $\mu\text{m}$ spherical structures embedded in meconium mucin threads relies solely on morphology without immunogold labeling or elemental/correlative confirmation to distinguish cocci from mucin granules, cellular debris, or extracellular vesicles. In the RNA-ISH assays ($n = 3$, Figure 4C–E), no scrambled-sequence negative control probe was run on the mucin-rich fetal gut lumen (which is prone to non-specific dye/probe trapping); instead, different tissues (liver and mLN) were used as negative controls, display intensities were manually rescaled (150% for EB-16S-rRNA, 120% for EPCAM), and the CellProfiler threshold correction factor was varied from 1 to 4 across images.

* **Fetal-isolated bacteria induce cognate memory T-cell activation and expansion in syngeneic fetal mLN co-cultures, proving prior *in utero* priming: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Co-culturing fetal mLN T cells with autologous DCs pulsed with heat-killed *Staphylococcus* or *Lactobacillus* resulted in 70% ($\pm 15\%$) and 55% ($\pm 13\%$) of T cells diluting CTV over 6 days (Figure 5C–F). In an unimmunized repertoire, true single-microbe cognate memory T cells occur at precursor frequencies of $10^{-5}\text{–}10^{-3}$; having 70% of all sorted T cells divide from a starting memory pool of $<12\%$ $\text{CD45RO}^+$ cells indicates polyclonal mitogenic or superantigen-driven activation (e.g., *Staphylococcus* enterotoxins/TSST-1 cross-linking MHC-II and TCR $\text{V}\beta$ domains, which remains fully active on 1% PFA-fixed DCs in Figure 6) or innate TLR-driven bystander proliferation. Furthermore, the PTI control experiment (Figure S6D–H) treated DCs with brefeldin A and monensin for only 2 hours and washed them out prior to the 6-day co-culture; because brefeldin A/monensin inhibition of Golgi transport is rapidly reversible upon washout, this control does not block cytokine secretion during the 6-day expansion.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Intrapartum Vaginal Exposure and Kitome Amplification Artifacts
* **WHY:** Delivering fetuses through the birth canal after prostaglandin induction exposes external tissues (placenta, skin) and open mucosal tracts (gut, lung) to cervicovaginal bacteria (*Gardnerella*, *Lactobacillus*, *Prevotella*, *Staphylococcus*), while laboratory PBS buffers never contact the birth canal. Additionally, nested 54+ cycle PCR on low-biomass samples amplifies reagent contaminants (*Bradyrhizobium*, *Afipia*, *Brevundimonas*).
* **WHAT:** Explicitly evaluate the overlap between detected fetal taxa and standard vaginal/reagent microbiomes, and control for carrier-DNA effects in qPCR and 16S sequencing.
* **HOW:** (1) Apply formal statistical decontamination tools (`decontam` in R using both frequency mode against BactQuant Ct values and prevalence mode against all negative controls) to the unrarefied OTU table in Table S3 before computing $\alpha$/$\beta$-diversity. (2) Re-classify the taxa in Figures 2G and 3D against published human vaginal microbiome databases (e.g., Ravel et al., 2011) and reagent contaminant lists (Salter et al., 2014; Eisenhofer et al., 2019). (3) Include spike-in or host-DNA-matched negative controls (e.g., certified microbe-free human genomic DNA in PBS) in BactQuant qPCR to verify that lower Ct values in tissues are not driven by 100 ng of host DNA cross-reacting with universal 16S primers/probes.

#### 2. Distinguish Cognate TCR Memory Recall from Superantigen and Bystander Activation
* **WHY:** A 70% proliferation rate across total mLN T cells in 6 days in response to *Staphylococcus*-pulsed (and even PFA-fixed) DCs is the classic hallmark of staphylococcal superantigens (which bind outside the peptide groove of MHC-II even on PFA-fixed APCs and cross-link TCR $\text{V}\beta$ families) or innate PAMP stimulation rather than peptide-specific memory recall. Moreover, a 2-hour pre-incubation with reversible protein transport inhibitors (brefeldin A/monensin) followed by washout does not block cytokine release over a 6-day culture.
* **WHAT:** Perform MHC class I/II blocking, naive vs. memory pre-sorting, and non-fetal bacterial specificity controls.
* **HOW:** (1) Repeat the DC–T cell co-culture in the presence and absence of pan-HLA-DR/DP/DQ and pan-HLA-A/B/C blocking antibodies, and assay TCR $\text{V}\beta$ skewing or CDR3 clonality to test for superantigen versus oligoclonal peptide-specific expansion. (2) Sort $\text{CD45RA}^+\text{CD45RO}^-$ naive T cells and $\text{CD45RO}^+$ memory T cells separately prior to CTV labeling to prove that the expanding $\text{CTV}^{\text{neg}}\text{CD45RO}^+$ cells originate from pre-existing $\text{CD45RO}^+$ memory cells rather than *de novo* activation of naive T cells converting to $\text{CD45RO}^+$ during the 6-day culture. (3) Include an unrelated environmental bacterium that the fetus could never have encountered *in utero* (e.g., *B. subtilis* or a marine bacterium) at the same MOI (1:10) to measure baseline innate/bystander T-cell proliferation.

#### 3. Correct the Differential Abundance Formula and Statistical Testing
* **WHY:** In STAR Methods ("Estimation of Differentially Enriched Bacterial Taxa in Gut"), the formula for "Relative Percentage Abundance" ($\text{RPA}_{\text{OTU}} = \frac{S_{\text{Gut}} \times 100}{S_{\text{Gut}} + S_{\text{Spleen/Thymus}} + S_{\text{PBS}}}$) actually computes an unnormalized sample-count prevalence ratio rather than read relative abundance, which is mathematically biased whenever group sample sizes ($N_{\text{Gut}}$, $N_{\text{Spleen}}$, $N_{\text{PBS}}$) differ. Furthermore, Figures 5 and 6 switch inconsistently between paired and unpaired $t$-tests on the exact same matched donor design ($n = 4$) without multiple-comparison adjustment.
* **WHAT:** Replace the ad hoc $\text{RPA}_{\text{OTU}}$ prevalence ratio with a sample-size-normalized compositional differential abundance method, and apply consistent paired, multiple-testing-corrected statistics.
* **HOW:** (1) Normalize prevalence by the total number of samples in each group ($P_{\text{group}} = S_{\text{group}} / N_{\text{group}}$) and use established compositional microbiome tools (e.g., ANCOM-BC or ALDEx2) with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction to generate Figures 2H and S2K. (2) In Figures 4D–E ($n=3$), 5D/F/H ($n=4$), and 6C/D/E ($n=4$), use a repeated-measures one-way ANOVA (or Friedman test) with post-hoc multiple-testing correction across all conditions rather than unadjusted pairwise unpaired $t$-tests.

#### 4. Standardize RNAscope Imaging Thresholds and Controls
* **WHY:** Varying the CellProfiler threshold correction factor between 1 and 4 across samples and lacking a scrambled probe control on serial fetal gut sections can create artificial signal differences between mucin-rich gut lumen and solid parenchymal organs (liver, mLN).
* **WHAT:** Re-quantify the RNA-ISH images using a fixed threshold parameter across all tissues and include negative control probe quantification on gut sections.
* **HOW:** Run the Erben et al. (2018) CellProfiler pipeline with an identical threshold correction factor across all gut, liver, and mLN fields of view without prior manual rescaling of channel intensities in inForm, and report the raw spot counts per $\text{mm}^2$ of tissue/lumen alongside negative-control probe (e.g., `dapB`) stained serial gut sections.
