### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript characterizes the 2023 transatlantic incursion of clade 2.3.4.4b highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) A(H5N5) viruses into Atlantic Canada, encompassing 41 wildlife detections across Prince Edward Island (PEI), Nova Scotia (NS), and New Brunswick (NB) between January and June 2023, including 25 pelagic/coastal birds on Sable Island and 7 dead mesocarnivores (4 raccoons, 2 red foxes, and 1 striped skunk). Using Oxford Nanopore whole-genome sequencing, maximum-likelihood phylogenetics (IQ-TREE), and segment-partitioned Bayesian time-resolved phylogeography (BEAST v1.10.4 on 62 non-reassortant genomes), the authors show that the Canadian isolates belong to a fully Eurasian genotype A6 lineage carrying a 22-amino-acid NA stalk deletion (positions 47–68) and an N154 glycosylation-restoring substitution in HA (`HA-A156T` in 40 isolates; `HA-A156S` in 1 red-tailed hawk isolate), with independently acquired mammalian-adaptive `PB2-E627K` mutations in two mammals and two gulls. Functional characterization of two early isolates (`A/American_Crow/PEI/FAV-0035-6/2023` and `A/Raccoon/PEI/FAV-0193-1/2023`, neither carrying `PB2-E627K`) demonstrates strict avian $\alpha$-2,3 sialic acid receptor binding (`3'SLN`), susceptibility to neuraminidase inhibitors (oseltamivir, zanamivir, peramivir) and baloxavir despite `NA-I117T` and `PA-T40A` polymorphisms, reduced HI cross-reactivity to North American H5N1 candidate vaccine sera relative to `A/Astrakhan/3212/2020`-like sera, and 100% lethality with widespread central nervous system neuroinvasion in inoculated ferrets ($n = 3$ survival donors per group). All 41 consensus genomes are deposited in GISAID (`EPI_ISL_18702952` to `EPI_ISL_18702992`).

The study provides a comprehensive surveillance, genomic, antigenic, and pathobiological assessment of an emerging HPAI A(H5N5) lineage in North America. Its primary limitations are: (1) reliance on discrete-trait CTMC phylogeography (BSSVS) on opportunistically collected wildlife carcasses to infer specific host-to-host transmission chains (e.g., northern fulmars $\to$ crows $\to$ raccoons) without controlling for severe sampling skew across species and locations; (2) claiming direct-contact ferret transmission for both isolates when nasal washes were negative in all 6 contacts, tissue/virological confirmation was unavailable for the 2 contact animals that died at day 5, and only 1 surviving contact in the raccoon-virus group seroconverted; (3) a mechanistic contradiction in the Discussion that cites `HA-T156A` (loss of glycosylation) to explain ferret transmission when the tested A(H5N5) viruses carry the reverse `HA-A156T` substitution (restoring glycosylation); and (4) collection date discrepancies between the main text and Table 1. These issues can be resolved through computational sensitivity analyses, careful reframing, and editorial corrections.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Canadian 2023 A(H5N5) viruses represent at least two transatlantic incursions of a non-reassortant Eurasian genotype A6 $\Delta$stalk lineage closely related to Norwegian seabird isolates (Results, Figures 1–2, S1): [Well Supported].**  
  Segment-by-segment ML phylogenies and partitioned Bayesian whole-genome time trees consistently place all 41 Canadian genomes within Eurasian clade 2.3.4.4b genotype A6 sharing the 22-residue NA stalk deletion. The phylogenetic separation and independent `HA-A156T` versus `HA-A156S` substitutions between the main Canadian cluster (40 isolates) and `A/Red-Tailed_Hawk/PEI/FAV-0165-1/2023` (clustering with two June 2022 Norwegian gull isolates) strongly support two separate introductions from northern Europe, consistent with SEATRACK pelagic seabird telemetry overlap (Figure 6).

* **A(H5N5) isolates retain avian $\alpha$-2,3 receptor specificity, show distinct HI reactivity, and remain fully susceptible to NA inhibitors and baloxavir despite `NA-I117T` and `PA-T40A` (Results, Table 1, Table S3, Figure S2): [Well Supported].**  
  Solid-phase glycan binding assays confirm preference for `3'SLN` over `6'SLN` (Figure S2). MUNANA fluorometric $\text{IC}_{50}$ and IRINA/plaque-reduction $\text{EC}_{50}$ assays demonstrate $\le 2$-fold changes relative to clade 2.3.4.4b reference viruses, ruling out clinically meaningful resistance from `NA-I117T` or `PA-T40A`. HI assays (Table 1) reproducibly show 4-fold homologous drops against `CBER-RG8A` (`A/Astrakhan/3212/2020`-like, titer 80 vs. 320) and poor reactivity against North American H5N1 reference sera (titer 10).

* **A(H5N5) causes 100% lethal, neurotropic systemic infection in intranasally inoculated ferrets and transmits inefficiently to direct contacts (Results, Figures 3–5): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Rapid lethality (humane endpoints by 4–5 dpi in 3/3 survival-cohort donors per virus), high respiratory and brain titers (up to $5.75\ \log_{10}\ \text{TCID}_{50}/\text{mL}$), and multi-route CNS neuroinvasion by IHC (Figures 4–5) are convincingly demonstrated in inoculated ferrets. However, the claim of direct-contact transmission in both groups rests on fragile evidence for the crow isolate: 0/6 contact ferrets had detectable infectious virus in nasal washes at 1–10 dpi (Figure 3G), the 2 contact animals euthanized at 5 dpi (1 per group) had no tissues collected to confirm IAV infection by titration, RT-qPCR, or IHC (line 120), and only 1/2 surviving contacts in the raccoon-virus group (and 0/2 in the crow-virus group) seroconverted at 25 dpi ($\text{HI} = 320$). In addition, attributing ferret contact transmission in the Discussion (line 120) to literature on `HA-T156A` is internally contradictory because the Canadian A(H5N5) isolates carry the reversion `HA-A156T` (which *restores* N154 glycosylation and abolishes $\alpha$-2,6 binding).

* **Northern fulmars on Sable Island acted as the primary viral source for gulls, crows, and foxes, with crows subsequently transmitting to raccoons (Results, Figure 2B): [Fragile].**  
  Ancestral host reconstruction via asymmetric continuous-time Markov chains (CTMC) with BSSVS in BEAST treats tip host frequencies and sampling dates as directly proportional to lineage abundance. Because the 41 Canadian sequences derive from opportunistic passive dead-bird/mammal surveillance heavily skewed toward Sable Island northern fulmars ($n = 13$ collected Feb–April 2023) and PEI crows/raccoons, standard CTMC discrete-trait models systematically assign the most heavily sampled state near the clade root (northern fulmars) as the ancestral "source" and under-sampled or later-sampled hosts as "sinks." Without tip-state randomization controls or structured coalescent modeling (e.g., BASTA/MASCOT), fine-scale directional host transitions (such as northern fulmars directly infecting red foxes or crows directly infecting raccoons without unsampled mainland avian intermediates) are over-interpreted.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Assess Sampling-Bias Sensitivity in Discrete-Trait Host and Location Phylogeography
* **WHY:** Standard asymmetric CTMC ancestral state reconstruction in BEAST (Figure 2B–C) is highly sensitive to uneven passive carcass sampling across host species ($n = 13$ northern fulmars vs. $n = 1$ skunk, $n = 2$ foxes) and sampling windows (January in PEI vs. February–April on Sable Island).
* **WHAT:** Perform tip-state randomization (and/or subsampling sensitivity analyses) to verify whether the inferred BSSVS Bayes factors ($\text{BF} \ge 3.0$) for host transitions exceed expectations driven solely by sampling frequencies, and temper the host-chain narrative accordingly.
* **HOW:** Using the existing BEAST v1.10.4 XML files for the 62-genome dataset, add a tip-state swap operator (`TipStateSwapOperator`) to randomize host and location labels across tips during MCMC while preserving the tree topology and branch lengths. Recompute BSSVS Bayes factors comparing the empirical posterior inclusion probabilities against the null tip-randomized prior/posterior, and revise the Results (line 72) and Figure 2B legend to frame the chord diagram as phylogenetic host associations among sampled carcasses rather than direct epidemiological transmission events.

#### 2. Clarify the Evolution and Functional Role of `HA-A156T/S` vs. `HA-T156A`
* **WHY:** Two sections of the manuscript contradict either the phylogenetic timeline or the biochemical direction of substitutions at residue HA-156:
  1. Line 111 states *"As the A(H5N5)-$\Delta$stalk virus spread from Norway to Canada, it acquired the reverse HA-A156T mutation,"* whereas lines 61–63 and Figure 2A show that `HA-A156T` and `HA-A156S` diverged in Eurasia in mid-to-late 2021 (tMRCA July–November 2021) and were already present in 2022 Norwegian isolates prior to transatlantic spread.
  2. Line 120 states *"Transmission of the A(H5N5)-$\Delta$stalk viruses is consistent with previous reports linking HA-T156A to airborne transmission among ferrets and guinea pigs,"* overlooking that the Canadian A(H5N5) viruses possess `HA-156T` (glycosylated N154, strict $\alpha$-2,3 binding), the exact opposite of the unglycosylated `HA-156A` mutant studied in references 87, 91, and 92.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Revise line 111 to state that the `HA-A156T` and `HA-A156S` reversions arose in Eurasia prior to the 2022–2023 transatlantic incursions into Canada. In line 120, remove the claim that A(H5N5) contact transmission is explained by `HA-T156A`, and instead note that low-level direct-contact transmission occurred *despite* the restoration of the N154 glycosylation sequon (`HA-156T`) and strict $\alpha$-2,3 receptor specificity.

#### 3. Temper Direct-Contact Ferret Transmission Claims and Report Exact Statistical Power
* **WHY:** In the ferret contact transmission study ($n = 3$ contacts per group), no infectious virus was recovered from any contact nasal wash, and no post-mortem tissues or swabs were tested from the two contact ferrets euthanized at 5 dpi (line 120). Thus, for `A/American_Crow/PEI/FAV-0035-6/2023`, there is zero virological or serological confirmation of infection in contact animals (0/3 nasal wash positive, 0/2 survivors seroconverted).
* **WHAT:** Distinguish confirmed contact transmission via seroconversion in the raccoon-virus group (1/3 contacts confirmed by $\text{HI} = 320$) from unconfirmed clinical mortality in the crow-virus contact group.
* **HOW:** In the Abstract (line 7), Results (line 78), and Discussion (line 120), explicitly state that virological confirmation of contact transmission was limited to seroconversion in 1 of 3 contacts exposed to `A/Raccoon/PEI/FAV-0193-1/2023`, while the two contacts euthanized at day 5 (one per virus group) developed clinical signs compatible with systemic/neurologic disease but lacked tissue or RT-qPCR confirmation. Additionally, report the exact $p$-values from the two-way ANOVA in Figure 3F comparing extrapulmonary tissue titers ($n = 3$ per group) between the raccoon and crow isolates.

#### 4. Technical, Table, and Reproducibility Corrections
* **WHY:** Collection dates in Table 1 conflict with the main text, a supplementary table cross-reference is misnumbered, and BEAST XML files are not publicly archived.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Table 1 vs. Main Text (Collection Dates):** In Table 1 (lines 205–206), the collection date of `A/Raccoon/PEI/FAV-0193-1/2023` is listed as **May 2023** and `A/American_Crow/PEI/FAV-0035-6/2023` as **February 2023**. However, the Results (line 35) state that `FAV-0035-6/2023` was found dead on **January 9, 2023** and `FAV-0193-1/2023` on **April 3, 2023**. Correct Table 1 to match the exact collection dates in the text and GISAID metadata.
  2. **Discussion (line 104):** Change *"early in the year (Table S3)"* to *"(Table S4)"*, as Table S3 contains antiviral susceptibility data (`IC50`/`EC50`) rather than European outbreak detection dates.
  3. **STAR Methods (line 159):** Clarify the statement *"screened for recombinants with RDP5 (v5.46) using default settings with linear sequences, and results were retained for further phylogenetic analysis"*—specify whether zero recombinants were detected or whether recombinant sequences were excluded.
  4. **Data Availability (lines 139–141):** Deposit the aligned FASTA files, IQ-TREE Newick tree files, and BEAST v1.10.4 `.xml` specification files on Zenodo or GitHub so that the time-scaled phylogenies and BSSVS transitions in Figure 2 can be reproduced directly.
