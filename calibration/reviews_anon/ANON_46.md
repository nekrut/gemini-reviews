### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates whether the T cell response to anti-PD-1 checkpoint blockade in human skin cancers arises from the reinvigoration of pre-existing tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) or the recruitment/expansion of novel T cell clonotypes ("clonal replacement"). The authors perform paired droplet-based 5′ single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and single-cell TCR$\alpha/\beta$ sequencing (scTCR-seq) on 79,046 cells (53,030 cells from $N=11$ advanced basal cell carcinoma [BCC] patients, yielding 33,106 TILs with 28,371 paired TCRs; plus 26,016 TILs from $N=4$ cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma [SCC] patients) collected from site-matched primary tumors before and after anti-PD-1 therapy (pembrolizumab or cemiplimab). They integrate these data with whole-exome sequencing (WES, 110$\times$ mean coverage) and bulk TCR$\beta$ sequencing (`immunoSEQ`, averaging 26,066 templates per tumor in 8/11 BCC patients and 113,528 templates per peripheral blood sample in 5 patients). They find that clonally expanded CD8$^+$ T cells are strongly enriched in chronically activated/exhausted clusters co-expressing tumor-reactivity markers (*ENTPD1*/CD39, *ITGAE*/CD103, *HAVCR2*/TIM-3, *PDCD1*, *TIGIT*), that clonotype identity tightly constrains transcriptional phenotype across timepoints and GLIPH specificity groups, and that 84% of post-treatment exhausted CD8$^+$ T cell clones per patient (and 55% of exhausted clones with $\ge 5$ cells across all patients, vs. 20% of memory clones) consist of novel clonotypes not detected in the pre-treatment biopsy, with 11.8% of novel exhausted TIL clonotypes detectable in pre-treatment peripheral blood. Raw and processed scRNA/TCR-seq data (`GSE123814`), WES data (`PRJNA533341`), bulk TCR-seq data (`doi:10.21417/KY2019NM`), and reference bulk accessions (`GSE118165`, `GSE113590`, `GSE58377`, `E-MTAB-5678`) are publicly deposited; custom analysis code is listed only as "available upon request."

This is a technically rigorous clinical single-cell and TCR tracking study that directly links single-cell transcriptional states to clonal lineage dynamics in paired pre-/post-therapy human biopsies across two tumor types (BCC and SCC) and validates droplet scTCR-seq sampling limits with 20-fold deeper bulk TCR$\beta$ sequencing and longitudinal pre-pre/post-post control biopsies (`su001`). The primary methodological limitations are: (1) finite Poisson/hypergeometric capture sensitivity in spatially heterogeneous tumor biopsies—where scTCR-seq captures a few hundred to a few thousand T cells per timepoint and bulk TCR-seq template counts drop as low as 554 templates in some tumor biopsies (Methods line 87)—meaning that "novel" post-treatment clones can include rare pre-existing intratumoral clones below the pre-treatment detection limit; (2) several statistical comparisons in the Extended Data (e.g., Extended Data Figures 9c and 10i) treat individual cells ($n = \text{number of cells}$) or specificity groups rather than patients as independent units in two-sample $t$-tests; and (3) clinical heterogeneity in the small cohort ($N=11$ BCC, where some patients received concurrent vismodegib and post-biopsy timing varied widely) limits power to link clonal replacement to clinical RECIST response.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Clonally expanded CD8$^+$ TILs in BCC and SCC are enriched in an exhausted/chronically activated transcriptional state (*CD39*/*ENTPD1*$^+$, *CD103*/*ITGAE*$^+$, *HAVCR2*$^+$) and maintain high phenotypic stability within clonotypes and GLIPH specificity groups: [Well Supported].**  
  The coupling of Gini-index clonality, clone size, proliferation scores, and exhaustion/tumor-reactivity signatures is replicated across both the 11-patient BCC cohort (Figure 2e–g) and the 4-patient SCC validation cohort (Extended Data Figure 10c–d), and permutation controls matched for clone size confirm that both exact TCR$\alpha\beta$ clonotypes and GLIPH motif clusters share transcriptional cluster identity significantly above chance (Figure 3a–c, Extended Data Figure 6a–e).

* **Following anti-PD-1 therapy, expanded exhausted CD8$^+$ T cell clones are preferentially composed of newly detected clonotypes ("clonal replacement") compared to memory and effector-memory CD8$^+$ T cell subsets: [Well Supported].**  
  By using memory and effector-memory CD8$^+$ T cell clones within the exact same biopsies as an internal control (20% novel clones in memory vs. 55% in exhausted for clones $\ge 5$ cells; 40% vs. 84% per patient, Figure 4b–c), validating in deeper bulk TCR$\beta$ sequencing (Figure 4e–f), replicating at an earlier 31-day post-treatment window in SCC (50% vs. 29%, Figure 4j–k), and demonstrating in sequential pre-1/pre-2 and post-1/post-2 biopsies from patient `su001` that replacement occurs across the treatment boundary rather than across time alone (Extended Data Figure 8d), the authors provide strong evidence for phenotype-specific clonal turnover.

* **Pre-existing exhausted TILs have limited reinvigoration capacity and the post-PD-1 exhausted TIL repertoire derives from newly entering T cell clones from the periphery: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While 11.8% of "novel" post-treatment exhausted TIL clonotypes were detected in pre-treatment peripheral blood (and 35.5% in post-treatment blood, Figure 4g), supporting peripheral recruitment for at least a subset of clones, bulk tumor TCR-seq template depth ranged down to 554 templates (Methods line 87). Under binomial sampling, a clone present at $0.1\%$ frequency in a pre-treatment tumor biopsy with 554–2,000 templates has a high probability of zero counts pre-treatment (`undetected`) before expanding 50-fold post-treatment. Furthermore, because TCR clonotypes are static genetic barcodes whereas transcriptional states are dynamic, a rare unexpanded *TCF7*$^+$ memory/precursor cell already residing in the tumor or tumor margin pre-treatment that proliferates and differentiates into the exhausted cluster post-treatment is scored as a "novel exhausted clone" whenever its pre-treatment precursor count fell below the capture threshold. The authors appropriately acknowledge this caveat in the Discussion (line 45), though the Abstract states more definitively that expansions did not derive from pre-existing TIL clones.

* **Persistent and expanding exhausted clones exhibit higher pre-treatment *TCF7*$^+$ stem-like gene signatures than contracting clones: [Fragile].**  
  As noted on line 33, only two pre-existing exhausted clones met the statistical threshold for significant expansion in the primary BCC analysis (requiring relaxation to 10 clones that increased in frequency), and the violin plot comparisons in Extended Data Figure 10i perform unpaired two-tailed $t$-tests across individual cells ($n = \text{number of cells}$) rather than aggregating by clone or patient, which inflates statistical significance via cell-level pseudoreplication within the few dominant clones.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Account for Pre-Treatment Sampling Depth Limits Using a Zero-Truncated Binomial / Negative Binomial Capture Model
* **WHY:** Defining a post-treatment expanded clone as "novel" (0 cells in scTCR-seq or 0 templates in bulk TCR-seq pre-treatment) depends strongly on the total number of T cells ($N_{\text{pre}}$) or bulk TCR templates ($T_{\text{pre}}$, range 554–99,264) captured in the pre-treatment sample. A sample with 554 bulk templates cannot detect pre-existing clones below $\sim 0.5\%$ frequency with $95\%$ confidence.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the upper bound on pre-treatment intratumoral frequency for each "novel" post-treatment clone given the sample-specific pre-treatment sequencing depth $N_{\text{pre}}$, and perform downsampling standardization across pre- and post-treatment pairs.
* **HOW:** Downsample pre- and post-treatment scTCR-seq and bulk TCR-seq libraries within each patient to the minimum template/cell count of the pair (excluding pairs like `su003` with almost no cells), compute the binomial probability $P(\text{count}_{\text{pre}} = 0 \mid f_{\text{pre}}, N_{\text{pre}})$, and verify that the excess of novel clones in the exhausted cluster relative to the memory cluster (Figure 4b, f, k) holds after depth standardization.

#### 2. Replace Cell-Level $t$-Tests with Clone-Level or Patient-Level Mixed Models for *TCF7* Signature Comparisons
* **WHY:** In Figure 3f and Extended Data Figure 10i, testing differences in *TCF7*$^+$ stem-like signature scores between expanded, persistent, and contracted groups using an unpaired $t$-test where $n$ is the number of individual cells violates independence because dozens or hundreds of cells belong to the same TCR clone and patient.
* **WHAT:** Aggregate *TCF7*$^+$ signature scores to the clone level (mean score per clone pre-treatment) and test across clones using a linear mixed-effects model with a patient random intercept `(1|Patient)` or a stratified Wilcoxon rank-sum test.
* **HOW:** Compute the pre-treatment mean `AddModuleScore` for *TCF7* for each clone, weight by clone size or model single-cell scores with `lmer(TCF7_score ~ Fate + (1|Patient/Clone))`, and report the clone-level $p$-values in Figure 3f and Extended Data Figure 10i.

#### 3. Deposit Custom Analysis Scripts in a Public Repository
* **WHY:** While raw and processed sequencing data are admirably deposited across GEO (`GSE123814`), SRA (`PRJNA533341`), and ImmuneACCESS (`doi:10.21417/KY2019NM`), the Code Availability statement (line 201) lists custom code only as *"available upon request"*.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Deposit the R/Python scripts used for Seurat clustering, heat-shock and cell-cycle regression, AUCell signature scoring, modified GLIPH motif clustering, and binomial clone expansion testing in a public GitHub or Zenodo repository and cite the permanent URL/DOI in the Code Availability section.
