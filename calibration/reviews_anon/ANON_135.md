### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This prospective cohort study uses UK Biobank data ($N = 271,068$ participants aged 38–73 years at recruitment after excluding baseline aortic disease, malignancies, and chronic inflammatory/autoimmune conditions; 629 incident abdominal aortic aneurysm [AAA] cases over a median follow-up of 13.89 years) to evaluate the association between nine baseline peripheral blood inflammatory markers (CRP, lymphocyte, monocyte, neutrophil, and platelet counts, plus NLR, PLR, LMR, and SII) and incident AAA. Using stepwise multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression, restricted cubic splines, Fine–Gray competing-risk models, and stratification by a 31-SNP AAA polygenic risk score (PRS) and demographic/clinical risk factors, the authors report that higher baseline $\log_{10}$-transformed Z-scores of CRP ($\text{HR} = 1.46$), neutrophils ($\text{HR} = 1.27$), monocytes ($\text{HR} = 1.27$), and lymphocytes ($\text{HR} = 1.17$) and lower platelets ($\text{HR} = 0.90$) and PLR ($\text{HR} = 0.82$) are independently associated with incident AAA. Within the guideline-recommended screening group of male current smokers aged $\ge 65$ years (baseline cumulative incidence 4.78%), restricting to the top CRP quartile and top PRS tertile identifies a subgroup with a 10.05% cumulative AAA incidence. Data are accessible via UK Biobank (Application 88192), though custom R/SPSS analysis code is not publicly deposited.

Leveraging a large prospective cohort with nearly 14 years of follow-up and Fine–Gray competing-risk sensitivity analyses provides well-powered epidemiological evidence linking pre-diagnostic systemic inflammation and leukocyte counts to incident AAA. However, the study's causal and clinical risk-refinement claims are tempered by four methodological issues: (1) omission of critical confounders from the multivariable Cox models—specifically smoking intensity (pack-years), body mass index (BMI)/central obesity, diabetes mellitus, and statin/lipid-lowering medication use; (2) lack of formal model discrimination and reclassification metrics ($\Delta\text{C-index}$, NRI) to substantiate that CRP and PRS improve screening performance beyond age, sex, and smoking; (3) an inappropriate missing-data imputation method ("linear interpolation" across cross-sectional participant rows); and (4) several numerical contradictions between the text and tables (including inverted education directionality and erroneous $p$-values in Table 1). All of these issues can be resolved through targeted re-analyses of the existing UK Biobank dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Higher baseline serum CRP and peripheral neutrophil, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts (and lower platelet counts) are prospectively associated with incident AAA: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Across 271,068 participants and 629 incident AAA events, the associations in Model 3 (Table 2) and the restricted cubic splines (Figure 2) are statistically consistent and survive Fine–Gray adjustment for the competing risk of death (Supplemental Table S11). However, the multivariable models only adjust for categorical smoking status (`Never`, `Previous`, `Current`) without adjusting for continuous **pack-years of smoking**, **BMI/waist circumference**, **diabetes mellitus**, or **statin use**. Because heavy smoking (e.g., 40 vs. 5 pack-years) strongly increases both leukocyte/CRP levels and AAA risk within the "Current" and "Previous" smoker categories, unadjusted dose-dependent smoking intensity represents a major source of residual confounding. Similarly, defining hypercholesterolemia in Table 1 solely by baseline total cholesterol $\ge 5.18\text{ mmol/L}$ without accounting for statin prescriptions explains why AAA cases appear to have *lower* hypercholesterolemia (53.9% vs. 67.8%) and *lower* total cholesterol ($5.38$ vs. $5.72\text{ mmol/L}$)—statin use in high-cardiovascular-risk individuals lowers both total cholesterol and CRP.

* **Sex and smoking status significantly modify the association between CRP/leukocytes and AAA risk, with the strongest effect in male current smokers: [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 3, multiplicative interaction $p$-values are reported across 9 biomarkers $\times$ 5 subgroup variables (45 interaction tests) without multiple-testing correction, and for smoking, `Former` and `Current` smokers were pooled when computing $P_{\text{interaction}}$. Moreover, because male current smokers have the highest cumulative tobacco exposure (pack-years) and highest absolute baseline hazard of AAA, apparent multiplicative effect modification by sex and smoking may reflect unmodeled pack-year dose differences rather than biological synergy.

* **CRP complements AAA polygenic risk score (PRS) and traditional risk factors to refine AAA screening (e.g., 16-fold risk in current smokers with high CRP vs. never smokers with low CRP; 20.9-fold risk in males with high CRP vs. females with low CRP; 10.05% incidence in older male smokers with high CRP + high PRS): [Overstated].**  
  First, comparing `Male + Q4 CRP` to `Female + Q1 CRP` ($\text{HR} = 20.9$) or `Current smoker + Q4 CRP` to `Never smoker + Q1 CRP` ($\text{HR} = 16.0$) in the text (Section *"CRP complements PRS..."*) conflates the massive baseline hazard of male sex and smoking with the effect of CRP (within-subgroup HRs for Q4 vs. Q1 CRP are $\sim 2.5\text{–}3.0$). Second, in the high-risk screening subgroup of men aged $\ge 65$ who currently smoke (overall incidence 4.78%), showing that the top quartile of CRP + top tertile of PRS ($1/4 \times 1/3 = 1/12$ of the subgroup) has a 10.05% incidence does not address the screening sensitivity trade-off: restricting ultrasound screening to this top $1/12$ stratum would miss the $>80\%$ of AAA cases occurring in the remaining $11/12$ of older male smokers. Formal discrimination metrics (Harrell's C-index, time-dependent AUC, and Net Reclassification Improvement) are needed to demonstrate clinical screening utility.

* **Sensitivity analysis using linear interpolation confirms robustness to missing data: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Methods ("Statistical analysis"), Sensitivity Analysis (1) states that missing values for exposures and covariates were imputed *"by linear interpolation"*. Linear interpolation estimates a missing value from the adjacent rows ($i-1$ and $i+1$) in a data table; applied to cross-sectional participant records, it imputes a participant's missing biomarker or covariate from two unrelated individuals who happen to have adjacent row indices.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Adjust for Smoking Pack-Years, BMI, Diabetes, and Lipid-Lowering Medication in Model 3
* **WHY:** Categorical smoking adjustment (`Never/Previous/Current`) leaves substantial residual confounding from smoking intensity (pack-years), which drives both leukocytosis/CRP elevation and AAA. Likewise, omitting BMI, diabetes mellitus (which is inversely associated with AAA but positively associated with CRP), and statin use (which lowers both cholesterol and CRP) biases the multivariable hazard ratios in Table 2.
* **WHAT:** Expand Model 3 (or add a Model 4) in Table 2 to include UK Biobank fields for smoking pack-years, BMI, baseline diabetes mellitus, and statin/lipid-lowering medication use, and redefine baseline dyslipidemia to include participants taking lipid-lowering drugs.
* **HOW:** Extract UK Biobank Field 20161 (pack-years of smoking), Field 21001 (BMI), Field 2443 (diabetes diagnosed by doctor / ICD-10 E10–E14), and Field 6177/6153 (cholesterol-lowering medication). Re-run the Cox models in Table 2 adjusting for these covariates and report whether the HRs for CRP, neutrophils, monocytes, lymphocytes, and platelets remain unchanged.

#### 2. Quantify Formal Risk Discrimination ($\Delta\text{C-index}$) and Sensitivity/Specificity Trade-Offs for Screening
* **WHY:** Reporting hazard ratios and subgroup cumulative incidences (4.78% vs. 10.05%) does not establish whether adding CRP and PRS meaningfully improves population screening discrimination or how many AAA cases would be missed if screening were restricted by CRP/PRS thresholds.
* **WHAT:** Compute Harrell's C-index (and 10-year time-dependent ROC AUC) for (a) clinical risk factors alone, (b) clinical risk factors + PRS, (c) clinical risk factors + CRP, and (d) clinical risk factors + PRS + CRP, and report the sensitivity (fraction of all 629 AAA cases captured) at fixed false-positive rates.
* **HOW:** Using the `survival` and `rms` packages in R (`rcorrcens` or `validate`), report the C-index and 95% CI for each nested model in the full cohort and within the $\ge 65$-year-old male ever-smoker screening target population, along with a contingency table showing the total number of participants and incident AAA cases in each CRP quartile $\times$ PRS tertile cell.

#### 3. Replace Row-Order Linear Interpolation with Multiple Imputation (`MICE`)
* **WHY:** Linear interpolation across rows of a participant dataset is only valid for ordered time-series data, not independent cross-sectional participants.
* **WHAT:** Replace linear interpolation in Supplemental Table S9 with Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (`mice` package in R) or predictive mean matching based on baseline demographic and clinical covariates.
* **HOW:** Run `mice(data, m = 5, method = 'pmm')` using age, sex, smoking, blood pressure, TDI, education, and outcomes (Nelson–Aalen cumulative hazard + event indicator) in the imputation model, pool the 5 Cox model fits using Rubin's rules (`pool()`), and update Supplemental Table S9.

#### 4. Technical, Mathematical, and Editorial Cleanup
* **WHY:** Several direct contradictions exist between the Results text and Table 1/Table 2 regarding participant counts, educational attainment directionality, and two-sample $t$-test $p$-values.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Results ("Baseline characteristics") vs. Table 1 (Education):** The text states *"Included participants with AAA were more likely to be ... had college or university degree"*, whereas Table 1 shows that participants with AAA were significantly **less** likely to have a college or university degree (`8.6%` vs. `12.4%`, $P = 0.004$). Correct the text to state *"less likely to have a college or university degree"*.
  * **Table 1 vs. Text vs. Table 2 (Participant Counts):** Reconcile the total sample sizes: the text states **$271,068$** included participants; the Table 1 header lists $N = 270,440$ without AAA + $N = 629$ with AAA (= **$271,069$**), while the subgroup rows inside Table 1 sum to **$270,439$** without AAA ($270,439 + 629 = 271,068$); and the Table 2 footnote states *"A total of 270,439 participants involved in this analysis"* (which is the count of non-AAA controls alone, omitting the 629 cases). Correct the Table 1 header and Table 2 footnote to reflect the exact number of cases and controls analyzed.
  * **Table 1 ($t$-test $P$-values for `PLR`, `LMR`, and `SBP`):** In Table 1, `PLR` is $140.62 \pm 55.10$ ($N=270,440$) vs. $125.64 \pm 51.59$ ($N=629$) with a reported Student's $t$-test $P$-value of `0.559`, yet the two-sample $t$-statistic for these summary statistics is $t = 6.82$ ($P < 0.001$, matching Model 1 in Table 2 where $P < 0.001$). Similarly, `LMR` ($4.65 \pm 3.10$ vs. $4.02 \pm 2.49$) gives $t = 5.09$ ($P < 0.001$, reported as `0.096`), and `SBP` ($138.59 \pm 19.51$ vs. $147.22 \pm 21.16$) gives $t = 11.08$ ($P < 0.001$, reported as `0.014`). Re-verify and correct the Table 1 $P$-value column.
  * **Results ("Systemic inflammatory markers and AAA risk"):** Fix the typo *"adoptive immunity marker (lymphocyte)"* to *"adaptive immunity marker (lymphocyte)"*, and in Table 1 fix *"C-reaction protein"* to *"C-reactive protein"*.
