### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript develops a mathematical framework linking frequency-dependent non-synonymous to synonymous substitution ratios ($\text{dN/dS}$) with stochastic birth-death models of clonal expansion in somatic tissues. By defining an integrated "interval $\text{dN/dS}$" ($\text{i-dN/dS}$) metric over cumulative clone-size ranges $[f_{\min}, f_{\max}]$ and coupling it to the single-progenitor population-asymmetry model of epithelial stem-cell homeostasis, the authors estimate gene-level proliferative self-renewal biases ($\Delta$) and stem-cell loss/replacement rates ($r\lambda$) from published deep targeted sequencing datasets of histopathologically normal human oesophagus and skin (Martincorena et al., 2015, 2018). They report a skewed distribution of fitness effects (DFE) across mutated panel genes, led by strong positive selection on *TP53* and *NOTCH1* ($\Delta \approx 0.03\text{--}0.09$ in oesophagus), and corroborate age-related clone expansion by fitting the site frequency spectrum (SFS) directly with Bayesian non-linear regression. The simulation code (Julia) and analysis workflow (R/Snakemake/Singularity) are publicly archived.

Combining locus-specific comparative genomics ($\text{dN/dS}$ mutation-rate normalization via `dndscv`) with clone-size demographic theory addresses an important gap in somatic evolution. However, several statistical and methodological flaws affect the quantitative inferences and their interpretation as a tissue-wide DFE: (1) fitting $\text{i-dN/dS}$ across nested, cumulative intervals $[f_{\min}, f_{\max}]$ using an independent normal-residual likelihood ignores severe cumulative autocorrelation and produces artificially narrow confidence intervals; (2) restricting gene-level DFE construction to gene-patient pairs with $\ge 8$ mutations and goodness-of-fit $R^2 > 0.6$ systematically censors neutral and weakly selected genes (for which a flat curve around $\text{i-dN/dS} = 1$ yields $R^2 \approx 0$); (3) the model equates all non-synonymous mutations in a gene with drivers sharing a single $\Delta$ (or an exponential distribution of strictly positive $\Delta$), ignoring the substantial fraction of neutral ($s=0$) and deleterious ($s<0$) missense sites within driver genes; and (4) the Materials and Methods contain multiple mathematical and typographical errors in the likelihood, exponential integral, and SFS equations. These issues can be resolved through computational re-analysis and textual revision without generating new experimental data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Linking frequency-dependent $\text{dN/dS}$ to birth-death stem-cell dynamics enables estimation of proliferative bias ($\Delta$): [Moderately Supported].**  
  The theoretical derivation connecting the ratio of non-synonymous and synonymous clone-size densities $g(\theta, \mu, s, A)$ to the birth-death scaling factor $N_\Delta(t)$ is conceptually sound under an independent-clone branching process. However, the statistical inference treats cumulative intervals $[f_{\min}, f_{\max}]$ as independent observations with homoscedastic Gaussian residuals (lines 254–262). Because consecutive $f_{\max}$ thresholds aggregate overlapping sets of mutations, the residuals are strongly autocorrelated and heteroscedastic, invalidating the Fisher information matrix 95% confidence intervals (e.g., reporting $\Delta = 0.0149$ with a 95% CI of $0.0148\text{--}0.0150$ in skin, line 159, or $\Delta = 0.094$, 95% CI $0.091\text{--}0.097$ for *TP53* nonsense, line 143).

* **Empirical measurement of the Distribution of Fitness Effects (DFE) of somatic mutations in normal human oesophagus and skin: [Overstated].**  
  The underlying sequencing data come from targeted panels of ~72–74 known cancer-associated genes, not an unbiased genome-wide survey. More critically, the authors only include gene-patient fits with $\ge 8$ mutations and $R^2 > 0.6$ in the DFE histograms (Figures 3c–d, 4f; lines 149, 262). Under the null hypothesis ($\Delta = 0$), the theoretical curve is a horizontal line ($\text{i-dN/dS} = 1$), for which $R^2$ against noisy empirical data is close to zero or undefined. Filtering on $R^2 > 0.6$ therefore creates severe conditioning bias, truncating neutral and weakly selected loci and transforming the reported DFE into a conditional summary of strongly selected driver genes. Furthermore, inferring a single gene-wide $\Delta$ conflates true driver mutations with neutral missense variants in the same gene, downward-biasing site-specific driver fitness while upward-biasing the apparent proportion of selected sites.

* ***TP53* and *NOTCH1* mutations confer the largest selective advantages in normal oesophagus and skin, driving age-dependent clonal expansion: [Well Supported].**  
  Both the $\text{i-dN/dS}$ trajectories and the independent Bayesian multi-level regressions of clone area versus donor age consistently identify *TP53* and *NOTCH1* as having the steepest age-dependent expansion slopes ($p = 0.004, R^2 = 0.47$ between methods; Figure 5—figure supplement 4). Robustness across two distinct $\text{dN/dS}$ estimators (`dndscv` and `SSB-dNdS`) further supports the relative ranking of these two major drivers.

* **The theoretical power-law-plus-exponential SFS model ($C = \frac{\alpha}{A} e^{-A/\exp(\beta)}$) outperforms pure power-law or pure exponential decay models: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Direct Bayesian fitting of the binned SFS across donors (Figure 5b–e) shows better predictive accuracy (WAIC and Bayes factors) for the theoretical birth-death functional form than for simplified single-term alternatives. However, aggregating all non-synonymous mutations across genes with heterogeneous $\Delta_i$ into a single patient-level SFS (Figure 5d–e) violates the single-$N_\Delta(t)$ assumption of Equation (7), as a mixture of exponentials with different scale parameters does not follow a single exponential cutoff.

* **Synonymous mutations in the oesophageal biopsies are not predominantly genetic hitchhikers: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The patch-level regression showing that each additional *NOTCH1* or *TP53* non-synonymous mutation adds only ~0.05 synonymous mutations per $2\text{ mm}^2$ biopsy ($R^2 < 0.02$) and does not significantly increase mean synonymous VAF ($p > 0.1$; line 154) is a reasonable empirical check, though it does not compare the observed high-frequency synonymous tail against the analytical hitchhiking expectation derived from the branching-process model itself.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Cumulative-Interval Ordinary Least Squares/NLL with a Non-Overlapping Multinomial or Poisson Process Likelihood
* **WHY:** Every data point in the $\text{i-dN/dS}(f_{\max})$ curve shares all mutations below $f_{\max}$ with all subsequent points. Treating $y(f_{\max}) - \hat{y}(f_{\max})$ as independent Gaussian errors violates conditional independence, understating parameter uncertainty by up to an order of magnitude.
* **WHAT:** Formulate the likelihood directly on non-overlapping frequency bins $[f_k, f_{k+1})$ or on the continuous unbinned clone sizes, and compute confidence intervals via non-parametric bootstrapping of mutations/biopsies per patient.
* **HOW:** Using the existing `dndscv` neutral mutation rate expectation $\mu_d/\mu_p$, model the observed number of non-synonymous mutations in disjoint intervals $[A_k, A_{k+1})$ conditional on the synonymous count (or jointly as two inhomogeneous Poisson processes with intensities $g(\theta, \mu_d, \Delta, A)$ and $g(\theta, \mu_p, 0, A)$). Alternatively, retain the $\text{i-dN/dS}$ estimator for point estimation but replace the Fisher information matrix CIs (line 261) with 1,000 bootstrap replicates resampling mutations (or tissue patches) with replacement.

#### 2. Remove the $R^2 > 0.6$ Selection Filter When Estimating the DFE and Account for Within-Gene Neutral Sites
* **WHY:** Requiring $R^2 > 0.6$ (lines 149, 262) discards genes with small $\Delta$ whose flat $\text{i-dN/dS} \approx 1$ trajectories have low $R^2$ solely because the slope is near zero. In addition, assuming all missense mutations in a gene have $s > 0$ (or are drawn from an exponential distribution, Figure 2—figure supplement 1) ignores the fact that a fraction $1 - \pi$ of missense sites in driver genes are functionally neutral ($\Delta = 0$).
* **WHAT:** Report the unfiltered distribution of $\hat{\Delta}$ across all genes meeting the minimum sample size ($\ge 8$ mutations), and extend the within-gene simulation test to a zero-inflated mixture model: $\Delta = 0$ with probability $1-\pi$ and $\Delta \sim \text{Exp}(\bar{\Delta})$ with probability $\pi$.
* **HOW:** (1) Re-plot Figures 3a–d and 4f without the $R^2 > 0.6$ cutoff, weighting or shading estimates by their bootstrap standard errors. (2) In `StemCellModels.jl`, simulate genes where only a fraction $\pi \in \{0.1, 0.25, 0.5\}$ of non-synonymous sites confer bias $\Delta > 0$ while $1-\pi$ have $\Delta = 0$, derive the analytical mixture density $g_{\text{mix}}(A) = (1-\pi)g(A; 0) + \pi g(A; \Delta)$, and quantify how the single-$\Delta$ estimator relates to $(\pi, \Delta)$.

#### 3. Reconcile the Mathematical Derivation of $g(\theta, \mu, s, A)$ and $\text{i-dN/dS}$ Across Main Text and Methods
* **WHY:** There is an internal algebraic inconsistency between Equation (4) in the main text (line 96), the Methods derivation (line 443), and the closed-form $\text{i-dN/dS}$ formula (line 271). For a birth-death process with $b = r\lambda(1+\Delta)$ and $d = r\lambda(1-\Delta)$, integrating $p_n(\tau) = (1-\alpha(\tau))(1-\beta(\tau))\beta(\tau)^{n-1}$ over $\tau \in [0, t]$ yields $\int_0^t p_n(\tau)\,d\tau = \frac{1}{b n}\beta(t)^n = \frac{1}{r\lambda(1+\Delta)n}\beta(t)^n$. Equations (4) and (443) omit the $(1+\Delta)$ factor in the denominator, whereas Equation (271) inserts $\frac{1}{1+\Delta}$ without explanation. Furthermore, Equation (271) contains a typo in the denominator where $\text{Ei}(-\rho A_{\max}/N(t)) - \text{Ei}(-\rho A_{\max}/N(t)) = 0$ (the second term should be $A_{\min}$).
* **WHAT:** Correct the birth-death integration in Equations (4) and (443) and fix the exponential integral expression in Equation (271).
* **HOW:** Explicitly show $\int_0^t p_n(\tau)\,d\tau = \frac{1}{r\lambda(1+\Delta)n}\beta(t)^n$ in the Methods, include $(1+\Delta)^{-1}$ in Equation (4), explain the origin of the Euler-Maclaurin endpoint correction terms $\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^{-\rho A_{\max}/N}}{\rho A_{\max}} + \frac{e^{-\rho A_{\min}/N}}{\rho A_{\min}}\right)$ in Equation (271) (note that in standard Euler-Maclaurin summation the upper endpoint has the same sign as the lower endpoint, whereas continuous integration of $\int_{A_{\min}}^{A_{\max}} A^{-1}e^{-\rho A/N}dA$ requires no endpoint correction at all), and change the second $A_{\max}$ in the denominator of Equation (271) to $A_{\min}$.

#### 4. Temper Title, Abstract, and Discussion Claims Regarding the Tissue-Wide DFE
* **WHY:** Because the data are restricted to a ~72-gene cancer panel and filtered to genes with $\ge 8$ mutations, the inferred distribution represents the distribution of gene-averaged proliferative biases among recurrently mutated driver candidates, not the general DFE of somatic mutations in human tissues.
* **WHAT:** Reframe claims in the Abstract, Results, and Discussion to specify that the measured distribution is the gene-level DFE across positively selected cancer-panel genes.
* **HOW:** Revise Abstract line 8 ("measure the DFE for somatic mutant clones in ostensibly normal oesophagus and skin") and Results line 143 to state "measure the distribution of gene-averaged selective coefficients across recurrently mutated cancer-panel genes in normal oesophagus and skin."
