### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates gut microbiome composition and peripheral blood mRNA expression of lipid metabolism enzymes (`ACC1`, `FASN`, and `CPT1A`, normalized to `GAPDH`) across 62 patients with drug-resistant tuberculosis—11 with pre-extensively drug-resistant TB (`pre-XDR-TB`), 23 with rifampicin-resistant TB (`RR-TB`), and 28 with multi-drug resistant TB (`MDR-TB`)—compared against 5 healthy controls (`CK`) recruited at Wenzhou Central Hospital between March and September 2019. Stool samples were pooled by clinical group (`300 mg` mixed per group), subjected to DNA extraction and sequencing classified via `RDP Classifier v2.10.1`, and compared alongside whole-blood qRT-PCR measurements using one-way ANOVA in SPSS v13.0. Raw sequencing reads and processed abundance tables are not deposited in any public repository.

The study is undermined by fatal experimental design flaws, internal contradictions across sections, and biochemical misinterpretations that render its empirical conclusions invalid. Most critically, the Abstract and Methods state that stool samples from patients within the same clinical group were physically mixed prior to DNA extraction and divided into four tubes, collapsing the biological sample size to $n = 1$ composite pool per group; consequently, the reported "three independent experiments" and one-way ANOVA $p$-values reflect technical replicate noise rather than inter-individual biological variation. Furthermore, the Methods contain irreconcilable contradictions regarding sampling timelines (0/2/4/6 months vs. 0/3 months vs. 2/8 weeks), exclusion criteria (excluding any subject with antibiotic use in the past 6 months while studying patients on active multi-drug anti-TB regimens), and patient demographics (reporting the healthy controls' BMI of $24.12\text{ kg/m}^2$ and age range of 36–59 years from Table 2 as the TB cohort's values in the Results text). Finally, no statistical correlation analysis ($r$, $\rho$, or $p$-values) is ever performed to support the central claim linking *Bacteroidetes* abundance to *de novo* fatty acid synthesis, and `CPT1A` (the rate-limiting enzyme of mitochondrial fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation) is mischaracterized as an enzyme of *de novo* fatty acid synthesis. Resolving these defects would require re-sequencing unpooled individual patient stool specimens and re-analyzing individual-level paired microbiome and gene expression data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Alpha-diversity (Chao1, ACE, Shannon, Simpson) and taxonomic composition (Phylum, Order, Family, and Genus) differ significantly between RR-TB, pre-XDR-TB, MDR-TB, and healthy controls: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In both the Abstract and the *Stool Sample and Faecal DNA Extraction* subsection, the authors state that *"300 mg of faeces collected from the same group of individuals was mixed and analyzed via DNA extraction, high-throughput sequencing, and bioinformatics. ... The extracted DNA was divided into four test tubes."* Physically pooling stool across all subjects within each group destroys all subject-level biological variance ($N = 1$ pool per group), making it impossible to estimate inter-individual $\alpha$-diversity ("The $\alpha$-diversity of each subject", Results Section 1) or to perform valid one-way ANOVA tests across groups (Figure 2). Moreover, the Abstract claims that specific genera (*Eubacterium, Faecalibacterium, Roseburia,* and *Ruminococcus*) were significantly increased in RR-TB patients, but genus-level data are never presented in the Results text or Figures 1–5. The text also contradicts itself on *Bacteroidetes* / *Bacteroidales*: the Results state that *Bacteroidales* disappeared in the TB groups, the Discussion first states that *Bacteroidetes* *"were only found in the healthy participants"* and four sentences later states that their abundance in patient feces was *"1–3%, 1–2%, and 0–2%"*.

* **Enhanced *de novo* fatty acid synthesis (`ACC1`, `FASN`, and `CPT1A` relative to `GAPDH`) is significantly correlated with antimicrobial-induced gut dysbiosis (specifically *Bacteroidetes*, *Bacteroidales*, and *Bacteroidaceae*): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  No correlation analysis (e.g., Pearson $r$, Spearman $\rho$, regression coefficients, or $p$-values) between microbial taxa and blood mRNA expression appears anywhere in the Methods, Results, Tables, or Figures. Because stool samples were pooled by group, individual-level correlation between a patient's gut microbiota and blood mRNA levels cannot be computed. In addition, the authors classify `CPT1A` (carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A) as a key enzyme in *de novo* fatty acid synthesis (Results Section 3 and Figure 6), whereas `CPT1A` catalyzes the rate-limiting step of mitochondrial fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation (catabolism) and is physiologically inhibited by malonyl-CoA generated by `ACC1` during *de novo* lipogenesis.

* **Longitudinal tracking of microbiome shifts during anti-TB antibiotic therapy: [Overstated].**  
  The Methods describe three mutually inconsistent longitudinal sampling schedules—0, 2, 4, and 6 months (*Subjects*); 0 and 3 months (*Subjects*, second paragraph); and 2 and 8 weeks (*Stool Sample and Faecal DNA Extraction*)—while also listing *"current (in the past six months) use of ... antibiotics"* as an exclusion criterion (*Study Design*). Despite claiming that levels of beneficial orders *"increased over time during anti-TB treatment"* (Results Section 2), Figures 1–6 present only a single cross-sectional bar per group (`Con`, `RR-TB`, `Pre-XDR`, `MDR-TB`) with no longitudinal time points shown.

* **Whole-blood mRNA expression of `ACC1/GAPDH` and `FASN/GAPDH` is elevated in drug-resistant TB cohorts (MDR-TB > pre-XDR-TB > RR-TB > healthy controls): [Fragile].**  
  While whole-blood qRT-PCR was performed using defined primer pairs (Table 3), the sample size of the healthy control group is only $n = 5$ (compared to 62 TB patients), the Figure 6 caption states that values represent *"means ± SD of three independent experiments"* rather than biological distributions across the $n = 11$, $23$, $28$, and $5$ subjects, and Results Section 3 claims that `GAPDH` itself showed mRNA expression differences despite `GAPDH` serving as the $\Delta\text{Ct}$ internal normalization reference.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Sequence Individual Patient Stool Samples Rather Than Group Pools and Control False Discovery Rates
* **WHY:** Mixing `300 mg` of feces across individuals within each clinical group collapses biological variation into a single composite sample ($n = 1$ per group). Technical replicates of a pooled DNA extract cannot be used in a one-way ANOVA to infer population-level differences between TB resistance classes.
* **WHAT:** If individual biobanked stool aliquots (`-80 °C`) remain available for the 62 TB patients and 5 controls, extract DNA and perform 16S rRNA sequencing on each patient individually. If only pooled DNA was sequenced, remove all $p$-values and error bars from the microbiome figures and explicitly label the microbiome profiles as descriptive composite group pools without statistical inference.
* **HOW:** For individual-level data, process raw reads using a modern denoising pipeline (`DADA2` or `QIIME 2`), specify the exact 16S rRNA hypervariable region and primer sequences (which are currently omitted from the Methods), compare $\alpha$-diversity across groups ($n = 11, 23, 28, 5$) using Kruskal–Wallis tests, and test differential taxonomic abundance using compositionally aware models (`ANCOM-BC2` or `MaAsLin2`) with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction.

#### 2. Compute Formal Subject-Level Correlations and Correct the Biochemical Interpretation of `CPT1A`
* **WHY:** The Abstract, Results, and Discussion assert that *Bacteroidetes*, *Bacteroidales*, and *Bacteroidaceae* are the "most important correlations" with *de novo* fatty acid synthesis without computing any correlation statistics, and misidentify `CPT1A` as a *de novo* lipogenesis enzyme.
* **WHAT:** Compute formal non-parametric correlation coefficients between subject-level microbial taxon abundances and whole-blood `ACC1/GAPDH`, `FASN/GAPDH`, and `CPT1A/GAPDH` expression, and correct the biochemical role of `CPT1A`.
* **HOW:** Using individual-level paired measurements ($n = 67$ subjects), calculate Spearman's rank correlation $\rho$ and BH-adjusted $p$-values between centered log-ratio (CLR)-transformed taxon abundances and $2^{-\Delta\Delta\text{Ct}}$ blood mRNA levels. Revise the text in Results Section 3 and the Discussion to distinguish *de novo* fatty acid synthesis (`ACC1`, `FASN`) from mitochondrial fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation (`CPT1A`).

#### 3. Reconcile Contradictory Study Design Timelines, Exclusion Criteria, and Cohort Statistics
* **WHY:** The manuscript contains direct contradictions regarding when samples were collected, whether patients were taking antibiotics, and what the mean BMI and age of the TB cohort were.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Cohort BMI and Age Swap (Results Section 1 vs. Tables 1–2):** Results Section 1 states that the 62 TB patients were *"aged between 36 and 59 years"* with a mean BMI of *"24.12 kg/m$^2$"*. However, Table 2 shows that `36–59` years and `24.12 ± 3.14 kg/m^2` are the values for the 5 healthy controls, whereas Table 1 reports an average age of `51.1` years and a BMI of `22.42 ± 3.99 kg/m^2` for the 62 TB patients. Correct the text in Results Section 1 to match Table 1, and report Demographic/Nutritional characteristics stratified by the three TB subgroups (`RR-TB`, `MDR-TB`, `pre-XDR-TB`).
  2. **Sampling Schedule Contradictions (Methods):** Reconcile the three conflicting descriptions of the sampling time points: *"start of the experiment and in the second, fourth, and sixth month"* (*Subjects*), *"start of anti-TB treatment and three months after"* (*Subjects*), and *"at two and eight weeks"* (*Stool Sample and Faecal DNA Extraction*). Clarify which single time point is plotted in Figures 1–6.
  3. **Antibiotic Exclusion Criterion Contradiction (*Study Design*):** Clarify whether *"current (in the past six months) use of ... antibiotics"* applied only to the healthy control (`CK`) group or only to non-TB broad-spectrum antibiotics prior to baseline anti-TB treatment initiation.
  4. **Table 1, Table 2, and Figure 6 Footnotes:** Replace the incorrect footnote *"Data are represented as means ± SD of three independent experiments"* in Tables 1–2 and Figure 6 with the actual biological sample sizes ($n = 62$ TB patients, or $n = 11, 23, 28$, and $n = 5$ controls).
  5. **Gene Symbol Typo (Table 3) and Data Deposition:** Correct `GADPH` to `GAPDH` in Table 3, and deposit the raw sequencing FASTQ files in NCBI SRA / ENA with accession numbers in the Data Sharing Statement.
