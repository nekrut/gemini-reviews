### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This pilot study conducts a genome-wide association study (GWAS) and polygenic risk score (PRS) evaluation for type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) in 997 self-identified Kinh Vietnamese individuals (503 T2DM cases and 494 controls; 957 passing quality control: 489 cases and 468 controls). Genotyping was performed on the Illumina Infinium Global Screening Array-24 v2.0 BeadChip (379,428 post-QC SNPs), followed by phasing (`SHAPEIT2`) and imputation (`IMPUTE2`) against the 1000 Genomes Phase 3 reference panel (`INFO >= 0.8`, yielding 7,587,388 autosomal SNPs). Single-variant association testing in `SNPTEST v2.5.6` identified no genome-wide significant loci ($p \le 5 \times 10^{-8}$), 107 suggestive variants ($p \le 5 \times 10^{-6}$, led pre-imputation by `rs11079784` on chromosome 17 at $p = 8.8 \times 10^{-7}$, $\text{OR} = 1.58$), and 37 variants reported as significant under a Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) of 20%. Using `PRSice-2`, the authors evaluated PRSs derived from East Asian (`EAS`), European (`EUR`), and multi-ancestry (`MIX`) T2DM summary statistics across four $p$-value clumping thresholds and three train/test split ratios (`20:80`, `50:50`, `80:20`). Data are available only upon request to the corresponding author, and no analysis scripts or summary statistics are publicly deposited.

Generating genomic data for underrepresented Southeast Asian populations such as the Kinh Vietnamese is an important and worthwhile effort for trans-ancestry GWAS meta-analyses. However, the statistical analysis and interpretation contain several substantial flaws: (1) the single-SNP GWAS in `SNPTEST` does not adjust for genetic principal components (population stratification), age, sex, or BMI, despite significant baseline case-control imbalances in age ($p < 0.01$), sex ($p = 0.01$), and BMI ($p < 0.001$); (2) the claim that 37 SNPs pass Benjamini–Hochberg FDR $< 0.20$ across 7,587,388 tested SNPs is mathematically inconsistent with the reported minimum $p$-value ($p \approx 8.8 \times 10^{-7}$) unless BH correction was erroneously applied only to a pre-filtered subset of SNPs; (3) the claim that the `MIX` PRS outperforms the `EAS` PRS (based on a single `80:20` split where $\text{AUC} = 0.70$ in $n = 195$ test samples) contradicts both the full-sample variance explained (`EAS` $R^2 = 8.4\%$ vs. `MIX` $R^2 = 7.3\%$, Fig. 2) and the larger `20:80` and `50:50` test splits (`MIX` $\text{AUC} = 0.63\text{–}0.64$, Table 2), reflecting random test-split variance rather than model superiority; and (4) Table 1 contains an obvious numerical error in total diastolic blood pressure (`10.0 ± 10.8 mmHg`) and an invalid comparison between case age-at-onset and control age-at-recruitment. All of these issues can be corrected through a straightforward re-analysis of the existing dataset.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Polygenic risk scores derived from large East Asian (`EAS`) and multi-ancestry (`MIX`) T2DM meta-analyses significantly predict T2DM status in the Kinh Vietnamese cohort and outperform European (`EUR`) weights: [Well Supported].**  
  Across all 957 post-QC individuals (Fig. 2), external T2DM PRS weights show highly significant associations with T2DM liability, with `EAS` summary statistics explaining $8.4\%$ of liability variance ($p = 5 \times 10^{-14}$), `MIX` explaining $7.3\%$ ($p = 2.1 \times 10^{-12}$), and `EUR` explaining $5.7\%$ ($p = 5 \times 10^{-10}$). This clearly demonstrates transferability of East Asian and trans-ancestry polygenic scores to the Kinh Vietnamese population.

* **The multi-ancestry (`MIX`) PRS provides the best predictive model ($\text{AUC} = 0.70$, 95% CI $0.62\text{–}0.77$) and outperforms the `EAS` PRS: [Overstated].**  
  In a univariate logistic regression where a single pre-computed external PRS is the sole predictor ($\text{logit}(P) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot \text{PRS}$), the predicted probability is a strictly monotonic transformation of the PRS itself as long as $\beta_1 > 0$. Consequently, the training split (`20%`, `50%`, or `80%`) does not learn any SNP weights—it only estimates a scalar slope $\beta_1$—and the ROC AUC in the testing split depends solely on which individuals happened to fall into the random test partition. In Table 2 (`p <= 5e-2`), when tested on 80% of the cohort ($n = 764$), `EAS` achieves $\text{AUC} = 0.66$ ($0.61\text{–}0.69$) whereas `MIX` achieves $\text{AUC} = 0.63$ ($0.59\text{–}0.67$); on 50% of the cohort ($n = 479$), both achieve $0.63\text{–}0.64$. Only when the test set is shrunk to 20% ($n = 195$) does `MIX` jump to $0.70$ ($0.62\text{–}0.77$) while `EAS` drops to $0.62$ ($0.54\text{–}0.70$). Moreover, across the full $N = 957$ dataset (Fig. 2), `EAS` explains *more* variance than `MIX` ($8.4\%$ vs. $7.3\%$). Highlighting the noisy $n = 195$ subsample estimate ($\text{AUC} = 0.70$) in the Abstract and Discussion while ignoring the full-cohort result misrepresents sampling fluctuation as ancestral model superiority.

* **37 SNPs are statistically associated with T2DM after Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment at 20% FDR, and `rs11079784` is a candidate marker: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  There are two major methodological issues with the single-SNP GWAS:
  1. **Mathematical impossibility of the BH FDR claim across 7.58M SNPs:** Under the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure across $m = 7,587,388$ tests at $q = 0.20$, for $k = 37$ SNPs to pass FDR $\le 0.20$, the 37th smallest $p$-value must satisfy $p_{(37)} \le \frac{37}{7,587,388} \times 0.20 = 9.75 \times 10^{-7}$, and the top SNP must have a much smaller $p$-value (or all 37 SNPs must cluster below $9.75 \times 10^{-7}$, which typically reflects a single LD block on chromosome 17 rather than 37 independent signals). Furthermore, if `p.adjust` was applied only to the 107 suggestive SNPs ($p \le 5 \times 10^{-6}$) rather than all $7,587,388$ tested SNPs, the FDR calculation is invalid.
  2. **Omission of covariates in `SNPTEST`:** Section 2.2.4 describes running single-SNP association tests in `SNPTEST v2.5.6` without including genetic principal components (PC1–PC10), age, sex, or BMI as covariates. Because cases and controls differ significantly in age ($51.2$ vs. $49.1\text{ yr}$, $p < 0.01$), sex ($44.3\%$ vs. $36.2\%$ male, $p = 0.01$), and BMI ($24.9$ vs. $23.4\text{ kg/m}^2$, $p < 0.001$), unadjusted association statistics are vulnerable to confounding.

* **Age of T2DM onset in cases is significantly lower than the age at recruitment of controls ($46.0 \pm 11.9$ vs. $49.1 \pm 12.6\text{ years}$, $p < 0.001$): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In Table 1 (line 82) and Results (line 70), the authors compare *retrospective age at onset* in prevalent T2DM cases (who had already lived with T2DM for a median of 3 [IQR 1–8] years prior to recruitment) against *current age at recruitment* in healthy controls. As shown in line 81 of Table 1, at the time of recruitment, the T2DM cases were actually significantly *older* than the controls ($51.2 \pm 12.6$ vs. $49.1 \pm 12.6\text{ years}$, $p < 0.01$). Subtracting disease duration from cases while not subtracting an equivalent time window from controls creates an artificial difference that has no epidemiological validity.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Re-Run `SNPTEST` Adjusting for Age, Sex, BMI, and Genetic Principal Components, and Report the Genomic Inflation Factor ($\lambda_{\text{GC}}$)
* **WHY:** Unadjusted case-control GWAS tests can be confounded by subtle population substructure within Vietnam or by the significant baseline differences in age, sex, and BMI documented in Table 1.
* **WHAT:** Compute the top 10 genetic principal components (`PLINK --pca 10`) on LD-pruned post-QC genotypes, re-run `SNPTEST` with age, sex, BMI (or without BMI as a sensitivity model), and PC1–PC10 as covariates (`-cov_names`), and report the genomic inflation factor $\lambda_{\text{GC}}$ alongside the QQ plot.
* **HOW:** Run `snptest -data cohort.gen cohort.sample -o results_adj.out -frequentist 1 -method score -pheno T2DM -cov_names age sex PC1 PC2 PC3 PC4 PC5`, compute $\lambda_{\text{GC}} = \text{median}(\chi^2_1) / 0.4549$, and display the QQ plot next to the Manhattan plot in Figure 1 (currently line 117 mentions *"Post-QC Manhattan and QQ plots"*, but Figure 1 only shows the Manhattan plot).

#### 2. Correct the Benjamini–Hochberg FDR Calculation and Perform LD Clumping on the Chromosome 17 Locus
* **WHY:** Because imputed SNPs in high linkage disequilibrium (LD) share nearly identical $p$-values, reporting "37 SNPs associated with T2DM mainly located in chromosome 17" conflates correlated imputed variants within a single LD block around `rs11079784` (`KPNB1-DT`/`NPEPPS`) with 37 independent genetic loci. Moreover, BH FDR must be computed across all $7,587,388$ tested SNPs.
* **WHAT:** Verify that BH adjustment was applied to the full vector of $7,587,388$ $p$-values (`p.adjust(p_all, method = "BH")`), report the exact number of independent LD-clumped loci ($r^2 < 0.1$, $500\text{ kb}$ window) among the 107 suggestive SNPs ($p \le 5 \times 10^{-6}$), and provide a regional association plot (`LocusZoom`) for the chromosome 17 signal (`rs11079784`).
* **HOW:** In Table S1, group the 107 suggestive SNPs into independent LD clumps using `PLINK --clump` and report the lead SNP, chromosome, position, effect/non-effect allele, EAF in cases and controls, imputation `INFO` score, OR (95% CI), raw $p$-value, and genome-wide BH-adjusted $q$-value.

#### 3. Report Full-Sample and Cross-Validated PRS AUCs Instead of Single Unstable Train/Test Splits
* **WHY:** Because external PRS weights ($\hat{\beta}_j$ from `EAS`, `EUR`, and `MIX` base GWAS) are fixed *a priori*, evaluating a single-variable logistic regression on a single random 20% holdout ($n = 195$, Table 2) introduces large Monte Carlo variance (95% CI width of $0.15\text{–}0.16$) and leads to the contradiction where `EAS` has higher $R^2$ ($8.4\%$ vs. $7.3\%$) and higher AUC in the 20:80 split ($0.66$ vs. $0.63$), yet `MIX` is claimed to be best due to the 80:20 split ($0.70$ vs. $0.62$).
* **WHAT:** Report the full-cohort ROC AUC ($N = 957$) and 10-fold cross-validated mean AUC (with 95% CIs) for `EAS`, `EUR`, and `MIX` across the four clumping thresholds, both for the PRS alone and for a combined model (`Age + Sex + BMI + PRS`).
* **HOW:** Using `pROC::roc(case_control ~ prs)` on all $N = 957$ individuals (and `pROC::roc.test` for paired DeLong tests between `EAS`, `MIX`, and `EUR` ROC curves), report the exact full-sample AUCs and DeLong $p$-values in Table 2, and update the Abstract and Discussion to reflect the full-cohort performance (where `EAS` and `MIX` perform comparably at $\text{AUC} \approx 0.65$).

#### 4. Fix Numerical and Epidemiological Errors in Table 1 and Deposit Summary Statistics
* **WHY:** Table 1 contains an obvious typo in Total DBP and a flawed comparison of case age-at-onset vs. control age-at-recruitment, and the GWAS summary statistics are not deposited in a public repository despite the stated goal of enabling future meta-analyses.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Table 1 (line 97): Change Total `DBP (mmHg)` from `"10.0 ± 10.8"` to `"80.0 ± 10.8"`.
  2. Table 1 (line 82) and Results (line 70): Remove the $p$-value comparing `Age of T2DM onset (T2DM)` (`46.0 ± 11.9`) against `age at recruitment (control)` (`49.1 ± 12.6`); report `Age of T2DM onset` as a case-only descriptive row (just like `Duration of T2DM` in line 83).
  3. Methods line 52 and Discussion line 184: Fix the spelling of `"Benjamini–Hocberg"` to `"Benjamini–Hochberg"`.
  4. Deposit the full genome-wide summary statistics (SNP ID, chromosome, position, effect allele, other allele, EAF, beta/OR, SE, $p$-value, `INFO`) in the NHGRI-EBI GWAS Catalog or Zenodo/Figshare to fulfill the manuscript's goal of supporting Southeast Asian T2DM meta-analyses.
