### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents the aggregation, joint variant calling, quality control, and loss-of-function (LoF) constraint modeling of 125,748 whole-exome and 15,708 whole-genome sequencing datasets from unrelated adult individuals across six global and eight sub-continental ancestry groups in the Genome Aggregation Database (gnomAD v2.1.1; publicly available at `https://gnomad.broadinstitute.org`). Using a standardized BWA-Picard-GATK pipeline in Hail and a random forest variant filtering model validated on gold-standard benchmarks (`NA12878`, synthetic diploid mixture) and 4,780 trios (achieving $>99\%$ SNV and $>98.5\%$ indel precision and $97.8\%$ transmitted singleton recall on held-out chromosome 20), the authors catalogue 14.9 million high-quality exome variants and 229.9 million genome variants. Applying the Loss-Of-Function Transcript Effect Estimator (LOFTEE) and a context-specific mutational model incorporating CpG methylation across 37 tissues and base-level coverage correction (capturing synonymous variant counts with $r = 0.979$), they identify 443,769 high-confidence predicted loss-of-function (pLoF) variants and introduce the loss-of-function observed/expected upper bound fraction (LOEUF), a continuous gene- and transcript-level constraint metric with built-in confidence intervals across 19,197 genes. All variant tables, constraint scores, QC pipelines, LOFTEE software (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`), and analysis Docker images (`konradjk/gnomad_lof_paper:0.2`) are openly available.

This is an exemplary genomic resource and population genetics study. Replacing the previously dichotomous pLI metric with the continuous upper confidence bound of the Poisson observed/expected ratio (LOEUF) properly separates point estimates of constraint from sample-size-dependent statistical uncertainty in short genes, and the orthogonal validations—spanning autosomal deletion structural variants ($r = 0.13, P = 9.8 \times 10^{-68}$), mouse heterozygous lethal knockouts ($P = 10^{-78}$), human CRISPR essentiality screens ($P = 9 \times 10^{-71}$), de novo pLoF burden in intellectual disability/developmental delay ($11$- to $15$-fold enrichment in the top LOEUF decile), and partitioned common-variant LD score regression heritability across 276 independent GWAS traits—are thorough and appropriately controlled for coding sequence length. Only minor technical issues exist, specifically a text-figure discrepancy in the reported de novo rate ratio for intellectual disability/developmental delay ($15$-fold in the main text vs. $11$-fold in the Figure 5a caption) and the reliance on a single European-dominated site-frequency saturation calibration when comparing LOEUF across downsampled ancestry groups with distinct demographic histories.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Aggregation and random-forest quality control of 125,748 exomes and 15,708 genomes yield a high-precision, high-recall catalogue of common and rare human coding variation, together with 443,769 LOFTEE-filtered high-confidence pLoF variants: [Well Supported].**  
  The multi-stage sample QC (PCA-HDBSCAN platform imputation, population- and platform-stratified 4-MAD outlier filtering, relatedness pruning) and random forest site filtering outperform GATK VQSR on `NA12878`, synthetic diploid mixtures, chromosome 20 transmitted singletons ($>97.8\%$ retained), and 375 validated de novo mutations ($>97.3\%$ recall, Extended Data Figures 1–3). LOFTEE filters out terminal truncations and splice-rescue artefacts without using allele frequency, yet selectively removes common false-positive pLoFs while sparing ClinVar pathogenic variants and retaining a high Mutability-Adjusted Proportion of Singletons (MAPS, Figure 2a–b).

* **The refined methylation- and coverage-aware mutational model and continuous LOEUF metric accurately rank 19,197 human genes and transcripts by intolerance to heterozygous inactivation: [Well Supported].**  
  Incorporating tissue-averaged CpG methylation levels and log-coverage calibration achieves $r = 0.979$ between observed and expected synonymous variant counts across transcripts (Extended Data Figure 6f). Using the 90% upper bound of the observed/expected confidence interval (LOEUF) cleanly resolves the small-gene identifiability problem (where $\text{obs}=0, \text{exp}=2$ yields $\text{LOEUF}=1.34$, whereas $\text{obs}=0, \text{exp}=100$ yields $\text{LOEUF}=0.03$), and the authors consistently control for coding sequence length or restrict to well-powered genes ($\text{expected pLoF} \ge 10$, representing $72.1\%$ of genes) in downstream regressions.

* **LOEUF correlates with structural variant depletion, mouse heterozygous lethality, cellular essentiality, protein interaction degree, tissue expression breadth, rare de novo disease burden, and common-variant polygenic heritability: [Well Supported].**  
  Each biological and clinical validation uses independent external datasets and adjusts for gene length, brain expression, and 74 baseline genomic annotations in stratified LD score regression ($\hat{\tau}^*$ in Extended Data Figure 10). The transcript-level LOEUF analysis across $n=1,740$ multi-transcript genes (Figure 4c) further demonstrates that within-gene constraint tracks isoform expression across disease-relevant GTEx tissues.

* **Cross-population comparisons of downsampled exomes ($n=8,128$) demonstrate lower mean LOEUF in African/African American individuals (0.49 vs. 0.62 in Europeans, $P = 4.1 \times 10^{-14}$), reflecting more efficient purifying selection in populations with larger effective population sizes: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While a higher effective population size ($N_e$) in African populations increases the total number of neutral polymorphic sites discovered at fixed sample size $n=8,128$, the expected variant count model in gnomAD is calibrated against synonymous site saturation in the full (predominantly European) cohort. If the neutral synonymous calibration is not re-fitted separately within each downsampled ancestry group ($n=8,128$) to match that population's neutral site-frequency spectrum and heterozygosity, differences in baseline genetic diversity or admixture can shift the expected denominator and confound the comparison of mean LOEUF across ancestries.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Reconcile Numerical Discrepancy Between Main Text and Figure 5a Caption for ID/DD De Novo Enrichment
* **WHY:** On line 77 of the main text, the authors state: *"In an independent cohort of 5,305 individuals with intellectual disability or developmental disorders and 2,179 controls, the rate of pLoF de novo variation in cases is 15-fold higher in genes belonging to the most constrained LOEUF decile, compared with controls (Fig. 5a)"*. However, the caption to Figure 5a on the very same line states: *"pLoF variants in the most constrained decile of the genome are approximately 11-fold more likely to be found in cases compared to controls"*, while the plotted point in Figure 5a appears near 11–15.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Verify the exact rate ratio $(n_{\text{cases}}/5305) / (n_{\text{controls}}/2179)$ in the first LOEUF decile (or top two deciles combined) from the underlying trio table and update either the main text (line 77) or the Figure 5a caption so both report the identical number and its 95% confidence interval.

#### 2. Verify Population-Specific Calibration of Expected Counts in the Cross-Ancestry LOEUF Comparison
* **WHY:** In Extended Data Figure 8d–e, LOEUF is compared across populations downsampled to $n=8,128$ (the size of the African/African American exome subset), finding a lower mean LOEUF in African/African American samples ($0.49$ vs. $0.62$ in Europeans for 927 genes with $\text{exp} \ge 10$). Because African/African American samples have higher neutral genetic diversity ($\theta = 4N_e\mu$) and less CpG saturation at $n=8,128$ than bottlenecked populations (e.g., Finnish or East Asian), the slope relating trinucleotide/methylation mutation rates to the *proportion of possible synonymous variants observed* (Extended Data Figure 6c–d) differs by ancestry.
* **WHAT:** Confirm and explicitly document whether the synonymous proportion-observed calibration curves (Extended Data Figure 6c–d) were re-estimated independently within each $n=8,128$ population subset, and check that the observed/expected synonymous ratio is centered at $1.00$ within each population subset.
* **HOW:** Plot the distribution of synonymous observed/expected ratios alongside pLoF observed/expected ratios for the 865 / 927 genes across each of the $n=8,128$ ancestry cohorts in Extended Data Figure 8e.

#### 3. Quantify the Sensitivity of LOFTEE's `END_TRUNC` GERP Cutoff to Circular Evolutionary Constraint
* **WHY:** Extended Data Figure 5b shows that LOFTEE's `END_TRUNC` filter (for stop-gained variants in the terminal 50 bp / last exon) uses the sum of GERP mammalian conservation scores of the truncated bases to decide whether a terminal variant is retained as a high-confidence pLoF. Because GERP conservation across mammals correlates with human selective constraint (LOEUF), using GERP to define which terminal variants count as expected and observed pLoFs introduces a slight coupling between cross-species conservation and human pLoF counting in the 3′ end of transcripts.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Report in the Supplementary Information the correlation between LOEUF computed with the sequence-only 50-bp NMD rule versus the GERP-tuned `END_TRUNC` rule to demonstrate that terminal exon filtering does not materially alter gene-level LOEUF decile assignments.
