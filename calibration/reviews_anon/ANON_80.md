### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript evaluates whether shotgun metagenomic sequencing of stool can noninvasively detect colorectal carcinoma (CRC) and reflect microbial community and metabolic shifts in the tumor microenvironment. In a French discovery cohort undergoing colonoscopy (Study Population F, $N=156$: 61 neoplasia-free controls, 27 patients with small adenomas $<1\text{ cm}$, 15 with large adenomas $\ge 1\text{ cm}$, and 53 with AJCC stage I–IV CRC), the authors train an $L_1$-penalized (LASSO) logistic regression classifier using 10-times repeated 10-fold cross-validation with nested 5-fold hyperparameter tuning. A 22-species consensus signature—dominated by *Fusobacterium nucleatum* subsp. *vincentii*, *F. nucleatum* subsp. *animalis*, *Porphyromonas asaccharolytica*, and *Peptostreptococcus stomatis*—achieves an cross-validated area under the ROC curve (AUC) of $0.84$ ($N=141$, excluding large adenomas), comparable to guaiac fecal occult blood testing (Hemoccult FOBT) and *wif-1* methylation, and reaching $\text{AUC} = 0.87$ ($>45\%$ relative sensitivity gain at matched specificity) when combined with FOBT. The classifier maintains sensitivity across early (stages I–II) and late (stages III–IV) CRC, shows a 24% false-positive rate in 25 inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) metagenomes, and achieves $\text{AUC} = 0.85$ when evaluated across an external German CRC cohort (Population G, $N=38$) and 297 European non-CRC controls (Population H). Targeted 16S rRNA sequencing of 48 newly collected and 79 published paired tumor–normal biopsies confirms mucosal enrichment of top fecal marker taxa, while KEGG and CAZy profiling indicates a shift from dietary fiber degradation to host mucin/glycan utilization, amino acid putrefaction, and lipopolysaccharide (LPS) metabolism. Raw reads (ENA: ERP005534) and processed matrices (Supplementary Datasets S1–S3) are publicly available.

The study is methodologically strong in its nested cross-validation pipeline, confounder checks (age, BMI, sequencing depth), and multi-modal integration of stool shotgun metagenomes with paired tumor/mucosa 16S data. Three analytical caveats merit attention: (1) in the external validation ROC analysis ($\text{AUC} = 0.85$), all 38 CRC cases come from Population G (German surgical patients sampled in RNAlater after colonoscopy/bowel cleanse) whereas 292 of the 297 controls come from Population H (historical Danish and Spanish MetaHIT cohorts frozen without RNAlater and without colonoscopic verification), confounding external case–control status with collection protocol and country; (2) patients with large adenomas ($\ge 1\text{ cm}$, $n=15$)—a primary target of clinical CRC screening—were excluded from classifier training and ROC evaluation; and (3) the principal component analyses in Figure 5 and Supplementary Figure S9B were computed on features pre-selected for differential abundance in the same cohort, introducing circularity into the reported progression $P$-values. These points can be addressed through supplemental sensitivity analyses and clearer framing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **A 22-species LASSO metagenomic classifier accurately distinguishes CRC patients from neoplasia-free/small-adenoma controls in cross-validation ($\text{AUC} = 0.84$) and improves sensitivity by $>45\%$ when combined with FOBT ($\text{AUC} = 0.87$) (Results lines 62–75, Figure 1A,B): [Well Supported].**  
  The LASSO modeling pipeline avoids feature-selection leakage by performing abundance filtering unsupervisely and nesting hyperparameter tuning inside each training fold of a 10-times repeated 10-fold cross-validation. DeLong and bootstrap tests support the gain of the combined metagenomic + FOBT predictor, and explicit checks (Supplementary Figure S5) show that age and BMI differences do not drive the classification scores. However, excluding the 15 large adenoma patients ($\ge 1\text{ cm}$) from the binary evaluation simplifies the classification boundary relative to real-world screening cohorts.

* **The metagenomic CRC classifier generalizes with $\text{AUC} = 0.85$ to independent patient and control populations ($N=335$) across countries and technical protocols (Results lines 77–82, Figure 1C): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Reporting sensitivity on Population G ($52.6\%$ at threshold $0.275$, $N=38$ German CRC cases) and specificity on Population H ($92.3\%$, $N=297$ controls) separately in Supplementary Figure S6A,B is informative. However, combining Population G (100% CRC cases, collected in RNAlater $\ge 10\text{ days}$ post-colonoscopy) with Population H (100% controls, mostly Danish/Spanish MetaHIT samples frozen without RNAlater) into a single ROC curve ($\text{AUC} = 0.85$) means that any systematic shift in prediction scores caused by RNAlater preservation, prior bowel cleanse, or geographic baseline directly shifts the joint AUC.

* **Early-stage CRC (AJCC stages I–II) is detected with sensitivity comparable to late-stage metastatic CRC (stages III–IV), whereas adenomas are largely indistinguishable from healthy controls (Results lines 59–60, 84–87, Figure 1D,E): [Well Supported].**  
  Stratified sensitivity comparisons in both Populations F and G confirm that stage I–II tumors (22 in F, 25 in G) are detected at rates similar to stage III–IV tumors (31 in F, 13 in G), and *Fusobacterium* subspecies and *P. stomatis* are already significantly elevated in stage I–II disease.

* **Fecal CRC marker species reflect microbial enrichment at the tumor mucosa relative to adjacent normal tissue (Results lines 100–109, Figure 3): [Well Supported].**  
  Cross-platform comparison of shotgun stool profiles ($N=141$), stool V4 16S amplicon profiles ($N=116$), newly sequenced paired tumor–normal surgical biopsies ($N=48$), and re-analyzed paired biopsies from Kostic et al. ($N=79$) shows concordant enrichment of *Fusobacterium* and *Peptostreptococcus stomatis* and depletion of *Eubacterium* spp. and *Streptococcus salivarius*.

* **CRC-associated functional shifts (host glycan/mucin degradation, amino acid putrefaction, LPS metabolism) correlate strongly with neoplastic progression along PC1 ($r_s = 0.45, P < 10^{-8}$) (Results lines 111–134, Figures 4 and 5): [Fragile].**  
  While the individual Wilcoxon tests for the 24 KEGG modules and 20 CAZy families are properly FDR-controlled ($q < 0.01$, fold change $>1.33$), Figure 5 computes a PCA exclusively on those 44 features that were already selected for significant differences between CRC and controls in Population F, and then tests PC1 for Spearman correlation across the four ordered progression groups (healthy, adenoma, stage I–II CRC, stage III–IV CRC) in the exact same Population F individuals. Because the input features were supervised on case–control status in Population F, PC1 is guaranteed by construction to separate controls from CRC cases, inflating $r_s = 0.45$ and $P < 10^{-8}$.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Report Classifier Performance When Large Adenomas ($\ge 1\text{ cm}$) Are Included
* **WHY:** Large adenomas ($\ge 10\text{ mm}$, $n=15$ in Population F) are clinically actionable precancerous lesions targeted by colonoscopy screening, yet they were omitted from both the case and control definitions in Figure 1B (`N = 141` out of `156`).
* **WHAT:** Report the distribution of metagenomic classifier prediction scores, sensitivity, and ROC AUCs when the 15 large adenoma patients are (a) evaluated as a separate target group, (b) included with CRC cases as "advanced colorectal neoplasia" ($53 + 15 = 68$ cases vs. $88$ controls), or (c) retained in the non-CRC screening control pool ($53$ CRC vs. $103$ non-CRC).
* **HOW:** Apply the out-of-fold LASSO models from Population F to the 15 held-out large adenoma samples, plot their prediction scores and positive call rate (at the $0.275$ threshold) alongside small adenomas and stage I–II CRC in Figure 1D and Figure 2A, and report the resulting AUCs for both "any CRC vs. all non-CRC ($N=156$)" and "advanced neoplasia (CRC + large adenoma) vs. normal/small adenoma ($N=156$)."

#### 2. Decouple Unsupervised Ordination from Supervised Feature Selection in Figure 5
* **WHY:** Computing PCA on the 44 KEGG/CAZy features selected for differential abundance between CRC and controls in Population F and then testing PC1 for correlation with CRC stage progression in Population F (Figure 5) is circular.
* **WHAT:** Perform PCA on the full set of abundance-filtered KEGG modules and CAZy families (unsupervised) and/or evaluate the 44-feature PC1 projection in the independent cohorts (G and H) and held-out adenoma samples.
* **HOW:** First, run PCA on all log-transformed KEGG modules and CAZy families passing the unsupervised $>10^{-4}$ abundance filter (Methods, line 109 of chunk 2) and report the correlation of the unsupervised PCs with stage progression. Second, project the held-out adenoma samples ($n=42$) and external cohorts G and H onto the PC1 loadings learned from healthy vs. CRC, and test whether adenoma and stage I–II vs. stage III–IV progression differences hold without circular selection.

#### 3. Address Protocol and Country Differences Between External Validation Cohorts G and H
* **WHY:** Combining Population G (38 German CRC cases in RNAlater, post-colonoscopy) and Population H (292 Danish/Spanish MetaHIT controls frozen without RNAlater + 5 German controls) into a single ROC curve (Figure 1C) conflates potential batch/preservation offsets with classifier discrimination.
* **WHAT:** Quantify whether the raw distribution of LASSO linear predictors shifts between French controls (Population F, frozen), German controls ($n=5$, frozen), Danish/Spanish controls (Population H, frozen), and German cases (Population G, RNAlater).
* **HOW:** Compare the median and interquartile range of the LASSO prediction scores across the four control subgroups (French neoplasia-free, French small adenoma, German healthy, Danish/Spanish MetaHIT) using a Kruskal–Wallis test, and explicitly note in the Results (lines 79–80) that while threshold-specific sensitivity in G ($52.6\%$) and specificity in H ($92.3\%$) are validated separately, future multicenter validation should collect matched cases and controls in the same preservation buffer prior to colonoscopy.
