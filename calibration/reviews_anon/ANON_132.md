### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This study investigates gut microbiome composition and serum metabolomic profiles in 20 patients with sporadic growth hormone-secreting pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (GH-PitNETs) compared with 30 healthy controls (HC) at a single clinical center, followed by *in vitro* experiments in rat GH3 pituitary adenoma cells and a 7-day oral gavage experiment in normal C57BL/6 mice ($n = 5$ per group) treated with indole-3-acetic acid (IAA, $100\text{ mg/kg/day}$). Using fecal 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing and untargeted ($n = 20$ GH vs. $n = 17$ HC) plus targeted ($n = 11$ GH vs. $n = 11$ HC) LC–MS serum metabolomics, the authors report shifts in *Bacteroides*, *Blautia*, *Bifidobacterium*, and *Intestinibacter bartlettii*, alongside elevated serum IAA. They further report that $0.01\text{–}1\ \mu\text{M}$ IAA increases GH3 cell proliferation, GH levels, and AHR/p-CREB/p-ERK1/2/Pit1 expression, which are attenuated by the AHR inhibitor BAY-218 and the adenylyl cyclase inhibitor SQ22536. Fecal 16S reads (PRJNA1244248) and metabolomics data (MTBLS12341) are deposited, whereas the GH3 RNA-seq data (`CON1–3`, `IAA1–3`) and analysis scripts are not publicly archived.

While integrating clinical microbiome/metabolome profiling with functional pituitary cell assays addresses an interesting neuroendocrine question, the study's core conclusions are compromised by severe clinical confounding, internal numerical contradictions, unadjusted multiple testing on small sub-cohorts, and overinterpretation of reverse-causal metabolic effects. Specifically, 35% of the GH-PitNET patients have diabetes mellitus versus 0% of controls ($p = 0.0008$) and patients have a markedly higher BMI ($27.16$ vs. $23.36\text{ kg/m}^2, p < 0.0001$), yet no multivariable adjustment for diabetes, antidiabetic medications, or BMI was performed. Furthermore, the reported microbial relative abundances are mathematically self-contradictory (genus *Bacteroides* is reported as 23.2% of the GH group while its parent phylum *Bacteroidota* is reported as only 7.5%), a non-existent bacterial phylum (*"Desmoplasia"*) is reported in the text, and single-timepoint serum GH measurements in $n = 5$ normal, tumor-free mice ignore the 10- to 100-fold ultradian pulsatility of murine GH secretion. Substantial statistical re-analysis controlling for metabolic confounders and major tempering of causal claims are required.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **GH-PitNET patients exhibit a disease-specific gut microbiota dysbiosis independent of host metabolic confounders: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  In the Results ("Characteristics of the research cohort"), the authors assert that Table 1 *"confirmed comparable characteristics between the two groups, eliminating potential confounding factors"*, yet Table 1 immediately shows that diabetes mellitus is present in 35% (7/20) of GH-PitNET patients vs. 0% (0/30) of controls ($p = 0.0008$) and BMI is $27.16 \pm 2.96\text{ kg/m}^2$ in patients vs. $23.36 \pm 2.63\text{ kg/m}^2$ in controls ($p < 0.0001$). Both type 2 diabetes (along with oral antidiabetic drugs such as metformin or acarbose, which were not excluded) and obesity are well-established primary drivers of increased fecal *Bacteroides* and depleted *Blautia* and *Bifidobacterium*. Moreover, the taxonomic summary in the text is internally contradictory: the phylum *Bacteroidota* is stated to comprise **7.5%** of the GH group (and 14.6% of HC), whereas two sentences later its constituent genus *Bacteroides* is stated to account for **23.2%** of the GH group (and 10.8% of HC)—a mathematical impossibility since a genus cannot exceed the relative abundance of its parent phylum.

* **Specific bacterial phyla (including *"Desmoplasia"*) and genera correlate with GH-PitNET clinical severity markers: [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 3, the authors compute unadjusted Spearman correlations across dozens of taxa and clinical indicators (Ki67, p53, Knosp grade, OGTT-GH nadir) that are only measured in the $n = 20$ GH-PitNET patients (`NA` in all 30 controls in Table 1) without false discovery rate (FDR) correction. In the text describing Figure 3A, the authors list *"Actinobacteriota, Verrucomicrobiota, Fusobacteriota, and Desmoplasia"* as four bacterial phyla positively correlated with severity; *"Desmoplasia"* is a pathological term for fibrotic tissue stroma, not a bacterial phylum (likely a mistranslation/corruption of *Desulfobacterota*).

* **Serum tryptophan metabolites (IAA, 5-HTP, and indole) are significantly elevated in GH-PitNETs and provide high diagnostic accuracy ($\text{AUC} > 0.9$): [Overstated].**  
  First, there is unexplained sample attrition: untargeted metabolomics analyzed only 17 of the 30 healthy controls ($n = 20$ GH vs. $n = 17$ HC), and targeted tryptophan metabolomics analyzed only 11 patients and 11 controls ($n = 11$ vs. $n = 11$, Figure 4F–G and Figure 5). No explanation is provided for how these subsets were chosen. Second, the ROC curves in Figure 5D reporting $\text{AUC} > 0.9$ were evaluated on the exact same small sample ($n = 11$ vs. $11$) used to select the top differentially abundant metabolites, without cross-validation or an independent test cohort, resulting in severe in-sample optimistic bias. Third, because GH/IGF-1 excess slows gastrointestinal transit time and alters hepatic/renal amino acid clearance, elevated serum indole derivatives in active acromegaly are highly plausible *consequences* of hypersomatotropism and secondary diabetes rather than upstream causes of pituitary tumorigenesis.

* **IAA drives GH-PitNET tumor progression and GH secretion *in vivo* and *in vitro* via the AHR–cAMP–PKA/CREB pathway: [Fragile].**  
  While the *in vitro* GH3 cell assays ($n = 3$ technical/biological replicates, Figure 7) show modest increases in CTG luminescence, EdU incorporation, and supernatant GH after $1\ \mu\text{M}$ IAA treatment, several methodological gaps weaken the mechanistic and *in vivo* claims:
  1. **No *in vivo* tumor model and unaddressed GH pulsatility:** The *in vivo* experiment (Figure 7E) did not use a pituitary tumor model (e.g., GH3 xenograft or somatotroph adenoma mouse model); it gavaged 5 normal 5-week-old male C57BL/6 mice with a high pharmacological dose of IAA ($100\text{ mg/kg/day}$) and measured serum GH from a single retro-orbital blood draw on day 8. Because physiological GH secretion in male mice is highly pulsatile (oscillating between $<1\text{ ng/mL}$ baseline troughs and $>50\text{ ng/mL}$ peaks every few hours), a single-point blood draw in $n = 5$ mice cannot reliably quantify GH hypersecretion.
  2. **Non-monotonic dose response and missing inhibitor controls:** In Figure 7A–B, GH3 proliferation peaks at $1\ \mu\text{M}$ IAA and declines at higher concentrations, and in Figure 7L–M (misreferenced as *"Fig. 6 L,M"* in the text), inhibitor-only controls (`BAY-218` alone and `SQ22536` alone without IAA) are not shown or tested via two-way ANOVA interaction terms to distinguish specific blockade of IAA signaling from baseline toxicity or basal cAMP suppression.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Control for Diabetes Mellitus and BMI Confounding in Microbiome and Metabolome Analyses
* **WHY:** Because 35% of the GH-PitNET cohort is diabetic (vs. 0% of HC) and mean BMI is $3.8\text{ kg/m}^2$ higher in the GH-PitNET cohort ($p < 0.0001$), unadjusted two-group comparisons ($t$-tests and Mann–Whitney tests) confound acromegaly-induced metabolic syndrome and antidiabetic medication effects with direct tumor–microbiome associations.
* **WHAT:** Re-analyze differential taxon and metabolite abundance while explicitly controlling for BMI and diabetes status.
* **HOW:** (1) Subgroup the GH-PitNET patients into non-diabetic ($n = 13$) vs. diabetic ($n = 7$) and compare the 13 non-diabetic GH-PitNET patients against BMI-matched healthy controls. (2) Run multivariable linear/generalized linear models (`MaAsLin2` or `ANCOM-BC2` in R) with `Group + BMI + Diabetes + Age + Sex` as covariates, and report FDR-corrected $q$-values for both the 16S ASVs/genera (Figure 2) and serum tryptophan metabolites (Figures 4–5).

#### 2. Reconcile Taxonomic Abundance Contradictions and Correct the Bioinformatics Pipeline Description
* **WHY:** Reporting that phylum *Bacteroidota* is 7.5% while genus *Bacteroides* is 23.2% in the GH group indicates a serious calculation or labeling error. In addition, the Methods state that UPARSE clustered sequences at $>97\%$ similarity into "operational taxonomic units (ASVs)" (confusing 97% OTUs with exact ASVs) and applied parametric Student's $t$-tests to zero-inflated compositional OTU counts.
* **WHAT:** Audit the phylum- and genus-level relative abundance tables, clarify whether 97% OTUs (UPARSE) or denoised ASVs (DADA2/unoise3) were generated, and use compositional/non-parametric differential abundance statistics.
* **HOW:** Re-sum the relative abundance tables at the phylum and genus levels from the raw feature table (PRJNA1244248) to ensure that all genera within *Bacteroidota* (*Bacteroides*, *Prevotella*, *Alistipes*, *Parabacteroides*) sum to less than or equal to the phylum *Bacteroidota* total in both groups. Replace the Student's $t$-test on relative abundances with Wilcoxon rank-sum tests or `ALDEx2`/`ANCOM-BC` with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction.

#### 3. Address Sample Attrition, Overfitted ROC Curves, and Single-Point Mouse GH Measurements
* **WHY:** Dropping from 30 HC to 17 HC in untargeted metabolomics and to 11 GH vs. 11 HC in targeted metabolomics without explanation introduces selection bias, and computing ROC AUCs on $n = 11$ vs. $11$ discovery features overstates biomarker performance. In mice, single-bleed GH ELISA is confounded by ultradian pulsatility, whereas serum IGF-1 integrates 24-hour GH secretion.
* **WHAT:** Explain the exact selection criteria for the $n=17$ and $n=11$ subsets, apply leave-one-out or repeated $k$-fold cross-validation with 95% confidence intervals to the ROC analysis, and measure serum IGF-1 (or serial GH profiles) in the mouse experiment.
* **HOW:** (1) State in the Methods why 13 HC serum samples were excluded from untargeted LC–MS and how the 11 GH and 11 HC samples were selected for targeted tryptophan LC–MS. (2) In `pROC`, compute stratified bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (`ci.auc`) and out-of-fold cross-validated AUCs for Figure 5D. (3) If stored serum from the Day 8 mouse experiment (Figure 7E) remains available, assay serum IGF-1 by ELISA (which is stable across the day and directly reflects integrated GH action) and verify whether actual physiological serum IAA concentrations achieved in the mice match those measured in human GH-PitNET patients.

#### 4. Deposit Missing RNA-Seq Data and Complete the Materials and Methods
* **WHY:** The GH3 cell RNA-seq dataset (`CON1–3`, `IAA1–3`, Figure 7F–H) is not included in the Data Availability statement, and several reagents and protocols in the Methods are incomplete or duplicated.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * **Data Availability:** Deposit the raw FASTQ files and gene count matrix for the 6 GH3 RNA-seq samples (`CON1–3`, `IAA1–3`) in NCBI GEO/SRA and add the accession number to the Data Availability section.
  * **Table 1 (Hypertension Row):** Check whether the Healthy Control hypertension counts (`Negative: 12 (40%), Positive: 18 (60%)`) are inverted; a 60% hypertension prevalence in healthy controls aged $43.7 \pm 10.0$ years is anomalous.
  * **Results ("Relationship between the gut microbiota and clinical phenotype"):** Replace the erroneous term *"Desmoplasia"* with the correct bacterial phylum name (e.g., *Desulfobacterota*).
  * **Figure 3B Caption:** Correct *"HC-positive bacterial phyla"* to *"HC-enriched bacterial genera"* to match the main text and panel contents.
  * **Results ("IAA contributes to GH3 cell growth hormone secretion and proliferation"):** Correct the cross-reference *"As shown in Fig. 6 L,M. BAY-218 and SQ22536..."* to *"Fig. 7 L,M"*, and add catalog numbers and vendor details for `BAY-218` and `SQ22536` to the *Reagents* subsection of the Methods.
  * **Methods ("Growth hormone measurement"):** Remove the duplicate text describing rat GH ELISA and clarify whether Figure 7D,K–M measured secreted GH in conditioned cell culture supernatant or intracellular GH in sonicated cell lysates.
