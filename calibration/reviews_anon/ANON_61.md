### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript introduces `CellPhenoX`, a computational workflow that combines neighborhood abundance matrix (NAM) dimensionality reduction (PCA or NMF, optionally batch-corrected via Harmony) with tree-based classifiers (Random Forest or XGBoost) and TreeSHAP feature attributions to assign a cell-level "Interpretable Score" ($\psi_m = \sum_i \phi_{m,i}$) linking single-cell states to sample-level clinical phenotypes and covariate interactions. The authors benchmark `CellPhenoX` against `MiloR` and `CNA` on custom Gaussian linear simulations (two binary case–control datasets and one sex-by-disease interaction dataset) and apply it to three public clinical single-cell datasets: COVID-19 PBMC CITE-seq ($n = 82$ donors across healthy, mild, and moderate stages; 43,128 cells), ulcerative colitis (UC) mucosal fibroblasts ($n = 18$ patients with paired inflamed and non-inflamed biopsies; 14,822 cells), and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) T cells following anti-PD1 therapy ($n = 18$ patients stratified by clonal expansion; 24,021 cells). Public links are provided for the three empirical datasets, but no source code repository or software package URL is provided for `CellPhenoX` or the simulation pipeline.

While the goal of detecting covariate-by-cell-state interactions in clinical single-cell cohorts is well motivated, the manuscript suffers from foundational mathematical and statistical flaws that compromise both its methodological claims and its benchmarking comparisons. First, the `CellPhenoX` Interpretable Score is defined as the sum of SHAP values across all features for a given cell ($\psi_m = \sum_i \phi_{m,i}$, Equation 17); by the local accuracy (efficiency) axiom of Shapley values, this sum is algebraically identical to the classifier's predicted value minus the global baseline ($\hat{f}(x_m) - E[\hat{f}(X)]$), contradicting the claim in Figure 2D that $\psi_m$ provides superior discriminative power compared to the Random Forest predictions. In the multi-class setting (Equation 16), selecting the SHAP vector of each cell's *predicted* class and summing its entries conflates high confidence for control/healthy states with high confidence for severe disease states. Second, the classification model is trained at the cell level ($M$ cells) to predict patient-level outcomes ($Y$) using patient-level covariates ($\gamma$: age, sex, smoking, days from onset) without donor-grouped cross-validation splits or out-of-fold graph construction, creating severe data leakage and pseudo-replication ($p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ from cell-level Wilcoxon tests on $n = 18$ patients). Third, the benchmark against `CNA` and `MiloR` is misconfigured: controlling for `sample` as a covariate in `CNA` in a case–control design removes the between-sample disease signal, and `MiloR` natively supports interaction terms via its `edgeR` generalized linear model design matrix. Addressing these issues requires restructuring the cross-validation and graph-embedding pipeline at the donor level, correcting the multi-class SHAP formulation, re-running `CNA` and `MiloR` with valid design matrices, and releasing the software code.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The `CellPhenoX` Interpretable Score ($\psi_m = \sum_i \phi_{m,i}$) separates disease-associated cell types more effectively than Random Forest predicted probabilities (Figure 2D): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  By the Shapley efficiency property implemented in `TreeExplainer` / `FastTreeSHAP`, the sum of SHAP values across all model features for observation $m$ equals $\hat{f}(x_m) - E[\hat{f}(X)]$ exactly. Therefore, summing all feature SHAP values is a constant shift of the model output and cannot carry different rank ordering or discriminative power than the model predictions unless covariate SHAP terms ($\phi_{\gamma}$) were silently omitted from Equation 17 or predictions and SHAP values were extracted from mismatched cross-validation folds. Furthermore, in the multi-class COVID-19 analysis (Equation 16), selecting the SHAP row $D_{\hat{c}(i)}[i, :]$ corresponding to each cell's predicted class $\hat{c}(i) \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ means that a cell strongly predicted to be Healthy ($\hat{c} = 0$) and a cell strongly predicted to be Moderate COVID-19 ($\hat{c} = 2$) both receive large positive SHAP sums $\psi_m$, making the aggregated score non-monotonic with respect to disease severity.

* **`CellPhenoX` outperforms `CNA` and `MiloR` in detecting disease-expanded and interaction-driven cell populations on simulated single-cell data: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Three problems invalidate the simulation benchmark. First, the Experimental Section states that for `CNA`, *"the sample was controlled as covariate from the simulation disease-control single-cell data."* Because disease status is nested within sample (donor) in a case–control study, including sample ID as a covariate in `CNA`'s linear model is collinear with disease status and regresses out the target signal. Second, the manuscript claims that `MiloR` lacks the functionality to detect sex-by-disease interactions, whereas `miloR::testNhoods()` accepts any `model.matrix` specification (e.g., `~ sex + disease + sex:disease`) in its underlying `edgeR` negative binomial GLM. Third, although the Introduction motivates `CellPhenoX` by arguing that existing methods fail to capture nonlinear relationships, the custom simulator generates features directly from an additive Gaussian linear model (Equations 2–6) rather than nonlinear single-cell count distributions.

* **Cell-level cross-validation and downstream statistical tests accurately quantify clinical predictive performance and biomarker significance in COVID-19, UC, and TNBC cohorts: [Overstated].**  
  In Equations 10–12, the classifier is trained on $M$ cells where the target $Y$ (clinical outcome) and covariates $\gamma$ (age, sex, smoking, days from onset) are donor-level variables repeated across every cell from the same donor. Using a standard cell-level stratified $k$-fold split places cells from the same patient into both training and test folds; a tree model can then achieve near-perfect accuracy simply by memorizing the unique combination of donor-level covariates (e.g., `age` + `sex` + `days_from_onset`) without using cellular transcriptional state at all. Even when only latent embeddings $X_i$ are used (as in the UC fibroblast analysis, reporting $\text{AUROC} = 0.80$), the kNN graph, NAM matrix, PCA/NMF decomposition, and Harmony integration are computed across all $M$ cells *before* cross-validation, leaking test-fold neighborhood topologies into the training embeddings. Subsequent Wilcoxon rank-sum tests on $\psi_m$ (e.g., $p < 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ in Figure 5C and Figure 5I) treat 14,822 fibroblasts or 24,021 T cells from 18 patients as independent samples, inflating Type I error via pseudo-replication and ignoring the paired within-patient biopsy design in the UC dataset.

* **Application to COVID-19 PBMCs, UC fibroblasts, and TNBC T cells recovers biologically plausible myeloid, inflammatory fibroblast (`CCL19`/`COL6A3`), and exhausted/effector T-cell (`LAG3`/`CXCL13`/`IFNG`/`GZMB`) signatures: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The marker genes and pathways correlated with the latent axes in COVID-19 (`SIGLEC1`, `CD33`, `C5AR1`, `CR1`), UC (`CCL19`, `CCL8`, `COL6A3` vs. `WNT2B`/`ADAMDEC1`/`CXCL12`), and TNBC (`LAG3`, `CXCL13`, `IFNG`, `GZMB`, `CTLA4`) are consistent with established findings from the original publications of these datasets. However, because these canonical markers were already used to define or characterize the underlying clusters in those studies, recovering them via correlation with $\psi_m$ demonstrates qualitative concordance rather than novel discovery power.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Fix the Mathematical Formulation of the Interpretable Score and Multi-Class SHAP Aggregation
* **WHY:** Summing all feature SHAP values ($\psi_m = \sum_i \phi_{m,i}$, Equation 17) reproduces $\hat{f}(x_m) - E[\hat{f}(X)]$ by the Shapley efficiency axiom, which includes the main-effect contributions of nuisance covariates $\gamma$ (e.g., batch or smoking status) and contradicts the claim that $\psi_m$ is distinct from model predictions. Meanwhile, Equation 16 selects SHAP values from each cell's *predicted* class $\hat{c}(i)$, assigning positive scores to cells strongly predicted to belong to the healthy control class.
* **WHAT:** Redefine the cell-level Interpretable Score so that it isolates the partial contribution of the cellular latent embeddings (and their interactions) toward a fixed reference outcome class (or ordinal severity axis), excluding the main-effect SHAP terms of patient-level nuisance covariates.
* **HOW:** Partition the predictor set $\beta$ into cellular embedding features $\mathcal{X} = \{X_1, \dots, X_k\}$, targeted biological interaction terms $\mathcal{I} = \{X_1 \delta, \dots, X_k \delta\}$, and nuisance covariates $\Gamma$. Define the cell-state attribution score for a fixed target disease class $c^*$ (or ordinal severity outcome) as:
  $$\psi_m^{(c^*)} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{X} \cup \mathcal{I}} \phi_{m,j}^{(c^*)}$$
  Verify and explicitly state in the Methods that $\sum_{j \in \beta} \phi_{m,j}^{(c^*)} = \hat{p}_{c^*}(x_m) - E[\hat{p}_{c^*}(X)]$, so any improvement in separating cell types in Figure 2D comes specifically from stripping out nuisance covariate attributions $\sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma} \phi_{m,\gamma}^{(c^*)}$ or isolating specific PCs rather than summing all SHAP values.

#### 2. Eliminate Donor-Level Data Leakage and Pseudo-Replication
* **WHY:** Splitting at the cell level allows the Random Forest to memorize patient-level covariates ($\text{age}, \text{sex}, \text{onset}$) shared by thousands of cells from the same donor, and computing the NAM/PCA/NMF/Harmony embeddings on the full dataset leaks test cells into training features. Testing cell-level Wilcoxon $p$-values across $10^4$ cells from $n = 18$ donors severely inflates false positives.
* **WHAT:** Implement strict donor-grouped cross-validation (and paired within-donor blocking for the 18 paired UC biopsies) and perform donor-level aggregation for statistical inference.
* **HOW:**
  1. Replace `StratifiedKFold` with ` StratifiedGroupKFold(n_splits=5)` grouped by `donor_id` (or `GroupKFold` by `patient_id` for the paired UC biopsies) so that all cells from a given donor reside entirely in either the training fold or the held-out fold.
  2. Compute PCA/NMF loadings on the training-fold donors and project held-out cells into the learned basis within each CV fold.
  3. To prevent tree models from splitting solely on donor-level metadata (where $\text{age}$ and $\text{sex}$ are constant within each donor), either residualize/stratify the outcome or evaluate a baseline model trained *only* on donor metadata ($\gamma$) versus metadata plus cellular embeddings ($X_i + \gamma + X_i \delta_i$), reporting the donor-level $\Delta\text{AUROC}$ and $\Delta\text{AUPRC}$.
  4. In Figures 4D, 5C, and 5I, aggregate $\psi_m$ to the donor-by-cluster mean (or median) and compute Wilcoxon signed-rank (for paired UC biopsies, $n = 18$) or rank-sum tests (for TNBC, $n = 8$ vs. $n = 10$) at the patient level, or fit a linear mixed-effects model with a random intercept for donor (`psi ~ cluster + (1 | donor_id)`).

#### 3. Correct the `CNA` and `MiloR` Benchmarks and Clarify Simulation Parameters
* **WHY:** Controlling for `sample` as a covariate in `CNA` in a case–control design removes the between-sample disease signal, and `MiloR` natively supports covariate interactions via `edgeR` design matrices. Additionally, the simulation uses Gaussian linear features rather than count distributions, and the description of Simulation Dataset 2 is internally contradictory.
* **WHAT:** Re-run `CNA` without `sample` as a fixed covariate (using sample ID only to define the sample-level NAM as intended by `CNA`), run `MiloR` with an explicit `~ sex * disease` design formula on the interaction simulation, and clarify the mathematical definition of the fold-change parameter $f$ in Equation 1.
* **HOW:**
  1. In `CNA` (`rcna::association.Seurat`), pass `samplem_key = 'sample_id'` and `test_var = 'disease'` (with `covs = NULL` for Dataset 1/2, or `covs = 'batch'` if batch was simulated), never `covs = 'sample_id'`.
  2. In `MiloR`, run `testNhoods(milo_obj, design = ~ sex + disease + sex:disease, design.df = sample_meta)` and benchmark the resulting interaction FDR against `CellPhenoX` in Figure 3 / Figure S4.
  3. Clarify Equation 1 ($\text{diff}_j = N \cdot p_j \cdot (1 + f - p_j) - N \cdot p_j \cdot f$, which simplifies algebraically to $N p_j (1 - p_j)$ independent of $f$, indicating a typo in the printed equation) and explain how "all ten clusters show a slightly higher proportion of disease-associated cells" in Dataset 2 when cluster proportions within a sample sum to 1, and why $f = 0.1$ represents a $+10\%$ increase ($1 + f = 1.1\times$) rather than a $0.1\times$ ratio.

#### 4. Provide Public Source Code and Fix Technical and Editorial Errors
* **WHY:** A methods paper introducing a software package (`CellPhenoX`) and a custom simulator cannot be reproduced or adopted without publicly accessible source code. Several equations and text passages also contain formatting and duplication errors.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. **Code and Data Availability:** Deposit the `CellPhenoX` package, the Gaussian mixture simulation scripts, and the benchmarking scripts in a permanent public repository (e.g., GitHub + Zenodo DOI) and replace the personal Google Drive link for the TNBC dataset with a permanent repository archive.
  2. **Equation (4) (Experimental Section, "Datasets—Simulation Methodology"):** Complete the truncated normalization constraint so it reads $\sum_f w_f^2 + w_{\text{noise}}^2 = 1$.
  3. **Equation (9) vs. Equation (10) Dimensionality Consistency:** Equation (9) defines the NAM $Q_{n,m}$ as an $N \text{ (samples)} \times M \text{ (neighborhoods/cells)}$ matrix. Clarify that CellPhenoX uses the $M \times k$ right singular vectors / cell loadings (rather than the $N \times k$ sample scores) as the cell-level feature matrix $X_i$ of length $M$.
  4. **Cross-Reference Typo (SHAP Value Calculation):** Change *"In Equation (7), all possible subsets of features..."* to *"In Equation (13), all possible subsets of features..."*.
  5. **Duplicate Paragraphs (Experimental Section):** Remove the duplicated text blocks in *Datasets—Simulation Overview* (items 1 and 2 repeated verbatim), *Datasets—Simulation Methodology* (items 1 and 2 repeated verbatim), and *Datasets—Real Datasets* (items 1, 2, and 3 repeated verbatim).
