## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This environmental and systems microbiology manuscript introduces `MATRIX` (Microbial Activity and Total cell quantification via Rapid Imaging and eXtraction), a standardized workflow combining two-step Nycodenz density-gradient soil cell extraction, independent fluorescence staining with SYBR Green I (total DNA-containing cells) and RedoxSensor Green (RSG; reductase-active cells), automated fluorescence microscopy (Logos QUANTOM Tx), custom watershed image segmentation (`process_quantom`), and Bayesian hierarchical modeling (`brms`/`RStan`). The authors validate `MATRIX` across batch growth curves of *E. coli* and *Pseudomonas protegens*, 18 switchgrass rhizosphere isolates at fixed $\text{OD}_{600} = 0.1$, and four French soils subjected to lysozyme treatment, bulk $\text{CO}_2$ micro-GC respiration profiling, and simulated diurnal heatwaves ($35^\circ\text{C}/45^\circ\text{C}$).

### Primary Claims Audit
1. **Claim 1:** Optical density ($\text{OD}_{600}$) scales nonlinearly with total cell counts ($\text{OD} = \text{OD}_{\text{blank}} + p_1 N^{p_2}$, with $p_2 \approx 0.30$ for *E. coli* and $0.55$ for *P. protegens*) and fails to capture divergent active-cell trajectories (e.g., *P. protegens* dropping below 25% RSG-active cells in stationary phase while *E. coli* remains near 100%) or 10-fold cell-concentration variations across 18 rhizosphere isolates at $\text{OD}_{600} = 0.1$. `[Well Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Triplicate batch growth time-courses and 18-isolate standardized $\text{OD}_{600} = 0.1$ comparisons analyzed via joint negative-binomial Bayesian hierarchical models, alongside log-cell-volume vs. specific growth rate regression confirming Shaechter's growth law (slope $= 19.0\ [16.6, 21.9]$).
2. **Claim 2:** Two-step Nycodenz extraction recovers $75\% \pm 18\%$ of spiked GFP-tagged *Azospirillum baldaniorum* cells from soil, and pairing `MATRIX` with lysozyme digestion partitions soil bacteria into four phenotypic pools (resistant-inactive, resistant-active, sensitive-active, sensitive-inactive), revealing that 45–53% of soil cells are both lysozyme-resistant and redox-active (RA). `[Moderately Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Well-executed Bayesian partitioning of paired untreated/lysozyme-treated subsamples across three soils ($n=4$ replicates each). However, recovery efficiency was only measured using a single laboratory-grown Gram-negative alpha-proteobacterium (*A. baldaniorum*) freshly spiked into one sandy-loam soil (`Galibier`), which overestimates detachment efficiency compared to indigenous biofilm-embedded, spore-forming, or actinomycete soil bacteria.
3. **Claim 3:** Total RSG fluorescence per gram of dry soil positively predicts both bulk soil respiration rate ($k$, slope $= 0.062\ [0.029, 0.094]$ on log-log scale) and initial labile carbon substrate pool ($C_0$, slope $= 0.125\ [0.062, 0.192]$) across three soils, two seasons, and three acclimation timepoints ($N = 48$ samples). `[Moderately Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Micro-GC $\text{CO}_2$ evolution kinetics paired with `MATRIX` RSG summation (Figure 5). However, the linear regression across all 18 group posterior medians (3 soils $\times$ 2 seasons $\times$ 3 timepoints) pools distinct soil types without soil-level random intercepts (Simpson's paradox / ecological fallacy risk across sites differing 4.5-fold in organic matter, Table 2) and yields a very shallow allometric exponent ($k \propto \text{RSG}^{0.062}$).
4. **Claim 4:** Simulated 72-hour diurnal heatwaves ($35^\circ\text{C}/45^\circ\text{C}$) increase the proportion of redox-active cells in both alpine (`Galibier`) and temperate (`Miribel 7`) soils while leaving per-cell RSG fluorescence intensity distributions unchanged. `[Moderately Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Six replicate mesocosms per soil/treatment. Note, however, that for `Galibier` total cell counts, the reported 95% credible interval of the fold-change ($1.9\times\ [0.7\times, 5.0\times]$, line 233) spans $1.0\times$, meaning the total cell increase is statistically uncertain, and residual post-mortem reductase activity (discussed by the authors as lasting up to 3h after heat killing) may contribute to RSG counts immediately after a $45^\circ\text{C}$ cycle.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Unconstrained Ratio / Over-Unity Risk in Unpaired SYBR and RSG Subsample Modeling
Because SYBR Green I and RedoxSensor Green share overlapping excitation/emission spectra ($470/30\text{ nm}$ excitation, $530/50\text{ nm}$ emission on the single-channel QUANTOM Tx counter), `MATRIX` cannot co-stain the same cells with both dyes. Instead, total cells ($Y_{\text{SYBR}}$) and active cells ($Y_{\text{RSG}}$) are counted in **two separate physical aliquots** drawn from each biological replicate.
In Results line 185, the authors describe their Bayesian model as:
$$Y_{\text{SYBR}} \sim \text{NegBinomial}(\mu_{\text{total}}, \phi), \qquad Y_{\text{RSG}} \sim \text{NegBinomial}(\mu_{\text{total}} \cdot p_{\text{active}}, \phi)$$
While constraining $p_{\text{active}} \in (0, 1)$ via a logit link in the Bayesian hierarchy is a major improvement over naive ratio division, two technical issues arise:
1. In several samples (e.g., *E. coli* at 2–8h in Figure 1A, and isolate `PvR045` / `PvR101` in Figure 3A), the raw observed RSG count $Y_{\text{RSG}}$ is equal to or slightly exceeds $Y_{\text{SYBR}}$. This happens not only from Poisson/negative-binomial sampling noise, but also because SYBR and RSG have different quantum yields and background characteristics under the fixed $6\sigma$ background-flattened intensity threshold (`process_quantom`, line 71): weakly stained high-GC or thick-walled Gram-positive cells may fall below $6\sigma$ in SYBR while bright localized formazan/RSG droplets exceed $6\sigma$, or vice versa.
2. Similarly, in the lysozyme experiment (Figure 4B), the four latent proportions ($\text{RI}, \text{RA}, \text{SA}, \text{SI}$ summing to $1$) are inferred from 4 separate counts ($Y_{\text{SYBR,pre}}, Y_{\text{RSG,pre}}, Y_{\text{SYBR,post}}, Y_{\text{RSG,post}}$)—an underdetermined system ($4$ equations for $1$ total scale parameter + $3$ independent simplex parameters) that assumes **lysozyme treatment neither activates nor quenches RSG reductase activity in resistant cells**. Yet incubating soil bacteria for 100 minutes at $37^\circ\text{C}$ in $50\text{ mM}$ Tris-HCl + $10\text{ mM}$ EDTA + $1\%$ Triton X-100 + $1\text{ mg/mL}$ lysozyme (when the original soil was at $4\text{--}7^\circ\text{C}$!) is a massive thermal and detergent shock that can directly alter membrane permeability to RSG and metabolic reductase rates in surviving cells.

### 2.2 Errors-in-Variables and Ecological Confounding in the Soil Respiration Regression (Figure 5)
In Methods line 159 and Figure 5, the authors fit a two-stage model: first, they estimate posterior medians for bulk respiration rate $k$ and initial substrate $C_0$ from $\text{CO}_2(t) \sim C_0(1 - e^{-k(t+d)})$ and posterior medians for total RSG fluorescence from a log-normal GLM; second, they fit a single ordinary linear regression on the log-log scale across the 18 group estimates across all 3 soils (`St Andre`, `Miribel 8`, `Miribel 7`), 2 seasons, and 3 acclimation times.
1. **Structural Identifiability of $C_0$ and $k$ over 7 Hours:** In a 7-hour soil incubation at $25^\circ\text{C}$, basal soil respiration is nearly linear ($\text{CO}_2(t) \approx C_0 k (t + d)$ when $kt \ll 1$). Fitting both the asymptote $C_0$ and the rate constant $k$ from a 7-hour window suffers from severe parameter collinearity ($C_0$ and $k$ trade off hyperbolically, where the actual well-determined quantity is the initial respiration flux $R_0 = C_0 k$).
2. **Omission of Soil Site Random Effects:** `Miribel 7` (forest clay-loam, $39.0\text{ g/kg}$ organic matter) has $4.5\times$ more organic matter than `Miribel 8` (grassland sandy-loam, $8.6\text{ g/kg}$). Fitting a single pooled regression line across all three soils without random intercepts for `Soil_site` conflates between-soil physicochemical differences with within-soil microbial activity dynamics. Moreover, a log-log slope of $0.062$ means that a **1,000-fold ($10^3$) increase in total RSG fluorescence corresponds to only a $10^{0.186} = 1.53$-fold increase in respiration rate $k$**, indicating very weak predictive scaling for $k$ itself (whereas initial flux $R_0 = C_0 k$ scales with exponent $0.062 + 0.125 = 0.187$).

### 2.3 Conversion Factor Discrepancy and Rotational Volume Assumption
1. **Inconsistent Slide Volume Conversion Factor:** On line 78 (Culture-based assays), the QUANTOM Tx conversion factor is stated as **$14,342\text{ cells/mL}$**, whereas on line 117 (Soil assays), the exact same QUANTOM Tx conversion factor is stated as **$14,319$**. While a 0.16% difference is numerically negligible, the discrepancy should be reconciled.
2. **Diffraction-Limited Volume Estimation:** In line 73, single-cell volume is estimated from the thresholded fluorescence mask (`> 6 SD` above background) assuming rotational symmetry. Because fluorescence object masks bloom with dye brightness (point-spread function convolution at $\sim 530\text{ nm}$ emission), brighter SYBR-stained cells appear larger on the sensor ("fluorescence blooming"), and the $6\sigma$ threshold depends on background noise variance in soil extracts vs. clean media.

1. **Duplicate SRA Accession in Table 1:**
   - Table 1, row `PvR045` (*Novosphingobium lindaniclasticum*, line 99): The SRA accession `SRR27348823` is printed twice (`SRR27348823 SRR27348823`).
2. **Missing 16-Hour OD Measurement:**
   - Line 78 transparently notes that the 16-h $\text{OD}_{600}$ samples are missing due to user error; this is good scientific transparency.
3. **Eukaryotic (Fungal/Protozoan) Contribution to Bulk Soil $\text{CO}_2$ Respiration:**
   - Bulk soil $\text{CO}_2$ respiration (Figure 5) includes respiration from fungi, archaea, and soil microfauna, whereas Nycodenz extraction + `MATRIX` specifically targets bacteria. Discussing fungal contributions to bulk soil respiration (which can exceed 50% in forest soils like `Miribel 7`) is important context for Figure 5.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Report Initial Respiration Flux $R_0 = C_0 k$ and Include Soil-Site Random Effects in Figure 5:**
   - Because $C_0$ and $k$ are weakly identifiable from a 7-hour $\text{CO}_2$ trace when $kt \ll 1$, compute the posterior distribution of the initial respiration flux $R_0 = \left.\frac{d\text{CO}_2}{dt}\right|_{t=0} = C_0 k$ ($\mu\text{g CO}_2\text{ g}^{-1}\text{ dry soil h}^{-1}$) and fit a joint Bayesian hierarchical errors-in-variables model with random intercepts/slopes by `Soil_site` to test whether RSG fluorescence predicts respiration *within* as well as *between* soil types.
2. **Control for $37^\circ\text{C}$ / Triton X-100 / EDTA Buffer Effects in the Lysozyme Assay:**
   - Include (or discuss the need for) a buffer+temperature mock control (incubating extracted soil cells for 100 min at $37^\circ\text{C}$ in Tris/EDTA/Triton X-100 *without* lysozyme, or in TSP buffer at $37^\circ\text{C}$) to verify that the $37^\circ\text{C}$ incubation and $1\%$ Triton X-100 do not artificially alter RSG permeability or reductase activity compared to the unincubated control.
3. **Evaluate PSF Blooming in Fluorescence-Based Cell Volume Estimates:**
   - Quantify the correlation between per-cell SYBR mean fluorescence intensity and estimated cell volume (or compare brightfield vs. SYBR mask dimensions on the pure-culture isolates) to verify that the cell-volume vs. growth-rate relationship (Figure 2D) is not confounded by higher DNA/SYBR brightness during rapid replication.
4. **Fix Minor Text/Table Discrepancies:**
   - Reconcile the $14,342$ vs. $14,319$ conversion factor (lines 78 and 117) and remove the duplicate `SRR27348823` string in Table 1.
