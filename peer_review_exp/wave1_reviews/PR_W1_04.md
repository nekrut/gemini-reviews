## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript conducts a retrospective comparative genomic analysis of 596 complete Rift Valley fever virus (RVFV) segment sequences—comprising L ($n=173$), M ($n=196$), and S ($n=227$) segments—sampled from 13 African countries between 1944 and 2022 and retrieved from NCBI Virus. The authors identify 7,339 amino acid differences relative to reference genomes (`NC_014397.1`, `NC_014396.1`, `NC_014395.1`), compare per-isolate mutation counts across countries and sampling years using Kruskal-Wallis tests, reconstruct maximum-likelihood and time-scaled phylogenies (`IQ-TREE 2.3.5` and `TreeTime`), infer ancestral host transitions on a midpoint-rooted M-segment tree using an Equal Rates (`ER`) Markov model in `ape::ace()`, and test for codon-level positive selection in human vs. non-human subsets using `FUBAR`, `SLAC`, and `FEL` on Datamonkey. They report that seven recurrent amino acid substitutions—L: N277S, N277D, S278N; M: I442S, I442V, V659A; and S (NSs): N133S—are under host-specific positive selection and drive polymerase optimization and immune escape.

While assembling a continent-wide dataset of L, M, and S segments is a worthwhile starting point, the manuscript's primary evolutionary and adaptive claims rest on severe methodological errors. Most critically, in Section "Selection Pressure" (line 109), the authors misread the `FUBAR` output parameters ($\beta/\alpha$, i.e., $dN/dS$) for two of their headline "positively selected" sites—S segment codon 133 (`dN/dS = 0.736 / 4.657`, where $dN = 0.736 < dS = 4.657$, i.e., $\omega = 0.158$) and M segment codon 659 (`dN/dS = 0.698 / 5.374`, where $dN = 0.698 < dS = 5.374$, i.e., $\omega = 0.130$)—reporting **purifying (negative) selection** posterior probabilities ($\Pr(\alpha > \beta) \ge 0.90$) as **positive (diversifying) selection**. Furthermore, measuring "mutation burden" as raw Hamming distance to a single fixed reference strain (`ZH-501`, Egypt 1977) hopelessly confounds evolutionary adaptation with phylogenetic patristic distance from Lineage A, and applying a symmetric Equal Rates (`ER`) ancestral state model to a midpoint-rooted tree of heavily human-biased outbreak sequences violates the assumptions of phylogeographic/host-jump reconstruction.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1: Codon 133 (N133S) of the NSs protein in human isolates and Codon 659 (V659A) of the M segment in non-human isolates are under diversifying (positive) selection.** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  Line 109 explicitly states: *"in the S segment, at codon 133 (N133S, NSs protein) was identified under moderate positive selection (posterior = 0.9038; dN/dS = 0.736/4.657)"* and *"Codon at site 659 (V659A) in the M segment and was supported by FUBAR (posterior = 0.9367 with dN/dS = 0.698/5.374)."* In `FUBAR`, $\beta$ ($dN$) is the nonsynonymous rate and $\alpha$ ($dS$) is the synonymous rate. For S codon 133, $\beta = 0.736$ and $\alpha = 4.657$ ($\beta / \alpha = 0.158 \ll 1$); for M codon 659, $\beta = 0.698$ and $\alpha = 5.374$ ($\beta / \alpha = 0.130 \ll 1$). Both sites are under strong **purifying selection** ($\alpha > \beta$), and the authors read the FUBAR column for $\Pr(\alpha > \beta)$ (negative selection) as positive selection.
* **Claim 2: S segment mutation N133S was positively selected specifically in human-derived sequences.** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  In addition to the inverted $\beta/\alpha$ ratio ($0.736/4.657$) above, line 102 contradicts itself within two consecutive sentences: *"For the S segment, the mutation N133S at codon 133 was detected in livestock and vector isolates sampled from Kenya, South Africa and Zimbabwe between the 1960s–2020s. Positive selection at this codon was restricted to human-derived sequences (Table 2)."* If N133S occurred in livestock and vector isolates, claiming human-specific positive selection based on a synonymous substitution excess in human sequences is both biologically and statistically invalid.
* **Claim 3: Egyptian isolates have a low "mutation burden" (mean = 2.4 mutations/isolate), whereas South African, Mauritanian, and Kenyan isolates underwent intense episodes of protein-coding diversification.** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  Mutations were called by aligning all sequences to the NCBI RefSeq reference genome (`NC_014395.1`–`NC_014397.1`), which is the **1977 Egyptian ZH-501 strain (Lineage A)**. Counting amino acid differences relative to a 1977 Egyptian reference guarantees by definition that 1970s Egyptian Lineage A isolates have ~2 differences, while distantly related Southern and West African lineages (Lineages C, H, K, L) have 12–18 fixed lineage-defining differences relative to Lineage A. Treating static phylogenetic distance from an arbitrarily chosen reference genome as "outbreak mutation accumulation" is a fundamental phylogenetic fallacy.
* **Claim 4: Ancestral host-state reconstruction proves 22 livestock-to-human spillover transitions, 3 vector-to-human transitions, and 0 wildlife-to-human transitions.** `[Fragile]`  
  The authors used `ape::ace()` with an Equal Rates (`ER`) model on a midpoint-rooted tree (lines 56–58). An `ER` model forces the rate of `Human -> Livestock` and `Human -> Mosquito Vector` transmission to equal the rate of `Mosquito/Livestock -> Human` transmission, which is epidemiologically implausible because humans are predominantly dead-end hosts for RVFV. Furthermore, deterministic maximum-likelihood node assignment (`ace`) without stochastic character mapping (`make.simmap`) or Tip-State Randomization is highly sensitive to outbreak sampling density (e.g., 321 of 596 sequences come from South Africa 2008–2011).

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

1. **Inversion of Purifying vs. Diversifying Selection ($dN < dS$) in FUBAR Analysis:**  
   In Section "Selection Pressure" (line 109), the authors report `dN/dS (β/α)` as a slash-separated pair of rates (`β/α`). For L codons 277 and 278 and M codon 442, the first number exceeds the second (`14.657/0.793`, `9.771/0.828`, `7.372/0.758`), whereas for S codon 133 (`0.736/4.657`) and M codon 659 (`0.698/5.374`), the synonymous rate is $6.3\times$ to $7.7\times$ higher than the nonsynonymous rate. Furthermore, neither `FEL` nor `SLAC` detected any positively selected sites across the entire dataset (`p <= 0.1`, line 109). Moreover, splitting a single viral phylogeny into separate FASTA files by tip host ("human" vs. "non-human", line 65) and running `FUBAR` on the pruned sub-trees does not test for host-specific selection on terminal human branches: internal branches of the "human-only" tree still represent decades of ancestral evolution in livestock and mosquito reservoirs! Testing host-specific selection requires a branch-site or contrast model on the unified tree (e.g., `Contrast-FEL` or `MEME` / `aBSREL` with human tips/clades labeled as foreground).
2. **Confounding Reference-Strain Bias with Evolutionary Diversification Rate:**  
   In Sections "Identification of protein-coding mutations" (lines 37–41) and Results (lines 78–89), all amino acid "mutations" are counted relative to the NCBI RefSeq reference (`ZH-501`, isolated in Egypt in 1977, Lineage A). Because all non-A lineages share deep ancestral branches separating them from Lineage A, counting differences from `ZH-501` measures patristic divergence from 1977 Egypt, not within-outbreak mutation accumulation. Similarly, the "stepwise year-to-year changes in mean protein-coding mutations" (line 80, Fig. 1b)—such as a "+11.3 mutation increase and -14.3 mutation decrease" in South Africa—simply reflect sampling different co-circulating RVFV lineages (e.g., Lineage C vs. Lineage H vs. Lineage K) in different years, not rapid forward and backward evolution of the same viral population.
3. **Segment Count vs. Isolate Count Confusion ($596$ Sequences $\neq 596$ Complete Genomes):**  
   The Abstract (line 9), Methods (line 32), and Discussion (line 116) repeatedly refer to *"596 complete genomes of the three RVFV segments... including L (n = 173), M (n = 196) and S (n = 227)"* and *"ranging from 0 to >20 mutations per isolate among the 596 RVFV isolates analyzed"* (line 78). Because RVFV is a tripartite virus requiring L + M + S segments to form one complete viral genome, $173 + 196 + 227 = 596$ is the total number of **individual segment sequences**, representing at most 173 complete three-segment viral genomes. In lines 87–88, the country sequence counts (`South Africa n = 321`, `Kenya n = 136`, `Madagascar n = 33`, `Zimbabwe n = 27`, `Egypt n = 16`, `Mauritania n = 11`, `Namibia n = 7`, `Senegal n = 5`, `Angola n = 3`) Pool L, M, and S segments together as if they were independent "isolates," even though an L segment (~6.4 kb) is nearly $4\times$ longer than an S segment (~1.7 kb) and naturally carries more absolute mutations! Comparing raw mutation counts per sequence across countries and hosts without normalizing by segment length (mutations per kilobase or per codon) or stratifying by segment invalidates the Kruskal-Wallis comparisons (line 41).
4. **Invalid Root and Transition Model in Ancestral Host Reconstruction:**  
   In lines 51–56, the authors generate a time-scaled tree using `TreeTime` (which inherently roots the tree by root-to-tip regression) using two different versions in the same paragraph (`TreeTime v0.11.4` in line 51 and `TreeTime v0.9.4` in line 51), but then **re-root the time-scaled tree at the midpoint** (`The tree was rooted midpoint`, line 56) before running `ape::ace()` with an Equal Rates (`ER`) model. Midpoint rooting destroys the temporal root of a heterochronous viral tree spanning 1944–2022, and an `ER` model is biologically inappropriate for asymmetric vector/reservoir-to-dead-end-host spillover.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

* **GARD Recombination Reporting:** Line 46 states that *"The amino acid (codon) alignment... was uploaded into the Datamonkey web-based server and screened for recombination breakpoints using GARD... ($\Delta\text{AIC-c} \approx 0$)."* `GARD` operates on nucleotide alignments (not amino acid alignments), and because RVFV is a segmented virus, reassortment between L, M, and S segments—the dominant mode of genetic exchange in bunyaviruses—cannot be detected by running intra-segment `GARD` on individual segments.
* **Contradictory Mutation Range in Abstract vs. Results:** The Abstract (line 11) states "ranging from 2–20 per isolate," whereas the Results (line 78) state "ranging from 0 to >20 mutations per isolate (range = 8–31 in South Africa)."
* **Missing Data and Code Availability:** No GitHub repository, custom Python/R scripts (referenced in lines 9, 37, 41), alignment files, or NCBI accession list of the 596 sequences are provided.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Correct the Codon Selection Analysis Using `Contrast-FEL` on the Unified Segment Phylogenies:**
   * **WHY:** S codon 133 ($\beta/\alpha = 0.736/4.657$) and M codon 659 ($\beta/\alpha = 0.698/5.374$) are under purifying selection, not positive selection, and running `FUBAR` on pruned "human-only" trees conflates ancestral reservoir branches with human evolution.
   * **WHAT:** Remove S codon 133 and M codon 659 from the list of positively selected sites, and re-run selection analyses on the full L ($n=173$), M ($n=196$), and S ($n=227$) trees using `Contrast-FEL` and `MEME` in HyPhy.
   * **HOW:** Label terminal branches leading to human isolates vs. livestock/vector isolates on the unified segment phylogenies and run `hyphy contrast-fel` to formally test whether $\omega_{\text{human}}$ differs significantly from $\omega_{\text{non-human}}$ at any site, reporting exact $q$-values and verified $\beta$ and $\alpha$ estimates.
2. **Replace Reference-Biased Mutation Counts with Patristic / Ancestral Branch Substitutions Normalized by Segment Length:**
   * **WHY:** Counting amino acid differences relative to the 1977 Egyptian `ZH-501` reference strain measures phylogenetic distance from Lineage A, and pooling L, M, and S segments confounds segment length (6.4 kb vs. 3.9 kb vs. 1.7 kb) with country diversity.
   * **WHAT:** Recompute amino acid substitutions relative to the reconstructed most recent common ancestor (MRCA) of each isolate's own lineage (or along terminal branches using `TreeTime` ancestral sequence reconstruction), and analyze L, M, and S segments separately (expressed as substitutions per 1,000 codons).
   * **HOW:** Extract branch-specific amino acid substitutions from the `TreeTime` ancestral state reconstruction for L, M, and S independently, and replace Figs. 1–3 with lineage-calibrated evolutionary rates and within-lineage nucleotide diversity ($\pi$).
3. **Use an Asymmetric Discrete-Trait Model on the Clock-Rooted Phylogeny with Subsampling Sensitivity:**
   * **WHY:** Midpoint rooting invalidates the temporal directionality of a 1944–2022 viral tree, and an Equal Rates (`ER`) model forces unrealistic human-to-vector/livestock back-transmission.
   * **WHAT:** Retain the clock-rooted `TreeTime` topology and fit an All-Rates-Different (`ARD`) or epidemiologically constrained transition model (or `TreeTime mugration`), accompanied by geographic/host subsampling to control for the heavy overrepresentation of South Africa ($n=321/596$).
   * **HOW:** Run 100 replicates of stochastic character mapping (`phytools::make.simmap` with `model="ARD"`) on the clock-rooted M, L, and S trees and compare observed transition counts against a tip-label randomization null distribution.
4. **Provide Complete Accession Lists and Analysis Scripts:**
   * **WHY:** Reproducibility requires knowing the exact 596 accessions and custom mutation-extraction scripts used.
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Deposit the metadata table of all 596 NCBI accessions (with strain linkages across L, M, and S segments), alignments, Newick trees, and Python/R scripts in a public Zenodo or GitHub repository.
