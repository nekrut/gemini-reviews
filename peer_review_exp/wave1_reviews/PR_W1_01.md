## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript applies the MIME-seq 2.0 methodology—which uses a chimeric HENMT1 methyltransferase fused to an Argonaute-binding T6B peptide (`HENMT1ΔC-T6B`) to 2'-O-methylate the 3' terminal ribose of Argonaute-associated small RNAs and protect them from sodium periodate ($\text{NaIO}_4$) oxidation—to mouse insulinoma (MIN6B1), human Jurkat T, and mouse C2C12 myotube cell lines. Beyond sequencing, the authors introduce an LNA-based RT-qPCR assay (miRCURY LNA RT Kit) that exploits the 3' poly(A) tailing/ligation requirement to quantify the percentage of oxidation-resistant RNA (`Remaining miRNA = 100 / 2^{\Delta Ct}`). They report that MIME protects canonical miRNAs (~25% recovery vs. <0.1% in wild-type cells) as well as specific Y-RNA fragments (Y1, Y3) and tRNA-derived fragments (tRF Val-CAC, Asp-GTC), which they validate by Flag-Ago2 RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP). Finally, they apply MIME-seq/qPCR to extracellular vesicle (EV) co-culture experiments (Jurkat $\rightarrow$ MIN6B1 and C2C12 $\rightarrow$ MIN6B1) to track intercellular miRNA transfer. Raw sequencing data are deposited under GEO accession GSE313744.

While the adaptation of periodate oxidation to an LNA RT-qPCR readout and the cross-species human-to-mouse EV transfer control (`hsa-miR-155-5p` vs. endogenous `mmu-miR-155-5p`) are useful experimental designs, the manuscript lacks a formal statistical section, omits replicate counts ($n$), test statistics, and $p$-values across the main text, relies on unnormalized $\Delta Ct$ ratios that conflate chemical recovery loss with enzymatic protection, and uses uncalibrated EV dosing ($0.5\text{ to }5 \times 10^{10}$ particles/mL—a 10-fold variation across replicates) without RNase/detergent or non-vesicular supernatant controls in the main text.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1: LNA RT-qPCR following $\text{NaIO}_4$ oxidation distinguishes MIME-methylated miRNAs from unmethylated RNAs in MIN6B1 cells.** `[Well Supported]`  
  In MIN6B1 cells expressing `HENMT1ΔC-T6B`, let-7b-5p and miR-7-5p recovery increases from <0.1% ($>10\ \Delta Ct$ loss upon oxidation in wild-type cells) to ~25% ($2\ \Delta Ct$ difference), whereas the non-Ago-binding snRNA U6 remains at ~1% recovery regardless of MIME expression.
* **Claim 2: Y-RNA fragments (Y1, Y3) and tRFs (Val-CAC, Asp-GTC) are methylated by MIME and physically associate with Ago2.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  Small RNA-seq and RT-qPCR show oxidation protection of Y1, Y3, and tRF Val-CAC (rising from 1.9% to 6.0% with MIME), and Flag-Ago2 RIP confirms enrichment over control IP (e.g., Y1 recovery of 1.9% vs. 0.004% in control). However, both `HENMT1ΔC-T6B` and `Flag-Ago2` are transiently overexpressed, which can drive mass-action loading of abundant housekeeping RNA degradation fragments onto exogenous Ago2 or non-specific proximity methylation by unbound `HENMT1ΔC-T6B`.
* **Claim 3: MIME-seq 2.0 quantitatively tracks EV-mediated transfer of functional miRNAs from Jurkat T cells and C2C12 myotubes into recipient MIN6B1 cells.** `[Fragile]`  
  The qualitative detection of oxidation-resistant `hsa-miR-155-5p`, `miR-142-3p/5p`, `miR-1-3p`, and `miR-133b` in MIN6B1 extracts after 24 h incubation with EVs from MIME-expressing donor cells is consistent with transfer. However, two PBS washes of intact cells do not remove surface-adsorbed or endocytosed/unreleased EVs, no RNase/proteinase protection or recipient Ago-IP is performed to prove cytoplasmic delivery, and the 10-fold variance in EV input concentration ($0.5\text{–}5 \times 10^{10}$ particles/mL) prevents quantitative interpretation.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

1. **Complete Absence of Statistical Framework, Sample Sizes, and Multiple-Testing Control:**  
   The Materials and Methods contain no "Statistical Analysis" section. Throughout the Results (lines 80–95), quantitative claims of "significant recovery," "significant binding," and "upregulated" miRNAs are presented without stating the number of biological replicates ($n$), the statistical tests applied (e.g., paired vs. unpaired $t$-test, ANOVA, non-parametric tests), degrees of freedom, or exact $p$-values. Similarly, the small RNA-seq pipeline (lines 68–70) delegates analysis to the "Qiagen RNA-seq Analysis Portal" and an external "Bioinformatics Competence Center" without specifying read depth, adapter trimming, mapping tolerances, normalization method (e.g., spike-ins vs. TMM/DESeq2 size factors, which are violated when global miRNA pools collapse by >99% in oxidized WT libraries), or false discovery rate (FDR) thresholds.
2. **Unnormalized $\Delta Ct$ Recovery Formula Conflates Precipitation Loss with Oxidation Sensitivity:**  
   In the RT-qPCR section (line 75), recovery is computed strictly as $\text{Remaining miRNA} = 100 / (2^{\Delta Ct})$ comparing the $\text{NaIO}_4$-oxidized sample to the water-treated control using equal elution volumes, without an exogenous 3'-2'-O-methylated synthetic spike-in RNA added prior to the $\text{NaIO}_4$ reaction and ethanol/glycogen precipitation step (line 65). Any variance in ethanol precipitation efficiency or pellet resuspension between the $\text{NaIO}_4$ (high salt) and water reactions directly biases $\Delta Ct$. Furthermore, in the EV transfer experiments (Figs. 4C–D, 5A–B), the manuscript does not specify what endogenous or exogenous reference gene was used to normalize relative expression before and after oxidation when endogenous RNAs (including U6 and `mmu-miR-155-5p`) are destroyed by $\text{NaIO}_4$.
3. **Lack of Controls for Surface-Bound EVs and Non-Ago Proximity Methylation:**  
   * *EV internalization vs. surface adsorption:* Recipient MIN6B1 cells were incubated with high doses of ultracentrifuged EVs ($0.5\text{ to }5 \times 10^{10}$ particles/mL) for 24 h and washed twice with PBS before Qiazol lysis (line 55). Standard PBS washes do not strip lipid vesicles bound to the plasma membrane or trapped in the extracellular matrix; thus, the qPCR signal measures total cell-associated EV cargo rather than proved cytosolic delivery into recipient cells.
   * *Overexpression artifacts and T6B-mutant control:* The RIP validation (line 38) relies on transient overexpression of `Flag-Ago2` rather than immunoprecipitation of endogenous Ago1–4, and the MIME experiments lack a catalytic-dead or T6B-mutant `HENMT1ΔC` control in MIN6B1 cells to rule out Ago-independent background methylation of abundant tRFs (where protection only rose from 1.9% to 6.0%, line 86).
4. **Severe Compositional Bias in Oxidized Small RNA-Seq Libraries:**  
   In wild-type oxidized samples, unmethylated miRNAs are depleted by >99.9%, causing naturally 3'-methylated species (such as endogenous piRNAs or potentially 2'-O-methylated tRNA fragments) and ligation background to dominate the fixed sequencing library depth. Comparing raw or standard library-size-normalized read counts between unoxidized and oxidized libraries without synthetic methylated spike-in calibrators introduces severe compositional distortion into the PCA (Fig. 2A) and differential recovery estimates (Supplementary Tables 2–3).

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

* **10-Fold Variation in EV Dosing and Missing EV Characterization:** Line 55 states that EV concentration ranged from $0.5\text{ to }5 \times 10^{10}$ particles/mL across experimental replicates, and particle concentration was assessed "using a ZetaSizer" (line 50). Dynamic light scattering (ZetaSizer) measures hydrodynamic size distributions and polydispersity, not absolute particle concentration (which requires Nanoparticle Tracking Analysis or tunable resistive pulse sensing), and MISEV guidelines require positive/negative EV protein markers (e.g., CD63, CD81, TSG101, Calnexin) and RNase protection assays.
* **Polybrene Concentration Typo:** Line 33 reports infecting C2C12 and Jurkat cells in the presence of "Polybrene ($8\ \mu\text{g}/\mu\text{L}$)," which is $8\text{ mg/mL}$ ($1000\times$ cytotoxic concentration); the standard concentration is $8\ \mu\text{g/mL}$.
* **Antibiotic Concentration Typo:** Lines 28 and 48 state that all media were supplemented with "$50\text{ mg/mL}$ streptomycin," which is $1000\times$ higher than the standard $50\ \mu\text{g/mL}$ working concentration.
* **Supernatant (SN) Control Mentioned in Methods but Omitted from Results:** Line 55 states that MIN6 cells were treated with "$100\ \mu\text{L}$ of PBS, SN [ultracentrifugation supernatant], or EV preparation," yet the Results (lines 90–95) never report the SN control data to demonstrate that transfer is strictly EV-mediated rather than carried by free ribonucleoprotein complexes.
* **Incomplete Reference:** Line 28 cites "(Rebeaud et al., 2008)" inline by author-year, whereas the rest of the manuscript uses numbered bracketed citations `[1–12]`.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Add a Formal Statistical Analysis Section and Report Exact Replicates and Test Statistics:**
   * **WHY:** Readers cannot evaluate the reliability or false-positive rate of the qPCR, RIP, and RNA-seq claims without sample sizes and variance metrics.
   * **WHAT:** Add a dedicated Statistical Analysis subsection in Materials and Methods and report exact $n$ (independent biological replicates), mean $\pm$ SD, statistical tests, and exact $p$-values/FDR in the text and figure legends for Figs. 1–5.
   * **HOW:** Specify the exact statistical models used for all RT-qPCR and RIP comparisons (e.g., two-way ANOVA or paired $t$-tests on $\Delta Ct$ values with Benjamini-Hochberg correction) and document the exact bioinformatics pipeline, read filtering, and normalization method used to generate Supplementary Tables 2 and 3.
2. **Clarify RT-qPCR Normalization and Control for Compositional/Precipitation Bias:**
   * **WHY:** Global RNA oxidation destroys endogenous reference RNAs (such as U6), making standard $\Delta\Delta Ct$ normalization across conditions invalid without an oxidation-resistant internal standard.
   * **WHAT:** Explicitly state the mathematical normalization procedure used for Figs. 4C–D and 5A–B, and quantify recovery variance across replicates.
   * **HOW:** Detail in the Methods whether expression in recipient cells was normalized to an exogenous synthetic 2'-O-methylated spike-in oligo, total input RNA mass, or an unoxidized split aliquot prior to $\text{NaIO}_4$ treatment. For the RNA-seq dataset, normalize oxidized vs. unoxidized read counts against the invariant endogenous methylated piRNA pool (`mmu-piR-67265`, `mmu-piR-68892`) to verify that tRF and Y-RNA enrichment is not a library-size compositional artifact.
3. **Report the Ultracentrifugation Supernatant (SN) Control and Qualify EV Internalization Claims:**
   * **WHY:** PBS washes alone do not distinguish plasma-membrane-adsorbed vesicles from functionally internalized cytoplasmic miRNAs, and the 10-fold variation in EV dose ($0.5\text{–}5 \times 10^{10}$ particles/mL) confounds quantitative comparisons.
   * **WHAT:** Include the SN control data (referenced in line 55) in Figs. 4 and 5, explain how ZetaSizer measurements were converted to particle counts, and temper claims of functional intracellular delivery.
   * **HOW:** Plot the RT-qPCR recovery of recipient cells treated with the $100,000 \times g$ supernatant alongside PBS and EV pellets; if trypsin/proteinase K + RNase treatment of recipient cells or recipient Flag-Ago2 RIP was not performed, explicitly state in the Discussion that the assay detects cell-associated EV-transferred miRNAs without proving RISC loading inside the recipient cell.
4. **Correct Methodological Unit Errors:**
   * **WHY:** Reporting $50\text{ mg/mL}$ streptomycin and $8\ \mu\text{g}/\mu\text{L}$ Polybrene hinders experimental reproducibility.
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Correct "$50\text{ mg/mL}$ streptomycin" to "$50\ \mu\text{g/mL}$" (lines 28, 48), "$8\ \mu\text{g}/\mu\text{L}$" Polybrene to "$8\ \mu\text{g/mL}$" (line 33), and format "(Rebeaud et al., 2008)" (line 28) into the numbered bibliography.
