## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study investigates how stimulus-specific dynamics of the transcription factor p53 (pulsatile under ionizing radiation, low-dose UV, and low-dose etoposide; single-pulse under high-dose UV and high-dose etoposide; monotonic under 5-FU and Nutlin-3a) regulate transcriptional bursting of four canonical target genes (*MDM2*, *PPM1D*, *CDKN1A*, and *BAX*) in A549 cells. The authors combine live-cell imaging of p53-mVenus, dual-color smFISH to quantify total cellular mRNA and nascent transcripts at active transcription sites (TSs), and a Bayesian MCMC framework fitting a steady-state $K$-allele random telegraph model augmented with a phenomenological Gamma variance parameter ($\beta$) for nascent exon fluorescence. They report that target gene mRNA trajectories frequently decouple from bulk nuclear p53 dynamics and DNA binding (ChIP-qPCR), correlate more closely with p53 C-terminal acetylation (K382ac), and are predominantly modulated via the active promoter fraction $f = \lambda/(\lambda+\gamma)$.

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision / Mixed & Salvageable (5.5 / 10.0).** While the experimental dataset spanning seven genotoxic/pharmacological conditions and four target genes is substantial, the mathematical and statistical inference framework suffers from foundational specification errors that undermine the quantitative kinetic parameters ($\lambda, \gamma, \mu, \delta$):
1. **Steady-State Likelihood Applied to Non-Stationary Pulsatile Trajectories:** The first likelihood factor $L_{1,c}(\theta; k, m)$ uses the analytical Peccoud–Ycart steady-state solution independently at each time point ($1.5\text{ h}, 3\text{ h}, 6\text{ h}, 9\text{ h}$, etc.) during acute DNA damage responses where p53 levels and mRNA counts are rapidly changing. Because mRNA degradation timescales ($1/\delta$, typically hours) are comparable to the 2.5–3 h p53 pulse period, the mature mRNA count $m(t)$ is out of equilibrium with instantaneous promoter switching rates, biasing $\delta$ and $\mu/\delta$ into non-physical time-varying effective parameters.
2. **Improper Uniform Priors Over Unbounded Positive Space & Posterior Scale Unidentifiability:** The authors place an improper uniform prior on $(\gamma, \lambda, \mu, \delta, \beta) \in (0, \infty)^5$ while sampling in $\log$-parameter space without demonstrating posterior propriety, and rely exclusively on single-chain MCMC visual trace inspection without multi-chain convergence diagnostics ($\hat{R}$, ESS).
3. **Typographical & Primer Errors:** In the Methods section, the *CDKN1A* forward and reverse RT-qPCR primers are identical sequences (`TGGACCTGTCACTGTCTTGT`), and the closed-form equation for $q_\theta(m)$ contains an algebraic sign/factorial transcription error ($(m - b)$ in the numerator, which becomes negative for $m < b$).

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **p53 accumulation dynamics and total mRNA profiles of target genes are frequently uncoupled across genotoxic stimuli: [Well Supported].**  
  Live-cell imaging (Figure 1C–G), Western blots, and smFISH mature RNA counts (Figure 2D–J) convincingly show that 2-fold differences in p53 concentration (5 µM vs. 50 µM etoposide) yield nearly identical mRNA trajectories, and monotonic p53 accumulation under 5-FU produces minimal target induction.
* **Promoter regulation across stimuli is predominantly driven by promoter switching fraction $f = \lambda/(\lambda+\gamma)$ with secondary stimulus-specific modulation of $\mu$ and $\delta$: [Fragile].**  
  While the active promoter fraction $f$ is directly anchored by the binomial count of intron-positive TSs ($k$ out of $K$), decomposing rates into $\mu$ and time-varying mRNA degradation $\delta$ relies on fitting a steady-state telegraph model to non-stationary transient time points. The inferred drops or spikes in $\delta$ (e.g., Figure 3F–G) are classic artifacts of fitting an equilibrium distribution to an accumulating mRNA pool.
* **Bayesian MCMC framework resolves all five kinetic parameters ($\lambda, \gamma, \mu, \delta, \beta$) with improved identifiability: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Likelihood $L_1$ only identifies the dimensionless ratios $\lambda/\delta$, $\gamma/\delta$, and $\mu/\delta$. Absolute scale identification depends entirely on $L_2$ (nascent TS fluorescence) where elongation rate is fixed at $v = 50\text{ bp/s}$ (even under UV-C and 5-FU, which the authors themselves note stall Pol II elongation). Furthermore, single-chain MCMC under an improper uniform prior lacks formal convergence guarantees.
* **C-terminal acetylation of p53 (K382ac) explains the disconnect between total p53/DNA binding and transcriptional output: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Qualitative Western blots (Figure 4C) show higher K382ac under IR and Nutlin than under etoposide or 5-FU at 3 h, correlating with 3 h smFISH induction, but the evidence is correlative, unquantified across biological replicates, and not measured at target promoters via K382ac-specific ChIP.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Steady-State Telegraph Likelihood ($L_1$) Applied to Non-Stationary Time-Course Data (Pillar 3 — Critical)
* **Location:** Methods ("The Likelihood factors from Bayesian inference", Line 56) and Results (Lines 113–125).
* **Mathematical/Statistical Flaw:** For each post-treatment time point (e.g., 1.5 h, 3 h, 6 h), the authors fit $L_{1,c}(\theta; k, m) = P[X=k, Y=m \mid \Theta=\theta]$ derived from the Peccoud–Ycart **steady-state** generating function of $K$ independent telegraph processes, treating each time point as an independent equilibrium system. However, after acute genotoxic stress or Nutlin addition, cellular mRNA $Y(t)$ obeys the master equation $\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[Y(t)] = K \mu f(t) - \delta \mathbb{E}[Y(t)]$. During the rising phase of transcription, $\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[Y(t)] > 0$, meaning $\mathbb{E}[Y(t)] < \frac{K\mu f(t)}{\delta_{\text{true}}}$. When a steady-state model is forced onto transient data where $f(t)$ has already jumped (measured instantaneously via intron-positive active TSs $k$) but cytoplasmic mRNA $m(t)$ is still accumulating, the inference engine is forced to artificially inflate $\delta$ at early time points and decrease $\delta$ at later time points (exactly as seen in Figure 3F, where $\delta$ decreases at late time points after Nutlin treatment).
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Either (a) replace the steady-state likelihood $L_1$ with the time-dependent master equation solution integrating across the sampled time points with a fixed or independently measured mRNA decay rate $\delta$ (e.g., from Actinomycin D chase or SLAM-seq), or (b) refrain from interpreting time-point-specific variations in $\delta$ and $\mu$ as biological regulation of RNA degradation, explicitly demonstrating via synthetic non-stationary simulations how steady-state fitting distorts $\hat{\delta}(t)$.

### Defect 2: Algebraic Error in the Partial Probability Mass Function $q_\theta(m)$ (Pillar 7 — Major)
* **Location:** Methods, Line 56.
* **Mathematical Flaw:** The partial mass function for an inactive promoter ($X_n = 0, Y_n = m$) is written as:
  $$q_\theta(m) = \frac{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(m+a)(m-b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(m+b-1)} M\left(m+a, m+b-1, -\frac{\mu}{\delta}\right)\frac{\mu^m}{m!\delta^m}$$
  Because $b = (\lambda+\gamma)/\delta > 0$, for $m = 0$ (and any $m < b$), the factor $(m - b)$ is **strictly negative**, whereas $\Gamma(b), \Gamma(m+a), \Gamma(a)$, and $M(\cdot)$ are positive, yielding a negative probability $P[X_n=0, Y_n=0] < 0$! In the exact Peccoud–Ycart / Raj et al. derivation for the joint state of a two-state promoter, the inactive-state marginal is $P(Y_n=m) - P(X_n=1, Y_n=m)$, which simplifies via Kummer's contiguous relations to $\frac{\gamma}{\lambda+\gamma} \frac{\Gamma(b+1)\Gamma(m+a)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(m+b+1)} M(m+a, m+b+1, -\mu/\delta) \frac{(\mu/\delta)^m}{m!}$.
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Audit the codebase at `tudatalib-1704` against Line 56 of the text, correct the closed-form equation for $q_\theta(m)$ in the main text and Supplementary Methods, and verify that $\sum_{m=0}^\infty [p_\theta(m) + q_\theta(m)] = 1$ with $p_\theta(m) \ge 0, q_\theta(m) \ge 0$ for all $m \ge 0$.

### Defect 3: Constant Elongation Rate Assumption ($v = 50\text{ bp/s}$) Under Elongation-Stalling Treatments (Pillars 3 & 6 — Major)
* **Location:** Methods, Line 60; Results, Line 104; Discussion, Line 1.
* **Methodological Flaw:** Absolute time-scale calibration of $(\lambda, \gamma, \mu, \delta)$ relies entirely on $L_2$, which simulates polymerase occupancy on the gene assuming a fixed elongation rate hyperparameter $v_{\text{elong}} = 50\text{ bp/s}$ across all conditions. Yet under UV-C irradiation, bulky cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers physically stall Pol II elongation (which the authors confirm via EU incorporation in Supplementary Figure 4 and acknowledge in the Discussion). If $v_{\text{elong}}$ drops by 2–5 fold under UV, nascent residence time $\tau_{\text{elong}} = L_{\text{gene}}/v_{\text{elong}}$ increases proportionally, making $\mu$ and $v_{\text{elong}}$ completely confounded in $L_2$.
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Perform a sensitivity analysis varying $v_{\text{elong}} \in [10, 80]\text{ bp/s}$ for UV and 5-FU conditions to quantify how elongation slowdown propagates into the posterior distributions of $\mu$ and $\delta$.

### Defect 4: Improper Uniform Prior in Log-Transformed MCMC & Single-Chain Visual Convergence (Pillar 4 — Major)
* **Location:** Results, Lines 113 & 119; Methods, Lines 62 & 67.
* **Statistical Flaw:** The authors state: *"An improper uniform prior over the non-negative real parameter space was chosen... inference was done on the logarithmic values."* First, an improper flat prior on $\theta \in (0, \infty)^5$ requires a Jacobian factor $\prod_i \theta_i$ when sampling in $\log \theta$ space (otherwise a flat prior in $\log \theta$ corresponds to the Jeffreys prior $p(\theta) \propto \prod_i \theta_i^{-1}$). Which prior was actually implemented is ambiguous. Second, when active TS counts $k$ are near 0 or $K$ (e.g., basal BAX or UV-inhibited time points), the likelihood plateaus as $\gamma \to \infty$ or $\beta \to \infty$, rendering the posterior improper under an unbounded flat prior. Third, running a single MCMC chain (30,000 samples after 10,000 burn-in) and assessing convergence "visually from the sample traces" is insufficient for a 5D correlated posterior.
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Specify properly bounded weakly informative log-normal or half-normal priors on $(\lambda, \gamma, \mu, \delta, \beta)$, explicitly state the log-space Jacobian adjustment, and run $\ge 4$ independent chains per condition reporting the Gelman–Rubin $\hat{R}$ statistic and effective sample size ($\text{ESS}$).

### Defect 5: Identical Forward and Reverse Primer Sequences for *CDKN1A* (Pillar 7 — Minor/Technical)
* **Location:** Methods ("Quantitative Real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)", Line 77).
* **Technical Flaw:** The text reports: `CDKN1A forward: TGGACCTGTCACTGTCTTGT; CDKN1A reverse: TGGACCTGTCACTGTCTTGT`. The forward and reverse primer sequences are identical.
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Replace the duplicated *CDKN1A* reverse primer sequence with the actual reverse oligonucleotide used in the RT-qPCR experiments.
