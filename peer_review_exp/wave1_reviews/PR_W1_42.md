## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript presents **NanoTag**, an antibody-free chromatin profiling protocol that fuses an anti-GFP nanobody directly to Tn5 transposase (`pMD1`, Addgene #215567) to tagment DNA around GFP- or Venus-tagged chromatin proteins in a single binding step. The authors benchmark NanoTag in duplicate ($400,000$ cells per replicate) in mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs) across three targets: an engineered H3K4me3 reader (`TAF3-eGFP`), `Nanog-VENUS`, and `AID-eGFP-CTCF`, comparing the resulting profiles against in-house anti-GFP CUT&Tag as well as public ChIP-seq, CUT&RUN, and ATAC-seq datasets. They also test protocol variations using fresh vs. frozen nuclei and centrifugation vs. concanavalin A magnetic beads.

**Editorial Verdict: Minor-to-Major Revision (Methodologically useful and well-documented resource, but requires transparent benchmarking of library complexity and pre-downsampling sensitivity).**  
The authors deserve credit for open-science practices: depositing the expression plasmid in Addgene (`#215567`), posting a step-by-step protocol on `protocols.io`, releasing analysis scripts on GitHub, and depositing sequencing reads in both GEO (`GSE302691`) and ENA (`PRJEB77284`). However, the benchmark comparisons between NanoTag and CUT&Tag are substantially skewed by a hidden library-complexity bottleneck: NanoTag required **21–22 PCR cycles** (compared to **14 cycles** for CUT&Tag on the same 400,000 cell input), indicating a $>100$-fold lower initial tagmentation yield and resulting in severe PCR duplication rates ($>80\text{–}90\%$, leaving only $\sim 2\text{ million}$ unique reads for CTCF from a targeted 25 million read pairs). Because the authors downsampled the deduplicated CUT&Tag BAM files down to NanoTag's low unique read count before calling peaks and computing metrics, the benchmark artificially penalizes CUT&Tag and overstates NanoTag's cost-effectiveness and sensitivity.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: NanoTag indirectly profiles H3K4me3 genome-wide via `TAF3-eGFP` with high replicate reproducibility and concordance with CUT&Tag (Figs 1–2).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Across $N=2$ replicates, `TAF3-NanoTag` achieves $>80\%$ inter-replicate peak overlap, strong promoter localization, and captures 83% of depth-matched `TAF3-CUT&Tag` peaks and 67% of `TAF3` ChIP-seq peaks with low background in wild-type (untagged) mESCs.
* **Claim 2: NanoTag accurately maps transcription factors (`Nanog-VENUS` and `AID-eGFP-CTCF`) with sensitivity comparable to CUT&Tag (Figs 3–4).** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  While `Nanog-NanoTag` performs well (capturing 73% of depth-matched CUT&Tag and ChIP-seq peaks with strong motif enrichment), performance on `CTCF` is modest: `CTCF-NanoTag` captures only **46%** of depth-matched CUT&Tag peaks, **30%** of CUT&RUN peaks (generated in the exact same `AID-eGFP-CTCF` cell line), and **17%** of ENCODE ChIP-seq peaks (line 41; Fig S2). Furthermore, CUT&Tag was downsampled after deduplication to match NanoTag's low unique read depth ($\sim 2\text{M}$ reads for CTCF), masking CUT&Tag's higher native library complexity.
* **Claim 3: NanoTag is more cost-effective than CUT&Tag despite high read duplication because "deeper sequencing... remains cost-effective" (Discussion line 70).** `[Overstated]`  
  Requiring 21–22 PCR cycles (vs. 14 cycles for CUT&Tag) and aiming for 25 million read pairs to recover 2–5 million unique deduplicated reads means that 80–90% of sequencing reads are discarded as PCR duplicates. At scale (or in single-cell applications, where high library complexity per cell is mandatory), throwing away 80–90% of sequencing output offsets the reagent savings of omitting a primary/secondary IgG antibody.
* **Claim 4: NanoTag performs equivalently across fresh cells, fresh nuclei, frozen nuclei, and bead-free centrifugation protocols (Fig 5).** `[Fragile]`  
  Fig 5 reports only a read-coverage correlation matrix (Fig 5b) and two locus browser tracks (`Sox2` and `Tbx3`, Fig 5c) for `TAF3` without stating the number of replicates per condition, unique read yield after deduplication, PCR cycle numbers, FrIP scores, or genome-wide peak recall.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Post-Deduplication Downsampling Asymmetry and Severe Library Complexity Loss (21–22 vs. 14 PCR Cycles)
* **Location:** Results line 41; Discussion line 70; Methods lines 105, 110, 115, 125.
* **Methodological & Statistical Flaw:**
  1. Starting from the exact same input of 400,000 cells, CUT&Tag libraries required **14 PCR cycles** (line 110), whereas NanoTag libraries required **21–22 PCR cycles** (line 105) to reach $\ge 2\text{ nM}$. In exponential PCR amplification, an extra 7–8 cycles reflects an approximately $2^7\text{ to }2^8$ ($128\text{–}256\times$) lower yield of tagmented library molecules per cell. (One likely biochemical cause is the $1:1$ monovalent stoichiometry of `nanobody-Tn5` compared to the amplified multivalent `primary IgG + secondary anti-rabbit IgG + pA-Tn5` complex in CUT&Tag, compounded by overnight incubation in 150 mM NaCl followed by three 300 mM NaCl washes).
  2. Consequently, even when sequenced to a target depth of 25 million paired-end reads (line 115), deduplicated unique reads dropped to $\sim 2\text{ million}$ for CTCF and $4\text{–}5\text{ million}$ for TAF3 and Nanog (line 41).
  3. In line 125, the authors state: *"For all comparisons, the datasets were matched in sequencing read depth by downsampling the CUT&Tag data for each target to the depth of the corresponding NanoTag data after filtering."* Downsampling *after* duplicate removal erases CUT&Tag's primary technical advantage (higher molecular complexity at equal raw sequencing depth). To evaluate two library preparation methods fairly, benchmarks must compare performance at **equal raw sequenced read pairs** (pre-deduplication) alongside equal unique reads.

### Defect 2: Selection Bias in Spearman Correlation Matrices Restricted to Common Triple-Intersected Peaks (Figs 2a, 3b, 4b)
* **Location:** Figure legends 2a, 3b, 4b and Methods line 125.
* **Statistical Flaw:** The correlation matrices in Figs 2a, 3b, and 4b compute Spearman correlations strictly *"at peaks common to the NanoTag, CUT&Tag and ChIP-seq datasets for the respective target."* For CTCF, NanoTag only calls 17% of ChIP-seq peaks and 46% of CUT&Tag peaks. Restricting correlation calculations to the small subset of high-affinity peaks where all three assays agree conditions on the outcome and inflates correlation coefficients while ignoring the 54–83% of binding sites where NanoTag fails to call a peak. Genome-wide binned correlations (e.g., 500 bp or 1 kb non-overlapping bins across the genome or across the union of all peaks) should be reported.

### Defect 3: Truncated / Non-Functional Cloning Primer Sequences in Methods (Lines 87–89)
* **Location:** Methods lines 87–89 ("Transposome preparation").
* **Reproducibility Defect:** The Methods list the PCR primers used to amplify the anti-GFP nanobody from `pGEX6P1-GFP-Nanobody` (Addgene #61838) as:
  * `FWD primer for anti-GFP nanobody containing HindIII site: GATCAAGCTTAC.` (12 nt)
  * `REV primer for anti-GFP nanobody containing EcoRI site: CAGAATTCGATC.` (12 nt)
  These 12-base sequences contain only the 5' clamp (`GATC`), the 6-bp restriction site (`AAGCTT` for HindIII; `GAATTC` for EcoRI), and 2 bases (`AC` or `CA`), completely omitting the 18–22 nt 3' template-annealing sequences required to PCR-amplify the nanobody coding sequence.

### Defect 4: Unmatched Genetic Backgrounds of Wild-Type Control vs. Knock-In mESC Lines and MACS2 Peak Calling on Sparse Controls
* **Location:** Methods lines 100 and 125.
* **Experimental Design Flaw:**
  1. A single wild-type mESC line (`HA36CB1`, `129 × C57BL/6` F1 hybrid) was used as the background control for all targets, whereas `Nanog-VENUS` is in `R1` (`129` background) and `AID-eGFP-CTCF` is in `E14Tg2a` (`129P2/OlaHsd`). Strain-level structural variants and SNPs between `C57BL/6` and `129` affect alignment and local background in WT-normalized MACS2 peak calling.
  2. Furthermore, calling peaks with `MACS2` (`q-value 0.1`, a permissive 10% FDR threshold) using ultra-sparse WT NanoTag/CUT&Tag tracks as the local background control (`-c`) is known in the CUT&Tag literature (e.g., Kaya-Okur et al. 2019, 2020) to inflate false-positive peaks wherever a few background reads land or fail to land, compared to calibration-free callers like `SEACR` or `MACS2 --nolambda`.

### Defect 5: Minor Technical & Formatting Issues
* **Primer Table Column Misalignment (Lines 137–151):** In the Enrichment qPCR primers table, all `_REV` primer rows are missing the middle `Target` column entry, shifting the 5'-to-3' primer sequence into the `Target` column.
* **Duplicate Data Availability Sections with Different Repository Accessions:** Line 120 states data were deposited to GEO under `GSE302691`, whereas Line 179 states data were deposited to ENA under `PRJEB77284` (and lines 12–16 at the end repeat both). If these two accessions mirror the same FASTQ files, this should be explicitly stated.
* **Sample Size ($N=2$) Without Uncertainty Estimates in Bar/Summary Plots:** Figs 2f, 3f, and 4f plot $N=2$ replicates without formal statistical testing, and Fig 5b omits replicate counts and quality control metrics (FrIP, duplication rate, peak count) for the frozen nuclei and centrifugation variants.
