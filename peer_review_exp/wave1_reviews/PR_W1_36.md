## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study resolves the nearly 200-year-old biochemical mystery of how the pyridine and pyrrolidine rings of $(S)$-nicotine ($\mathbf{1}$) are coupled from nicotinic acid ($\mathbf{2}$) and $N$-methylpyrrolinium ($\mathbf{3}$). By identifying a root-co-expressed biosynthetic gene cluster pairing the isoflavone-reductase-like oxidoreductase *A622* and transporter *MATE1/2* with a previously uncharacterized vacuolar $\beta$-glucosidase ($\beta\text{-GD1/2}$), alongside a topping-induced UDP-glucosyltransferase (*UGT1*), the authors discover that nicotine biosynthesis proceeds through a cryptic activating $N$-glucosylation cascade: (1) UGT1 (NaGT) converts nicotinic acid ($\mathbf{2}$) to nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside ($\mathbf{10}$), activating the pyridine ring for reduction; (2) A622 (NaGR) catalyzes NADPH-dependent C6 reduction of $\mathbf{10}$ to unstable 1,2-dihydropyridine glucoside ($\mathbf{4b}$); (3) BBLa (NicGS) directs the stereoselective formation/oxidation of coupled $(S)$-dihydronicotine glucoside ($\mathbf{5b}$) to $(S)$-nicotine glucoside ($(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$) with stereospecific abstraction of the C6 *pro-S* hydride; and (4) $\beta$-GD1 (NicGH) hydrolyzes the activating glucose moiety to release $(S)$-nicotine. The authors validate every step via in vitro reconstitution, alternative electrophiles (yielding nornicotine and anabasine), deuterium kinetic isotope tracing ($2\text{-}d_4$ and $2\text{-}d_6$), ligand-bound X-ray crystal structures of both NaGR (1.3 Å) and NicGS (2.5 Å) showing opposite facial hydride transfer ($re$-face addition at 3.4 Å vs. $si$-face abstraction at 3.2 Å), *in planta* agroinfiltration with $d_4$-nicotinamide feeding, and metabolite profiling of *N. tabacum* and *N. benthamiana* $\Delta A622$ and $\Delta BBL$ knockout roots.

**Editorial Verdict: Accept / Exemplary (9.1 / 10.0).** This is an exceptionally rigorous, multi-orthogonal biochemical and structural biology manuscript where every link in the causal chain is verified in vitro, structurally at atomic resolution, and *in planta* with isotope tracing and genetic knockouts. Moreover, the authors commendably avoid overclaiming on the one remaining mechanistic ambiguity—explicitly discussing (Results Line 32, Discussion Line 75, and Extended Data Fig. 7) whether BBLa (NicGS) directly catalyzes the C–C bond-forming Mannich condensation or operates via dynamic kinetic resolution / stereoselective oxidation of rapidly equilibrating $(R,S)\text{-}\mathbf{5b}$. Minor technical/editorial defects (a crystallographic $R_{\text{cryst}}/R_{\text{free}}$ elevation in the apo-NicGS structure, a compound-numbering typo in Line 66, and a figure cross-reference typo in Line 42) are easily corrected.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Nicotine biosynthesis requires a cryptic four-enzyme $N$-glucosylation/deglucosylation cascade (NaGT/UGT1, NaGR/A622, NicGS/BBLa, NicGH/$\beta$-GD1) converting nicotinic acid ($\mathbf{2}$) and $N$-methylpyrrolinium ($\mathbf{3}$) into $(S)$-nicotine ($\mathbf{1}$): [Well Supported].**  
  Unambiguously demonstrated via complete and drop-out one-pot in vitro enzymatic cascades (Fig. 2, Extended Data Fig. 2), chiral HPLC showing exclusive $(S)\text{-}\mathbf{1}$ enantiomer formation in the presence of BBLa (Fig. 2C–D), and transient *in planta* reconstitution in *N. benthamiana* leaves fed with nicotinamide-$d_4$ (Fig. 4).
* **NaGR (A622) delivers a hydride from NADPH to the C6 $re$-face (*pro-R*) of nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside ($\mathbf{10}$), while NicGS (BBLa) abstracts the C6 $si$-face (*pro-S*) hydride (deuteride) from 1,2-dihydronicotine glucoside ($\mathbf{5b}$): [Well Supported].**  
  Supported by concordant evidence from $2\text{-}d_4$ and $2\text{-}d_6$ isotopic labeling (yielding $1\text{-}d_3$ and $1\text{-}d_0$ enzymatically, vs. kinetic-isotope-effect retention of $1\text{-}d_4$ and $1\text{-}d_6$ under non-enzymatic oxidation; Fig. 3A–B) and the 1.3 Å NaGR-NADP$^+$-$\mathbf{10}$ (`9RDD`) and 2.5 Å NicGS-FAD-$(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$ (`9RDR`) crystal structures (Fig. 3E–H).
* **The four-enzyme cascade is modular with respect to the iminium electrophile, forming nornicotine ($\mathbf{15}$) from $\Delta^1$-pyrrolinium ($\mathbf{14}$) and anabasine ($\mathbf{6}$) from $\Delta^1$-piperideinium ($\mathbf{8}$): [Well Supported].**  
  Demonstrated both with chemically synthesized iminium substrates and in situ enzymatic generation via OLADO from ornithine/lysine in vitro (Fig. 2E, Fig. 3C–D) and *in planta* (Extended Data Fig. 6).
* **Endogenous *N. tabacum* and *N. benthamiana* roots accumulate the predicted glucoside intermediates/shunt products ($\mathbf{10}, \mathbf{12}, \mathbf{13}$) in patterns matching $\Delta A622$ and $\Delta BBL$ knockout predictions: [Well Supported].**  
  Confirmed by LC-MS against synthetic standards in wild-type and knockout roots (Fig. 5), though a typographical slip in Line 66 mislabels compound $\mathbf{13}$ as $\mathbf{10}$ (see Defect 2 below).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Elevated $R_{\text{cryst}} / R_{\text{free}}$ ($29.3\% / 32.4\%$) in the Apo-NicGS Crystal Structure (`9RUW`) and Incomplete Glucosyl Density in `9RDD` (Pillar 4 — Minor)
* **Location:** Results (Lines 47, 52) and Methods ("X-ray structure elucidation", Line 119).
* **Technical Observation:** While the 1.3 Å NaGR complex (`9RDD`, $R_{\text{cryst}}/R_{\text{free}} = 17.1\%/21.0\%$) and the 2.5 Å ligand-bound NicGS complex (`9RDR`, $R_{\text{cryst}}/R_{\text{free}} = 21.5\%/26.9\%$) have strong refinement statistics, the 2.5 Å "apo" NicGS structure (`9RUW`—which was actually co-crystallized in the presence of 10 mM $(S)$-nicotine glucoside $\mathbf{13}$ vs. 50 mM for `9RDR`, Line 114) has notably high $R_{\text{cryst}}/R_{\text{free}}$ values of $29.3\%/32.4\%$, likely due to disorder in the unliganded E348 substrate-recognition loop or partial low-occupancy ligand binding from the 10 mM $(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$ present in the crystallization drop. Similarly, in `9RDD`, the authors candidly note that the sugar electron density of $\mathbf{10}$ was incomplete (Line 47).
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Clarify in the text and Table S5 that `9RUW` was crystallized in the presence of 10 mM $(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$ (rather than strictly ligand-free buffer) and briefly note whether weak residual density in the active site or disordered surface loops accounts for the $29.3\%/32.4\%$ $R$-factors at 2.5 Å.

### Defect 2: Compound Numbering Typo in Knockout Root Metabolite Analysis & Figure Cross-Reference Typo (Pillar 7 — Minor)
* **Location:** Results, Line 42, Line 66; Methods, Line 114.
* **Editorial/Technical Typos:**
  1. **Line 66:** Reads *"The NicGS (BBL) knockout accumulated pyridine glucoside ( 12 ) and nicotine glucoside ( 10) , similar to wildtype N. tabacum roots ( Fig. 5A )."* Nicotine glucoside is compound **$\mathbf{13}$** (whereas $\mathbf{10}$ is nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside)—and indeed, in a $\Delta BBL$ knockout, non-enzymatic oxidation of $\mathbf{5b}$ produces racemic $(R,S)$-nicotine glucoside ($\mathbf{13}$) alongside $\mathbf{10}$ and $\mathbf{12}$.
  2. **Line 42:** Refers to `(Extended Data Fig. 32, Table S4 )` instead of `(Extended Data Fig. 3, Table S4)`.
  3. **Line 114:** Refers to `5 mM nicotinic acid N-glucoside 1` instead of `nicotinic acid N-glucoside 10`.
  4. **Extended Data Fig. 7 legend (Line 170):** Refers twice to Mannich reaction *"between 3 and 10"* and *"opening (R)-5b into 3 and 10"* where compound **$\mathbf{4b}$** (1,2-dihydropyridine glucoside), not $\mathbf{10}$ (nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside), is the nucleophile that reacts with $\mathbf{3}$.
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Correct `(10)` to `(13)` (or clarify whether $\mathbf{10}$ or $\mathbf{13}$ is meant) in Line 66, change `Extended Data Fig. 32` to `Extended Data Fig. 3` in Line 42, change `nicotinic acid N-glucoside 1` to `10` in Line 114, and change `10` to `4b` in the last two sentences of the Extended Data Figure 7 legend.
