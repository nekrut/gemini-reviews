## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study investigates whether stress-associated phytohormones—abscisic acid (ABA) and salicylic acid (SA)—reactivate dormant root-zone bacteria. Root-zone soils collected near common bean (*Phaseolus vulgaris*) and switchgrass (*Panicum virgatum*) were composited by crop, dried at $55^\circ\text{C}$ for 3 days to induce dormancy, acclimated at 25% water holding capacity (WHC) for 2 weeks, and then brought to 50% WHC across four treatments ($n=4$ mesocosm jars per treatment per soil; 32 jars total): water only, methanol carrier control ($1\text{ mL}$ methanol per $100\text{ g}$ dry soil), ABA in $1\text{ mL}$ methanol, or SA in $1\text{ mL}$ methanol. Three $0.5\text{-g}$ subsamples were scooped from each jar on Days 1, 7, and 14, co-extracted for DNA and RNA, and sequenced for 16S V4 rDNA and cDNA (rarefied to 12,000 reads). Taxa with $\text{16S rRNA}:\text{rDNA} \ge 1$ were designated "active," and `DESeq2`, `PERMANOVA`, and `ANOVA` were used to test three hypotheses regarding plant-specific (`H1`), general (`H2`), and hormone-specific (`H3`) resuscitation.

**Editorial Verdict: Reject / Major Revision Required (Severe solvent confounding, $9\times$ pseudoreplication, and indirect statistical contrasts).**  
While the co-extraction of DNA and RNA across 333 samples and open deposition of data (`PRJNA932434`) and code (`GitHub`) represent substantial effort, the experimental design and statistical analysis suffer from four fundamental flaws:
1. **Severe Methanol Carrier Confounding & Stoichiometric Contradiction (Line 132):** Delivering phytohormones in **$1\text{ mL}$ of pure methanol per $100\text{ g}$ dry soil** introduces $792\text{ mg}$ of methanol ($\mathbf{2,970\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}\text{ dry soil}}$)—which is **$6\text{ to }12\times$ more organic carbon than the phytohormones themselves** ($252\text{–}540\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$) and represents a toxic/methylotrophic solvent pulse. Moreover, Line 132 states both `0.3 M` and `3M` in consecutive sentences and falsely claims that hormone additions were normalized by carbon per gram of soil (equal molar volumes of $\text{C}_{15}$ ABA and $\text{C}_7$ SA differ in carbon mass by $2.14\times$).
2. **Three-Level Pseudoreplication (Lines 47, 117, 134, 168):** (a) Four field plots per crop were composited into a single bucket prior to the experiment ($N=1$ true field replicate per crop); (b) **three $0.5\text{-g}$ technical scoops** were taken from each mesocosm jar at each time point ($4\text{ jars} \times 3\text{ scoops} = 12$ pseudoreplicates per condition); and (c) the same 16 jars were repeatedly sampled on Days 1, 7, and 14 without mixed-effects random intercepts or repeated-measures blocking in `ANOVA`, `PERMANOVA`, or `DESeq2`.
3. **Indirect Baseline Contrasts Instead of Direct Hormone vs. Carrier Control Testing (Lines 57–64):** Rather than testing `ABA/SA (in MeOH, 50% WHC)` directly against `Methanol Control (50% WHC)` on Day 1 in `DESeq2`, the authors tested `Day 1 (50% WHC + MeOH + Hormone)` against the **pre-treatment `Water-Acclimated` baseline (`25% WHC`, no MeOH)** and then qualitatively compared lists of $\log_2\text{FC} > 2$ hits—even admitting that the headline *Microbispora* OTUs *"were detected as active in methanol controls"* (Line 63).
4. **Taxonomic Misclassification (Line 59):** The manuscript classifies Family *Myxococcaceae* (phylum Myxococcota, Gram-negative predatory bacteria) as an *"Actinobacteriota OTU."*

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: Specific Actinobacterial (*Microbispora*, *Streptomyces*) and *Myxococcaceae* OTUs resuscitate from dormancy within 24 hours specifically in response to ABA and SA as "cry for help" signals (Abstract, Figs 2, S2–S3).** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  Enrichment was calculated by comparing Day 1 (50% WHC + 1 mL methanol + millimolar hormone) against the pre-treatment 25% WHC baseline rather than directly testing `Hormone + Methanol vs. Methanol Carrier` at Day 1. Line 63 explicitly notes that the two headline *Microbispora* OTUs were also active in the methanol carrier controls. Furthermore, dosing rootless, $55^\circ\text{C}$-baked bulk soil with $250\text{–}540\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$ of unbuffered hormone (5–6 orders of magnitude above nanomolar rhizosphere signaling concentrations) in $2,970\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$ of methanol tests high-dose carbon/solvent utilization, not nanomolar plant signaling.
* **Claim 2: Bean and switchgrass root-zone soils exhibit statistically distinct active microbiome structures and richness trajectories (`H1`, PERMANOVA $F=29.05, p=0.001$; ANOVA $F=36.73, p<0.001$, Lines 70, 86).** `[Overstated]`  
  Because all four field cores for each crop were composited and homogenized into a single bucket before mesocosm setup (Line 117), "crop type" is completely confounded with farm location (MSU Agronomy Farm vs. Kellogg Biological Station, Line 115) at an effective field sample size of $N=1$ composite soil per crop, inflated by treating 144 technical/longitudinal jar subsamples per soil as independent replicates.
* **Claim 3: *Microbispora* acts as a generalist phytohormone responder across crops (`H2`), whereas cross-crop hormone-specific activation (`H3`) is not supported (Lines 75–85).** `[Fragile]`  
  The absence of shared hormone-specific responders (`H3`) is transparent, but the generalist activation claim for a single *Microbispora* OTU (`H2`) rests on thresholded $\text{rRNA}:\text{rDNA} \ge 1$ transitions without a direct statistical interaction test against the methanol carrier control.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Three-Tier Pseudoreplication (Field Compositing, Within-Jar Triplicate Subsampling, and Unmodeled Longitudinal Repeated Measures)
* **Location:** Results lines 47, 57, 70, 86; Methods lines 117, 130, 134, 168.
* **Statistical & Experimental Design Flaw:**
  1. **Field-level compositing ($N=1$ per crop):** Line 117 states that field cores from four replicate plots were *"composited into ethanol-sterilized buckets and hand-mixed to homogenize."* Compositing eliminates field-plot variance; statistical tests of "crop type" ($F = 29.05$ and $F = 36.73$) only compare two homogenized soil buckets from two different geographic sites.
  2. **Within-jar triplicate subsampling ($3\times$ inflation):** There were only **4 replicate jars** per treatment per soil (16 jars per soil, Line 47). However, Line 134 reveals: *"With four replicate mesocosms for four treatments and three time points collected in three replicates, n = 144 total samples for each soil type after water acclimation."* Taking three $0.5\text{-g}$ scoops from the same $100\text{-g}$ jar yields technical subsamples, not independent experimental replicates.
  3. **Repeated sampling of the same jars over time ($3\times$ inflation):** Each jar was non-destructively sampled on Days 1, 7, and 14 (Line 132). Treating $4\text{ jars} \times 3\text{ scoops} \times 3\text{ days} = 36$ measurements per treatment as 36 independent observations in Type III `ANOVA`, `PERMANOVA`, and `DESeq2` commits $9\times$ pseudoreplication. (Scoops within the same jar at a given timepoint must be pooled or averaged, and longitudinal models must include `Mesocosm_Jar_ID` as a random effect or permutation block).

### Defect 2: Massive Methanol Carrier Carbon Pulse ($2,970\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$ Soil), Unbuffered Millimolar Hormone Dosing, and Molarity/Carbon Contradictions
* **Location:** Methods line 132 ("Mesocosm set-up, phytohormone treatment, and maintenance").
* **Biochemical & Stoichiometric Flaw:**
  1. **Molarity contradiction (`0.3 M` vs. `3M`) and unequal carbon dosing:** Line 132 states: *"Phytohormones were dissolved in methanol to create a **0.3 M** solution, and 1 mL of the phytohormone-methanol solution was filter sterilized... We normalized the hormone addition based on the amount of carbon added per g of dry soil, and a **3M** solution for ABA and SA resulted in approximately 250-500 ug C g-1 dry soil for SA and ABA."*
     * Stoichiometric check: $1\text{ mL}$ of **$0.3\text{ M}$** salicylic acid ($\text{C}_7\text{H}_6\text{O}_3$, $7\text{ mol C/mol}$) per $100\text{ g}$ dry soil delivers $0.3\text{ mmol} \times 84\text{ mg C/mmol} / 100\text{ g} = \mathbf{252\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}\text{ dry soil}}$. Meanwhile, $1\text{ mL}$ of **$0.3\text{ M}$** abscisic acid ($\text{C}_{15}\text{H}_{20}\text{O}_4$, $15\text{ mol C/mol}$) per $100\text{ g}$ dry soil delivers $0.3\text{ mmol} \times 180\text{ mg C/mmol} / 100\text{ g} = \mathbf{540\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}\text{ dry soil}}$. Thus, `0.3 M` (not `3M`) is the concentration that yields $252\text{–}540\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$, and the two hormones were added at **equal molarity ($0.3\text{ M}$), not normalized to equal carbon** (ABA added $2.14\times$ more carbon than SA).
  2. **Methanol solvent overwhelms the hormone carbon input:** Adding $1\text{ mL}$ of pure methanol ($\rho = 0.792\text{ g/mL}$, $37.5\%\text{ C}$ by mass) to $100\text{ g}$ dry soil adds **$2,970\ \mu\text{g C g}^{-1}\text{ dry soil}$**—roughly **$6\text{ to }12\times$ more carbon than the ABA or SA solute**! This massive methanol pulse (plus the simultaneous moisture jump from 25% WHC to 50% WHC) explains why bacterial resuscitation on Day 1 was dominated by the methanol response (Lines 63, 65; Figs S3E, S4).

### Defect 3: Misapplication of `DESeq2` to Rarefied, Ratio-Filtered, Phantom-Imputed OTU Counts and Indirect Baseline Contrasts
* **Location:** Results lines 57–64; Methods lines 161–168.
* **Bioinformatic & Statistical Flaw:**
  1. `DESeq2` requires unrarefied, unfiltered raw integer read counts to estimate library size factors (`median-of-ratios`) and negative-binomial dispersion parameters across all features. Here, the authors (a) rarefied libraries to 12,000 reads, (b) replaced $\text{DNA} = 0$ with $\text{DNA} = 1$ for every "phantom taxon" where $\text{cDNA} \ge 1$ without a minimum read threshold (Line 163, 168), (c) discarded all OTUs with $\text{rRNA}:\text{rDNA} < 1$, and (d) ran `DESeq2` on the truncated "active DNA" matrix. Zeroing out inactive observations and imputing `1`s violates `DESeq2`'s variance and size-factor model.
  2. In Lines 57–63, differential abundance on Day 1 was tested by comparing `Day 1 Hormone (at 50% WHC + MeOH)` against the `Water-Acclimated pre-treatment sample (at 25% WHC, no MeOH)` and then excluding OTUs that also reached $\log_2\text{FC} > 2$ in the methanol control. Testing two conditions separately against baseline and comparing which ones cross a threshold commits the "difference in nominal significance" fallacy; `DESeq2` must directly test the contrast `Day 1 Hormone+MeOH (50% WHC) vs. Day 1 MeOH Control (50% WHC)`.

### Defect 4: Taxonomic and Editorial Errors
* **Taxonomic Error (Line 59):** Line 59 states: *"These included Actinobacteriota OTUs from Family Myxococcaceae, genus Microbispora, and genus Streptomyces."* Family *Myxococcaceae* belongs to phylum **Myxococcota**, not phylum Actinobacteriota.
* **Spelling & Typographical Errors:** Line 39 spells *Phaseolus vulgaris* as `Phasoelus vulagris` and *Fabaceae* as `Fabacaea`; Line 52 writes `OUT table` instead of `OTU table`; Line 95 writes `shot dry mass` instead of `shoot dry mass`.
