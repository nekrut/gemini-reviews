## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript presents the **All-GCL dataset**, an open-access aggregation of ex vivo two-photon $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ imaging recordings (`OGB-1`) from **84,451 somata** in the ganglion cell layer (GCL) of $n=203$ retinae ($n=139$ C57BL/6J mice across 139 experimental sessions collected over nine years by eight experimenters). All recordings share a core visual stimulation battery—a full-field frequency/contrast-modulated "chirp" and an 8-direction moving bar, plus binary dense noise for spatio-temporal receptive field (RF) mapping in a subset. Each recorded soma is probabilistically assigned to one of 46 functional groups (32 retinal ganglion cell [RGC] groups and 14 displaced amacrine cell [dAC] groups) using an uncertainty-calibrated Random Forest classifier trained on the reference dataset of Baden et al. (2016). The authors validate the resource by reconstructing 2D retinal density maps across all 46 functional groups and quantifying technical vs. biological batch effects using date-blocked and leave-one-experimenter-out classifiers. Both raw/processed Neurodata Without Borders (`.nwb`) files and a consolidated `.parquet` feature table are publicly hosted on Hugging Face (`eulerlab/all-gcl`), accompanied by versioned Python/DataJoint repositories on GitHub.

**Editorial Verdict: Accept with Minor Revisions (High-value, well-standardized neurophysiological resource with minor analytical and editorial issues).**  
The resource is a substantial contribution to visual neuroscience and time-series machine learning. Particularly commendable is the batch-effect validation (Fig 5), which blocks train/test splits by recording date to prevent session leakage and uses leave-one-experimenter-out validation to prove that an apparent age effect ($59.0\% \pm 3.7\%$ accuracy) was actually an artifact of experimenter-specific age confounding (dropping to chance under cross-experimenter testing). Minor revisions are warranted to clarify (1) an arithmetic discrepancy in the percentage of responsive cells ($52,189 / 84,451 = 61.8\%$, whereas the text reports $65.6\%$), (2) the consequences of re-introducing SR101-positive (damaged) cells via `Cellpose 3` and assigning functional labels to the ~32,000 unresponsive cells after unit-amplitude noise normalization, (3) the mathematical parameterization of the "time warping" applied to align chirp stimuli with Baden et al. (2016), and (4) duplicated bullet/paragraph blocks in the manuscript text.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: The All-GCL dataset provides standardized, quality-controlled functional recordings and metadata for 84,451 GCL cells across 139 sessions (Data Overview, Fig 2).** `[Well Supported]`  
  The dataset is balanced across sex and left/right eyes, spans P15–P200, includes exact retinal coordinates for 72,475 cells (85.5%), and provides both single-session `.nwb` files and an aggregated `.parquet` table with explicit signal-to-noise quality indices ($\text{QI}_{\text{chirp}}$ and $\text{QI}_{\text{MB}}$).
* **Claim 2: Supervised probabilistic classification maps All-GCL cells onto 46 functional RGC and dAC groups whose retinal topographies match known anatomical and transcriptomic gradients (Figs 3–4).** `[Well Supported]`  
  The 5-fold logistic-calibrated Random Forest achieves $76.4\%$ held-out accuracy across 75 sub-clusters (chance $= 1.3\%$) on the Baden et al. reference data, and the resulting 2D kernel density maps reproduce established dorsal `Off` (`G1–G9`), ventral `On` (`G15–G28`), temporal-ventral `On alpha sustained` (`G24`), and naso-temporal `On contrast suppressed` (`G28`) specializations.
* **Claim 3: Non-biological batch effects across setups and experimenters are small ($\approx 60\text{–}63\%$ binary classification accuracy) and biological covariates (sex, scan field, retinal location, age) are near chance (Fig 5).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Evaluated over 20 random date-grouped train/test splits with balanced classes, setup ID ($59.6\% \pm 4.6\%$) and certain experimenter pairs ($\le 63\%$) show modest predictability, while leave-one-experimenter-out cross-validation cleanly resolves the apparent age effect.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Arithmetic Discrepancy in Responsive Cell Fraction and Unconditional Classification of Unit-Normalized Noise Traces (Lines 82, 89, 164, 177)
* **Location:** Methods lines 82, 89, 94; Data Overview line 164; Functional Classification line 177.
* **Quantitative & Methodological Issue:**
  1. **Percentage mismatch:** Line 164 reports a total of $84,451$ GCL somata, *"with ~65.6% (52,189 cells) exceeding the selected response criterion ($\text{QI}_{\text{chirp}} \ge 0.35$ or $\text{QI}_{\text{MB}} \ge 0.6$)."* However, $52,189 / 84,451 = 61.80\%$, not $65.6\%$ (which would correspond to a denominator of $\sim 79,556$ cells). The authors should clarify whether $79,556$ cells had complete recordings for both stimuli or correct the percentage to $61.8\%$.
  2. **Re-inclusion of SR101-filled damaged cells and classification of unresponsive ROIs:** Line 82 notes that cells with strong Sulforhodamine-101 (`SR101`) filling—which labels damaged GCL cells (line 51)—and other unlabelled somata were automatically added back to manual ROI masks using `Cellpose 3`. Then, in line 89, every ROI trace is normalized to unit maximum amplitude ($\max_t |r(t)| = 1$), and in line 177 the functional group classifier is applied to **all recorded cells** (including the ~32,262 unresponsive cells failing the QI criteria). Dividing baseline-subtracted noise by its random maximum peak forces noise traces onto the unit sphere and assigns them to functional RGC/dAC groups. The authors should explicitly state whether an `is_responsive` or `SR101_intensity` flag is included in `all_GCL_table.parquet` so downstream users do not inadvertently include dead/unresponsive cells in population analyses.

### Defect 2: Unspecified "Time Warping" Transformation Applied to Align Chirp Responses with Baden et al. (2016) (Line 111)
* **Location:** Methods line 111 ("Classification of functional ganglion cell layer groups").
* **Methodological Ambiguity:** Line 111 states: *"However, the 'chirp' they used [in Baden et al. (7)] was slightly different in its timing properties to the 'chirp' used in this dataset. To align the responses as good a possible, we warped the time of the new data to maximize agreement with the Baden et al. ( 7 ) dataset."* The manuscript does not specify the mathematical form of this time warping (e.g., linear resampling of specific stimulus epochs vs. nonlinear dynamic time warping), what objective function was maximized to measure "agreement," or whether the warping was fixed globally from stimulus hardware triggers or fitted to neural responses. Because the 20 sparse-PCA chirp features depend sensitively on response latency and frequency-sweep phase, the exact warping specification should be documented in the Methods.

### Defect 3: Unexplained Tension Between Dorsal-Ventral Group Topography (Fig 4) and 50% Retinal Location Classification Accuracy (Fig 5b–c)
* **Location:** Technical Validation lines 191–195 (Fig 4) vs. lines 204–205 (Fig 5b–c).
* **Analytical Nuance:** Fig 4 demonstrates clear dorsal enrichment of `Off` supergroups (`G1–G9`) and ventral enrichment of `Fast On` / `Slow On` supergroups (`G15–G28`). Yet in Fig 5b–c, a high-capacity Random Forest trained on single-cell chirp and moving bar responses achieves only ~50% (chance) accuracy at predicting whether a cell is located in the dorsal vs. ventral retina. The authors should briefly explain this apparent paradox in the text: because every functional group is still present across both halves of the retina (with modest relative density shifts of ~1.2–1.5$\times$ in Fig 4) and the achromatic/equiluminant chirp does not probe the strong dorsal-ventral M/S-cone chromatic opsin gradient directly, single-cell achromatic responses cannot predict retinal hemisphere above chance even though cell-type density ratios shift topographically.

### Defect 4: Duplicated Text Blocks and Minor Typographical Errors
* **Duplicated List of Input Studies (Lines 28 vs. 30–42):** The seven constituent studies listed inline in paragraph 28 are repeated verbatim as seven standalone lines in lines 30–42.
* **Duplicated List of Classifier Features (Lines 99 vs. 101–107):** The four classifier input features in line 99 are repeated verbatim in lines 101–107.
* **Duplicated List of NWB Metadata Fields (Lines 147 vs. 149–157):** The five metadata categories in line 147 are repeated verbatim in lines 149–157.
* **Typos:** Line 5 writes both `A ll -GCL` (with a space) and `ALL-GCL`; line 51 writes `0.5 L-glutamineatpH 7.4` (missing spaces); line 111 writes `as good a possible`; line 233 writes `.paquet` instead of `.parquet` and `exports the Neurodata` instead of `exports in the Neurodata`.
