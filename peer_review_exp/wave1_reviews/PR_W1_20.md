## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This population genetics study genotypes $n = 654$ *Anopheles funestus* s.s. mosquitoes across 10 administrative regions of mainland Tanzania and $n = 28$ *Anopheles parensis* mosquitoes from Dodoma (used as an interspecific outgroup) at 12 microsatellite loci (filtered from 18 initial loci) to characterize genetic diversity, Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (HWE), isolation by distance (IBD), Rift Valley barrier effects, and population clustering via neighbor-joining ($F_{ST}$) trees and `STRUCTURE`.

### Primary Claims Audit
1. **Claim 1:** Mean allelic richness ($N_a$) and expected/observed heterozygosity ($H_S, H_O$) do not differ significantly across the ten *An. funestus* s.s. populations in mainland Tanzania. `[Methodologically Flawed]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* In Results line 153, the authors write: *"No significant differences in $H_S$ or $H_O$ were found across the ten populations (Kruskal–Wallis: $\chi^2 > 16.3, \text{df} = 9, P < 0.050$ in both cases)."* A Kruskal–Wallis test with $P < 0.050$ (for $\chi^2 = 16.3, \text{df} = 9$, $P = 0.061$, whereas for $\chi^2 > 16.92, \text{df} = 9$, $P < 0.05$) **contradicts** the claim of "no significant difference"! Either the reported inequality $P < 0.050$ is a typographical error for $P > 0.050$, or the test was statistically significant and misstated as non-significant.
2. **Claim 2:** Significant multi-locus heterozygote deficits ($H_O < H_S$, $P < 0.005$) in 9 of 11 populations are driven by "excessive statistical power" rather than population substructuring (Wahlund effect), null alleles, or inbreeding. `[Overstated]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* The authors already had to discard 6 of their 18 microsatellite loci (33% of the panel) because those loci deviated from HWE in $>4/11$ populations (line 132). Even among the remaining 12 loci, 9 of 11 populations show significant multi-locus heterozygote deficits, with text reporting "mean heterozygote deficits... range 0.407–0.660" (line 158). Furthermore, in 3 regions (`Dodoma`, `Tanga`, `Morogoro`), samples were obtained by **larval dipping** (line 112), which samples sibling cohorts from shared breeding sites (causing family structure / Wahlund deficits), and collections were pooled over a **4-year window (December 2018 – December 2022)** (line 112), creating temporal Wahlund effects.
3. **Claim 3:** There is no isolation by distance ($R^2_{\text{adj}} = -0.016, P = 0.569$) and no significant barrier effect of the Great Rift Valley on *An. funestus* s.s. gene flow, which the authors attribute to passive anthropogenic mosquito transport along railway and road corridors (e.g., linking Kigoma and Pwani, or Lindi/Ruvuma and Rukwa). `[Overstated]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Comparing all pairwise $F_{ST}$ values via a single 1D Kruskal–Wallis test (`same side vs. opposite side of Rift Valley`, $\chi^2 = 0.02, P = 0.900$, line 203) violates pairwise matrix non-independence (requires a partial Mantel test or Multiple Matrix Regression with Randomization, MMRR) and is heavily skewed by the extreme outlier population `Mtwara` ($F_{ST} = 0.195\text{--}0.288$ against all other populations, including nearby `Lindi` and `Ruvuma` on the same side of the Rift). Moreover, no road/railway distance or traffic volume metrics were statistically tested, and the authors themselves note (line 244) that a parallel whole-genome SNP study on the **exact same samples** [ref 50] *did* find pronounced genomic divergence across the Rift Valley!
4. **Claim 4:** The coastal southeastern *An. funestus* s.s. population from Mtwara forms a highly divergent genetic cluster ($F_{ST} = 0.195\text{--}0.288$, `STRUCTURE` $K=3$), approaching the interspecific divergence of the outgroup *An. parensis* ($F_{ST} = 0.208\text{--}0.378$). `[Moderately Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Consistent signal in pairwise $F_{ST}$, NJ clustering, and `STRUCTURE`. However, as discussed below, chromosomal inversion linkage (6 of the 12 retained loci reside inside or near polymorphic paracentric inversions on arms 2R, 3R, and 3L; Figure 2) and potential batch/scoring shifts or sibling-species misidentification (`An. funestus-like` / `An. vaneedeni`) must be ruled out.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Confounding of Larval Sibling Sampling and 4-Year Temporal Pooling with Population Structure
1. **Larval Sampling Bias (Allendorf–Phelps / Family Structure Effect):** In Methods line 112, the authors note that in `Dodoma`, `Tanga`, and `Morogoro`, where indoor adult catches were insufficient, *"larval collections were performed using standard larval dippers. The larvae were reared to adults, sorted by species, and processed."* Sampling mosquito larvae from a small number of breeding pools captures full- and half-sibling families from a few ovipositing females. Genotyping sibling cohorts inflates allele frequency variance, creates artifactual HWE and linkage disequilibrium deviations, and biases pairwise $F_{ST}$ and `STRUCTURE` clustering (the classic Allendorf–Phelps effect in vector genetics).
2. **Four-Year Temporal Pooling (2018–2022):** Collections spanned December 2018 to December 2022 across ~40–50 mosquito generations (including major ITN/IRS vector control campaigns and seasonal bottlenecks). Pooling mosquitoes collected across different years and seasons into single regional populations without reporting collection dates per region or testing for temporal differentiation induces a temporal Wahlund effect (explaining the pervasive heterozygote deficits in 9/11 populations) and confounds spatial $F_{ST}$ with temporal drift.

### 2.2 Biased HWE Locus Filtering and Unestimated Null Allele Frequencies
In Methods line 132, the authors state that the 18 markers were originally selected *"for having no evidence of null alleles [21]"*, and then they excluded 6 of the 18 loci because they deviated from HWE in $>4/11$ populations.
1. Just because a microsatellite locus lacked null alleles in a previous study in West/Central Africa does **not** mean it lacks null alleles in East African *An. funestus* s.s. or across a divergent sibling species (*An. parensis*, where priming site mutations frequently cause allele dropout!).
2. Rather than running standard microsatellite null-allele estimators (`Micro-Checker`, `FreeNA`, or `ML-NullFreq`) to estimate null allele frequencies per locus and compute ENA-corrected $F_{ST}$ values (Chapuis & Estoup 2007), the authors simply discarded the 6 worst HWE-violating loci and assumed the remaining 12 loci were free of null alleles—even though 9 of 11 populations still exhibited significant multi-locus HWE heterozygote deficits! In high-mutation microsatellites, differential null allele dropout or allele-size homoplasy between *An. parensis* and *An. funestus* (and potentially in `Mtwara`) substantially distorts $F_{ST}$.

### 2.3 Chromosomal Inversion Non-Neutrality Among the 12 Microsatellite Loci
In Methods line 132, the authors claim they filtered HWE to *"eliminate loci that did not conform to neutrality, resulting in a set of 12 preferred microsatellite loci (Fig. 2)."* However, as Figure 2 explicitly illustrates ("with associated chromosomal inversions indicated in brackets"), *An. funestus* s.s. harbors extensive polymorphic paracentric chromosomal inversions (`3Ra`, `3Rb`, `3La`, `2Ra`, `2Rh`, `2Rd`, `2Rab`) that are under strong climatic and ecological selection (defining the "Folonzo" and "Kiribina" chromosomal forms). Microsatellites located inside or near inversion breakpoints hitchhike with adaptive inversion karyotypes. Therefore, ecological divergence in inversion frequencies between coastal estuarine `Mtwara` and inland highland sites will drive elevated $F_{ST}$ at inversion-linked microsatellites without reflecting genome-wide neutral reproductive isolation.

### 2.4 Outdated `STRUCTURE v2.2` Analysis and Invalid Kruskal–Wallis Test on Pairwise $F_{ST}$ Matrix
1. **Pairwise Matrix Non-Independence:** Testing whether $F_{ST}$ values across the Rift Valley differ from $F_{ST}$ values on the same side of the Rift using a standard Kruskal–Wallis test ($\chi^2 = 0.02, \text{df} = 1, P = 0.900$, line 203) treats the $\binom{10}{2} = 45$ pairwise $F_{ST}$ values as independent observations, violating dyadic dependence. Moreover, keeping the extreme outlier `Mtwara` ($F_{ST} \approx 0.26$ with neighboring `Lindi` and `Ruvuma`) in the "same side of the Rift" group artificially inflates the within-side mean $F_{ST}$ to $0.18$, masking regional structure among the remaining 9 populations.
2. **Selection of $K=3$ in `STRUCTURE`:** The authors used `STRUCTURE v2.2` (released in 2007) without reporting the Evanno $\Delta K$ metric, cross-entropy (e.g., `ADMIXTURE` / `sNMF`), or Principal Component Analysis / Discriminant Analysis of Principal Components (`DAPC`, which does not assume HWE—critical here since 9/11 populations violate HWE!). Furthermore, including the interspecific outgroup *An. parensis* ($n=28$) alongside uneven sample sizes of *An. funestus* s.s. in `STRUCTURE` is well known to dominate the top hierarchy at $K=2, 3$ and mask subtler intraspecific substructure among the remaining 9 *An. funestus* populations.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Direct Statistical Contradiction on Line 153:**
   - Line 153 states: *"No significant differences in $H_S$ or $H_O$ were found across the ten populations (Kruskal–Wallis: $\chi^2 > 16.3, \text{df} = 9, P < 0.050$ in both cases)."* As noted in Section 1, $P < 0.050$ indicates statistical significance, directly contradicting the sentence. Moreover, on line 242, the Discussion later argues the opposite: *"Variations in observed heterozygosity among populations further support this hypothesis, with comparatively higher levels of heterozygosity observed in the coastal region of Pwani and the western region of Kigoma than in all other populations."*
2. **Inconsistent Description of "Heterozygote Deficits" Range on Line 158:**
   - Line 158 reports: *"mean heterozygote deficits were generally moderate (range 0.407–0.660)."* That range ($0.407\text{--}0.660$) is close to the range of observed/expected heterozygosity ($H_O$ or $H_S$), whereas the inbreeding coefficient $F_{IS} = (H_S - H_O)/H_S$ measuring heterozygote deficit in Table S4/Additional file 4 is much smaller (or if $F_{IS}$ truly ranged from $0.407$ to $0.660$, that is an **extreme**, not moderate, heterozygote deficit!).
3. **Lack of Raw Microsatellite Genotype Data Deposition:**
   - The Data Availability statement claims *"All data supporting the conclusions of this article are provided within the text"*, yet the individual multi-locus microsatellite genotype table ($N = 682$ mosquitoes $\times$ $12$ loci in `GENEPOP`/`STRUCTURE` format) is not provided in the main text or in a public repository (Dryad/Zenodo/Figshare).

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Perform Null-Allele Correction (`FreeNA`) and HWE-Free Ordination (`DAPC` / PCA):**
   - Run `FreeNA` (Chapuis & Estoup 2007) across all 12 (and original 18) loci to quantify null allele frequencies in each population and recompute ENA-corrected pairwise $F_{ST}$ values to verify that the divergence of `Mtwara` and *An. parensis* is not inflated by locus-specific allele dropout.
   - Perform a model-free PCA and Discriminant Analysis of Principal Components (`adegenet::dapc`)—which does not assume Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium—and run `STRUCTURE` **within the 9 non-Mtwara *An. funestus* s.s. populations alone** (excluding *An. parensis* and `Mtwara`) with Evanno $\Delta K$ plots across $K = 1\text{--}10$.
2. **Stratify by Chromosomal Inversion Location and Collection Method/Year:**
   - Recompute pairwise $F_{ST}$ separately for loci located *inside* known polymorphic inversions vs. *outside* inversions (Figure 2) to test whether `Mtwara`'s divergence is driven by chromosomal inversion hitchhiking.
   - Report the exact collection year/month and sample size ($n$) for each of the 11 populations in a main-text table, and verify that removing larval-sampled sites (`Dodoma`, `Tanga`, `Morogoro`) does not alter the IBD or clustering conclusions.
3. **Re-Run IBD and Rift Valley Barrier Tests With and Without the `Mtwara` Outlier Using Partial Mantel / MMRR:**
   - Test isolation by distance and the Rift Valley barrier both across all 10 *An. funestus* populations and across the 9 non-outlier populations using a partial Mantel test or Multiple Matrix Regression with Randomization (MMRR), rather than an invalid Kruskal–Wallis test on pairwise $F_{ST}$ entries.
4. **Fix Statistical Reporting Contradictions and Deposit Raw Genotypes:**
   - Correct the $P < 0.050$ vs. "no significant difference" contradiction on line 153, clarify the $F_{IS}$ vs. $H_O$ numbers on line 158, and deposit the complete $682 \times 18$ raw microsatellite genotype matrix in an open repository or Additional file.
