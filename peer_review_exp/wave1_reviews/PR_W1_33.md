## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study probes human cortico-limbic and cortico-cortical effective connectivity at the single-trial level across the sleep-wake cycle by delivering repeated 3 mA, 1 ms intracortical electrical pulses across 37,850 effective connections in 15 epilepsy patients over multi-day recordings. Defining single-trial response probability $P_{AB}$ via a compound waveform-resemblance line-length metric ($\pm\rho^2 LL$) and directionality via $DI_{AB} = (P_{AB} - P_{BA}) / \max(P_{AB}, P_{BA})$, the authors report that local connections ($<25\text{ mm}$) are nearly 100% responsive and bidirectional ($DI \approx 0$), whereas long-range connections show asymmetric directionality. Most prominently, they claim that limbic structures (amygdala and hippocampus) send twice as many signals to neocortex as they receive ($DI = +0.61$ and $+0.35$) across both wakefulness and sleep, challenging rodent memory consolidation models that posit a reversal of cortico-limbic information flow during sleep.

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision / Mixed & Salvageable (5.8 / 10.0).** While the multi-day single-trial SPES dataset across sleep stages is an impressive clinical neurophysiology resource with open data/code, the headline claims ("limbic structures send twice as many signals as they receive" and "absence of cortico-limbic flow reversal during sleep") suffer from three severe methodological and biophysical confounds:
1. **Asymmetric Stimulation Current Density, Volume-To-Node Size Mismatch, and Electrode Implantation Bias:** Delivering a fixed macroscopic 3 mA current across bipolar macro-contacts (0.8 mm diameter, 2 mm length, 3.5 mm spacing) inside small, densely packed nuclear/allocortical structures (amygdala or hippocampal CA/subiculum, where perforant path/output fibers are bundled in tight white-matter tracts like the alveus, fimbria, and uncinate fasciculus) recruits a much larger fraction of output projection axons than delivering the same 3 mA point-source current into a single 3.5 mm locus of vast six-layered neocortex, where projections to the hippocampus are diffusely distributed and gated via the entorhinal cortex. Equating the probability of an orthodromic/antidromic macro-stimulation CCEP ($P_{AB}$) with physiological *spontaneous* neural information flow conflates electrical stimulation recruitment geometry with endogenous directional signaling.
2. **In-Sample Circularity in Single-Trial Detection & Omission of Multiple-Testing Correction:** Connection-specific centroids are estimated via K-means ($K=2$) on the post-stimulation trials and then used to score the *same* trials via $\pm\rho^2 LL$ (where $\rho$ is the max cross-correlation over a $\pm 10\text{ ms}$ lag window), while the pre-stimulation baseline surrogates are not used to construct the centroid, inflating positive bias. Moreover, the Methods (Line 153) explicitly state that single-trial responses were tested *without* FDR correction at $p < 0.05$, guaranteeing a minimum 5% false-positive floor ($P \ge 0.05$) on dead/unresponsive reverse directions ($P_{BA}$) and distorting the ratio $DI_{AB}$.
3. **Pseudoreplication in Connection-Level Statistics:** In Figure 2 ($N=37,850$ connections; $N=2,365$ for $ExI$), Kruskal–Wallis and Pearson correlation tests treat thousands of electrode pairs nested within only 15 patients (and sharing common stimulating or recording contacts) as independent observations, yielding artificially deflated $p$-values ($p = 0$, $p < 10^{-168}$) and uncalibrated logistic regression standard errors (Line 158, which lacks random effects for patient and electrode).

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Single-trial CCEP responses exhibit trial-to-trial variability that correlates with connection excitability ($ExI$) and distance: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The empirical relationship between single-trial response rate $P$ and the intensity-response AUC ($ExI$, $r = 0.60$) is biologically sensible, though the reported $p < 10^{-168}$ is severely inflated by treating 2,365 connections across 15 patients as independent.
* **Local connections ($<25\text{ mm}$) are consistently short-latency ($<65\text{ ms}$), high-probability ($P = 99\%$), and reciprocal ($|DI| = 0.01$), whereas long-range connections ($\ge 25\text{ mm}$) are more variable and directional: [Well Supported].**  
  Even accounting for volume conduction at the shortest distances (after 10–15 ms artifact interpolation), the contrast in reliability and reciprocity between local intra-regional and long-range inter-regional propagation is robust across patients.
* **Limbic structures (amygdala and hippocampus) physiologically broadcast twice as many signals to neocortex as they receive ($DI = +0.61$ and $+0.35$): [Overstated].**  
  The data show that macro-stimulation (3 mA) in the amygdala or hippocampus evokes detectable CCEPs in neocortical contacts more frequently than focal 3 mA stimulation at a single neocortical contact evokes CCEPs in the hippocampus/amygdala. However, this asymmetry is heavily confounded by anatomical convergence/divergence geometry (focal stimulation in a compact limbic hub excites major output bundles, whereas neocortical-to-hippocampal inputs are polysynaptically gated through entorhinal/parahippocampal relays and require convergent spatial summation) and potentially by antidromic axonal activation. Calling this "actual brain signaling" or "information flow" overreaches what macro-SPES measures.
* **Cortico-limbic signaling does not reverse direction during NREM or REM sleep in humans, challenging the two-stage memory consolidation model: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The rodent two-stage memory model (Buzsáki et al.) posits a reversal in *spontaneous endogenous neuronal firing and oscillatory coordination* (e.g., sharp-wave ripples driving neocortical spindles/slow oscillations), NOT a reversal in passive orthodromic/antidromic axonal impedance to exogenous 3 mA electrical shocks. Furthermore, the authors' own data show that hippocampal efferent excitability ($ExI$) and signaling probability *decrease* during NREM ($-6.3\%, p < 10^{-5}$) and REM ($-5.1\%$) relative to wakefulness—the exact *opposite* of enhanced hippocampal-to-neocortical broadcasting during sleep.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Conflation of Macro-Electrode Perturbation Susceptibility with Endogenous Directed Information Flow & Antidromic Confounding (Pillars 1, 3 & 6 — Critical)
* **Location:** Abstract (Line 5), Introduction (Lines 12–16), Results (Lines 50, 60), Discussion (Lines 65, 71, 73).
* **Biophysical & Conceptual Flaw:** Bipolar stimulation at 3 mA (1 ms) injects $3\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ per phase across contacts in small, high-neuronal-density limbic structures (amygdala, hippocampus) versus large neocortical gyri. First, extracellular electrical stimulation depolarizes axons of passage and axon terminals (creating **antidromic** spikes as well as orthodromic spikes) at lower thresholds than somata. Stimulating the hippocampus or amygdala directly excites dense afferent/efferent fiber tracts, whereas stimulating a single 3.5 mm dipole in neocortex excites only a tiny fraction of the diffuse cortical mantle, which must traverse the entorhinal gate to reach the hippocampus. Second, testing the rodent "cortico-limbic flow reversal during sleep" hypothesis using exogenous 3 mA shocks confuses *passive excitability to artificial stimulation* with *endogenous directed spike/LFP transmission*.
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** (1) Reframe the title, abstract, and discussion to accurately state that the study maps *stimulation-evoked effective connectivity and excitability ($P, DI, ExI$)* rather than endogenous spontaneous information flow. (2) Separate short-latency ($<30\text{ ms}$) direct/antidromic responses from longer-latency polysynaptic responses when computing $DI_{AB}$ between limbic and neocortical regions. (3) Compare the SPES-derived $DI_{AB}$ against directed functional connectivity metrics (e.g., Granger causality, transfer entropy, or phase-slope index) computed on the *non-stimulated 5-minute baseline iEEG segments* across wake and NREM sleep in the same 15 patients.

### Defect 2: In-Sample Centroid Overfitting & Uncorrected Multiple Testing in Single-Trial Scoring (Pillars 2 & 4 — Major)
* **Location:** Methods ("Effective connections" & "Single-trial cortical responses", Lines 123–128; "Connection level", Line 153).
* **Statistical Flaw:** For each electrode pair, two centroid waveforms are computed via K-means clustering ($K=2$) on the post-stimulation trials. Then, each individual post-stimulation trial $x_i$ is scored using $\pm\rho^2 LL$, where $\rho$ is the maximum cross-correlation between $x_i$ and the in-sample centroid over a $\pm 10\text{ ms}$ lag window, and compared against 400 pre-stimulation baseline surrogates. Because the centroids are fit to the post-stimulation trials themselves (including their noise realization) and maximizing correlation over a $\pm 10\text{ ms}$ window (11 shifts at 500 Hz) positively biases $\rho^2$, in-sample post-stimulation trials have an artificially inflated $\pm\rho^2 LL$ relative to out-of-sample pre-stimulation baseline segments. Compounding this, Line 153 states that single-trial responses are thresholded at the 95th percentile (one-sided $p < 0.05$) *without* FDR correction. Consequently, a completely non-responsive reverse pathway $B \to A$ has an expected false-positive signaling probability of $\mathbb{E}[P_{BA}] \ge 0.05$, and under K-means centroid overfitting, even higher.
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Use leave-one-trial-out (or split-half) cross-validation when computing the K-means centroids so that trial $i$ is never present in the centroid against which it is correlated. Apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across single trials within each connection, or subtract the empirical baseline false-positive rate $P_0 = 0.05$ before computing $DI_{AB} = (P_{AB} - P_{BA}) / \max(P_{AB}, P_{BA})$.

### Defect 3: Unmodeled Hierarchical Clustering / Pseudoreplication in Connection-Level Models (Pillar 4 — Major)
* **Location:** Results (Lines 40, 45, 50) and Methods (Lines 153, 158).
* **Statistical Flaw:** The logistic regression model in Line 158:
  $$\text{logit}(P) = \beta_0 + \beta_1(\text{Region}_{\text{stim}} : \text{Region}_{\text{resp}}) + \beta_2 d$$
  omits random intercepts for Participant ($N=15$), Stimulating Electrode, and Recording Electrode, treating 37,850 non-independent dyads as independent Bernoulli/binomial observations. Furthermore, in Line 40, the authors report `p=0 for P and DI, one-way Kruskal-Wallis` (reporting $p=0$ due to floating-point underflow instead of $p < 10^{-16}$) and in Line 45 report `Pearson's rho = 0.60, p < 10^-168` across 2,365 connections without accounting for within-patient and within-electrode clustering. Also, in Line 133, the authors write *"with exponential increases for |DI| > 0.5"*, whereas the ratio $P_{AB}/P_{BA} = 1/(1 - DI_{AB})$ for $DI_{AB} > 0$ is a **hyperbolic** function $(1 - DI)^{-1}$, not an exponential function.
* **Concrete Fix (HOW):** Fit a generalized linear mixed-effects model (GLMM) with crossed random effects for patient, stimulating contact, and recording contact: `logit(P_ij) ~ Region_stim * Region_resp + distance + (1 | Patient) + (1 | Elec_stim) + (1 | Elec_resp)`. Replace `p = 0` with exact bounds or patient-level cluster-bootstrapped $p$-values, and correct "exponential" to "hyperbolic" in Line 133.
