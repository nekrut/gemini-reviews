## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript presents the "LeishDog Atlas," a 10X Genomics 3' scRNA-seq dataset of 68,323 peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and low-density granulocytes across 16 samples derived from 3 non-*Leishmania*-infected control (NLC) samples (two individual US dogs and one pooled sample of 3 UK laboratory beagles) and 13 US dogs naturally infected with *Leishmania infantum* via vertical transmission, staged cross-sectionally across LeishVet clinical stages LV1 ($n=2$), LV2 ($n=6$), LV3 ($n=3$), and LV4 ($n=2$).

### Primary Claims Audit
1. **Claim 1:** The LeishDog Atlas resolves 47 transcriptionally distinct canine peripheral immune cell subsets across B cell, T/NK cell, and myeloid lineages in healthy and *L. infantum*-infected dogs. `[Well Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Multi-level Seurat (`SCTransform` + anchor integration) clustering, canonical marker curation against `canFam6`, and cross-species reference mapping against human PBMC `Azimuth` annotations.
2. **Claim 2:** Across clinical CanL stages (LV1 to LV4), peripheral immune composition shifts significantly from T/NK cell predominance in early disease (LV1–LV2) to myeloid/monocyte dominance in advanced disease (LV3–LV4), alongside a significant decline in the $\text{CD4}^+ : \text{CD8}^+$ T cell ratio. `[Fragile]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* The authors fit a mixed-effects logistic regression (`glmer`), which suffered a **singular fit** (zero random-effect variance), reducing the model to an unclustered fixed-effects binomial GLM that treats $N = 68,323$ individual cells—rather than $N = 16$ dogs (with $n=2$ in LV1 and $n=2$ in LV4)—as independent Bernoulli trials. This inflates statistical degrees of freedom by $>4,000$-fold and invalidates all reported $p$-values and 95% confidence intervals in Figures 1H and 3E (Table S3).
3. **Claim 3:** Within individual B, $\text{CD4}^+$ T, $\text{CD8}^+$ T, and myeloid subpopulations, the transcript abundance of cluster-defining top-50 signature DEGs fluctuates non-monotonically across LeishVet disease stages (e.g., peaking at LV2/LV3 or LV4) independently of changes in cell frequency. `[Methodologically Flawed]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Computing the average expression of the 50 most significant cluster-marker DEGs across disease stages ($k=5$ stages with only $n=2$ dogs in LV1 and LV4), row-scaling each gene to a Z-score across the 5 stage averages, and comparing against a bootstrap ribbon of 50 random non-signature genes ignores patient-level biological variance and is heavily driven by sample-level batch effects in small-$n$ stages ($n=2$).
4. **Claim 4:** Monocytes dynamically modulate their inflammatory status across CanL progression, with pro-inflammatory monocytes peaking at LV1, declining at LV2/LV3 as anti-inflammatory monocytes rise, and undergoing a terminal "pro-inflammatory burst" at LV4. `[Fragile]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Module score (`AddModuleScore`) thresholding of curated gene lists plotted across stages without dog-level biological error bars or statistical testing at the animal level ($n=2$ dogs at LV1 and $n=2$ dogs at LV4).

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Catastrophic Pseudoreplication via Singular Binomial GLMM in Compositional Analysis
In the Star Methods ("Mixed-effects logistic regression analysis", second part, lines 15–21), the authors specify the `lme4::glmer` formula:
$$\texttt{cbind(frequency, total\_freq - frequency)} \sim 1 + \texttt{LV.stage} * \texttt{cellgroup} + (1 \mid \texttt{orig.ident})$$
Look at what this model actually fits:
1. For a given dog (`orig.ident`), $\sum_{\text{cellgroup}} \text{frequency} = \text{total\_freq}$. Thus, the sum of proportions across the three major cell groups (T/NK, B, Myeloid) within every dog is identically $1.0$. Because every dog has the exact same total proportion across the three rows, the dog-level random intercept `(1 | orig.ident)` **cannot capture compositional variation between dogs within a cell type** (which would require a random slope `(0 + cellgroup | orig.ident)` or an observation-level random effect `(1 | row_id)` to model overdispersion!).
2. Consequently, as the authors themselves report on line 21: *"In our datasets, the estimated variance of the random intercept approached zero (singular fit)... Consequently, the model behaves equivalently to a fixed-effects binomial GLM, and coefficients were interpreted as population-level effects."*
3. When a binomial GLM is fit to cell counts without an observation-level/sample-level random effect per cell type, **the model assumes every single cell ($N = 68,323$) is an independent binomial trial**, completely ignoring inter-dog biological variance! With $n=2$ dogs in LV1, $n=2$ dogs in LV4, and $n=3$ samples in NLC, treating 10,000+ cells per group as independent samples shrinks standard errors by $\sqrt{N_{\text{cells}} / N_{\text{dogs}}} \approx 65\times$, guaranteeing minuscule false-positive $p$-values for any inter-dog variability. Proper compositional analysis of single-cell data with $n=2\text{--}6$ biological replicates per group requires either a Dirichlet-multinomial / beta-binomial model (`scCODA`, `speckle::propeller` logit/arcsine-transformed linear models where $N = 16$ dogs) or an observation-level random intercept `(1 | sample_celltype)` in `glmer`.

### 2.2 Inverted QC Filtering Thresholds Reported in Methods (`Log10GenesPerUMI > 0.83` and `percent.mt < 5%` Excluded)
In the Star Methods ("Processing scRNA-seq data", first part, line 141), the authors state:
> *"Cells with a Log10GenesPerUMI (log10(nFeature_RNA) / log10(nCount_RNA)) of >0.83, and mitochondrial/total RNA expression (percent.mt) of <5% were excluded."*

In standard scRNA-seq quality control, high library complexity (`Log10GenesPerUMI > 0.80`) and low mitochondrial transcript leakage (`percent.mt < 5%`) are the **healthy, high-quality cells** that are **retained**, whereas low-complexity droplets (`Log10GenesPerUMI < 0.80`) and dying/lysed cells (`percent.mt > 5%` or `> 10%`) are excluded! Either the authors inverted both inequality signs (`>` vs `<`, "excluded" vs "retained") in the manuscript text, or the pipeline discarded all healthy intact cells and retained only high-mtRNA apoptotic/lysed cells. Given the downstream biological markers, this is almost certainly a severe reporting inversion in the Methods text, which must be corrected alongside publishing the exact per-sample `nCount_RNA` and `nFeature_RNA` thresholds.

### 2.3 Confounding of the NLC Reference Baseline by Geography, Breed, and Sample Pooling
The 13 infected dogs and 2 of the NLC dogs (`NLC2PBMC`, `NLC3PBMC`) are privately owned US dogs (predominantly Foxhounds where vertical transmission of *L. infantum* is enzootic in the US, living in varied home environments with potential tick-borne exposures). The third control sample (`NLC1PBMC`) is a **pooled sample of 3 laboratory beagles from the UK (ENVIGO)** (Methods line 111). Thus, one-third of the control group differs simultaneously in breed (Beagle vs. US hounds), country of origin (UK vs. US), husbandry (SPF laboratory facility vs. private homes), and technical structure (a 3-dog biological pool vs. single-dog libraries, without genetic demultiplexing via `souporcell`/`demuxlet` to separate the 3 beagles or remove inter-individual doublets).

### 2.4 Flawed Z-Score Bootstrap Benchmark for Signature Gene Expression Across Stages
In Figures 4H, 5H, and 6H (Methods second part, line 30), the authors take the 50 top cluster-defining DEGs, compute their `AverageExpression` across all cells pooled by stage (NLC, LV1–LV4), transform each gene across the 5 stages into a Z-score (mean 0, SD 1 across the 5 points), and plot the mean $\pm$ SD of the 50 Z-scores against a 95% confidence ribbon from bootstrapping 50 random non-signature genes.
1. Because the 50 signature genes were selected specifically because they are **co-regulated markers of that cell state**, their expression across samples is strongly positively correlated (high inter-gene covariance), whereas 50 randomly drawn genes from the genome are uncorrelated (so their stage-wise Z-scores cancel out by the Central Limit Theorem, yielding a narrow grey ribbon near 0).
2. Thus, exceeding the random-gene bootstrap ribbon does **not** prove non-random stage-specific biological regulation; any set of 50 co-regulated genes (e.g., ribosomal genes or MHC class I genes) that varies across individual dogs will move in unison across the $n=2$ dogs in LV1 or LV4 and exceed an uncorrelated random-gene null! Dog-level variation must be tested by treating the **dog** ($N=16$) as the unit of replication.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Manual Marker-Based Doublet Removal Instead of Computational Doublet Detection:**
   - Doublets were removed only by manual inspection of canonical marker co-expression at Level 2/3 subclustering (`Sc2`, line 166), rather than running standard computational doublet callers (`scDblFinder`, `DoubletFinder`, or `Scrublet`) on each 10X library prior to integration—especially critical for `NLC1PBMC` where 3 dogs were pooled into a single lane at ~40,000 cells/mL.
2. **Monocle3 Trajectory Inference on Integrated UMAP Coordinates:**
   - Running `Monocle3::learn_graph` directly on `SCTransform`-integrated 2D UMAP coordinates (Methods second part, line 35) rather than diffusion maps or unintegrated PCA space is prone to UMAP-topology distortion, particularly when forcing `CD8_TPEX` into a linear developmental path without TCR clonotype sharing (`scTCR-seq`).
3. **Typos in Text:**
   - Line 16: `IFNγ-producmg` ($\to$ `producing`).
   - Line 26: `GP93` ($\to$ `GP9`).
   - Methods second part line 1: `TEBB1` ($\to$ `TUBB1`).

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Replace the Singular Binomial GLMM with Proper Sample-Level Compositional Testing:**
   - Re-analyze cell-type proportions across NLC and LV1–LV4 treating the **individual dog ($N = 16$)** as the statistical unit of replication—for example, using `speckle::propeller` (empirical Bayes moderated ANOVA on logit/arcsine-transformed sample proportions), `scCODA`, or a beta-binomial / observation-level random-effect model `(1 | sample_id:cellgroup)`. Update Figures 1H, 3E, and Table S3 with valid dog-level $p$-values and confidence intervals.
2. **Dog-Level Pseudobulk Analysis of Signature and Inflammatory Module Scores:**
   - In Figures 4H, 5H, 6H, and 6I–J, compute mean signature scores and pro-/anti-inflammatory monocyte proportions **per dog ($N=16$)** and plot individual dog data points across stages (NLC, LV1–LV4) with dog-level non-parametric tests (e.g., Kruskal-Wallis / Jonckheere-Terpstra trend test across ordered clinical stages) rather than pooling cells by stage and bootstrapping uncorrelated random genes.
3. **Genetically Demultiplex the Pooled UK Beagle Control (`NLC1PBMC`) and Run `scDblFinder`:**
   - Use SNP-based demultiplexing (`souporcell` or `vireo`) on the BAM file of `NLC1PBMC` to separate the 3 pooled UK beagles and identify inter-dog heterotypic/homotypic doublets, and verify that NLC vs. CanL conclusions hold when restricting controls to the US-owned NLC dogs.
4. **Correct the QC Threshold Description and Release Code Publicly:**
   - Fix the inverted inequality description for `Log10GenesPerUMI` and `percent.mt` in the Methods, report exact per-sample filtering cutoffs in Table S1, and make the GitHub repository (`DanHolbrook/LeishDog_Atlas`) and GEO series (`GSE313451`) publicly accessible.
