## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript extends Felber's (1991) panmictic autotetraploid establishment model by tracking haploid ($x_t$), diploid ($y_t$), and triploid ($z_t$) gamete frequencies across discrete generations, incorporating triploid relative fitness ($\phi \le 1$) and triploid meiotic gamete-ploidy proportions ($\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$), while assuming tetraploids also produce unreduced $4x$ gametes at rate $\upsilon$. The authors show analytically that when $\phi = 0$, the saddle-node bifurcation threshold for tetraploid takeover increases from Felber's $\approx 17.1\%$ to $\upsilon_{\max} = 1/5 = 20\%$, and demonstrate numerically that $\upsilon_{\max}$ decays approximately exponentially as $\phi$ increases. They then embed these rules into a 2D spatially explicit individual-based model (IBM) on a $100 \times 100$ lattice with mating/dispersal radius $R$, and finally add a 100-bit binary chromosome matching model ($\psi = e^{-d_{\min}}$) across a $200 \times 100$ linear gradient and a gridded WorldClim (Bio12) precipitation map of Australia to model the *Neobatrachus* autopolyploid complex. Java simulation code is deposited on GitHub.

While the analytical insight that spatial structure and low edge density reduce local effective population size—allowing drift and local mating neighborhoods to overcome minority cytotype exclusion—is ecologically useful, the manuscript suffers from several mathematical, biological, and implementation flaws. Most critically, the eco-evolutionary simulation is framed as a strictly "neutral" null model with respect to ploidy adaptive potential, yet the fitness function $\psi = e^{-d_{\min}}$ (where $d_{\min}$ is the minimum Hamming distance between the environmental target $E(x,y)$ and any of the individual's $p$ chromosomes) confers an intrinsic mutational-buffering and order-statistic advantage to tetraploids ($p=4$) over diploids ($p=2$). In addition, the spatial simulations rely on a biologically unrealistic triploid bridge ($\phi = 0.30$ with $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 1$, meaning $30\%$ triploid fertility composed entirely of euploid $1x, 2x, 3x$ gametes), all numbered display equations (Eqs. 1–5 and NND) are omitted from the manuscript text, and multiple geometric/algebraic formulas in the text contain factor-of-2 or sign errors.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: In a panmictic population without viable triploids ($\phi = 0$) where tetraploids also produce unviable unreduced gametes at rate $\upsilon$, the critical unreduced gamete rate for tetraploid fixation is $\upsilon_{\max} = 1/5 = 0.20$.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Under the authors' normalization denominator $x_t^2 + 2\phi x_t y_t + (y_t^2 + 2x_t z_t)(1-\upsilon)$, setting $\phi = 0$ and $z_t = 0$ yields the 1D map $x_{t+1} = (1-\upsilon)x_t^2 / [x_t^2 + (1-\upsilon)(1-x_t)^2]$. Solving the tangency conditions $x_{t+1}(x) = x$ and $\frac{dx_{t+1}}{dx} = 1$ yields $x = 1/3$ and $\upsilon_{\max} = 1/5 = 0.20$. However, see Defect 1 regarding the asymmetric treatment of pre- vs. post-syngamy gamete culling in this denominator.
* **Claim 2: Increasing triploid fitness ($\phi$) and reducing local population size ($N$ or mating radius $R$) lower the threshold $\upsilon_{\max}$ required for polyploid establishment.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  Qualitatively, a viable triploid bridge ($\phi > 0$) and demographic stochasticity in small mating neighborhoods ($R \ll L$) facilitate local polyploid fixation and spatial clustering (Figs. 1B and 2). However, the quantitative threshold reduction reported in the main text ($\upsilon_{\max} \approx 1.8\%$ at $\phi = 0.30$) and used in all spatial simulations ($\upsilon = 10^{-3}, \phi = 0.30$) assumes that 100% of gametes produced by fertile triploids are euploid ($\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 0.25 + 0.25 + 0.50 = 1.0$), ignoring the overwhelming aneuploid gamete wastage ($>90\%$) characteristic of natural autotriploid meiosis.
* **Claim 3: In the absence of ploidy-specific adaptive differences, polyploids emerge at range expansion fronts and monopolize novel niches purely through ecological drift and priority effects.** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  In the eco-evolutionary IBM (Results line 55; Methods Part 2 line 3), individual reproductive success is defined as $\psi = e^{-d_{\min}}$, where $d_{\min} = \min_{i \in \{1,\dots,p\}} d_H(B_i, E(x,y))$ is the smallest Hamming distance among the individual's $p$ chromosomes. Because tetraploids possess $p=4$ chromosomes and diploids possess $p=2$, tetraploids can maintain one locally adapted chromosome ($d_H = 0, \psi = 1$) while accumulating mutations across three spare homologous chromosomes (versus one in diploids) without fitness penalty, and take the minimum of four distances rather than two when dispersing into adjacent gradient cells. Thus, the simulation does not isolate ploidy-neutral ecological drift; it embeds a built-in genetic buffering and evolvability advantage for tetraploids.
* **Claim 4: The spatially explicit neutral model reproduces the phylogeographic radiation and precipitation niche divergence of Australian *Neobatrachus* burrowing frogs.** `[Overstated]`  
  In Fig. 4C, the empirical Normalized Niche Divergence (NND) between diploid and autotetraploid *Neobatrachus* occurrences is $2.22\%$, whereas the simulated average NND is $8.75\%$ (nearly fourfold higher), no formal statistical test comparing the empirical value to the null distribution is reported, the text contradicts the figure legend on the number of simulation replicates (1,000 in line 64 vs. 100 in the Fig. 4C legend, line 67), and pooling raw GBIF occurrence points across five diploid and three tetraploid species ignores spatial sampling bias and species-level phylogenetic non-independence.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Hidden Ploidy-Dependent Fitness Advantage in the "Ploidy-Neutral" Eco-Evolutionary Model (Pillar 3 & Pillar 5)
* **Defect:** The central thesis of the paper is that polyploid niche divergence and range-edge monopolization emerge without any intrinsic adaptive or genetic advantage of polyploidy (Abstract; Results line 53: *"individual fitness (independent of ploidy level)"*). Yet in Methods (Part 2, line 3), the probability of reproduction is defined as $\psi = e^{-d_{\min}}$, where $d_{\min}$ is the smallest Hamming distance between any single chromosome $B_i \in \Lambda_p$ ($i \in \{1,\dots,p\}$) and the local 100-bit environmental string $E(x,y)$, coupled with bivalent pairing of two randomly selected chromosomes during meiosis. Under this "chromosome-level dominance" rule, a tetraploid ($p=4$) has three masked chromosomes free to explore sequence space via mutation ($\mu = 5 \times 10^{-5}$) and recombination ($m = 0.5$) while a single conserved chromosome preserves maximum fitness ($\psi = 1$), whereas a diploid ($p=2$) has only one masked chromosome. Furthermore, for any distribution of chromosome distances, the minimum of $p=4$ draws is stochastically smaller than the minimum of $p=2$ draws. Consequently, tetraploids enjoy a higher rate of beneficial adaptation along the environmental gradient in Figs. 3 and 4.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** A model where $p=4$ provides twice the chromosomal targets and three times the masked mutational capacity under a $\min_{i=1\dots p} d_i$ fitness function cannot be used to claim that adaptation and niche monopolization occur independently of ploidy-level genetic architecture.
  * **WHAT:** Re-run the linear gradient (Fig. 3) and *Neobatrachus* (Fig. 4) simulations under a truly ploidy-invariant genetic architecture.
  * **HOW:** Either (i) decouple environmental adaptation from chromosome copy number by evolving a single quantitative niche trait $z_{\text{ind}}$ with identical mutational variance $\sigma_\mu^2$ in diploids, triploids, and tetraploids, or (ii) define fitness using the mean chromosomal distance $\bar{d} = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^p d_H(B_i, E(x,y))$ (additive dosage) and compare it directly against the $d_{\min}$ dominance model to quantify how much of the range-front monopolization in Fig. 3B is driven by ecological drift versus chromosomal dominance buffering.

### 2. Asymmetric Pre- vs. Post-Syngamy Gamete Culling and Biologically Inflated Triploid Bridge (Pillar 3 & Pillar 6)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Inconsistent life-cycle culling in Eqs. 1–3:* In line 27, the total normalizing denominator for the gamete pool at $t+1$ is given as $x_t^2 + 2\phi x_t y_t + \{y_t^2 + 2x_t z_t\}(1-\upsilon)$. By excluding the fraction $\upsilon$ of unreduced ($4x$) gametes produced by tetraploids from the gamete pool *prior* to syngamy, the model assumes $4x$ gametes never fuse with $1x, 2x,$ or $3x$ gametes. Conversely, triploid ($3x$) gametes $z_t$ *are* placed into the gamete pool $(x_t + y_t + z_t)^2$, and zygotes above $4x$ ($2y_t z_t + z_t^2$) are culled *after* syngamy. If $4x$ gametes ($w_t$) participate in random syngamy like $z_t$ and zygotes $>4x$ die post-syngamy, $4x$ gametes waste haploid and diploid gametes ($2x_t w_t + 2y_t w_t$), altering the recursion and invasion threshold.
  2. *Unrealistic euploid triploid output:* Setting $\lambda_1 = 0.25, \lambda_2 = 0.25, \lambda_3 = 0.50$ ($\sum \lambda_i = 1.0$) with $\phi = 0.30$ in Figs. 2–4 assumes that triploids have $30\%$ of the total viable euploid gamete output of diploids, and that half of all gametes produced by triploids are fully unreduced $3x$ gametes. In empirical autopolyploid systems, random chromosome segregation in triploids produces $(1/2)^k$ euploid $1x$ or $2x$ gametes (for base chromosome number $k$), so total euploid fertility $\phi \sum \lambda_i$ is typically $\ll 0.05$.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Mixing pre-syngamy gametic selection for $4x$ gametes with post-syngamy zygotic selection for $3x$ gametes is mathematically inconsistent, and setting $\phi = 0.30$ with 100% euploid gametes overstates the triploid bridge.
  * **WHAT:** Harmonize the syngamy recursion so all inviable ploidies ($>4x$) are culled at the same life-cycle stage (zygotic selection), and evaluate spatial establishment at empirically realistic effective triploid euploid fertilities ($\phi_{\text{eff}} \in [0, 0.05]$).
  * **HOW:** Include tetraploid gametes $w_t$ in the syngamy expansion $(x_t + y_t + z_t + w_t)^2$ and normalize cytotypes by the viable zygote sum $x_t^2 + 2\phi x_t y_t + y_t^2 + 2x_t z_t$, and report a sensitivity analysis of Fig. 2C and Fig. 3B for $\phi = 0, 0.01,$ and $0.05$.

### 3. Geometric, Algebraic, and Sign Errors in Spatial Neighborhood and Mating Formulas (Pillar 3 & Pillar 7)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Neighborhood size formula:* Results line 41 states: *"The neighborhood size, i.e., the number of grids cells around the seeker, is simply $(2R-1)^2$."* In contrast, Methods (Part 2, line 1) states $(2R+1)^2$. For a Moore neighborhood of radius $R$ on a 2D square lattice, the number of grid cells is $(2R+1)^2$ (including the seeker's cell) or $(2R+1)^2 - 1$ (excluding it). For $R=1$ (used in Figs. 3 and 4), $(2R-1)^2 = 1$, which is incorrect.
  2. *Disc area formula:* Results line 46 states that on a continuous 2D landscape of density $\rho$, a seeker in the interior has $2\rho \pi R^2$ mating partners and a seeker at a corner has $0.5\rho \pi R^2$ partners ("since the disc sector is exactly $1/4$ of the disc"). The area of a disc of radius $R$ is $\pi R^2$, not $2\pi R^2$, so the expected counts are $\rho \pi R^2$ and $0.25\rho \pi R^2$ (or $\rho(2R+1)^2$ and $\rho(R+1)^2$ on the discrete Moore lattice).
  3. *Inverted Hamming distance mating rule:* Methods (Part 2, line 3) states: *"a successful mating event happens when the Hamming distance between two randomly selected chromosomes is at least 95%, which drives speciation and spatial clustering of genetically similar individuals."* Because Hamming distance counts the number of *mismatched* bits out of 100, a Hamming distance of $\ge 95\%$ means the two individuals are $\ge 95\%$ *different*. The authors meant sequence identity $\ge 95\%$ (i.e., Hamming distance $d_H \le 5$).
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Incorrect mathematical formulas in the text confuse readers and obscure what was actually coded in the Java implementation.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Replace $(2R-1)^2$ with $(2R+1)^2$ in line 41; replace $2\rho \pi R^2$ and $0.5\rho \pi R^2$ with $\rho \pi R^2$ and $0.25\rho \pi R^2$ in line 46; and correct "Hamming distance... is at least 95%" to "Hamming distance is at most 5% (i.e., sequence similarity of at least 95%)" in Methods (Part 2, line 3).

### 4. Omitted Display Equations, Replicate Count Discrepancy, and Uncalibrated Empirical Comparison (Pillar 4 & Pillar 7)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Missing equations:* All five numbered equations in the Results (Eqs. 1–3 on line 26, Eq. 4 on line 30, Eq. 5 on line 35) and the Normalized Niche Divergence (NND) formula in Methods (Part 2, line 6) are blank in the manuscript text.
  2. *Replicate discrepancy:* Results line 64 states that niche divergence was measured *"across 1,000 independent simulations,"* whereas the Fig. 4C legend (line 67) states *"Data from simulations are the result of 100 independent runs of the models."*
  3. *Uncalibrated empirical test:* In Fig. 4C, the empirical NND ($2.22\%$) is compared visually to the simulated NND distribution (mean $8.75\%$) without reporting a $p$-value, quantile position, or confidence interval, and without accounting for spatial autocorrelation in GBIF records. Also, Part 2 lines 9–11 and 42–44 contain duplicated movie legends with typos ("leA-most corner", "southwestern corn of the country").
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** A mathematical biology paper cannot be evaluated or reproduced when its governing equations are missing from the text and simulation replicate counts conflict.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Restore the full LaTeX expressions for Eqs. 1–5 and the NND equation in the text; reconcile whether Fig. 4C represents $N=100$ or $N=1,000$ runs; report the exact empirical percentile and two-sided $p$-value of the observed *Neobatrachus* NND ($2.22\%$) within the simulated distribution; and remove the duplicate movie captions and typos ("left-most", "southwestern corner").
