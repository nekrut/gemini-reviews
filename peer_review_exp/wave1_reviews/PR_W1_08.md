## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This quantitative genetics study evaluates pedigree-based (BLUP, `A`-matrix) versus marker-based (GBLUP, VanRaden `G`-matrix) variance components, forward genomic prediction accuracies, and prediction biases across seven growth, welfare, and feather traits—juvenile weight (`JW`), slaughter body weight (`BW`), ultrasound breast depth (`BD`), primary feather length (`PRF`), gait score (`GAIT`, 1–5 ordinal scale), average daily gain (`ADG`), and footpad dermatitis (`FPD`, 1–5 ordinal scale)—in two commercial Pekin duck lines (Line A: $N=13,645$ spanning Generations 1–6; Line D: $N=13,020$ spanning Generations 1–5). Animals were genotyped on a 427-SNP parentage panel and imputed (`AlphaImpute`) to a 60K SNP chip (~47.2K and 46.3K SNPs post-QC) using training reference sets of 1,767 (Line A) and 1,536 (Line D) animals. Using `ASReml`, the authors pre-correct phenotypes for fixed effects (`sexhatch`, `age of dam`, and for `BW`/`BD` also `feeding pen`), fit univariate animal models (`A` vs. `G`), and evaluate forward prediction into the final generation (Gen 1–5 $\rightarrow$ Gen 6 in Line A; Gen 1–4 $\rightarrow$ Gen 5 in Line D). They conclude that GBLUP consistently achieves higher prediction accuracies ($0.49\text{–}0.73$) and lower prediction biases than pedigree BLUP, especially for low-heritability welfare traits (`GAIT` $h^2 \approx 0.06\text{–}0.07$).

Despite the substantial commercial sample size (~26.6K ducks), the manuscript contains major statistical and algebraic errors in how prediction accuracy and bias are defined, computed, and reported in Tables 4 and 5, alongside severe inconsistencies between the text, tables, and equations:
1. In Tables 4 and 5, the column labeled **`r^2`** and the column labeled **`acc`** satisfy the exact algebraic identity **`r^2 = acc * sqrt(h^2)`** (for example, in Table 4 Line A `JW` G-matrix: $h^2 = 0.25 \implies \sqrt{h^2} = 0.50$, `acc` $= 0.49$, and `r^2` $= 0.24 = 0.49 \times 0.50$; for `BW` G-matrix: $h^2 = 0.32 \implies \sqrt{h^2} = 0.566$, `acc` $= 0.68$, and `r^2` $= 0.39 = 0.68 \times 0.566$!). That means the column labeled `r^2` is **not** a squared correlation ($r^2$), but the **unsquared Pearson correlation $r_{gy} = \text{corr}(\text{PGEBV}, y^*)$**, which the authors then misreport in the Abstract (line 50) and Results (line 261) as $r^2 = 0.68\text{–}0.70$ and $r^2 = 0.49$ (confusing `acc` with $r^2$!).
2. Simultaneously, the footnote to Tables 4 and 5 (lines 282, 305) defines `Accuracy (acc) = corr(EBV_no_pheno, TBV_with_pheno) / sqrt(h^2)`, which divides the correlation with *estimated breeding values* (rather than phenotypes $y^*$) by $\sqrt{h^2}$—a well-known invalid formula that double-corrects for heritability and can exceed $1.0$.
3. Pre-correcting phenotypes for contemporary group (`sexhatch` and `feeding pen`) across the *entire* dataset (including the validation generation, lines 134–175) before masking validation phenotypes leaks validation-generation phenotypic means into the pre-corrected residuals $y^*$.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1: Imputation from the 427-SNP low-density parentage panel to the 60K medium-density array achieves 0.93 accuracy in Line A and 0.92 in Line D.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  Masking 200 high-density animals per line yields mean concordance/correlation of 0.92–0.93 (with lower accuracy at telomeres and on the Z chromosome, Fig. 1). However, the Methods (line 125) do not state whether the 200 masked validation ducks were sampled from the latest generation (Gen 5/6) or randomly across generations, nor whether imputation accuracy was computed as allele dosage Pearson correlation ($r$) or raw genotype concordance rate (which is inflated by common alleles).
* **Claim 2: Discrete 1–5 ordinal welfare scores (`GAIT` and `FPD`) adhere to normality across both lines (`skewness` and `kurtosis` within $\pm 0.5$), validating Gaussian linear mixed modeling.** `[Overstated]`  
  In Results (line 218), the authors claim that 6 of 7 traits display "excellent adherence to normality" based on skewness and kurtosis, ignoring their own **Tests for Normality** columns in Table 1 (lines 97–115), where `GAIT` and `FPD` exhibit massive Kolmogorov-Smirnov ($0.29\text{–}0.35$), Cramer-von Mises ($176\text{–}341$), and Anderson-Darling ($906\text{–}1606$) test statistics rejecting normality due to discrete 5-point ordinal concentration.
* **Claim 3: GBLUP yields high prediction accuracies (reported as $r^2 = 0.68\text{–}0.70$ for `BW` and $0.58$ for `GAIT` in Line A; $0.71$ for `BD` in Line D) compared to pedigree BLUP.** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  The numbers in the text and Tables 4–5 are internally contradictory: (a) the Abstract (line 50) and Results (line 284) state that in Line D, `BD` accuracy improved **"from 0.50 to 0.71 using the G-matrix,"** yet Table 5 (line 298) reports Line D `BD` G-matrix **`acc = 0.50`** and A-matrix **`acc = 0.28`** (the number `0.71` does not appear anywhere in Table 5 for `BD`; it only appears in Table 3 line 251 as the ratio $h^2_G / h^2_A = 0.15 / 0.21 = 0.71$!); (b) the column `r^2` in Tables 4–5 is actually the unsquared correlation $r_{gy} = \text{acc} \times \sqrt{h^2}$; and (c) the text in line 197 vs. table footnotes in lines 282/305 give two incompatible definitions of `acc` ($r(PGEBV, y^*)/\sqrt{h^2}$ vs. $r(EBV_{\text{no\_pheno}}, TBV_{\text{with\_pheno}})/\sqrt{h^2}$).
* **Claim 4: GBLUP substantially reduces prediction bias ($\tilde{b}$) compared to pedigree BLUP across traits.** `[Fragile]`  
  In line 205, bias is computed by regressing "True" EBV (`TBV_with_pheno`, i.e., EBVs estimated from the full dataset) on `EBV_no_pheno`. Regressing full-data EBVs from an `A`-matrix model on partial-data EBVs from an `A`-matrix model (or `G` on `G`) when variance components are re-estimated or when pre-corrected residuals $y^*$ are used instead of the Legarra-Reverter (`LR`) joint mixed-model equations confounds Mendelian sampling shrinkage with dispersion bias.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

1. **Mathematical Contradictions and Mislabeling of $r_{gy}$, $r^2$, and `acc` in Text and Tables 4–5:**  
   Let us audit the exact numbers in Table 4 (Line A, lines 273–279) and Table 5 (Line D, lines 296–302):
   * Across every single row of Tables 4 and 5, the column labeled **`r^2`** equals **`acc` $\times \sqrt{h^2}$**:
     - Line A `JW` (G): $0.49 \times \sqrt{0.25} = 0.49 \times 0.50 = 0.245 \rightarrow \mathbf{0.24}$
     - Line A `BW` (G): $0.68 \times \sqrt{0.32} = 0.68 \times 0.5657 = 0.385 \rightarrow \mathbf{0.39}$
     - Line A `GAIT` (G): $0.58 \times \sqrt{0.06} = 0.58 \times 0.2449 = 0.142 \rightarrow \mathbf{0.14}$
     - Line A `GAIT` (A): $0.24 \times \sqrt{0.08} = 0.24 \times 0.2828 = 0.068 \rightarrow \mathbf{0.07}$
     - Line D `BW` (G): $0.69 \times \sqrt{0.40} = 0.69 \times 0.6325 = 0.436 \rightarrow \mathbf{0.44}$
   * By Legarra et al. [60] (cited in line 197), $\text{acc} = r_{gy} / \sqrt{h^2}$, which means $r_{gy} = \text{acc} \times \sqrt{h^2}$. Thus, the column header **`r^2`** in Tables 4 and 5 is **not $r^2$ (the coefficient of determination)**, but the **unsquared correlation $r_{gy}$**!
   * Even worse, in the Abstract (line 50) and Results (line 261), the authors take the values from the **`acc`** column (`0.68` and `0.49`) and write them in the text as **`r^2 = 0.68–0.70`** and **`r^2 of 0.49`**.
   * Furthermore, the footnote to Tables 4 and 5 (lines 282, 305) states: `Where Accuracy (acc) = corr(EBV_no_pheno, TBV_with_pheno) / sqrt(h^2)`, which contradicts line 197 (`corr(PGEBV, y*) / sqrt(h^2)`). If `corr(EBV_no_pheno, TBV_with_pheno)` was divided by $\sqrt{h^2}$, the accuracy estimates are upwardly biased because `TBV_with_pheno` is already shrunk by reliability $r^2_{\text{EBV}} < 1$.
2. **Phantom Number (`0.71`) for Line D Breast Depth (`BD`) in Abstract and Results:**  
   In the Abstract (line 50), the authors write: *"Similar improvements were found for line D, with BD estimates improving from 0.50 to 0.71 using the G-matrix."* In the Results (line 284), they write: *"The estimates for BD were similar in the two lines, however, the use of slaughter body weight greatly improved the G-Matrix estimates from 0.50 to 0.71 unlike in line A where they remained at 0.50."* Yet in Table 5 (Line D, line 298), `BD` has **`acc = 0.50`** (`G-Matrix`) and **`acc = 0.28`** (`A-Matrix`)—identical to Line A (`0.50` vs. `0.26` in Table 4, line 275)! No model with slaughter body weight as a covariate for `BD` in Line D is shown in Table 5, and `0.71` is the $h^2_G / h^2_A$ ratio for `BD` in Table 3 (line 251).
3. **Missing Equations (Blank Table Cells) and Ambiguous Covariate Specification in Methods:**  
   In the Statistical Analysis section (lines 138–169, 201, 209), **all six mathematical equations (Equations 1, 2, 3, 4, the Legarra accuracy equation, and the Lipschutz-Powell bias equation) are completely missing** (rendered as empty markdown table cells `|  | 1 |`). Furthermore, line 150 says *"y_ijk is phenotype for BW and BD. S=sexhatch, A=age of dam and P=feeding pen. We also fitted BW as a covariate."* Fitting `BW` (slaughter body weight) as a covariate when `BW` itself is the response variable $y_{ijk}$ is impossible (a variable cannot be regressed on itself); presumably `BW` was only fitted as a covariate for `BD` (or `BD` was analyzed both with and without `BW`), which must be explicitly written out.
4. **Two-Step Pre-Correction Data Leakage Across Training and Validation Generations:**  
   In lines 134–156 and 175, the authors first pre-correct all phenotypes using fixed-effect linear models (`sexhatch`, `age of dam`, `feeding pen`) and then mask the pre-corrected residuals $y^*$ of Generation 6 (Line A) or Generation 5 (Line D) for genomic prediction. Because `sexhatch` (hatch batch $\times$ sex) and `feeding pen` are contemporary group effects nested within generations, pre-correcting Generation 6 phenotypes using the Generation 6 `sexhatch` and `feeding pen` means requires observing the phenotypes of Generation 6 selection candidates! Worse still, if genetic trend exists across Generations 1 to 6, absorbing batch/generation effects into fixed `sexhatch` effects without simultaneously fitting the random animal genetic effect (`A` or `G`) removes the across-generation genetic trend and biases both $h^2$ and forward prediction accuracy.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

* **Contradictory Statements on Population Structure in Discussion:** Line 314 states within two consecutive sentences: *"Multidimensional plots revealed no discernible population structure, indicating a uniform genetic background within the lines. However, PCA plot shows two distinct lines from PC1 (Fig. 2)."* Moreover, Results line 220 states: *"In addition, the PCA also identified distinct clusters within each line separated by PC2 (Fig. 2)."* Thus, there *is* clear sub-structure within each line along PC2 (7.61% variance), directly contradicting line 314.
* **Inconsistent Gait Heritability Numbers Between Results and Discussion:** In Results (line 237 and Table 3, line 253), `GAIT` heritability is reported as **$0.07 \pm 0.01$** (G-matrix) and **$0.10 \pm 0.01$** (A-matrix) in both lines, and in Tables 4–5 as **$0.06 \pm 0.01$** and **$0.08\text{–}0.10 \pm 0.01\text{–}0.02$**. Yet in the Discussion (line 320), the authors write: *"The current estimates of heritability for gait were low (**0.06 ± 0.06 and 0.12 ± 0.06**)"*—numbers and standard errors that do not match any table in the paper.
* **Inconsistent Juvenile Weight (`JW`) and Slaughter Weight (`BW`) Heritability Summary in Abstract and Results:** In the Abstract (line 48), the authors write: *"juvenile weight (JW) heritability ranged from 0.22 in line A and 0.25 in line D using markers, to 0.39 and 0.50, respectively, using the pedigree."* Yet in Table 3 (lines 249–250), `JW` is **$0.23 \pm 0.01$** and **$0.25 \pm 0.01$** (G-matrix) and **$0.41 \pm 0.02$** and **$0.46 \pm 0.02$** (A-matrix), whereas `0.50 ± 0.02` is the A-matrix heritability for **`BW`** in Line D! This same mix-up between `JW` and `BW` is repeated in Results line 237.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Reconcile the Definition and Calculation of `r_{gy}`, `r^2`, and `acc` Across Text and Tables 4–5:**
   * **WHY:** Labeling $r_{gy} = \text{acc} \times \sqrt{h^2}$ as `r^2` in Tables 4–5, reporting `acc` as `r^2` in the Abstract/Results, and giving two incompatible formulas for `acc` in line 197 vs. lines 282/305 invalidates the quantitative benchmark.
   * **WHAT:** Clarify whether correlations were computed against pre-corrected phenotypes $y^*$ (`corr(PGEBV, y*)`) or against full-data EBVs (`corr(PGEBV, EBV_full)`), relabel the `r^2` column in Tables 4 and 5 to $r_{gy}$ (or square the values if reporting true $r^2$), and fix all references in the Abstract and Results.
   * **HOW:** Following Legarra & Reverter (2018, *Genet Sel Evol*, ref [63]), compute and report both the predictive correlation on adjusted phenotypes $r(PGEBV, y^*) / \sqrt{h^2}$ and the LR cross-validation estimator $\hat{\rho}_{w,p} = \frac{\text{cov}(\hat{u}_p, \hat{u}_w)}{\sqrt{\text{var}(\hat{u}_p)\,\text{var}(\hat{u}_w)}}$ and dispersion slope $b_{w,p} = \frac{\text{cov}(\hat{u}_w, \hat{u}_p)}{\text{var}(\hat{u}_p)}$, where $\hat{u}_p$ and $\hat{u}_w$ are estimated from the same model class.
2. **Fit Fixed Effects Jointly Inside the Single-Step / Animal Mixed Model (`ASReml`) Rather Than Pre-Correcting Across All Generations:**
   * **WHY:** Pre-correcting phenotypes for `sexhatch` and `feeding pen` without the random genetic effect absorbs genetic trends into contemporary groups, and pre-correcting validation animals uses their own contemporary group phenotypic means.
   * **WHAT:** Fit `sexhatch`, `age of dam`, and `feeding pen` directly as fixed effects inside the `ASReml` mixed model ($y = X\beta + Zu + e$).
   * **HOW:** With $N \approx 13,600$ records per line, `ASReml` easily solves a univariate animal model with fixed effects and a $13,600 \times 13,600$ `G`-matrix on a standard workstation; for validation, adjust $y_{\text{val}}$ using only fixed-effect levels estimable without validation phenotypes (or evaluate within-contemporary-group correlations $r(PGEBV, y - X\hat{\beta})$).
3. **Restore All Six Missing Equations and Correct All Text-to-Table Numerical Discrepancies:**
   * **WHY:** Equations 1–4 and the accuracy/bias formulas are blank in the manuscript text, and multiple numbers in the Abstract, Results, and Discussion contradict Tables 3–5.
   * **WHAT & HOW:**
     1. Insert the full LaTeX/math expressions for Equations 1–4 (lines 138–169) and the accuracy/bias formulas (lines 201, 209), explicitly distinguishing the model for `BW` from the model for `BD` with `BW` as a covariate.
     2. Reconcile the `0.71` Line D `BD` figure in the Abstract (line 50) and Results (line 284) with Table 5 (where `acc = 0.50`).
     3. Correct the `JW` vs. `BW` heritability numbers in the Abstract (line 48) and Results (line 237) to match Table 3 (`0.23, 0.25, 0.41, 0.46`).
     4. Correct the `GAIT` heritability values in Discussion line 320 (`0.06 ± 0.06 and 0.12 ± 0.06`) to match Table 3 (`0.07 ± 0.01 and 0.10 ± 0.01`), and harmonize the PCA within-line sub-cluster description between lines 220 and 314.
