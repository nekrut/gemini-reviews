## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This genome resource manuscript presents the first chromosome-scale de novo genome assembly and multi-tissue annotation of a male Ansell's mole-rat (*Fukomys anselli*, $2n=68$), a long-lived social subterranean rodent. Combining Oxford Nanopore R10.4.1 long reads ($157.41\text{ Gb}$, ~$46\times$ coverage after filtering), PCR-free Illumina NovaSeq X Plus short reads ($400.81\text{ Gb}$, $>175\times$), and Arima Hi-C data ($355.93\text{ Gb}$, $13.3\text{ million}$ unique inter-contig contacts), the authors assemble and manually curate a $2.27\text{ Gb}$ reference genome across 412 scaffolds ($\text{scaffold N50} = 72.4\text{ Mb}$, $\text{L50} = 12$; $\text{contig N50} = 19.8\text{ Mb}$), alongside an independently verified $17,006\text{ bp}$ circular mitochondrial genome. Using short-read RNA-seq across nine tissues together with OrthoDB v12 vertebrate and 41 rodent RefSeq proteomes in `BRAKER v3.0.8`, they annotate 18,256 protein-coding genes (29,094 transcripts) and 41.3% repetitive sequence (`Earl Grey v5.1.1`), achieving $99.54\%$ `compleasm` (`glires_odb10`) completeness and $92.35\%$ `OMArk` Hystricomorpha hierarchical orthogroup completeness.

The assembly and annotation pipeline is well designed and thoroughly benchmarked: the authors evaluated intermediate assembly states with `Merqury`, `QUAST`, and `compleasm` (appropriately omitting `Medaka` polishing when it degraded QV and k-mer completeness), cross-validated the enlarged mitochondrial control region using independent short-read (`GetOrganelle`) and long-read (`Flye`) assemblies, and contextualized their 41.3% repeat content against long-read vs. short-read Bathyergidae assemblies (`mHetGlaV3` at 40.4%). The primary limitations are: (1) an aggressive contamination filter that discarded 6,715 contigs (~140 Mb of sequence between the 2.41 Gb contig stage and 2.27 Gb scaffold stage) simply for returning "no BLASTn hits," which risks stripping rapidly evolving satellite/centromeric and Y-chromosome ampliconic sequences; (2) lack of explicit identification of the Y chromosome in a male ($XY$) assembly; and (3) omission of public accession numbers (NCBI BioProject/SRA/GenBank) from the manuscript text.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1: The hybrid ONT + Illumina + Hi-C assembly achieves chromosome-scale contiguity ($\text{N50} = 72.4\text{ Mb}$) with 34 major scaffolds ($>10\text{ Mb}$) representing 99% of the $2.27\text{ Gb}$ genome and matching the expected $n=34$ ($2n=68$) karyotype.** `[Well Supported]`  
  The 34 chromosome-scale scaffolds (>10 Mb) contain 99% of the assembled sequence, match the `GenomeScope2` k-mer estimate ($2.23\text{ Gb}$), and show 99.54% `compleasm` (`glires_odb10`) completeness and clear macro-synteny with *Fukomys damarensis*.
* **Claim 2: Scaffold 1 corresponds to the X chromosome, corroborated by conserved X-linked markers and male hemizygous read depth.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Synteny with *F. damarensis* scaffold `NW_022900951.1` (harboring *Ar*, *Dmd*, *Fmr1*, and *Mecp2*) and a 50% reduction in male sequencing depth (Figure S2d) provide strong orthogonal confirmation of the X chromosome.
* **Claim 3: The $17,006\text{ bp}$ *F. anselli* mitogenome is ~620–634 bp larger than those of *F. damarensis* and *H. glaber* due to an expanded control region rather than assembly error.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Independent de novo assembly from ONT reads spanning >70% of the mitogenome (`Flye` + `Pilon`) agreed with the short-read `GetOrganelle` assembly to within 1 bp across 17,006 bp, ruling out short-read repeat collapse or tandem duplication artifacts.
* **Claim 4: Repetitive elements comprise 41.3% of the *F. anselli* genome, reflecting improved repeat resolution of long-read over historical short-read mole-rat assemblies.** `[Well Supported]`  
  While short-read assemblies of *H. glaber* (`HetGla_female_1.0`, 28.9%) and *F. damarensis* (`DMR_v1.0_HiC`, 30.3%) missed ~10% of repetitive sequence, comparing *F. anselli* (41.3% total; 35.6% retroelements) against the long-read *H. glaber* assembly (`mHetGlaV3`, 40.4% total; 36.2% retroelements) confirms that the higher repeat fraction is driven by sequencing technology rather than lineage-specific repeat expansion.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

1. **Unvalidated Removal of 6,715 Contigs (~140 Mb) with "No BLASTn Hits" During Decontamination:**  
   In the Assembly Methods (line 61), the authors state: *"Contigs were removed as contamination if they returned no hits (6715 contigs) or were assigned to orders other than Rodentia and did not match any known mole-rat sequences (8 contigs)."* Next, in the Results (lines 95–97), the assembly size drops from **2.41 Gb across 7,581 contigs** to **2.27 Gb across 412 scaffolds**—a loss of **140 Mb** of sequence. In non-model mammalian genomes, contigs composed of species-specific satellite DNA, pericentromeric heterochromatin, subtelomeric repeats, or rapidly evolving Y-chromosome amplicons routinely yield "no hits" under standard `blastn` parameters against `nt`. Discarding all 6,715 "no-hit" contigs (rather than retaining those with expected rodent GC/coverage profiles and Hi-C contacts to the 34 chromosomes, and only removing contigs that *positively match* non-eukaryotic/microbial taxa or have anomalous coverage) risks purging ~140 Mb of genuine *F. anselli* heterochromatin and Y-chromosome sequence.
2. **Failure to Identify or Characterize the Y Chromosome in a Male ($XY$, $2n=68$) Assembly:**  
   In a male mammal with $2n=68$ chromosomes ($33\text{ autosome pairs} + X + Y$), there are **35 distinct nuclear chromosomes** (33 autosomes + X + Y), not 34. In line 101, the authors note that the **34 largest scaffolds** (>10 Mb) represent 99% of the genome and identify Scaffold 1 as the X chromosome via hemizygous coverage (Figure S2d), leaving 33 autosomal scaffolds. However, the manuscript never states which scaffold(s) represent the Y chromosome (or whether the Y chromosome was fragmented into the smaller 378 unplaced scaffolds or inadvertently removed among the 6,715 "no-hit" contigs during BLASTn filtering). Screening the assembly for conserved mammalian Y-linked genes (*Sry*, *Zfy*, *Uty*, *Ddx3y*, *Kdm5d*, *Eif2s3y*) and male-specific half-coverage scaffolds is essential for a male reference genome.
3. **Missing Data Availability Statement and Public Repository Accessions:**  
   The manuscript contains no Data Availability section and provides no NCBI BioProject, BioSample, SRA (for ONT, Illumina WGS, Hi-C, and 9-tissue RNA-seq), or GenBank accession numbers for the nuclear and mitochondrial assemblies, nor are the Merqury QV and error-rate numbers (mentioned in Methods line 70 as having been computed) stated in the Results text.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

* **Inconsistent Description of Skin RNA-Seq Anatomical Site:** Line 54 states that RNA was extracted from "abdomen skin tissue," whereas line 118 refers to "back skin."
* **`purge_dups` Restricted Only to Contig Ends:** Line 61 notes that *"Only haplotigs and overlaps at the end of contigs were selected for removal to avoid omitting false positive duplications in the middle of contigs."* It would be helpful to report the exact `compleasm` duplicated gene rate and `Merqury` k-mer multiplicity to confirm that no internal haplotigs remain.
* **Citation Formatting:** Line 125 cites "(personal communication from Radim Šumbera)"; if a taxonomic revision of *F. anselli* is underway, providing the museum voucher or colony provenance of the sequenced Duisburg-Essen individual (e.g., original geographic collection locality in Zambia) is critical so future comparative studies can link this reference genome to the exact karyotypic/taxonomic deme.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Audit the 6,715 Removed "No-Hit" Contigs and Identify Y-Chromosome Sequences:**
   * **WHY:** Removing 6,715 contigs (~140 Mb) solely because they lacked `blastn` hits risks deleting species-specific satellite repeats and Y-chromosome sequences, and a $2n=68$ male ($33\text{A} + X + Y$) has 35 distinct chromosomes.
   * **WHAT:** Query both the 412 retained scaffolds and the 6,715 removed "no-hit" contigs for conserved mammalian Y-chromosome genes (*Sry*, *Uty*, *Kdm5d*, *Ddx3y*, *Zfy*, *Rbmy*) using `tblastn`/`miniprot` and inspect their sequencing depth and Hi-C contact profiles.
   * **HOW:** Identify which scaffold(s) among the 412 scaffolds (or removed contigs) carry Y-linked markers and exhibit ~50% autosomal coverage ($~23\times$ ONT / $~88\times$ Illumina), and verify via `BlobToolKit` coverage-GC plots and Hi-C trans-contacts that the 6,715 removed contigs were not genuine *F. anselli* heterochromatic or Y-linked sequences.
2. **Report Exact `Merqury` QV, k-mer Completeness, and Duplicated `compleasm` Metrics in the Main Text:**
   * **WHY:** Methods line 70 states that `Merqury` consensus quality (QV), error rate, and k-mer completeness were computed, and Table 1/2 are cited in the text (lines 97–99), but the numerical QV and k-mer completeness values are omitted from the Results narrative.
   * **WHAT & HOW:** State the exact `Merqury` QV score, base error rate, k-mer completeness percentage, and `compleasm` Single vs. Duplicated percentages in the first two paragraphs of Results and Discussion (lines 95–99).
3. **Add a Complete Data Availability Section and Colony Provenance Details:**
   * **WHY:** A reference genome manuscript cannot be reused without public database accessions and specimen provenance.
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Add a Data Availability section listing the NCBI BioProject, SRA accessions for ONT, Illumina WGS, Hi-C, and all 9 RNA-seq tissues, GenBank/RefSeq assembly accession, mitochondrial accession, and Zenodo/Figshare links for the `BRAKER3` GFF3/FASTA annotation and `Earl Grey` repeat library, along with the geographic founder provenance of the Duisburg-Essen *F. anselli* colony. Also reconcile "abdomen skin" (line 54) vs. "back skin" (line 118).
