## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study examines how maternal behavioral unpredictability (quantified as the first-order Markov conditional Shannon entropy of transitions among five coded maternal sensory behaviors during a 5-min "No Toy" interaction at 2–6 months, $N = 255$) relates to infant visual orienting behavior (VOB; gaze return shifts to/from the mother), subsequent toddler inhibitory control on a 30-s glitter wand delay task at 19–28 months ($n = 194$), and concurrent infant gut shotgun metagenomic profiles ($n = 87$ after excluding one *Streptococcus mitis* outlier). The authors report that maternal unpredictability correlates positively with concurrent infant VOB at both Session 1 ($r = 0.35, p = 1 \times 10^{-8}$; age-residualized $r = 0.314$) and Session 2 ($r = 0.194, p = 0.005$, $n = 207$) without cross-lagged longitudinal correlation. In a linear model predicting toddler wait time ($n = 194$), they report a three-way interaction among Session 1 infant age, maternal unpredictability, and VOB ($F(1,185) = 4.056, p = 0.045$), where at older Session 1 ages (+1 SD $\approx 4.56$ months), maternal unpredictability negatively predicts wait time among low-VOB infants ($b = -1.027, p = 0.016$) but not high-VOB infants ($b = +0.039, p = 0.940$). In the $n = 87$ metagenomic subsample, PERMANOVA and dbRDA variance partitioning indicate that VOB ($R^2 = 3.8\%$ taxa, $2.3\%$ functional genes) and infant feeding practice ($R^2 = 6.0\%$ taxa, $6.7\%$ genes) explain orthogonal variance (`shared adjusted R^2 ≈ 0`). Taxonomic linear models and Random Forests identify opposing associations of VOB with *Bifidobacterium longum* ($\beta = -0.611, q = 0.016$) and *Bifidobacterium breve* ($\beta = +0.556, q = 0.09$), and logistic regression Feature Set Enrichment Analysis (FSEA, $q < 0.20$) highlights shifts in neuroactive gene sets (tryptophan, glutamate, GABA, acetate, and quinolinic acid pathways). Raw reads (`PRJNA1128723`), processed profiles (Dryad), and Julia/R code (GitHub/Zenodo) are openly archived.

Collecting frame-by-frame dyadic behavioral coding, longitudinal executive function follow-up, and deep shotgun metagenomics in an underrepresented sub-Saharan African infant cohort is a substantial empirical effort, and the variance partitioning demonstrating orthogonality between feeding practices and VOB is well executed. However, several statistical and methodological flaws weaken the core conclusions: (1) the taxonomic regression equation in the Methods (`species ~ VOB`, restricted to non-zero samples) contradicts the Results and Figure 4A–B (`VOB ~ species` including zero-abundance samples); (2) the FSEA enrichment pipeline applies a standard Wilcoxon rank-sum test across `UniRef90` gene-level $Z$-scores without accounting for strong gene-to-gene collinearity across co-inherited bacterial chromosomes/operons; (3) $k$-means clustering on PCoA axes (Fig. 3E / Fig. S3) is performed post hoc after visually selecting PC1 vs. PC3 for VOB separation; (4) the 30-s glitter wand task is right-censored at 30 s, and the three-way interaction ($p = 0.045$) is fragile to censoring and multiple testing; and (5) the text contains missing display equations (lines 58, 119), degrees-of-freedom and sample-size errors (`F(1,253)` in a 2-predictor ANCOVA; `N = 254` for a $2 \times 2$ table summing to $255$), and a contradiction in coding "holding object" during the "No Toy" condition.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: Higher maternal sensory unpredictability is concurrently associated with higher age-adjusted infant visual orienting behavior (VOB) at 2–6 months and ~8.6 months.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Supported by significant concurrent correlations at Session 1 ($r = 0.35, p = 1 \times 10^{-8}$; age-adjusted $r = 0.314, p = 3 \times 10^{-7}, N = 255$) and Session 2 ($r = 0.194, p = 0.005, n = 207$), though iterative re-coding of videos failing the 80% reliability check (line 50) and the coding of object-holding during the "No Toy" condition (lines 43, 48) require clarification.
* **Claim 2: Infant VOB and feeding status explain independent, orthogonal fractions of gut taxonomic and functional $\beta$-diversity ($n = 87$), with VOB tracking a *Bifidobacterium breve* vs. *Bifidobacterium longum* gradient.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  Marginal PERMANOVA and partial dbRDA confirm near-zero shared variance between VOB and 3-category feeding status (Fig. 3A–D), and both linear models and Random Forests rank *B. longum* and *B. breve* as the top taxonomic correlates of VOB. However, the specification of the linear model conflicts between Methods and Results (Defect 1), and cross-validated Random Forest prediction of VOB from the microbiome alone is modest ($R = 0.240$) and does not significantly improve upon maternal unpredictability ($R = 0.386$ vs. $0.380, p > 0.05$; Part 2, line 17).
* **Claim 3: High VOB under high maternal unpredictability buffers infants against poorer inhibitory control at 19–28 months.** `[Fragile]`  
  This conclusion rests on a three-way `Age × Maternal Unpredictability × VOB` interaction ($F(1,185) = 4.056, p = 0.045, \eta_p^2 = 0.022$) in an ordinary least-squares GLM fit to a 30-s right-censored wait-time variable (line 66, 171), where seven main and interaction terms are tested without multiple-testing correction and the simple slope of maternal unpredictability is only significant in the older +1 SD infant age slice ($p = 0.016$) and not at $-1\text{ SD}$ age ($p = 0.105$).
* **Claim 4: Neuroactive microbial metabolic gene pathways (tryptophan, glutamate, GABA, acetate, quinolinic acid) differentially enrich across combinations of VOB and maternal unpredictability.** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  In Methods line 122, logistic regression $Z$-statistics (`gene_presence ~ covariates + VOB * MatEnt`) are computed for each `UniRef90` gene family, and pathway enrichment is tested via a Wilcoxon rank-sum test on the gene-level $Z$-statistics at $q < 0.20$. Because genes within the same microbial pathway/operon reside on the same bacterial genomes (*B. breve*, *B. longum*, *E. coli*), their presence/absence across the 87 stool samples is highly correlated. Treating co-inherited genes on the same chromosome as independent observations in a Wilcoxon rank-sum test violates independence and severely inflates false discovery rates.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Non-Independent Gene-Level Wilcoxon FSEA and Contradictory Taxonomic Linear Model Specification (Pillar 3 & Pillar 4)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Gene-to-gene non-independence in FSEA (Methods lines 118–122):* To test neuroactive gene sets, the authors fit a logistic regression per `UniRef90` gene family and apply a Wilcoxon rank-sum test comparing the $Z$-scores of genes inside a pathway against other genes (`q < 0.20`). In shotgun metagenomics of low-diversity infant guts (mean 34 species/sample, dominated by *B. longum* and *B. breve*), all genes belonging to a species' tryptophan or glutamate biosynthesis pathway are physically linked on the same bacterial genome and co-occur across hosts with near-unity correlation. It is well established in gene-set testing (e.g., Goeman & Bühlmann, 2007; Wu & Smyth, 2012) thatcompetitive gene-ranking tests (like Wilcoxon rank-sum) produce extreme false-positive rates when inter-gene correlation is ignored.
  2. *Contradictory linear model specification (Methods line 106 vs. Results Part 2 line 10):* Methods line 106 states that the model was `species ~ VOB + control factors` where `species` is the arcsin-normalized abundance *"when present in the sample"* (citing MaAsLin v3). Yet Results Part 2 line 10 states *"We modeled normalized VOB as an outcome for each microbial feature ($m = 36$)"* (`VOB ~ species + control factors`), and Fig. 4A–B plots VOB on the Y-axis with zero-abundance ("absent") samples included in the regression.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Ignoring inter-gene genomic linkage invalidates FSEA $p$/$q$-values, and reversing the dependent and independent variables (while inconsistently handling zero abundances) changes model residuals and zero-inflation leverage.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Replace the gene-level Wilcoxon FSEA with a sample-level model: either aggregate/sum stratified pathway abundances per sample (from `HUMAnN v3.7` or `gutSMASH`/GMMs) and regress pathway abundance against `VOB * MatEnt + covariates` across the $n = 87$ infants, or compute FSEA $p$-values by permuting infant `VOB` and `MatEnt` labels ($10,000$ permutations) to preserve inter-gene linkage. Reconcile the exact linear model formula between Methods line 106 and Results Part 2 line 10, and report both the hurdle prevalence (presence/absence) and non-zero abundance coefficients for *B. breve* and *B. longum*.

### 2. Right-Censoring in the 30-s Glitter Wand Task and Post-Hoc Circularity in PCoA $k$-Means Clustering (Pillar 2, Pillar 3 & Pillar 4)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Right-censored wait times (Methods line 66; Results lines 169–173):* In the glitter wand task, wait time is terminated at **30 seconds** (*"If 30 s passes and the child has not yet touched it... The score is the wait time"*). Toddler delay tasks typically exhibit a large point mass at the 30 s ceiling (and near 0 s). Fitting a standard Gaussian GLM to a bounded/right-censored outcome compresses slopes near the ceiling and can induce spurious multi-way interactions (such as the borderline `Age × Maternal Unpredictability × VOB` interaction, $F(1,185) = 4.056, p = 0.045$).
  2. *Post-hoc clustering on selected PCoA axes (Results Part 2, lines 1–3):* After observing no VOB pattern on PC1 vs. PC2 (Fig. S2), the authors inspected PC1 vs. PC3 (Fig. 3E), visually noted a cluster of high-VOB infants, ran $k$-means ($k=4$), and then computed a $p$-value ($p = 0.002$) for VOB differences across those clusters. Selecting the subspace based on visual separation of VOB and testing VOB on the resulting clusters is circular ("double dipping").
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Ceiling effects in bounded wait times bias OLS interaction tests, and post-hoc significance testing on visually selected PCoA clusters inflates Type I error.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Report the proportion of children reaching the 30 s ceiling in the glitter wand task and verify whether the `Age × Maternal Unpredictability × VOB` interaction holds in a right-censored Tobit regression or Cox proportional hazards model of time-to-touch. Reframe the $k$-means analysis on PC1 vs. PC3 (Part 2, line 3) as descriptive/exploratory rather than a formal confirmatory test.

### 3. Behavioral Coding Contradictions ("No Toy" vs. "Holding Object") and Iterative Reliability Re-Coding (Pillar 2 & Pillar 3)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Toy vs. No Toy state space:* Methods line 43 states that **only the No Toy condition** was annotated and analyzed here. However, lines 48 and 55 define maternal behavioral entropy over five maternal signals including **"mother holds object"** and **"mother points"** (and line 48 refers to *"instances where mother went out of view to grab a new toy"*). If no toys/objects were present in the No Toy condition, two of the five binary state dimensions should be structurally zero.
  2. *Iterative reliability re-coding:* Line 50 states that whenever inter-observer reliability fell below $80\%$ on a video, *"the indicated coders revised their coding separately for that participant and the reliability check process was repeated until interrater reliability criteria of at least 0.8 were met."* Re-coding disagreed videos until they pass $0.80$ artificially truncates inter-rater disagreement, and no pre-revision reliability statistics (Cohen's $\kappa$ or intraclass correlation) are reported.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Readers need to know which maternal behavioral states actually occurred in the No Toy condition and what the true initial inter-rater reliability was before iterative revision.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Clarify in Methods lines 43–55 whether objects (e.g., pacifiers, clothing) occurred in the No Toy condition or whether entropy in the No Toy condition was restricted to the 3 non-object behaviors (vocalizing, holding, touching), and report the mean and range of initial (first-pass) inter-rater reliability across coded videos.

### 4. Missing Equations and Degrees-of-Freedom / Sample-Size Reporting Errors (Pillar 4 & Pillar 7)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Omitted equations:* Both the conditional Shannon entropy formula (Methods line 58) and the logistic regression interaction equation for FSEA (Methods line 119) are completely blank in the manuscript text.
  2. *Impossible ANCOVA degrees of freedom (Results line 144):* In a single ANCOVA model on $N = 255$ infants predicting VOB from both infant age and maternal unpredictability ($df_{\text{res}} = 255 - 2 - 1 = 252$), line 144 reports `F(1,252) = 6.483` for infant age, but `F(1,253) = 29.099` for maternal unpredictability *"while controlling for infant age in the model"*. A 2-predictor model on $N = 255$ cannot have $253$ residual degrees of freedom.
  3. *Contingency table sample size mismatch (Results line 159):* Line 159 reports four quadrant counts for the mean-split $2 \times 2$ table: $n = 25$ and $n = 85$ (low unpredictability) and $n = 80$ and $n = 65$ (high unpredictability), which sum to $25 + 85 + 80 + 65 = \mathbf{255}$ (and indeed yield $\chi^2 = 27.185$). Yet the text reports `[(1, N = 254) = 27.18, p = 2 × 10^-7]`, missing the $\chi^2$ symbol and misstating $N = 254$. Similarly, lines 154, 164, and 169 inconsistently report `r(253)` ($N-2$), `r(206)` ($N-1$ for $N=207$), and `r(194)` ($N$ for $N=194$).
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Missing equations and inconsistent degrees of freedom obscure the exact statistical models executed.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Insert the missing mathematical expressions at lines 58 and 119; verify the ANCOVA residual degrees of freedom (`F(1,252)`) at line 144; fix `[(1, N = 254) = 27.18]` to `χ²(1, N = 255) = 27.18` at line 159; and standardize all Pearson correlation notations to $r(df)$ where $df = N - 2$.
