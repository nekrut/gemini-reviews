## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study characterizes the anterior nares microbiome in healthy adult blood donors from the English CARRIAGE cohort ($1,180$ baseline swabs sequenced by V1–V2 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing; $1,055$ passing QC; $1,091$ participants completing three weekly nasal swabs cultured for *S. aureus*). Using hierarchical clustering on Bray-Curtis distances and the gap statistic, the authors define seven nasal Community State Types (CST1–7). They show that persistent *S. aureus* carriers ($3/3$ positive weekly swabs, $28.0\%$) predominantly occupy an *S. aureus*-dominated state (CST1, accounting for $72.4\%$ of CST1 samples), whereas non-carriers ($0/3$ swabs, $54.4\%$) distribute across CST2–7 dominated by *Corynebacterium* spp. (*C. accolens*, *C. jeikeium*, *C. pseudodiphtheriticum*), *Dolosigranulum pigrum*, *Staphylococcus epidermidis*, and *Moraxella catarrhalis*, with women significantly enriched in CST6 and CST7. Crucially, intermittent carriers ($1\text{–}2/3$ positive swabs, $17.5\%$) do not form a distinct ecological state in ordination, differential abundance (ANCOM-BC2), or Random Forest classification ($0\%$ sensitivity for the intermittent class), splitting instead into non-carrier-like ($1/3$ positive) and persistent-carrier-like ($2/3$ positive) profiles. Finally, whole-genome sequencing of $172$ *S. aureus* isolates suggests higher *S. aureus* relative abundance in phylogenetic Cluster B (ST30, ST34, ST398, ST45) than Cluster A ($p = 0.041$). Sequencing accessions are provided in Supplementary Tables S6–S7.

Pairing $>1,000$ anterior nares 16S profiles with the historical gold standard of three weekly longitudinal *S. aureus* cultures provides compelling empirical resolution of the long-standing "intermittent carrier" question. However, the statistical and bioinformatic analyses have four notable flaws: (1) the Random Forest classifier predicting *S. aureus* culture status includes *S. aureus* 16S relative abundance itself as the top predictor (taken from the same baseline swab transport medium), creating circular target leakage without testing whether the *non-aureus* background community predicts carriage; (2) four separate binary (two-group) PERMANOVA analyses in the Results are reported with `F(2)` numerator degrees of freedom instead of `F(1)`; (3) abundance and clustering analyses concatenate samples rarefied to $10,004$ reads with unrarefied low-depth samples down to $500$ reads; and (4) removing $95\%$ of OTUs ($<0.1\%$ abundance) and stripping uncultured/environmental taxa from the SILVA reference database *prior* to running `Decontam` and `ANCOM-BC2` distorts compositional normalization and contamination modeling.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: The adult anterior nares microbiome partitions into seven Community State Types (CST1–7), with persistent *S. aureus* carriers concentrated in *S. aureus*-dominated CST1 and females enriched in CST6 (*C. accolens*) and CST7.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Supported by gap-statistic clustering on $N = 1,055$ post-QC samples and multinomial logistic regression showing reduced male odds for CST6 ($\text{OR} = 0.53, 95\%\text{ CI } 0.30\text{–}0.93$) and CST7 ($\text{OR} = 0.67, 95\%\text{ CI } 0.47\text{–}0.96$) relative to CST1 (Fig. 2).
* **Claim 2: "Intermittent" *S. aureus* carriers do not possess a distinct nasal microbiome state, instead representing a mixture of transient/low-abundance carriers resembling non-carriers (1/3 positive swabs) and high-abundance carriers resembling persistent carriers (2/3 positive swabs).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Demonstrated consistently across Shannon/Simpson alpha diversity, Bray-Curtis PCoA/NMDS ordinations, ANCOM-BC2 (zero non-*aureus* taxa differentially abundant between non-carriers and intermittent carriers), and Random Forest classification ($16/25$ test intermittent carriers classified as non-carriers and $9/25$ as persistent carriers).
* **Claim 3: Machine learning on the nasal microbiome accurately predicts *S. aureus* colonization status (75.2% accuracy; 83.0% sensitivity and 88.2% specificity for persistent carriage).** `[Overstated]`  
  Because 16S sequencing was performed on the same baseline swab fluid used for *S. aureus* culture and the Random Forest includes the *S. aureus* 16S OTU as an input feature (which dominates variable importance in Fig. 4C–D), the classifier is largely using 16S PCR detection of *S. aureus* to predict culture detection of *S. aureus*, rather than predicting colonization susceptibility from the resident non-*S. aureus* microbiota.
* **Claim 4: Specific *S. aureus* phylogenetic lineages (Cluster B: ST30, ST34, ST398, ST45) achieve higher nasal abundance and reshape beta diversity compared to Cluster A.** `[Fragile]`  
  The Mann-Whitney test on rarefied *S. aureus* abundance ($n = 125$, $p = 0.041$) and the binary PERMANOVA ($p = 0.04$, misreported as `F(2) = 2.33`) are borderline significant without multiple-testing correction, lose $47/172$ sequenced isolates when rarefying ($n=172 \rightarrow 125$), and do not test whether CST1 membership differs significantly between Cluster A ($38/74, 51.4\%$) and Cluster B ($33/51, 64.7\%$; Fisher's exact $p = 0.198$, non-significant).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Circular Feature Inclusion (*S. aureus* 16S Reads) in the Random Forest Classifier and Compositional Closure (Pillar 2 & Pillar 3)
* **Defect:** In Results lines 61–67 and Methods (Part 2, lines 4–6), a Random Forest model is trained on relative abundances to predict culture-defined *S. aureus* carriage status. However, the *S. aureus* 16S OTU is retained in the predictor matrix and is by far the single strongest feature (Fig. 4C–D). Because the 16S amplicon library was generated from the same Amies transport medium as the baseline culture swab (line 109), using *S. aureus* 16S relative abundance to predict whether *S. aureus* grew on selective agar is circular. Moreover, because relative abundances are compositional ($\sum_k p_k = 1$), when *S. aureus* constitutes $>50\text{–}75\%$ of reads in persistent carriers (line 40), the relative abundances of *Corynebacterium*, *D. pigrum*, and *S. epidermidis* are mechanically depressed even if their absolute cell densities are unchanged. (Note also that Methods Part 2 line 6 writes the R model formula backwards as `relative_microbial_abundance_data ~ carriage_status` instead of `carriage_status ~ .`).
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** To claim that the nasal *microbiome community* predicts *S. aureus* carriage (or that other taxa are independent determinants in the Random Forest), the model must be evaluated without the target organism's own 16S reads.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Remove the *S. aureus* OTU from the feature table, re-close (or centered-log-ratio transform) the remaining 52 OTUs across samples, retrain the 80:20 Random Forest classifier, and report its test-set accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and ROC-AUC alongside the *S. aureus*-included model. Fix the formula notation in Methods Part 2 line 6.

### 2. Systematic Degrees-of-Freedom Error (`F(2)`) Across Four Binary PERMANOVA Tests (Pillar 4 & Pillar 7)
* **Defect:** In a one-way PERMANOVA (`vegan::adonis2`) comparing $g$ groups, the numerator degrees of freedom is $df_1 = g - 1$. For the 3-group carriage comparison (persistent vs. intermittent vs. non-carrier), reporting `F(2) = 36.67` ($df_1 = 3 - 1 = 2$) on line 35 is correct. However, the authors also report `F(2)` for four strictly **two-group ($g = 2, df_1 = 1$)** comparisons:
  1. Line 35: *S. aureus* culture-positive vs. culture-negative: `F(2) = 59.01, p < 0.001`
  2. Line 35: Female vs. Male sex: `F(2) = 2.83, p = 0.006, R2 = 0.39%`
  3. Line 54: Intermittent carriers with 1 positive swab vs. 2 positive swabs: `F(2) = 3.19, p = 0.003`
  4. Line 72: *S. aureus* phylogenetic Cluster A vs. Cluster B: `F(2) = 2.33, p = 0.04`
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** A binary comparison cannot have 2 numerator degrees of freedom unless an unremoved `NA`/"Unknown" third category was accidentally included as a factor level in `adonis2`.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Audit the R objects passed to `adonis2` for all four binary comparisons: if `NA` levels were included as a third group, drop `NA`s and recompute the 1-df PERMANOVA $F$-statistics, $R^2$, and $p$-values; if `F(2)` was a typographical carry-over from the 3-group test, correct the notation to `F(1, df_residual)`.

### 3. Concatenation of Rarefied (`10,004` reads) and Unrarefied Low-Depth (`500–10,000` reads) Samples (Pillar 3 & Pillar 4)
* **Defect:** Methods lines 136 and 146 state that while diversity analyses used samples rarefied to $10,004$ reads ($n = 795$), abundance and clustering analyses *"combined samples with greater than 500 high quality reads with samples that had greater than 10000 reads and rarefied"* ($n = 1,055$). Combining samples subsampled to $10,004$ reads with unrarefied samples having as few as $500$ reads ($20\times$ lower depth, where richness curves in Supplementary Fig. S5 are still steeply rising) introduces severe depth-dependent zero inflation into Bray-Curtis distances and CST assignments. Furthermore, sample denominators fluctuate across the Results without a clear accounting table: line 28 reports $1,091$ participants with longitudinal swabs and $1,055$ post-QC 16S samples, but line 40 sums to $275 + 532 + 169 = 976$ participants across carriage states and $382 + 672 = 1,054$ samples by culture result.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Mixing $500$-read and $10,004$-read profiles biases Bray-Curtis clustering in low-biomass nasal swabs, and shifting sample denominators confuse readers.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Verify that CST assignments (Fig. 2A) and multinomial regression odds ratios (Fig. 2E) remain invariant when restricted to the uniform $n = 795$ dataset rarefied to $10,004$ reads, and add a CONSORT-style participant/sample accounting table explaining the exact denominators ($1,180 \rightarrow 1,091 \rightarrow 1,055 \rightarrow 976 \rightarrow 795$, and $172 \rightarrow 125$ in Fig. 5).

### 4. Aggressive Pre-Filtering of 95% of OTUs Before `Decontam` and `ANCOM-BC2` and Internal Text Contradictions (Pillar 2, Pillar 3 & Pillar 7)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Pre-filtering before `Decontam` and `ANCOM-BC2`:* Methods lines 136 and 141 state that removing taxa with $<0.1\%$ abundance eliminated $2,207$ of $2,322$ OTUs ($95.0\%$) *before* running correlation-based batch filtering and `Decontam` (`isNotContaminant`), leaving only 53 OTUs (24 species). Similarly, `ANCOM-BC2` was run after filtering taxa below $0.1\%$ (Part 2, line 1). `ANCOM-BC2` estimates sample-specific sampling fractions and log-ratio bias correction across the full community; stripping $95\%$ of taxa to just 24 species violates the assumption that the majority of reference taxa are non-differentially abundant, especially when *S. aureus* occupies $50\text{–}75\%$ of reads in one group.
  2. *Self-contradictory sentence on sex (line 35):* Line 35 states: *"We only observed an association of sex with variation in the Bray-Curtis distance... (F(2) = 2.83, p = 0.006, R2 = 0.39%). There was no association with sex, smoking, pet ownership..."*
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Running `ANCOM-BC2` on 24 highly compressed taxa where a single taxon (*S. aureus*) dominates one group can induce false-positive negative associations via imperfect bias correction.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Run `ANCOM-BC2` on the pre-thresholded decontamination-cleaned count matrix (or validate negative associations using quantitative spike-in/16S qPCR or a reference-frame method invariant to *S. aureus* dominance, such as testing ratios among non-*aureus* taxa), and delete the word *"sex"* from the negative list in the final sentence of line 35.
