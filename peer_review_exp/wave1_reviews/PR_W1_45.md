## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study analyzes 87 longitudinal serum samples from $N=33$ participants in the UK SIREN cohort who experienced a sequence-confirmed primary infection with either SARS-CoV-2 Alpha ($n=23$) or Delta ($n=10$), received a third vaccine dose (`V3`), and subsequently experienced an Omicron `BA.1` ($n=9$) or `BA.2` ($n=24$) reinfection. Sera collected between 7 days post-`V3` and 7 days pre-reinfection were assayed for anti-spike (`anti-S`) IgG and live-virus microneutralization (`LV-N`, quantitative range $40\text{–}2560$) against Ancestral, Alpha, Delta, Omicron BA.1, and Omicron BA.2. The authors fit linear and Tobit mixed-effects models to estimate post-`V3` peak titres and weekly decay rates, then use those fitted models to predict ("infer") individual titres at 12 weeks post-`V3` (or 7 days prior to BA.1/BA.2 reinfection) and apply two-sample Welch's $t$-tests to the model-predicted values.

**Editorial Verdict: Reject / Fundamentally Flawed Statistical Inference ("Stats-on-Stats" Shrinkage Fallacy and Severe Confounding).**  
The manuscript's primary conclusions rest on a textbook two-stage statistical error: running two-sample Welch's $t$-tests directly on point predictions (empirical Bayes / BLUP trajectories) extracted from mixed-effects models without propagating the estimation variance of the fixed effects or random-effect shrinkage. In Stage 1 (the actual mixed-effects models fit to the 87 observed data points, Line 130–135), there is **no statistically significant difference** between Delta and Alpha primary infections in either post-`V3` peak intercept or decay slope for **anti-S** ($p = 0.31$ for peak, $p = 0.27$ for decay slope) or **LV-N Omicron BA.2** ($p = 0.36$ for peak, $p > 0.27$ for slope). Yet in Stage 2 (Lines 142 and 150, Figs 3–4), when the authors plug the mixed-model point predictions at week 12 into an ordinary Welch's $t$-test, the $p$-values artificially collapse to **$p = 0.002$** for anti-S and **$p = 0.0009$ / $p = 0.001$** for LV-N BA.2! Because individual random effects in sparse longitudinal data ($87 / 33 \approx 2.6$ samples/participant) are heavily shrunk toward the group fixed-effect mean, treating model-predicted values as independent raw observations fabricates statistical precision. Compounding this flaw, all 33 participants were selected *because* they had an Omicron reinfection (no uninfected control group), invalidating Discussion claims regarding comparative "protection against Omicron variants," and primary variant identity is collinear with time since infection ($417\text{ days}$ for Alpha vs. $198\text{ days}$ for Delta, $p < 0.001$) and pre-infection vaccination status.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: At peak response (1 week post-`V3`), participants with Delta primary infection had higher LV-N titres against Ancestral, Alpha, Delta, and borderline higher titres against BA.1 ($p = 0.05$), but no significant difference for anti-S ($p = 0.31$) or LV-N BA.2 ($p = 0.36$) (Fig 2, Line 130).** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  Derived from the Stage 1 Tobit mixed-effects model Wald test on the 87 observed sera. However, these estimates are based on only $n=10$ Delta participants, most of whose Ancestral/Alpha/Delta titres were right-censored above the upper limit of quantification (`2560`, reported as "complete neutralisation"), and are confounded by whether primary infection occurred before vs. after primary two-dose vaccination.
* **Claim 2: Prior to Omicron BA.1 and BA.2 reinfection (12 weeks post-`V3` / 7 days pre-reinfection), Delta first-infected participants had highly significantly elevated anti-S ($p = 0.002\text{–}0.01$), LV-N BA.1 ($p = 0.001\text{–}0.004$), and LV-N BA.2 ($p = 0.0009\text{–}0.001$) titres compared to Alpha first-infected participants (Abstract, Figs 3–4, Lines 142 & 150).** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  These highly significant $p$-values are mathematical artifacts of applying Welch's $t$-test to point predictions generated by the Stage 1 mixed-effects models without propagating parameter or random-effect prediction uncertainty. Because the Stage 1 mixed models showed neither a significant intercept difference ($p = 0.31$ for anti-S; $p = 0.36$ for BA.2) nor a significant slope interaction ($p \ge 0.27$), linear combinations of the fixed effects at $t = 12\text{ weeks}$ cannot achieve $p = 0.0009$ when estimation variance is properly retained.
* **Claim 3: Neutralizing antibody responses against Ancestral virus remain higher than against subsequent variants regardless of whether the first infection was Alpha or Delta, consistent with ancestral vaccine imprinting (Fig 5, Line 160).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Because 79% of participants received at least one Ancestral-strain vaccine dose prior to their primary infection and all 33 received three Ancestral vaccine doses by `V3`, higher neutralization against Ancestral spike than against Omicron BA.1/BA.2 is consistent with visual inspection of raw data and established antigenic cartography.
* **Claim 4: "Our results suggested that a previous Delta infection conferred more protection against Omicron variants than Alpha infection, even when controlling for time since first infection" (Discussion Line 172).** `[Overstated]`  
  Every participant in the study ($33/33$) was selected under the inclusion criterion of having a confirmed Omicron BA.1 or BA.2 reinfection (Line 37). Without an uninfected comparator group or person-time incidence denominators, a case-only serological series of reinfected individuals cannot estimate relative protection against reinfection.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Two-Stage "Stats-on-Stats" Shrinkage Fallacy — Applying Welch's $t$-Tests to Mixed-Model Point Predictions (Lines 64, 69, 74, 130–150)
* **Location:** Methods lines 64–75; Results lines 130–150 (Figures 2, 3, and 4).
* **Mathematical/Statistical Flaw:**
  1. In Stage 1 (Line 64), log-titres $y_{ij}$ for participant $i$ at time $t_{ij}$ post-`V3` are modeled via a mixed-effects model:
     $$y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 D_i + \beta_2 t_{ij} + \beta_3 (D_i \times t_{ij}) + u_{0i} + u_{1i} t_{ij} + \epsilon_{ij}$$
     (with $u_{1i} = 0$ for Tobit LV-N models, where only random intercepts were supported). Under this model, the Stage 1 Wald tests find **no significant difference** between Delta ($D_i = 1$) and Alpha ($D_i = 0$) for `anti-S` intercept ($p = 0.31$), `LV-N BA.2` intercept ($p = 0.36$), or any decay slope interaction $\beta_3$ ($p \ge 0.27$).
  2. In Stage 2 (Lines 69, 74), the authors evaluate the fitted model at $t^* = 12\text{ weeks}$ (or $t_i^* = t_{\text{reinfection},i} - 7\text{ days}$) to obtain point predictions $\hat{y}_i(t^*) = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 D_i + (\hat{\beta}_2 + \hat{\beta}_3 D_i) t^* + \hat{u}_{0i} + \hat{u}_{1i} t^*$, and then plug $\{\hat{y}_i(t^*)\}_{i=1}^{33}$ into a two-sample Welch's $t$-test.
  3. Because each empirical Bayes random effect $\hat{u}_{0i}$ is shrunk toward zero by the shrinkage factor $\lambda_i = \sigma_u^2 / (\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\epsilon^2 / n_i)$ (where mean $n_i = 87/33 = 2.64$ samples), the within-group variance of the predictions $\text{Var}(\hat{y}_i(t^*) \mid D_i)$ is artificially compressed, while the uncertainty of the fixed-effect estimates $\text{Var}(\hat{\beta}_1 + \hat{\beta}_3 t^*)$ is treated as **zero** by Welch's $t$-test. Furthermore, in the model without interaction (used after finding $p \ge 0.27$ for slopes), the group difference $\hat{\beta}_1$ is constant over time, meaning every participant's predicted value shifts by the exact deterministic constant $\hat{\beta}_1$! Testing a linear contrast at $t^* = 12\text{ weeks}$ must be performed *inside* the mixed-effects model via a Wald/likelihood-ratio contrast using the full covariance matrix $\mathbf{c}^\top \widehat{\text{Cov}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) \mathbf{c}$, not by running a $t$-test on shrunk point predictions.

### Defect 2: Collinearity Between Variant Wave, Time Since Primary Infection, and Vaccination Order in a Small Sample ($n=23$ vs. $n=10$)
* **Location:** Results lines 86, 123, 130; Discussion lines 174, 178; Figure S1 legend (line 198).
* **Epidemiological Confounding:**
  1. Primary Alpha infections occurred during the winter/spring 2020–2021 wave (median $417\text{ days}$ before Omicron reinfection), whereas primary Delta infections occurred during the summer/autumn 2021 wave (median $198\text{ days}$ before reinfection, $p < 0.001$, Line 123). Because the Alpha and Delta calendar epochs scarcely overlapped (Line 178), `Variant` and `Time since first infection` have near-zero common support in $N=33$ participants.
  2. Line 174 reveals that 79% of participants received at least one vaccine dose *before* their first infection, yet Figure S1 explicitly omits pre-infection vaccine doses (*"For the purpose of this illustration, vaccine doses administrated before first infection are not shown"*, Line 198). Because UK healthcare workers received doses 1 and 2 in early 2021, Delta "first infections" (mid-to-late 2021) occurred predominantly in fully double-vaccinated individuals (resulting in a massive hybrid boost), whereas early Alpha "first infections" occurred prior to or shortly after Dose 1.

### Defect 3: Truncation/Substitution of Right-Censored Titres Above `2560` and Subgroup Sample Size Attrition
* **Location:** Methods lines 54, 69, 74; Results lines 121, 150.
* **Methodological Flaw:**
  1. While Stage 1 used a Tobit mixed model to account for censoring outside $[40, 2560]$, Stage 2 (Line 69) states: *"We replaced values outside the quantitative range (full neutralisation) with the upper end of the quantitative range [2560]"* before running Welch's $t$-test and computing geometric means. Capping right-censored values at $2560$ truncates the upper tail and distorts variance estimates in Welch's test.
  2. In the BA.2-only analysis (Fig 4, Line 148–150), restricting to BA.2 reinfections ($N=24$ total out of $33$) leaves at most $n \approx 6\text{–}8$ participants in the Delta group, where asymptotic normality and variance estimates for Welch's test on imputed trajectories are unreliable.

### Defect 4: Numerical Copy-Paste Errors, Corrupted Typography, and Duplicated Text Blocks
* **Identical Numbers Copy-Pasted for Two Different Viruses (Line 150):** Line 150 reports for BA.2-reinfected participants: `LV‐N Ancestral 1875 vs. 826 ( p = 0.0001), LV‐N Alpha 1875 vs. 826 ( p < 0.0001)`. Having the exact same geometric means (`1875 vs. 826`) to four significant digits for two different live-virus neutralization assays (Ancestral and Alpha) while reporting different $p$-values (`p = 0.0001` vs. `p < 0.0001`) indicates a data transcription error.
* **Missing Spaces Merging Virus Names with Titre Numbers & Out-of-Range Value `32` (Lines 142 and 150):**
  * Line 142 writes `LV‐N Omicron BA.1746 vs. 32 ( p = 0.001)` and `LV‐N Omicron BA.2861 vs. 409 ( p = 0.0009)` instead of `BA.1 746 vs. [320?]` and `BA.2 861 vs. 409`. Moreover, a geometric mean of `32` for Alpha-primed BA.1 at 12 weeks contradicts Line 150 (where Alpha-primed BA.1 is `252` at a later timepoint!) and implies a 23.3-fold difference rather than the ~2–3-fold differences shown across the text.
  * Line 150 writes `LV‐N Omicron BA.1477 vs. 252` and `LV‐N Omicron BA.2544 vs. 288` instead of `BA.1 477` and `BA.2 544`.
* **Inconsistent Fold-Difference in Abstract vs. Main Text:** Abstract (Line 9) states pre-Omicron reinfection `LV-N BA.2 (FD = 2.06, p = 0.001)`, whereas Line 150 ($p = 0.001$) reports `544 vs. 288`, whose ratio is $544 / 288 = 1.89$, not $2.06$ (while in Line 142, $861 / 409 = 2.11$ with $p = 0.0009$).
* **Duplicated Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria (Lines 37 vs. 39–49):** The six inclusion and exclusion bullet points in Line 37 are duplicated verbatim in Lines 39–49.
