## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This systems microbiology study profiles 1,224 directional pairwise growth interactions across 36 human gut bacterial strains (spanning 6 phyla; 34 receiver strains grown across 36 donor spent media and 1:1 nutrient-replenished spent media in Gifu Anaerobic Medium [GAM]). Quantifying growth via area under the curve (AUC) up to strain-specific early stationary phase cutoffs (`median CV = 8.5%`), the authors find that 25.1% (307/1,224) of spent-medium interactions are inhibitory ($|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$) and 4.2% (51/1,224) are growth-promoting. Nutrient replenishment reduces inhibitory interactions >8-fold (to 38/1,224, 3.1%), and spent-medium pH strongly predicts growth inhibition ($r = 0.49\text{ to }0.54, p \le 10^{-73}$), which is reversed in 56 tested pairs by neutralizing spent media to pH 7.0. The authors then dissect two positive interactions mechanistically: (1) using TMT-based quantitative proteomics (`MSFragger` + `limma`), DLS, negative-stain TEM, and ultracentrifugation ($100,000 \times g$), they show that *Clostridium perfringens* promotes the growth of *Mediterraneibacter gnavus* (1.4-fold) via heat-labile ~58 nm extracellular vesicles rather than free nucleobases/nucleosides; and (2) combining proteomics, targeted UHPLC-QqQ metabolomics, and qPCR-deconvolved 3-species co-cultures, they show that *Veillonella parvula* raises environmental pH (enhanced by 1 mM guanine supplementation) and rescues the growth of acid-sensitive *Parabacteroides merdae* in the presence of diverse acidifying bacteria (*S. parasanguinis*, *L. paracasei*, *B. adolescentis*, and *B. ovatus*).

The experimental design moves logically from high-throughput phenotypic screening (with nutrient-replenished and pH-adjusted counter-screens) to proteomics, targeted metabolomics, biochemical fractionation, and synthetic multi-species validation. The primary methodological weaknesses are: (1) unadjusted $p$-values and non-independent $t$-tests in the pairwise phylogenetic correlation comparison (Fig. 1g, where $N=561$ strain pairs have strong vertex-level network dependence) and the high-throughput interaction screen (which relies on a hard $|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$ cutoff on $n=2$ replicates without FDR estimation); (2) an internal inconsistency in the number of growth-promoting interactions in the text (`51 interactions` in line 81 vs. `27 out of 58 interactions` two sentences later in line 81); and (3) a subjective curve-filtering rule in Methods (line 179) where replicates were discarded by "visual inspection" if the rich-medium control "deviated from the overall behavior."

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1: Pairwise spent-medium interactions among 36 gut bacteria are predominantly inhibitory (25.1% vs. 4.2% positive) and largely driven by media acidification and nutrient depletion.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Replenishing nutrients reduces inhibitory interactions from 307 to 38 (Fig. 1d–e), spent-medium pH correlates strongly with growth fold-change ($r = 0.49, p = 10^{-73}$), and readjusting pH to 7.0 in 56 pairs neutralizes most growth inhibition while isolating pH-independent antagonism (e.g., *B. fragilis* and *B. uniformis* against *B. fragilis* ET and *A. muciniphila*).
* **Claim 2: Taxonomically related strains exhibit significantly higher correlations in their growth responses across spent media.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  In Fig. 1g, column-wise Pearson correlations of growth profiles are higher for pairs sharing a taxonomic rank. However, applying standard two-sample $t$-tests to pairwise correlation coefficients ($t(403) = 4.2$, etc., line 79) treats $\binom{34}{2} = 561$ pairwise edges among 34 strains as independent observations, inflating degrees of freedom; a Mantel test or node-permutation test is required for dyadic correlation matrices.
* **Claim 3: *Clostridium perfringens* promotes *Mediterraneibacter gnavus* growth via heat-labile extracellular vesicles.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Fractionation by $100,000 \times g$ ultracentrifugation localizes the 1.4-fold growth promotion exclusively to the 58 nm vesicle pellet ($p = 0.0054$) and not the vesicle-depleted supernatant ($p = 0.79$), with activity and vesicle morphology abolished by boiling ($98^\circ\text{C}$, 10 min; Fig. 2d, Supplementary Fig. 2).
* **Claim 4: *Veillonella parvula* deacidifies the extracellular environment (enhanced by guanine) and rescues acid-sensitive *Parabacteroides merdae* in multi-species communities.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Both conditioned-medium transfer (Fig. 4b) and 3-member co-cultures quantified by species-specific qPCR and OD$_{600}$ across four acidifiers (Fig. 5b–c, Supplementary Fig. 4) demonstrate reproducible pH elevation and *P. merdae* rescue, amplified by 1 mM guanine.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

1. **Pseudoreplication in Pairwise Taxonomic Correlation $t$-Tests (Fig. 1g):**  
   In Fig. 1g (caption, line 79), the authors compare the distribution of pairwise column-wise Pearson correlation coefficients $r_{ij}$ between strains sharing vs. not sharing a taxonomic rank (phylum, class, order, family, genus) using standard two-sided $t$-tests with degrees of freedom equal to the number of pairs (`t(403) = 4.2`, `t(227) = 4.43`, `t(157) = 3.7`, `t(92) = 3.7`, `t(28) = 3.27`). Because all $\binom{34}{2} = 561$ pairs are formed from only $N=34$ strains, the pairwise correlations $r_{ij}$ and $r_{ik}$ share strain $i$ and are mathematically non-independent. Treating dyadic matrix entries as independent samples inflates the effective sample size from $N=34$ to $N=561$ and invalidates parametric $t$-test $p$-values; significance must be evaluated via a Mantel / quadratic assignment permutation (permuting the 34 strain labels across the taxonomy tree).
2. **Contradictory Count of Growth-Promoting Interactions in the Same Paragraph (`51` vs. `58`):**  
   In the Results (line 81), the first sentence states: *"When considering a two-fold change compared to rich medium ($|\log_2\text{ fold change}| > 1$), we observed 307 inhibitory interactions (25.1%), and **51 interactions** where growth was promoted (4.2%) (Fig. 1d)."* Two sentences later in the exact same paragraph (line 81), the text states: *"In contrast, a large proportion of growth promotion interactions (**27 out of 58 interactions**) were unchanged even after nutrient replenishment, suggesting potential cross-feeding."* Whether the denominator of growth-promoting interactions in spent medium is 51 or 58 (or whether 7 interactions only crossed the threshold in replenished medium) is internally inconsistent and must be clarified.
3. **Subjective Outlier Removal and Floor Imputation in Growth Curve Processing:**  
   In the Methods (line 179), the authors state: *"All growth curves were then visually inspected, and replicates where the rich medium condition deviated from the overall behavior of that strain in rich medium (observed in all other experiments) were removed."* Visual removal of control replicates without an objective quantitative criterion (e.g., $>3\text{ SD}$ or $>2\times\text{ IQR}$ from the strain's historical rich-medium AUC median) introduces subjective filtering bias. In the same paragraph (line 179), negative AUCs (caused by blank subtraction when no growth occurred) were imputed to *"the lowest [positive] AUC value for that strain in the respective media (so that a fold-change could be calculated)."* Because the lowest positive AUC across 36 conditions is a random order statistic that varies widely from strain to strain, this floor imputation introduces arbitrary strain-specific bounds on maximum inhibition fold-changes.
4. **Dilution Mismatch (1:50) in the Crude Vesicle Assay vs. Undiluted Spent Medium:**  
   In the Methods (line 194), 500 mL of *C. perfringens* culture was ultracentrifuged and resuspended in 1 mL PBS ($500\times$ concentration factor), and then **"isolated vesicles were diluted 1:50 in GAM prior to testing,"** resulting in a **$10\times$ higher final vesicle concentration** ($500 / 50 = 10\times$) than in the native *C. perfringens* spent medium (which was tested undiluted). Furthermore, the vesicle pellet was resuspended in fresh GAM ($100\%$ nutrients), whereas native spent medium is nutrient-depleted over 24 h of *C. perfringens* growth. While the boiled vesicle control ($98^\circ\text{C}$, 10 min in fresh GAM) and PBS sham control properly isolate the heat-labile vesicle effect in Fig. 2d, the $10\times$ vesicle concentration difference relative to native spent medium should be explicitly stated in the Results text.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

* **Multiple-Testing Correction in Targeted Metabolomics (Fig. 4e):** While proteomics differential abundance was properly corrected for multiple comparisons via Benjamini-Hochberg FDR in `limma` (Figs. 2b, 4c), the targeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics screen across amino acids and purines (`n = 3` biological replicates, $2\text{ df}$, Fig. 4e, line 128 and Second part line 5) used unadjusted one-sample $t$-tests; reporting BH-adjusted $q$-values alongside the raw $p$-values in Supplementary Data 12 is recommended.
* **Optical Density $\times$ qPCR Fraction as Biomass Proxy in Co-Cultures (Fig. 5b):** Multiplying total community $\text{OD}_{600}$ by the genomic copy-number fraction of *P. merdae* (Second part line 15) assumes similar per-cell optical cross-sections and DNA extraction efficiencies across *P. merdae*, *V. parvula*, and the four acidifiers (including Gram-positive *S. parasanguinis*, *L. paracasei*, and *B. adolescentis*, which have thicker peptidoglycan cell walls than Gram-negative *P. merdae*). Plotting absolute qPCR copy numbers ($\text{copies}/\mu\text{L}$ normalized to culture volume) alongside $\text{OD}_{600}$-scaled abundance would confirm that differential lysis efficiency does not skew the fractional estimate.
* **Missing Data/Code Availability Section in Manuscript Text:** Although Supplementary Data 1–16 are extensively referenced, the manuscript excerpt ends at the Reporting Summary (Second part line 25) without listing the PRIDE/ProteomeXchange accession for the TMT proteomics raw files (`MSFragger`) or the repository for the LC-MS/MS metabolomics and plate-reader processing scripts.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Replace Parametric Pairwise $t$-Tests in Fig. 1g with a Node-Label Permutation / Mantel Test:**
   * **WHY:** Testing $\binom{34}{2} = 561$ pairwise correlations with standard $t$-tests treats shared-strain edges as independent observations and inflates degrees of freedom by $>15\times$.
   * **WHAT:** Permute the taxonomic labels across the 34 strains ($10,000$ permutations) to generate an exact null distribution for the difference in mean/median correlation at each taxonomic rank in Fig. 1g.
   * **HOW:** Recompute the permutation $p$-values for groups `a–e` in Fig. 1g and report them alongside the parametric $t$-statistics in the caption (line 79).
2. **Reconcile the `51` vs. `58` Growth-Promoting Interaction Counts and State the $10\times$ Vesicle Concentration Factor:**
   * **WHY:** Line 81 reports 51 growth-promoting interactions in the first sentence and 58 in the third sentence, and Methods line 194 indicates a $500\times / 50 = 10\times$ effective vesicle concentration in Fig. 2d.
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Clarify in line 81 whether 51 interactions were growth-promoting in spent medium alone and 7 additional interactions emerged in replenished medium (totaling 58), and note in line 100 that the purified vesicle fraction was assayed at $10\times$ native culture equivalents in fresh GAM against a matched boiled-vesicle control.
3. **Document Quantitative Criteria for Growth Curve QC and Add Proteomics/Code Accessions:**
   * **WHY:** Reproducibility requires objective QC thresholds and public raw proteomics data accessions.
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Specify in Methods (line 179) how many replicates out of the total screen were excluded during visual inspection and what quantitative deviation from the rich-medium median triggered exclusion; add a Data & Code Availability statement providing the ProteomeXchange/PRIDE accession for the TMT mass spectrometry datasets and the repository link for the curve-trimming/AUC analysis code.
