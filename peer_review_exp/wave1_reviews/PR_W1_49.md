## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study analyzes 2,005 *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (Mtb) whole-genome sequences collected across Thailand between 1994 and 2020, combining 816 archival European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) genomes (1994–2016) with 1,189 newly sequenced isolates from Siriraj Hospital (2017–2020). Using `TB-Profiler` (v6.2.2), the authors characterize lineage composition (78.3% Lineage 2, predominantly Beijing `L2.2.1` at 65.7%) and genotypic drug resistance (67.3% MDR-TB, 14.6% Pre-XDR-TB), benchmark genotypic predictions against 12,640 phenotypic drug susceptibility tests (pDSTs) across $n=1,826$ isolates (91.1% overall concordance), and identify a 22-isolate `L2.2.1` cluster carrying the delamanid-resistance mutation `ddn Gly81Ser` in samples collected prior to drug rollout (2005–2017). They then define 206 putative transmission clusters using a Gaussian Mixture Model 13-SNP threshold, apply logistic regression and `GEMMA` linear mixed models to test for genetic and clinical predictors of clustering, and run `BEAST2` and `TransPhylo` on the largest cluster ($n=288$).

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision (Valuable clinical WGS surveillance resource and pDST concordance benchmark, but severely confounded clustering regression/GWAS and flawed `TransPhylo` reconstruction).**  
The genomic surveillance dataset ($N=2,005$ with ENA accessions), the 12,640-test pDST concordance evaluation, and the identification of pre-existing `ddn Gly81Ser` delamanid resistance in Thailand are clinically important contributions. However, the downstream transmission-clustering regressions, GWAS, and `TransPhylo` models suffer from major methodological flaws:
1. **Severe Ascertainment Bias in Clustering Logistic Regression and `GEMMA` GWAS (Lines 26, 149–151):** The cohort was assembled via convenience sampling specifically targeting drug-resistant referrals (88.4% drug-resistant vs. only 11.6% drug-sensitive). Because the probability that an isolate has a sampled neighbor within $\le 13$ SNPs scales directly with the sampling fraction of its subpopulation, oversampling MDR/Pre-XDR-TB by $>50\times$ relative to pan-sensitive TB guarantees that MDR/Pre-XDR isolates cluster at much higher rates than sensitive isolates (`OR 5.82` and `OR 10.98`) and forces the primary isoniazid-resistance mutation `katG Ser315Thr` (`OR 7.94`) to emerge as the top "transmissibility" GWAS hit.
2. **Inclusion of Repetitive `pe/ppe` Genes in Variant Calling and GWAS (Lines 31, 151, 172):** Unlike standard Mtb WGS pipelines that mask `pe/ppe` regions due to short-read paralog mismapping and structural deletions (`RD304`), the authors retained them and report `ppe8 V2309I` as a GWAS locus for transmissibility.
3. **Uncalibrated `TransPhylo` Reconstruction Producing Chronologically and Biologically Impossible Transmission Events (Line 174):** Running `TransPhylo` with default settings on a 20-year cluster ($n=288$) where only collection *year* is known produced inferred transmissions backwards in time (e.g., `2008 to 2006`) and from `Pre-XDR-TB` back to `MDR-TB`.
4. **Multiple Numerical Discrepancies Between Text and Tables:** Percentages for `pncA Ile31Thr` and `embB Met306Val` in the text (Line 95) do not match Table 2, and phenotypic resistance percentages in Line 138 use the wrong denominator ($2,005$ instead of $1,826$).

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: WGS-based drug resistance prediction (`TB-Profiler`) achieves 91.1% overall concordance with phenotypic DST across 12,640 tests ($\sim 96\%$ sensitivity/specificity for INH and RIF) and identifies 88 candidate uncatalogued resistance mutations (Table 2, Tables S3–S7, Lines 136–143).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Supported by a large sample size ($n=1,826$ isolates with pDST data across 9 drugs) and transparent reporting of ethambutol's lower specificity (60.4%).
* **Claim 2: The `ddn Gly81Ser` mutation associated with delamanid/pretomanid resistance is present in a 22-isolate `L2.2.1` pre-XDR clade circulating in Central Thailand between 2005 and 2017, predating delamanid rollout (Lines 95, 147, 166).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Directly demonstrated by sequence alignment and temporal metadata across both archival ENA (2005–2017) and newly sequenced isolates (2007–2014), with clear clinical relevance for `BPaLM` regimen stewardship.
* **Claim 3: MDR-TB (`OR 5.82`), Pre-XDR-TB (`OR 10.98`), and SNPs in `katG Ser315Thr` (`OR 7.94`), `folC S150G` (`OR 6.5`), and `ppe8 V2309I` (`OR 2.81`) significantly increase the risk of Mtb transmission clustering (Lines 149–151, 170–172).** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  Confounded by convenience-sampling ascertainment bias (88.4% of sequenced isolates are drug-resistant, whereas drug-sensitive TB—which represents $>90\%$ of total TB incidence in Thailand—makes up only 11.6% of the sample), unadjusted univariate odds ratios in `GEMMA`, and unmasked `pe/ppe` mapping artifacts (`ppe8`).
* **Claim 4: `BEAST2` and `TransPhylo` reconstruct who-infected-whom transmission pathways and 132 unsampled cases within the 288-isolate `L2.2.1` clade (Fig 3, Lines 154–156, 174).** `[Fragile]`  
  While the `BEAST2` evolutionary rate estimate ($1.10 \times 10^{-7}\text{ substitutions/site/year}$, $\sim 0.48\text{ SNPs/genome/year}$, $\text{tMRCA} = 1989$) is consistent with published Mtb L2 clock rates, the `TransPhylo` reconstruction using default priors and coarse collection years produces chronological inversions (`2008 -> 2006`) and resistance reversions (`Pre-XDR -> MDR`, Line 174), invalidating individual who-infected-whom links.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Ascertainment Bias in Convenience Sampling Invalidates MDR Clustering Odds Ratios and `GEMMA` GWAS Hits (`katG Ser315Thr`, `folC S150G`)
* **Location:** Methods lines 26, 36; Results lines 44, 149–151; Discussion lines 170–172.
* **Epidemiological & Statistical Flaw:**
  1. In Thailand (2014–2020), total TB incidence was $>100,000\text{ cases/year}$ ($>700,000$ total cases), of which $6,992$ were laboratory-confirmed MDR/RR-TB (Line 44). This study sequenced $\sim 8.5\%$ of all national MDR/RR-TB cases, but only $233$ drug-sensitive isolates across 26 years ($<0.02\%$ of national drug-sensitive cases).
  2. In any transmission network subsampled at fraction $f$, the probability that a true transmission partner is also captured in the sample is proportional to $f$. Because $f_{\text{MDR}} \gg f_{\text{Sensitive}}$ by more than two orders of magnitude in this referral dataset, sensitive isolates appear as singletons while MDR/Pre-XDR isolates form dense clusters.
  3. Furthermore, while Line 36 claims that `GEMMA` *"accounted for the kinship matrix, incorporating SNPs, lineage, and drug resistance to control for relatedness among isolates,"* Line 151 reports the crude unadjusted odds ratio for `katG Ser315Thr` (`OR 7.94, 95% CI [6.39, 9.87]`), which is simply a proxy for isoniazid/MDR resistance ($1,436/2,005$ isolates carry `katG Ser315Thr`, and $1,265/2,005$ are in clusters). If drug resistance and kinship were properly conditioned on, `katG Ser315Thr` could not have an odds ratio of $7.94$ on top of the MDR-TB effect (`OR 5.82`).

### Defect 2: Retention of Repetitive `pe/ppe` Regions in Short-Read Variant Calling, Pairwise SNP Distances, and GWAS (`ppe8 V2309I`)
* **Location:** Methods line 31; Results line 151; Discussion line 172.
* **Bioinformatic Flaw:** Line 31 states: *"and pe/ppe gene regions were not excluded, but results from these regions were interpreted with caution due to known mapping challenges."* In Illumina 100–150 bp short-read sequencing of Mtb, the high GC content, repetitive motifs, and structural deletions (`RD304` spanning `ppe8` in L2) of `PE/PPE` multigene families generate systematic false-positive heterozygous and homozygous SNP calls when mapped with `bwa-mem`. Standard Mtb phylodynamic and transmission pipelines mask all `PE/PPE` genes, mobile elements, and drug-resistance-associated homoplastic genes before computing `snp-dists`, `IQ-TREE`, `BEAST2`, and GWAS. Retaining `pe/ppe` regions inflates the 13-SNP GMM cutoff and produces the likely artefactual `ppe8 V2309I` GWAS signal.

### Defect 3: Uncalibrated `TransPhylo` Priors and Unmasked Homoplasy Leading to Backwards-in-Time (`2008 -> 2006`) and Resistance-Reverting (`Pre-XDR -> MDR`) Inferences
* **Location:** Methods line 31; Results line 156 (Fig 3); Discussion line 174.
* **Phylodynamic Modeling Flaw:**
  1. `TransPhylo` was executed *"using the default settings"* (Line 31) on the $n=288$ `BEAST2` MCC tree spanning 1994–2020. `TransPhylo`'s default generation-time and infection-to-sampling gamma distributions (`shape=2, scale=1 year`) and uniform sampling proportion $\pi$ are violated by a 26-year convenience sample where sampling density jumped sharply in 2013–2020 and where collection dates were only recorded to the nearest calendar year.
  2. Line 174 admits that the resulting `TransPhylo` MAP tree (Fig 3) contains *"transmission from Pre-XDR-TB to MDR-TB cases or from more recent years to earlier years (e.g., 2008 to 2006)."* In a valid time-calibrated transmission tree where tip dates constrain host sampling times, an infection event cannot occur after the recipient's sampling date unless tip dates were jittered/misassigned or post-hoc node coloring (in 5-year bins in Fig 3) misrepresented the internal node heights. Likewise, inferring transmission from a fluoroquinolone-resistant (`Pre-XDR`) host to a fluoroquinolone-susceptible (`MDR`) host indicates that convergent/homoplastic `gyrA` mutations were not masked during `BEAST2` tree construction, artificially grouping independent `Pre-XDR` acquisitions with ancestral `MDR` lineages.

### Defect 4: Numerical Discrepancies Between Main Text and Tables 1–2, Plus Pairwise Correlation Pseudoreplication
* **Mismatched Mutation Percentages (Line 95 vs. Table 2):**
  * Line 95 states `pncA Ile31Thr (16·0%, pyrazinamide)` and `embB Met306Val (14·4%, ethambutol)`.
  * However, **Table 2** (Lines 110 and 116) reports `embB Met306Val` as $261 / 2005 = \mathbf{13\cdot 02\%}$ and `pncA Ile31Thr` as $289 / 2005 = \mathbf{14\cdot 41\%}$! The text in Line 95 shifted `16.0%` from `embB Gly406Asp` ($320/2005 = 15.96\%$) onto `pncA Ile31Thr`, and shifted `14.4%` from `pncA Ile31Thr` onto `embB Met306Val`.
* **Wrong Denominator Used for Phenotypic DST Percentages (Line 138):**
  * Line 138 states that phenotypic DST was performed on a subset of **$n = 1,826$** samples ($1,819$ with composite profiles in Line 142), yet reports: *"Overall, 1,522 isolates (75·9%) were classified as MDR-TB, followed by 201 isolates (10·0%) with Pre-XDR+ TB, and 59 isolates (2·9%) being pan-sensitive."* Dividing $1,522$, $201$, and $59$ by **$2,005$** (the total WGS cohort, including the 179 isolates *without* pDST!) yields $75.9\%$, $10.0\%$, and $2.9\%$; divided by the actual pDST denominator ($1,819$), the percentages are **83.7%**, **11.1%**, and **3.2%**.
* **Incomplete Table 1 and Rounding Inconsistencies:** Table 1 (Line 83) lists only `Lineage | L2 | 1569 | 78·2` (omitting L1, L3, and L4, while the Abstract and Line 44 write `78·3%`), and lists `XDR-TB | 5 | 0·3` while Line 90 writes `0·2% (5/2005)`.
* **Pairwise Distance Pseudoreplication (Line 147):** Testing the association between pairwise geographic distance and pairwise SNP distance via standard Kruskal-Wallis ($P < 0.001$) and Spearman correlation ($\rho = 0.046$) treats $\binom{N}{2}$ non-independent pairs as independent observations instead of using a Mantel permutation test.
* **Corrupted Funding String (Line 191):** Line 191 contains a repetitive machine-generated metadata dump (`HSRI 65-113, HSRI 65-113, HSRI 65-113...`).
