## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This genome resource and comparative genomics manuscript reports a hybrid de novo genome assembly and annotation for the green algal facultative endosymbiont *Micractinium conductrix* strain 186b, isolated from the ciliate host *Paramecium bursaria* 186b. Combining PacBio HiFi ($168\times$), Oxford Nanopore R10.4.1 ($59\times$), and PCR-free Illumina NovaSeq ($60\times$) reads with BlobToolKit GC/coverage partitioning and telomere-aware HiFiASM reassembly, the authors produce a 62.57 Mbp nuclear assembly across 30 contigs ($\text{N50} = 3.83\text{ Mbp}$, $\text{L50} = 6$, $0\text{ Ns/100 kbp}$, $99.1\%$ Chlorophyta BUSCO completeness), a circular 75.29 kbp mitochondrial genome, and a partial 56.17 kbp chloroplast assembly. Using light/dark short-read RNA-seq and light-phase PacBio Iso-Seq alongside BRAKER3 and an updated DIAMOND-based reciprocal BLAST pipeline (`fdfBLAST v1.2`), they evaluate putative gene fusion rates across 10 Chlorellaceae gene predictions, showing that *M. conductrix* SAG 241.80 has an elevated rate of putative gene fusions ($21.5\%$, 9,217 genes, mean length 5,247 bp) compared to their RNA-seq-informed 186b annotation ($5.6\%$, 12,545 genes, mean length 3,696 bp) despite $99.87\%$ whole-genome FastANI identity. All raw reads, assemblies, annotations, and code are publicly deposited in ENA (`PRJEB104497`), Zenodo, and GitHub.

The nuclear assembly is contiguous and well-curated, and the comparative analysis of annotation-induced false gene fusions across Chlorellaceae genomes addresses a practical problem in non-model algal genomics. The primary technical weaknesses are: (1) an arithmetic error in the telomere count in Table 1 and Results ($9 + 14 + 8 = 31$ contigs instead of 30) that undermines the $(14/2) + 9 = 16$ chromosome number inference; (2) an unresolved 10.7 Mbp discrepancy between the k-mer genome size estimate (51.9 Mbp) and the 62.57 Mbp assembly that cannot be explained by the 8 short contigs totaling 315 kbp; (3) an incomplete chloroplast genome (56.2 kbp vs. ~123.6 kbp expected) likely caused by strict GC-cutoff read filtering prior to organelle assembly; and (4) retaining three ultra-low-coverage contaminant/variant contigs inside the primary nuclear reference fasta.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1: The hybrid assembly of *M. conductrix* 186b achieves near chromosome-scale contiguity and high gene completeness.** `[Well Supported]`  
  The 30-contig gapless assembly ($\text{N50} = 3.83\text{ Mbp}$, Chlorophyta $\text{BUSCO} = 99.1\%$, $\text{OMARK} = 85.0\%$ total detected, $\text{PSAURON} = 97.7$) substantially improves upon the 300-contig *M. conductrix* SAG 241.80 assembly ($\text{N50} = 1.21\text{ Mbp}$, $\text{BUSCO} = 96.0\%$).
* **Claim 2: Telomere distribution across contigs indicates that *M. conductrix* 186b possesses 16 chromosomes ($9 + 14/2 = 16$).** `[Fragile]`  
  In line 186, the initial telomere search finds $9 + 13 + 8 = 30$ contigs; after TELOMORE adds a telomere to one contig, the authors update the 1-telomere count from 13 to 14 *without* decrementing the 0-telomere count from 8 to 7 (or incrementing the 2-telomere count from 9 to 10), resulting in $9 + 14 + 8 = 31$ contigs in Table 1 (line 78) and line 104 for a 30-contig assembly. Moreover, pairing 14 single-telomere contigs into 7 chromosomes without Hi-C or syntenic scaffolding against the 20-contig *M. tetrahymenae* assembly is speculative.
* **Claim 3: *M. conductrix* 186b shares 99.87% whole-genome ANI with *M. conductrix* SAG 241.80, and the discrepancy in gene count (12,545 vs. 9,217) is largely driven by false gene fusion calls in earlier annotations.** `[Well Supported]`  
  FastANI and HiFi read mapping confirm ~99.8–99.9% nucleotide identity between 186b and SAG 241.80, while `fdfBLAST` and mean gene length comparisons (3,696 bp vs. 5,247 bp) demonstrate that 21.5% of SAG 241.80 gene models represent amalgamated ORFs that split into distinct genes under RNA-seq-guided BRAKER3 prediction.
* **Claim 4: The 10.7 Mbp discrepancy between the LVgs k-mer genome size estimate (51.9 Mbp) and the 62.57 Mbp assembly is explained by the 8 short variant/accessory contigs.** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  In line 102, the authors note that the 8 short contigs (<100 kbp) collectively sum to only **~315 kbp**, yet in the next sentences they suggest that "The inclusion of these variant contigs may also lead to variant bioinformatic completion size estimates, as seen in our LVgs analyses [51.9 Mbp] compared to the total genome assembly recovered [62.57 Mbp]." A 315 kbp set of low-coverage contigs cannot mathematically account for a **10.67 Mbp (17.1%)** difference in genome size, nor does k-mer counting on raw HiFi reads depend on which contigs are retained in the downstream assembly.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

1. **Contig Count Arithmetic Error in Telomere Analysis ($9 + 14 + 8 = 31 \neq 30$):**  
   In Table 1 (line 78) and Results (line 104), the number of contigs with 2, 1, and 0 telomeres for *M. conductrix* 186b is reported as **9, 14, 8**, which sums to **31 contigs**, contradicting the total of **30 contigs** reported in line 70. The origin of this error is visible in the Methods (line 186): initial searches identified "9 contigs with telomere-like signal at both ends, 13 at one end, and 8 with no detectable signal" ($9 + 13 + 8 = 30$). When `TELOMORE` "was only able to add a telomere signal to the end of one additional contig," that contig had to move either from the 0-telomere bin to the 1-telomere bin (yielding **9, 14, 7**) or from the 1-telomere bin to the 2-telomere bin (yielding **10, 12, 8**). Note also that Table 1 (line 78) has off-by-one telomere sum errors for *Chlorella vulgaris* ($1 + 11 + 33 = 45 \neq 44$ contigs) and *Chlorella sorokiniana* 1602 ($2 + 21 + 135 = 158 \neq 159$ contigs).
2. **Unresolved 10.67 Mbp Discrepancy Between k-mer Genome Size (51.9 Mbp) and Assembly Length (62.57 Mbp):**  
   Both *M. conductrix* 186b (62.57 Mbp) and *M. conductrix* SAG 241.80 (61.02 Mbp) assemble to ~61–62.6 Mbp, yet `LVgs v1.0.0` ($k=27$) on the HiFi reads estimates 51.9 Mbp (line 188). As noted above, attributing a 10.67 Mbp difference to 315 kbp of low-coverage contigs (line 102) is mathematically invalid. Furthermore, BUSCO Duplicated scores in 186b are roughly double to triple those of SAG 241.80 across all three databases (Eukaryota D: $2.4\%$ vs. $0.8\%$; Viridiplantae D: $4.5\%$ vs. $2.1\%$; Chlorophyta D: $3.6\%$ vs. $1.7\%$; OMARK Duplicated: $4.39\%$ vs. $2.94\%$, Table 1). Because no k-mer multiplicity spectrum (e.g., GenomeScope2/Merqury) or whole-genome self-alignment is presented, the authors have not tested whether ~1.5–2 Mbp of the 62.57 Mbp assembly (relative to the 61.02 Mbp SAG 241.80 assembly) represents unpurged segmental duplications/haplotigs or high-GC k-mer coverage bias in `LVgs`.
3. **Fragmented Chloroplast Assembly (56.2 kbp vs. 123.6 kbp) Due to Pre-Assembly BlobToolKit GC Filtering:**  
   In line 178, the initial 101.15 Mbp HiFiASM assembly was partitioned by GC cutoffs in BlobToolKit into putative nuclear (~65% GC), mitochondrial (~30% GC), and chloroplast (~34% GC) scaffolds, and SAMtools was used to extract only the HiFi reads mapping to each GC-filtered subset before running `MitoHiFi`. Chloroplast genomes contain inverted repeats (rRNA operons) and regions with elevated GC content (>45–50% GC); filtering out scaffolds outside the ~34% GC blob before read extraction strips reads spanning the inverted repeats and higher-GC regions, explaining why `MitoHiFi` recovered less than half of the chloroplast genome (56,167 bp out of ~123,552 bp, line 122) despite $168\times$ HiFi coverage.
4. **Polishing High-Accuracy PacBio HiFi Contigs with Higher-Error ONT Reads via Medaka:**  
   In line 184, the HiFiASM assembly (built from $168\times$ PacBio HiFi CCS reads with >99.9% base accuracy) was polished with one round of `Medaka v2.2.1` using Oxford Nanopore reads ($59\times$), followed by `Pilon v1.24` with Illumina reads. Polishing a HiFi assembly with ONT reads in Medaka is a known anti-pattern that frequently introduces homopolymer and methylation-motif base errors that short-read Pilon polishing cannot fully revert in repetitive regions. No Merqury consensus quality value (QV) before and after Medaka/Pilon polishing is reported to verify that polishing improved base accuracy.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

* **N50 (L50) Typo for *Micractinium* sp. CCAP 211/92 in Table 1:** In Table 1 (line 73), *Micractinium* sp. CCAP 211/92 has a total length of $50,300,000\text{ bp}$, 227 contigs, and a **Largest contig of 1,261,288 bp** (line 71), yet its **N50 is listed as 3,623,000 (46)**. An N50 of 3.62 Mbp cannot exceed the largest contig (1.26 Mbp), nor can $\text{L50} = 46$ with an N50 of 3.62 Mbp fit inside a 50.3 Mbp genome ($46 \times 3.62\text{ Mbp} > 166\text{ Mbp}$). The true N50 is likely ~362,300 bp.
* **Inconsistent Mean Gene Size and OMARK Numbers Between Text and Table 1:**  
  * Line 115 states a mean gene length of **3,688 bp**, whereas Table 1 (line 89) and line 127 state **3,696 bp**.  
  * Line 106 states that "OMARK v0.4.1 analyses identified a score of **80.2%** completion (Table 1)," whereas Table 1 (line 83) reports Single: $80.62\%$, Duplicated: $4.39\%$ (total complete = $85.01\%$), and Missing: $14.99\%$.
* **Retention of 3 Ultra-Low Coverage Contigs in Primary Nuclear Assembly:** Three short contigs have ONT coverage of $4.2\times$, HiFi coverage of $15.0\times$, and Illumina coverage of $25.2\times$ (vs. $168\times$ HiFi mean, line 102) and lack gene annotations or telomeres, yet were kept in the primary nuclear assembly rather than separated into an unplaced/contaminant file.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Fix the Telomere Contig Count Arithmetic and Perform Syntenic Alignment to Validate Chromosome Count:**
   * **WHY:** Reporting $9 + 14 + 8 = 31$ contigs in a 30-contig assembly (Table 1, line 78; text, line 104) invalidates the arithmetic used to claim 16 chromosomes.
   * **WHAT:** Audit the `TAPESTRY`/`quartet`/`TELOMORE` outputs to report the exact distribution of 2-, 1-, and 0-telomere contigs summing to 30, and fix the off-by-one sums for *C. vulgaris* and *C. sorokiniana* in Table 1.
   * **HOW:** Align the 22 nuclear contigs (9 two-telomere + 13/14 one-telomere) against the 20-contig *M. tetrahymenae* assembly (`GCA_047372815.1`, which has 12 two-telomere contigs) and *M. conductrix* SAG 241.80 using `minimap2`/`JupiterPlot` or `SyRI` to test whether the single-telomere contigs syntenically pair as chromosome arms across centromeric/rDNA breaks.
2. **Investigate the 10.67 Mbp k-mer vs. Assembly Size Discrepancy and Elevated Duplicated BUSCOs:**
   * **WHY:** The 8 short contigs total 315 kbp and cannot explain why `LVgs` estimates 51.9 Mbp while the assembly is 62.57 Mbp (with $3.6\text{–}4.5\%$ duplicated BUSCOs).
   * **WHAT:** Remove the incorrect explanation in line 102 attributing the 10.7 Mbp `LVgs` difference to the 315 kbp contigs, and run `Merqury` / `GenomeScope2` on the HiFi and Illumina reads.
   * **HOW:** Plot the k-mer spectrum and copy-number k-mer spectra (`Merqury`) comparing the reads against the 62.57 Mbp assembly to determine (a) consensus QV before and after Medaka/Pilon polishing, (b) whether the 3.6% duplicated Chlorophyta BUSCOs reside on a single duplicated contig/haplotig compared to SAG 241.80 (1.7% duplicated), and (c) why `LVgs` underestimates the ~61–62 Mbp genome size by ~10 Mbp.
3. **Reassemble the Chloroplast Genome from Unfiltered HiFi/ONT Reads:**
   * **WHY:** Filtering scaffolds by a ~34% GC cutoff in BlobToolKit before extracting reads excluded the higher-GC inverted repeat regions of the plastome, truncating the chloroplast assembly to 56.2 kbp (vs. ~123.6 kbp in *M. simplicissimum*).
   * **WHAT:** Run `MitoHiFi` (or `GetOrganelle` / `oatk`) directly on the complete, unfiltered HiFi and ONT read sets using `NC_071969.1` as the seed reference.
   * **HOW:** Extract all reads mapping to the *M. simplicissimum* plastome (`minimap2 -ax map-hifi`) without prior BlobToolKit GC filtering, assemble with `HiFiASM`/`Flye`, and circularize the full ~120 kbp quadripartite plastome.
4. **Correct Table 1 and Text Discrepancies:**
   * **WHY:** Numerical typos in benchmark tables reduce the utility of a reference genome paper.
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Correct the N50 (L50) of *Micractinium* sp. CCAP 211/92 in Table 1 (line 73), reconcile the mean gene length (3,688 bp in line 115 vs. 3,696 bp in Table 1 and line 127), and harmonize the OMARK completion percentage (80.2% in line 106 vs. 80.62% single / 85.01% complete in Table 1).
