## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript reports a chromosome-level nuclear and organellar genome assembly for an axenic culture of the chlorophyte photobiont *Trebouxia* sp. 'A48' (*T. decolorans* species complex, clade A), isolated from the sunburst lichen *Xanthoria parietina*. Using 22.4 Gbp of Oxford Nanopore PromethION long reads assembled with Hifiasm v0.24.0 and manually curated for chimeric plastid-nuclear and internal telomeric misassemblies, the authors recover a 69.1 Mbp nuclear genome across 20 contigs (N50 = 3.7 Mbp, 16 contigs bearing `CCCTAAA` telomeric arrays at both ends, 97.2% BUSCO `chlorophyta_odb10` completeness, 0.6% duplication) along with circular plastid (320.5 kbp) and mitochondrial (109.5 kbp) genomes. Supported by 8 Gbp of Illumina PE150 RNA-seq data, `funannotate` predicts 12,820 nuclear protein-coding genes. The authors compare 'A48' against the metagenome-assembled *Trebouxia* sp. SL0000003 (`PRJEB86095`) and 23 chlorophyte proteomes, infer haploidy via variant allele frequencies, catalog meiotic (`DMC1`, `MND1`, `ZIP4`, `HOP2`, `GEX1`, `HAP2`), flagellar (42 genes), and pyrenoid carbon-concentrating mechanism (CCM; bestrophin-like channels, carbonic anhydrases, `HLA3`, `LCIA`) genes, phylogenetically validate two ancient fungal-to-algal horizontal gene transfers (HGTs), and characterize 103 predicted secreted proteins.

The assembly contiguity (16 of 20 contigs telomere-to-telomere) and the deposit of a live axenic photobiont strain at SAG (`NA 2025.008`) represent valuable community resources, and the biological interpretations—such as noting that C4-pathway enzymes are housekeeping enzymes across non-C4 eukaryotes and that flagellated cells may represent asexual zoospores—are commendably measured. However, the manuscript is held back by (1) the absence of deposited raw reads, genome assembly, and annotations in any public database at the time of review (*"submission pending"*), as well as the omission of Table 1 from the manuscript text; (2) the lack of base-level consensus quality (QV) or homopolymer frameshift validation for an unpolished ONT-only assembly; (3) an asymmetric gene annotation comparison with *T.* sp. SL0000003 that inflates lineage-specific orthogroup counts; and (4) methodological flaws in the long-read ploidy calling pipeline.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: The nuclear genome of *Trebouxia* sp. 'A48' is assembled into 20 chromosome-scale contigs (69.1 Mbp), 16 of which are telomere-to-telomere, with circular plastid and mitochondrial genomes.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Manual inspection of coverage, GC content, and internal `CCCTAAA` repeats resolved chimeric joins (Fig. S1), yielding 20 nuclear contigs with 97.2% BUSCO completeness and strong macro-synteny with *T.* sp. SL0000003 across 19 of 20 contigs (Fig. 3c). Base-level QV and indels remain unquantified (Defect 2).
* **Claim 2: *Trebouxia* sp. 'A48' is haploid and retains a nearly complete repertoire of meiotic, gamete-fusion, and flagellar genes.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  The low BUSCO duplication rate (0.6%), absence of a 50% heterozygous allele frequency peak (Fig. 2e), and identification of `DMC1`, `MND1`, `ZIP4`, `HOP2`, `GEX1`, `HAP2`, and 42 flagellar genes support a haploid sexual/zoosporic capacity, though the VarScan ploidy pipeline applies short-read duplicate removal to ONT reads and an inconsistent coverage filter (Defect 3).
* **Claim 3: Two *Trebouxia* genes (an oxidoreductase-like gene and a sulfite efflux/TDT transporter-like gene) originate from ancient fungal-to-algal horizontal gene transfer.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Physical linkage on contiguous nuclear contigs flanked by canonical chlorophyte genes (Fig. 4b) rules out fungal contaminant contigs, and maximum-likelihood phylogenies across expanded NCBI nr/nt databases nest the chlorophyte clade inside basal fungi rather than Lecanoromycetes (Fig. 4c–d).
* **Claim 4: *T.* sp. 'A48' possesses 1,864 unique orthogroups compared to 448 in *T.* sp. SL0000003, and its 103-protein secretome is significantly enriched in hydrolases and redox enzymes.** `[Fragile]`  
  Because *T.* sp. SL0000003 was annotated without RNA-seq by supplying the *T.* sp. 'A48' proteome as homology evidence (Methods line 51), gene models absent or diverged in 'A48' were systematically under-predicted in SL0000003. Furthermore, Fig. 5b reports secretome functional enrichment via `ClusterProfiler` without providing exact $p$-values, FDR values, or background gene universe sizes in the text.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Unreleased Primary Genome Data ("Submission Pending") and Missing Table 1 (Pillar 7)
* **Defect:** For a genome resource manuscript, the primary scientific deliverable is the annotated reference sequence. Yet Line 145 states: *"The genome assembly and annotation and the raw data will be made publicly available (submission pending the assignment of a permanent SAG collection number)."* No NCBI/ENA/DDBJ BioProject, BioSample, SRA, or GenBank/ENA assembly accession numbers are provided for *T.* sp. 'A48'. Furthermore, `Table 1` is cited three times in the Results (lines 80, 87, 88) for core assembly and annotation metrics comparing *T.* sp. 'A48' and *T.* sp. SL0000003, but **Table 1 is completely missing from the manuscript**.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Peer reviewers and readers cannot verify or reuse a genome resource paper when the sequence data are not deposited in an INSDC repository and the summary metrics table is missing.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Deposit the raw ONT reads, Illumina RNA-seq reads, nuclear/organellar assemblies, and GFF3/FASTA annotations in ENA/NCBI immediately (INSDC BioProject/SRA submissions do not require a permanent culture collection ID—the temporary strain ID `SAG NA 2025.008` can be updated post-submission) and insert `Table 1` into the main text.

### 2. Lack of Base-Level Consensus Quality (QV) and Indel Assessment for an ONT-Only Assembly (Pillar 3 & Pillar 5)
* **Defect:** The assembly relies solely on Oxford Nanopore PromethION reads (`FLO-PRO114M`, Dorado v7.4.13) assembled with Hifiasm v0.24.0 (lines 35, 42). The Methods do not specify which Dorado basecalling accuracy model (`fast`, `hac`, or `sup`) or Hifiasm mode (`--ont`) was used, nor was any short-read genomic polishing performed. Even with R10.4.1 (`FLO-PRO114M`) chemistry, unpolished ONT assemblies can retain residual homopolymer indels that cause artificial frameshifts during `funannotate` gene prediction.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** BUSCO completeness (97.2%) only checks conserved single-copy orthologous protein presence and is insensitive to base-level consensus errors and frameshifts in non-BUSCO genes.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Specify the exact Dorado model and Hifiasm flags in Methods; align the 8 Gbp of high-accuracy Illumina PE150 RNA-seq reads to the nuclear assembly to calculate the coding-region base-level QV and homopolymer indel rate (using `Merqury` on transcribed $k$-mers or `bcftools` variant calling of RNA-seq reads against the assembly); and report the proportion of predicted gene models showing fragmented/split ORFs relative to UniProt/Chlorophyta orthologs.

### 3. Asymmetric Gene Annotation Pipeline Biases Orthogroup Comparisons with *T.* sp. SL0000003 (Pillar 2 & Pillar 5)
* **Defect:** In Methods line 51, the authors state that *T.* sp. SL0000003 was annotated by skipping the RNA-seq `funannotate train` and `update` steps and instead supplying *"the predicted proteome of T. sp. 'A48' as evidence for the 'predict' module."* In Results lines 88–89, they then compare the resulting proteomes in OrthoFinder and report 1,864 orthogroups unique to 'A48' versus only 448 unique to SL0000003. Training Augustus with 'A48' RNA-seq for 'A48' while forcing SL0000003 gene prediction to rely on 'A48' protein homology guarantees that 'A48' will appear to have more genes and more unique gene families.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Asymmetric annotation evidence creates an artificial disparity in predicted gene counts and species-specific orthogroups.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Re-run `funannotate predict` on *T.* sp. SL0000003 using the pre-trained Augustus parameters from *T.* sp. 'A48' *plus* mapped RNA-seq reads from 'A48' (since both belong to the *T. decolorans* complex) and a multi-species Chlorophyta protein database rather than the 'A48' proteome alone, or validate putative 'A48'-unique genes by tblastn/miniprot searches directly against the unannotated SL0000003 genomic contigs before claiming gene family loss/gain.

### 4. Flawed Long-Read Ploidy Pipeline and Unreported Enrichment Statistics (Pillar 3, Pillar 4 & Pillar 7)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Ploidy analysis (Methods line 53):* The authors aligned ONT long reads with Minimap2, *"removed duplicated reads with Picard v2.21.2"*, called variants with VarScan (`--p-value 0.1 --min-var-freq 0.005`), and retained *"only positions with coverage depth 200-240x."* First, Picard `MarkDuplicates` identifies duplicates by identical 5' alignment coordinates and is invalid for unamplified native long reads with stochastic shearing/pore entry ends. Second, line 42 states that nuclear contigs had coverage **180–210x**, meaning a 200–240x filter excludes the majority of the nuclear genome. Third, `--min-var-freq 0.005` (0.5%) captures sequencing error noise rather than the 0.1–0.9 biallelic spectrum needed for ploidy inference (`Smudgeplot` or `nQuire`).
  2. *Missing enrichment statistics (Results line 115):* Secretome GO/functional overrepresentation (Fig. 5b) is stated without sample sizes, hypergeometric $p$-values, or Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted $q$-values.
  3. *Duplicated Abstract (lines 5–13):* The four bullet points of the Abstract are repeated verbatim as four paragraphs immediately below the keywords.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Applying short-read duplicate removal and an out-of-range coverage filter to long reads distorts allele frequency histograms, and omitting $p$/$q$-values prevents assessment of secretome enrichment.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Re-compute the allele frequency histogram (Fig. 2e) without Picard deduplication across the full modal nuclear coverage range (e.g., 150–230x) restricted to biallelic SNPs with minor allele frequency $\in [0.10, 0.90]$ (or run `nQuire`/`Smudgeplot`); add exact hypergeometric $p$-values and BH-adjusted $q$-values from `ClusterProfiler` to line 115 and Fig. 5b; and remove the duplicated Abstract paragraphs in lines 7–13.
