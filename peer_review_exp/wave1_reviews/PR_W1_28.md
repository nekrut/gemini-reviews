## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript presents `amr.watch` (`https://amr.watch`), a continuously updated interactive web platform built on top of `Pathogenwatch` that harvests, assembles, quality-controls, types, and screens publicly deposited Illumina whole-genome sequencing (WGS) reads from 15 WHO priority bacterial pathogens in the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA/INSDC). Polling the ENA Portal API every four hours for paired-end Illumina genomic runs with decodable collection year ($\ge 2010$), country location, and $\ge 20\times$ coverage, the pipeline assembles reads with SPAdes v3.15.3, verifies species/serotype identity via Speciator v4.0.0 and SISTR v1.1.1, assigns strain nomenclature (7-gene MLST, GPSC for *S. pneumoniae*, LIN clonal groups for *K. pneumoniae*, and GenoTyphi for *S. Typhi*), and calls curated antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes and point mutations via AMRFinderPlus v3.10.23. Surveying the database as of 31 March 2025, the authors report $620,700$ qualifying genomes while transparently documenting that $605,154$ public genomes were excluded due to missing geotemporal metadata and $12,348$ due to species misannotation. They quantify the severe global and socioeconomic skew of public pathogen sequencing ($89.6\%$ from high-income countries, with $43.8\%$ of those from the USA and $22.0\%$ from the UK; $89/249$ ISO countries with zero qualifying genomes since 2010, rising to $146$ countries since 2020) as well as clinical AMR ascertainment bias (e.g., $60.5\%$ of *K. pneumoniae* genomes carry carbapenemases vs. $28.7\%$ estimated global nosocomial resistance), and illustrate regional clone and mechanism trends (*K. pneumoniae* ST11/*bla*KPC-2 in China, ST258 in the USA, *bla*NDM-1 in Thailand, *bla*OXA-48 in Spain, and the post-2013 rise of *bla*NDM-5). All assemblies, variant calls, and accession lists are openly downloadable.

As a community bioinformatics resource, `amr.watch` provides a well-engineered, standardized, and transparent lens into public bacterial WGS archives, and the authors' candid quantification of metadata attrition ($49\%$ of archived genomes lacking year/country), travel-associated distortion (*S. Typhi* in the UK), and resistance ascertainment bias is a major strength. The primary technical limitations are (1) the use of a frozen December 2021 AMRFinderPlus reference database (`2021-12-21.114`) and `v3.10.23` software to evaluate genomes collected through March 2025, alongside framing around the 2017 rather than 2024 WHO priority pathogen list; (2) the plotting of raw country/year resistance proportions in the web interface and Figs. 3–5 without binomial confidence intervals, minimum-denominator thresholds, or travel-import flags; (3) the exclusion of Oxford Nanopore and PacBio long-read datasets; and (4) an unexplained 140-genome discrepancy in the World Bank income-group breakdown (line 74) and the absence of a public source-code repository link for the `amr.watch` web/ingestion application itself.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: Automated ingestion, SPAdes assembly, species verification, and QC filtering of INSDC paired-end Illumina WGS data through 31 March 2025 yields 620,700 high-quality genomes with geotemporal metadata, while 605,154 genomes fail geotemporal metadata completeness and 12,348 fail species verification.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Documented with clear stage-by-stage filtering criteria (`amr.watch/summary/all`, S1 Appendix, S1–S3 Tables) and downloadable accession-level CSV tables.
* **Claim 2: Publicly archived bacterial pathogen genomes exhibit extreme socioeconomic and geographic skew (89.6% high-income countries; 146/249 ISO countries with zero qualifying genomes since 2020) and substantial pre-selection bias toward drug-resistant isolates.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Quantified across all 15 priority pathogens and benchmarked against global clinical surveillance estimates (e.g., 60.5% carbapenemase prevalence in *K. pneumoniae* genomes vs. 28.7% nosocomial clinical prevalence) and foodborne network volumes (GenomeTrakr/PATH-SAFE).
* **Claim 3: `amr.watch` accurately captures established regional and temporal dynamics of high-risk clones (e.g., *K. pneumoniae* ST11 in China vs. ST258 in the USA) and AMR determinants (*bla*KPC-2, *bla*NDM-1, *bla*OXA-48, and emerging *bla*NDM-5).** `[Well Supported]`  
  The regional dominance of ST11/*bla*KPC-2 in China ($69.9\%$ and $88.4\%$), ST258 in the USA ($30.3\%$), *bla*NDM-1 in Thailand ($61.4\%$), and *bla*OXA-48 in Spain ($62.8\%$) aligns closely with independent multicenter molecular epidemiology studies.
* **Claim 4: The `amr.watch` platform delivers ongoing, contemporary situational awareness of global AMR mechanisms to guide prescribing, stewardship, and drug/vaccine development.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  While valuable for exploratory situational awareness, relying on an AMRFinderPlus database from December 2021 (`2021-12-21.114`) for a platform analyzed in March 2025 means recently emerged $\beta$-lactamase/carbapenemase variants (e.g., novel KPC, NDM, OXA, and PBP3/FtsI variants conferring resistance to cefiderocol or ceftazidime-avibactam, which the authors highlight on line 91) are not captured until the database is updated, and raw country-level proportions can mislead policy users when sample sizes are small or travel-associated.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Outdated AMRFinderPlus Database (`2021-12-21.114`) Used for a March 2025 Surveillance Analysis (Pillar 3 & Pillar 6)
* **Defect:** Methods line 46 states that AMR genes and point mutations were identified using **AMRFinderPlus v3.10.23, database version `2021-12-21.114`**. Evaluating pathogen genomes up to **31 March 2025** (lines 5, 69) with a reference catalog frozen more than three years earlier (December 2021) misses numerous resistance alleles and point mutations added to the NCBI Pathogen Detection Reference Gene Catalog between 2022 and 2025. Similarly, the platform is structured around the **2017** WHO priority pathogen list (line 24) rather than the updated **2024** WHO Bacterial Priority Pathogens List (cited in line 10 as `[3]`).
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** An "always-on" contemporary AMR surveillance platform requires an up-to-date resistance determinant catalog, particularly when highlighting newer agents such as cefiderocol and $\beta$-lactam/$\beta$-lactamase inhibitor combinations (line 91).
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Establish a scheduled (e.g., quarterly) update protocol for `AMRFinderPlus` and its reference database (updating to current 2024/2025 releases), version-stamp the active AMRFinderPlus database version visibly on the `amr.watch` UI and CSV exports, and document a roadmap for aligning the pathogen/drug-class matrix with the 2024 WHO Bacterial Priority Pathogens List.

### 2. Unadjusted Raw Proportions Without Binomial Uncertainty Intervals or Small-Sample Suppression (Pillar 2 & Pillar 4)
* **Defect:** In `amr.watch` choropleth maps (Figs. 1, 5) and temporal trend panels (Figs. 3, 4, 5), the platform displays the raw percentage of genomes matching a selected ST or AMR mechanism per country or per year. Because 146 countries have zero genomes since 2020 and many LMICs have $<10$ genomes per pathogen-year (sourced from single hospital outbreak investigations), displaying raw percentages without uncertainty intervals or minimum-denominator thresholds causes a country with $2/2$ ($100\%$) outbreak isolates carrying *bla*NDM-5 to appear more affected than a country with routine surveillance reporting $500/5,000$ ($10\%$). Likewise, travel-associated isolates in high-income countries (such as the $52.4\%$ of global *S. Typhi* genomes sequenced in the UK, line 74) are mapped to the country of sample collection rather than the country of travel/acquisition.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Raw proportions calculated on small, outbreak-biased, or travel-imported denominators can mislead policy-makers comparing countries or time points.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Add 95% binomial confidence intervals (e.g., Wilson score intervals) to the temporal proportion charts (Figs. 3, 5) and implement a default minimum-denominator filter (e.g., $N \ge 20$ genomes, or hatching/greyscale for low-$N$ countries) when coloring country-level proportion maps. Where ENA metadata fields (`isolation_source`, `host_health_state`, or travel notes) indicate travel-related surveillance (especially for *S. Typhi* and *Shigella*), flag or annotate this caveat directly in the organism dashboard.

### 3. Arithmetic Gap in Income-Group Genome Counts and Missing Web Application Source Code Link (Pillar 7)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Unaccounted 140 genomes in income-group sum (Results line 74):* Line 74 states that out of $620,700$ total genomes, $556,307$ originated from high-income countries, $39,843$ from upper-middle-income, $18,988$ from lower-middle-income, and $5,422$ from low-income countries. Summing these four categories gives $556,307 + 39,843 + 18,988 + 5,422 = \mathbf{620,560}$, leaving **$140$ genomes** unaccounted for without explanation (presumably from ISO territories or countries unclassified by the World Bank, such as Venezuela).
  2. *Missing repository link for `amr.watch` code:* While the SPAdes assembly pipeline URL (`https://gitlab.com/cgps/ghru/pipelines/assembly`) is provided (line 36) and data are downloadable as CSVs, no public code repository (GitHub/GitLab) or software license is cited in the Methods or Data Availability section for the `amr.watch` Next.js/React application or the ENA API harvesting/QC scripts.
  3. *Minor typographical errors:* Discussion line 105 contains `KlebNet for k. pneumoniae` (lowercase `k` and non-italicized species name) and `genomic dats resources` (`dats` $\rightarrow$ `data`).
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Exact numerical accounting across demographic strata and open source code archival are standard requirements for bioinformatics platform papers.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Note in line 74 (and the Fig. 2C legend) that $140$ genomes ($0.02\%$) originated from countries/territories without a World Bank income classification; provide a public GitLab/GitHub link and archival Zenodo DOI for the `amr.watch` codebase and curation tables (`S1` and `S2 Tables`); and fix the typos `k. pneumoniae` $\rightarrow$ *K. pneumoniae* and `dats` $\rightarrow$ `data` on line 105.
