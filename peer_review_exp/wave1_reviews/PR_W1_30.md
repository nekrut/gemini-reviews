## 1. Blinded Metadata Perception
* **Perceived Study Design:** Experimental molecular biology and genomics methods paper presenting an on-bead, circularization-free Ribosome Profiling (RP) and selective ribosome profiling (SeRP) library preparation workflow that combines 3' biotinylated barcoded linker ligation (`L1`), streptavidin magnetic bead immobilization, solid-phase reverse transcription, and single-stranded DNA splint-bridge ligation (`L2`/`L3`) using T4 DNA ligase, benchmarked across *Escherichia coli*, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, human HEK293-T cells, and mouse brain tissue across standard and low-input (down to 12.5 fmol / 10 ng RNA) regimes. *(Note: The manuscript text at lines 225–251 contains unredacted author/funding metadata, which was ignored during strictly internal technical evaluation.)*
* **Perceived Rigor & Scope:** The solid-phase magnetic-bead workflow and splint-assisted T4 DNA ligation (`L2`/`L3` with variable overhangs and optional LNA primer-dimer blocking) represent a genuinely practical, well-engineered improvement over classical multi-gel, CircLigase-dependent ribosome profiling protocols. However, the quantitative benchmarking contains three notable methodological and reporting flaws: (1) the headline "~30% vs. 2% footprint-to-PCR conversion efficiency" calculation (Methods line 101; Results line 182) divides the mass (ng) of a ~170 bp double-stranded DNA PCR amplicon ($\text{MW} \approx 105\text{ kDa}$) directly by $2^{n-1}$ and the input mass (ng) of a 28 nt single-stranded RNA control oligo ($\text{MW} \approx 9\text{ kDa}$) without molar conversion, which inflates absolute molar conversion efficiency by $\sim 11.7\times$; (2) Table 2 (lines 18–35) omits the final post-PCR 6% PAGE gel extraction and overnight $-70^\circ\text{C}$ isopropanol precipitation (still required in Methods line 101), enzymatic `L1` pre-adenylation (`Mth` RNA ligase, line 108), LNA blocker cost/steps, and Cas9 `Ribocutter` rRNA depletion (4.5 h + an extra PAGE purification + re-PCR, line 127) when claiming a reduction from 5 days to 2 days and ~30% cost; and (3) critical volume unit errors appear in the bench protocol (`780 ml of isopropanol` in a 1.5 ml tube on line 132; `1 volume μl` on line 74).

## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict
This manuscript introduces a streamlined ribosome profiling (RP) library construction method that replaces intermediate PAGE purifications (after linker ligation and reverse transcription) and intramolecular cDNA circularization (CircLigase I/II) with on-bead reactions on streptavidin-conjugated magnetic beads followed by splint-assisted intermolecular T4 DNA ligation of a double-stranded adapter (`L2`/`L3`) directly to the 3' end of the immobilized cDNA. The authors demonstrate high reproducibility ($R^2 = 0.96$ between replicates at 2 pmol input; $R = 0.96\text{–}0.98$ down to 50 fmol of footprints and 10 ng of input RNA prior to footprint gel purification) across bacteria, yeast, mammalian cultured cells, and mouse brain tissue, as well as compatibility with selective ribosome profiling (SeRP) of the yeast nascent-chain-associated complex (NAC) and Cas9-guided (`Ribocutter`) rRNA depletion.

The biochemical design of the protocol is sound and solves a well-known bottleneck in ribosome profiling (the high cost and variable efficiency of CircLigase and losses during multiple crush-and-soak PAGE extractions). Nevertheless, before publication, the authors must correct the mathematical formula used to claim a "30% footprint conversion rate" (which conflates mass and molarity across a 28 nt ssRNA input and a ~170 bp dsDNA PCR product), revise Table 2 so that all required steps (including the final PAGE gel extraction, overnight precipitation, and `L1` adenylation) are transparently accounted for, report quantitative 3-nucleotide periodicity and UMI-deduplicated library complexity metrics across the low-input titration series, and fix dangerous unit typos in the step-by-step protocol.

### Evaluation of Core Scientific Claims
1. **Immobilizing 3'-biotinylated-linker-ligated footprints on streptavidin beads and ligating a splinted dsDNA adapter (`L2`/`L3`) via T4 DNA ligase yields highly reproducible ribosome profiling libraries across *E. coli*, *S. cerevisiae*, HEK293-T, and mouse brain:** `[Well Supported]` — Gene-level RPM correlations between technical/biological replicates are $R^2 = 0.96$ ($r \approx 0.98$; Fig. 2C), correlations with the classical CircLigase protocol are $R = 0.91$ (Fig. 2G), and SeRP enrichment profiles of NAC on *APE2* and *CYS4* match published datasets (Fig. 2H–J).
2. **The new protocol achieves a ~30% conversion rate of input RNA footprints into amplifiable library molecules compared to ~2% for the classical protocol:** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — According to Methods line 101, the conversion rate was calculated by quantifying the ~160–180 bp dsDNA final PCR band (ng) on a gel, dividing by $2^{n-1}$ (where $n$ is the PCR cycle number), and dividing by the input mass (ng) of the 28 nt ssRNA control oligonucleotide (`rOligo`). Because a ~170 bp dsDNA amplicon has a molecular weight of $\approx 105\text{ kDa}$ whereas a 28 nt ssRNA oligo has a molecular weight of $\approx 9.0\text{ kDa}$ (an $11.7\times$ mass ratio per molecule), dividing nanograms of dsDNA by nanograms of ssRNA without converting to femtomoles overestimates the true molar conversion efficiency by more than an order of magnitude ($\sim 30\% / 11.7 \approx 2.6\%$ true molar yield). In addition, assuming 100% PCR doubling efficiency ($E = 2.0$) penalizes the classical protocol (amplified for 9–11 cycles vs. 5–7 cycles for the new protocol; Fig. 4C) if late-cycle PCR efficiency plateaus below 2.0.
3. **The protocol eliminates PAGE-based purifications and reduces total library preparation time from 5 days to 2 days and reagent cost to ~30% (31.2 vs. 86.7 relative units in Table 2):** `[Overstated]` — The workflow eliminates *intermediate* PAGE purifications (after L1 ligation), but it still requires two PAGE crush-and-soak gel extractions with overnight $-70^\circ\text{C}$ isopropanol precipitations: (1) initial 15% denaturing PAGE footprint isolation (line 74), and (2) final 6% PAGE-TBE PCR library purification and overnight precipitation (line 101)—plus a *third* 6% PAGE-TBE gel extraction and overnight precipitation if Cas9 `Ribocutter` rRNA depletion is performed (line 127). Table 2 (lines 18–35) omits the post-PCR PAGE extraction and overnight precipitation, omits the enzymatic 5'-adenylation of linker `L1` using the NEB 5' DNA Adenylation Kit (`Mth` RNA ligase, line 108, which is a major cost driver unless synthesized pre-adenylated), and omits the LNA primer-dimer blocking step (lines 113–115).
4. **Reliable ribosome profiling data can be generated from as little as 50 fmol of isolated footprints or 10 ng of RNase-digested RNA without loss of ribosome profiling quality:** `[Moderately Supported]` — While gene-level RPKM correlations remain high ($R = 0.89\text{–}0.98$, Fig. 4A–B, E–F), gene-level correlation is a coarse metric that can remain high even when library complexity collapses or 3-nucleotide reading-frame periodicity degrades. Despite incorporating degenerate UMI bases in `L1` (5 N's) and `L2_v2` (5 N's) (Table 3, lines 44–49) and running `umi_tools dedup` (line 154), the manuscript does not report the PCR duplicate rate, unique molecule yield, or quantitative 3-nt reading-frame periodicity (% in frame 0) across the 12.5–1000 fmol and 10–10,000 ng titration series.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Major Methodological & Quantitative Defects

* **Defect 1: Mass-vs.-Molarity Error ($11.7\times$ Inflation) and Idealized $2^{n-1}$ PCR Assumption in the Footprint Conversion Rate Calculation**
  * **Location:** Methods line 101; Results lines 180–183; Figures 1B and 4C–D; Abstract line 2.
  * **WHY it matters:** Methods line 101 states: *"To estimate the conversion rate, the amount of sequenceable PCR product variants per cycle was determined by dividing the product amount by $2^{n-1}$, where $n$ represents the cycle number. The resulting value was then divided by the input amount used for the library preparation, to obtain the conversion rate."* Because `rOligo` input is a 28 nt single-stranded RNA ($\text{MW} \approx 9.0\text{ kDa}$) and the final PCR product is a ~165–175 bp double-stranded DNA ($\text{MW} \approx 105\text{ kDa}$), 1 fmol of input ssRNA weighs 9.0 pg whereas 1 fmol of dsDNA PCR product weighs 105 pg. Dividing dsDNA mass by ssRNA mass without converting to moles inflates the apparent conversion percentage by $\frac{\text{MW}_{\text{dsDNA}}}{\text{MW}_{\text{ssRNA}}} \approx 11.7\times$. Furthermore, comparing 5–7 PCR cycles (new protocol) against 9–11 PCR cycles (classical protocol) using an idealized $2^{n-1}$ divisor assumes $100\%$ amplification efficiency per cycle; if per-cycle efficiency is $E = 1.80\text{–}1.90$, dividing by $2^{n-1}$ systematically underestimates the pre-PCR cDNA yield of higher-cycle reactions.
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Recalculate the footprint-to-cDNA conversion rate on a strict **molar** basis (fmol pre-PCR cDNA divided by fmol input 28 nt ssRNA) and validate pre-PCR cDNA yield directly via qPCR (or empirical PCR efficiency $E$) rather than assuming $E = 2.0$.
  * **HOW to fix it:** Convert both the input 28 nt ssRNA mass and the quantified 170 bp dsDNA PCR band mass to femtomoles before dividing by $(1+E)^{n-1}$:
    $$\text{Molar Conversion Rate (\%)} = 100 \times \frac{\text{fmol}_{\text{dsDNA PCR product}} / (1 + E)^{n-1}}{\text{fmol}_{\text{28 nt ssRNA input}}}$$
    Update Results line 182, Abstract line 2, and Figure 4D with the molar-corrected values (or explicitly report both the relative fold-improvement between protocols and the true molar conversion percentage).

* **Defect 2: Incomplete Accounting of Time, PAGE Gel Purifications, and Enzymatic Adenylation Costs in Table 2**
  * **Location:** Introduction lines 12–35 (Table 2); Figure 1; Methods lines 74, 101, 108, 113–115, and 127; Results lines 161 and 177.
  * **WHY it matters:** Table 2 claims the new workflow takes "12 hours (~2 days)" and "31.2" cost units because it lists `Final PCR: 1 h, cost 1.3` as the last step. However, in the actual bench protocol (Methods line 101), the final PCR is followed by a **6% PAGE-TBE gel run, band excision, overnight crush-and-soak extraction, and overnight $-70^\circ\text{C}$ isopropanol precipitation** (adding $\ge 16\text{–}20\text{ h}$ and an extra day to both protocols). Moreover, Table 2 sets the cost of linker ligation in the new protocol to `4.2` (just T4 RNA Ligase 2 truncated KQ + RNasin + Dynabeads), omitting: (a) the enzymatic 5'-adenylation of the 5'-phosphorylated/3'-biotinylated `L1` oligo using the NEB 5' DNA Adenylation Kit (`Mth` RNA Ligase, line 108) and Oligo Clean & Concentrator spin columns, (b) the custom 3'-biotin + 5'-phosphate dual modification synthesis cost of barcoded `L1` oligos, and (c) the optional LNA blocking step (line 113) or Cas9 `Ribocutter` step (line 127, which adds 4.5 h + another PAGE gel + re-PCR).
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Update Table 2 and the main text so that all steps actually performed in Methods lines 74–127 are included in the time and cost totals.
  * **HOW to fix it:** In Table 2 (lines 18–35), add a row for `Post-PCR 6% PAGE purification & precipitation` (or state how bead-based SPRI/AMPure size selection can replace it if validated), include the amortized per-reaction cost and preparation time of `L1` enzymatic adenylation (line 108), and add footnotes detailing the additional time/cost when LNA dimer blocking or Cas9 rRNA depletion is used.

* **Defect 3: Missing Quantitative Benchmarks for 3-Nucleotide Periodicity, UMI Deduplication Rate, and Replicate Sample Sizes ($n$) in Low-Input Titration Experiments**
  * **Location:** Results lines 168–191; Figures 2, 3, and 4; Methods lines 151–156.
  * **WHY it matters:** For a ribosome profiling method, the hallmark of bona fide ribosome-protected footprints (as opposed to random RNA degradation fragments or adapter artifacts) is strong 3-nucleotide triplet periodicity across coding sequences (CDS) and low PCR duplication after UMI collapsing. In the low-input experiments (50 fmol to 1 pmol footprints in Fig. 4A–B; 10 ng to 10 $\mu\text{g}$ RNA in Fig. 4E–F; LNA blocking in Fig. 3F–I), the authors show only gene-level log10 RPKM scatterplots and start/stop metagene line plots without reporting: (a) the fraction of in-frame (frame 0) reads, (b) the percentage of unique reads remaining after `umi_tools dedup` (especially critical at 12.5–50 fmol and 10 ng input where PCR cycles increase), or (c) the number of independent replicates ($n$) in Fig. 3E–I and Fig. 4C–F (Fig. 3E and Fig. 4D show error bars without stating $n$ or whether error bars represent SD or SEM).
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Report quantitative 3-nt periodicity, UMI duplication rates, rRNA/linker dimer fractions, and exact sample sizes ($n$) for all benchmarking experiments.
  * **HOW to fix it:** Add a summary benchmark table (or supplementary table) listing for every sequenced library across all four organisms and input dilutions: total raw reads, % linker dimers (`L1-L2`), % rRNA reads, % uniquely mapped CDS reads, **% unique reads post-UMI deduplication**, and **% frame-0 triplet periodicity**; also state exact $n$ and error-bar definitions (SD vs. SEM) in the legends of Figures 3 and 4.

### 3.2 Protocol & Typographical Defects

* **Defect 4: Severe Volume Unit Typos in the Bench Protocol (`780 ml of isopropanol`, `1 volume μl`, `concentrated to 6`)**
  * **Location:** Methods line 74, line 132, and Table 2 line 29.
  * **WHY it matters:** Bench protocols in methods papers are followed verbatim by experimentalists. On line 132, the acid-phenol RNA extraction protocol states: *"About 700 μl of the aqueous phase were transferred to a fresh tube, and 3 μl of GlycoBlue™, 77.7 μl of 3 M NaOAc, pH 5.5, and **780 ml of isopropanol** were added, and vortexed for 15 sec, in a safe-lock 1.5 ml tube."* `780 ml` is a $1000\times$ unit error for **`780 μl`**. Immediately below in the same paragraph (line 132), it states: *"thirty micrograms of RNA were diluted into 300 μl of Polysome buffer, incubated with 1.5 μl RNase I ... and **concentrated to 6** with RNA Clean & Concentrator Kit"* (missing the unit **`6 μl`**). On line 74, the footprint precipitation states: *"in the presence of **1 volume μl** isopropanol"* (should be **`1 volume of isopropanol`**). In Table 2 line 29, `Phophorylation` is misspelled.
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Correct all unit and spelling errors in the Methods section and Table 2.
  * **HOW to fix it:** Change `780 ml of isopropanol` to `780 μl of isopropanol` and `concentrated to 6` to `concentrated to 6 μl` on line 132; change `1 volume μl isopropanol` to `1 volume of isopropanol` on line 74; and fix `Phophorylation` to `Phosphorylation` in Table 2 (line 29).
