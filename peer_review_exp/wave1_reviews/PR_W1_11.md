## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript employs Oxford Nanopore Direct RNA Sequencing (DRS, SQK-RNA002 chemistry) combined with `nanopolish` poly(A) tail length estimation and `ninetails` non-adenosine (mixed-tailing) detection to characterize poly(A) tail dynamics across six human herpesviruses (HSV-1, HSV-2, VZV, HCMV, KSHV, EBV), one non-human primate gammaherpesvirus (HVS), and three comparator viruses from distinct families (SARS-CoV-2, Mpox virus, and human Adenovirus type 41).

### Primary Claims Audit
1. **Claim 1:** Herpesvirus protein-coding mRNAs across alpha-, beta-, and gammaherpesvirus subfamilies uniformly possess extended poly(A) tail length distributions (modal lengths 96–150 nt) compared to uninfected and infected host cellular mRNAs (modal lengths 42–54 nt, except in VZV, HCMV, and SARS-CoV-2 infections). `[Moderately Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Direct comparison of read-level `nanopolish` modal poly(A) tail lengths across viral and host transcriptomes. However, cross-virus comparisons are confounded by heterogeneous cell lines, reactivation vs. de novo lytic infection protocols, disparate multiplicities of infection (MOI 1 to 2500 genomes/cell), and single-replicate designs for several viruses.
2. **Claim 2:** Elongated viral poly(A) tails are not a universal consequence of nascent viral transcription ("mRNA youth") because nuclear-replicating hAdV41 (modal 54–73 nt), cytoplasmic Mpox (modal 68–85 nt), and SARS-CoV-2 sgmRNAs (~50 nt) do not reach herpesviral tail lengths. `[Moderately Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* Time-course comparison of hAdV41 (12, 24, 48 hpi) and Mpox (4, 10 hpi) alongside temporal-class stratification of HSV-1 and HCMV transcripts. Without metabolic labeling (e.g., 4sU-DRS) to directly measure transcript age and turnover rates, the exclusion of transcript age relies on indirect inference across viruses with fundamentally different host shutoff kinetics.
3. **Claim 3:** Herpesviral noncoding RNAs (HCMV RNA4.9, RNA2.7, RNA1.2; KSHV PAN) exhibit divergent poly(A) tail length profiles (e.g., PAN modal 69 nt vs. RNA2.7 modal 156 nt) that do not correlate simply with nuclear retention or translation status. `[Well Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* High-coverage read distributions of abundant viral ncRNAs compared against internal host controls (`NEAT1`, modal 269 nt) and viral mRNAs within the same libraries.
4. **Claim 4:** Non-adenosine "mixed tailing" increases during HCMV infection on both host (6.5% to 13.3%) and viral (6.6% to 12.2%) transcripts and is enriched for 3'-terminal guanosines on specific substrates (HCMV RNA2.7, UL69; host B2M, HSP90AB1), but occurs at insufficient overall frequency (<25% even in HSV-2, and 6–7% in KSHV/VZV) to explain bulk poly(A) tail elongation across herpesviruses. `[Well Supported]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* `ninetails` neural network classification benchmarked against synthetic pure-poly(A) IVT RN7SK standards (~1% false positive rate) and stratified by transcript coverage ($\ge 30$ reads).
5. **Claim 5:** Extended poly(A) tails represent a conserved pan-herpesviral mechanism to advantage viral gene expression and translational efficiency. `[Overstated]`
   - *Epistemic basis:* The manuscript contains zero measurements of translation efficiency, polysome association, PABPC1 occupancy, or mRNA half-life for any of the profiled viruses. Extrapolating steady-state poly(A) tail length to a functional translational advantage contradicts literature showing that in somatic mammalian cells (unlike early oocytes/embryos), poly(A) tail length beyond ~50–60 nt poorly correlates with translational efficiency.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Confounding of Read-Level and Transcript-Level Distributions by Ultra-Abundant Viral Transcripts
In global violin plots (Figures 1–3) and modal summaries (Table S1), the authors pool all aligned protein-coding reads without transcript-level weighting or downsampling. In late-stage lytic herpesvirus infections, a small number of hyper-abundant transcripts (e.g., Kaposin/PAN in KSHV, late glycoprotein or capsid genes in HSV-1/VZV/HCMV) account for a massive fraction of total viral DRS reads. Consequently, a global read-level distribution reflects the poly(A) tail length of the top 5–10 most abundant viral transcripts rather than a uniform property across the viral proteome. Furthermore, herpesvirus genomes are characterized by extensive polycistronic, nested, and coterminal 3' overlapping transcript families (e.g., HSV-1 and HCMV coterminal gene clusters). Aligning 3'-biased Nanopore DRS reads to standard reference transcriptomes using `minimap2 -ax map-ont -L -p 0.99 --secondary=no` without 5'-cap validation causes reads from shorter downstream coterminal transcripts to be ambiguously or preferentially assigned to overlapping isoforms, distorting gene-level and temporal-class stratifications (Figures 4e and 5c).

### 2.2 Absence of Biological Replication and Pseudoreplication in Statistical Testing
For several newly generated datasets (VZV EMC-1 in MeWo, HSV-2 333 in ARPE-19, KSHV reactivation in iSLK.219, EBV reactivation in HEK293, HVS in OMK, and Mpox in NHDF), the Methods and Results do not specify the number of independent biological replicates (only HCMV 72 hpi is explicitly noted in the Figure S1 caption as having two additional biological replicates). Treating tens of thousands of individual Nanopore reads from a single flowcell/infection dish as independent observations constitutes severe pseudoreplication ($N = \text{reads}$ instead of $N = \text{biological replicates}$). While `glmmTMB` is listed in the R package inventory (line 86), no generalized linear mixed-effects model specification, random effects structure (e.g., `(1 | biological_replicate)` or `(1 | transcript_id)`), or formal hypothesis test results are reported in the main text for the primary claims in Figures 1–5. Instead, claims rely entirely on descriptive comparisons of point modes ("modal values of 96–150 vs. 42–52").

### 2.3 Uncontrolled Host Shutoff and Transcript Age Asymmetry
The authors argue against viral mRNA "youth" as the driver of longer poly(A) tails by citing the ~10-hour median half-life of human mRNAs in unperturbed cells and comparing herpesviruses to hAdV41. However, alphaherpesviruses (HSV-1, HSV-2 via vhs/UL41) and gammaherpesviruses (KSHV via SOX/ORF37, EBV via BGLF5) express potent viral endonucleases that actively degrade cytoplasmic host mRNAs and block host transcription/splicing, whereas HCMV and SARS-CoV-2 remodel the host transcriptome via distinct mechanisms (with SARS-CoV-2 NSP1 degrading cytosolic translating host mRNAs while nuclear-retained host RNAs accumulate with long ~170 nt tails, as seen in Figure 2a). Comparing the steady-state poly(A) tail length of newly synthesized late viral transcripts against a decaying or nuclear-trapped remnant pool of host mRNAs without controlling for transcription initiation vs. cytoplasmic decay rates cannot isolate deadenylation resistance from synthesis/export kinetics.

### 2.4 Misapplication of Human-Trained `ninetails` Reclassification to Non-Human Primate and Viral Genomes
In the Methods (line 81), `ninetails` predictions were post-processed using `reclassify_ninetails_data [ref='hsapiens']`. Applying a human-specific reclassification model across viral transcripts with radically different GC content (e.g., HSV-1/2 at ~68% GC vs. VZV at ~46% GC) and local 3'-UTR homopolymer/k-mer contexts near the poly(A) junction risks introducing sequence-context-dependent signal distortions in the ionic current segmentation at the 5' boundary of the poly(A) tail. Moreover, in Figure S2 (line 148), applying a Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn's test with Bonferroni correction across transcripts filtered for $\ge 30$ reads in all timepoints introduces severe survival bias toward hyper-abundant housekeepers (e.g., ribosomal proteins, `B2M`, `HSP90AB1`), which are not representative of the broader cellular transcriptome.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Inconsistent and Typographical Errors in Reagent Concentrations and Gene Symbols:**
   - Line 46 & Line 51: Antibiotic selection concentrations are reported as "250 mg/mL G418" and "100 mg/mL hygromycin"—these are 1,000-fold too high (should be $\mu\text{g/mL}$).
   - Line 125: "HSP9011A-201" is not a valid HGNC gene symbol or Gencode transcript identifier (likely a typographical corruption of `HSP90AA1-201` or `HSP90AB1-201`).
   - Line 146: Duplicate label "(a)" in the figure legend fragment at the end of the manuscript.
2. **Poly(A) Selection Bias Against Short Tails:**
   - Isolating RNA via Oligo(dT) Dynabeads (line 66) prior to DRS systematically depletes transcripts with short poly(A) tails (<15–20 nt). This truncates the left tail of all violin plots and artificially inflates modal estimates for heavily deadenylated populations.
3. **Lack of Explicit Modal Estimation Bandwidth:**
   - "Modal values" of continuous kernel density estimates (or integer-rounded `nanopolish` outputs) depend heavily on bin width or Gaussian kernel bandwidth, which is never specified in the Methods.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Transcript-Weighted and Coterminal-Resolved Poly(A) Analysis:**
   - Recompute all host and viral poly(A) tail length distributions in Figures 1–3 using **transcript-level medians** (weighting each expressed gene equally above a minimum read threshold, e.g., $\ge 20$ reads) alongside raw read-level distributions to prove that the long-tail phenotype is not an artifact of a few hyper-abundant late viral transcripts.
   - For HSV-1 and HCMV (Figures 4–5), restrict gene-level temporal class analyses to unique 5'-end-validated reads (reaching within 50 nt of the annotated TSS) so that nested coterminal transcripts are not cross-contaminated.
2. **Formal Mixed-Effects Statistical Modeling:**
   - Specify and report the exact `glmmTMB` mixed-effects model (e.g., log-normal or gamma family with `transcript_id` and `biological_replicate` as random intercepts) to test whether viral vs. host poly(A) tail length differences are statistically significant across independent biological replicates. Explicitly report the number of biological replicates ($n$) for every virus in Table S1.
3. **Temper Functional Claims on Translation Efficiency:**
   - Revise the Abstract and Discussion to remove unsubstantiated claims that extended poly(A) tails "advantage herpesviral gene expression" via translational stimulation unless direct ribosome profiling / polysome-DRS data are provided. Frame the findings accurately as a characterization of steady-state poly(A) tail length and mixed-tailing stoichiometry.
4. **Correct Methodological Reporting:**
   - Fix the G418/hygromycin unit errors ($\text{mg/mL} \to \mu\text{g/mL}$), correct the `HSP9011A-201` identifier, specify the kernel density bandwidth used to compute modal tail lengths, and deposit all newly generated DRS datasets (MPXV, VZV, HSV-2, KSHV, EBV, HVS) into a public repository (SRA/ENA) with accession numbers listed in a dedicated Data Availability section.
