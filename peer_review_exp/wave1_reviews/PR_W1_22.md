## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study investigates how ADAR-mediated A-to-I RNA editing prevents endogenous double-stranded RNAs (dsRNAs) from activating the cytoplasmic antiviral sensor MDA5. Using an in vitro RNase A/T1 protection assay with recombinant mouse $\text{MDA5}\Delta\text{2CARD}$ on total liver RNA from 8–9-day-old $\text{Adar1}^{-/-};\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ and control $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ mice ($n=3$ biological replicates), the authors identify 1,190 peaks uniquely protected in ADAR1-deficient liver, ~63–64% of which overlap repetitive elements (predominantly LINEs, SINEs, and LTRs). Selecting representative inverted SINEs in the 3' UTRs of *Rnf168* and *Knl1* and an LTR hairpin in *Zkscan3*—transcribed with loopback-deficient $\text{T7}^{\text{FAFAG}}$ polymerase (G47A/884G)—they demonstrate that unedited dsRNAs stimulate $\text{MDA5}\Delta\text{CARD}$ ATPase activity and filament assembly in vitro and induce ISGs (*Stat1*, *Irf7*) and G3BP1 stress granules in transfected MEFs. In vitro editing of these substrates by any of the three recombinant isoforms (ADAR1p150, ADAR1p110, or ADAR2) abolishes MDA5 polymerization and cellular ISG/stress granule induction, whereas stochastic replacement of guanosines with inosines (6%, 10%, or 35% via ITP during in vitro transcription) fails to suppress activation. Sequencing comparisons across in vitro reactions, ADAR-reconstituted $\text{Adar1}^{-/-};\text{Adarb1}^{-/-};\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ MEFs, mouse liver, and $\text{Adar1p150}^{-/-}$ MEFs show a shared subset (~6–8%) of edited adenosines, ~85% of which convert A:U pairs into I:U wobble pairs. Raw sequencing data (GSE293616) and analysis scripts (GitHub/Zenodo) are publicly available.

This is a well-executed biochemical study with thoughtful technical controls—notably the use of loopback-mutant $\text{T7}^{\text{FAFAG}}$ polymerase to eliminate untemplated dsRNA artifacts, phosphatase treatment to prevent RIG-I activation, and a poly(I:C) + ADAR2 mock-editing control (Supplementary Fig. S12) to rule out residual ADAR protein carryover. Its primary limitations lie in the bioinformatics peak-calling pipeline (which uses the ChIP/ATAC-seq caller Genrich and hard base-pair set subtraction rather than a count-based differential enrichment model normalized to transcript expression), the inability of stochastic ITP-for-GTP transcription to decouple base-pair identity (I:U vs. I:C) from site-specific positional clustering, the lack of $\text{Mda5}^{-/-}$ vs. $\text{Pkr}^{-/-}$ genetic controls in the cellular transfection/stress granule assays, and several numerical/editorial inconsistencies between the text and figure legends.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: Structured 3' UTRs harboring inverted SINEs (*Rnf168*, *Knl1*) or LTRs (*Zkscan3*), but not single SINEs or unstructured LINE fragments, support $\text{MDA5}\Delta\text{CARD}$ ATPase activity and filament formation.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Synthesized with loopback-free $\text{T7}^{\text{FAFAG}}$ polymerase, *Rnf168*, *Knl1*, and *Zkscan3* dsRNAs drive ATP hydrolysis and 70–120 nm TEM filaments comparable to *ZNF-708* inverted Alus and poly(I:C), whereas the single-SINE (*1/2 Rnf168*) and LINE-b controls are inactive (Fig. 2A–C).
* **Claim 2: In vitro A-to-I editing by ADAR1p150, ADAR1p110, or ADAR2 is equally capable of suppressing MDA5 filamentation and cellular ISG induction, indicating that ADAR1p150's unique in vivo role is not due to an exclusive intrinsic substrate specificity.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Purified ADAR1p150, ADAR1p110, and ADAR2 all edit overlapping hotspots on *Rnf168*, *Knl1*, and *Zkscan3* (6–14% average adenosine editing) and fully suppress $\text{MDA5}\Delta\text{CARD}$ ATPase activity, filament formation, and *Stat1*/*Irf7* induction upon transfection (Figs. 3–4).
* **Claim 3: Replacing canonical A:U base pairs with a small number of specific I:U wobble pairs—rather than inosine incorporation per se—is critically required and sufficient to prevent MDA5 polymerization.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  The contrast between ADAR editing (~85% A:U $\rightarrow$ I:U conversions at 6–14% total A editing) and ITP-substituted IVT (~8–11% G:U $\rightarrow$ I:U and ~90% G:C $\rightarrow$ I:C at up to 35% G substitution) strongly implicates I:U wobble base pairing over isosteric I:C pairing, further supported by *trans* co-transfection of short I:U vs. I:C oligos (Supplementary Fig. S13). However, because ITP incorporation during T7 transcription only replaces guanosines stochastically, the experiment does not test whether stochastic A:U $\rightarrow$ I:U replacement would also suffice, nor does it directly test synthetic RNAs bearing only the minimal candidate ADAR1p150 I:U sites (Fig. 5).
* **Claim 4: RNase protection-seq with Genrich identifies 1,190 endogenous RNA regions uniquely bound by MDA5 in ADAR1-deficient liver.** `[Fragile]`  
  Defining "genotype-unique" MDA5 targets by calling binary peaks against input with Genrich (an ATAC/ChIP-seq caller) and performing hard base-pair subtraction between $\text{Adar1}^{-/-};\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ and $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ ignores continuous quantitative enrichment differences, replicate variance, and differential baseline transcript abundance between genotypes (visible in Supplementary Fig. S2).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Binary Peak Subtraction with an ATAC/ChIP-seq Caller (Genrich) Instead of Differential Enrichment Modeling (Pillar 3 & Pillar 4)
* **Defect:** In Methods (lines 59–70) and Results (lines 170–171), protected RNA fragments from the $\text{MDA5}\Delta\text{2CARD}$ RNase A/T1 assay ($n=3$ per condition) are identified using Genrich (`p < 0.01`, `AUC >= 500`), followed by hard base-pair set subtraction: *"where $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ peaks and $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ RNase peaks were subtracted from the $\text{Adar}^{-/-};\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ peaks on a bp-wise basis."* Genrich models genomic DNA read pileups against a uniform/input genome background and does not account for wide dynamic ranges in cellular transcript abundance or differential gene expression between $\text{Adar1}^{-/-};\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ and $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ livers. Furthermore, classifying peaks as "uniquely protected" via binary threshold subtraction treats a peak with $q = 0.009$ in $\text{Adar1}^{-/-};\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ and $q = 0.011$ in $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ as genotype-exclusive without testing the interaction term $(\text{Protected}_{\text{KO}}/\text{Input}_{\text{KO}}) / (\text{Protected}_{\text{WT}}/\text{Input}_{\text{WT}})$.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Hard set subtraction of thresholded peaks conflates borderline significance and transcript expression shifts with differential MDA5 binding.
  * **WHAT:** Supplement the Venn diagram (Fig. 1A) and Supplementary Data File 2 with a formal count-based differential protection analysis across the three biological replicates.
  * **HOW:** Quantify read counts across the union of candidate protected regions in all input, RNase-only, and MDA5+RNase libraries, and fit a generalized linear model in DESeq2 or edgeR with an interaction design (`~ genotype + assay + genotype:assay`) to report $\log_2$ fold-enrichment of MDA5 protection between $\text{Adar1}^{-/-};\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ and $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ along with Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted $p$-values.

### 2. Attribution of Cellular ISG and Stress Granule Readouts without $\text{Mda5}^{-/-}$ / $\text{Pkr}^{-/-}$ Controls or Explicit Recipient MEF Genotype (Pillar 2 & Pillar 5)
* **Defect:** In Fig. 4, dephosphorylated RNAs are transfected into immortalized MEFs to measure *Stat1*/*Irf7* induction (Fig. 4A) and G3BP1 stress granule formation (Fig. 4B–C). First, neither the Methods (line 129) nor the Results/legend (Part 2, lines 19–31) specify the genotype of the recipient MEFs used in Fig. 4 (wild-type MEFs express endogenous ADAR1p150, which is further induced by IFN during the 24 h incubation and can edit transfected substrates in cellulo). Second, while dephosphorylation eliminates 5'-triphosphate RIG-I sensing, long dsRNAs in mammalian cells activate both **MDA5** (driving IFN-$\beta$/ISGs via MAVS) and **PKR** (driving eIF2$\alpha$ phosphorylation and G3BP1 stress granule assembly). Presenting stress granule suppression (Fig. 4B–C) as evidence of impaired MDA5 activation without testing $\text{Mda5}^{-/-}$ and $\text{Pkr}^{-/-}$ cells conflates PKR and MDA5 pathways.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Readers need to know whether recipient MEFs in Fig. 4 contain endogenous ADARs, and whether stress granule suppression is mediated via PKR, MDA5, or both.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Explicitly state the exact genotype of the immortalized MEFs used in Fig. 4A–C in the Methods and figure legend, and clarify in the Results/Discussion that G3BP1 stress granule suppression in Fig. 4B–C likely reflects concomitant inhibition of PKR (or both PKR and MDA5) by I:U-modified dsRNAs, consistent with the Introduction (line 10).

### 3. Uncorrected Multiple Pairwise $t$-Tests Across Multi-Group Experiments (Pillar 4)
* **Defect:** Methods line 144 states: *"Mean number of stress granules, standard deviation, and T test were performed using GraphPad Prism. ATPase assays and qPCR assays for testing the expression of interferon-stimulated genes were performed in triplicate. Mean, standard deviation, and T-test were performed in GraphPad Prism."* In Figs. 3A–C, 3F–G, 4A, and 4C, each panel compares 8 to 10 treatment groups (Unedited, ADAR2, ADAR1p110, ADAR1p150, 10% ITP, 20% ITP, 50% ITP, 1/2 element, poly I:C). Running multiple unadjusted pairwise $t$-tests with $n=3$ replicates inflates family-wise Type I error.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Multi-group comparisons against a control require one-way ANOVA followed by post-hoc multiple-comparison correction.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Re-compute significance in Figs. 3 and 4 using one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's post-hoc test (comparing each edited or ITP-substituted condition against the unedited full-length dsRNA control) and report exact adjusted $p$-values.

### 4. Internal Numerical and Methodological Contradictions Between Text and Legends (Pillar 7)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Fig. 1B/1C peak counts and percentages:* Line 174 (Fig. 1B legend) reports `~64% (760 peaks)` for $\text{Adar}^{-/-};\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$ MDA5-protected repeats and `13% (766 peaks)` for input repeats, whereas line 176 (Results text) reports `~63%, 749 peaks` and `12% (717 peaks)`, respectively. Similarly, Fig. 1C legend (line 174) reports LINEs at `61%–76%` and SINEs at `7%–12%` (`7%` in $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$), whereas line 176 reports LINEs at `67%–76%` and SINEs at `8%–12%` (`8%` in $\text{Mavs}^{-/-}$).
  2. *RT-qPCR housekeeping gene contradiction:* Methods line 134 states *"Mouse actin mRNA was used as the internal normalization control"*, whereas the Fig. 4A legend (Part 2, line 22) states *"with GAPDH used as a reference."*
  3. *Mutant T7 RNA polymerase residue typo:* Methods line 87 correctly specifies the **G47A** and 884G mutations from Dousis et al. [46], but Results line 182 writes **G474A**.
  4. *Interferon type contradiction:* Methods line 36 and Results Part 2 line 40 state that $\text{Adar1p150}^{-/-}$ MEFs were treated with **interferon beta**, whereas the Fig. 5 legend (Part 2, line 43) says **interferon-alpha**.
  5. *Gene symbol typos:* Part 2 line 1 writes `Rnf160` instead of `Rnf168`, and Part 2 line 40 writes `Zkscan168` instead of `Zkscan3`.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Conflicting peak counts, housekeeping controls, and mutation coordinates across sections undermine experimental traceability.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Reconcile the exact peak counts and percentages between line 174 and line 176; clarify whether *Actb* or *Gapdh* (or both) normalized the RT-qPCR data in Fig. 4A and Supplementary Fig. S11; fix `G474A` to `G47A` on line 182; fix `interferon-alpha` to `interferon-beta` in the Fig. 5 legend; and correct `Rnf160` and `Zkscan168` to `Rnf168` and `Zkscan3`.
