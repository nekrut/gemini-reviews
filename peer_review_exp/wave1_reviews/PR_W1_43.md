## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study introduces two integrated platforms for engineering switchable allosteric proteins in *Escherichia coli*: (1) **POGO-PANCE** (Protein-switch On/Off Gene Optimization using Phage-Assisted Non-Continuous Evolution), which alternates positive selection (`pBAD -> gIII`) and negative selection (`pBAD -> gIII-Neg` with basal IPTG-driven `gIII`) under opposing light/dark inputs; and (2) **RAMPhaGE** (Recombitron-Assisted Multiplexed Phage Gene Evolution), which adapts the `Eco1` retron, single-stranded annealing protein `CspRecT`, and dominant-negative `MutL` to introduce programmed deep mutational scanning (DMS) point mutations and up to 15-bp (5-amino-acid) insertions/deletions into single-stranded M13 phage genomes in vivo. Applying these tools to chimeric fusions of the transcription factor AraC and the *Avena sativa* LOV2 photosensor (`AraC S170 AsLOV2`), the authors first evolve constitutive AraC stepping stones (`R2`, `R5`), then evolve blue-light-inactivated switches achieving ~1,000-fold dark/light dynamic range, and track adaptive trajectories across cycles using Illumina and Oxford Nanopore sequencing.

**Editorial Verdict: Accept with Minor Revisions (Strong, well-controlled methodological and empirical contribution).**  
The experimental architecture is rigorous: the authors validate negative selection via head-to-head plaque competition (`WT` vs. `R5`, Fig 2I), include an inverted-light selection control (`P3`, Figs 3A and 4B) that confirms switch emergence requires input-aligned selection, validate retron editing in M13 against non-targeting and reverse-transcriptase-dead controls (Fig 5B, S11 Fig), benchmark their lead switch against the published `BLADE` optogenetic AraC tool (S10 Fig), and dissect promoter half-site (`O2` vs. `I2`) dependencies (S18 Fig). Raw sequencing data (SRA `PRJNA1442006` and Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.15650047`), code (`GitHub`), and plasmids (`Addgene`) are fully available. Minor methodological revisions are needed to address (1) descriptive co-occurrence claims that lack formal linkage disequilibrium / epistasis statistics, (2) unpropagated error and lack of autofluorescence subtraction in dark/light fold-change ratios, (3) baseline subtraction of Nanopore error using Day-1 mutagenized pools rather than unmutagenized WT template, and (4) over-interpretation of static AlphaFold3 models for linker $\alpha$-helix extension.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: POGO-PANCE converts poorly active `AraC-LOV2` chimeras into blue-light-inactivated genetic switches with ~1,000-fold dynamic range (Figs 2–4).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Validated across duplicate evolution pools, 28 unique isolated clones in plate-reader mRFP assays (Fig 4B), single-cell flow cytometry (`R2-LOV P1.1.3` and `P2.1.3` showing ~1,000-fold median fluorescence shift, Fig 4C), an inverted-light negative control regime (`P3`, which yielded non-switching variants), and head-to-head comparison outperforming `BLADE` (S10 Fig).
* **Claim 2: RAMPhaGE enables programmable, multiplexed recombineering of point mutations (DMS) and multi-codon InDels (up to 15 bp) in ssDNA M13 phage (Fig 5A–F).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Demonstrated by >40% programmed DMS editing across all 2,820 single-amino-acid substitutions in LOV2 (Spearman $r = 0.77$ between plasmid input and phage output, >3-fold higher per-site rate and far lower mutational bias than `pLlacO1-DP6`), successful installation of 1–5 AA linkers/deletions ($r = 0.79$ left junction, $r = 0.41$ right junction), and clean negative controls (RT-inactive, non-targeting retron).
* **Claim 3: Mutational co-occurrence patterns (e.g., `E144K + V223M` vs. mutual exclusion of `V223M + S225R`) reveal epistatic trajectories and incompatible adaptive solutions (Fig 4A, S9B, S17).** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  The experimental incompatibility between RAMPhaGE-evolved left-linker mutation `LdelK` and DP6-evolved `R2-1.1.3` / `R2-2.1.3` backgrounds is directly proved by site-directed mutagenesis (S17B Fig). However, the sequencing-based co-occurrence claims in S9B Fig rely on descriptive joint read counts ($\ge 9.5\%$ cutoff) in small, bottlenecked phage populations without formal statistical testing for linkage disequilibrium ($D'$ or Fisher's exact test against marginal product expectations $p_A p_B$) to separate true epistasis from clonal hitchhiking.
* **Claim 4: Left-linker substitutions (`SG -> K/R/Q/N`, `LdelK`) enhance allosteric coupling by extending a continuous $\alpha$-helix across the AraC–LOV2 junction (Fig 5I–J).** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  While the functional convergence on `LdelK` (>40% of reads in both replicates) and its digital switching phenotype (Fig 5J) are strongly demonstrated, the structural claim of continuous $\alpha$-helix extension rests entirely on a static `AlphaFold3` prediction (Fig 5I) without biophysical confirmation (e.g., CD spectroscopy, NMR, or X-ray crystallography).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Absence of Linkage Disequilibrium Statistics for Co-Occurrence Claims and Clonal Hitchhiking Confounding (Fig 4A, Supplementary Fig. 9B)
* **Location:** Results line 68; Discussion lines 124–126; Methods line 297.
* **Statistical Flaw:** In Supplementary Fig. 9B, pairwise co-occurrence of mutations within long reads is displayed by plotting single-site frequencies ($p_A, p_B$) alongside raw joint frequencies ($p_{AB}$) for mutations present in $\ge 9.5\%$ of reads. In asexual M13 populations subjected to serial $10^3\text{ pfu/mL}$ bottlenecks without sexual recombination, two neutral or independently beneficial mutations arising in the same lineage will exhibit high raw joint frequency $p_{AB}$ purely via clonal hitchhiking, and two mutations arising in separate lineages will appear "mutually exclusive" ($p_{AB} \approx 0$) due to clonal interference. The authors should compute formal linkage disequilibrium metrics ($D = p_{AB} - p_A p_B$ or log-odds ratios) and explicitly note clonal interference when interpreting within-pool mutual exclusivity.

### Defect 2: Unpropagated Variance and Missing Autofluorescence Subtraction in Dark/Light Fold-Change Ratios (Figs 4B–C, 5J, S17–S18)
* **Location:** Results lines 71–73; Methods lines 191–195 ("mRFP reporter assays").
* **Methodological Flaw:**
  1. In plate-reader reporter assays (line 193), fluorescence is normalized strictly to $\text{OD}_{600}$ ($\text{RFP} / \text{OD}_{600}$) without subtracting background autofluorescence of empty-vector / non-fluorescent *E. coli* TOP10 cells in LB medium.
  2. In Fig 4B, dynamic range ("fold change (dark/light)") is reported as a point ratio of mean $\text{RFP}/\text{OD}_{600}$ values without standard error propagation or confidence intervals. When light-state repression drives mRFP close to baseline cellular autofluorescence, small fluctuations in the denominator produce large variance in the point ratio.
  3. Line 195 notes that the $n = 3$ "biological replicates" were grown on separate days *"from the same glycerol stock"* rather than from 3 independent plasmid co-transformations.

### Defect 3: Nanopore Baseline Correction Using Day-1 Mutagenized Pool and Post-Hoc Site Exclusions (`nt 446` and `R38`)
* **Location:** Methods lines 271, 291–295 ("Illumina/Nanopore read processing and analysis").
* **Bioinformatic Caveat:**
  1. In Fig 3C (temporal Nanopore tracking of `R2-LOV P1.2`), systematic Nanopore basecalling errors were corrected *"by subtracting baseline mutation frequencies measured after the initial mutagenesis passage (Day 1)"* (line 293). Because Day 1 phages had already undergone an overnight passage on `pLlacO1-DP6` mutagenesis host cells, subtracting Day 1 frequencies underestimates mutations that were induced at high frequency during the first mutagenesis round. Sequencing the unmutagenized parental `R2-LOV` phage template on the same flow cell is the proper technical error baseline.
  2. In line 271, nucleotide position `446 (G -> A)` was excluded from the Illumina DMS analysis because it exhibited a **~95% mutation rate**, which the authors attribute to *"an unannotated silent mutation or early off-target propagation event."* A 95% frequency across the phage pool indicates that this silent mutation was fixed in the parental phage stock prior to DMS library passage; this should be stated transparently as a parental stock variant rather than an "off-target propagation event."

### Defect 4: Minor Unit and Inequality Typos in Methods
* **aTc Concentration Typo (1,000-fold error):** Line 256 states that DMS/linker and DP6 mutagenesis cultures were initiated with **"50 ng/µL aTc"** (which equals $50\text{ \mu g/mL}$), whereas lines 204, 213, and 245 correctly state **"50 ng/mL aTc"**.
* **Inverted NanoDrop Purity Inequality:** Line 284 states *"Only samples with OD260/280 < 1.8 and OD260/230 between 2.0–2.2 were used for downstream processing, in accordance with ONT's quality guidelines."* Pure dsDNA requires $\text{OD}_{260/280} \ge 1.8$ (typically $1.8\text{–}2.0$), not $< 1.8$.
* **Typo in Statistical Section:** Line 311 writes *"Spearmen correlation"* instead of *"Spearman correlation"*.
