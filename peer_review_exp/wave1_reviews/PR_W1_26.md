## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript investigates the metabolic reprogramming of murine B cells stimulated ex vivo with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and interleukin-4 (IL-4). Using single-shot Data-Independent Acquisition (DIA) mass spectrometry and the histone-ruler method across $8,030$ identified proteins ($7,187$ quantified in both naïve and 24 h LPS+IL-4-activated B cells), the authors show that total cellular protein mass doubles upon activation (median copy-number $\log_2\text{FC} = 1.04$) and identify $863$ proteins selectively upregulated in intracellular concentration (and $301$ uniquely induced), enriched for cell cycle progression, ribosome biogenesis, amino acid transport, and sterol biosynthesis. They demonstrate that the System L transporter light chain SLC7A5 (together with heavy chain SLC3A2/CD98) is strongly induced, mediates the majority of BCH-sensitive kynurenine uptake, and is genetically required ($\text{Slc7a5}^{\text{fl/fl}}/\text{Vav-iCre}^+$) for B cell survival, division, and IgG1 class switching. In parallel, LPS+IL-4 induces HMG-CoA reductase (HMGCR), squalene monooxygenase (SQLE), and the LDL receptor (LDLR), elevating filipin-stained cellular cholesterol. By combining normal versus silica-depleted cholesterol-free (CF) serum, HMGCR inhibitors (Fluvastatin, Rosuvastatin), an SQLE inhibitor (NB-598), prenylation inhibitors (FGTI-2734, FTI-277, GGTI-298), a second DIA proteomics dataset (Normal vs. CF $\pm$ Fluvastatin on an Orbitrap Astral), and metabolite rescues (mevalonate and geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate [GGPP]), the authors show that B cell proliferation requires both cholesterol and protein geranylgeranylation, and that GGPP rescues Fluvastatin-treated B cells in normal serum (by restoring prenylation-dependent LDL cholesterol uptake) but fails in CF serum. Both proteomics datasets are publicly deposited in PRIDE (`PXD057739`, `PXD074568`).

The experimental design dissecting the branched mevalonate pathway—specifically the comparison of HMGCR vs. SQLE vs. FTase/GGTase inhibition and mevalonate vs. GGPP rescue across normal and lipoprotein-depleted serum—is biochemically rigorous and resolves an important paradox regarding statin action in lymphocytes. However, the manuscript's presentation and statistical reporting have several weaknesses: (1) biological replicate counts ($n$), test statistics, degrees of freedom, and exact $p$-values are completely omitted from the main text, and all Figure Legends (`Figures 1–10`) and Methods Tables (`Tables 1–4`) are missing from the manuscript file; (2) the primary proteomics experiment and several flow cytometry assays stimulated *unpurified bulk lymph node or spleen cell suspensions* for 24–72 h prior to sorting/gating on $\text{CD19}^+$ B cells, introducing paracrine myeloid/T-cell cytokine signals (such as the IFN-$\alpha$ signature noted in line 34); (3) the false discovery rate cutoff for the proteomics analysis is inconsistently reported as `q < 0.05` in the Results (lines 28, 74) versus `Q = 0.01` in the Methods (Part 2, line 118); and (4) the TLR9 comparison uses the human-optimal CpG oligonucleotide `ODN 2006` rather than a murine-optimal CpG motif.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: LPS + IL-4 stimulation doubles total B cell protein mass within 24 h while selectively increasing the intracellular concentration of cell cycle regulators, ribosomes, amino acid transporters, and sterol biosynthesis enzymes.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Using the histone-ruler normalization (`Wiśniewski et al., 2014`) properly separates global cell-size scaling (seen for core glycolytic/TCA/OXPHOS enzymes in Fig. S2) from selective concentration increases in CDK1/2/4/6, Cyclins D/A/B, 40S/60S subunits, SLC7A5/SLC3A2, HMGCR, SQLE, and LDLR (Figs. 1–5).
* **Claim 2: The System L amino acid transporter subunit SLC7A5 is essential for LPS + IL-4-driven B cell survival, proliferation, and class switching ex vivo.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Purified B cells from $\text{Slc7a5}^{\text{fl/fl}}/\text{Vav-iCre}^+$ mice show loss of BCH-sensitive kynurenine transport and severe failure of viability, CTV division, and IgG1 switching at 72 h (Fig. 4F–K).
* **Claim 3: B cell proliferation downstream of HMGCR requires both cholesterol availability (via de novo synthesis or LDL uptake) and protein geranylgeranylation, with GGPP restoring cholesterol levels in statin-treated B cells only when extracellular LDL is present.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Demonstrated by the concordant anti-proliferative effects of downstream SQLE inhibition (NB-598) and GGTase inhibition (GGTI-298), the compensatory upregulation of HMGCR/SQLE/LDLR in CF media + Fluvastatin (Fig. S9), and the strictly serum-lipoprotein-dependent rescue of filipin staining and division by GGPP (Figs. 6–8).
* **Claim 4: MAPK (MEK1/2 and p38$\alpha/\beta$) and mTORC1 signaling downstream of MyD88 regulate B cell cholesterol accumulation and proliferation.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  $\text{Myd88}^{-/-}$ abolishes acute LPS-induced p38, ERK1/2, AKT, and S6 phosphorylation (Fig. S11), and PD184352 + VX745 or rapamycin reduce 48 h filipin staining and CTV division (Fig. 9). However, measuring filipin at 48 h when cell growth (FSC, Fig. 9E) and division are arrested by rapamycin or MEK/p38 inhibition can reflect smaller cell surface membrane area rather than direct transcriptional inhibition of sterol enzymes.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 1. Complete Omission of Sample Sizes ($n$), Exact $p$-Values, Figure Legends (Figs. 1–10), and Methods Tables (Tables 1–4) (Pillar 4 & Pillar 7)
* **Defect:** Throughout the entire Results section (lines 22–101) and Methods, the number of independent biological replicates ($n$ mice per group) and exact $p$-values are never stated in the text—not even for the two core DIA proteomics datasets (`PXD057739` and `PXD074568`). Furthermore, while Methods Part 2 line 123 states that *"the tests used are indicated in the figure legends"*, **all ten Figure Legends (`Figure 1` through `Figure 10`) and all four Methods Tables (`Table 1` biotin depletion panel, `Table 2` inhibitors, `Table 3` flow antibodies, `Table 4` Western/intracellular antibodies) are missing from the manuscript**.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Readers cannot evaluate statistical power, variance, or reagent identity when $n$, $p$-values, figure legends, and antibody/inhibitor tables are absent.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Insert the complete Figure Legends for Figures 1–10 and Tables 1–4 into the manuscript; explicitly state the number of biological replicate mice ($n$) and exact $p$/$q$-values in the main text for all key comparisons (including the exact number of biological replicates in both DIA-MS datasets in the Proteomics Methods section).

### 2. Confounding of Unfractionated Lymph Node / Splenocyte Stimulation with Purified B Cell Stimulation (Pillar 2 & Pillar 6)
* **Defect:** The manuscript alternates between two distinct stimulation protocols without highlighting the biological confound:
  1. For the primary Exploris 480 proteomics dataset (Methods Part 2, line 90) and flow cytometry assays of CTV proliferation (Results line 24), p-STAT1 (Part 2, line 55), OPP incorporation (Part 2, line 65), filipin cholesterol (Part 2, line 75), and MitoTracker (Part 2, line 80), **unfractionated total lymph node cells or total splenocytes** were stimulated with $20\ \mu\text{g/mL}$ LPS + $10\ \text{ng/mL}$ IL-4 for 24–72 h, and $\text{CD19}^+$ B cells were only sorted or gated *after* stimulation.
  2. For the Astral proteomics dataset (Part 2, line 92), the $\text{Slc7a5}^{\text{fl/fl}}/\text{Vav-iCre}^+$ assays (Results line 52), kynurenine uptake (Part 2, line 70), and immunoblotting (Part 2, line 85), **MACS-purified B cells (~97% CD19+)** were stimulated.
  As acknowledged in Results line 34, stimulating bulk lymph node or spleen suspensions with LPS activates co-cultured myeloid cells to secrete type I interferons and inflammatory cytokines, creating a secondary paracrine signature in B cells.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Paracrine factors from LPS-activated myeloid and T cells in unfractionated cultures can alter B cell survival, size, and metabolic gene expression compared to cell-autonomous TLR4 activation in purified B cells.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Clearly state in each Results subsection and figure legend whether bulk lymphocytes/splenocytes or MACS-purified B cells were stimulated; additionally, cross-compare the 24 h LPS+IL-4 vs. Naïve fold-changes between the lymph-node-sorted Exploris dataset (`PXD057739`, bulk-stimulated) and the MACS-purified splenic B cell Astral dataset (`PXD074568`, purified before stimulation) to confirm that the core amino acid transporter and cholesterol enzyme inductions are fully B-cell-intrinsic.

### 3. Contradictory Proteomics FDR Thresholds (`q < 0.05` vs. `Q = 0.01`) and Unnormalized Filipin Fluorescence Across Cell Sizes (Pillar 3 & Pillar 4)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Conflicting FDR cutoffs:* Results lines 28 and 74 state that differentially regulated proteins were defined using **`q < 0.05`** and $|\log_2\text{FC} - \text{median}| > 1\text{ SD}$, whereas Methods Part 2 line 118 states that the Benjamini–Krieger–Yekutieli two-stage step-up procedure was applied with **`Q value of 0.01`**.
  2. *Filipin scaling with cell surface area:* Because filipin stoichiometrically binds unesterified membrane cholesterol and activated B cells double in protein mass and substantially increase forward scatter (FSC, Fig. 1B–C), total cellular filipin MFI increases partly due to plasma membrane expansion. Conversely, in Fig. 9E–F, rapamycin and PD184352+VX745 reduce both cell size (FSC) and filipin MFI.
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** Reporting two different FDR thresholds (`0.05` vs. `0.01`) creates ambiguity over how the 863 upregulated and 409 downregulated proteins were selected, and raw filipin MFI conflates membrane surface area with cholesterol density.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Reconcile the exact $q$-value cutoff between Results (lines 28, 74) and Methods (Part 2, line 118); for the inhibitor experiments in Figs. 5–9, also report filipin MFI normalized to forward scatter ($\text{Filipin MFI} / \text{FSC-A}$) to distinguish specific cholesterol depletion from reduced blastogenesis.

### 4. Suboptimal Human TLR9 Agonist (`ODN 2006`) in Murine Cells and Methodological Typos (Pillar 6 & Pillar 7)
* **Defect:**
  1. *Species-mismatched CpG ODN:* Methods Part 2 line 40 specifies `1 ug/ml ODN 2006 (tlrl-2006, Invivogen)` for TLR9 stimulation of murine B cells. `ODN 2006` carries the human-optimal `GTCGTT` hexamer motif rather than the murine-optimal `GACGTT` motif (`ODN 1826`), explaining its weaker proliferative potency on mouse B cells in Fig. 10D–E.
  2. *Unit and text typos:*
     - Methods Part 2 line 94: *"400 **ml** of lysis buffer... was added to each sample"* (should be **$400\ \mu\text{L}$**).
     - Methods Part 2 line 109: *"orbitrap resolution at **2400000**"* (should be **240,000**).
     - Discussion Part 2 line 9: cites `(Figure 12A-B)` instead of `(Figure S12A-B)`, and line 1 writes `SLCA5` instead of `SLC7A5`.
     - Methods Part 2 lines 36, 75, 80, 85: uses capital `I` instead of `l` or `L` in microliter units (`1μI`, `30μI`, `50μI`, `300μI`).
* **Actionable Remediation:**
  * **WHY:** A $1000\times$ lysis buffer volume error and $10\times$ mass analyzer resolution typo impede protocol reproduction.
  * **WHAT & HOW:** Note the use of human-selective `ODN 2006` when interpreting the lower amplitude of TLR9 proliferation in Fig. 10; fix `400 ml` to `400 μl` (Part 2, line 94), `2400000` to `240,000` (Part 2, line 109), `Figure 12A-B` to `Figure S12A-B` (Part 2, line 9), `SLCA5` to `SLC7A5` (Part 2, line 1), and `μI` to `μl`.
