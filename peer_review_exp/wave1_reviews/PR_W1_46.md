## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript investigates whether the Greenland shark (*Somniosus microcephalus*), which lives for centuries in cold, dim Arctic deep waters and frequently carries corneal copepod parasites (*Ommatokoita elongata*), retains a functional visual system. Integrating retinal histology ($n=3$), chromatin immunostaining (`H3K27ac`, `H4K20me3`), RNAscope in situ hybridization, LC-MS fatty acid/VLC-PUFA profiling, in vitro rhodopsin regeneration and spectrophotometry, a short-read draft genome assembly, and retinal bulk RNA-seq ($n=3$), the authors demonstrate that adult Greenland sharks possess morphologically intact, rod-dominated retinas with blue-shifted rhodopsin ($\lambda_{\max} = 458\text{ nm}$), high DHA (41%) and C36 VLC-PUFA content, intact and actively transcribed rod phototransduction genes, and pseudogenized/unexpressed cone phototransduction and melanopsin (`opn4`) genes. They further propose that retention of `ercc1` and elevated expression of `ercc4` (`XPF`) support century-scale retinal preservation.

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision (Compelling primary biological discovery undermined by a flawed RNA-seq TPM extrapolation method and artefactual DNA-repair gene-loss claims).**  
The core organismal conclusion—that the Greenland shark is not blind, but instead maintains an intact, purely rod-based (scotopic) retina tuned to deep-sea blue light ($\lambda_{\max} = 458\text{ nm}$)—is supported by convergent histological, biochemical, and transcriptomic evidence. However, several bioinformatic and experimental components require major correction: (1) rather than assembling the retinal transcriptome to compute standard Transcripts Per Million (TPM), the authors mapped Greenland shark Ribo-Zero total RNA reads only to manually mined target genes and "extrapolated" TPM values using a 5-point linear regression of TPM vs. raw mapped-read fraction fitted across five other shark species (Lines 124–129); (2) the claim that shorter-lived sharks lack the essential nucleotide excision repair gene `ercc1` while longer-lived sharks retain it (Line 50) is an artifact of GFF gene-name filtering and incomplete draft assemblies (75.6–76.0% BUSCO completeness); (3) commercial **mouse and human** RNAscope probes were used on a chondrichthyan (diverged ~450 Ma) without sequence identity validation or negative controls (Line 111); and (4) codon selection tests (`PAML`, `RELAX`, `aBSREL`) included pseudogenes after manually stripping stop codons and frameshifts (Line 94).

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1: Adult Greenland sharks possess an intact, non-degenerated, rod-dominated retina with blue-shifted rhodopsin ($\lambda_{\max} = 458\text{ nm}$) and elevated retinal DHA (41%) and C36 VLC-PUFAs (45%) (Figs 1b, 2e–f).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Light microscopy of 1-$\mu\text{m}$ radial resin sections across three adult specimens confirms intact nuclear and photoreceptor layers with densely packed elongated rods, LC-MS demonstrates elevated DHA and C36 VLC-PUFAs relative to bovine retina, and 1D4-purified recombinant rhodopsin bleached difference spectroscopy confirms a deep-sea hypsochromic shift to $\lambda_{\max} = 458\text{ nm}$.
* **Claim 2: The Greenland shark retains and expresses the complete rod phototransduction cascade while cone opsin/phototransduction genes (`rh2`, `gnat2`, `pde6c`, `cnga3`, `cngb3`) and melanopsin (`opn4`) are pseudogenized (Figs 1c, 3).** `[Well Supported]`  
  Supported by phylogenetic verification of all 13 rod phototransduction coding sequences (`PV442159–PV442194`) and their robust detection in three retinal RNA-seq libraries, contrasted with premature stop codons/frameshifts and absent expression in the cone pathway. (Note: claiming complete genomic absence of `arrc` from a fragmented 150-bp insert `ABySS` assembly should be tempered to "not recovered").
* **Claim 3: Cross-species quantitative RNA-seq comparison shows Greenland shark rod gene expression matches adult sharks and `ercc4` expression is elevated, while shorter-lived sharks have lost `ercc1` (Fig 3, Line 50).** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  First, Greenland shark TPMs were not calculated from a transcriptome; they were extrapolated from a linear regression of TPM against raw mapped-read proportions across five other species (Lines 126–128), confounding library prep differences (Greenland shark Ribo-Zero total RNA vs. public poly(A)-selected mRNA) and mapping denominator differences (mapping only to target CDSs vs. whole genomes). Second, `ERCC1` is a conserved vertebrate housekeeping DNA repair factor; its apparent absence in certain comparison sharks reflects GFF gene-symbol filtering (`AGAT` name matching) and assembly fragmentation (e.g., *R. typus* at 75.6% BUSCO, which is actually one of the longest-lived sharks, lifespan ~70–130 years!).
* **Claim 4: RNAscope confirms cell-type specific expression of `rh1`, `gad1`, `pkcα`, `glul`, `rbpms`, and `slc6a9` across Greenland shark retinal layers (Fig 2d, Line 37).** `[Fragile]`  
  Line 111 reveals that commercial RNAscope probes *"specific for mouse and human transcripts"* were applied to shark cryosections. Because RNAscope double-Z probes require high sequence identity (~85–100% over 50-nt target regions) and mammalian vs. elasmobranch transcripts diverge substantially at synonymous sites, probe homology percentages and `dapB` negative controls must be reported to rule out non-specific tyramide signal amplification.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### Defect 1: Invalid Regression-Based "TPM Extrapolation" Across Incompatible RNA-Seq Protocols and Reference Spaces (Lines 124–129, Fig 3, Fig S43)
* **Location:** Methods lines 124–129 ("Phototransduction and DNA repair gene expression"); Results lines 46–50 (Fig 3).
* **Mathematical/Bioinformatic Flaw:**
  1. Standard $\text{TPM}_i = 10^6 \times \frac{c_i / L_i}{\sum_{j=1}^{G} (c_j / L_j)}$ normalizes for both gene length $L_i$ and the total molar pool of all $G$ transcripts in the sample.
  2. For the five public shark species, reads were mapped to **whole annotated genomes** with `STAR`, and $\text{TPM}_i$ was regressed against the raw proportion of mapped reads $p_i = c_i / N_{\text{total}}$ across the 5 species (Fig S43). For the Greenland shark, reads were mapped **only to the manually mined target sequences**, and the 5-species regression equations were used to extrapolate "TPM" from $p_i$.
  3. This procedure is invalid for three reasons: (a) the Greenland shark libraries were prepared with **TruSeq Ribo-Zero Gold (human/mouse/rat) total RNA** (line 121), where residual un-depleted shark rRNA and intronic pre-mRNA inflate $N_{\text{total}}$ relative to poly(A)-selected public datasets; (b) mapping only to ~20 target CDSs with `--outFilterMultimapNmax 1` alters multimapping competition compared to whole-genome alignment; and (c) across different tissues/species, the scaling factor between $c_i / N_{\text{total}}$ and $\text{TPM}_i$ is the inverse average transcript length of the entire transcriptome, not a gene-specific linear intercept.
  4. **Simple Fix:** Assemble the three Greenland shark retinal RNA-seq datasets de novo (e.g., with `Trinity`) or against the `ABySS` draft genome with `StringTie`, and quantify true TPMs directly using `Salmon` or `kallisto`.

### Defect 2: Assembly Fragmentation (`ABySS` with 150-bp Insert Size), `HISAT2` Cross-Species Genomic Consensus Calling, and False Gene-Loss Inference (Lines 73, 85, 87)
* **Location:** Methods lines 73 ("Whole-genome sequencing") and 85–87 ("Phototransduction and DNA repair gene mining").
* **Genomic Methodology Flaw:**
  1. Sequencing a multi-gigabase shark genome using a single **150-bp insert size** library with 150-bp paired-end reads (meaning forward and reverse reads completely overlap the same 150-bp fragment, providing zero mate-pair scaffolding span!) and assembling with `ABySS` ($k=80$) yields a highly fragmented contig assembly.
  2. When `TBLASTN + EXONERATE` failed on fragmented contigs, the authors mapped raw Greenland shark genomic DNA reads directly to heterologous reference sequences using `HISAT2` (an RNA-seq aligner) and extracted consensus sequences in Geneious Prime (line 85). Mapping short DNA reads to a divergent reference CDS drops reads at divergent exon boundaries and risks collapsing paralogs.
  3. For the comparison sharks (`S. canicula`, `R. typus`, `C. plagiosum`), genes were mined merely *"by filtering the annotation (GFF) file to keep only matched gene names"* (line 87). NCBI RefSeq/GenBank GFF files frequently annotate orthologs under `LOC...` identifiers rather than human HGNC gene symbols; filtering strictly by matched gene symbol creates false "gene absences" (such as the implausible claim that shorter-lived sharks lack `ercc1`, line 50).

### Defect 3: Inclusion of Frame-Corrected Pseudogenes in Codon Selection Models (`PAML`, `RELAX`, `aBSREL`) and Unpenalized Free-Ratio Model Selection (Lines 92–96)
* **Location:** Methods lines 92–96 ("Selection tests on phototransduction and DNA repair genes").
* **Evolutionary Modeling Flaw:**
  1. Line 94 states that protein alignments were generated *"after removing stop codons and frameshifts present in pseudogene sequences"* and then used to run `PAML`, `RELAX`, and `aBSREL`. Once a gene becomes a pseudogene, it evolves free of selective constraint ($\omega = d_N/d_S \approx 1$) and accumulates indels that corrupt local codon reading frames; including pseudogenes alongside functional orthologs biases background $\omega$ distributions and can trigger artefactual branch-site positive selection signals in `aBSREL`.
  2. Line 94 states: *"For all genes, the best model was always the free-ratio model (Model 1)."* Model 1 assigns an independent $\omega_b$ parameter to every branch in the tree ($2N - 3$ parameters); it always has the highest raw log-likelihood $\ell$, but is only statistically supported if $2(\ell_1 - \ell_0) > \chi^2_{2N-4, 0.05}$, which is not reported.
  3. No false discovery rate (FDR / Benjamini-Hochberg) correction is reported across the multiple genes tested with `RELAX`, `aBSREL`, and `PAML` Model 2 vs. Model 3.

### Defect 4: Unvalidated Mammalian RNAscope Probes and 10% DDM Concentration in Rhodopsin Purification (Lines 101, 111)
* **Location:** Methods lines 101 and 111.
* **Experimental Details:**
  1. Line 111 states that RNAscope was performed using *"Probes specific for mouse and human transcripts."* Sequence identity between the commercial mammalian probe target regions and the Greenland shark transcripts (`rh1`, `gad1`, `pkcα`, `glul`, `rbpms`, `slc6a9`) and negative control images must be provided.
  2. Line 101 states that membrane pellets were solubilized and washed in **10% DDM** ($n$-dodecyl-$\beta$-D-maltoside). Standard rhodopsin solubilization uses **1% DDM** and column washing/elution uses **0.02%–0.1% DDM**; if 10% is a typographical error for 1% and 0.1%, it should be corrected.
