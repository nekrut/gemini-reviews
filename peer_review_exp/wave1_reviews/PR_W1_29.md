## 1. Blinded Metadata Perception
* **Perceived Study Design:** Two-site functional MRI (fMRI) cohort study ($N = 415$ healthy adults: Site 1 Bonn $n = 58$, Siemens Skyra 3T; Site 2 Ulm $n = 357$, Siemens Prisma 3T; recruited within the BrainCYP pharmacogenetics study) examining rapid within-block Time-on-Task (ToT) effects (~15 s foraging patches of 12–13 binary decision trials at mean ISI = 1.23 s) and monetary reward magnitude (high reward = 20 cents vs. low reward = 1 cent per patch) using SPM12 general linear modeling (GLM), dual-smoothing (8 mm and 4 mm FWHM) subcortical small-volume mask analyses, and trial-by-trial finite impulse response (FIR) time-course fits.
* **Perceived Rigor & Scope:** The large sample size ($N = 415$) provides high statistical power for detecting group-level behavioral and BOLD changes. However, the inferential link between a centered linear trial index across a 15-second rapid block and tonic ascending neuromodulatory arousal (locus coeruleus [LC] noradrenaline and nucleus basalis of Meynert [NBM/Ch4] acetylcholine) is weakened by four major methodological issues: (1) unmodeled hemodynamic collinearity between a 15-second linear ramp regressor and the canonical HRF temporal/dispersion derivatives of the block-level boxcar response; (2) severe spatial smoothing and partial-volume bleed-in across adjacent subcortical masks at 3 mm native EPI resolution, resulting in identical BOLD peaks (separated by only 1.4–2.0 mm) being reported twice as distinct activations in Ch4/NBM and the centromedial amygdala; (3) an internal contradiction between the Results text ("corrected at voxel level [peak level]") and the Methods section ("cluster-defining threshold $p = 0.001$, uncorrected") alongside unadjusted testing across six separate subcortical masks; and (4) reporting typos ($t = 9.58$ for a pallidum deactivation, "5% confidence intervals") and lack of public unthresholded statistical maps.

## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict
This study investigates how rapid, continuous task engagement over ~15-second foraging blocks ("short Time-on-Task", ToT) and reward opportunity modulate reaction times, accuracy, and cortical/subcortical BOLD activity in 415 healthy participants. Behaviorally, reaction times increase by ~0.78 ms/trial and errors increase slightly over the 12–13 trials of a patch after an initial Trial 1 switching cost, with smaller performance decrements in high-reward (20-cent) patches. In the fMRI analysis, a linear parametric modulator of within-patch trial number correlates positively with widespread cortical, thalamic, striatal, amygdala, NBM/Ch4, and brainstem/LC BOLD signals—effects that are paradoxically *stronger* in high-reward patches where behavioral ToT decrements are smaller—leading the authors to conclude that the ToT $\times$ Reward interaction tracks "effort-related proactive control" and tonic neuromodulatory arousal rather than fatigue.

While the behavioral interaction in $N = 415$ participants is robust, the neuroimaging attribution of within-patch linear BOLD slopes to specific subcortical nuclei (LC and NBM/Ch4) and proactive control requires substantial revision. Because 12–13 trials occur within ~15 seconds (mean ISI = 1.23 s) and are convolved with a canonical 20–32 s hemodynamic response function (HRF) without modeling temporal or dispersion derivatives of the main block regressor, any region with a slightly delayed BOLD peak or sustained late-block hemodynamic accumulation will load onto the linear ToT slope. Furthermore, at a native EPI voxel size of $3 \times 3 \times 2.5\text{ mm}$ smoothed at 4 mm FWHM, the subcortical small-volume analyses report virtually identical MNI coordinates as separate anatomical peaks in the NBM/Ch4 (`21, -4, -11`, $t = 9.19$) and the centromedial amygdala (`21, -4, -13`, $t = 9.24$—a Euclidean separation of 2.0 mm, less than one native voxel).

### Evaluation of Core Scientific Claims
1. **Behavioral reaction times and error rates show a modest gradual decrement across the 12–13 trials of a ~15 s foraging patch (after an initial Trial 1 boundary cost), which is attenuated by high reward (20 cents vs. 1 cent):** `[Well Supported]` — In $N = 415$ subjects, excluding Trial 1 (which is 84.6 ms slower due to patch initiation), RT increases by 0.78 ms/trial ($t = 6.91, p < 0.001$) and displays a negative ToT $\times$ Reward interaction ($-0.55\text{ ms/trial}, t = -3.77, p < 0.001$; Results lines 45–46), with concordant generalized linear mixed-effects results on accuracy (lines 47–48).
2. **The linear within-patch ToT regressor specifically captures neural time-on-task adaptation rather than slow hemodynamic ramp-up or unmodeled HRF latency shifts:** `[Fragile]` — Each foraging block lasts only ~15 s (mean ISI = 1.23 s), which is shorter than a single canonical HRF cycle. In the first-level GLM (Part 2, lines 1–2), trials are modeled as sticks convolved with a canonical HRF plus a centered linear trial-number modulator, without including temporal or dispersion derivatives of the block response. Under canonical HRF convolution over a 15 s window, a centered linear ramp closely approximates the temporal derivative (latency shift) of the block boxcar. Conversely, the large Trial 1 response-initiation transient (84.6 ms slower RT) creates an early BOLD peak in primary motor cortex that decays across the block, which can artificially drive the apparent negative linear "ToT slope" in left sensorimotor cortex (`-38, -25, 54`, $t = -16.95$; line 50).
3. **Standard 3 mm whole-brain T2* EPI (resampled to 1.5 mm and smoothed at 4 mm FWHM) dissociates independent ToT and ToT $\times$ Reward signals in adjacent basal forebrain Ch4/NBM, centromedial amygdala, pallidum, and brainstem locus coeruleus (LC) nuclei:** `[Overstated]` — Native voxel dimensions are $3.0 \times 3.0 \times 2.5\text{ mm}$ with a 0.5 mm gap (Part 1, line 183). In Results line 67, the ToT peak assigned to the centromedial amygdala (`25, -1, -11`, $t = 14.09$; `-24, -1, -11`, $t = 11.81$) and the peak assigned to lateral Ch4/NBM (`24, -1, -10`, $t = 13.20$; `-22, -1, -11`, $t = 11.61$) are separated by only **1.41 mm** on the right and **2.00 mm** on the left. Similarly, in the ToT $\times$ Reward interaction (line 84), the right NBM/Ch4 peak (`21, -4, -11`, $t = 9.19$) and the right centromedial amygdala peak (`21, -4, -13`, $t = 9.24$) are separated by exactly **2.00 mm**—less than a single native 3 mm voxel. Reporting the same smoothed activation cluster twice under separate mask names overstates anatomical specificity.
4. **Second-level fMRI inferences are voxel-level (peak-level) family-wise error corrected across the whole brain and across six subcortical masks:** `[Methodologically Flawed]` — Results lines 33, 50, 55, 67, and 84 repeatedly state that effects were "corrected at voxel level (peak level)." However, Methods (Part 2, line 3) states that permutation testing used a *"cluster-defining threshold $p = 0.001$, uncorrected"*, which defines cluster-extent inference rather than voxel-level FWE inference. Moreover, testing two smoothing kernels (8 mm and 4 mm FWHM) and six separate small-volume masks (LC, thalamus, NBM/Ch4, amygdala, VTA/SN, NAcc; line 84) without adjusting the FWE $\alpha$ across the six masks inflates the family-wise error rate.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Major Methodological & Statistical Defects

* **Defect 1: Hemodynamic Collinearity Between the 15-Second Linear ToT Modulator, Block-Onset Transients, and HRF Latency/Dispersion**
  * **Location:** Results lines 45, 50–55, 84–94; Figures 2 and 5; Methods Part 1 lines 173–179 and Part 2 lines 1–8.
  * **WHY it matters:** Convolving stick functions spaced by 1.23 s over a ~15 s patch with a canonical 20–32 s HRF acts as a strong temporal low-pass filter. Across a 15 s block, a centered linear parametric modulator ($t_k - \bar{t}$) is mathematically nearly collinear with the first temporal derivative (and dispersion) of the block-level boxcar response: regions whose BOLD response peaks late in the 15 s window (or accumulates slowly) will exhibit a strong positive linear "ToT" coefficient, whereas regions with a strong Trial 1 initiation transient (where RT is 84.6 ms slower; line 45) followed by adaptation will exhibit a negative linear "ToT" coefficient. Indeed, the FIR time courses in Figure 5 show slow block-level trajectories across the 18 s window rather than trial-resolved oscillations.
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Explicitly test whether the linear ToT and ToT $\times$ Reward effects survive when (a) Trial 1 (the patch-initiation boundary trial, which was already excluded in the behavioral mixed models on line 45) is modeled as a separate regressor in the first-level fMRI GLM, and (b) canonical HRF temporal and dispersion derivatives of the main block/trial onset regressor are included and orthogonalized prior to the linear ToT modulator.
  * **HOW to fix it:** Re-estimate the first-level SPM12 GLM with a separate stick regressor for Trial 1 of each patch (and/or trial-by-trial RT as a competing nuisance parametric modulator) plus the canonical HRF temporal and dispersion derivatives on the main trial regressor, and report the resulting whole-brain and ROI $t$-statistics for ToT and ToT $\times$ Reward.

* **Defect 2: Duplicate Reporting of Single Subcortical BOLD Peaks Across Adjacent Ch4/NBM and Centromedial Amygdala Masks & Spatial Resolution Limits for Locus Coeruleus**
  * **Location:** Results lines 67–74 and line 84; Figures 3 and 4; Discussion lines 124–144; Methods Part 1 lines 183–191 and Part 2 lines 3–6.
  * **WHY it matters:** With native $3 \times 3 \times 2.5\text{ mm}$ EPI voxels resampled to 1.5 mm and smoothed at 4 mm FWHM (effective smoothness $\approx 5\text{–}6\text{ mm}$ FWHM), structures separated by 1–2 mm cannot be resolved as independent sources. In Results line 67, the right centromedial amygdala peak (`25, -1, -11`, $t = 14.09$) and right Ch4/NBM peak (`24, -1, -10`, $t = 13.20$) are 1.41 mm apart; in line 84, the right NBM/Ch4 peak (`21, -4, -11`, $t = 9.19$) and right centromedial amygdala peak (`21, -4, -13`, $t = 9.24$) are 2.00 mm apart. Because small-volume correction (SVC) was run separately inside each mask (line 84), the exact same underlying BOLD activation gradient at the basal forebrain / dorsal amygdala border is reported twice as independent neurobiological findings. Similarly, attributing dorsal pontine BOLD signal at 3 mm whole-brain EPI resolution without neuromelanin-sensitive T1/TSE imaging or brainstem-specific registration (e.g., spatially unbiased infratentorial template) to the ~2 mm wide LC requires explicit caveats regarding partial-volume contamination from adjacent pontine nuclei and the fourth ventricle (which shows strong CSF negative signal in Fig. 3A, left panel).
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Stop reporting peaks separated by $<4\text{ mm}$ across abutting masks as distinct nuclei, quantify native-space / unsmoothed ROI signals, and add explicit spatial-resolution caveats for LC and NBM/Ch4.
  * **HOW to fix it:** (1) Combine abutting basal forebrain (Ch4/NBM) and centromedial amygdala masks into a single composite basal forebrain/extended amygdala region or extract mean unsmoothed parameter estimates from eroded, non-overlapping masks separated by at least 3–4 mm; (2) report the Euclidean distance between subcortical peaks and state explicitly in Results lines 67 and 84 that the NBM/Ch4 and centromedial amygdala coordinates represent a single contiguous BOLD cluster spanning the border of both probabilistic masks; and (3) temper claims regarding LC specificity in the Abstract and Discussion to "dorsal pontine / putative LC region."

* **Defect 3: Contradiction Between Voxel-Level (Peak FWE) Claims in Results and Cluster-Defining Threshold ($p = 0.001$) in Methods, Plus Uncorrected Multi-Mask SVC**
  * **Location:** Results lines 33, 50, 55, 67, and 84 vs. Methods Part 2 lines 3–6; Supplementary Tables S1–S5.
  * **WHY it matters:** Throughout the Results section, every reported $p$-value is described as *"corrected at voxel level"* or *"peak level"* (e.g., line 33: *"p < 0.001, corrected at voxel level"*; line 84: *"p = 0.014, peak level corrected"*). Yet Methods Part 2 line 3 states: *"Contrast images were taken to the second level and tested by permutation (8000 resamples, cluster-defining threshold p = 0.001, uncorrected)."* Voxel-level (maximum-$t$) permutation FWE does not use a cluster-defining threshold, whereas cluster-extent FWE does not control the false-positive rate of individual voxel peaks. Furthermore, line 84 conducts separate small-volume permutation tests across 6 anatomical masks (LC, thalamus, NBM/Ch4, amygdala, VTA/SN, NAcc) without Bonferroni/Šidák correction across the 6 masks; under a 6-mask correction ($\alpha = 0.05 / 6 = 0.0083$), the reported left LC effect ($p = 0.014$, line 84) is no longer statistically significant.
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Clarify whether permutation inference used maximum-$t$ voxel-wise FWE or cluster-extent FWE at CDT $p = 0.001$, and apply multiple-testing correction across the 6 subcortical ROIs.
  * **HOW to fix it:** Revise Methods Part 2 line 3 and Results lines 33–84 so the exact FWE correction method (voxel-wise max-$t$ vs. cluster-extent) is stated consistently, and report both per-mask and 6-mask Bonferroni-adjusted FWE $p$-values for all subcortical ROI analyses (explicitly noting that the left putative LC interaction at $p = 0.014$ is marginal after correcting across 6 masks).

### 3.2 Secondary & Reporting Defects

* **Defect 4: Sign Error in Bilateral Pallidum Deactivation $t$-Statistic and Confidence Interval Typo in Figure 5**
  * **Location:** Results line 67; Figure 5 legend (line 87) vs. Methods Part 2 line 7.
  * **WHY it matters:** Line 67 states: *"ToT was associated with activity decreases in the pallidum (-15, -4, -8, $t = -12.67$, and 16, -2, -7, $t = 9.58$, $p < 0.001$)."* A positive $t = 9.58$ contradicts an "activity decrease" and should be $t = -9.58$. Additionally, line 85 and the Figure 5 legend (line 87) state that the shaded bands show *"5% confidence intervals"*, whereas Methods Part 2 line 7 states *"95% confidence intervals"*.
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Correct the typographical errors in the text and figure legend.
  * **HOW to fix it:** Change `t = 9.58` to `t = -9.58` on line 67 (verifying against Table S3) and change `5% confidence intervals` to `95% confidence intervals` on lines 85 and 87.

* **Defect 5: Absence of Individual-Difference Brain–Behavior Correlations and Public Unthresholded Group Statistical Maps**
  * **Location:** Results lines 78–84; Discussion lines 111–116; Data Availability Statement (Part 2, line 25).
  * **WHY it matters:** The manuscript's central functional interpretation is that stronger positive ToT $\times$ Reward BOLD slopes in high-reward blocks reflect proactive control that counteracts behavioral RT/accuracy decrements (lines 111–116). With $N = 415$ participants, this hypothesis is directly testable across individuals (or within subjects across blocks): participants with larger ToT $\times$ Reward BOLD increases in frontoparietal/thalamic/NBM regions should exhibit smaller behavioral RT or accuracy ToT decrements. No brain–behavior correlation is reported. In addition, the Data Availability Statement (Part 2, line 25) restricts data to "reasonable request" without depositing the unthresholded group-level $t$-maps (8 mm and 4 mm FWHM) and behavioral summary table on a public repository (e.g., NeuroVault / OSF).
  * **WHAT needs to be done:** Test and report the across-subject correlation between behavioral ToT/Reward slopes and neural ToT/Reward parameter estimates, and deposit group-level statistical maps publicly.
  * **HOW to fix it:** Compute the correlation (controlling for site, age, and sex) between individual behavioral ToT and ToT $\times$ Reward random slopes (from the mixed-effects models on lines 45–48) and individual BOLD contrast estimates in the core cortical/subcortical ROIs, and upload the unthresholded group $t$-maps to NeuroVault.
