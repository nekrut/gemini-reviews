## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This study combines high-density surface electromyography (HD-sEMG) decomposition of 1,669 motor units across six human muscles (vastus lateralis [VL], vastus medialis [VM], gastrocnemius medialis [GM], soleus [SOL], tibialis anterior [TA], and first dorsal interosseous [FDI]) at two isometric contraction intensities (10% and 40% MVC in 6 male participants) with a leaky integrate-and-fire spiking network simulator (`Brian 2`) and Sequential Neural Posterior Estimation (`SNPE` with Masked Autoregressive Flows in `sbi`). First, the authors demonstrate through forward simulations that standard synchronization cross-histogram proxies (trough area, trough timing, peak height) and intramuscular coherence (15–35 Hz) are non-uniquely determined because disynaptic Renshaw recurrent inhibition acts as a resonant bandpass filter that amplifies 15–35 Hz synchrony and cross-modulated common input features. To solve this inverse problem, they train neural density estimators on 12,000 within-muscle and 20,000 between-synergist simulations mapping 16 summary statistics (mean, median, SD, IQR of trough area, trough timing, peak height, and firing rate) to posterior distributions over physiological parameters. Applying this validated pipeline to experimental spike trains, they show that homonymous recurrent inhibition is lowest in FDI, decreases from 10% to 40% MVC in GM, TA, and FDI ($p_\Delta \ge 0.95$), but increases from 10% to 40% MVC in VL and VM ($p_\Delta \ge 0.83$, alongside an increase in VL$\leftrightarrow$VM heteronymous inhibition), and that larger, higher-threshold motor neurons deliver stronger recurrent inhibition as indexed by backward trough area ($20/34$ regressions significant at $P < 0.05$). All raw HD-sEMG signals, decomposed spike trains, simulation code, and trained `sbi` models are openly available on Recherche Data Gouv (`10.57745/4CKBPO`) and GitHub.

This is a rigorously executed methodological and physiological study. The authors explicitly demonstrate the ill-posedness of naive feature regressions (Fig. 3), report held-out accuracy and posterior coverage calibration curves (fig. S2), conduct posterior-predictive checks on the experimental data (reporting both correlations and the systematic bias in trough area RMSE = 1.41, Fig. 4B), perform spike-train shuffling null controls (fig. S4), and test model misspecification against two heterogeneous common inputs, variable pool sizes ($N=100$ motor neurons, 30 Renshaw cells), and recording lengths (fig. S5). The primary methodological limitation is that experimental motor units were pooled across all six participants into a single 16-element summary vector per muscle $\times$ intensity condition before inference, which treats inter-subject variance as within-pool motor neuron heterogeneity and precludes hierarchical estimation of between-subject uncertainty.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1: Synchronization cross-histogram features (trough area, peak height) and beta-band intramuscular coherence (15–35 Hz) are confounded by nonlinear interactions between Renshaw recurrent inhibition and high-frequency common synaptic input.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Forward spiking simulations (Fig. 3A–D) convincingly demonstrate that Renshaw inhibition alone generates 15–35 Hz coherence scaled by IPSP time constants, acts as a bandpass amplifier in the presence of broadband input, and allows opposite parameter regimes to generate nearly identical cross-histograms ($R^2 = 0.79$) and coherence spectra ($R^2 = 0.84$).
* **Claim 2: The `SNPE` simulation-based inference pipeline accurately and with calibrated uncertainty recovers recurrent inhibition strength and high-frequency common input amplitude, though not exact input bandwidth parameters.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Held-out validation (fig. S2) shows $\text{RMSE} \le 0.124$ and $r \ge 0.90$ for baseline excitation, recurrent inhibition strength, and high-frequency common input amplitude with mean coverage calibration error $\le 3.9\%$, while transparently reporting unidentifiability of $f_{\text{center}}$ and $f_{\text{half-width}}$ ($r \approx 0$) and the between-group common input correlation $\rho$.
* **Claim 3: Homonymous recurrent inhibition is minimal in FDI, decreases from 10% to 40% MVC in GM, TA, and FDI, but increases from 10% to 40% MVC in the knee extensors (VL and VM) alongside emergent VL$\leftrightarrow$VM heteronymous inhibition.** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  The condition-level posteriors (Fig. 6A–B) and shuffle controls (fig. S4) support these contrasts ($p_\Delta \ge 0.83\text{–}0.97$). However, because motor units were pooled across all 6 participants prior to computing SD and IQR summary statistics, and because soma diameter priors were held fixed ($60 \pm 5\ \mu\text{m}$) between 10% and 40% MVC despite recruitment of larger motor units at 40% MVC, a portion of the 10% vs. 40% MVC contrast could reflect unmodeled recruitment of higher-conductance motor neurons or inter-individual heterogeneity.
* **Claim 4: Larger, higher-threshold motor neurons deliver stronger recurrent inhibition to the motor pool at 40% MVC.** `[Well Supported]`  
  Evaluating backward trough area (negative lags) on the inhibition-receiver cross-histogram cleverly isolates delivered inhibition from the receiver neuron's input resistance ($R_i$), showing positive within-participant regressions against recruitment threshold in 20 of 34 muscle-participant combinations ($R^2 = 0.38 \pm 0.19$, BH-corrected $P < 0.05$).

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

1. **Pooling Motor Units Across Participants Inflates Dispersion Summary Statistics (SD and IQR) Fed to the Neural Density Estimator:**  
   In the Results (line 121) and Methods (line 192), the authors state that for each muscle $\times$ intensity condition, *"motor units were pooled across participants, and each observable feature was characterized by its mean, median, SD, and interquartile range. These summaries yielded a 16-element feature-summary vector."* In the training simulations, however, each 16-element summary vector was computed from a single simulated pool of 30 motor neurons sharing identical common input parameters ($I_{\text{baseline}}$, $\sigma(I_{\text{common, high}})$, $\mu_{\text{disynaptic}}$). When experimental motor units from 6 different human participants (who naturally differ in baseline firing rates, subcutaneous tissue filtering, and common drive) are concatenated into a single mega-pool ($n = 27\text{ to }254$ motor units), the empirical SD and IQR of the 4 features combine **within-pool neuron-to-neuron variance** with **between-participant variance**. Because the neural density estimator was trained on within-simulation SD and IQR without inter-subject variance, this mismatch in dispersion statistics likely contributes to the systematic bias observed in the experimental posterior-predictive checks (where trough area had an $\text{RMSE} = 1.41\text{ SD}$, line 106, Fig. 4B).
2. **Fixed Motor Neuron Soma Size Distribution ($60 \pm 5\ \mu\text{m}$) Across 10% and 40% MVC Conditions:**  
   In the Motor Neuron Model (line 38 and Table 1, lines 212–213), all simulations instantiate 30 motor neurons with soma diameters drawn from a fixed normal distribution ($60 \pm 5\ \mu\text{m}$), from which $R_i$, $C_i$, $\tau_i$, $\tau_{\text{AHP}}^i$, $I_{\text{rheobase}}^i$, and input scaling $S_i = R_i / R_{\text{min}}$ are deterministically derived. In human muscles (especially VL and VM, which rely heavily on recruitment coding up to 50–60% MVC), increasing force from 10% to 40% MVC recruits substantially larger, higher-threshold motor units with lower membrane resistance $R_i$, shorter membrane time constants $\tau_i = R_i C_i$, and shorter AHP time constants $\tau_{\text{AHP}}^i$. Because the simulator holds $\mu(D) = 60\ \mu\text{m}$ fixed at both 10% and 40% MVC, changes in cross-histogram trough timing and area driven by the recruitment of faster-time-constant motor units at 40% MVC must be absorbed by the inferred synaptic parameters ($\mu_{\text{disynaptic}}$ and $I_{\text{baseline}}$).

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

* **Two-Stage Sequential Estimation of Between-Muscle Posteriors:** In the between-muscles (`VL↔VM` and `GM↔SOL`) inference (line 197), within-muscle parameters were sampled from the already-inferred single-muscle posteriors rather than inferred jointly from the combined self- and cross-histograms. If substantial heteronymous recurrent inhibition (`VL↔VM` at 40% MVC) contributes to the within-muscle firing rate and cross-histogram trough area of VL and VM, conditioning on the single-muscle posteriors (which attributed 100% of the trough area to homonymous inhibition) can slightly double-count total recurrent inhibition.
* **Single-Seed Reporting for `SNPE` Posteriors:** Line 246 notes that `MAF` training was repeated across multiple random seeds and that "Posteriors reported in Results are derived from a representative training run." Averaging posterior draws across an ensemble of 5–10 independently trained normalizing flows (deep ensemble posterior averaging) is standard practice in `sbi` to eliminate seed variance, especially for broad posteriors like SOL at 40% MVC.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Report Subject-Level Inferences or Hierarchical Summary Aggregation to Separate Within- vs. Between-Participant Variance:**
   * **WHY:** Concatenating motor units across 6 participants inflates the empirical SD and IQR summary statistics relative to single-pool simulations and masks inter-individual variability.
   * **WHAT:** Run the trained neural density estimator at the individual-participant level (for all participant $\times$ muscle $\times$ intensity blocks meeting the spike-count QC threshold) and/or compute the 16 summary statistics *within* each participant first and feed the across-participant average of the within-participant SD and IQR into the estimator.
   * **HOW:** Add a supplementary figure comparing the hierarchical/participant-averaged posterior distributions against the pooled posteriors in Fig. 6A–B, and test whether using within-participant SD/IQR reduces the posterior-predictive bias in trough area ($\text{RMSE} = 1.41$ in Fig. 4B).
2. **Conduct a Sensitivity Analysis Varying Simulated Soma Diameter Distributions ($\mu(D)$) at 40% MVC:**
   * **WHY:** Holding $\mu(D) = 60 \pm 5\ \mu\text{m}$ constant between 10% and 40% MVC ignores the recruitment of larger motor units with smaller $R_i$ and shorter $\tau_i$ at 40% MVC, particularly in VL and VM.
   * **WHAT:** Run a misspecification check (analogous to the 100-MN and 2-input checks in lines 261–265) in which 40% MVC spike trains are generated from a pool shifted toward larger soma diameters (e.g., $\mu(D) = 68 \pm 8\ \mu\text{m}$).
   * **HOW:** Pass synthetic features from the larger-soma pool through the standard $\mu(D) = 60\ \mu\text{m}$ neural density estimator to verify that shifting soma size does not artificially inflate inferred $\mu_{\text{disynaptic}}$, thereby confirming that the observed increase in VL and VM recurrent inhibition at 40% MVC is robust to size recruitment.
3. **Ensemble Posterior Draws Across Multiple `MAF` Training Seeds:**
   * **WHY:** Line 246 notes seed-to-seed variability in high-uncertainty conditions (such as SOL at 40% MVC) when reporting a single representative seed.
   * **WHAT & HOW:** Pool an equal number of posterior samples (e.g., 2,000 draws each) from 5 independently initialized `SNPE` normalizing flows to form an ensemble posterior for Fig. 6A–B.
