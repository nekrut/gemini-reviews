## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study analyzes whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data from 2,005 *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (Mtb) isolates collected across four regions of Thailand between 1994 and 2020 (including 1,189 newly sequenced isolates from Siriraj Hospital, 2017–2020, and 816 public ENA genomes, 1994–2016). Using `TB-Profiler v6.2.2`, `IQ-TREE v2.2.6`, `GEMMA v0.98.5`, `BEAST2 v2.7.6`, and `TransPhylo v1.4.10`, the authors report that (1) Lineage 2 (78.3%), specifically the Beijing sub-lineage L2.2.1 (65.7%), predominates in this predominantly drug-resistant convenience cohort (67.3% MDR-TB, 14.6% pre-XDR-TB); (2) genotypic drug resistance predictions achieve 91.1% overall concordance across 12,640 phenotypic drug susceptibility tests (pDSTs), including ~96% sensitivity and specificity for isoniazid and rifampicin; (3) a 22-isolate L2.2.1 cluster in Central Thailand carries the *ddn* Gly81Ser mutation associated with delamanid resistance, with isolates collected between 2005 and 2017 predating delamanid rollout; (4) applying a 13-SNP pairwise threshold groups 63.1% of isolates ($n = 1,265$) into 206 putative transmission clusters, with logistic regression and GWAS linking L2, MDR/pre-XDR status, and *katG* Ser315Thr / *folC* S150G / *ppe8* V2309I to clustering; and (5) `BEAST2` analysis of the largest cluster ($n = 288$, TMRCA 1989) estimates a substitution rate of $1.10 \times 10^{-7}$ substitutions/site/year ($\sim 0.48\text{ SNPs/genome/year}$). Raw sequencing data are deposited in ENA (Table S1).

**Editorial Verdict: Moderate-to-Major Revision Required (Mixed / Salvageable).** This is a valuable genomic surveillance resource for Thailand, and the core clinical microbiology findings—the high concordance of `TB-Profiler` with first-line pDST, the identification of 88 candidate resistance mutations in discordant isolates, the pre-rollout circulation of the *ddn* Gly81Ser delamanid-resistance mutation, and the L2.2.1 molecular clock estimate—are well supported and directly actionable for National TB Control Programmes. However, the epidemiological transmission clustering, GWAS, and `TransPhylo` analyses suffer from major methodological flaws: (1) because the cohort was assembled via convenience sampling heavily enriched for MDR/XDR-TB referral patients (88.4% drug-resistant vs. 11.6% pan-sensitive) and Central Thailand (64.8%), the higher clustering rate ($\le 13\text{ SNPs}$) of MDR/pre-XDR and Central isolates in logistic regression is a direct artifact of differential sampling density rather than higher intrinsic transmissibility; (2) the `GEMMA` GWAS claims in Methods line 32 to control for drug resistance, yet identifies *katG* Ser315Thr (the primary INH resistance mutation) as its top transmissibility hit *because* most clusters are MDR-TB, and does not mask hypervariable repetitive *ppe8* or homoplastic drug-resistance loci; (3) `TransPhylo` reconstruction on a 31-year endemic clade ($n = 288$) using a posterior probability cut-off of `> 0.2` produces biologically implausible Pre-XDR $\to$ MDR reversions and backwards-in-time (`2008 to 2006`) transmission links; and (4) Results line 52 calculates phenotypic DST percentages by dividing $n = 1,826$ subset counts by $N = 2,005$.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1 (Genotypic resistance profiling via `TB-Profiler` achieves 91.1% overall concordance with phenotypic DST across 12,640 tests [~96% sensitivity/specificity for INH and RIF], and identifies a 22-isolate Central Thailand cluster carrying the delamanid-resistance-associated *ddn* Gly81Ser mutation prior to drug rollout):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Backed by 12,640 paired genotypic-phenotypic comparisons across 1,826 isolates (Tables S3–S7) and clear phylogenetic clustering of the 22 *ddn* Gly81Ser L2.2.1 isolates collected between 2005 and 2017—an important surveillance warning for BPaLM/delamanid regimens.
* **Claim 2 (`BEAST2` phylodynamic analysis of the largest L2.2.1 clade [$n = 288$] estimates a substitution rate of $1.10 \times 10^{-7}$ substitutions/site/year [95% HPD: $9.89 \times 10^{-8}\text{--}1.22 \times 10^{-7}$], equivalent to ~0.48 substitutions/genome/year, with a TMRCA of 1989):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Consistent with published *M. tuberculosis* Lineage 2 evolutionary rates globally, with all MCMC parameters achieving $\text{ESS} > 200$.
* **Claim 3 (MDR-TB [$\text{OR} = 5.82$], pre-XDR-TB [$\text{OR} = 10.98$], Lineage 2 [$\text{OR} = 7.41$], and Central/Northeastern regions [$\text{OR} > 2.5$] are significant risk factors for recent transmission clustering):** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  In any incomplete sample, the probability that a sequenced isolate has a close genetic neighbor within $\le 13\text{ SNPs}$ scales strongly with the **sampling fraction** of that subpopulation. As stated in Methods line 22, this cohort was collected via convenience sampling *"predominantly from drug resistance cases (e.g., MDR-TB and XDR-TB)"* at a specialist referral hub in Central Thailand (Siriraj Hospital), leaving pan-sensitive TB ($n = 233$ over 26 years across all of Thailand) massively undersampled relative to its true incidence (~100,000 cases/year). Comparing clustering odds across strata with orders-of-magnitude differences in sampling fraction confounds ascertainment density with biological transmissibility.
* **Claim 4 (GWAS via `GEMMA` identifies four genetic loci—*katG* Ser315Thr, *folC* S150G, *ppe8* V2309I, and *folK* Gln47His—significantly associated with increased Mtb transmissibility):** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  First, Methods line 32 states that `GEMMA` *"accounted for the kinship matrix, incorporating SNPs, lineage, and drug resistance"*, yet Results line 65 states that *katG* Ser315Thr was detected ($\text{OR} = 7.93, P < 0.00001$) *"as most clusters are linked to at least MDR-TB"*—proving that INH/MDR resistance was not controlled for when testing *katG*! Second, *ppe8* (`Rv0355c`) is a repetitive polymorphic PPE gene containing structural deletions (`RD304` in L2) where short-read alignment artifacts routinely generate false-positive SNP calls; standard Mtb bioinformatic pipelines explicitly mask PE/PPE genes. Third, $P < 0.001$ (and $P < 0.05$ for *ppe8* V2309I) does not meet genome-wide significance across common SNPs.
* **Claim 5 (`TransPhylo` reconstructs "strongly supported" who-infected-whom transmission pathways [posterior probability $> 0.2$] and estimates only 132 unsampled cases across the 288-isolate clade spanning 1989–2020):** `[Overstated]`  
  A posterior probability threshold of $> 0.20$ means up to an 80% posterior probability that the inferred direct infector-infectee link is incorrect; labeling $P > 0.20$ as *"strongly supported transmission links"* (line 70) is statistically untenable. Furthermore, estimating only 132 unsampled cases over 31 years in a convenience sample implies an unrealistic ~69% case capture rate, and the authors note in Discussion line 85 that the tree inferred transmission from Pre-XDR-TB to MDR-TB and from 2008 back to 2006.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Ascertainment Bias and Unmasked Homoplasy in Transmission Clustering (Pillars 2, 3, 5)
* **Convenience Sampling Bias in Clustering Logistic Regressions:** Only 233 pan-sensitive Mtb isolates were sequenced across the entire 1994–2020 period versus 1,647 MDR/pre-XDR/XDR isolates, and 64.8% of all isolates came from the Central region (lines 22, 40, 45). Because a pair of cases can only fall within $\le 13\text{ SNPs}$ if both cases are sampled and sequenced, logistic regression of cluster membership on MDR/pre-XDR status and region (line 63; Table S9) reflects referral and sequencing selection bias rather than transmission fitness.
* **Inclusion of Unmasked PE/PPE Genes and Convergent Drug-Resistance Mutations in SNP Distances and Phylogenies:** Methods line 27 does not mention masking repetitive PE/PPE gene families, mobile insertion elements (`IS6110`), or known drug-resistance loci prior to computing the 75,060-SNP alignment, `snp-dists` pairwise distances, `IQ-TREE`, or `BEAST2` trees. Under drug selection, resistance mutations in *rpoB*, *katG*, *pncA*, *embB*, *rpsL*, and *gyrA* arise repeatedly by independent convergent evolution (homoplasy). Leaving homoplastic resistance SNPs in the alignment artificially pulls unrelated drug-resistant strains closer together and explains why `TransPhylo` inferred apparent "reversions" from Pre-XDR-TB to MDR-TB along internal branches (Discussion line 85).

### 3.2 Contradictions in `GEMMA` GWAS Specification and Significance Thresholds (Pillars 3, 4)
* **Failure to Control for Drug Resistance Despite Methods Claim:** Methods line 32 states that `GEMMA` linear mixed models incorporated *"SNPs, lineage, and drug resistance"* as covariates/random effects. Yet the top hit in Results line 65 is `katG Ser315Thr` ($\text{OR} = 7.93, P < 0.00001$), which the authors explicitly attribute to the fact that *"most clusters are linked to at least MDR-TB."* Additionally, `GEMMA` fits a linear mixed model (outputting regression coefficients $\beta$), whereas line 65 reports unadjusted univariate Odds Ratios (`OR 7.93`, `OR 6.5`) and inconsistent $p$-values (stating all 4 loci had `P < 0.001` in sentence 1 of line 65, then reporting `P < 0.05` for `ppe8 V2309I` four sentences later).

### 3.3 Denominator Mismatch in Phenotypic DST Percentages and Matrix Non-Independence (Pillars 4, 7)
* **Dividing $n = 1,826$ Phenotypic Counts by $N = 2,005$ Total Isolates (Results line 52):**
  * Line 52 states that phenotypic DST was performed on a subset of **$n = 1,826$** samples, and then reports: *"Overall, 1,522 isolates (75.9%) were classified as MDR-TB, followed by 201 isolates (10.0%) with Pre-XDR TB+, and 59 isolates (2.9%) being pan-sensitive."*
  * Dividing by the actual tested denominator $n = 1,826$ gives **$83.4\%$** ($1,522/1,826$) for MDR-TB, **$11.0\%$** ($201/1,826$) for Pre-XDR TB+, and **$3.2\%$** ($59/1,826$) for pan-sensitive! The reported percentages (`75.9%`, `10.0%`, `2.9%`) were erroneously calculated using $N = 2,005$ (which includes 179 isolates that never underwent pDST).
* **Composite Concordance Penalized by Missing Second-Line Phenotypic Testing:** In line 56, 114 of the 181 "discordant" MDR classifications occurred because WGS identified fluoroquinolone/second-line mutations (`pre-XDR-TB`) in isolates where phenotypic DST was only performed for first-line INH/RIF (noting from line 52 that LFX was only tested in 1,602, MFX in 1,101, and LZD in 1,097 isolates). Missing phenotypic tests for second-line drugs should be excluded from the composite MDR vs. pre-XDR concordance denominator rather than scored as discordances.
* **Kruskal–Wallis Test Applied to Pairwise Distance Matrices:** In Methods line 32 and Results line 61, the relationship between pairwise geographic distances and pairwise SNP distances across the 2,005 isolates was tested using a Kruskal–Wallis test (`P < 0.001`) and Spearman correlation (`rho = 0.046`). Pairwise matrix entries ($\sim 2 \times 10^6$ pairs) are non-independent; a Mantel permutation test is required to obtain valid $p$-values.

### Actionable Remediation Plan
1. **Mask PE/PPE Genes and Drug-Resistance Loci Prior to Phylogenetic, Clustering, and GWAS Analyses:** Exclude repetitive PE/PPE genes (including *ppe8*), `IS6110`/phage regions, and canonical drug-resistance-associated genes (*rpoB*, *katG*, *inhA*, *embB*, *pncA*, *rpsL*, *rrs*, *gyrA*, *folC*) before computing `snp-dists` transmission clusters, `IQ-TREE`/`BEAST2` phylogenies, and `GEMMA` transmissibility GWAS. This will remove homoplastic drug-resistance clustering artifacts and eliminate the apparent Pre-XDR $\to$ MDR reversions in `TransPhylo`.
2. **Stratify Clustering Analyses by Sampling Era/Site and Calibrate `TransPhylo` Sampling Priors:** To mitigate convenience-sampling bias, restrict logistic regression models of clustering ($\le 5$ and $\le 12\text{ SNPs}$) to uniformly sampled time windows/sites (e.g., Siriraj Hospital 2017–2020 MDR-TB isolates) rather than pooling across pan-sensitive and MDR strata with vastly different sampling fractions. In `TransPhylo`, set an informative prior on the sampling proportion $\pi$ reflecting the true fraction of Thai TB cases sequenced, and raise the posterior probability threshold for reporting direct transmission links from $>0.20$ to $\ge 0.50\text{--}0.80$.
3. **Correct the Phenotypic DST Denominator and Matrix Permutation Tests:**
   * In Results line 52, recalculate the percentages of phenotypic MDR-TB ($1,522 / 1,826 = 83.4\%$), Pre-XDR TB+ ($201 / 1,826 = 11.0\%$), and pan-sensitive TB ($59 / 1,826 = 3.2\%$) using the actual tested sample size ($n = 1,826$) rather than $N = 2,005$, and compute composite MDR/pre-XDR concordance strictly on isolates with complete phenotypic testing for both first-line and fluoroquinolone drugs.
   * Replace the Kruskal–Wallis test on pairwise geographic vs. SNP distances (line 61) with a Mantel permutation test.
