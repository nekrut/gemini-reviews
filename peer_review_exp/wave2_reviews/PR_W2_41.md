## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript presents a chromosome-level nuclear and organellar genome assembly for *Trebouxia* sp. 'A48' (deposited as live strain SAG 2671), an axenically cultured green algal photobiont isolated from the model sunburst lichen *Xanthoria parietina*. Using 22.4 Gbp of Oxford Nanopore PromethION long reads (`hifiasm v0.24.0`) and four Illumina RNA-seq libraries (`funannotate`), the authors assemble a 69.09 Mbp nuclear genome across 20 contigs (N50 = 3.71 Mbp, 97.2% BUSCO completeness, 0.6% duplication), of which 16 possess telomeric repeats (`CCCTAAA`) at both termini, alongside complete circular plastid (320.5 kbp) and mitochondrial (109.5 kbp) genomes. The authors leverage this reference to examine synteny with the metagenome-assembled genome (MAG) *T.* sp. SL0000003, test ploidy and meiotic gene inventory, confirm two ancient fungal horizontal gene transfers (HGTs), characterize the predicted secretome (103 proteins), and compare gene expression between axenic solid-medium cultures ($n=3$) and field-collected *X. parietina* metatranscriptomes ($n=8$). Raw data (`PRJEB95800`), the biological strain (`SAG 2671`), and bioinformatic scripts (`GitHub`) are publicly available.

**Editorial Verdict: Minor-to-Moderate Revision (Strong / Sound Resource).** The primary deliverable—a near-telomere-to-telomere assembly and live axenic reference strain for a model lichen photobiont—is of high technical quality and immediate value to the algal and symbiosis communities. However, the secondary comparative and transcriptomic analyses contain several methodological caveats that require remediation or explicit qualification: (1) the culture vs. thallus RNA-seq comparison is confounded by pseudoreplication (8 paired libraries from 4 thalli treated as independent against 3 culture libraries), cross-OTU sequence divergence in `kallisto` k-mer pseudoalignment, and the presence of rifampicin and carbendazim in the culture medium; (2) orthogroup comparisons between *T.* sp. 'A48' and *T.* sp. SL0000003 are biased by asymmetric annotation protocols (RNA-seq-trained `funannotate update` vs. protein-only `funannotate predict`); and (3) assembly quality metrics lack a k-mer-based consensus quality value (QV) and rely on a narrow GC/coverage window (48–50% GC, 180–210$\times$) for nuclear contig retention.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1 (Chromosome-level genome assembly of *Trebouxia* sp. 'A48' into 20 nuclear contigs, 16 telomere-to-telomere, with 97.2% BUSCO completeness and circular organelle genomes):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Supported by high-depth ONT sequencing (~200$\times$ nuclear coverage), manual inspection and splitting of a 390$\times$ plastid-nuclear chimera and internal telomeric fusions, high single-copy BUSCO recovery (`chlorophyta_odb10`), and strong macrosynteny with *T.* sp. SL0000003 (Fig. 3c). Base-level consensus QV (e.g., via `Merqury`) should be added.
* **Claim 2 (*Trebouxia* sp. 'A48' is haploid and retains a complete core meiotic and flagellar genetic toolkit despite lacking *Chlamydomonas* mating-type loci *FUS1*/*MTD1*):** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  The inventory of meiotic genes (*DMC1*, *MND1*, *ZIP4*, *HOP2*, *HAP2*, *GEX1*) and 42 flagellar genes, together with confocal observation of flagellated cells (Fig. 1b), is solid. However, variant calling on self-mapped reads from a single-colony clonal isolate (`VarScan` allele frequencies <0.15 or >0.85; Fig. 2e) only demonstrates homozygosity, which cannot distinguish a haploid from an automictic/endoreduplicated homozygous diploid—a caveat the authors appropriately acknowledge in the Discussion by noting the absence of flow cytometry.
* **Claim 3 (Confirmation of two ancient horizontal gene transfers from basal fungi into *Trebouxia*—an SDR oxidoreductase `GTX0536PRED_004195` and a SLAC1/TDT-like anion channel `GTX0536PRED_006449`):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Contig-level assembly context (flanking genes returning exclusively Viridiplantae hits; Fig. 4b) rules out fungal contig contamination, and maximum-likelihood phylogenies across expanded database sampling place the chlorophyte clade nested within basal fungi rather than Lecanoromycetes (Fig. 4c,d).
* **Claim 4 (The predicted secretome of 103 proteins is enriched in hydrolases, redox enzymes, and carbohydrate-binding proteins, while *T.* sp. 'A48' possesses 1,864 unique orthogroups relative to *T.* sp. SL0000003):** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  The secretome pipeline (`SignalP 5` $\cap$ `deepTMHMM` $\cap$ `WolfPSORT`) and domain enrichment analyses are sound. However, the claim of 1,864 orthogroups unique to 'A48' vs. 449 in SL0000003 is inflated by annotation asymmetry: 'A48' was annotated using `funannotate train` and `update` with RNA-seq data (12,820 genes; 14,492 protein isoforms), whereas SL0000003 was annotated without transcriptomic evidence using 'A48' proteins as hints (10,061 genes including tRNAs; 9,928 proteins).
* **Claim 5 (Differential expression between axenic culture and lichen thalli reveals photosynthetic suppression and stress/DNA-repair induction in culture due to mixotrophic growth):** `[Fragile]`  
  While biologically plausible, this comparison is confounded by three unmodeled factors: (i) the culture medium contained rifampicin (50 $\mu$g ml$^{-1}$, an inhibitor of bacterial and plastid-encoded RNA polymerase) and carbendazim (25–40 $\mu$g ml$^{-1}$, a benzimidazole $\beta$-tubulin/mitotic inhibitor), which directly induce cellular stress and perturb plastid transcription; (ii) the 8 thallus metatranscriptomes represent 4 paired thalli (center + edge), violating sample independence when contrasted against 3 culture replicates without a random/blocking effect for thallus ID; and (iii) mapping field metatranscriptome reads from potentially distinct *Trebouxia* OTUs to the 'A48' transcriptome via exact 31-mer matching in `kallisto` biases downward the apparent expression of rapidly evolving genes in field thalli.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Confounding and Pseudoreplication in Culture vs. Thallus Transcriptomics (Pillars 2, 3, 4, 6)
* **Antibiotic and Fungicide Exposure in Culture Medium:** In Materials and Methods (line 54), algal cultures were isolated and bulked on TONM medium containing rifampicin (50 $\mu$g ml$^{-1}$) and carbendazim (25–40 $\mu$g ml$^{-1}$). Line 61 states that the three DEG libraries were grown on solid TONM medium for 2 months (without clarifying whether antibiotics/fungicides were omitted during the 2-month growth phase). Rifampicin inhibits plastid-encoded RNA polymerase (PEP) in chlorophytes, and carbendazim binds eukaryotic $\beta$-tubulin, arresting spindle assembly and inducing DNA damage/stress responses. Attributing the downregulation of photosynthesis genes and upregulation of DNA repair and heat-shock proteins in culture (line 170; Fig. 6c,d; Fig. S4) solely to mixotrophic glucose/peptone utilization overlooks chemical stress from antimicrobial treatment and 2-month stationary-phase aging on solid plates.
* **Pseudoreplication Across Paired Thallus Libraries:** The 8 lichen metatranscriptome libraries consist of 4 individual thalli, each sampled twice (older center and younger edge; line 174). When testing `thallus edge vs. center`, the authors blocked by thallus ID in `sleuth` (line 94). However, in the `culture (n=3) vs. lichen thallus (n=8)` comparison, thallus ID is nested strictly within the lichen group and cannot be included as a fixed blocking factor in a standard two-group `sleuth` design; treating the 8 libraries as independent replicates underestimates within-group variance and inflates the 755 reported DEGs.
* **Cross-OTU Pseudoalignment Bias in `kallisto`:** Field *X. parietina* thalli associate with multiple *Trebouxia* OTUs (e.g., A48, A40, A33, SL0000003; Fig. 3b). `kallisto` (line 94) relies on exact k-mer matching ($k=31$). If any of the 4 field thalli carried a non-A48 *Trebouxia* OTU, conserved housekeeping and photosynthetic genes (high sequence identity) will retain high k-mer mapping rates, whereas rapidly evolving lineage-specific or secreted genes will suffer k-mer dropout, artificially appearing downregulated in lichen thalli.

### 3.2 Asymmetric Genome Annotation & OrthoFinder Bias (Pillars 3, 5)
* **Annotation Protocol Mismatch:** *T.* sp. 'A48' was annotated with `funannotate` using RNA-seq training (`train`) and PASA/UTR refinement (`update`), yielding 12,820 genes and 14,492 predicted proteins (line 73; Table 1). Conversely, for *T.* sp. SL0000003, the authors skipped `train` and `update` and passed the 'A48' proteome as evidence to `funannotate predict`, yielding 10,061 genes and 9,928 proteins. (Note also that Table 1 reports more genes than proteins for SL0000003 because `tRNAscan-SE` tRNA models are included in the gene count, whereas in 'A48' alternative splicing isoforms from `funannotate update` push the protein count above the gene count).
* **Impact on Unique Gene Family Claims:** Running `OrthoFinder` across proteomes generated by asymmetric pipelines (and using only `BUSCO` `chlorophyta_odb10` single-copy core genes for *T. lynnae*, *T. gelatinosa*, and *T.* sp. TZW2008; line 84, Fig. 3d) systematically overestimates the number of orthogroups unique to 'A48' (1,864 relative to SL0000003; 267 across the genus; line 122).

### 3.3 Assembly Filtering, Mitochondrial Reassembly Version Mismatch, and Ploidy Pipeline Caveats (Pillars 3, 7)
* **Hard GC and Coverage Thresholding:** Nuclear contigs were retained only if they fell within `48–50%` GC content and `180–210x` coverage (line 66). While effective at removing bacterial contaminants, a 2% GC window is unusually tight for a eukaryotic nuclear genome and could exclude legitimate subtelomeric, centromeric, or rDNA-bearing contigs with skewed GC or copy number.
* **Inconsistent `hifiasm` Versions (`v0.24.0` vs. `v0.18.5`) and Missing ONT Consensus QV:** The nuclear draft assembly used `hifiasm v0.24.0` (which supports native ONT simplex correction via `--ont`), whereas the mitochondrial reassembly from the top 30,000 reads used `hifiasm v0.18.5` (line 68), a legacy version designed exclusively for PacBio HiFi reads prior to the introduction of ONT support in `hifiasm v0.21`. Furthermore, no base-level consensus quality score (QV) or k-mer completeness estimate (e.g., `Merqury` using the 8 Gbp PE150 Illumina reads) is provided.
* **`Picard MarkDuplicates` on Shotgun Long Reads:** In the ploidy analysis (line 75), the authors ran `Picard v2.21.2` to remove duplicate reads from `minimap2`-aligned ONT data before running `VarScan`. `Picard MarkDuplicates` identifies duplicates by identical 5' alignment coordinates, which is inappropriate for high-coverage (200$\times$) sheared/fragmented long reads and artificially discards legitimate reads starting at the same genomic coordinate.

### Actionable Remediation Plan
1. **Report Consensus Base-Level QV and Clarify Contig Filtering:** Run `Merqury` (or `Yak`) using the Illumina PE150 RNA-seq reads (or R10.4.1 ONT k-mers) to report a base-level QV score for the nuclear and organelle assemblies, and confirm in Table S5/Fig. S1a that no genuine algal contigs with $>50\%$ or $<48\%$ GC were discarded during contamination filtering. Clarify in the Table 1 footnote that "Number of genes" includes tRNA genes while "Number of proteins" includes alternative splice isoforms in 'A48'.
2. **Control for Pseudoreplication and Cross-OTU Divergence in Transcriptomics:** Re-run the culture vs. thallus differential expression analysis either by collapsing/averaging the center and edge counts per thallus ($n=4$ independent biological thalli vs. $n=3$ cultures) or by fitting a linear mixed model (`dream`/`limma-voom` or `DESeq2` on collapsed biological replicates), and verify from ITS/rbcL reads in the 8 metatranscriptome libraries which specific *Trebouxia* OTU each field thallus harbored. Explicitly state in the Methods and Discussion whether rifampicin and carbendazim were present in the 2-month solid TONM cultures used for RNA-seq and discuss their potential contribution to stress/DNA-repair gene upregulation.
3. **Symmetrize Comparative Gene Family Counts:** To ensure an apples-to-apples orthogroup comparison between *T.* sp. 'A48' and *T.* sp. SL0000003, either compare only longest-isoform protein sets generated under identical *ab initio* parameters (`funannotate predict` without RNA-seq UTR/isoform inflation) or perform `tblastn`/`miniprot` searches of the "1,864 unique 'A48' orthogroups" against the unannotated SL0000003 genome sequence to distinguish true gene gain/loss from unannotated loci.
