## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript investigates the population genetic diversity, geographic differentiation, and gene flow of the malaria vector *Anopheles funestus s.s.* ($n = 654$) across 10 administrative regions in mainland Tanzania, alongside a single population of sibling species *An. parensis* ($n = 28$) from Dodoma as an outgroup, using 12 microsatellite markers.

* **Claim 1:** Genetic diversity (allelic richness $N_a$ and expected/observed heterozygosity $H_S, H_O$) does not differ significantly among the 10 Tanzanian *An. funestus s.s.* populations. `[Methodologically Flawed]`
* **Claim 2:** Deviations from Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium (HWE) occur in 9 of 11 populations (heterozygote deficits), but do not reflect sub-structuring or assortative mating, instead resulting from "excessive statistical power" due to sample size. `[Overstated]`
* **Claim 3:** There is no isolation by distance (IBD) across mainland Tanzania ($R^2 = -0.016, p = 0.569$), and the eastern arm of the Great Rift Valley does not act as a barrier to gene flow ($F_{ST} = 0.18$ vs. $0.17, p = 0.900$). `[Moderately Supported]`
* **Claim 4:** Passive anthropogenic transport along major road and railway corridors (specifically the central railway linking Pwani and Kigoma, and the southern highway linking Lindi, Ruvuma, and Rukwa) drives long-distance gene flow in *An. funestus s.s.* `[Overstated]`
* **Claim 5:** The southeastern coastal population of Mtwara forms a highly distinct genetic cluster ($F_{ST}$ up to $0.288$, separating in STRUCTURE at $K=3$ and branching basally in the neighbor-joining tree), reflecting ecological isolation, cryptic lineage structure, or connectivity with northern Mozambique. `[Fragile]`

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Direct Mathematical Contradiction in Diversity Hypothesis Testing
In the Results section (line 70), the manuscript reports:
> *"No significant differences in $H_S$ or $H_O$ were found across the 10 populations (Kruskal-Wallis: $\chi^2 > 16.3, \text{df} = 9, p < 0.050$ in both cases)."*

This statement contains two simultaneous mathematical errors:
1. A p-value of $p < 0.050$ indicates a **statistically significant** difference, directly contradicting the claim that *"No significant differences in $H_S$ or $H_O$ were found."*
2. Under a central $\chi^2$ distribution with $\text{df} = 9$, the critical threshold for $\alpha = 0.05$ is $\chi^2_{0.95, 9} = 16.919$. A test statistic of $\chi^2 = 16.3$ with $\text{df} = 9$ yields $p = 0.0609 > 0.05$. Thus, either the $\chi^2$ statistic, the degrees of freedom, the p-value inequality, or the substantive conclusion is erroneous.

### 2.2 Confounding of Spatial Structure by Larval Sibship Sampling and Multi-Year Collection Span
The sampling protocol introduces two major population-genetic confounders that are neither modeled nor controlled for:
1. **Larval sampling in three regions (Dodoma, Tanga, and Morogoro):** When adult captures were insufficient, larvae were dipped from breeding sites, reared to adulthood, and genotyped alongside indoor-captured adults (line 35). In *Anopheles* population genetics, larval collections from a limited number of aquatic habitats notoriously sample full- and half-sib families (the "Allendorf-Phelps effect"). Genotyping related larvae artificially inflates within-population linkage disequilibrium, distorts allele frequencies, generates spurious heterozygote deficits or excesses, and inflates pairwise $F_{ST}$. Notably, all three regions where larval sampling was conducted (Dodoma, Tanga, Morogoro) exhibited significant multi-locus HWE departures.
2. **Four-year temporal span (December 2018 to December 2022):** Assuming a generation time of ~3–4 weeks for *An. funestus*, a 4-year window spans ~40–50 mosquito generations. If different regions were sampled in different years or seasons, temporal genetic drift and seasonal bottlenecks are completely confounded with geographic divergence ($F_{ST}$), yet collection dates per site are omitted from the analysis.

### 2.3 Flawed Locus Filtering and Dismissal of Pervasive Heterozygote Deficits
Six of the 18 candidate microsatellite markers (33%) were discarded because they deviated from HWE in more than 4 of the 11 populations, with the rationale: *"To eliminate loci that did not conform to neutrality"* (line 55).
* In microsatellite datasets, pervasive locus-specific HWE departures (specifically heterozygote deficits) are almost universally caused by **null alleles** (primer binding site mutations), large-allele dropout, or stutter scoring artifacts—not natural selection.
* No null-allele frequency estimation (e.g., via Micro-Checker or FreeNA) was performed. Even after dropping 6 loci, 9 out of 11 populations still exhibited significant multi-locus heterozygote deficits (Bonferroni $p < 0.005$), with reported mean $F_{IS}$ / deficit values of $0.407\text{--}0.660$ (line 75).
* In the Discussion (line 118), the authors dismiss these substantial deficits ($0.407\text{--}0.660$) as *"small deviations... driven by excessive statistical power, due to large sample sizes (up to 96 mosquitoes per population)"* and claim that deviations occurred in random directions, contradicting the Results section which explicitly states *"significant heterozygote deficits in 9 out of 11 populations"* and an overall mean $H_O = 0.585$ across populations only because Pwani had an extreme heterozygote excess ($H_O = 0.781$ vs. $H_S = 0.703$). Uncorrected null alleles bias $F_{ST}$ upward, particularly in differentiated populations, and violate the within-cluster HWE assumption of STRUCTURE.

### 2.4 Arbitrary Model Selection in STRUCTURE and Over-Interpretation of NJ Tree Topology
* **STRUCTURE ($K=3$) inference:** The authors used STRUCTURE v2.2 with 10 replicates per $K$ (50,000 burn-in, 250,000 MCMC iterations) and selected $K=3$ based on raw $\Pr(X|K)$ alongside subjective concordance with neighbor-joining trees (line 60). Neither the Evanno $\Delta K$ method nor Puechmaille's estimators (which account for uneven sample sizes across populations, such as $n=28$ in Dodoma vs. $n \approx 65\text{--}96$ in *An. funestus* sites) are reported, nor is it specified whether the admixture or no-admixture model or correlated vs. uncorrelated allele frequencies were used. Including an interspecific outgroup (*An. parensis*) in STRUCTURE alongside uneven sample sizes is well known to dominate the likelihood surface at low $K$, masking hierarchical intraspecific structure.
* **Kruskal-Wallis test on non-independent pairwise $F_{ST}$ values:** To test the effect of the Rift Valley (line 87), the authors applied a Kruskal-Wallis test ($\chi^2 = 0.02, \text{df} = 1, p = 0.900$) directly to pairwise $F_{ST}$ values located on the same vs. opposite sides of the Rift Valley. Pairwise distance matrix entries share common populations and violate the independence assumption of the Kruskal-Wallis test; a partial Mantel test or AMOVA (Analysis of Molecular Variance) grouping populations by side of the Rift Valley is required.
* **Unsubstantiated anthropogenic transport claims:** The claim that clustering of Kigoma with Pwani is driven by passive mosquito transport along the 1914 Central Railway, and Lindi/Ruvuma/Rukwa by truck corridors to Zambia, is post-hoc storytelling. No spatial covariates (railway/road network distance vs. Euclidean distance, traffic volume, or resistance allele sharing) were tested, and the authors themselves note in line 110 that Kagera and Kigoma actually had the lowest pairwise $F_{ST}$ yet were separated into different NJ clades as an artifact of neighbor-joining tree construction on reticular population distances.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Software obsolescence:** The analyses rely on severely outdated software versions, including STRUCTURE v2.2 (released 2007), FSTAT v2.9.3 (2001), and MEGA 5 (2011).
2. **Inconsistent reporting of $F_{IS}$ vs. heterozygote deficit:** Line 75 states *"the mean heterozygote deficits were generally mioderate [sic] (range: 0.407 - 0.660)"*, which either represents $F_{IS}$ (in which case 0.41–0.66 is extremely high, equivalent to close inbreeding or severe null allele burden) or actually quotes $H_O$ or $H_S$ values by mistake.
3. **Cross-species microsatellite amplification bias:** Microsatellite primers developed in *An. funestus s.s.* were amplified in *An. parensis* ($n=28$). Ascertainment bias and flanking-region divergence between sibling species can cause allele size homoplasy and differential null allele rates.
4. **Typos and formatting:** Line 75 ("mioderate"), line 118 ("Funestu s"), and missing exact sample sizes ($n$) per administrative region in the main text.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Resolve the diversity statistics and $F_{IS}$ reporting:** Re-calculate and report the exact Kruskal-Wallis $\chi^2$, degrees of freedom, and exact p-values for $N_a$ (rarefied for sample size differences!), $H_S$, and $H_O$ across the 10 *An. funestus s.s.* populations, and clarify whether the range $0.407\text{--}0.660$ in line 75 refers to $H_S$, $H_O$, or $F_{IS}$.
2. **Audit and correct for null alleles and larval relatedness:**
   * Run `Micro-Checker` and `FreeNA` across all 18 microsatellite loci to estimate locus-specific null allele frequencies and compute ENA-corrected pairwise $F_{ST}$ values (comparing uncorrected vs. ENA-corrected $F_{ST}$ to verify that Mtwara's divergence is not driven by differential dropout).
   * Separate or exclude larval-reared samples in Dodoma, Tanga, and Morogoro (or run sibship reconstruction in `COLONY` to remove full/half-siblings prior to population genetic analysis) and report exact collection dates per site to test for temporal stratification.
3. **Replace invalid matrix tests with AMOVA and DAPC:**
   * Replace the Kruskal-Wallis test on pairwise $F_{ST}$ across the Rift Valley with a hierarchical AMOVA in `Arlequin` (partitioning variance among groups across the Rift Valley, among populations within groups, and within populations).
   * Run STRUCTURE excluding the interspecific outgroup (*An. parensis*), report Evanno's $\Delta K$ and Puechmaille's cuatro statistics across $K=1\text{--}10$, and complement STRUCTURE with a model-free Discriminant Analysis of Principal Components (DAPC in `adegenet`), which does not assume HWE.
   * Formally test the transport-corridor hypothesis using a multiple matrix regression with randomization (MMRR) or partial Mantel test comparing least-cost road/rail network distance against Euclidean geographic distance.
4. **Deposit raw genotype data:** Provide the complete individual-level microsatellite genotype matrix (all 682 individuals across all 18 scored loci, with region, collection date, and larval/adult collection method metadata) in a permanent repository (e.g., Dryad or Zenodo).
