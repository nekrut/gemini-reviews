## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript investigates the mechanism by which PARP inhibitor (PARPi)-induced trapped PARP1 is cleared from chromatin in mammalian cancer cells, proposing a cytoprotective selective autophagy (nucleophagy) pathway orchestrated by the selective autophagy receptor TEX264 and the AAA+ ATPase/segregase p97 (VCP).

* **Claim 1:** PARPi treatment upregulates cytoprotective autophagy, and chemical or genetic inhibition of autophagy (bafilomycin A1, `ATG7`, `STX17`, `ATG9A`, `BECN1` depletion) increases chromatin-trapped PARP1 and hypersensitizes cells specifically to trapping PARPi (talazoparib, niraparib) but not non-trapping veliparib. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 2:** Trapped PARP1 is delivered to lysosomes in an autophagy-, p97-, SUMOylation-, and ubiquitination-dependent manner (verified by LysoIP and `mCherry-PARP1-GFP` imaging), operating in parallel with the previously described RNF4–UFD1–p97 proteasomal pathway. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 3:** TEX264 directly binds PARP1 via its C-terminal region (mapped by recombinant pull-down, HDX-MS, and deletion mutagenesis) and requires both its p97-interacting SHP motif and LC3-interacting LIR motif to clear trapped PARP1 to lysosomes, prevent replication-associated DNA damage, and promote cell survival. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 4:** Trapped PARP1 exits the nucleus independently of the nuclear pore complex through ATR-induced local disruptions of lamin A/C nuclear envelope architecture. `[Overstated]`
* **Claim 5:** Inactivation of the TEX264–autophagy axis leads to cytotoxic PARP1 aggregate accumulation, re-sensitizes olaparib-resistant `BRCA1-/-` cells to PARPi, and stratifies clinical overall survival in homologous recombination-deficient (HRD) triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) patients. `[Moderately Supported]`

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Pervasive Pseudoreplication, $n = 2$ Parametric ANOVAs, and Unblinded Manual Scoring
Several key quantitative comparisons violate fundamental biostatistical principles regarding the unit of independent biological replication:
1. **Parametric ANOVA on $n = 2$ biological replicates:**
   * In Fig. 5i (LysoIP rescue in `TEX264-/-` cells with WT, `SHP*`, and `LIR*` variants, p. 46080 line 7), the caption explicitly notes: *"n = 3 biological replicates, except for TEX264−/− cells, which was included in two replicates, normalized to the WT control; mean ± s.e.m.; one-way ANOVA compared with WT."* Running a parametric one-way ANOVA on a group with $n = 2$ biological replicates (and normalizing all replicates to the WT control so WT has zero variance) is statistically invalid.
   * In Fig. 5j (colony-formation rescue in `CAL51 TEX264-/-` cells, p. 46080 line 7), the caption states: *"Six technical replicates from two biological replicates; mean ± s.e.m.; two-way ANOVA compared with WT."* Treating 6 technical well replicates across only $n = 2$ biological repeats as independent observations in a two-way ANOVA is classic pseudoreplication.
   * Similarly, Fig. 1h ($n = 371, 172, 148, 164$ cells) and Extended Data Fig. 4c ($n = 167\text{--}92$ cells) are derived from only **two biological repeats**, yet one-way ANOVA with Šídák's test is applied at the single-cell level ($P \le 0.0001$).
2. **Single-cell pooling and automated outlier pruning in immunofluorescence/PLA:** Across Figs. 1h, 4i, 5g, and Extended Data Figs. 4c,e, 7d,f, and 8b,d,g, single cells ($n = 111\text{--}1,035$ cells pooled across 2–4 biological repeats) are treated as independent experimental units in parametric one-way ANOVAs. Furthermore, the Methods (p. 92160, line 64) state: *"For some imaging experiments with large datasets, Prism's inbuilt rapid regression and outlier removal method [ROUT] was used. A q value of 1 was applied... Data distribution was assumed to be normal, but this was not formally tested."* Nuclear foci counts and fluorescence intensities per cell are right-skewed; applying ROUT outlier removal to skewed single-cell distributions systematically clips the upper tail before inflating degrees of freedom via cell-level pooling. SuperPlots (testing replicate-level means across $N \ge 3$ independent biological repeats via mixed-effects models or paired/blocked ANOVA) should be used instead.
3. **Unblinded manual quantification of cytosolic puncta:** In the `mCherry-PARP1-GFP` fixed imaging assay (Fig. 2g,h and Fig. 3e,f; p. 92160 line 8 and line 64), the Methods state: *"Quantification was performed manually by observing the number of red cytosolic puncta per cell, with only puncta in proximity to the nucleus counted"* and *"Data collection and analysis were not performed blind to the conditions of other experiments."* Unblinded manual counting of subjective "perinuclear" puncta is highly susceptible to observer bias and should be replaced or verified by an automated CellProfiler pipeline (as used for nuclear foci).

### 2.2 Mechanistic Overreach on "Nuclear Pore Independence" and ATR/ATM Inhibitor Confusion
In the Results (p. 46080, line 19) and Extended Data Fig. 6b–e, the authors conclude that *"Selective autophagy of trapped PARP1 is TEX264-dependent but nuclear pore-independent"* and that *"trapped PARP1 exits the nucleus through ATR-induced local disruptions in lamin A/C architecture"* based solely on two 3-hour drug treatments:
1. **Leptomycin B is a CRM1 (Exportin-1) inhibitor, NOT a general nuclear pore blocker:** Leptomycin B ($10\text{ nM}$, 3 h) alkylates Cys528 of CRM1/XPO1, inhibiting only leucine-rich nuclear export signal (NES)-dependent active transport. It does **not** block the nuclear pore complex (NPC) generally, nor does it inhibit other karyopherins/exportins (e.g., XPO4, XPO5, XPO7,NXF1) or NPC remodeling. Claiming that trapped PARP1 export is "nuclear pore-independent" solely because 10 nM Leptomycin B had no effect is mechanistically unwarranted.
2. **Inconsistency between ATR and ATM inhibition and lack of positive controls:** In the main text (p. 46080, line 19), VE-822 ($1\text{ \mu M}$) is described as an **"ATR inhibitor VE-822"**, whereas in the caption of Extended Data Fig. 6d (p. 46080, line 25), it is explicitly labeled as **"ATM inhibitor VE-822 (1 µM)"**. Furthermore, no immunoblot control confirming target engagement (e.g., loss of pCHK1 for ATRi or pCHK2/pATM for ATMi, or nuclear accumulation of a known CRM1 cargo such as RanBP1/NF-$\kappa$B for Leptomycin B) is shown, nor is nuclear envelope rupture or lamin A/C phosphorylation directly measured under PARPi treatment.

### 2.3 Clinical Survival Analysis Confounding in the SCAN-B Cohort
In Fig. 6h,i and Extended Data Fig. 10 (p. 46080, line 66; p. 92160, lines 51–59), the authors stratify $n = 712$ TNBC patients from the SCAN-B cohort by NMF-inferred HRD status ($n = 203$ HRD, $n = 509$ HRP) and `TEX264` mRNA expression quartiles, showing that `TEX264`-low HRD patients have better 10-year overall survival than `TEX264`-high HRD patients, and conclude under the section heading *"The p97–TEX264 autophagy axis is relevant to PARPi resistance"* that high `TEX264` promotes cancer cell survival in response to trapped PARP1.
* **Absence of PARPi treatment in SCAN-B:** Patients in the historical SCAN-B TNBC cohort (with 10-year follow-up) were treated with standard-of-care chemotherapy (anthracyclines, taxanes, cyclophosphamide/platinum), **not PARP inhibitors**.
* While endogenous or chemotherapy-induced DNA–protein crosslinks (such as TOP1cc or endogenous trapped PARP1) could plausibly interact with TEX264 in HRD tumors, presenting an unadjusted univariate log-rank survival curve from a non-PARPi-treated cohort (without multivariable Cox regression controlling for age, tumor stage, node status, proliferation/Ki67, or chemotherapy regimen) as clinical validation of **PARPi resistance** overstates the clinical evidence.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Ratio normalization to zero-variance control in LysoIP bar graphs:** In Figs. 2c, 2e, 3b, 3d, 3h, 3j, 5b, 5c, 5i, and Extended Data Fig. 3d,i,k,6c,e, biological replicates are normalized by setting one reference condition (e.g., untreated or treated control) equal to $1.0$ in every replicate, and then running a one-way ANOVA or unpaired $t$-test against that zero-variance reference bar. When replicates are ratio-normalized to a control condition per blot, that control has $\text{s.e.m.} = 0$ by construction; statistical testing should be performed on log-transformed unnormalized (HA-normalized) intensities using a randomized block / paired design (`~ Condition + Replicate`), or a one-sample $t$-test on $\log_2(\text{fold change})$ against 0.
2. **Incomplete domain boundary testing in TEX264–PARP1 interaction:** In Fig. 5e (p. 46080, line 1), truncating TEX264 at Ser272 (`TEX264-272`) deletes **both** the LIR motif (residues 273–276, FELL) and the SHP motif (residues 304–311) simultaneously. It would be valuable to show whether point mutations `LIR*` or `SHP*` alone affect the direct co-IP of TEX264 with chromatin-bound PARP1 (as opposed to LysoIP delivery in Fig. 5h).
3. **Missing GEO accession for RNA-seq datasets:** While ProteomeXchange accession `PXD071389` is provided for the HDX-MS data (p. 92160, line 38), no GEO/SRA accession number is provided in the manuscript text for the newly generated `CAL51` and `HeLa` WT vs. `TEX264-/-` RNA-seq datasets (Fig. 1a and Extended Data Fig. 7a).

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Eliminate pseudoreplication and complete $N = 3$ biological replicates:**
   * Add a third independent biological replicate for the `TEX264-/-` condition in Fig. 5i, the colony-formation rescue in Fig. 5j, and the pre-extraction IF experiments in Fig. 1h and Extended Data Fig. 4c.
   * Re-analyze all single-cell foci and PLA datasets (Figs. 1h, 4i, 5g; Extended Data Figs. 4c,e, 7d,f, 8b,d,g) as SuperPlots using the biological replicate mean/median ($N = 3\text{--}4$) as the unit of statistical inference (or a linear mixed-effects model with biological repeat as a random effect), without ROUT outlier clipping.
   * Re-quantify the fixed `mCherry-PARP1-GFP` cytosolic puncta (Figs. 2g,h and 3e,f) using an automated, blinded CellProfiler pipeline.
2. **Revise the nuclear export claims and fix the VE-822 description:**
   * Correct the caption of Extended Data Fig. 6d from "ATM inhibitor VE-822" to "ATR inhibitor VE-822" (vebusertib) and include immunoblot validation of pCHK1 (Ser345) inhibition for VE-822 and CRM1 cargo retention for Leptomycin B.
   * Revise the section heading and text (p. 46080, lines 18–20) from "nuclear pore-independent" to **"CRM1/Exportin-1-independent"** and frame the ATR–lamin A/C nuclear envelope budding model explicitly as a working hypothesis.
3. **Contextualize the SCAN-B survival analysis with multivariable Cox models:** Clarify in the Results and Discussion that SCAN-B TNBC patients received standard adjuvant/neoadjuvant chemotherapy rather than PARPi, and provide a multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression adjusting for age, TNM stage, and chemotherapy treatment to verify that `TEX264` expression is an independent prognostic factor in HRD TNBC.
4. **Deposit RNA-seq data:** Deposit the `CAL51` and `HeLa` RNA-seq FASTQ and count matrices in NCBI GEO/ENA and report the accession number in the Data Availability section.
