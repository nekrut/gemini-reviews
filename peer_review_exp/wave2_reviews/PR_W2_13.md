## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript profiles 1,224 directional pairwise growth interactions (34 responder strains grown across spent media and 1:1 nutrient-replenished spent media from 36 donor strains) in Gifu Anaerobic Medium (GAM) using continuous $\text{OD}_{600}$ monitoring over 48 hours. Using an area-under-the-curve (AUC) threshold of $|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$, the authors identify 307 inhibitory interactions (25.1%) and 51 growth-promoting interactions (4.2%) in unreplenished spent media, with 88% of inhibitory interactions (down to 38, or 3.1%) abolished upon 1:1 nutrient replenishment with 2× GAM. The authors then investigate two specific mechanisms using TMT quantitative proteomics and follow-up physiological assays: (1) *Clostridium perfringens* spent medium promotes *Mediterraneibacter gnavus* growth via a heat-labile, ultracentrifugation-pelletable crude extracellular vesicle (EV) fraction, and (2) media acidification strongly drives growth inhibition across Bacteroidota, Fusobacteriota, and Pseudomonadota ($r = 0.49, p = 10^{-73}$ overall), while *Veillonella parvula* neutralizes acidic media—an effect enhanced by 1 mM guanine supplementation—and rescues the acid-sensitive species *Parabacteroides merdae* in three-species co-cultures with acidifying bacteria.

The experimental screen is broad and the combination of spent-media phenotyping, TMT proteomics, pH neutralization controls, and qPCR-deconvolved three-member synthetic communities is well-designed. However, the study has significant methodological and reporting weaknesses: the "nutrient replenishment" control simultaneously dilutes spent medium 1:1 and alters pH, visual outlier rejection and negative-AUC imputation are subjective, DLS peaks in uninoculated rich medium (149 nm) confound the physical characterization of crude EVs, the guanine stock preparation protocol contains a chemical impossibility (1 mM stock cannot yield 1 mM final concentration after dilution), and there is no Data or Code Availability statement or repository accession for the MS proteomics data.

### Assessment of Specific Conclusions
* **Medium acidification is a primary driver of spent-medium growth inhibition among Bacteroidota: [Well Supported].**  
  Direct pH adjustment of spent media from *B. ovatus*, *B. fragilis*, *B. fragilis* ET, and *P. merdae* back to the pH of fresh GAM (Fig. 3c) reverses growth inhibition without adding nutrients, cleanly separating pH toxicity from resource depletion.
* ***Veillonella parvula* raises extracellular pH in acidic media and rescues *P. merdae* growth in three-member communities, amplified by guanine: [Well Supported].**  
  Demonstrated across initial pH titrations (Fig. 4a,d), sequential spent-media conditioning (Fig. 4b), and qPCR-quantified 3-species communities with three of four acidifiers (Fig. 5b).
* ***C. perfringens* promotes *M. gnavus* growth via extracellular vesicles delivering nucleic acid components: [Overstated].**  
  While the ultracentrifuged pellet from *C. perfringens* spent medium is heat-labile and promotes *M. gnavus* growth (whereas the post-ultracentrifugation supernatant does not; Fig. 2d), (i) direct supplementation with nucleobases, nucleosides, DNA, and RNA completely failed to promote growth (Fig. 2c), and (ii) no TEM, iodixanol density-gradient purification, or nuclease/protease protection assays were performed on the crude 29,000 rpm pellet to distinguish intact membrane vesicles from co-pelleted macromolecular protein complexes or phage tails.
* **Ecological classification of 1,224 spent-medium pairs into competition (-/-), exploitation (-/+), amensalism (-/0), and mutualism (+/+) (Fig. 1f) and correlation with taxonomic relatedness (Fig. 1g): [Fragile].**  
  Calling interaction types solely from fixed $|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$ cutoffs on $n = 2$ replicates without formal statistical testing or propagation of replicate variance (median CV = 8.5%, IQR = 13.6%), and correlating pairwise profiles without controlling for non-independence of shared strains, inflates false discovery and pseudoreplication.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1. Missing Proteomics / Raw Data Depositions and Absence of a Data Availability Statement [Methodologically Flawed]
Unlike standard quantitative biology manuscripts, this manuscript contains **no Data Availability Statement** and **no Code Availability Statement**. Specifically, two major TMT-labeled LC-MS/MS proteomics experiments were conducted (*M. gnavus* in *C. perfringens* spent medium and *V. parvula* in *S. parasanguinis* spent medium; line 103), yet no ProteomeXchange/PRIDE accession number is provided. Similarly, raw 48-hour $\text{OD}_{600}$ time-series curves (sampled every 3 minutes across >1,200 conditions) and analysis scripts are not deposited in any public repository.

### 2.2. Confounding of Nutrient Replenishment with 2-Fold Dilution of Inhibitory Toxins and pH Buffering [Fragile]
In lines 24–26 and 93, the "nutrient replenished spent media" condition was constructed by mixing 50% spent medium with 50% 2× concentrated GAM. The authors observe that inhibitory interactions drop from 307 (25.1%) to 38 (3.1%) and conclude that "most inhibitory interactions are due to depleted resources or changes to the media that limit growth." However, mixing 50% spent medium with 50% 2× GAM also **dilutes any secreted inhibitory metabolite, bacteriocin, or organic acid by 50%** (a 1-log$_2$ reduction, which directly crosses the $|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$ threshold!) and buffers the pH upward via the fresh 2× GAM buffering capacity. Without a 50% spent medium + 50% 1× PBS/saline dilution control (or lyophilized nutrient spike-in without volume dilution), a secreted toxin with a steep dose-response curve near 50% concentration cannot be distinguished from resource depletion.

### 2.3. Uncharacterized Crude Ultracentrifugation Pellet and DLS Artifacts in the EV Assay [Overstated]
In lines 39 and 113–118, the authors isolate "crude extracellular vesicles" by filtering *C. perfringens* spent GAM through $0.2\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ and ultracentrifuging at 29,000 rpm for 4 hours, then characterize particles by Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS). However, the authors note that uninoculated standard rich medium (GAM) itself contains DLS-detectable particles of 149 nm diameter (line 39). Because GAM is an undefined complex medium rich in peptone, yeast extract, digested serum, and meat extract, ultracentrifugation pellets macromolecular aggregates, protein complexes, and medium precipitates alongside any bacterial membrane vesicles. Moreover, DLS intensity scales with the sixth power of particle radius ($I \propto r^6$) and cannot resolve vesicles from protein aggregates. Claiming specifically that membrane vesicles (and vesicle-encapsulated nucleic acids, despite free nucleic acids having zero effect in Fig. 2c) mediate the growth promotion requires either density-gradient purification (e.g., OptiPrep/iodixanol), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), or protease vs. detergent + nuclease sensitivity controls.

### 2.4. Subjective Curve Trimming, Visual Replicate Removal, and Threshold Discretization on $n = 2$ Replicates [Fragile]
In the Methodology (line 98), the authors state:
1. Growth curves were trimmed at "strain-specific time points when they reached stationary phase... (Table S1)." If a spent medium delays lag phase but permits eventual growth, or alters stationary-phase onset relative to fresh GAM, fixed-time trimming conflates lag duration, growth rate, and yield.
2. "All growth curves were then visually inspected, and replicates where the rich medium condition deviated from the overall behavior of that strain in rich medium... were removed." Manual visual exclusion without an objective mathematical criterion (e.g., >3 SD from historical control AUC) introduces observer bias.
3. Interactions were classified using a hard cutoff ($|\log_2\text{FC}| > 1$) on duplicate measurements ("at least in duplicate", line 93) without a $p$-value or confidence interval filter, meaning pairs with $\log_2\text{FC} = -1.01$ and $-0.99$ are placed into qualitatively different ecological categories (competition vs. amensalism vs. neutral in Fig. 1f), and numbers in line 26 are internally inconsistent ("51 interactions where growth was promoted... 27 out of 58 interactions were unchanged").

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Mathematical Impossibility in Guanine Stock Concentration (Line 123):** In "Strain growth at different pH" (line 123), the authors write: "Stocks of guanine (1 mM, prepared by dissolving in 1 M NaOH) and arginine (20 mM, prepared by dissolving in sterile water) were prepared. These stocks were first added to one-third of the required volume of 2x GAM... where two-thirds of 2x GAM was added, and finally diluted to the desired volume with sterile double-distilled water." Yet in line 108 and line 128, the **final** concentration of guanine in the medium is 1 mM! It is physically impossible to dilute a 1 mM stock solution into 2× GAM and water and end up with a 1 mM final concentration; the stock concentration must have been higher (e.g., 10 mM or 50 mM in 1 M NaOH).
2. **Numerical Contradiction in Promoted Interactions (Line 26):** Line 26 first states that "we observed 307 inhibitory interactions (25.1%), and **51** interactions where growth was promoted (4.2%)" and two sentences later states "a large proportion of growth promotion interactions (27 out of **58** interactions) were unchanged even after nutrient replenishment." The total number of growth-promoting interactions cannot simultaneously be 51 and 58.
3. **Conversion of Relative qPCR Gene Copies to Species-Specific $\text{OD}_{600}$ (Line 133):** In the three-member community experiments, the authors calculate each species' abundance as $\text{OD}_{600,\text{species } i} = \text{OD}_{600,\text{total}} \times f_{\text{qPCR}, i}$. Because cell size, geometry, and per-cell optical cross-section at 600 nm differ substantially across *V. parvula* (a tiny coccus, $\sim 0.3\text{--}0.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$), *P. merdae*, and *L. paracasei* / *B. adolescentis*, multiplying bulk $\text{OD}_{600}$ by genomic DNA copy fraction does not yield true species-specific optical density; reporting absolute genome copy numbers per mL (already measured via standard curves!) is much more accurate.
4. **Typographical Errors:** Line 93: "centrifUged" -> "centrifuged", and "0.2 pm membrane filter" -> "0.2 $\mu$m membrane filter". Line 133: "manufacturer^ instructions" -> "manufacturer's instructions".

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

### 1. Deposit Raw Proteomics and Growth Curve Data and Add a Data/Code Availability Section
* **WHY:** Scientific reproducibility requires public access to raw LC-MS/MS spectra, raw 48-hour $\text{OD}_{600}$ growth curves, and analysis scripts.
* **WHAT:** Deposit the TMT proteomics raw files in PRIDE/ProteomeXchange and the raw $\text{OD}_{600}$ time-series tables and R scripts in Zenodo/GitHub.
* **HOW:** Upload the Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 480 `.raw` files and MSFragger outputs to PRIDE, upload the raw plate-reader CSV files and R scripts for AUC integration and `limma` analysis to Zenodo, and add a formal Data and Code Availability section with accession numbers.

### 2. Temper EV Cargo Claims or Perform Protease/Detergent Sensitivity Controls
* **WHY:** A 29,000 rpm ultracentrifugation pellet from complex rich medium (GAM) contains both extracellular vesicles and macromolecular protein/surface-layer complexes, and free nucleic acids failed to stimulate *M. gnavus* growth (Fig. 2c).
* **WHAT:** Reframe the active factor as a "heat-labile, ultracentrifugation-pelletable macromolecular or extracellular vesicle fraction," and if claiming intact membrane vesicles, include a detergent (Triton X-100) + protease/nuclease control or TEM validation.
* **HOW:** Revise the Abstract, Results (lines 35–41), and Discussion to clearly state that free nucleic acids did not phenocopy the effect and that the active moiety in the crude EV pellet is heat-labile (likely proteinaceous or requiring intact vesicles).

### 3. Report Absolute qPCR Copy Numbers per mL Alongside Estimated $\text{OD}_{600}$ in Figure 5
* **WHY:** *V. parvula*, *P. merdae*, and the four acidifiers have very different cell volumes and light-scattering properties per genome copy, making $\text{OD}_{600,\text{total}} \times f_{\text{DNA}}$ an unnecessary distortion of the direct qPCR standard-curve measurements.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Since plasmid standard curves ($10^2\text{--}10^8$ copies) and sample volumes (1 unit of $\text{OD}_{600}$) were recorded for every timepoint (lines 128–133), compute and plot absolute genome equivalents per mL of culture for each species in Figure S2 / Table S10 to verify that the *P. merdae* rescue and *V. parvula* growth trends hold in absolute cell/genome units.

### 4. Correct Internal Numerical Contradictions and Protocol Typos
* **WHY:** Inconsistent counts of promoted interactions (51 vs. 58) and an impossible stock concentration (1 mM guanine stock diluted to 1 mM final) prevent exact replication.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Line 26: Reconcile "51 interactions" vs. "27 out of 58 interactions."
  2. Line 123: Correct the concentration of the guanine stock solution in 1 M NaOH (e.g., 50 mM or 100 mM stock to achieve 1 mM final concentration).
  3. Line 98: Specify the exact quantitative criterion used to exclude aberrant rich-medium control plates and state the total number of excluded plates.
  4. Line 93 & 133: Fix "centrifUged", "0.2 pm" ($\to 0.2\text{ }\mu\text{m}$), and "manufacturer^ instructions".
