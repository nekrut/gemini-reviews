## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript introduces MitoPerturb-Seq, a single-cell multiomic CRISPR screening platform combining CROP-seq, whole-cell 10X Multiome (scRNA-seq + scATAC-seq), TAP-seq gRNA enrichment, and hybrid-capture mtDNA enrichment to interrogate how perturbing 13 candidate nuclear genes affects mitochondrial DNA copy number (mtDNA CN), heteroplasmy, nuclear gene expression, and cell-cycle progression in heteroplasmic `m.5024C>T` (`mt-Ta`) mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs).

* **Claim 1:** Combining whole-cell 10X Multiome with TAP-seq gRNA amplification, NUMT-masked reference alignment, hybrid-capture mtDNA enrichment, and multi-SNV haplotype phasing enables simultaneous single-cell profiling of gRNA identity ($62.7\%$ in Exp 1, $43\%$ in Exp 2), nuclear transcriptome/chromatin accessibility, mtDNA copy number ($\sim 60.6\times$ mean depth across $6,551$ gRNA-assigned cells), and heteroplasmy. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 2:** Knockdown/knockout of `Tfam`, `Opa1`, and `Polg` significantly depletes single-cell mtDNA CN without altering mean `m.5024C>T` heteroplasmy; the observed increase in per-cell heteroplasmy variance in these groups is explained by stochastic sampling across the reduced mtDNA read-depth bottleneck. `[Moderately Supported]`
* **Claim 3:** `Tfam`, `Opa1`, and `Polg` perturbations trigger a shared mitochondrial integrated stress response (mtISR), but `Opa1` loss induces a stronger nuclear transcriptional response and greater mtRNA reduction per unit of mtDNA depletion than `Polg` loss, indicating pleiotropic mtDNA-independent signaling. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 4:** SCENIC regulon inference and ATF4 DamID-seq demonstrate that ATF4 directly binds near the TSS of $\sim 39\%$ of differentially expressed genes (126/325 DEGs), acting as a major—but non-exclusive—regulator alongside other bZIP/AP-1 transcription factors. `[Moderately Supported]`
* **Claim 5:** Severe mtDNA depletion (`Tfam`/`Polg` KO) causes a uniform delay across all cell-cycle phases (preserving phase proportions), whereas OXPHOS dysfunction driven by high mtDNA deletion heteroplasmy under low glucose selectively arrests cells at the G1/S transition; meanwhile, mtDNA replication is completely relaxed across the cell cycle. `[Well Supported]`

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Confounding of Biological Genetic Bottleneck Variance with Technical Read-Sampling Variance
In Fig. 2l–n and Extended Data Fig. 5j–k (lines 126 and p. 92160 lines 25–33), the authors observe that heteroplasmy variance is not significantly elevated in `Tfam`-, `Opa1`-, or `Polg`-KD cells when restricted to cells with combined SNV read depth $\ge 20$ (adjusted $P = 0.87$, Fig. 2l), but becomes highly significant when cells with $< 20$ reads are included (adjusted $P = 3.31 \times 10^{-22}$ for `Tfam`-KD, Fig. 2m). They then perform binomial downsampling of control cells to the read depths of the KD groups (Fig. 2n) and conclude:
> *"Together, these results indicate that increased heteroplasmy variance may be explained by a perturbation-mediated genetic bottleneck through a reduction in mtDNA CN and read depth. Thus... our data indicate that mtDNA CN can be manipulated to modulate the range of heteroplasmy levels within single cells."*

This interpretation conflates two mathematically distinct sources of variance:
1. **Technical sequencing sampling variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{seq}}$):** When $d$ sequencing reads are sampled from a cell with true intracellular heteroplasmy $p_i$, the observed read fraction $\hat{p}_i$ has binomial sampling variance $\text{Var}(\hat{p}_i \mid p_i) = \frac{p_i(1-p_i)}{d}$.
2. **Biological intercellular heteroplasmy variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{bio}}$):** By Kimura's neutral drift / Wright-Fisher vegetative segregation model, a reduction in actual intracellular mtDNA copy number $N_{\text{mtDNA}}$ over $g$ cell divisions increases true cell-to-cell heteroplasmy variance according to $\sigma^2_{\text{bio}} \approx p_0(1-p_0)\left[1 - \left(1 - \frac{1}{N_e}\right)^g\right]$.
3. **Why the simulation proves technical noise, not biological drift:** In the authors' binomial simulation (p. 92160, line 27), control cells were downsampled to the sequencing read depth $d$ of the KD groups (where many cells have $d = 1\text{--}19$ reads). Because $\text{Var}(\hat{p}) = \sigma^2_{\text{bio}} + \mathbb{E}\left[\frac{p_i(1-p_i)}{d}\right]$, and $d \ll N_{\text{mtDNA}}$ (e.g., $d \approx 5\text{--}20$ reads across 7 SNVs vs. $N_{\text{mtDNA}} \approx 300\text{--}1,750$ molecules per cell in Extended Data Fig. 7a), the binomial sampling term $\frac{p(1-p)}{d}$ completely dominates at $d < 20$. Finding that binomial downsampling to read depth $d$ fully accounts for the observed variance ($P = 1.0$, Fig. 2n) actually proves that the apparent variance increase in Fig. 2m is **100% attributable to hyper-noisy low-depth sequencing calls ($d < 20$)**, and that **zero detectable biological increase in true intercellular heteroplasmy variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{bio}}$)** occurred beyond measurement noise! The claim that `Tfam`/`Opa1`/`Polg` KD modulated true intracellular heteroplasmy levels across the biochemical threshold must be corrected to distinguish measurement precision from biological segregation.

### 2.2 Use of Unnormalized mtDNA ATAC Coverage as a Proxy for mtDNA Copy Number
In the Methods (p. 92160, line 7), the authors note:
> *"For mtDNA CN analysis, mtDNA coverage is correlated with total (nuclear + mtDNA) ATAC-seq coverage per cell and others have previously included a normalization step 46. We chose not to include normalization as it would have prevented the cell-cycle analysis in Fig. 5... Normalization could be applied for comparison between perturbation groups (Fig. 2j) but we opted for a homogeneous approach to estimate mtDNA CN across the manuscript."*

Omitting library-size / nuclear-ATAC normalization in the perturbation comparisons (Fig. 2j, Fig. 3b,c,g,i) introduces technical transposition and droplet capture variance directly into the estimated mtDNA CN:
* Even if mean nuclear ATAC fragment counts do not differ significantly across gRNA groups (Extended Data Fig. 5c), individual cells vary by more than an order of magnitude in total nuclear ATAC fragments ($1,000$ to $>50,000$, Extended Data Fig. 1g–i).
* Consequently, in the single-cell linear regression used to adjust nuclear gene expression for mtDNA CN (`expression_i = β0 + β1 * mtDNA_CN + ε`, Fig. 3c,i; p. 92160 line 51), the regressor `mtDNA_CN` (raw mtDNA coverage) is heavily confounded with per-cell ATAC capture efficiency. For cells in G1 phase (or using nuclear ATAC fragment counts adjusted for cell-cycle pseudotime $\pi$), normalizing mtDNA reads to diploid-equivalent nuclear ATAC reads provides a far more rigorous single-cell copy number metric.

### 2.3 Context Mismatch in ATF4 DamID-Seq Validation
To validate SCENIC predictions that ATF4 drives the transcriptional response to mtDNA depletion in `Tfam`-, `Opa1`-, and `Polg`-KO cells, the authors performed DamID-seq in **unperturbed** heteroplasmic MEFs by transiently overexpressing `pCAG-mCherry-P2A-i4Dam-Atf4` driven by the strong constitutive CAG promoter (p. 46080 line 92 and line 106).
* Overexpressing Dam–ATF4 in baseline unperturbed MEFs maps genomic sites accessible to ectopic ATF4 under normal mtDNA copy number, rather than endogenous ATF4 occupancy induced during acute mtDNA depletion or mtISR activation.
* Because bZIP transcription factors (ATF4, CHOP/DDIT3, CEBPG, JUN/FOS) form context-dependent heterodimers with distinct motif specificities depending on stress-induced partner abundance (as reflected by the AP-1 motif ranking #1 and the canonical ATF4–CEBPG motif ranking #11 in Extended Data Fig. 8e), baseline Dam–ATF4 overexpression may overestimate AP-1 cobinding or miss mtISR-specific enhancers that open only upon `Tfam`/`Opa1` loss (which the scATAC-seq modality of the Multiome dataset could directly verify!).

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Under-utilization of the scATAC-seq chromatin accessibility modality:** Although whole-cell 10X Multiome (scRNA-seq + scATAC-seq) was performed on all 11,990 cells, the nuclear scATAC-seq peaks ($11,783$ unique peaks per cell, line 73) are used only for WNN UMAP visualization (Fig. 1d,e) and total nuclear fragment counting across the cell cycle (Extended Data Fig. 10a,b). Running chromVAR / motif accessibility enrichment on the nuclear scATAC-seq data across `Tfam`-, `Opa1`-, and `Polg`-KO cells would directly validate the SCENIC regulon and ATF4 DamID-seq findings in the exact perturbed single cells.
2. **Statistical test mislabeling in Fig. 3b,c caption:** The caption for Fig. 3b,c (line 143) states that *"A two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction for multiple comparisons was used"* to compare nuclear PC1 across perturbation groups (`NT`, `Opa1`, `Polg`, `Tfam`). Because these are unpaired groups of distinct cells with different sample sizes, the test performed must be an unpaired Wilcoxon **rank-sum** (Mann–Whitney $U$) test, not a paired signed-rank test.
3. **Missing public accession numbers for sequencing datasets:** The manuscript text does not provide GEO/SRA/ENA accession numbers for the single-cell Multiome, TAP-seq, bulk RNA-seq, or DamID-seq datasets (redirecting instead to a redacted online content link).

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Deconvolve biological heteroplasmy drift from technical sequencing noise:** Use a beta-binomial hierarchical model (or Moments/Method-of-Moments estimator subtracting expected binomial read-sampling variance $\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \frac{\hat{p}_i(1-\hat{p}_i)}{d_i - 1}$ from total observed variance $S^2_{\hat{p}}$) to estimate the true biological intercellular variance $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{bio}}$ in `NT`, `Tfam`-, `Opa1`-, and `Polg`-KO groups. Revise the text on lines 126–127 to accurately state that the observed variance inflation at low read depths is explained by binomial sequencing sampling noise rather than proving biological heteroplasmy shifts across the disease threshold.
2. **Include nuclear-ATAC-normalized mtDNA CN sensitivity analysis:** Compute a cell-cycle-corrected nuclear ATAC normalization factor (dividing per-cell mtDNA depth by nuclear ATAC fragment count scaled by tricycle pseudotime $\pi$ ploidy expectation) and verify that the perturbation comparisons (Fig. 2j) and mtDNA-CN-adjusted nuclear PC1 regressions (Fig. 3c,i) remain invariant.
3. **Cross-validate ATF4 and AP-1 activity using the nuclear scATAC-seq data:** Run `Signac` / `chromVAR` motif accessibility analysis on the nuclear scATAC-seq peaks in `Tfam`-, `Opa1`-, and `Polg`-KO cells vs. `NT` controls, and intersect differentially accessible chromatin peaks with the ATF4 DamID-seq peaks to confirm stress-induced chromatin remodeling at ATF4/bZIP target loci.
4. **Provide explicit repository accessions and fix statistical nomenclature:** Add the GEO/SRA/ENA accession codes for all sequencing modalities directly in a Data Availability section, and correct "Wilcoxon signed-rank test" to "Wilcoxon rank-sum test" in the Fig. 3b,c legend.
