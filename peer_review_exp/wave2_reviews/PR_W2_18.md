## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This bioinformatics methods paper introduces `SNooPy`, a single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) caller designed for long-read metagenomic datasets where ploidy is unknown, multiple conspecific strains coexist at unequal abundances, and neural-network callers trained on diploid human genomes may underperform. Building on the multi-locus read-correlation strategy of `HairSplitter` and `Strainminer`, `SNooPy` partitions BAM alignments into windows (half the median read length), converts pileups at candidate polymorphic sites ($>5\%$ minor allele frequency) into a binary read-by-locus matrix ($N \times M$), clusters correlated columns via pairwise $\chi^2$ tests ($p < 0.05$, complete linkage), and applies a union-bound combinatorial test: $\binom{N}{n}\binom{M}{m} e^{nm} < 0.001$, where $n$ reads share non-reference bases across $m$ loci and $e$ is set to $3\times$ the empirical read-to-reference divergence. To recover isolated or noisy variants, `SNooPy` adds three rescue steps: a single-locus binomial test ($p < 0.001$ using error rate $e$), a secondary $\chi^2$ correlation rescue ($p < 0.001$), and iterative masking for multi-allelic sites. The authors benchmark `SNooPy` (v0.4.3) against `bcftools`, `Longshot`, `NanoCaller`, `Clair3`, and `DeepVariant` on three real ONT R10.4.1 datasets (Zymobiomics 5-strain *E. coli* mock community, human gut microbiome, and a 921-contig subset of soil microbiome validated against matched PacBio HiFi reads) and `meta-NanoSim`/`Badread` *E. coli* simulations. Code, benchmark scripts, and datasets are publicly archived on GitHub and Zenodo.

`SNooPy` addresses a genuine gap in long-read metagenomics, is fast and open-source, and demonstrates strong recall when multiple linked variants distinguish strains at uneven coverages. However, the manuscript has three important methodological and benchmarking weaknesses: (1) all mathematical symbols and equations in the Materials and Methods were dropped during LaTeX-to-text/XML conversion, leaving blank spaces where formulas should be; (2) the core statistical claim that `SNooPy`'s "only assumption is that sequencing errors occur independently across distinct reads" is violated both biologically (strand-specific systematic basecalling/context errors in ONT and PCR/chimerism) and algorithmically (the "rescue" steps call isolated SNPs using a naive single-site binomial model and unadjusted $\chi^2$ tests); and (3) the benchmark excludes contigs with multi-allelic loci where `DeepVariant` crashed and defines real-data ground-truth recall relative to the union of variants called by the tested tools, creating selection and circularity biases.

### Assessment of Specific Conclusions
* **Multi-locus read-haplotype correlation enables high recall of linked strain variants in multi-strain metagenomes with uneven strain abundance: [Well Supported].**  
  Both the Zymobiomics 5-strain *E. coli* mock community and the controlled *E. coli* mixture simulations (Fig. 2 left, Fig. 3i, iv) clearly show that diploid-assuming callers (`Longshot`, `Clair3`, `NanoCaller`) lose sensitivity when $>2$ strains or low-frequency strains ($<15\%$) are present, whereas `SNooPy` retains high recall.
* **`SNooPy`'s statistical test makes no assumptions on sequencing error profiles and relies *only* on independent errors across distinct reads: [Overstated].**  
  In reality, the combinatorial test $\binom{N}{n}\binom{M}{m} e^{nm} < 0.001$ assumes that errors are independent **both across reads ($n$) and across loci ($m$)** with a uniform upper-bound error probability $e = 3 \times \text{divergence}$, and more importantly, the **isolated-SNP binomial rescue step** (line 99) calls any single locus where the alternative allele count exceeds $\text{Binomial}(c, e)$ at $p < 0.001$—which is a standard single-site independent-error model! Furthermore, because $e$ is set to $3 \times$ the *read-to-reference divergence* (which includes true biological SNPs between abundant divergent strains and the consensus assembly!), high strain divergence inflates $e$ above the true sequencing error rate, which can suppress recall of rare strains or exceed $e = 1$ if divergence $> 33.3\%$.
* **`SNooPy` outperforms `DeepVariant` on complex real metagenomes (gut and soil): [Moderately Supported].**  
  While `SNooPy` achieves higher recall on the gut and soil datasets (Fig. 2) and low-coverage/high-strain-count simulations (Fig. 3), two benchmark choices favor `SNooPy`: (i) all contigs where `DeepVariant` v1.9.0 crashed on multi-allelic sites were excluded, and (ii) on the gut and soil datasets, the "ground truth" denominator for recall is the union of HiFi-confirmed calls made by the tested tools (rather than all variants present in an independent HiFi variant callset), which rewards the tool that makes the largest number of candidate calls.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1. Conflation of True Strain Divergence with Sequencing Error Rate in Parameter $e$ (Lines 79, 87) [Methodologically Flawed]
In Materials and Methods (line 79) and the Figure 1 caption (line 87), the authors define the error rate upper bound $e$ as:
> "The error rate is estimated as the divergence between the reads and the reference. To account for error-prone regions such as homopolymers and ensure that our error rate parameter always remains higher than the actual local error rate, we empirically set [ $e$ ] as three time the measured error rate. Here, there are 31 non-reference bases out of 336, hence [ $e = 3 \times 31/336 = 0.277$ ]."

Notice the flaw in this estimator: in the Figure 1 toy example (and in real multi-strain metagenomic pileups), the 31 non-reference bases out of 336 total bases include the **true biological SNPs** carried by the non-reference strains! In a window where two equally abundant strains differ by 5% true nucleotide divergence and ONT R10.4.1 has a 1% sequencing error rate, the observed divergence is $\sim 3.5\%$, so $e$ is set to $3 \times 3.5\% = 10.5\%$ ($10\times$ the true sequencing error rate). In the single-SNP binomial rescue test ($\text{Binomial}(c, e)$ at $p < 0.001$, line 99), setting $e = 0.105$ or $e = 0.277$ makes it impossible to rescue true isolated SNPs from strains below 15–35% abundance! Conversely, in a conserved region with 0% true divergence and a 0.5% ONT R10.4.1 mean error rate ($e = 1.5\%$), a systematic context-specific basecalling error or homopolymer indel alignment artifact occurring in 6% of reads at a single site ($>5\%$ MAF filter) will easily pass $\text{Binomial}(c = 100, e = 0.015)$ at $p < 0.001$ ($\text{Pr}(X \ge 6) = 3.8 \times 10^{-4} < 0.001$), generating false positives.

### 2.2. Uncorrected Multiple Testing and Data Dredging in the Combinatorial and Rescue Tests (Lines 71–104) [Fragile]
The variant-calling pipeline applies three sequential hypothesis tests without coherent family-wise error control:
1. **Column clustering + submatrix selection:** Within each window, all pairs of columns with $>5\%$ non-reference bases are tested by $\chi^2$ ($p < 0.05$), clustered by complete linkage, and then $n$ reads and $m$ loci are selected where all bases are non-reference. The union bound $\binom{N}{n}\binom{M}{m} e^{nm} < 0.001$ bounds the probability of *some* $n \times m$ all-1 submatrix existing under i.i.d. Bernoulli($e$) entries, **assuming $e$ is the marginal probability of a 1 in the filtered $N \times M$ matrix**. However, because the $M$ columns were **pre-filtered to only include columns with $>5\%$ non-reference bases** (line 79: "we only include loci where alternative alleles appear in $>5\%$ of reads. This filtering substantially reduces the matrix dimensions, accelerating computations while strengthening the statistical test through a reduced value of $M$"), conditioning on $\text{MAF} > 5\%$ violates the unconditional $\text{Bernoulli}(e)$ marginal distribution whenever $e < 0.05$! Specifically, if $e = 0.015$ and a column is only included in the $M$ columns if it already has $\ge 5\%$ errors, the conditional probability of an error in that selected column is $\ge 0.05 \gg e$, so using $\binom{M}{m} e^{nm}$ with the post-selection $M$ underestimates the null probability by orders of magnitude.
2. **Rescue phase $\chi^2$ testing (line 103):** In the recovery phase, all uncalled loci are tested for correlation with confirmed SNPs using $\chi^2$ tests at $p < 0.001$ without Bonferroni/BH correction for the number of candidate loci $\times$ number of confirmed SNP groups tested.

### 2.3. Missing Mathematical Equations and Symbols Throughout the Text (Lines 71–103, 162) [Methodologically Flawed]
Due to a typesetting/conversion error in the manuscript file, **every inline mathematical symbol and equation** in lines 71–103 and 162 is missing (rendered as empty whitespace before punctuation or between words, e.g., `into a binary matrix , where rows represent`, `Let be the number of reads and the number of loci... among a matrix totalling reads and loci. Let denote an upper bound`, `under our null hypothesis: . We reject the null hypothesis when .`). A statistical methods paper cannot be published or replicated from text when its core probability formulas are blank.

### 2.4. Benchmark Circularity in Real-Data Recall Definition and Omission of Metagenomic Callers [Moderately Supported]
1. On the human gut and soil datasets (line 137), recall is defined as "the proportion of HiFi-confirmed SNPs that each software successfully identified, relative to the total pool of HiFi-confirmed SNPs called by any software." Why not call variants directly on the PacBio HiFi alignments (or assemble the HiFi reads into strain haplotypes) to establish a tool-independent ground truth? Defining the denominator as the union of the tested ONT callers biases recall toward whichever caller outputs the largest set of low-frequency candidates.
2. While the authors note that no *long-read* metagenomic SNP caller previously existed, short-read/pileup metagenomic callers (`lofreq`, `freebayes --pooled-continuous`, `inStrain`, `VarScan2`) are widely applied to long-read BAM files in metagenomics when ploidy-free calling is needed; comparing only against diploid callers (`bcftools call -m` default ploidy=2, `Longshot`, `Clair3`, `NanoCaller`, `DeepVariant`) omits the pooled/haploid frequency callers actually used in practice.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Duplicate Sentence in Simulation Methods (Line 128):** Line 128 contains a copy-paste duplication: "The experiments are detailed in Supplementary table S1 . using Badreads [ 24 ] with the error model 'nanopore2023', varying the number of strains, coverage, and error rate of the reads, as detailed in Supplementary Table S1 ."
2. **Coordinate-Based Read Clustering Threshold (Line 112):** Line 112 states that within each window (of length = $0.5 \times \text{median read length}$), "weonly consider reads that span the full window" (line 110), and then immediately in line 112 states "Reads frequently map to only partial segments of the reference genome... we cluster reads within each window based on their mapping coordinates. Groups of at least five reads sharing exactly the same mapping coordinates are grouped together." These two paragraphs contradict each other: if all reads spanning the full window are clipped to the window boundaries, they all share the exact same window coordinates; if partial reads terminating inside the window are included, their ragged alignment clip-points on ONT reads will rarely match to the *exact* base pair across $\ge 5$ reads unless soft-clipping coordinates are binned within a tolerance window (e.g., $\pm 20\text{ bp}$).

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

### 1. Restore All Mathematical Equations and Correct the Post-Filtering Union Bound
* **WHY:** All inline mathematical symbols ($M, N, m, n, e$, binomial coefficients, and probability inequalities) are missing from lines 71–103, and reducing $M$ to only loci with $>5\%$ alternative allele frequency invalidates substituting the unconditional error rate $e$ into $\binom{M}{m}\binom{N}{n}e^{nm}$.
* **WHAT:** Restore all LaTeX mathematical expressions in the text, and either (a) use the total window length $M_{\text{total}}$ in the union bound $\binom{M_{\text{total}}}{m}\binom{N}{n}e^{nm}$, or (b) replace $e^{nm}$ with the conditional probability of observing $n$ errors in a column given that the column already has $\ge 0.05 N$ errors.
* **HOW:** When a column is pre-selected because its error count $K_j \ge k_0 = \lceil 0.05 N \rceil$, the conditional probability that a specific subset of $n$ reads all have errors in that column is bounded by $\binom{N}{n}^{-1} \text{E}\left[\binom{K_j}{n} \;\middle|\; K_j \ge k_0\right]$ (or simply use $M_{\text{total}}$, the total number of bases in the window prior to the $5\%$ filter, in $\binom{M_{\text{total}}}{m}\binom{N}{n}e^{nm}$, which is mathematically valid without conditioning bias).

### 2. Decouple Sequencing Error Rate Estimation ($e$) from Biological Strain Divergence
* **WHY:** Estimating $e$ as $3 \times$ the total read-to-reference mismatch rate conflates true biological polymorphism in multi-strain windows with sequencing error, inflating $e$ in polymorphic regions and suppressing sensitivity in the binomial rescue step.
* **WHAT:** Estimate the sequencing error rate from singleton mismatches or base quality scores rather than total read-to-reference divergence.
* **HOW:** Compute the empirical mismatch rate specifically across sites with minor allele frequency $< 2\%$ (or singleton mismatches divided by coverage), which reflects sequencing error rather than common strain polymorphisms, or cap $e$ using the platform/basecaller Phred quality distribution.

### 3. Add a Ploidy-Free / Pooled Variant Caller Baseline (`LoFreq` or `FreeBayes --pooled-continuous`) and Tool-Independent HiFi Truth Set
* **WHY:** Comparing `SNooPy` exclusively against diploid-configured callers leaves open whether a standard pooled-frequency caller (`LoFreq` or `FreeBayes --pooled-continuous`) achieves comparable recall on metagenomic BAMs, and defining real-data recall against the union of ONT calls introduces circularity.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Run `LoFreq` (or `FreeBayes --pooled-continuous -F 0.05`) on the three real datasets and simulated mixtures and add its Precision/Recall curves to Figures 2 and 3.
  2. For the gut and soil datasets, define an independent ground-truth SNP set by calling variants directly on the high-accuracy PacBio HiFi BAMs (requiring $\ge 3$ supporting HiFi reads and $\ge 5\%$ allele frequency at sites with $\ge 10\times$ ONT coverage) and report recall against this tool-independent HiFi truth set.
  3. Clarify the apparent contradiction between line 110 ("only consider reads that span the full window") and line 112 ("Groups of at least five reads sharing exactly the same mapping coordinates"), and remove the duplicated sentence on line 128.
