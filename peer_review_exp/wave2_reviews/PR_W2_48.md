## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study evaluates whether the Greenland shark (*Somniosus microcephalus*)—the longest-living vertebrate, inhabiting dim Arctic waters and frequently parasitized by the corneal copepod *Ommatokoita elongata*—possesses a vestigial/degenerated or functional visual system. Integrating retinal histology ($n=3$), chromatin immunostaining (H3K27ac/H4K20me3; $n=1$), TUNEL apoptosis staining ($n=1$), RNAscope in situ hybridization ($n=1$), LC-MS fatty acid profiling ($n=2$ sharks vs. $n=5$ cattle), in vitro HEK293S rhodopsin regeneration and UV-Vis spectrophotometry, corneal transmittance measurements ($n=6$ corneas), short-read whole-genome sequencing (`ABySS`), and bulk retinal RNA-seq ($n=3$) compared against five other elasmobranchs, the authors report that the Greenland shark possesses an intact, pure-rod retina with densely packed photoreceptors, no detectable TUNEL apoptosis, high DHA (41%) and 36-carbon VLC-PUFAs (45%), a blue-shifted rod opsin ($\lambda_{\max} = 458\text{ nm}$), intact and actively transcribed rod phototransduction genes, and pseudogenized cone phototransduction genes (*rh2*, *gnat2*, *pde6c*, *cnga3*, *cngb3*) and melanopsin (*opn4*). Raw sequences (`PRJNA1246101`, `PV442159–PV442194`) and supplemental data (`Figshare`) are publicly deposited.

**Editorial Verdict: Moderate-to-Major Revision Required (Mixed / Salvageable).** The primary conclusion—that adult Greenland sharks over a century old maintain an anatomically intact, pure-rod scotopic retina with a deep-sea blue-shifted rhodopsin ($\lambda_{\max} = 458\text{ nm}$) and high VLC-PUFA membrane content—is well supported by the histological, spectrophotometric, and lipidomic data. However, several genomic, transcriptomic, and optical methods suffer from substantial technical flaws that undermine the secondary claims regarding cross-species gene expression levels, DNA repair gene loss/upregulation (*ercc1*/*ercc4*), and selection tests: (1) Greenland shark "TPM" values in Figure 4 were not computed from a transcriptome-wide annotation, but were **extrapolated via linear regression** from raw read proportions after mapping Ribo-Zero total RNA reads *exclusively* against the ~35 mined CDS sequences at a relaxed 40% read-length match threshold (`--outFilterMatchNminOverLread 0.4`); (2) assembling a giant (~7–10 Gbp) repetitive elasmobranch genome from 150 bp insert short reads on a single NovaSeq SP flow cell with `ABySS` and completing fragmented CDSs by `HISAT2` mapping to heterospecific references confounds draft assembly dropout with gene loss (e.g., claiming shorter-lived sharks "lack *ercc1*") and risks introducing chimeric/consensus errors into `PAML`/`HyPhy` $dN/dS$ tests; (3) the RNAscope methods state that *"Probes specific for mouse and human transcripts were designed by the manufacturer"*; and (4) corneal transmittance (up to "100%") was measured on PFA-fixed 5-mm punches taken away from the peripheral copepod attachment site.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1 (Adult Greenland sharks over a century old retain an anatomically intact, pure-rod retina with all canonical nuclear layers, active chromatin marks, and no widespread photoreceptor apoptosis):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Demonstrated by resin-embedded 1-$\mu\text{m}$ toluidine blue histology ($n=3$; Fig. 1b, Figs. S45–S46), H3K27ac/H4K20me3 immunofluorescence ($n=1$; Fig. 2a–c), and negative TUNEL staining validated against a positive DNase I control ($n=1$; Fig. 2e).
* **Claim 2 (Greenland shark rhodopsin [`RH1`] is functionally intact and exhibits a pronounced short-wavelength shift [$\lambda_{\max} = 458\text{ nm}$] matched to deep Arctic blue light, supported by high retinal DHA [41%] and C36 VLC-PUFAs [45%]):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Directly measured via in vitro opsin expression in HEK293S cells, 11-*cis*-retinal reconstitution, 1D4 immunoaffinity purification, and dark-minus-bleached difference spectrophotometry (Fig. 3a–b), alongside LC-MS lipidomics (Fig. 2f).
* **Claim 3 (The Greenland shark genome retains a complete rod phototransduction cascade while cone opsins/phototransduction genes [*rh2, gnat2, pde6c, cnga3, cngb3*] and melanopsin [*opn4*] are pseudogenized):** `[Well Supported]`  
  The presence of premature stop codons and frameshifts across five independent cone phototransduction genes and *opn4*—while all 13 rod phototransduction genes remain intact and expressed in retinal RNA-seq—provides strong convergent evidence of cone pathway pseudogenization, consistent with pure-rod histology. (However, claiming *arrc* is evolutionarily absent cannot be distinguished from short-read assembly drop-out).
* **Claim 4 (Rod phototransduction genes are under both relaxed [*rh1*] and diversifying [*gnat1*, *sag*] selection in the Greenland shark):** `[Fragile]`  
  In Methods (Part 1 lines 113, 122), fragmented CDSs in the short-read `ABySS` assembly were completed by mapping reads to heterospecific reference sequences with `HISAT2` and extracting a consensus, and pseudogene sequences had stop codons/frameshifts manually stripped before protein alignment and `trimAl` codon back-translation. Any residual frame-shifted codons or heterospecific reference-guided consensus errors on the Greenland shark terminal branch will inflate terminal-branch $\omega$ in `aBSREL` and `PAML`. Furthermore, claiming in line 122 that the parameter-rich free-ratio model (`Model 1`) was *"always the best model for all genes"* appears to rely on raw log-likelihoods without penalizing degrees of freedom.
* **Claim 5 (Long-lived sharks selectively retain *ercc1* while shorter-lived sharks lack *ercc1*, and the Greenland shark upregulates *ercc4* [XPF] to maintain retinal integrity over centuries):** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  *ERCC1* is a core eukaryotic nucleotide excision repair gene; its apparent "absence" in draft genomes of comparison sharks (two of which have BUSCO completeness of only 75.6%–76.0%, and one of which—the whale shark *Rhincodon typus*—is itself a famous long-lived species living ~70–130 years!) is a classic artifact of incomplete genome assembly/annotation rather than true evolutionary gene loss. Moreover, the apparent elevation of *ercc4* "TPM" in Greenland shark (Fig. 4d) relies on an invalid cross-species TPM extrapolation from Ribo-Zero libraries mapped solely to ~35 mined CDSs.
* **Claim 6 (Parasitized Greenland shark corneas maintain 70–100% light transmittance):** `[Overstated]`  
  Measurements were taken on 5-mm punches of PFA-fixed corneas where parasites were attached only to the corneal edges (Fig. S47), avoiding both the opaque parasite body/egg sacs that hang across the visual axis in vivo and the focal attachment scar. Furthermore, a transmittance of "100%" through biological tissue across 425–600 nm exceeds the physical Fresnel reflection limit (~94–96%) and human cornea controls, indicating reference-blank calibration drift or scattering into the integrating sphere.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Invalid Cross-Species "TPM Extrapolation" and Target-Restricted `STAR` Mapping (Pillars 3, 4, 5)
* **Mapping Whole-Transcriptome Reads Exclusively to ~35 Mined CDSs:** In Methods (Part 2, lines 28–30), for the five comparison species, reads were mapped to whole annotated genomes with `STAR` (`--outFilterMatchNminOverLread 0.4`, requiring only a 40% read-length match) to compute standard TPMs. For the Greenland shark, however, *"an annotation file was manually generated for all genes that were mined, and this was used to guide mapping against those sequences using STAR... The equations generated using the other five shark species were used to extrapolate TPM values from the proportion of mapped reads for each gene expressed in the Greenland shark."*
* **Three Compounding Biases in This Pipeline:**
  1. **Competitive Mapping Absence (Decoy Bias):** Mapping total retinal RNA-seq reads only against ~35 target CDS sequences (without the rest of the genome/transcriptome as background decoys) and allowing a permissive 40% match length (`40 bp` out of `100 bp` PE reads) forces reads from paralogous gene families across the unannotated genome to map onto those 35 targets.
  2. **Ribo-Zero Total RNA vs. Poly-A mRNA Denominator Mismatch:** The Greenland shark libraries were prepared with the **Illumina TruSeq Stranded Total RNA Ribo-Zero Gold kit** (which uses mammalian rRNA depletion probes that inefficiently deplete elasmobranch rRNA and retain intronic pre-mRNA), whereas public comparison shark transcriptomes are poly-A mRNA libraries. Regressing TPM against `mapped_reads / total_raw_reads` across poly-A libraries and applying that linear regression to Ribo-Zero libraries violates the assumption that total raw reads contain an identical fraction of mature coding mRNA.
  3. **Solution:** The authors sequenced three deep RNA-seq libraries (`100 bp PE` on a NovaSeq 6000 S4 flow cell) for the Greenland shark. A standard *de novo* transcriptome assembly (e.g., `Trinity` or `rCorrector` + `StringTie` on the draft genome) allows direct, mathematically valid `Salmon`/`kallisto` TPM quantification across the entire Greenland shark retinal transcriptome without ad-hoc linear regression extrapolation!

### 3.2 Short-Read Draft Genome Fragmentation and Reference-Guided Consensus Artifacts (Pillars 2, 3)
* **150 bp Insert Size `ABySS` Assembly of a Giant Chondrichthyan Genome:** Methods (Part 1, line 101) states that genomic libraries were prepared with a **150 bp insert size** and sequenced as 150 bp PE reads on a NovaSeq 6000 SP flow cell, then assembled with `ABySS v2.3.1` ($k = 80$). Because a 150 bp insert with 150 bp paired-end reads results in completely overlapping read pairs (effectively 150 bp single-end reads that cannot bridge repeats $>150\text{ bp}$) for a repeat-rich ~7–10 Gbp squaliform shark genome, the resulting assembly is highly fragmented (assembly N50 and BUSCO score are relegated to Table S3 and not disclosed in the main text).
* **`HISAT2` Consensus Extraction from Heterospecific References:** When `TBLASTN`/`EXONERATE` failed or yielded fragmented exons, the authors mapped raw Greenland shark genomic reads to *other* shark reference CDSs using `HISAT2` (an RNA splice-aware aligner!) and extracted the consensus in `Geneious Prime` (line 113). Using a heterospecific reference-guided consensus for downstream `PAML`/`RELAX`/`aBSREL` $dN/dS$ analysis is hazardous: any unmapped divergent codon or paralogous read pileup alters nonsynonymous sites on the Greenland shark terminal branch.

### 3.3 Copy-Paste Error in RNAscope Probe Species and Figure Cross-Reference Typos (Pillar 7)
* **Mouse/Human RNAscope Probes Stated in Methods:** In Methods (`Fluorescence in situ hybridization`, Part 2 line 3), the text states: *"Probes specific for **mouse and human** transcripts were designed by the manufacturer (see Table S5)."* If mammalian probes were used on an elasmobranch separated by ~450 Myr of evolution, sequence divergence would prevent specific dual-Z hybridization; if custom *S. microcephalus* probes were designed, this sentence is a copy-paste error from a mammalian study that must be corrected. Additionally, no negative control probe (e.g., `dapB`) is mentioned to rule out lipofuscin autofluorescence in $>100$-year-old retinal tissue.
* **Broken Figure Cross-References in Results:**
  * Line 49 twice cites **"Fig. 2e"** (which is the TUNEL assay) when discussing LC-MS DHA and C36 VLC-PUFA percentages, which are actually shown in **Fig. 2f**.
  * Line 69 cites **"Fig. 3d"** (which is corneal transmittance) when discussing *ercc1* and *ercc4* retinal gene expression, which is actually shown in **Fig. 4d**.

### Actionable Remediation Plan
1. **Replace Linear-Regression "TPM Extrapolation" with Standard De Novo Transcriptome Quantification:** Assemble the three Greenland shark retinal RNA-seq libraries *de novo* using `Trinity` (or `rnaviralSPAdes`/`rnaSPAdes`), remove rRNA reads with `SortMeRNA`, and compute genuine transcriptome-wide TPMs across all shark species using `Salmon` or `kallisto` (or housekeeping-gene-normalized $\Delta\text{TPM}$ / rank percentiles to account for Ribo-Zero vs. poly-A library differences).
2. **Verify *ercc1* Presence in Unannotated Genomes and Audit `HISAT2`-Completed CDSs in Selection Tests:** Run `TBLASTN` / `miniprot` searches of the raw genome assemblies AND raw retinal RNA-seq reads of *S. canicula*, *R. typus*, and *C. plagiosum* before claiming these species evolutionarily lack *ercc1*. In the `PAML`/`RELAX`/`aBSREL` analyses, exclude pseudogenes from coding alignments, restrict Greenland shark sequences strictly to contiguous `ABySS` or `Trinity` de novo exons (excluding heterospecific `HISAT2`-guided consensus segments), and apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across all tested genes.
3. **Fix Methods and Figure Cross-Reference Errors:**
   * Correct `"Probes specific for mouse and human transcripts"` in Methods (Part 2 line 3) to reflect the actual *S. microcephalus* RNAscope probe targets, and state how lipofuscin autofluorescence was controlled.
   * Report the `ABySS` draft genome size, scaffold N50, and BUSCO completeness score directly in the main text (line 36 / line 101).
   * Fix the broken figure citations in line 49 (`Fig. 2e` $\to$ `Fig. 2f`) and line 69 (`Fig. 3d` $\to$ `Fig. 4d`), and explain how corneal transmittance reached `100%` in Figure 3d.
