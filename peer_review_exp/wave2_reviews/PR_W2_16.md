## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript reanalyzes an *in vivo* 5'-ethynyl uridine (EU) RNA-tagging dataset (from a previously published adoptive transfer of EU-labeled NOD BDC2.5 $\text{CD4}^+\text{CD25}^-$ T cells into NOD.SCID mice; ref. [21]) to identify small intron-derived RNAs (sinRNAs) transferred from infiltrating T lymphocytes to pancreatic $\beta$-cells. The authors focus on two candidates: `sinR-D` (`sinR-D1/D2/D3`, sharing a 16-nt core mapped to an intronic enhancer of *Dnaaf1*) and `sinR-T` (24 nt, mapped to an intron of *Ttll11*). They report that `sinR-D3` and `sinR-T` are elevated in islets of 8-week-old prediabetic NOD mice (vs. 4-week-old NOD mice, with no change in age-matched NOD.SCID mice), are unchanged by 24 h exposure to proinflammatory cytokines (IL-1$\beta$, TNF-$\alpha$, IFN-$\gamma$), and are present in activated T cells and their ultracentrifuged extracellular vesicles (EVs). Incubation of mouse or human islet cells with T-cell EVs ($1\times 10^{11}\text{/mL}$) increases intracellular `sinR-D3` levels. Transfection of synthetic `sinR-D3` mimics into dispersed mouse islet cells increases cleaved caspase-3+ $\beta$-cell apoptosis without altering insulin content or glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. Finally, biotinylated `sinR-D3` pull-downs and FLAG-Ago2 RIP-qPCR in MIN6B1 cells detect an interaction with Ago2, and DIA-PASEF proteomics of islets co-transfected with `sinR-D2/D3` mimics identifies nominally altered proteins ($p < 0.05$). Proteomics data are deposited at ProteomeXchange (`PXD065257`), whereas all other data are listed as "available upon request."

While the concept of immune-to-$\beta$-cell small RNA transfer is interesting, the manuscript suffers from multiple methodological flaws: (1) a confounded control in the *in vivo* EU-tagging experiment (saline injection rather than unlabeled T cells or EU-labeled non-infiltrating cells, plus potential free EU carry-over), (2) invalid one-sample $t$-tests on fold-change-normalized data, (3) unadjusted $p < 0.05$ thresholding across thousands of proteins in the DIA proteomics volcano plot with left-censored missing-value imputation, (4) supraphysiological Lipofectamine mimic transfections without loss-of-function (antagomir/inhibitor) validation in EV-treated islets, and (5) an cross-species sequence conservation oversight in the human EV transfer experiment.

### Assessment of Specific Conclusions
* **`sinR-D3` and `sinR-T` levels are elevated in 8-week-old insulitic NOD islets and in activated $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells/EVs, but are not induced in $\beta$-cells by IL-1$\beta$/TNF-$\alpha$/IFN-$\gamma$: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The comparisons across 4w vs. 8w NOD and NOD.SCID islets and cytokine-treated C57BL/6 islets (Fig. 2, S3) are consistent with an immune-cell origin or non-cytokine-driven accumulation, though infiltrating T cells present within whole 8-week NOD islets (unlike FACS-sorted $\beta$-cells in Fig. 1) directly contribute T-cell RNA to the whole-islet RNA extract in Fig. 2A.
* **`sinR-D` and `sinR-T` are directly packaged into EVs by infiltrating $\text{CD4}^+$ T cells and transferred to $\beta$-cells *in vivo* to initiate T1D: [Overstated].**  
  In the *in vivo* experiment (Fig. 1A), T cells were incubated with $200\text{ }\mu\text{M}$ EU *in vitro* and injected IV into NOD.SCID mice, with **saline injection** as the negative control. First, adoptive transfer of BDC2.5 T cells vs. saline does not control for free or degraded EU nucleotides released by dying T cells *in vivo* and salvaged by host $\beta$-cells during nascent transcription; second, the *in vivo* experiment did not block EV release or purify EVs *in vivo*; third, no loss-of-function inhibition of `sinR-D` was performed *in vivo* or in EV-treated islets to show that endogenous EV-delivered `sinR-D` is causal for $\beta$-cell death.
* **Human T-cell EVs transfer `sinR-D3` to human islet cells (Fig. 3D): [Fragile].**  
  `sinR-D1/D2/D3` is defined as a mouse intronic sequence from the mouse *Dnaaf1* intron (`mm10`, line 109, 126). Intronic sequences are poorly conserved between mouse and human, yet the authors use the mouse `sinR-D3` qRT-PCR assay on human islets treated with human donor T-cell EVs (Fig. 3D) without demonstrating whether the exact 16-nt core sequence exists in the human *DNAAF1* intron or human genome (`GRCh38`).
* **Overexpression of `sinR-D2/D3` regulates specific pro- and anti-apoptotic target proteins (`RBL2`, `OIT3`, `ITGB1BP1`, `SUPT3`, `CA4`, `LZTR1`, `ENDOV`, `CTDSPL2`, `ST3GAL5`) via Ago2 to drive $\beta$-cell apoptosis: [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The DIA-PASEF proteomics analysis (Fig. 5C) uses an **unadjusted $p < 0.05$** threshold across the entire proteome without Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction, combines this with deterministic down-shifted normal imputation (1.8 SD shift) that creates artificial fold changes for missing values, and does not test whether any of the highlighted genes contain sequence complementarity (seed matches) to `sinR-D2/D3` or whether they are merely secondary consequences of lipofection-induced apoptosis.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1. Uncorrected Multiple Hypothesis Testing ($p < 0.05$) and Imputation Bias in Proteomics (Fig. 5C) [Methodologically Flawed]
In Section 2.12 (lines 114–116) and Figure 5C (line 166), the authors quantify the mouse islet proteome by DIA-PASEF mass spectrometry (searching 54,739 UniProt sequences), impute missing values from a normal distribution down-shifted by 1.8 SD (width 0.3 SD), and call proteins "significantly upregulated" or "significantly downregulated" at **unadjusted $p < 0.05$** (Fig. 5C caption: "Significantly upregulated proteins are shown in red, and significantly downregulated proteins are shown in blue ($p < 0.05$)"). When testing ~3,000–6,000 quantified protein groups, an unadjusted $p < 0.05$ threshold produces **150–300 false positives by chance alone** under the global null hypothesis. Furthermore, no seed-match / target-prediction analysis or 3'UTR luciferase reporter assay was performed to link any of the 9 highlighted proteins (`RBL2`, `OIT3`, `ITGB1BP1`, `SUPT3`, `CA4`, `LZTR1`, `ENDOV`, `CTDSPL2`, `ST3GAL5`) to direct Ago2-`sinR-D` binding.

### 2.2. Confounding of Whole-Islet Immune Infiltration with $\beta$-Cell Transfer in Figure 2A [Methodologically Flawed]
In Section 3 (line 134, Fig. 2A), to test whether `sinR-D3` and `sinR-T` increase in $\beta$-cells during natural prediabetes, the authors compare RNA extracted from **whole islets** of 4-week-old NOD mice (devoid of insulitis) vs. 8-week-old NOD mice ("where immune cells have begun to invade the islets"). Because 8-week-old NOD islets contain a massive infiltrate of $\text{CD4}^+$ and $\text{CD8}^+$ T lymphocytes (which themselves express high levels of `sinR-D3` and `sinR-T`, Fig. 3A–B) whereas 4-week-old NOD islets and 8-week-old NOD.SCID islets contain no lymphocytes, measuring RNA in unsorted whole islets cannot distinguish sinRNA accumulation inside $\beta$-cells from the physical presence of infiltrating T cells inside the islet preparation! Additionally, normalizing whole-islet sinRNAs to the $\beta$-cell-enriched microRNA `miR-184` (which is known to be strongly downregulated in 8-week NOD islets during insulitis!) artificially inflates the apparent fold-change of any RNA normalized against it.

### 2.3. Invalid Statistical Testing on Control-Normalized Fold-Change Data (Section 2.13) [Methodologically Flawed]
In Section 2.13 (line 121), the authors state: "A two-tailed one-sample t-test was conducted to compare the datasets against a control value set at 1. Unpaired t-tests were used for comparing two datasets. One-way Anova followed by Dunnett post hoc test was used for multiple dataset comparison."
1. When experimental replicates are normalized by dividing each treated sample by its paired control (setting the control to 1.0 with zero variance), running a one-way ANOVA + Dunnett test or unpaired $t$-test against a zero-variance column of 1s violates homoscedasticity and inflates Type I error.
2. Conversely, running a one-sample $t$-test of fold-changes ($T_i / C_i$) against $1.0$ is asymmetrical and positively biased because the ratio of two noisy positive random variables has $\text{E}[T/C] > \text{E}[T]/\text{E}[C]$ by Jensen's inequality; paired tests must be conducted on $\Delta C_t$ (log-scale) values before exponentiation.

### 2.4. Absence of Loss-of-Function Validation and Reliance on Supraphysiological Mimic Lipofection [Overstated]
In Figure 4A, $\beta$-cell apoptosis is only demonstrated by transfecting synthetic RNA mimics with Lipofectamine 2000 (which typically increases intracellular small RNA copy numbers by $10^3$–$10^5$-fold and delivers RNA into endolysosomal compartments where TLR7/8 innate immune sensors reside), whereas EV treatment increases `sinR-D3` by only ~1.5–2-fold (Fig. 3C–D). Without showing that an antisense inhibitor (antagomir) against `sinR-D3` reduces the apoptosis induced by T-cell EVs, the causal claim in the Abstract ("suggests that the transfer of sinR-D contributes to $\beta$-cell destruction and the onset of type 1 diabetes") is unsubstantiated.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Inconsistent Reference Gene Normalization Across qRT-PCR Experiments:** The authors switch reference genes across figures without justification: `C. elegans miR-238` spike-in in Fig. 1B, `Let7a` in Fig. 1C, **2B, 3B, 3C, 3D**, `miR-184` in **Fig. 2A**, and raw $2^{-C_t}$ without a reference gene in **Fig. 3A**. As noted above, `miR-184` is suppressed in prediabetic NOD islets, making its use exclusively in Fig. 2A problematic.
2. **Host Gene Name Typo in Discussion:** Line 181 writes "`sinR-T` is derived from *Ttll1*", whereas line 126 (Results) states "`sinR-T`... maps to the intronic region of the *Ttll11* gene."
3. **Western Blot Secondary Antibody Discrepancy (Section 2.10, Line 104):** Line 104 states that nitrocellulose membranes were incubated with "secondary antibody AlexaFluor 555 anti-rabbit (1:400, Invitrogen, USA). The blots were developed using ECL substrate (cat# 34,075, Thermo Fisher, USA)." AlexaFluor 555 is a fluorophore used for immunofluorescence (Section 2.8), not a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody capable of reacting with chemiluminescent ECL substrate!
4. **Data Availability ("Available Upon Request"):** Except for the proteomics dataset (`PXD065257`), the small RNA-seq counts/scripts and all qRT-PCR/imaging source data are listed as "All the data are available upon request" (lines 195, 207), and oligonucleotide sequences (`Supplementary Table S2`) are not included in the main text.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

### 1. Apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR Correction and Sequence-Match Filtering to the Proteomics Dataset
* **WHY:** Calling differentially expressed proteins at unadjusted $p < 0.05$ across thousands of proteins guarantees hundreds of false positives.
* **WHAT:** Re-analyze the `PXD065257` DIA-PASEF dataset using `limma` moderated $t$-statistics with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjustment ($q < 0.05$ or $q < 0.10$) and test for enrichment of `sinR-D2/D3` complementary seed sites in the 3'UTRs/CDS of downregulated proteins.
* **HOW:** Using the log$_2$ MaxLFQ protein matrix (prior to 1.8 SD deterministic imputation, or using `limma`/`proDA` dropout modeling), compute BH-adjusted $p$-values. Next, search mouse 3'UTRs and coding sequences for 6-mer/7-mer complementarity to the 5' core of `sinR-D2/D3` (`CTCGGTAGAACCTCCA`) and perform a cumulative distribution (KS) test comparing log$_2$ fold-changes of seed-matched vs. non-seed-matched proteins.

### 2. Re-Quantify 4w vs. 8w NOD Islet sinRNAs in FACS-Sorted $\text{CD45}^-$ $\beta$-Cells Normalized to `Let7a`
* **WHY:** Whole 8-week NOD islets contain infiltrating T cells (which express high `sinR-D3`/`sinR-T`), and `miR-184` decreases during insulitis, confounding both cell-type source and normalization in Fig. 2A.
* **WHAT:** Measure `sinR-D3` and `sinR-T` normalized to `Let7a` (matching Figs. 1C, 2B, 3B–D) in FACS-sorted $\text{CD45}^-$ autofluorescent $\beta$-cells (which the authors already describe in Section 2.4, line 74!) from 4-week and 8-week NOD mice.
* **HOW:** Re-run qRT-PCR on the existing 4w and 8w NOD RNA samples using `Let7a` as the reference gene to verify that the increase in Fig. 2A is not an artifact of `miR-184` downregulation, and test FACS-sorted $\text{CD45}^-$ $\beta$-cells (or normalize against T-cell marker *Cd3e* RNA) to confirm that the signal resides in $\beta$-cells rather than co-isolated lymphocytes.

### 3. Perform Statistical Tests on $\Delta C_t$ (Log-Scale) Values and Verify Human Sequence Conservation
* **WHY:** One-sample $t$-tests on exponentiated ratios ($2^{-\Delta\Delta C_t}$) are statistically biased, and human EV transfer (Fig. 3D) requires showing that `sinR-D3` exists in the human genome/transcriptome.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Re-compute all qRT-PCR and caspase-3 statistical comparisons using paired $t$-tests or mixed-effects models on the un-normalized $\Delta C_t$ (or logit/arcsine-transformed apoptosis proportions) across biological replicates.
  2. Provide the exact genomic alignment of `sinR-D1/D2/D3` and `sinR-T` between mouse (`mm10`) and human (`GRCh38`) in Figure S1 to justify testing `sinR-D3` in human T-cell EVs and human islets (Fig. 3D).
  3. Correct the secondary antibody description in Section 2.10 (line 104) and the *Ttll1* vs. *Ttll11* gene symbol in the Discussion (line 181).
