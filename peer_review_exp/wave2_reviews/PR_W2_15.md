## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript combines mathematical modeling, single-cell and bulk RNA-seq, ChIP-seq reanalysis, a genome-wide REMI-seq forward genetic screen, and live-cell single-cell tracking in *Dictyostelium discoideum* to investigate how deterministic cell-cycle cues and stochastic transcriptional variation jointly regulate prestalk vs. prespore fate proportioning. The authors formulate an analytical threshold-probit ("stochastic-deterministic") model in which a cell-cycle-associated factor ($\text{CCAF}(t) = A_0 - \beta t$) and a normally distributed cell-cycle-independent stochastic factor ($\text{CCIF} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, derived from a Bernoulli telegraph sum across $G$ genes) sum to determine whether a cell exceeds a stalk-differentiation threshold $R$, yielding stalk propensity $P_t = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \text{erf}\left[\frac{C_0^* - \beta t}{\sqrt{2}}\right]\right)$. Reanalyzing high-depth Fluidigm C1 scRNA-seq ($N = 81$ vegetative cells), they identify 1,057 cell-cycle-independent hypervariable ("stochastic") genes and 1,016 cell-cycle-associated hypervariable genes (`scran::modelGeneCV2`, $\text{FDR} < 0.05$). They show that these stochastic genes are enriched for developmental upregulation, prestalk/prespore specificity, and functional requirement for stalk differentiation in a REMI-seq screen (244 DIF-1 insensitive mutants mapped to 199 genes). Finally, they show that stochastic genes are enriched for H3K4me1 and depleted for H3K4me3 around the TSS, that deleting the H3K4 methyltransferase *Set1* (or COMPASS subunit *Ash2* or HAT *Gcn5*) upregulates variable genes while reducing their $\text{CV}^2$ in ICELL8 scRNA-seq ($N = 310$ WT and $310\text{ }set1^-$ cells), and that *set1* loss shifts cells toward prestalk fate across all cell-cycle phases and synthetically disrupts fruiting body morphogenesis when combined with cold-shock cell-cycle perturbation.

This is an integrative, mechanistically rich study that links theoretical biophysics to forward genetics, chromatin biology, and single-cell developmental phenotypes. Its main vulnerabilities lie in the statistical treatment of the 6-timepoint curve-fitting benchmark (mean-centering four heterogeneous conditions, post-hoc removal of the hour-5 outlier, and comparing a 2-parameter sigmoid against an unspecified exponential decay), the reliance on $N = 81$ cells partitioned into three small clusters ($n = 25, 31, 25$) to define cell-cycle independence, the potential confounding of Poisson UMI sampling noise with transcriptional burst frequency in the $\text{CV}^2$ decrease upon *Set1* deletion, and missing public accession numbers for the newly generated sequencing datasets.

### Assessment of Specific Conclusions
* **Neither a purely deterministic threshold nor a purely cell-cycle-independent noise model explains the smooth post-mitotic decline in stalk propensity, whereas an additive Gaussian-noise threshold model ($P_t = \frac{1}{2}[1 + \text{erf}((C_0^* - \beta t)/\sqrt{2})]$) captures the empirical curve: [Well Supported].**  
  The mathematical formulation (Equations 1–3) is clean, identifiable in its two-parameter rescaled form ($C_0^*, \beta$), and directly explains why isogenic cells at the exact same post-mitotic time $t$ exhibit probabilistic fate allocation rather than a step function.
* **Genes exhibiting high cell-cell expression variability in vegetative cells are enriched for developmental genes and genes functionally required for DIF-1-induced stalk differentiation (REMI-seq screen): [Well Supported].**  
  The combination of scRNA-seq variability analysis with an unbiased genome-wide REMI-seq screen (including growth and 8-Br-cAMP competence counter-screens and 8/9 individual mutant recreations) provides strong functional validation.
* **Set1/COMPASS-mediated H3K4 methylation and Gcn5-mediated H3K9/14 acetylation restrain prestalk differentiation and are required for prespore fate proportioning: [Well Supported].**  
  Demonstrated by FACS-sorted chimeric RNA-seq, clonal RNA-seq, *ecmO*/*ecmB*/*pspA* reporters, single-cell DIF-1 dose-response assays, cell-cycle tracking in *set1*$^-$, and phenocopying by *ash2*$^-$, *gcn5*$^-$, and hypomorphic *Set1-FLAG*.
* **Upregulation and decreased $\text{CV}^2$ of Set1-target genes in *set1*$^-$ scRNA-seq specifically proves an increase in transcriptional burst frequency ($p_i$) rather than burst size ($b_i$): [Fragile].**  
  In droplet/nanowell scRNA-seq (ICELL8) and FACS reporters, any increase in mean expression $\mu$ automatically reduces total measured $\text{CV}^2 = \frac{1}{\mu} + \text{CV}_{\text{bio}}^2$ due to the $1/\mu$ Poisson technical counting noise floor, and the theoretical derivation on line 60 ($\text{CV}^2 = \frac{1-p_i}{p_i} + \frac{\varepsilon^2}{p_i b_i^2}$) also decreases when burst size $b_i$ increases if $\varepsilon^2$ has an additive measurement noise component!
* **The linear CCAF decay model is "98 times more likely" ($\Delta\text{AIC} \approx 9.17$) than exponential decay based on fitting 6 timepoints after mean-aligning four conditions and dropping an outlier: [Moderately Supported].**  
  While linear decay is parsimonious, mean-centering four distinct genotype/nutrient datasets (WT G+, WT G$-$, *gefE*$^-$ G+, *gefE*$^-$ G$-$) onto a global mean and dropping the $t = 5\text{ h}$ *gefE*$^-$ G$-$ datapoint inflates degrees of freedom and goodness-of-fit ($R_{\text{adj}}^2 = 0.92$).

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1. Ambiguity Between Burst Frequency ($p_i$), Burst Size ($b_i$), and Technical Poisson Shot Noise in $\text{CV}^2$ Reduction (Lines 60–62) [Fragile]
In lines 60–62, the authors model single-gene expression as $g_i = e_i y_i$ with $e_i \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p_i)$ and $y_i \sim \mathcal{N}(b_i, \varepsilon^2)$, yielding:
$$\text{E}[g_i] = p_i b_i, \qquad \text{Var}(g_i) = p_i \varepsilon^2 + p_i(1 - p_i)b_i^2, \qquad \text{CV}^2 = \frac{1 - p_i}{p_i} + \frac{\varepsilon^2}{p_i b_i^2}.$$
They then argue that because upregulated Set1-dependent genes show higher mean expression and lower $\text{CV}^2$ in *set1*$^-$ cells (Fig. 5E–F), *Set1* disruption must act by increasing burst frequency ($\Delta p_i > 0$). However, inspect the second term of their own equation, $\frac{\varepsilon^2}{p_i b_i^2}$: **if burst size $b_i$ increases ($\Delta b_i > 0$) while $p_i$ and $\varepsilon^2$ remain constant, $\text{E}[g_i] = p_i b_i$ increases and $\text{CV}^2$ also decreases!** Moreover, in UMI-based scRNA-seq (ICELL8 3' DE, 310 cells per genotype), observed UMI counts follow a Negative Binomial / Poisson-mixture distribution where technical sampling variance adds $1/\mu$ to $\text{CV}^2$. Thus, any gene whose mean expression $\mu$ increases (for any reason, whether burst size, burst frequency, or mRNA stability) will exhibit a lower raw $\text{CV}^2$ unless the technical Poisson component is deconvolved or the fraction of non-zero cells ($\hat{p}_i$) is estimated directly after downsampling UMIs to matched capture depth.Fortunately, in Fig. 5G–H and Supplementary Fig. 6, the fluorescence distribution of *GtaU*-RFP and *HspF-2*-RFP shows a shift in the proportion of RFP-positive cells, which is much more direct evidence for $p_i$ than raw scRNA-seq $\text{CV}^2$.

### 2.2. Mean-Alignment and Outlier Removal in the 6-Timepoint Probit Curve Fitting (Lines 38, 68) [Moderately Supported]
In Methods (line 68), to fit the two-parameter model $P_t = \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \text{erf}\left[\frac{C_0^* - \beta t}{\sqrt{2}}\right]\right)$, the authors pooled four experimental groups from a previous study (WT G+, WT G$-$, *gefE*$^-$ G+, *gefE*$^-$ G$-$, measured at $t = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\text{ h}$) by "adjusting the values in set such that their mean propensity matched the overall global mean propensity." However, because $P_t = \Phi(C_0^* - \beta t)$ is a **nonlinear** (probit) function bounded on $[0, 1]$, differences in baseline threshold $C_0^*$ between *gefE*$^-$ or G$-$ cells shift the probit curve horizontally/nonlinearly in probability space, not by an additive constant in $P_t$! Subtracting group means in probability space distorts the curvature and removes 3 degrees of freedom that are not accounted for when computing AIC ($-56.37$) and adjusted $R^2$ ($0.92$) in `NonlinearModelFit`. Fitting a mixed-effects probit regression (`glm(..., family = binomial(link = "probit"))` with group-specific intercepts $C_{0,\text{group}}^*$ and common slope $\beta$ on the raw binomial cell counts) avoids ad-hoc mean shifting and properly weights each timepoint by the number of tracked cells.

### 2.3. Power and Classification Noise when Partitioning $N = 81$ Cells into Cell-Cycle vs. Stochastic Genes (Line 53) [Moderately Supported]
To separate cell-cycle-dependent variable genes from cell-cycle-independent ("stochastic") variable genes, the authors clustered $N = 81$ Fluidigm C1 cells into three clusters of $n = 25, 31,$ and $25$ cells and computed AUROC for each gene across the three clusters, classifying genes with $\text{AUROC} \le 0.65$ as cell-cycle-independent (1,057 genes) and $> 0.65$ as cell-cycle-dependent (1,016 genes). If a gene oscillates sharply during a narrow window of the cell cycle (e.g., M/early S phase, which in *D. discoideum* lasts <30–45 min out of an 8–10 hour cycle, whereas G2 occupies >85% of the cycle), a sample of 81 asynchronous cells will contain only 3–5 mitotic/S-phase cells, and a 3-cluster partition ($n = 25, 31, 25$) will dilute those cells across a larger cluster, yielding low AUROC and misclassifying a periodic M-phase gene as "cell-cycle-independent stochastic."

### 2.4. Missing Public Repository Accession Numbers for New Sequencing Datasets [Fragile]
While reanalyzed external datasets are cited with SRA/GEO accessions (`PRJNA543665`, `GSE137604`, `SAMN07833758`–`SAMN0783383`), the manuscript generates four major new sequencing datasets: (1) bulk RNA-seq of WT vs. *set1*$^-$ vegetative cells, (2) bulk RNA-seq of FACS-sorted WT vs. *set1*$^-$ chimeric slug cells and clonal slugs, (3) ICELL8 scRNA-seq of 799 (620 QC-passed) WT and *set1*$^-$ cells, and (4) REMI-seq gDNA pools across DIF-1 and 8-Br-cAMP selections. No GEO/SRA/ENA accession numbers are provided for these new datasets.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Formula Typo in Rescaled Threshold Definition (Line 38):** Line 38 defines the rescaled constant as "$C_0^* = C_t - R$" before substituting it into $\frac{1}{2}(1 + \text{erf}[(C_0^* - \beta t)/\sqrt{2}])$. Since $C_t = C_0 - \beta t$ already contains $-\beta t$ and $\sigma$ was absorbed into standard deviation units, the definition should be $C_0^* = (C_0 - R)/\sigma$ (and $\beta^* = \beta / \sigma$), not $C_t - R$.
2. **Quality Control Filter Wording in ICELL8 scRNA-seq (Line 58):** Line 58 states: "reads were then quality controlled (reads shorter than 15bp discarded, a minimum of 30% N's allowed, phred score of 20)." Presumably this means a **maximum** of 30% Ns allowed, not a minimum.
3. **Truncated SRA Accession Range (Line 53):** `SAMN07833758 - SAMN0783383` is missing a digit in the second accession (likely `SAMN07833838`).
4. **Grammar / Typos:** Line 76: "increases will burst frequency" $\to$ "increases with burst frequency"; "exhibit a higher probability of adopting the pre-stalk cell fate being higher throughout the cell cycle" (duplicated phrase).

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

### 1. Directly Estimate Burst Frequency ($\hat{p}_i$) vs. Burst Size ($\hat{b}_i$) in the ICELL8 scRNA-seq Dataset
* **WHY:** A decrease in raw $\text{CV}^2$ accompanying an increase in mean expression can be caused by an increase in burst size $b_i$ (via the $\varepsilon^2/(p_i b_i^2)$ term) or by reduced Poisson technical relative variance ($1/\mu$), rather than exclusively by burst frequency $p_i$.
* **WHAT:** Deconvolve burst frequency (detection probability among UMI-downsampled cells) from burst size (mean UMI count among expressing cells, $g_i > 0$) in WT vs. *set1*$^-$ ICELL8 scRNA-seq.
* **HOW:** Downsample all 310 WT and 310 *set1*$^-$ cells to an identical total UMI count per cell (or fit a zero-inflated/Poisson-beta hierarchical model). For each Set1-upregulated variable gene, compute (i) the fraction of cells with $>0$ UMIs ($\hat{p}_i$) and (ii) the mean UMI count conditional on $>0$ UMIs ($\hat{b}_i$). Report paired Wilcoxon tests for $\Delta \hat{p}_i$ vs. $\Delta \hat{b}_i$ alongside Fig. 5E–F.

### 2. Fit the Probit Model Directly to Unadjusted Group Data with Group-Specific Intercepts
* **WHY:** Mean-shifting four conditions in probability space before fitting a nonlinear probit curve distorts error structure and undercounts fitted parameters in AIC.
* **WHAT:** Fit the probit model $P_{g,t} = \Phi(C_{0,g}^* - \beta t)$ directly to the unshifted raw data from the four conditions ($g \in \{\text{WT G+}, \text{WT G}-, gefE^-\text{ G+}, gefE^-\text{ G}-\}$).
* **HOW:** In R or Mathematica, fit a 5-parameter model (four group intercepts $C_{0,g}^*$ and one shared decay slope $\beta$) to the 24 unadjusted data points (with and without the hour-5 *gefE*$^-$ G$-$ point) and report the resulting $\beta$, AIC, and $R^2$ alongside the mean-centered visual overlay in Fig. 1C.iii.

### 3. Deposit All New RNA-seq, scRNA-seq, and REMI-seq Datasets and Correct Equation/Method Typos
* **WHY:** Public availability of the new bulk RNA-seq, ICELL8 scRNA-seq, and REMI-seq datasets is essential for reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Deposit raw FASTQ and processed count matrices for bulk RNA-seq, ICELL8 scRNA-seq, and REMI-seq in NCBI GEO/SRA and add the accession numbers to the STAR Methods.
  2. Correct $C_0^* = C_t - R$ on line 38 to $C_0^* = (C_0 - R)/\sigma$ (with $\beta$ rescaled by $\sigma$).
  3. Correct "minimum of 30% N's allowed" on line 58 to "maximum of 30% N's allowed" and fix `SAMN0783383` on line 53 to `SAMN07833838`.
