## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study characterizes the anterior nares microbiome in ~1,100 community participants from the CARRIAGE cohort in England, pairing V1–V2 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing of the first nasal swab's Amies transport medium with three weekly nasal swabs cultured for *Staphylococcus aureus* (defining persistent [3/3 positive], intermittent [1–2/3 positive], and non-carriers [0/3 positive]) and whole-genome sequencing of 172 *S. aureus* isolates. Using hierarchical clustering on Bray-Curtis distances (identifying 7 Community State Types, CSTs), `ANCOM-BC2` differential abundance, `NetCoMi` SparCC co-occurrence networks, and a Random Forest classifier, the authors report that ~70% of persistent carriers belong to an *S. aureus*-dominated CST I, that intermittent carriers do not form a distinct microbiome state (splitting between non-carrier-like 1-swab positives and persistent-like 2-swab positives), that persistent carriage is negatively associated with three *Corynebacterium* species, *Dolosigranulum pigrum*, *S. epidermidis*, and *Moraxella catarrhalis*, that a Random Forest classifier predicts persistence from the microbiome, and that *S. aureus* phylogenetic Cluster B reaches higher abundance than Cluster A. Raw 16S and WGS reads are deposited in ENA and OTU nodes on Zenodo (`10.5281/zenodo.17160106`).

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision (Mixed / Salvageable).**  
Pairing longitudinal *S. aureus* culture with nasal 16S sequencing and rigorous contaminant removal across >1,000 community adults is a valuable epidemiological resource, and the empirical demonstration that "intermittent" carriers bifurcate into non-carrier-like (1/3 swabs) and persistent-like (2/3 swabs) profiles is well supported. However, the study's statistical modeling and machine learning analyses contain five major methodological defects that require re-analysis:
1. **Target Leakage / Circularity in the Random Forest Classifier:** To claim that *"the microbiome can be exploited with machine learning to accurately predict the persistence of S. aureus colonisation"* (Abstract; Fig. 5), the authors trained a Random Forest classifier on the 16S relative abundance table **including *S. aureus* 16S relative abundance itself**, which was extracted from the exact same swab liquid used for the first culture! As shown in Fig. 5c–d, *S. aureus* is overwhelmingly the top feature driving the classifier. Using 16S *S. aureus* reads from Swab 1 to predict whether Swab 1 (and Swabs 2–3) is culture-positive for *S. aureus* simply measures 16S-vs-culture concordance for the target organism rather than predicting colonization from the resident non-*S. aureus* microbiota.
2. **Unaddressed Compositional Closure Bias (`>50%–75%` *S. aureus* Dominance) and Inhomogeneous Merging of Rarefied and Unrarefied Counts:** In ~50% of persistent carriers, *S. aureus* accounts for $>50\%\text{–}75\%$ of all 16S reads (line 43). On a fixed-sum simplex without total 16S qPCR load quantification, a massive bloom of *S. aureus* mechanically depresses the relative abundance of all other resident taxa and lowers Shannon/Simpson diversity even if absolute *Corynebacterium* or *D. pigrum* cell densities are unchanged. Moreover, with only 24 species remaining after filtering, `ANCOM-BC2`'s median-based sampling fraction estimator is vulnerable to a >50% dominant taxon. Worse, for abundance analyses, the authors **merged samples rarefied to 10,004 reads ($n = 795$) with unrarefied samples having 500–10,000 reads ($n = 260$)** (Methods lines 58, 68), creating an inhomogeneous count table with artificial library-size stratification.
3. **Impossible Degrees of Freedom (`F(2)`) Reported for Every Two-Group PERMANOVA:** Throughout the Results (lines 28, 38, 67, 91), every two-group PERMANOVA comparison—Culture Positive vs. Negative (`F(2) = 59.01`), Male vs. Female (`F(2) = 2.83`), 1 vs. 2 Positive Swabs (`F(2) = 3.19`), and Cluster A vs. Cluster B (`F(2) = 2.33`)—is reported with **2 numerator degrees of freedom (`F(2)`) instead of `F(1)`**. Either `NA`/missing values were inadvertently included as a third factor level in `vegan::adonis2` (contaminating the test statistic), or degrees of freedom were misreported across all two-group tests.
4. **Numerical Floating-Point Zero (`EV = 9.88 × 10⁻¹⁷`) Misinterpreted as a "Keystone Hub Taxon" in Network Analysis:** In Results line 60, the authors identify *Klebsiella pneumoniae* as the "hub taxon" of Group 4 with an eigenvector centrality of `EV = 9.88 × 10⁻¹⁷` and suggest it plays *"keystone roles in community organisation"*. A value of $9.88 \times 10^{-17}$ is IEEE 754 double-precision machine zero ($\epsilon \approx 10^{-16}$) for a disconnected network component, not a biological hub.
5. **Tautological Inclusion of *S. aureus* Reads in the Phylogenetic Cluster Beta-Diversity Test and Underpowered Longitudinal Stability Claim:** In Figure 6c–d (line 91), the authors test whether the nasal microbiome differs between *S. aureus* phylogenetic Cluster A and Cluster B by running PERMANOVA on Bray-Curtis distances **that include *S. aureus* relative abundance** ($p = 0.04$), which merely recapitulates the difference in *S. aureus* abundance shown in Fig. 6b ($p = 0.04$). In line 62, the claim that persistent ($\rho = 0.54, n = 13$) and intermittent ($\rho = 0.79, n = 7$) carriers have greater longitudinal stability than non-carriers ($\rho = 0.30, n = 14$) commits pseudoreplication across repeated week pairs and compares nominal $p$-values ($p = 0.028$ vs. $p = 0.268$) when the difference between $\rho = 0.54$ and $\rho = 0.30$ is statistically non-significant ($p = 0.51$).

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1 (The adult anterior nares microbiome clusters into 7 CSTs, with persistent *S. aureus* carriers predominantly occupying an *S. aureus*-dominated CST I and females enriched in CST VI/VII; Figs. 1–2): [Well Supported].**  
  Supported by gap-statistic clustering on $N = 1,055$ post-QC samples and multinomial logistic regression controlling for lifestyle covariates, though two-group PERMANOVA degrees of freedom (`F(2)`) require correction.
* **Claim 2 (Intermittent *S. aureus* carriers do not possess a distinct nasal microbiome, instead partitioning into non-carrier-like [1 positive swab] and persistent-like [2 positive swabs] profiles; Fig. 4): [Well Supported].**  
  Consistently demonstrated by ordination overlap (Fig. 4a–d), stratified abundance barplots (Fig. 4e–f), CST distributions (Fig. 4g), and 0% class sensitivity for the intermittent category in Random Forest classification (Fig. 5b).
* **Claim 3 (Persistent carriage is negatively associated with three *Corynebacterium* spp., *D. pigrum*, *S. epidermidis*, and *M. catarrhalis*, and four subcommunity hub taxa—including *K. pneumoniae* at `EV = 9.88e-17`—organize the network; Fig. 3): [Fragile].**  
  While negative associations with *D. pigrum*, *Corynebacterium* spp., and *S. epidermidis* are biologically plausible, the absence of total 16S qPCR load measurements means compositional closure from $>50\%\text{–}75\%$ *S. aureus* read dominance cannot be separated from true competitive exclusion, and calling `EV = 9.88e-17` (machine zero) a keystone hub taxon is a numerical error.
* **Claim 4 (Machine learning on the nasal microbiome accurately predicts *S. aureus* colonization persistence; Abstract, Fig. 5): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  The Random Forest classifier includes *S. aureus* 16S relative abundance from Swab 1 as its primary feature (Fig. 5c–d) to predict culture positivity on Swabs 1–3. Re-training the model *without* *S. aureus* (using only the remaining 23 species) is required to test whether the resident microbiota predicts carriage.
* **Claim 5 (*S. aureus* Cluster B lineages achieve higher nasal abundance and shape a distinct nasal microbiome compared to Cluster A; Fig. 6): [Fragile].**  
  Sample size drops unexplainedly from $n = 172$ sequenced isolates to $n = 111$ in Fig. 6b with a borderline unadjusted $p = 0.04$, and the Bray-Curtis PERMANOVA in Fig. 6c–d ($p = 0.04$) is confounded by including *S. aureus* reads in the distance matrix.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Target Leakage in Random Forest Prediction of *S. aureus* Carriage by Including *S. aureus* 16S Reads (Pillars 1, 2, 5)
* **Location:** Abstract (line 5), Results ("Predicting colonisation status from the nasal microbiome", lines 79–86, Fig. 5), Discussion (line 20 of part 2), and Methods (line 83).
* **Defect:** The Random Forest model predicts culture-defined *S. aureus* carriage status (0, 1–2, or 3 positive weekly swabs, where Swab 1 is the exact same swab sequenced by 16S rRNA) using the relative abundance of all 24 taxa **including *S. aureus***. Unsurprisingly, *S. aureus* relative abundance is by far the dominant predictor in both Mean Decrease in Accuracy and Mean Decrease in Gini (Fig. 5c, d). Predicting whether a sample grows *S. aureus* in culture from how many *S. aureus* 16S reads are in that same sample is circular and does not show that the *resident background microbiome* predicts colonization persistence.
* **Remediation:** Train and evaluate a second Random Forest classifier where *S. aureus* is **excluded** from the feature matrix (using either the re-normalized relative abundances or centered log-ratios of the 23 non-*S. aureus* species), and compare its ROC-AUC, sensitivity, and specificity side-by-side with the *S. aureus*-inclusive model.

### 3.2 Incorrect Degrees of Freedom (`F(2)`) in All Two-Group PERMANOVA Tests and Inconsistent Sample Sizes Across Figures (Pillars 4, 7)
* **Location:** Results (lines 28, 38, 43, 45, 67, 91; Figs. 1, 4, 6) and Methods (lines 58, 68, 83).
* **Defect:**
  1. **Impossible `F(2)` in Two-Group PERMANOVAs:** A one-way PERMANOVA comparing $k = 2$ groups has $k - 1 = 1$ numerator degree of freedom (`F(1, N-2)`). Yet the manuscript reports `F(2)` for every two-group comparison:
     - *S. aureus* culture positive vs. negative (lines 28, 38): `F(2) = 59.01, p < 0.001`
     - Sex: Female vs. Male (line 38): `F(2) = 2.83, p = 0.006`
     - Intermittent carriers: 1 positive swab vs. 2 positive swabs (line 67): `F(2) = 3.19, p = 0.003`
     - Phylogenetic Cluster A vs. Cluster B (line 91): `F(2) = 2.33, p = 0.04`
     This indicates either that samples with `NA`/missing metadata were passed to `adonis2` as a third group (meaning the test statistic is confounded by missing-data samples), or a systematic reporting error.
  2. **Unreconciled Sample Size Variations:** Across the text and legends, sample sizes fluctuate without a clear CONSORT-style accounting table in the main text: post-QC samples = $1,055$ (line 28); sum of persistent + intermittent + non-carriers in line 43 = $275 + 169 + 532 = 976$; sum in Fig. 1b–c legend = $210 + 120 + 413 = 743$; sum of culture-negative + positive in Fig. 1d–e legend = $510 + 284 = 794$ vs. rarefied dataset $n = 795$ in line 83; and WGS isolates drop from $n = 172$ (Fig. 6a) to $n = 125$ (CST match) and $n = 111$ (Fig. 6b).
  3. **Merging Rarefied and Unrarefied Samples (lines 58, 68):** Combining 795 samples rarefied to exactly 10,004 reads with 260 unrarefied samples having 500–10,000 reads distorts count-based models.
* **Remediation:** Audit all `vegan::adonis2` calls to ensure `NA` samples are filtered out before running PERMANOVA and report both numerator and residual degrees of freedom `F(df1, df2)`; provide a clear sample-size flow table in Results reconciling $N = 1,055$, $976$, $795$, $794$, $743$, $172$, $125$, and $111$; and avoid mixing rarefied and unrarefied count tables.

### 3.3 Machine-Epsilon Zero (`EV = 9.88 × 10⁻¹⁷`) Misreported as a Keystone Hub Taxon and Underpowered Longitudinal Stability Claim (Pillars 3, 4)
* **Location:** Results (lines 60, 62; Fig. 3b).
* **Defect:**
  1. **Machine-Zero Eigenvector Centrality (line 60):** In the SparCC network (Fig. 3b), the authors report the "hub taxon" for each of the four subcommunities by maximum eigenvector centrality (`EV`), listing *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (Group 4, `EV = 9.88 × 10⁻¹⁷`) and stating *"These may represent keystone roles in community organisation."* Because the Perron-Frobenius eigenvector of a disconnected graph is strictly zero on non-dominant connected components, `9.88 × 10⁻¹⁷` is numerical floating-point zero ($\approx 10^{-16}$), meaning Group 4 has zero eigenvector centrality.
  2. **Longitudinal Stability Pseudoreplication and Difference-in-Significance Fallacy (line 62):** In a subset of 34 participants ($13$ persistent, $7$ intermittent, $14$ non-carriers; $75$ swabs total), the authors compute Spearman correlations of alpha diversity across consecutive weeks (`rho = 0.54, p = 0.028` for persistent; `rho = 0.79, p = 0.028` for intermittent; `rho = 0.30, p = 0.268` for non-carriers) and conclude that persistent and intermittent carriers have *"greater stability compared to non-carriers"*. Comparing $p = 0.028$ against $p = 0.268$ at $n = 13\text{–}14$ commits the "difference between significant and not significant" fallacy (a Fisher $z$-test comparing $\rho = 0.54$ at $n = 13$ vs. $\rho = 0.30$ at $n = 14$ yields $p = 0.51$, non-significant), and pooling multiple consecutive week-pairs from the same 34 participants into a simple Spearman correlation violates independence.
* **Remediation:** Compute within-module degree or within-component eigenvector centrality for disconnected network components in Fig. 3b (and remove the claim that `EV = 9.88e-17` indicates a hub taxon); for the longitudinal subgroup ($n = 34$), compute the within-subject Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) or within-subject median Bray-Curtis stability with a formal between-group Kruskal-Wallis test.

### 3.4 Confounding of *S. aureus* Reads in Phylogenetic Cluster Beta-Diversity and Compositional Closure Caveats (Pillars 3, 5, 6)
* **Location:** Results (lines 55, 91; Figs. 3a, 6b–d) and Code Availability (line 126).
* **Defect:**
  1. In Figure 6c–d (line 91), the authors test whether *S. aureus* phylogenetic Cluster A and Cluster B are associated with different nasal microbiomes using Bray-Curtis PERMANOVA ($p = 0.04$). Because *S. aureus* reads were left inside the relative abundance table used to compute Bray-Curtis distance—and *S. aureus* often comprises $>50\%$ of all reads—this test is directly driven by the difference in *S. aureus* read abundance shown in Fig. 6b ($p = 0.04$) rather than differences in the non-*S. aureus* community.
  2. Without total 16S bacterial load quantification (e.g., 16S qPCR), a massive increase in absolute *S. aureus* load in persistent carriers induces compositional closure across the 24-species simplex.
  3. Line 126 states `"No custom code was developed for the aforementioned purposes"`, yet all statistical, clustering, network, and Random Forest analyses were executed via custom R scripts in R v4.4.1.
* **Remediation:** Re-run the Cluster A vs. Cluster B Bray-Curtis PERMANOVA after removing *S. aureus* reads (testing whether the resident non-*S. aureus* community differs between lineages); explicitly discuss compositional closure (absence of 16S qPCR total load) as an alternative explanation for negative relative-abundance associations in persistent carriers; and deposit the R analysis scripts on Zenodo or GitHub.
