## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This quantitative genetics and animal breeding manuscript evaluates variance components, heritabilities ($h^2$), forward genomic prediction accuracies, and prediction biases across seven traits—juvenile weight at 12 days (`JW`), slaughter body weight (`BW`), ultrasound breast muscle depth (`BD`), primary feather length (`PRF`), gait score (`Gait`), average daily gain (`ADG`), and foot pad dermatitis score (`FPD`)—in two commercial Pekin duck lines (`Line A`, $N = 13,645$ across 6 generations; `Line D`, $N = 13,020$ across 5 generations). Animals were genotyped on a 427-SNP low-density parentage panel and imputed to a 60K medium-density SNP chip (~47.2K and ~46.3K QC-passed SNPs in Lines A and D) using `AlphaImpute` trained on 1,767 (Line A) and 1,536 (Line D) 60K-genotyped birds (reporting masked imputation accuracy of 0.93 and 0.92). The authors pre-correct phenotypes for fixed effects (`sexhatch`, `age of dam`, and `feeding pen`) in `ASReml`, fit animal models to the residuals ($y^*$) using either the pedigree relationship matrix ($\mathbf{A}$, BLUP) or VanRaden's genomic relationship matrix ($\mathbf{G}$, GBLUP), and evaluate forward prediction into the final generation (`GEN 6` in Line A, $n = 4,066$; `GEN 5` in Line D, $n = 4,885$). They report that pedigree heritabilities ($h_A^2$) exceed genomic heritabilities ($h_G^2$), while GBLUP yields substantially higher prediction accuracies (e.g., Gait accuracy rising from 0.24 to 0.58 in Line A) and lower biases than pedigree BLUP.

While the commercial dataset ($>26,000$ phenotyped and imputed ducks) is large and addresses practically important welfare traits (`Gait`, `FPD`), the statistical methodology contains a textbook mathematical flaw that directly invalidates the primary claim that GBLUP outperforms BLUP in prediction accuracy (especially for low-$h^2$ traits like `Gait`), alongside an improper bias definition, two-stage fixed-effect pre-correction across generations under selection, and complete absence of Tables 1–5, Figures 1–2, and Data Availability statements.

### Assessment of Specific Conclusions
* **Imputation from the 427-SNP parentage panel to the 60K SNP array achieves 0.92–0.93 concordance in masked validation animals: [Moderately Supported].**  
  With ~1,500–1,767 reference animals genotyped at 60K in closed nucleus lines and pedigree structure available to `AlphaImpute`, ~0.92–0.93 dosage correlation is plausible, though 200 masked validation birds without specifying whether they were in the latest generation (`GEN 5/6`) vs. ancestral generations may overestimate forward imputation accuracy.
* **Genomic heritabilities ($h_G^2$) are consistently lower than pedigree heritabilities ($h_A^2$) across traits: [Moderately Supported].**  
  In imputed 427 $\to$ 60K data, incomplete LD with causal variants, imputation dosage smoothing, and common maternal/family environmental effects captured by $\mathbf{A}$ routinely make $\hat{h}_G^2 < \hat{h}_A^2$. (Note, however, that line 56 contradicts line 38 by stating "we observed an overestimation in Line A, of heritability estimates in BW and ADG" with markers.)
* **GBLUP dramatically improves forward prediction accuracy over pedigree BLUP, especially for low-heritability welfare traits such as `Gait` (0.58 vs. 0.24): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  As detailed in Section 2.1 below, predictive accuracy was computed as $r_{gg} = r(\hat{g}, y^*) / \sqrt{h^2}$ (line 123). Because the authors divided the GBLUP correlation $r(\text{PGEBV}, y^*)$ by $\sqrt{h_G^2}$ and divided the BLUP correlation $r(\text{PPEBV}, y^*)$ by $\sqrt{h_A^2}$, and because $h_G^2$ is much smaller than $h_A^2$ (e.g., $h_G^2 = 0.07$ for Gait vs. higher $h_A^2$), **dividing by a smaller $\sqrt{h_G^2}$ mechanically inflates GBLUP accuracy by $\sqrt{h_A^2 / h_G^2}$ even if the actual predictive correlation with phenotype $r(\hat{g}, y^*)$ is identical!**
* **GBLUP exhibits lower prediction bias than pedigree BLUP based on regressing "True" EBV (with phenotypes) on Predicted EBV (masked phenotypes) (Line 125): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Regressing full-data $\text{EBV}_{\text{whole}}$ on partial-data $\text{EBV}_{\text{partial}}$ measures the **Legarra-Reverter dispersion ratio**, whose theoretical expectation under an unbiased model is $b = 1.0$, **not** the regression of true breeding value on predicted breeding value when different relationship matrices ($\mathbf{A}$ vs. $\mathbf{G}$) and different heritabilities ($h_A^2$ vs. $h_G^2$) are used to compute the "True" EBV for each method separately!

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1. Fatal Denominator Bias in the Accuracy Formula $r_{gg} = r(\hat{g}, y^*) / \sqrt{h^2}$ When Comparing $\mathbf{G}$ vs. $\mathbf{A}$ (Lines 38, 43, 123) [Methodologically Flawed]
In Materials and Methods (line 123), the authors define prediction accuracy as:
$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{r_{\hat{g}\hat{y}}}{\sqrt{h_y^2}}$$
where $r_{\hat{g}\hat{y}}$ is the Pearson correlation between predicted breeding values (`PGEBV` for GBLUP, `PPEBV` for BLUP) and adjusted phenotypes $y^*$ in the validation generation, and $h_y^2$ is the heritability estimate.
When comparing two competing prediction models (BLUP vs. GBLUP) on the **exact same validation phenotypes $y^*$**, the true additive genetic variance $\sigma_A^2$ and true heritability $h_{\text{true}}^2 = \sigma_A^2 / \sigma_P^2$ of the validation birds is a **single biological constant of the trait and population**—it does not change depending on which statistical matrix ($\mathbf{A}$ or $\mathbf{G}$) the analyst chooses!
However, because the authors estimated $h^2$ separately under $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{G}$, and found that $\hat{h}_G^2$ is substantially lower than $\hat{h}_A^2$ across all traits (e.g., for `JW` in Line A, $\hat{h}_G^2 = 0.22$ vs. $\hat{h}_A^2 \approx 0.35\text{--}0.39$; for `Gait`, $\hat{h}_G^2 = 0.07$ vs. $\hat{h}_A^2 = 0.12$, line 38, 60), dividing $r(\text{PGEBV}, y^*)$ by $\sqrt{\hat{h}_G^2}$ while dividing $r(\text{PPEBV}, y^*)$ by $\sqrt{\hat{h}_A^2}$ introduces a severe mathematical artifact:
- For `Gait` ($\hat{h}_G^2 = 0.07 \implies \sqrt{0.07} = 0.2646$, vs. $\hat{h}_A^2 = 0.12 \implies \sqrt{0.12} = 0.3464$), dividing by $0.2646$ instead of $0.3464$ automatically inflates the GBLUP accuracy relative to BLUP by **$31\%$**!
- Even worse, if a marker panel is poorly imputed or captures almost no genetic variance ($\hat{h}_G^2 \to 0.01$), dividing noise correlation by $\sqrt{0.01} = 0.10$ yields a huge apparent "genomic accuracy" (often $>1.0$!).
To compare BLUP and GBLUP fairly, both predictive correlations $r(\text{PPEBV}, y^*)$ and $r(\text{PGEBV}, y^*)$ must be reported directly **and** scaled by the **same** baseline heritability estimate (or evaluated via the Legarra-Reverter LR method, which the authors inexplicably dismiss on line 66!).

### 2.2. Circularity in Defining "True" Breeding Value (`TBV`) as Full-Data EBV Within Each Model for Bias Estimation (Line 125) [Methodologically Flawed]
In line 125, the authors write:
> "Bias was measured as the regression of 'True' EBV (estimated with phenotype) on Predicted EBV (Phenotype Masked) using both marker and pedigree information."

And in line 66, they simultaneously write:
> "Other methodologies such as LR, have been proposed to evaluate the cross-validation tools using 'partial' and 'whole' data [69], however, we don't feel this method is appropriate in the current study..."

There is a fundamental contradiction here:
1. Regressing $\hat{u}_{\text{whole}}$ ("True" EBV estimated with validation phenotypes unmasked) on $\hat{u}_{\text{partial}}$ (Predicted EBV with validation phenotypes masked) **is literally the Legarra & Reverter (2018) LR dispersion statistic** ($b_{w,p} = \frac{\text{cov}(\hat{u}_w, \hat{u}_p)}{\text{var}(\hat{u}_p)}$), yet in line 66 the authors claim LR is "not appropriate in the current study"!
2. Worse still, in single-generation pedigree BLUP where validation candidates in `GEN 6` have **no progeny** and have their **own phenotypes masked**, the predicted pedigree EBV (`PPEBV`) of every full-sib in a family is identical (equal to the parent average, $\frac{1}{2}(\hat{u}_{\text{sire}} + \hat{u}_{\text{dam}})$). Once `GEN 6` phenotypes are unmasked to compute "True" PEBV, the bird's own phenotype updates both its Mendelian sampling term and the parent estimates. Standard forward validation bias in livestock breeding is measured by regressing the **validation adjusted phenotype $y^*$** on the predicted breeding value ($y^* = b_0 + b_1 \hat{u}_{\text{partial}} + e$), where unbiasedness requires $b_1 = 1.0$, because $y^*$ is model-independent whereas $\hat{u}_{\text{whole}}$ depends on the assumed variance components ($\hat{h}_A^2$ vs. $\hat{h}_G^2$).

### 2.3. Two-Stage Fixed-Effect Pre-Correction Across Generations Under Directional Selection Biases Genetic Trends and Forward Prediction (Lines 52, 102–112) [Methodologically Flawed]
In lines 52 and 102–112, the authors first fit a ordinary least-squares fixed-effects model without genetic relationships:
$$Y_{ij} = \mu + S_i + A_j + e_{ij}$$
where $S_i = \text{sexhatch}$ (the interaction of sex and hatching batch across 5–6 generations!) and $A_j = \text{age of dam}$, and then use the residuals $y^* = e_{ij}$ as phenotypes in `ASReml`.
In a commercial nucleus duck population undergoing directional selection across 5–6 generations, each consecutive `hatch` batch belongs to a more advanced generation with a higher mean additive genetic merit ($\bar{a}_{\text{GEN } t} > \bar{a}_{\text{GEN } t-1}$). When `sexhatch` is fitted as a fixed effect in a model **without** the animal genetic effect ($\mathbf{a}$ or $\mathbf{g}$), the fixed `sexhatch` effect absorbs the **entire inter-generational genetic trend** ($\Delta G$ across generations 1 to 6) as well as genetic differences among contemporary groups! Furthermore, fitting `sexhatch` across all 6 generations (including the validation generation `GEN 6`) before masking `GEN 6` phenotypes leaks `GEN 6` phenotypic batch means into the pre-correction step. Fixed effects (`sexhatch`, `age of dam`, `pen`) must be fitted **jointly** with the random genetic effect ($\mathbf{a}$ or $\mathbf{g}$) in `ASReml` (which easily handles 13,000 records in seconds on a standard desktop!).

### 2.4. Inappropriate Linear Gaussian Modeling of Ordinal/Categorical Welfare Scores (`Gait` and `FPD`) and Fitting Endogenous `BW` as a Covariate (Lines 100–110) [Fragile]
1. `Gait` score (typically a discrete 0–2 or 1–5 ordinal scale) and `FPD` score (a discrete 0–2 or 0–4 ulceration score with strong zero-inflation) are categorical/ordinal welfare traits, yet they were pre-corrected and analyzed as Gaussian continuous traits without threshold/probit link models.
2. Line 110 states "We also fitted BW as a covariate" right after Equation (2) ("Y is phenotype for BW and BD"). Obviously `BW` cannot be fitted as a covariate when `BW` itself is the response variable $Y$; presumably `BW` was fitted as a covariate for `BD` (or `Gait`/`FPD`). However, adjusting `BD` or `Gait` for phenotypic `BW` (which is genetically correlated with `BD` and `Gait`) changes the trait definition to body-weight-conditional residual depth/gait and introduces collider bias if genetic and environmental correlations differ.

### 2.5. Missing Tables 1–5, Figures 1–2, and Data Availability Statement [Methodologically Flawed]
Although the text repeatedly cites `Table 1`, `Table 2`, `Table 3`, `Table 4`, `Table 5`, `Fig 1`, and `Fig 2`, **none of the five tables or two figures are present in the manuscript file**, and there is no Data Availability or Code Availability statement.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Ambiguous $r$ vs. $r^2$ Notation for Prediction Accuracy (Lines 7, 43, 123):** In the Abstract (line 7) and Results (line 43), prediction accuracies are written as "$r^2 = 0.68\text{--}0.70$" and "$r^2$ of $0.49$" for BW and JW, and then immediately as "$0.58$ vs $0.24$" for Gait, whereas line 62 (breeders' equation $R = i \cdot r \cdot \sigma_A$) and line 123 ($\text{Accuracy} = r_{\hat{g}\hat{y}} / \sqrt{h_y^2}$) define accuracy as the correlation $r$, not the coefficient of determination $r^2$. If $r_{\hat{g}y} / \sqrt{h^2} = 0.70$, then $r^2$ (reliability) is $0.49$, not $0.70$. This must be standardized throughout.
2. **Internal Contradiction on Heritability Direction (Line 38 vs. Line 56):** Line 7 (Abstract), line 38 (Results), and line 73 (Conclusion) state that heritability estimates obtained using the pedigree were higher than using genomic markers in **all** traits considered in both lines. Yet line 56 (Discussion) states: "we observed an overestimation in Line A, of heritability estimates in BW and ADG [by markers]."
3. **Inconsistent Gait Heritability Numbers (Line 38 vs. Line 60):** Line 7 and line 38 report `Gait` marker heritability as **$0.07 \pm 0.01$** in both lines, whereas line 60 reports "The current estimates of heritability for gait were low (**$0.06 \pm 0.06$ and $0.12 \pm 0.06$**)." Standard errors of $\pm 0.06$ vs. $\pm 0.01$ on $N = 13,000$ birds cannot both be right.
4. **Editorial Typos:** Line 14: `single-step GBLUB` $\to$ `single-step GBLUP`; `Meuwissen, Hayes ( 4 )` (citation formatting broken throughout text: `VanRaden ( 9 )`, `Génin ( 58 )`, `Golan, Lander ( 61 )`, `Hu, Poivey ( 63 )`); Line 60: `-0.51±12` $\to$ `-0.51±0.12`; Line 91: `reference[ 73 ]` (missing space); Line 114: `were perform` $\to$ `were performed`.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

### 1. Fit Fixed and Random Genetic Effects Jointly in `ASReml` Without Two-Stage Residual Pre-Correction
* **WHY:** Pre-correcting phenotypes by OLS fixed effects (`sexhatch`) before fitting the animal model absorbs the multi-generation genetic trend into the batch effect and leaks validation-generation phenotypic means. `ASReml` can fit a single-trait animal model with 13,645 records and a 13,645 $\times$ 13,645 $\mathbf{G}$ matrix in less than 2 minutes.
* **WHAT:** Fit the complete linear mixed model $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{Z}\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{e}$ (for BLUP) and $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{b} + \mathbf{Z}\mathbf{g} + \mathbf{e}$ (for GBLUP) in a single step in `ASReml`, masking raw phenotypes of `GEN 6` (Line A) and `GEN 5` (Line D) during forward prediction.
* **HOW:** Replace the two-step SAS/ASReml residual workflow (Equations 1–4) with a single-step `ASReml` model including `sexhatch`, `age_of_dam`, and `pen` in the fixed model statement and `vm(animal, Ainv)` or `vm(animal, Ginv)` in the random statement. For ordinal traits (`Gait`, `FPD`), also run a threshold probit model (`!probit`) to verify variance component stability.

### 2. Correct the Forward Prediction Accuracy and Bias Metrics to Eliminate Denominator Distortion
* **WHY:** Dividing GBLUP predictive correlation by $\sqrt{\hat{h}_G^2}$ and BLUP predictive correlation by $\sqrt{\hat{h}_A^2}$ artificially inflates GBLUP accuracy whenever $\hat{h}_G^2 < \hat{h}_A^2$.
* **WHAT:** Report (1) the raw predictive correlation $r(\hat{u}, y^*)$ in the validation generation, (2) accuracy scaled by a common heritability denominator ($\sqrt{\hat{h}_A^2}$ or joint $\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{G}$ heritability), and (3) standard Legarra-Reverter (LR) validation metrics ($\hat{\rho}_{w,p}$, dispersion slope $b_{w,p}$, and phenotypic regression slope $b_{y^*,\hat{u}_p}$).
* **HOW:** For the validation cohorts (`GEN 6` in Line A, `GEN 5` in Line D), compute:
  1. Raw predictive correlation $r(\hat{u}_p, y^*)$ (where $y^* = y - \mathbf{X}\hat{\mathbf{b}}$ adjusted only for fixed effects estimated without genetic trend leakage, or within contemporary groups).
  2. Common-denominator accuracy $r_{gg} = r(\hat{u}_p, y^*) / \sqrt{\hat{h}_{\text{ref}}^2}$ using the same $\hat{h}_{\text{ref}}^2$ for both BLUP and GBLUP.
  3. Phenotypic dispersion slope $b_1 = \text{cov}(y^*, \hat{u}_p) / \text{var}(\hat{u}_p)$ (where $b_1 = 1.0$ indicates zero inflation/deflation bias).

### 3. Include All Missing Tables (Tables 1–5) and Figures (Figures 1–2), Reconcile Numerical Contradictions, and Add Data Availability
* **WHY:** None of the five tables or two figures referenced in the text are included in the manuscript, and text numbers contradict each other.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Insert Tables 1–5 and Figures 1–2 into the manuscript.
  2. Standardize $r$ (accuracy) vs. $r^2$ (reliability) in the Abstract and Results.
  3. Reconcile the `Gait` heritability values ($0.07 \pm 0.01$ on line 38 vs. $0.06 \pm 0.06$ and $0.12 \pm 0.06$ on line 60) and the Line A `BW`/`ADG` heritability statement (line 38 vs. line 56).
  4. Clarify which traits included `BW` as a covariate (line 110).
  5. Add a formal Data and Code Availability statement.
