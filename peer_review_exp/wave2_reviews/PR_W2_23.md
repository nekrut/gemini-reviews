## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study examines the epistatic relationship between Hedgehog (HH) and TGF-beta signalling and downstream transcriptional programs during zebrafish larval tail regeneration (48–120 hours post-fertilization). Using standard and hybridization chain reaction (HCR) in situ hybridization alongside pharmacological inhibitors (`cyclopamine` for HH and `SB431542` for TGF-beta), the authors report that notochord-bead HH signalling activates `tgfb1a` transcription after injury, while TGF-beta signalling feeds back to repress notochordal `ihha`/`ihhb` and promote its own expression. Using bulk 3' QuantSeq RNA-seq at 18 hours post-excision (hpe) across a $2 \times 2$ design (`Unoperated/Operated` $\times$ `DMSO/SB431542`, $n = 3$ replicates of 100 tail pieces each; `GSE296469`), re-analysis of published regeneration and developmental scRNA-seq datasets (`GSE145497`, `GSE223922`), and live imaging (`ubi:h2b-dendra` photoconversion and `actb2:h2b-mCherry` light-sheet microscopy), the authors identify two oppositely regulated keratin gene sets (`krt_up` represented by `krt97` in the moving finfold epithelium, and `krt_down` represented by `krt5` in stationary trunk epithelium) and propose that TGF-beta drives collective epithelial migration by switching keratin expression.

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision (Mixed / Salvageable).**  
The epistatic in situ experiments linking HH to `tgfb1a` induction and documenting the compartmentalized expression of `krt97` and `krt5` during development and regeneration are informative and visually clear. However, the manuscript's central mechanistic claims and quantitative analyses suffer from four major defects:
1. **Unsubstantiated Causal Claim Linking Keratin Switching to Epithelial Migration:** The study performs no genetic or functional perturbation of `krt97`, `krt5`, or any other keratin gene, nor does it measure epithelial cell migration under `SB431542` treatment. The claim that `krt_up` enables migration while `krt_down` inhibits mobility rests entirely on anatomical correlation between finfold vs. trunk compartments.
2. **Omission of a Formal $2 \times 2$ Interaction Model in Bulk RNA-seq Analysis:** Despite collecting a balanced $2 \times 2$ factorial dataset (`Operation` $\times$ `Drug`), the authors do not fit a formal interaction term (`~ Operation * Drug`) in DESeq2. Instead, they rely on visual quadrant/slope heuristics and classify keratin genes using unadjusted $P < 0.05$ in "at least one comparison" (and even include genes with $P > 0.5$).
3. **Simpson's Paradox / Cell-Type Confounding in Single-Cell Co-Expression Analysis:** In Figure 8, Pearson correlations between `krt` genes are computed after pooling three distinct cell clusters (`periderm`, `fin epithelium`, and `epithelium`) in the Daniocell scRNA-seq dataset. Pooling transcriptionally distinct cell layers automatically creates positive within-cluster and negative between-cluster correlations among compartment markers without demonstrating intracellular co-regulation.
4. **Underpowered Live-Imaging Assays, Internal `ihha`/`ihhb` Contradictions, and Missing Methods Details:** Developmental light-sheet tracking used only $n = 3$ larvae (staged inconsistently at 22 hpf and 24 hpf, tracking 3 nuclei per region); the text contradicts itself on whether TGF-beta represses `ihha` (Introduction, Results) or `ihhb` (Discussion); and the Methods omit drug concentrations (`cyclopamine`, `SB431542`), phylogenetic methods (Fig. 4b), and contain misassigned software citations.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1 (HH signalling acts upstream of `tgfb1a` after tail excision, and TGF-beta signalling negatively regulates HH ligand expression while auto-activating `tgfb1a`; Fig. 2): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Supported by whole-mount and HCR in situ assays with `cyclopamine` and `SB431542`, though the text inconsistently identifies the repressed HH ligand as `ihha` in Results (Fig. 2c) versus `ihhb` in Discussion (line 66), and relies solely on single-dose pharmacological inhibitors without genetic validation.
* **Claim 2 (Keratin family genes partition into TGF-beta-activated (`krt_up`) and TGF-beta-repressed (`krt_down`) synexpression groups with evolutionary correlation in Type I keratins; Figs. 3–4): [Fragile].**  
  Bulk RNA-seq classification uses unadjusted $P < 0.05$ across multiple pairwise comparisons without a formal DESeq2 interaction model, includes non-significant genes ($P > 0.5$) in the phylogenetic narrative, and omits all methods and bootstrap support for the phylogenetic tree in Fig. 4b.
* **Claim 3 (`krt97` (`krt_up`) and `krt5` (`krt_down`) occupy mutually exclusive epidermal compartments and show conserved co-expression modules across developmental scRNA-seq; Figs. 5–8): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Spatial compartmentalization of `krt97` (finfold) and `krt5` (trunk/periderm) is well demonstrated by HCR in situ, but the scRNA-seq Pearson correlation matrix in Fig. 8 is confounded by pooling distinct epithelial and peridermal cell types and ignoring scRNA-seq sparsity.
* **Claim 4 (The `krt_up` set promotes collective epithelial migration while `krt_down` inhibits mobility to initiate TGF-beta-dependent regeneration; Abstract, Fig. 9): [Overstated].**  
  Cell movement was only compared between anatomical regions (proximal finfold vs. trunk) in untreated embryos/larvae ($n = 6$ operated photoconversion, $n = 3$ developmental time-lapse). Neither TGF-beta inhibition (`SB431542`) nor keratin perturbation was tested in the migration assays.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Absence of Functional Perturbation or `SB431542` Migration Controls for Causal Mobility Claims (Pillars 1, 5)
* **Location:** Abstract (lines 5–6), Introduction (line 18), Results (line 61, Fig. 9), and Discussion (line 66).
* **Defect:** The manuscript concludes that `krt_up` genes allow collective epithelial migration whereas `krt_down` genes inhibit mobility under TGF-beta control. However, Figure 9 only measures baseline migration differences between two anatomical regions—the proximal finfold (which naturally undergoes morphogenesis/outgrowth) and the trunk epidermis—in wild-type/untreated animals. The authors did not test whether `SB431542` blocks finfold epithelial migration in the `ubi:h2b-dendra` photoconversion assay, nor did they perturb `krt97` or `krt5` to test causality.
* **Remediation:** Either perform the `ubi:h2b-dendra` photoconversion migration assay under `SB431542` vs. `DMSO` treatment to show that TGF-beta inhibition abolishes proximal finfold cell migration, or tone down the Abstract, Introduction, Results, and Discussion to state that `krt97` and `krt5` expression domains *correlate* with regional differences in epithelial mobility.

### 3.2 Lack of a Formal $2 \times 2$ Factorial Interaction Analysis and Unadjusted $P$-Value Thresholding in Bulk RNA-seq (Pillars 3, 4)
* **Location:** Results (lines 31–44, Figs. 3–4) and Methods ("Bulk RNA-seq analysis", line 85).
* **Defect:**
  1. The bulk RNA-seq experiment is a $2 \times 2$ factorial design (`Unoperated DMSO`, `Operated DMSO`, `Unoperated SB431542`, `Operated SB431542`, $n = 3$ each). Instead of testing the interaction term (`~ operation + treatment + operation:treatment`) in DESeq2 with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control ($p_{\text{adj}} < 0.05$), the authors rely on visual scatter-plot quadrants (Fig. 3d) and regression slope comparisons (`slope = 0.9` vs. `1.0` in Fig. 3e) to define injury-specific TGF-beta targets.
  2. In Figure 4a (line 42), keratins are assigned to `krt_up` or `krt_down` based on unadjusted `P < 0.05 in at least one of the comparisons`, without multiple-testing correction. Even more concerningly, line 44 states: *"Intriguingly, the members that show regulation but a P value that is greater than 0.5 do still show a correlation in their relatedness"* (`krt1-c5` and `krt1-19d`), treating statistical noise ($P > 0.5$, or an unedited typo for $P > 0.05$) as biological regulation.
* **Remediation:** Fit the full $2 \times 2$ DESeq2 design `~ operation + drug + operation:drug`, report Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-adjusted $q$-values ($p_{\text{adj}}$) for all main and interaction effects, and restrict `krt_up` and `krt_down` membership to genes passing $p_{\text{adj}} < 0.05$.

### 3.3 Simpson's Paradox and Sparsity Bias in Pooled scRNA-seq Pearson Correlation Analysis (Pillars 3, 4)
* **Location:** Results (line 59, Fig. 8).
* **Defect:** To test whether `krt_up` and `krt_down` genes are co-regulated across development, the authors *"pooled periderm, fin epithelium and epithelium clusters from the Daniocell scRNA-seq dataset and assessed the degree of co-expression between krt up and krt down using Pearson's correlation coefficient analysis ( Fig. 8 )."* Because `krt5` is a marker of the `periderm` cluster while `krt97` is enriched in `fin epithelium` and `epithelium` clusters (as shown in Fig. 6b), pooling these three distinct cell populations induces artificial positive correlations among genes expressed in the same cell type and negative correlations between markers of different cell types (Simpson's paradox). Furthermore, standard Pearson correlation on zero-inflated single-cell UMI counts is sensitive to library-size variation and dropout.
* **Remediation:** Compute gene-gene correlations *within* each cell cluster separately (`periderm` alone, `fin epithelium` alone, and `epithelium` alone) using a compositionally/sparsity-aware metric (e.g., Spearman correlation on normalized residuals or metacells/pseudobulk across developmental timebins).

### 3.4 Underpowered Live-Imaging Sample Sizes and Extreme-Value Metric Bias (Pillar 4)
* **Location:** Results (line 61, Fig. 9) and Methods ("PhotoConversion" and "Time-lapse", lines 95–100).
* **Defect:**
  1. The developmental light-sheet time-lapse experiment (Fig. 9c–f, h) analyzed only $n = 3$ fish total, and those 3 fish were not even started at the same developmental stage (*"two started at 22hpf and one at 24hpf"*). Within each fish, only 3 nuclei in the finfold and 3 nuclei in the trunk were manually chosen and tracked, treating individual nuclei or $n = 3$ heterogeneous embryos as sufficient for parametric confidence intervals.
  2. In the photoconversion assay (Fig. 9a, b, g; $n = 6$ operated, $n = 3$ unoperated), migration was quantified using *"the maximum distance travelled by fin fold cells"* relative to the trunk. The sample maximum is an order statistic that scales upward with the number of photoconverted cells in the ROI rather than estimating mean or median collective sheet displacement.
* **Remediation:** Report median or mean displacement across all tracked photoconverted nuclei per larva (alongside the maximum) to eliminate cell-count bias, and explicitly report the animal-level vs. nucleus-level degrees of freedom and exact statistical tests in Figure 9g–h.

### 3.5 Internal Contradiction (`ihha` vs. `ihhb`), Missing Drug/Phylogenetic Methods, and Misassigned Citations (Pillar 7)
* **Location:** Introduction (line 18), Results (lines 26, 44), Discussion (line 66), and Methods (lines 78–106).
* **Defect:**
  1. **Internal Contradiction on `ihha` vs. `ihhb`:** The Introduction (line 18) and Results (line 26, Fig. 2c) state that `SB431542` treatment increases `ihha` expression, showing that TGF-beta represses `ihha`. However, the Discussion (line 66) states: *"The TGF-beta pathway then induces more expression of tgfb1a whilst inhibiting expression of ihhb in the notochord."*
  2. **Missing Pharmacological and Phylogenetic Methods:** The Methods section contains no information on how `cyclopamine` and `SB431542` were prepared or applied (working concentrations, % DMSO vehicle, embryonic stage at treatment onset, or duration of exposure). Likewise, the Methods section contains no description of how the human/zebrafish Keratin phylogenetic tree in Figure 4b was constructed (sequence accession numbers, alignment software, evolutionary model, tree inference algorithm, or branch support).
  3. **Misassigned Citations and Editorial Typos:**
     - Line 16 cites `Love, Huber, and Anders 2014` (the DESeq2 software paper) as a reference for *"transgenic fish carrying fluorescent proteins driven by krt promoters"*.
     - Line 31 cites `Choi, Schwarzkopf, and Pierce 2020` (the HCR in situ hybridization protocol) as a reference for processing bulk RNA-seq data in DESeq2.
     - Line 18: *"In this study we have propose"*; Line 80: unformatted reference superscript `standard methods49`; Line 85: incomplete reference `(Romero et al)`; Line 90: incomplete reference `(Thisse and Thisse)`.
* **Remediation:** Reconcile `ihha` vs. `ihhb` in the Discussion; add subsections to Methods detailing exact drug concentrations/timings and the phylogenetic tree inference pipeline for Fig. 4b; and fix all misassigned citations and typographical errors.
