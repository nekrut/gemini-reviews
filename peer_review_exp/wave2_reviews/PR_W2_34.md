## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript demonstrates that TBLR1 (*TBL1XR1*), a subunit of the NCoR corepressor complex, acts as a physical scaffold linking two of the most frequently mutated proteins in human neurodevelopmental disorders—ANKRD11 (mutated in KBG syndrome) and SETD5 (mutated in SETD5 syndrome)—to NCoR1 and HDAC3, forming a mammalian ortholog of the yeast Set3 histone deacetylase complex (SET3C). Using CRISPR-engineered *TBLR1*-null Flp-In T-REx 293 cells reconstituted with 24 pathogenic *TBLR1* missense variants, quantitative IP-MS ($n = 3$), co-IP western blots, and NanoLuc complementation assays, the authors show that N-terminal LisH/F-box mutations in TBLR1 selectively abolish binding to a 292-residue C-terminal domain of ANKRD11 (without affecting MeCP2 or core NCoR binding), whereas C-terminal WDR mutations disrupt binding to both MeCP2 and a newly identified MeCP2-like NCoR-interaction domain (NID; `PLKKWKS`) in SETD5. Reciprocally, five pathogenic KBG syndrome missense mutations in the C-terminus of ANKRD11 and two patient mutations adjacent to the SETD5 NID (`R837C`, `Y838C`), but not four nearby benign gnomAD variants, disrupt SET3C assembly. Engineering a single point mutation in the mouse SETD5 NID ($Setd5^{\text{W834C}}$, analogous to Rett-causing $MeCP2^{\text{R306C}}$) abolishes endogenous SETD5–NCoR1 binding in brain without altering SETD5 protein stability, causing growth retardation in heterozygotes and ~85% prenatal/perinatal lethality in homozygotes ($4/110 = 3.6\%$ vs. 25% expected), phenocopying $Setd5$-null mice. Finally, RNA-seq in mutant mouse ESCs and re-analysis of ~11,000 CRISPRi perturbations in human K562 cells show that disrupting `ANKRD11`, `SETD5`, `NCOR1`, `GPS2`, or `HDAC3` induces highly correlated transcriptional changes, preferentially upregulating highly transcribed genes. Proteomics data are deposited in PRIDE (`PXD063846`).

This is a rigorous, mechanistically compelling study that unifies multiple monogenic neurodevelopmental syndromes into a single molecular pathway through orthogonal biochemical, genetic, and transcriptomic validation. The manuscript has no fatal flaws, but several methodological and reporting issues require attention prior to publication: (1) the RNA-seq analysis in mouse ESCs pools two genetically distinct alleles ($2\times Setd5^{\text{W834C/W834C}} + 1\times Setd5^{\Delta\text{KWK}/\Delta\text{KWK}}$), does not clarify whether the 3 technical outgrowths per clone ($3 \times 3 = 9$ libraries) were collapsed prior to DESeq2 testing (which could inflate the 11,063 DEGs in $Ankrd11^{\text{yod/yod}}$), and does not explain how the *Drosophila* S2 and ERCC spike-ins (Methods line 142) were used; (2) the enhanced survival of $Setd5^{\text{W834C/W834C}}$ progeny from surviving homozygous parents is confounded by segregating `129/Ola` $\times$ `C57BL/6J` hybrid background modifiers; (3) the Mass Spectrometry Methods state that all files were searched against a 2017 *Mus musculus* database even though most IP-MS experiments were performed in human HEK293/Flp-In 293 cells; and (4) the public accession number for the mouse ESC RNA-seq dataset is missing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **TBLR1 scaffolds a mammalian SET3C-like assembly by bridging the C-terminal domain of ANKRD11 (via the TBLR1 N-terminal domain) and an MeCP2-like NID motif (`PLKKWKS`) in SETD5 (via the TBLR1 WDR domain): [Well Supported].**  
  Demonstrated across reciprocal co-IPs, quantitative mass spectrometry ($n = 3$), NanoLuc complementation, deletion mapping, biotinylated peptide pull-downs from mouse brain extracts, and loss of ANKRD11–SETD5–HDAC3 co-precipitation in *TBLR1*-null cells (Figs. 1–3, Extended Data Figs. 1–3).
* **Pathogenic patient missense variants in *TBLR1*, *ANKRD11*, and *SETD5* (but not benign gnomAD variants) disrupt SET3C assembly: [Well Supported].**  
  The panel of 24 pathogenic *TBLR1* variants (5 N-terminal, 18 WDR, plus controls), 5 C-terminal *ANKRD11* KBG variants, 2 *SETD5* NID-adjacent patient variants (`R837C`, `Y838C`), and 4 negative-control gnomAD variants (`D826G`, `L827V`, `K835R`, etc.) provides strong human genetic specificity.
* **Specific disruption of the SETD5–TBLR1 interface in vivo ($Setd5^{\text{W834C}}$) phenocopies $Setd5$ loss-of-function growth defects and embryonic lethality without destabilizing SETD5 protein: [Well Supported].**  
  Brain nuclear co-IP confirms complete loss of endogenous SETD5–NCoR1 interaction at normal SETD5 protein abundance (Fig. 4d–e), and the Mendelian deficit ($4/110$ homozygotes at weaning, $p < 0.0001$) and heterozygous growth/kidney phenotypes ($n = 24$ vs. $n = 17$ WT littermates) are well powered. However, the secondary hypothesis that surviving homozygotes carry a de novo genetic suppressor (line 57, Extended Data Fig. 4c) is confounded by mixed `129/Ola` $\times$ `C57BL/6J` background segregation.
* **Disruption of mammalian SET3C subunits causes convergent transcriptional dysregulation and preferential upregulation of highly expressed genes: [Well Supported].**  
  While the mouse ESC RNA-seq is partially confounded by pluripotency destabilization/differentiation and pooling of $2\times \text{W834C} + 1\times \Delta\text{KWK}$ clones, the authors' integration of human K562 CRISPRi Perturb-seq across ~11,000 gene knockdowns (Fig. 5d–g) provides an independent, genome-wide demonstration that `ANKRD11`, `SETD5`, `NCOR1`, `GPS2`, and `HDAC3` form the top mutually correlated transcriptional module.

## 3. Forensic Technical Audit & Constructive Remediation Plan

### Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

#### 3.1. ESC RNA-Seq Design: Pooling of Distinct *Setd5* Alleles, Outgrowth Pseudoreplication Risk, and Unreported Spike-In Normalization
In the Results (line 62) and Methods (line 142), three methodological ambiguities affect the mouse ESC RNA-seq analysis:
1. **Pooling of $Setd5^{\text{W834C/W834C}}$ and $Setd5^{\Delta\text{KWK}/\Delta\text{KWK}}$ clones:** The *Setd5* mutant group ($n = 3$ clones) combines two independent `W834C` missense clones with one `ΔKWK` (3-amino-acid in-frame deletion `K833–K835`) clone that shows a moderate reduction in SETD5 protein abundance (Extended Data Fig. 5a). Pooling distinct genetic lesions into a single group assumes transcriptional equivalence without showing separate PCA/DEG concordance between `W834C` and `ΔKWK`.
2. **Treatment of $n = 3$ clones $\times$ $n = 3$ outgrowths per clone:** Line 142 states that RNA was isolated from *"n = 3 separate clones per genotype and n = 3 separate outgrowths per clone."* The Methods do not specify whether differential expression was tested on $n = 3$ biological replicates (after collapsing technical outgrowths per clone) or on $n = 9$ libraries treating replicate outgrowths of the same clone as independent biological samples. Treating 9 outgrowth libraries as independent replicates underestimates inter-clonal variance and would explain why $Ankrd11^{\text{yod/yod}}$ ESCs yielded **11,063 DEGs** (more than half of the expressed mouse transcriptome).
3. **Unreported *Drosophila* S2 and ERCC spike-in normalization:** Line 142 explicitly documents that both *Drosophila* S2 cells (5% of cell count) and ERCC RNA spike-in controls were added to every ESC RNA-seq sample. Because the manuscript proposes that SET3C restrains highly transcribed genes (which could alter total cellular mRNA content per cell), it is essential to report whether the ESC RNA-seq data were normalized to the S2/ERCC exogenous spike-ins or via standard library-size normalization, and whether total mRNA per cell changed.

#### 3.2. Confounding of Putative "Genetic Suppression" in Surviving $Setd5^{\text{W834C/W834C}}$ Mice by Mixed `129/Ola` $\times$ `C57BL/6J` Strain Background
In line 57 and Extended Data Fig. 4c, crossing rare surviving $Setd5^{\text{W834C/W834C}}$ mice with $Setd5^{\text{W834C/+}}$ mice yielded a much higher proportion of viable homozygous offspring than the initial $Setd5^{\text{W834C/+}} \times Setd5^{\text{W834C/+}}$ intercross ($3.6\%$), leading the authors to suggest that survivors *"might have been rescued by a secondary genetic change."* However, according to the Methods (line 108), $Setd5^{\text{W834C/+}}$ mice were generated by injecting **`129/Ola` (`E14TG2a`) ESCs into `C57BL/6J` blastocysts** and mating chimeras to `C57BL/6J` without 10-generation congenic backcrossing. In mouse genetics, embryonic lethality of chromatin regulator mutants (including *Setd5*, *Mecp2*, and *Ncor1*) is notoriously sensitive to `129` vs. `C57BL/6J` modifier alleles. Rare F2/F3 homozygotes that survive the developmental bottleneck are strongly enriched for permissive `129/Ola` background alleles across the genome; breeding those survivors naturally transmits their permissive polygenic background to their progeny without requiring a de novo suppressor mutation.

#### 3.3. Species Database Mismatch in Mass Spectrometry Methods (`Mus musculus` Database Listed for Human HEK293 Experiments)
In the Methods ("Mass spectrometry", lines 126–128), both the MaxQuant (DDA) and DIA-NN (DIA) pipelines state that raw files were searched exclusively against the **`Mus musculus` protein database (released in July 2017)**. However, all IP-MS experiments in Fig. 1b, Fig. 2c, Fig. 3c, and Extended Data Fig. 3a were performed in **human** `Flp-In T-REx 293` or `HEK293` cells pulling down endogenous human NCoR complex subunits, ANKRD11, and SETD5. Searching human proteomic samples against a mouse reference proteome would fail to quantify any peptide containing human-specific amino acid substitutions. Either the human UniProt database used for the HEK293 experiments was omitted from the Methods text, or the human datasets were inadvertently searched against the mouse FASTA.

### Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Missing GEO / ENA / ArrayExpress Accession for ESC RNA-Seq Data:** While the PRIDE accession (`PXD063846`) is provided for the mass spectrometry data (line 130), no public repository accession is provided for the mouse ESC RNA-seq dataset (`Setd5` and `Ankrd11` mutant ESCs, Fig. 5a–c).
2. **Inconsistent Amino Acid Numbering for the ANKRD11 Yoda Mutation (`E2522K` vs. `E2523K`):** The main text (lines 40 and 62) refers to the KBG/Yoda mutation as **`E2522K`**, whereas the Methods (line 97) refers to the engineered ESC line as **`ANKRD11 E2523K/E2523K`**. The human vs. mouse reference isoform numbering should be explicitly cross-referenced.
3. **Mass Tolerance Unit Typo:** Line 126 states *"for the main search peptide tolerance was set to 4.5 pm"* instead of `4.5 ppm`.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Clarify Clonal Aggregation and Spike-In Normalization in the ESC RNA-Seq Pipeline
* **WHY:** Treating 3 outgrowths $\times$ 3 clones ($n = 9$) as independent biological replicates in DESeq2 causes pseudoreplication, and omitting the results of the S2/ERCC spike-ins leaves a gap in testing global transcriptional amplification.
* **WHAT:** Confirm that outgrowth counts were collapsed per clone ($n = 3$ independent clones per genotype) or modeled with clone as a random effect, show that the two $Setd5^{\text{W834C/W834C}}$ clones cluster with the $Setd5^{\Delta\text{KWK}/\Delta\text{KWK}}$ clone, and report the S2/ERCC spike-in normalization results.
* **HOW:** Using the existing count matrices, run `DESeq2::collapseReplicates` by clone ID ($n = 3$ vs. $n = 3$) and report the resulting DEG counts and log2 fold-change correlation ($r$) between $Setd5^{\text{W834C/W834C}}$ (2 clones), $Setd5^{\Delta\text{KWK}/\Delta\text{KWK}}$ (1 clone), and $Ankrd11^{\text{yod/yod}}$ (3 clones). Additionally, compute size factors from the *Drosophila* S2 and ERCC spike-in reads (Methods line 142) to test whether highly expressed genes are upregulated in absolute per-cell terms as well as relative terms.

#### 2. Acknowledge Segregating `129/Ola` $\times$ `C57BL/6J` Background Modifiers in $Setd5^{\text{W834C/W834C}}$ Survival
* **WHY:** Because the line was derived from `129/Ola` ESCs injected into `C57BL/6J` blastocysts, selection for permissive `129/Ola` background alleles in F2/F3 survivors is a more parsimonious explanation for increased homozygous survival in subsequent crosses than a de novo secondary suppressor mutation.
* **WHAT & HOW:** In the Results (line 57) and Methods (line 108), state the backcross generation (N-number) of the mice used in Fig. 4b and Extended Data Fig. 4c, and revise line 57 to note that enriched survival in progeny of homozygous escapers likely reflects selection for permissive `129/Ola` vs. `C57BL/6J` strain background modifier alleles (or a secondary genetic modifier).

#### 3. Deposit ESC RNA-Seq Data and Correct the Proteomics Database and Textual Discrepancies
* **WHY:** Public availability of the RNA-seq dataset and accurate database documentation are required for full reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * Deposit the raw FASTQ files and processed count tables for the mouse ESC RNA-seq experiment in NCBI GEO or ArrayExpress and add the accession number to the Data Availability / Methods section alongside `PXD063846`.
  * In the Mass Spectrometry Methods (lines 126–128), specify the exact human UniProt reference proteome (and release date) used to search the human `Flp-In T-REx 293` and `HEK293` IP-MS datasets (or, if they were searched against mouse UniProt, re-search them against human UniProt).
  * Harmonize `E2522K` (lines 40, 62) vs. `E2523K` (line 97) by stating the corresponding human (`NP_037407.4`) and mouse (`NP_001074841.1`) residue numbers, and fix `4.5 pm` to `4.5 ppm` on line 126.
