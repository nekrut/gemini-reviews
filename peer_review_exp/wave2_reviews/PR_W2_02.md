## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript investigates the molecular recognition mechanism of the barley and wheat tandem kinase proteins (TKPs) Rmo2 and Rwt7 toward *Magnaporthe oryzae* (blast fungus) effectors PBY2 and PWT7, and tests whether structure-guided engineering of their N-terminal integrated heavy metal-associated (HMA) domains can expand effector recognition specificity.

* **Claim 1:** The integrated HMA domains of Rmo2 ($\text{Rmo2}^{\text{HMA}}$) and Rwt7 ($\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA}}$) directly and specifically bind their cognate effectors PBY2 ($K_D = 53.5\text{ nM}$) and PWT7 ($K_D = 2.92\text{ nM}$) in vitro, utilizing spatially distinct interaction interfaces resolved by X-ray crystallography at $2.2\text{ \AA}$ and $1.6\text{ \AA}$ resolution. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 2:** Structure-guided single amino-acid substitutions at the binding interfaces ($\text{PBY2}^{\text{E42R}}$ and $\text{PWT7}^{\text{N39R}}$) abolish HMA–effector binding in vitro, disrupt full-length receptor binding in *Nicotiana benthamiana* split GAL4 RUBY assays, and abrogate Rmo2- and Rwt7-mediated disease resistance in barley and wheat infection assays with complemented *M. oryzae* strains. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 3:** Structure-guided resurfacing of $\text{Rmo2}^{\text{HMA}}$ ($\text{Rmo2}^{\text{HMA+}}$) and $\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA}}$ ($\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA+}}$) confers nanomolar dual-binding affinity to both PBY2 and PWT7 in vitro (including a resolved tripartite $\text{Rmo2}^{\text{HMA+}}/\text{PBY2}/\text{PWT7}$ co-crystal structure) and in planta split GAL4 RUBY assays. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 4:** Incorporating the resurfaced $\text{Rmo2}^{\text{HMA+}}$ domain into full-length Rmo2 ($\text{Rmo2+}$) expands immune recognition to trigger cell death in response to both PBY2 and PWT7 in wheat protoplast assays, whereas engineered $\text{Rwt7+}$ fails to translate gain of PBY2 binding into an immune cell-death response. `[Moderately Supported]`

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Pseudoreplication and Omission of Batch Random Effects in In Planta and Protoplast ANOVAs
In the split GAL4 RUBY assays (Figs. 2A–B and 4A–D) and the wheat protoplast luciferase assays (Fig. 4E), the experimental design involves nested hierarchical replication:
* For split GAL4 RUBY (Fig. 2A–B, line 87; Fig. 4A–D, line 115): *"Each experiment consisted of 6 or 12 leaf replicates [or 24 leaf replicates in Fig. 4], performed three times independently. Different letters indicate significant differences, as determined by one-way ANOVA and post-hoc Tukey's honestly significant difference tests at $P < 0.05$."*
* For wheat protoplasts (Fig. 4E, line 115): *"Experiments consisting of six technical replicates (replicate reactions from the same batch of protoplasts) were performed three times independently. Different letters indicate significant differences, as determined by one-way ANOVA and post hoc Tukey's honestly significant difference tests at $P < 0.05$."*

If the 18 protoplast technical reactions ($6 \times 3$) or 72 leaf discs ($24 \times 3$) were pooled directly into a standard one-way ANOVA without blocking by independent experiment or treating the biological batch ($N = 3$) as the unit of replication, the residual degrees of freedom are artificially inflated by 6- to 24-fold (pseudoreplication). Technical replicates from the same protoplast isolation batch share correlated baseline viability and transfection efficiency. A linear mixed-effects model with batch as a random intercept (`Activity ~ Construct * Effector + (1 | Batch)`) or a randomized block ANOVA on the batch means ($N = 3$) is statistically required.

### 2.2 Ratio Normalization to Mutant Controls in the Protoplast Cell-Death Assay
In Fig. 4E (line 115 and line 175), relative luciferase activity in wheat protoplasts was normalized by dividing each condition by the luminescence obtained with the non-interacting effector mutant ($\text{PBY2}^{\text{E42R}}$ for Rmo2/Rmo2+, and $\text{PWT7}^{\text{N39R}}$ for Rwt7/Rwt7+).
1. **Baseline autoactivity confounding:** As the authors note in line 120 (referencing fig. S13), *"The engineered Rmo2+ and Rwt7+ receptors displayed generally lower luciferase activity compared to wild-type."* Normalizing $\text{Rmo2+}/\text{PBY2}$ and $\text{Rmo2+}/\text{PWT7}$ against $\text{Rmo2+}/\text{PBY2}^{\text{E42R}}$ compresses the dynamic range of the assay when baseline $\text{Rmo2+}$ expression already depresses luciferase activity (either via mild autoactivity or protein toxicity).
2. **Degenerate variance in the control group:** Normalizing each replicate or batch against the mutant control forces the reference control group to have a mean of 1.0 and distorted variance, violating the homoscedasticity assumption of one-way ANOVA and Tukey's HSD test when the reference condition is included in the multiple-comparison family.
3. **Lack of protein accumulation controls in wheat protoplasts:** While immunoblots are provided for *N. benthamiana* infiltrations (fig. S7), no immunoblot verification of WT vs. engineered receptor ($\text{Rmo2}, \text{Rmo2+}, \text{Rwt7}, \text{Rwt7+}$) and effector protein levels is reported in transfected wheat protoplasts. Thus, whether $\text{Rwt7+}$ failed to respond to PBY2 due to lower affinity, steric orientation, or reduced full-length $\text{Rwt7+}$ stability in cereal protoplasts remains unresolved.

### 2.3 Absence of Stable In Planta Pathogen Challenge for Resurfaced Receptors
While wild-type Rmo2 and Rwt7 recognition of wild-type and point-mutant effectors is rigorously validated in whole-plant *M. oryzae* spray inoculation assays on barley and wheat cultivars (Fig. 2C–D), the resurfaced dual-specificity receptor $\text{Rmo2+}$ is only functionally tested in transient wheat protoplast luciferase assays (Fig. 4E). Because $\text{Rmo2+}$ shows reduced baseline luciferase activity in protoplasts (fig. S13), demonstrating that $\text{Rmo2+}$ confers genuine resistance to *M. oryzae* expressing PWT7 without deleterious autoimmune lesion mimicry or yield penalty in stable transgenic barley or wheat remains a key unverified step for the translational claim of crop resistance engineering.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Global ITC fitting constraints:** In the ITC Methods (line 170), the authors state that a global 1:1 fit was performed in AFFINImeter across three replicates with $K_a$, $\Delta H$, and heat of dilution shared, while *"the concentration ratio of the HMA domain in the cell was fixed to 1."* It should be clarified whether the active fraction / stoichiometry parameter ($N$) of the titrant in the syringe was floated per replicate to account for concentration errors, and the individual $K_D$, $\Delta H$, $-T\Delta S$, and $N$ values with $95\%$ confidence intervals should be tabulated in the main text for all WT and resurfaced pairs (particularly since Discussion line 129 argues that $\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA+}}$ bound PBY2 with lower affinity than native pairs, but the exact $K_D$ values for $\text{Rmo2}^{\text{HMA+}}$ and $\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA+}}$ from Fig. 3B,D are not stated in the text).
2. **Surrogate chimeric backbone in RUBY assays:** Because full-length $\text{Rwt7-GAL4}$ failed to produce betalain in the split GAL4 RUBY assay (fig. S6), the authors tested $\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA}}$ and $\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA+}}$ in planta using a chimeric $\text{Rmo2}^{\text{Rwt7-HMA}}\text{-GAL4}$ backbone (Figs. 2B, 4C–D). While well justified, the text in line 109 should explicitly state that in planta binding was demonstrated for the chimeric $\text{Rmo2}^{\text{Rwt7-HMA+}}$ receptor rather than native full-length $\text{Rwt7+}$.
3. **Duplicate text in Infection Assay Methods:** Lines 185 contains a verbatim duplicated sentence defining the 0–5 lesion rating scale (*"The size of lesions was rated on a progressive scale from 0 to 5..."* followed two sentences later by *"Lesion size scores are rated on six progressive grades as follows: 0, no visible infection..."*).

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Re-analyze RUBY and protoplast assays with mixed-effects models:** Fit a linear mixed-effects model (`lme4` / `emmeans`) treating experimental batch ($N = 3$) as a random effect (or perform ANOVA on the 3 independent biological experiment means) for Figs. 2A–B and 4B, D, E. For the protoplast luciferase data (Fig. 4E), perform the statistical test on log-transformed unnormalized (or empty-vector normalized) luminescence values so that baseline differences between WT and engineered receptors (`Rmo2` vs. `Rmo2+`, `Rwt7` vs. `Rwt7+`) and effector-induced reductions are evaluated simultaneously without ratio-normalization distortion.
2. **Report complete thermodynamic parameters in the main text:** Add a summary table in the main text (or expand Fig. 1 / Fig. 3 captions) listing exact $K_D$, $\Delta G$, $\Delta H$, $-T\Delta S$, and stoichiometry $n$ ($\pm\text{SD}$ or $95\%$ CI) for all ITC titrations ($\text{Rmo2}^{\text{HMA}}$, $\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA}}$, $\text{Rmo2}^{\text{HMA+}}$, and $\text{Rwt7}^{\text{HMA+}}$ with WT and mutant PBY2/PWT7).
3. **Add protein expression verification in wheat protoplasts:** Include an immunoblot (or NanoLuc/split-tag quantification) of $\text{Rmo2}, \text{Rmo2+}, \text{Rwt7}$, and $\text{Rwt7+}$ in transfected Kronos wheat protoplasts to confirm whether $\text{Rwt7+}$ accumulates to comparable levels as $\text{Rwt7}$ and $\text{Rmo2+}$.
4. **Temper translational claims and remove duplicated Methods text:** Explicitly note in the Abstract/Discussion that functional immune signaling of $\text{Rmo2+}$ was established in cereal protoplasts and that stable transgenic cereal pathology assays will be required to assess whole-plant resistance and potential autoactivity costs. Remove the duplicated lesion-scoring sentence in the Materials and Methods (line 185).
