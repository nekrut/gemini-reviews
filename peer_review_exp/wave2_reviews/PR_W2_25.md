## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study investigates chromosome-level macro-synteny (12 annotated chromosome-level genomes via MCScanX), gene-level micro-synteny (25 chromosome-level genomes via BLAST), gene duplication (53 anuran genomes and raw reads), tissue expression (14 species with RNA-seq, 8 with gonadal data), and codon substitution rates ($d_N/d_S = \omega$ via PAML `codeml` free-ratio, branch, and branch-site models) for four candidate sex-determining genes (`Dmrt1`, `Foxl2`, `Sox3`, and `Bod1l`). The authors report strong conservation of chromosome-level synteny across >200 Myr of anuran evolution, rare complete or partial duplications of `Dmrt1` (and none for `Foxl2`, `Sox3`, or `Bod1l`), no translocations of the four genes, strong purifying selection ($\omega \ll 1$) on single-copy orthologs regardless of sex-chromosome linkage, and elevated $\omega$ on duplicated `Dmrt1` paralogs (`DmW` in *Xenopus laevis* and a duplicate in *Pelodytes ibericus*). From these findings, the authors conclude that master sex-determining genes in anurans rarely evolve via gene duplication or translocation, and instead evolve primarily via allelic diversification, while duplicated copies evolve under positive selection.

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision / Reject (Flawed / Overstated).**  
While the macro- and micro-synteny curation across 53 amphibian genomes is a useful comparative resource, the manuscript's central evolutionary conclusions are undermined by three major methodological and logical flaws:
1. **Fundamental Ascertainment Bias in Testing Y/W Gene Duplication and Translocation Using Homogametic (`XX` / `ZZ`) Reference Genomes:** A sex-specific master sex-determining duplicate (analogous to Y-linked `DmY` in medaka or W-linked `DmW` in *X. laevis*) or a sex-determining translocation onto a Y or W chromosome exists *only* in the heterogametic sex (`XY` or `ZW`). As the authors briefly acknowledge late in the Discussion (line 19, part 2), most chromosome-level frog assemblies were generated from the homogametic sex (`XX` females or `ZZ` males) and lack Y or W chromosomes entirely. Concluding that anuran master sex-determining genes rarely arise via duplication or translocation by querying assemblies that lack the Y/W sex chromosome is a severe ascertainment fallacy.
2. **Conflation of Relaxed Purifying Selection ($\omega = 0.47\text{–}0.92 < 1$) with Positive Selection ($\omega > 1$):** For the two `Dmrt1` duplicates highlighted in the study (`DmW` in *X. laevis* with $\omega = 0.47$, and the *Pelodytes ibericus* duplicate with $\omega = 0.92$), $\omega$ is strictly less than 1.0, and the DM-domain branch-site tests are explicitly noted as *not statistically significant* (`Table S12D`; Results line 10, part 2). A branch model where $\omega_{\text{fg}} \in (0.47, 0.92) > \omega_{\text{bg}} \approx 0.05$ demonstrates **relaxed purifying selection** (or incipient pseudogenization in *P. ibericus*, where no expression or raw read data exist), not positive selection. Moreover, the Discussion (line 25, part 2) falsely states that *"more mutations contributed to dN than dS"* in these two duplicates, directly contradicting their reported $\omega < 1$.
3. **Uncorrected Multiple Hypothesis Testing in PAML Scans and Non-Phylogenetic GLM Regression:** Running >50 separate foreground branch tests per gene without false discovery rate (FDR) correction, and fitting a non-phylogenetic Gamma GLM (`Glm: ω ~ chromosome + duplication`) and Spearman correlation across tip branches of a tree without Phylogenetic Generalized Least Squares (PGLS)—where the `duplication` category contains only $n = 2$ sequences—violates statistical independence.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1 (Chromosome-level macro-synteny and local micro-synteny around `Dmrt1`, `Foxl2`, `Sox3`, and `Bod1l` are highly conserved across >200 Myr of anuran evolution; Figs. 1, 3, 4): [Well Supported].**  
  Demonstrated across 12 annotated chromosome-level assemblies with MCScanX and 25 chromosome-level assemblies via flanking-gene BLAST synteny.
* **Claim 2 (Master sex-determining genes in anurans rarely arise via gene duplication and sex chromosome turnover is not driven by gene translocation; Abstract, Fig. 2): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Because the vast majority of analyzed assemblies are from the homogametic sex (`XX` or `ZZ`) or lack sex-specific Y/W haplotypes, sex-specific Y- or W-linked gene duplications and translocations are unobservable in the input data. Furthermore, in 45 of the 53 species, the identity of the actual master sex-determining gene is unknown; showing that four candidate genes did not translocate in their autosomal/X/Z copies does not prove that sex chromosome turnover lacks translocations.
* **Claim 3 (`Dmrt1` duplicates evolve under positive selection whereas single-copy sex-linked and autosomal `Dmrt1`, `Foxl2`, and `Sox3` evolve under strong purifying selection; Abstract, Fig. 5): [Overstated].**  
  Single-copy purifying selection ($\omega \approx 0.05$) is well supported, but the claim of positive selection on duplicates mistakes relaxed purifying selection ($\omega = 0.47$ and $\omega = 0.92$, both $< 1.0$) and statistically non-significant branch-site tests for adaptive evolution.
* **Claim 4 (Ranidae-specific DM-domain substitutions `L30M`, `A34S`, `P44L`, `D56E` represent lineage-specific functional tuning; Fig. 6): [Fragile].**  
  The sequence alignment accurately identifies clade-specific substitutions in Ranidae, but codon selection models show no positive selection at these sites, and no structural modeling or DNA-binding assays are presented.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Homogametic (`XX`/`ZZ`) Assembly Ascertainment Bias Invalidates Negative Claims on Y/W Duplication and Translocation (Pillars 1, 2, 6)
* **Location:** Abstract (line 5), Results (lines 89–110), Discussion (lines 19–23 of part 2), and Conclusions (line 32 of part 2).
* **Defect:** When a master sex-determining gene evolves via duplication onto a proto-Y or proto-W chromosome (e.g., `DmY` in *Oryzias latipes* or `DmW` in *Xenopus laevis*) or via translocation onto a new Y/W sex chromosome (e.g., `sdY` in salmonids), the ancestral copy remains at its conserved syntenic autosomal/X/Z locus while the novel duplicate/translocated copy exists **only in heterogametic (`XY` or `ZW`) individuals**. Because most assemblies in Table S1 were generated from homogametic (`XX` or `ZZ`) individuals—or collapsed into a single haploid pseudoreference ignoring short hemizigous Y/W contigs—searching these assemblies will always recover the conserved X/Z/autosomal syntenic copy and miss Y/W-specific duplications or translocations.
* **Remediation:** Explicitly stratify the 53 species in Table S1 and Figure 2 by whether the sequenced individual was the heterogametic sex (`XY` male / `ZW` female) vs. the homogametic sex (`XX` female / `ZZ` male) or unknown sex. Restrict claims about the absence of Y/W-specific duplications and translocations strictly to the subset of species where heterogametic (`XY` or `ZW`) genomes and raw reads were analyzed, and temper the Abstract and Conclusions accordingly.

### 3.2 Misclassification of $\omega < 1$ (`0.47` and `0.92`) as Positive Selection and Internal Contradiction on $d_N$ vs. $d_S$ (Pillars 3, 4)
* **Location:** Abstract (line 5), Results (lines 115–121; line 10 of part 2), Discussion (line 25 of part 2), and Conclusions (line 32 of part 2).
* **Defect:**
  1. In Results (lines 115–117), `DmW` in *X. laevis* has $\omega = 0.47$ and the duplicated `Dmrt1` in *Pelodytes ibericus* has $\omega = 0.92$. Both values are less than $1.0$. In PAML `codeml`, a significant likelihood ratio test between `M0` (one $\omega$) and a two-ratio branch model ($\omega_{\text{fg}} \neq \omega_{\text{bg}}$) when $\omega_{\text{bg}} = 0.05$ and $\omega_{\text{fg}} \in \{0.47, 0.92\}$ only proves that $\omega_{\text{fg}} > \omega_{\text{bg}}$ (**relaxation of purifying selection**). To demonstrate positive selection at the branch level, $\omega_{\text{fg}}$ must exceed $1.0$ and significantly outperform the fixed neutral model ($\omega_{\text{fg}} = 1.0$).
  2. Furthermore, in *Pelodytes ibericus*, the authors note (line 93) that no raw genomic reads and no transcriptomic data exist to confirm whether the duplicated copy on chromosome 10 is even expressed or is an unexpressed pseudogene drifting neutrally ($\omega = 0.92 \approx 1.0$).
  3. In Results (line 10 of part 2), the authors admit that the branch-site tests on the DM domain for `DmW` and *P. ibericus* *"are not statistically significant ( Table S12D )"*, yet the Discussion (line 25 of part 2) states: *"Interestingly, the only two cases where genes tended towards positive selection and more mutations contributed to d N than d S were the duplicated copy of Dmrt1 in X. laevis and Pelodytes ibericus."* Since $\omega = d_N / d_S = 0.47$ and $0.92$, $d_N$ is strictly *less* than $d_S$, making the statement *"more mutations contributed to dN than dS"* mathematically false.
* **Remediation:** Replace claims of "positive selection" on `Dmrt1` duplicates in the Abstract, Results, Discussion, and Conclusions with "relaxed purifying selection (or neutral drift)". Remove the incorrect statement that $d_N > d_S$ in `DmW` and *P. ibericus*, and clearly distinguish statistically significant branch-site results (after multiple-testing correction) from non-significant trends.

### 3.3 Uncorrected Multiple Testing in PAML Branch Scans and Non-Phylogenetic GLM (Pillar 4)
* **Location:** Materials and Methods (lines 67–69) and Results (lines 117–123, Fig. 5).
* **Defect:**
  1. The authors performed >50 separate foreground branch tests and branch-site tests across all branches of the 54-species tree for `Dmrt1`, `Foxl2`, and `Sox3` without applying Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction to the likelihood ratio test $p$-values (Tables S12–S14), and without addressing extreme $d_S \approx 0$ division artifacts (e.g., *Oophaga pumilio* $d_S = 0.000004$ and *Rana septentrionalis* $d_S = 0.000007$).
  2. In Results (line 121), the authors fit an ordinary Generalized Linear Model (`Glm: ω ~ chromosome + duplication, family = gamma`) and Spearman correlation ($\rho = 0.74$) across species/branches without accounting for shared evolutionary history (phylogenetic autocorrelation) and with only $n = 2$ observations in the `duplication = TRUE` class (`DmW` and *P. ibericus*).
* **Remediation:** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across all tested branches in Tables S12–S14; filter out terminal branches with $d_S < 0.001$ before computing $\omega$ ratios in GLM/correlation analyses; and replace the non-phylogenetic GLM with Phylogenetic Generalized Least Squares (PGLS) or a permutation test conditioned on the tree.

### 3.4 Methodological Contradictions (`HKY` vs. `GTR`, `Bod1l` / `E2F1` Omissions) and Cross-Species `STAR` Mapping Flaw (Pillars 3, 7)
* **Location:** Materials and Methods (lines 39, 57, 62, 69) and Results (line 115).
* **Defect:**
  1. **Ignored Model Selection (line 39):** Methods states that ModelTest-NG selected `HKY+I+G4` by BIC, but the very next sentence states that RAxML-NG was run using `GTR+I+G4`.
  2. **Contradictory Statements on `Bod1l` and `E2F1`:** Line 57 states `Bod1l` was not analyzed for duplications; line 62 states `Bod1l1` was analyzed for micro-synteny; line 115 states `Bod1l` was analyzed for molecular evolution ($d_N/d_S$), yet no `Bod1l` $d_N/d_S$ results exist in the text or tables. Conversely, line 69 states that `E2F1` was analyzed as a control transcription factor across 12 species, but `E2F1` is never mentioned again in the Results or Discussion.
  3. **Cross-Species RNA-seq Mapping with Default `STAR` Parameters (line 57):** Mapping RNA-seq reads from unannotated frog species to *"the annotated genomes of closely related species... using STAR 81 with default parameters"* is bioinformatically unsound because `STAR` default mismatch thresholds (`--outFilterMismatchNmax 10`) reject reads from divergent species, causing false-negative expression calls (which may explain why `Dmrt1` expression was undetected in *Pelobates cultripes* tadpoles/adults; line 93).
  4. **Embargoed Code Repository:** The GitHub link (`[GITHUB_REPO_REDACTED]`) is not public ("will make public upon paper acceptance").
* **Remediation:** Reconcile the `HKY+I+G4` vs. `GTR+I+G4` discrepancy; remove `Bod1l` from line 115 and either report the 12-species `E2F1` control selection results or remove `E2F1` from Methods; verify RNA-seq expression in unannotated species using de novo transcriptome assembly or translated alignment (`tblastn`/`minimap2`) rather than cross-species default `STAR`; and make the GitHub repository publicly accessible.
