## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript develops a population pharmacokinetic (popPK) model for oral pretomanid (200 mg daily as part of BPaL, BPaLM, or BPaLC regimens) using 952 plasma observations from 94 patients with rifampicin-resistant tuberculosis (RR-TB) enrolled in the `TB-PRACTECAL` PK-PD substudy across South Africa and Belarus, alongside baseline MGIT minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) distributions from 478 trial participants. Using `nlmixr2` (FOCE-I), the authors fit a one-compartment model with first-order absorption ($k_a = 0.316\text{ h}^{-1}$) and elimination ($CL/F = 3.10\text{ L/h}$, $V/F = 102\text{ L}$) scaled allometrically by fat-free mass (FFM). They then perform 2,000-subject Monte Carlo simulations assuming 85%–95% plasma protein binding to evaluate probability of target attainment (PTA) against preclinical targets ($\%fT > \text{MIC}$ of 22%, 48%, and 77%; and $f\text{AUC}_{0-24}/\text{MIC} \ge 167$).

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision (Mixed / Salvageable).**  
Characterizing longitudinal pretomanid exposures in RR-TB patients co-treated with modern BPaL/M/C regimens (including 41.5% HIV-positive patients on integrase inhibitors) and pairing them with 478 clinical MGIT MICs provides clinically useful pharmacometric data. However, the modeling, numerical consistency, and PK-PD interpretation contain five major flaws that must be resolved:
1. **Mathematical Impossibility in Table 3 ($\max C_{\text{trough}} > \max C_{\max}$) and Arithmetic Contradiction in Reported PTA at the Median MIC:**
   - In Table 3 (line 86), the model-predicted range for steady-state trough concentration $C_{\text{trough}}$ is reported as `65–7702 µg/L`, whereas the range for maximum inter-dose concentration $C_{\max}$ is `1382–6351 µg/L`. Because $C_{\text{trough}} \le C_{\max}$ for every individual within any dosing interval, it is mathematically impossible for the maximum $C_{\text{trough}}$ (`7,702 µg/L`) to exceed the maximum $C_{\max}$ (`6,351 µg/L`).
   - In Results line 109, the authors state that at the median baseline MIC of $0.125\text{ mg/L}$ (assuming 85% protein binding, $f_u = 0.15$), *"only 50% would have reached the fAUC/MIC target of 167"*. However, attaining $f\text{AUC}_{0-24}/\text{MIC} \ge 167$ at $\text{MIC} = 0.125\text{ mg/L}$ and $f_u = 0.15$ requires a total $\text{AUC}_{0-24} = 167 \times 0.125 / 0.15 = 139.2\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L}$ (`139,167 µg·h/L`), which exceeds the **maximum** $\text{AUC}_{0-24}$ across all 94 patients in Table 3 (`138,179 µg·h/L`) and corresponds to $<1\%$ PTA (not 50% PTA, which occurs at $\text{MIC} = 0.063\text{ mg/L}$, where required $\text{AUC}_{0-24} = 70.1\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L}$ near the cohort median of `64,000 µg·h/L`). Furthermore, `99.67%` (line 109) is impossible as a simple fraction of $n = 2,000$ Monte Carlo simulations (whose resolution is $0.05\%$).
2. **Unmodeled Non-Adherence in On-Treatment BLQ Samples (`M1` Deletion Bias) and Inflated Additive Residual Error:** The assay LLOQ is $7\text{ ng/mL}$ ($0.007\text{ mg/L}$), more than 250-fold below the median steady-state trough ($1,789\text{ ng/mL}$) of a daily drug with $t_{1/2} \approx 22.8\text{ h}$. Observing 9.5% of on-treatment samples below $7\text{ ng/mL}$ (plus low outliers noted in Fig. 1 and troughs down to $65\text{ ng/mL}$) indicates unrecorded missed doses / non-adherence prior to sparse clinic visits. Simply discarding on-treatment BLQ samples via the `M1` method while retaining near-BLQ non-adherent samples under nominal daily dosing assumptions biases population parameters and inflates the additive residual error to `0.368 mg/L` ($368\text{ ng/mL}$—52 times the LLOQ!).
3. **Sparse Absorption Sampling and High $\eta$-Shrinkage on $V/F$ (`36.7%`) Invalidating Empirical Bayes Covariate Screening:** Only a single post-dose time point (`2 h` on Day 1) was collected before typical pretomanid $T_{\max}$ ($\sim 4.5\text{ h}$), resulting in high uncertainty on $k_a$ (`19.6% RSE`), inability to estimate inter-individual variability on $k_a$, and high $\eta$-shrinkage on $V/F$ (`36.7%`). Screening covariates via correlation matrices against individual $\eta$ estimates (line 139) is unreliable when $\eta$-shrinkage exceeds 30%.
4. **Asymmetric PK-PD Target Comparison and Circular Claim of "Confirming Clinical Relevance":** The authors compare $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ targets for **1.0 to 1.59 $\text{log}_{10}$ kill** (48% and 77%) against an $f\text{AUC}/\text{MIC}$ target (`167`) defined for **$>2.0\text{ log}_{10}$ kill** (line 145), and perform **no clinical outcome or bacteriological exposure–response analysis** in the trial cohort. Concluding that the study "confirmed the clinical relevance of the time dependent index and target" because $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ was achieved in patients treated with a potent 3–4 drug combination regimen (`BPaL/M/C`) is an unsupported causal leap.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1 (A one-compartment model with first-order absorption/elimination and FFM allometric scaling describes pretomanid PK with $CL/F = 3.10\text{ L/h}$ and no significant effect of HIV status or companion drugs moxifloxacin/clofazimine; Tables 1–2, Figs. 1–2): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Clearance (`3.10 L/h`, 3.35% RSE, 9.65% shrinkage) is well estimated and consistent with prior studies, but sparse absorption sampling causes 36.7% $\eta$-shrinkage on $V/F$, the reference FFM for allometric scaling is omitted from Table 2, and unrecorded non-adherence (9.5% on-treatment BLQ + low outliers) inflates the additive residual error ($368\text{ ng/mL}$ vs. $7\text{ ng/mL}$ LLOQ).
* **Claim 2 (All 478 baseline *M. tuberculosis* isolates in TB-PRACTECAL had pretomanid MGIT MICs at or below the provisional critical concentration of $1.0\text{ mg/L}$, with a median of $0.125\text{ mg/L}$; Fig. 3): [Well Supported].**  
  Directly supported by prospective MGIT susceptibility testing across the trial sites.
* **Claim 3 (At the baseline median MIC of $0.125\text{ mg/L}$, 99.67% of patients exceed the 77% $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ target while 50% reach the $167\ f\text{AUC}_{0-24}/\text{MIC}$ target; Results line 109, Fig. 4): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  While $>99\%$ attainment of $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ at $0.125\text{ mg/L}$ is consistent with the median trough ($1.79\text{ mg/L}$ total $\Rightarrow 0.27\text{ mg/L}$ free $> 0.125\text{ mg/L}$), the claim of "50%" attainment for $f\text{AUC}_{0-24}/\text{MIC} \ge 167$ at $\text{MIC} = 0.125\text{ mg/L}$ is mathematically impossible given median $\text{AUC}_{0-24} = 64\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L}$ ($\text{median } f\text{AUC}/\text{MIC} = 76.8$; required total $\text{AUC}_{0-24} = 139.2\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L} > \text{cohort max}$).
* **Claim 4 (The study confirms the clinical relevance of the $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ index and target over the $167\ f\text{AUC}/\text{MIC}$ target; Abstract, Discussion line 126): [Overstated].**  
  No exposure–efficacy analysis (e.g., time to sputum culture conversion or unfavorable clinical outcome vs. individual $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ or $f\text{AUC}/\text{MIC}$) was performed, the two preclinical targets correspond to different bactericidal effect sizes ($1.59\text{ log}_{10}$ vs. $>2\text{ log}_{10}$ kill), and all patients simultaneously received bedaquiline and linezolid ($\pm$ moxifloxacin/clofazimine).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Mathematical Contradictions in Table 3 ($\max C_{\text{trough}} > \max C_{\max}$) and Line 109 PTA Arithmetic (Pillars 3, 4, 7)
* **Location:** Abstract (line 5), Results (lines 86–94, Table 3; lines 102–109, Fig. 4).
* **Defect:**
  1. **Impossible $C_{\text{trough}}$ vs. $C_{\max}$ Range in Table 3:** Table 3 reports secondary parameters derived from the model across the 94 participants: median $C_{\text{trough}} = 2000\ \mu\text{g/L}$ (`Range: 65–7702`) and median $C_{\max} = 3,000\ \mu\text{g/L}$ (`Range: 1382–6351`). For any individual at steady state, $C_{\text{trough}}$ cannot exceed $C_{\max}$, so $\max_i(C_{\text{trough},i})$ across the population cannot be larger than $\max_i(C_{\max,i})$ (`7702 > 6351`). In addition, all three medians in Table 3 (`64,000`, `2000`, `3,000`) are aggressively rounded to 1–2 significant digits while ranges are reported to exact integers (and further rounded to `1,000–6,000` and `31,000–140,000` in the Abstract).
  2. **Arithmetic Error in Reported $f\text{AUC}_{0-24}/\text{MIC}$ PTA at $\text{MIC} = 0.125\text{ mg/L}$ (line 109):** With 85% protein binding ($f_u = 0.15$) and $\text{MIC} = 0.125\text{ mg/L}$, reaching $f\text{AUC}_{0-24}/\text{MIC} \ge 167$ requires $\text{AUC}_{0-24} \ge 167 \times 0.125 / 0.15 = 139.17\text{ mg}\cdot\text{h/L}$ (`139,167 µg·h/L`). Because the median $\text{AUC}_{0-24}$ is `64,000 µg·h/L` ($\text{CV} = 32.9\%$) and the maximum across all 94 patients is `138,179 µg·h/L`, the PTA at $\text{MIC} = 0.125\text{ mg/L}$ is $<1\%$, not `50%` as stated on line 109 (50% PTA corresponds either to $\text{MIC} = 0.063\text{ mg/L}$ or to the cumulative MIC distribution across all 478 isolates).
  3. **Impossible Monte Carlo Percentage Granularity (`99.67%`):** With $n = 2,000$ simulated subjects (lines 102, 145), single-MIC PTA percentages have a step size of $1/2000 = 0.05\%$; `99.67%` ($1993.4 / 2000$) is only possible if weighted across the empirical MIC distribution of the 478 isolates (cumulative CFR rather than single-MIC PTA at $0.125\text{ mg/L}$).
* **Remediation:** Audit the script that generated Table 3 to fix the $C_{\text{trough}}$ (`7702 µg/L`) vs. $C_{\max}$ (`6351 µg/L`) discrepancy and report unrounded medians (e.g., to 3 significant figures) consistently in the Abstract and Table 3. In line 109, clarify whether `99.67%` and `50%` represent the Cumulative Fraction of Response (CFR) integrated over the baseline MIC distribution or fix the single-MIC PTA value at $\text{MIC} = 0.125\text{ mg/L}$.

### 3.2 Unmodeled Non-Adherence in On-Treatment BLQ Samples (`M1` Method) and Sample-Count Discrepancy (Pillars 2, 3, 4)
* **Location:** Results (lines 25, 57, Fig. 1, Table 2) and Materials and methods (line 133).
* **Defect:**
  1. **Pre-Dose vs. On-Treatment BLQ Accounting:** Line 25 states there were 952 total samples: 86 collected *before* the first dose (Day 1, $0\text{ h}$) and 866 collected *after* the first dose. It then states: *"A total of 234 samples were below the limit of quantification (BLQ), of which 151 samples were collected after treatment completion, rendering 9.5% of the pretomanid concentration BLQ during treatment."* Subtracting $234 - 151$ leaves 83 BLQ samples, which is $9.58\%$ of 866 post-dose samples—leaving **zero** BLQ samples accounted for among the 86 pre-first-dose (Day 1, $0\text{ h}$) samples, which should all be BLQ!
  2. **Non-Adherence Confounding and `M1` Bias:** During daily 200 mg dosing ($t_{1/2} = \ln(2) \times 102 / 3.10 = 22.8\text{ h}$, median trough $1,789\text{ ng/mL}$), a plasma concentration can only fall below the $7\text{ ng/mL}$ LLOQ after $>8$ consecutive days of missed doses. Discarding ~83 on-treatment BLQ samples via the `M1` method while keeping partially non-adherent low outliers (visible in Fig. 1 and the $65\text{ ng/mL}$ trough in Table 3) under an assumed 100% daily adherence dosing record forces the model to absorb missed doses into an inflated additive residual error (`0.368 mg/L = 368 ng/mL`, $52\times$ the LLOQ) and inflated $CL/F$ and $V/F$ variance.
* **Remediation:** Reconcile the exact breakdown of the 952 samples into (i) Day 1 pre-dose ($n = 86$), (ii) on-treatment Weeks 1–24, and (iii) post-treatment Weeks 32 & 72, specifying how many were BLQ in each category. Perform a sensitivity analysis excluding visits with suspected non-adherence (or using the `M3` censored-likelihood method) and report whether additive residual error (`0.368 mg/L`) decreases.

### 3.3 Sparse Absorption Sampling, High $V/F$ Shrinkage (`36.7%`), and Missing Allometric Scaling Equations (Pillars 3, 4, 7)
* **Location:** Results (lines 55, 69, Table 2) and Materials and methods (lines 133, 139).
* **Defect:**
  1. Because post-dose sampling only included $2\text{ h}$ and $23\text{ h}$ on Day 1, $6.5\text{ h}$ and $23\text{ h}$ at Week 8, and single time points at Weeks 12–24, very few observations capture the absorption peak ($T_{\max} \approx 4.5\text{ h}$). Consequently, $V/F$ has high $\eta$-shrinkage (`36.7%`, Table 2), which shrinks individual post-hoc $\eta_{V/F}$ estimates toward the population mean and reduces the power and validity of $\eta$-based covariate screening (line 139) and individual $C_{\max}$ estimation (shrinking max predicted $C_{\max}$ to $6,351\text{ ng/mL}$ when observed concentrations reached $11,566\text{ ng/mL}$).
  2. Table 2 and Methods (line 139) state that $CL/F$ and $V/F$ were allometrically scaled by FFM with exponents $0.75$ and $1.0$, but **never state the centering/reference FFM value** $\text{FFM}_{\text{ref}}$ in the equations $CL/F = \theta_{CL} \times (\text{FFM} / \text{FFM}_{\text{ref}})^{0.75}$ and $V/F = \theta_V \times (\text{FFM} / \text{FFM}_{\text{ref}})^{1.0}$, nor the formula used to calculate FFM (e.g., Janmahasatian equation).
* **Remediation:** Write out the exact mathematical equations for $CL/F$, $V/F$, FFM calculation, and the combined residual error model in the Methods or Table 2 legend (specifying $\text{FFM}_{\text{ref}}$), and explicitly note the 36.7% $V/F$ $\eta$-shrinkage as a limitation affecting individual $C_{\max}$ predictions and covariate tests on $V/F$.

### 3.4 Unsubstantiated Claim of "Confirming Clinical Relevance" and Asymmetric Preclinical Target Comparison (Pillars 1, 5)
* **Location:** Abstract (line 5), Discussion (line 126), and Materials and methods (lines 131, 145).
* **Defect:**
  1. Although Methods (line 131) states that `PRACTECAL-PKPD`'s efficacy objective was *"to establish an exposure-response model for each drug and regimen to both bactericidal activity and long-term treatment outcomes"*, no clinical or microbiological outcome data are analyzed in the paper. Claiming that the study *"confirmed the clinical relevance of the time dependent index and target"* (Abstract and line 126) solely because simulated $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ is high in a trial where patients received three or four active drugs (`BPaL/M/C`) is circular.
  2. In line 145, the $f\text{AUC}_{0-24}/\text{MIC} \ge 167$ target corresponds to **$>2\text{ log}_{10}$ CFU kill**, whereas the $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ targets of 48% and 77% correspond to **$1.0\text{ log}_{10}$ and $1.59\text{ log}_{10}$ CFU kill**, respectively. Comparing PTA across unmatched bactericidal kill thresholds biases the comparison against $f\text{AUC}/\text{MIC}$.
  3. **Editorial errors:** Line 53 cites `(Table 1 and appendix 1)` instead of `Table 2` for PK model parameters; Table 2's footnote defines two-compartment parameters (`Vc/F`, `Q/F`, `Vp/F`) not present in the table; and Table 1 has a double parenthesis typo `Female, n (%))`.
* **Remediation:** Reframe the Abstract and Discussion to state that at 200 mg daily dosing, clinical exposures consistently exceed preclinical $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ thresholds ($1\text{–}1.59\text{ log}_{10}$ kill) but do not reach the more stringent $>2\text{ log}_{10}$ kill $f\text{AUC}_{0-24}/\text{MIC}$ threshold of 167, while avoiding claims that clinical efficacy was formally linked to $\%fT > \text{MIC}$ in this study. Fix the Table 1/2 cross-reference and footnote typos.
