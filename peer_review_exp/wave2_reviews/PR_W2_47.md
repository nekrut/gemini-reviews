## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript investigates the fine-scale organization of motor control over cephalopod skin chromatophores in the hummingbird bobtail squid (*Euprymna berryi*) and European cuttlefish (*Sepia officinalis*). Using the `CHROMAS` computer-vision pipeline, each segmented chromatophore is partitioned into 36 radial slices, and spontaneous anisotropic deformations are decomposed via Principal Component Analysis (PCA; `KneeLocator` elbow selection) and Independent Component Analysis (`FastICA`), followed by `HDBSCAN` or Affinity Propagation clustering across thousands of independent components (ICs). In *S. officinalis* (1,829 chromatophores in head-fixed sedated `HF+S` and 1,456 in awake `HF` recordings), the authors detect an average of $3.72 \pm 1.21$ PCs/ICs per chromatophore—forming contiguous "petal-shaped" radial territories—which cluster into 754 putative motor units (MUs) innervating $9.00 \pm 4.76$ ICs (90% spanning $<14$ chromatophores). Crucially, the authors validate their computational inferences in a reduced brain–mantle preparation of *E. berryi* via simultaneous whole-cell current-clamp stimulation of single posterior chromatophore lobe motor neurons ($n = 16$ neurons across 12 animals) and skin videography, directly demonstrating that individual motor neurons innervate $4.38 \pm 0.55$ nearby chromatophores and pull adjacent radial sectors toward a shared center of mass ("virtual chromatophores"). Software (`PyPI`/`GitLab`) and data/videos (`public.brain.mpg.de`) are openly shared.

**Editorial Verdict: Minor-to-Moderate Revision (Strong / Sound).** Combining non-invasive sub-chromatophore computer vision with direct intracellular single-motor-neuron stimulation in *E. berryi* provides a compelling proof of concept that cephalopod chromatophores function as fractionated, cross-coordinated radial territories rather than uniform pixels. The authors also include a remarkably candid `Technical issues` section discussing mechanical coupling and clustering assumptions. However, four quantitative and statistical issues require correction prior to publication: (1) a mathematical and unit contradiction in the convex hull area statistics (Results line 47 reports a **median of $136,199\ \mu\text{m}^2$** and **max of $915,088\ \mu\text{m}^2$** alongside a **90th percentile of $51,513\ \mu\text{m}^2$**, inside a camera ROI whose total area is only $400 \times 400\ \mu\text{m} = 160,000\ \mu\text{m}^2$); (2) the permutation null model for second-order co-innervation ($Z = 49.36$) permutes chromatophore IDs globally across the entire field of view without conditioning on spatial proximity, chromatophore color class, or per-chromatophore degree; (3) the `KneeLocator` elbow heuristic on 36 slices cannot return $k = 1$ component, biasing the per-chromatophore IC distribution away from single-neuron innervation; and (4) all *S. officinalis* quantitative metrics derive from $N = 1$ individual cuttlefish, and the expansion-vs-contraction speed test ($t = 19.50, n = 2,157$ events) treats repeated slice events from 10 chromatophores as independent.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1 (Individual chromatophores deform anisotropically via contiguous "petal-shaped" radial territories that coordinate across adjacent chromatophores to form "virtual chromatophores"):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Directly validated by intracellular current-clamp stimulation of single motor neurons in the posterior chromatophore lobe of *E. berryi* ($n = 16$ neurons from 12 animals; Fig. 6, Fig. SV1), which evokes partial, directional petal-like expansion of $4.38 \pm 0.55$ neighboring chromatophores converging toward a shared virtual center, matching the `HDBSCAN` and `FastICA` decompositions of spontaneous activity.
* **Claim 2 (Chromatophores in *S. officinalis* are controlled on average by ~3.7 independent components, and putative motor units span an average of ~9 ICs [90% involving $<14$ chromatophores], unaffected by light ethanol sedation):** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  Consistent between `HF+S` ($3.715 \pm 1.211$ PCs; $9.003 \pm 4.755$ ICs/MU) and `HF` ($3.683 \pm 1.224$ PCs; $8.551 \pm 5.753$ ICs/MU) and aligned with classical electrophysiological estimates (Florey, 1969; Messenger, 2001). However, `kneed.KneeLocator` applied to a 36-point cumulative variance curve has a structural lower bound of $\ge 2$ components (cannot detect $k = 1$ uniformly innervated chromatophore), and all *Sepia* measurements come from a single head-fixed animal ($N = 1$).
* **Claim 3 (Chromatophore expansion is significantly faster and more stereotyped than passive elastic retraction at 20 fps):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Biophysically expected from active radial muscle contraction vs. passive cytoelastic sacculus recoil and clearly visible across amplitude bins (Fig. 5, Fig. S2). The statistical test ($t = 19.50, p < 0.001$), however, should be reported at the level of the $n = 10$ chromatophores (or via a mixed model) rather than treating $n = 2,157$ slice-events as independent replicates.
* **Claim 4 (90% of motor units have a convex hull area $< 51,513\ \mu\text{m}^2$, matching the grain size of Mediterranean fine sand, while median and mean areas are $136,199\ \mu\text{m}^2$ and $209,794\ \mu\text{m}^2$):** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  Results line 47 contains a direct mathematical impossibility: the 50th percentile (median = $136,199.10\ \mu\text{m}^2$) cannot exceed the 90th percentile ($51,513.25\ \mu\text{m}^2$), and the maximum reported convex hull area ($915,087.66\ \mu\text{m}^2$) is $5.7\times$ larger than the entire $400 \times 400\ \mu\text{m}$ ($160,000\ \mu\text{m}^2$) camera ROI! Given the conversion factor of $10\text{ pixels} = 1\ \mu\text{m}$ ($100\text{ px}^2 = 1\ \mu\text{m}^2$; line 58, line 128), unscaled $\text{pixel}^2$ values ($136,199\text{ px}^2 = 1,362\ \mu\text{m}^2$; $915,088\text{ px}^2 = 9,151\ \mu\text{m}^2$) have been mixed with or mislabeled as $\mu\text{m}^2$.
* **Claim 5 (Repeated co-innervation of chromatophore pairs across $\ge 2$ motor units [1,073 pairs vs. $263.7 \pm 16.9$ expected, $Z = 49.36$] proves non-random cooperative co-innervation):** `[Overstated]`  
  The permutation null model (Methods Part 2, line 58) reassigns chromatophore IDs uniformly at random across the entire field of view without preserving spatial locality, chromatophore color class, or per-chromatophore degree. Because real motor units are spatially compact and monochromatic, any spatially local clustering will produce far more shared pairs among nearby chromatophores than a global spatially uniform permutation.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Mathematical Contradiction and Pixel-to-Micron Area Conversion Error in Convex Hull Statistics (Pillars 3, 4, 7)
* **50th Percentile > 90th Percentile and Cluster Areas Exceeding the Camera Field of View:**
  * In Methods Part 1 line 128, the *S. officinalis* recording ROI is specified as $3,968 \times 3,968\text{ pixels}$ at $9.9\text{ px}/\mu\text{m}$ ($10\text{ px} = 1\ \mu\text{m}$ in Results line 58), corresponding to a **$400 \times 400\ \mu\text{m}$** field of view (or $0.5 \times 0.5\text{ mm}$ in Results line 31), which has a total area of **$160,000\ \mu\text{m}^2$** (or $250,000\ \mu\text{m}^2$).
  * Yet Results line 47 states: *"Across 754 clusters, convex hull areas ranged from $25.18\ \mu\text{m}^2$ to $915,087.66\ \mu\text{m}^2$, with a median of $136,199.10\ \mu\text{m}^2$ and a mean of $209,793.70\ \mu\text{m}^2$. Ninety percent of clusters had a convex hull area smaller than $51,513.25\ \mu\text{m}^2$ ($0.0515\text{ mm}^2$)."*
  * First, a distribution cannot have a median (50th percentile) of $136,199$ and a 90th percentile of $51,513$. Second, a convex hull of points inside a $160,000\ \mu\text{m}^2$ square cannot have a mean of $209,794\ \mu\text{m}^2$ or a maximum of $915,088\ \mu\text{m}^2$. Dividing those numbers by $10^2 = 100\text{ px}^2/\mu\text{m}^2$ yields a median of $1,362\ \mu\text{m}^2$, mean of $2,098\ \mu\text{m}^2$, and max of $9,151\ \mu\text{m}^2$, indicating that areas in $\text{pixels}^2$ were either not divided by $100$ or were divided by $10$ instead of $100$. This directly impacts the ecological calculation in Discussion line 85 equating $51,513\ \mu\text{m}^2$ ($\sim 227\ \mu\text{m}$ square) to Mediterranean fine sand grain size.

### 3.2 Spatially Unconstrained Null Model for Second-Order Co-Innervation (Pillars 3, 5)
* **Global Permutation Ignores Spatial Distance, Color Class, and Degree Distribution:** In Methods Part 2 line 58, the null distribution for second-order co-occurrence ($263.72 \pm 16.94$ vs. $1,073$ observed; $Z = 49.36$) was generated by randomly reassigning chromatophore IDs from all $1,829$ chromatophores across the 754 clusters while preserving only cluster size (`"maintains the original cluster sizes but not the per-chromatophore memberships"`). Because real motor units group nearby chromatophores of the same color (within-cluster NND $= 40.5\ \mu\text{m}$), two neighboring chromatophores of the same color have a high baseline probability of being co-sampled by overlapping local motor fields, whereas under global uniform permutation the probability that any two chromatophores co-occur twice is $\sim O(1/N^2)$ across all $N = 1,829$ chromatophores. A valid null model must permute memberships locally within spatial neighborhoods and color classes while preserving both cluster size and per-chromatophore IC count.

### 3.3 Algorithmic Bias in `KneeLocator` PCA Initialization and Expansion-Only Filtering for ICA (Pillars 3, 4)
* **Inability of `KneeLocator` to Return $k = 1$ Component:** For a single chromatophore innervated uniformly by $1$ motor neuron (as observed in a subset of *E. berryi* patch-clamp recordings; Fig. 6c, bottom right), the first principal component (PC1) captures the global expansion while PC2–PC36 capture segmentation and contour noise. However, finding the discrete point of maximum curvature (`kneed.KneeLocator`) on the cumulative explained variance curve across 36 PCs places the "elbow" at the transition between the steep initial drop and the flat tail ($k \ge 2\text{--}3$), never at $k = 1$. A variance-explained threshold (e.g., $\ge 80\%$ variance or parallel analysis against circularly shifted slice traces) should be compared to verify how often a single component suffices.
* **Undefined Expansion-Only Filtering Prior to `FastICA`:** Methods Part 2 line 39 states: *"To focus only on active motor signals, we filtered the data to retain only expansion events, resulting in independent components localized to one side."* It is not explained mathematically how non-simultaneous expansion events across the 36 slices of a chromatophore were filtered or masked in the $(\text{frames} \times 36)$ matrix prior to running `FastICA` (e.g., whether negative first differences $\Delta r < 0$ were rectified to zero—half-wave rectification of $\Delta r(t)$—or whether time frames were subsetted).

### 3.4 Sample Size ($N = 1$ *Sepia*) and Statistical Reporting Details (Pillars 4, 7)
* **Single-Animal *Sepia* Dataset and Event-Level Pseudoreplication:** Although 12 *S. officinalis* individuals were filmed during protocol development (Methods Part 1 line 152), all quantitative *Sepia* analyses in Figures 2–5 derive from a single head-fixed animal (Methods Part 2 line 1). Additionally, the paired $t$-test comparing expansion vs. contraction speeds ($t = 19.50, p < 0.001, n = 2,157$ events; Results line 58) treats 2,157 slice-level events drawn from 10 chromatophores as independent observations; the test should also be reported with chromatophore ($n = 10$) as the unit of replication.
* **Minor Numerical Discrepancies:**
  * In Results line 36, comparing $3.715 \pm 1.211$ ($n = 1,829$) vs. $3.683 \pm 1.224$ ($n = 1,456$) yields a two-sample Welch $t = 0.75$ ($p = 0.45$), whereas the text reports $t = 0.21, p = 0.83$.
  * Reconcile the *Sepia* ROI dimensions between Results line 31 (`"0.5 by 0.5 mm"`) and Methods line 128 (`"400 × 400 μm"`).

### Actionable Remediation Plan
1. **Recalculate and Reconcile Convex Hull Areas in $\mu\text{m}^2$:** Audit the pixel-to-micron area conversion factor ($1\ \mu\text{m}^2 = 100\text{ px}^2$ at $10\text{ px}/\mu\text{m}$) in the convex hull script, correct the reported min, median, mean, 90th percentile, and max convex hull areas in Results line 47 and Figure 3d–f so that the median is smaller than the 90th percentile and all areas fit within the $400 \times 400\ \mu\text{m}$ ($160,000\ \mu\text{m}^2$) ROI, and update the sand-grain scale comparison in Discussion line 85 accordingly.
2. **Implement a Spatially and Degree-Constrained Null Model for Co-Innervation:** Replace the global uniform ID permutation in Methods Part 2 line 58 with a spatially constrained permutation (swapping chromatophore memberships only among chromatophores of the same color class within a local radius comparable to the motor unit FND, while preserving each chromatophore's total IC degree) to test whether pairwise co-innervation exceeds what is expected from spatially overlapping monochromatic fields alone.
3. **Clarify `FastICA` Expansion Filtering, Test $k = 1$ PC Sensitivity, and Report Chromatophore-Level Kinetics:** Specify the exact mathematical transformation used to "retain only expansion events" before `FastICA` (Methods Part 2 line 39), evaluate how many chromatophores are explained by $k = 1$ component under a fixed variance-explained threshold (or shuffle parallel analysis), and report the expansion-vs-contraction speed comparison aggregated across the $n = 10$ chromatophores alongside the event-level test.
