## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript resolves the long-standing biochemical pathway for the biosynthesis of $(S)$-nicotine ($\mathbf{1}$) from nicotinic acid ($\mathbf{2}$) and $N$-methylpyrrolinium ($\mathbf{3}$) in *Nicotiana tabacum*. By identifying homeologous biosynthetic gene clusters on chromosomes 12 and 16 containing the reductase *A622*, the transporter *MATE1/2*, and a previously uncharacterized $\beta$-glucosidase (*$\beta$-GD1/2*), alongside a co-expressed topping-induced UDP-glycosyltransferase (*UGT1* / *UGT709L18*) across 824 RNA-seq samples, the authors discover that nicotine biosynthesis proceeds via a cryptic activating $N$-glucosylation sequence. They reconstitute the complete four-enzyme stereoselective cascade both in vitro ($n = 3$) and in planta via transient expression and $\text{nicotinamide-}d_4$ feeding in *N. benthamiana* leaves ($n = 8$): (1) nicotinic acid $N$-glucosyltransferase (`NaGT` / `UGT1`) converts $\mathbf{2}$ to nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside ($\mathbf{10}$); (2) nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside reductase (`NaGR` / `A622`) catalyzes NADPH-dependent reduction and decarboxylation of $\mathbf{10}$ to the unstable nucleophile 1,2-dihydropyridine $N$-glucoside ($\mathbf{4b}$); (3) $(S)$-nicotine glucoside synthase (`NicGS` / `BBLa`) couples/oxidizes the intermediate with $\mathbf{3}$ to yield $(S)$-nicotine $N$-glucoside ($(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$); and (4) nicotine glucoside hydrolase (`NicGH` / `$\beta$-GD1`) cleaves the glucose moiety to release $(S)$-nicotine ($(S)\text{-}\mathbf{1}$). Substituting $\mathbf{3}$ with $\Delta^1$-pyrrolinium or $\Delta^1$-piperideinium (chemically or via `OLADO`) yields $(S)$-nornicotine and $(S)$-anabasine. Using ring-$d_4$ and C6-monodeuterated nicotinic acid, combined with X-ray crystal structures of `NaGR` bound to $\text{NADP}^+$ and $\mathbf{10}$ ($1.3\text{ \AA}$, `PDB: 9RDD`) and `NicGS` bound to FAD and $(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$ ($2.5\text{ \AA}$, `PDB: 9RDR`; apo `9RUW`), the authors prove that `NaGR` transfers a hydride to the $re$-face (pro-$R$) of C6 while `NicGS` abstracts the pro-$S$ hydride/deuteride from the $si$-face of C6.

This is an exemplary biochemical and structural study where every step of the proposed cascade is supported by synthetic standards, chiral resolution, regioselective isotope tracing, atomic-resolution crystal structures, in planta drop-out experiments, and root knockout metabolomics. Furthermore, the authors show commendable epistemic restraint in discussing the unresolved questions—such as whether `NicGS` catalyzes stereoselective Mannich C–C bond formation versus dynamic kinetic resolution of $(R,S)\text{-}\mathbf{5b}$ (Fig. 9), and how intermediates traverse the cytosol–vacuole boundary. Only minor compound-numbering typos in the text, ambiguous isotopic nomenclature (`2-d_6` for C6-monodeuterated `[6-d_1]-2`), and the absence of steady-state Michaelis-Menten kinetic parameters ($K_m, k_{\text{cat}}$) for the individual reactions warrant revision.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Nicotine biosynthesis requires a cryptic activating $N$-glucosylation of nicotinic acid by `NaGT` (`UGT709L18`) and terminal deglucosylation by `NicGH` (`$\beta$-GD1`): [Well Supported].**  
  Demonstrated by complete one-pot in vitro reconstitution with purified enzymes and chemically synthesized standards ($\mathbf{10}, \mathbf{12}, \mathbf{13}$), strict substrate specificity of `NaGT` (accepting $\mathbf{2}$ but not $\mathbf{1}$) and `NicGH` (accepting $\mathbf{13}$ but not $\mathbf{10}$, $\text{Glc-}\mathbf{11}$, or $\mathbf{12}$), in planta `NicGH` drop-out causing accumulation of labelled $\mathbf{13}$ (Fig. 7), and detection of endogenous $\mathbf{13}$ in wild-type *N. tabacum* roots (Fig. 8B).
* **`NaGR` (`A622`) catalyzes NADPH-dependent C6 $re$-face reduction of nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside ($\mathbf{10}$), and `NicGS` (`BBLa`) catalyzes stereoselective $si$-face pro-$S$ hydride abstraction to form $(S)$-nicotine $N$-glucoside ($\mathbf{13}$): [Well Supported].**  
  The $1.3\text{ \AA}$ crystal structure of `NaGR` (`9RDD`, C4 of $\text{NADP}^+$ $3.4\text{ \AA}$ from the C6 $re$-face of $\mathbf{10}$) and the $2.5\text{ \AA}$ structure of `NicGS` (`9RDR`, reactive N5 of FAD $3.2\text{ \AA}$ from the C6 $si$-face of $(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$) provide direct atomic-level confirmation of the C6 deuterium-loss experiments (Fig. 5B, C) and the kinetic isotope effect observed during non-enzymatic oxidation in the absence of `NicGS`.
* **The four-enzyme cascade is modular with respect to the iminium electrophile, generating nornicotine ($\mathbf{16}$) and anabasine ($\mathbf{6}$) when supplied with $\Delta^1$-pyrrolinium ($\mathbf{15}$) or $\Delta^1$-piperideinium ($\mathbf{8}$): [Well Supported].**  
  Verified both with chemically synthesized iminium substrates and via coupled enzymatic generation from ornithine/lysine using `OLADO` in vitro (Fig. 4C–D, Fig. 5D–E) and in planta (Supplementary Fig. 11).
* **Subcellular partitioning of cytosolic `NaGT`/`NaGR` and vacuolar `NicGS`/`NicGH` argues against a single physical "nicotine synthase" metabolon: [Well Supported].**  
  The known cytosolic localization of `A622` vs. vacuolar localization of `BBL` and predicted signal/vacuolar sorting peptides of `$\beta$-GD1` (paired with `MATE1/2` clustering) coherently explain why Friesen & Leete's 1990 ammonium sulfate fraction contained soluble activities of all four enzymes without forming a physical multi-enzyme complex.

## 3. Forensic Technical Audit & Constructive Remediation Plan

### Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

No major methodological or statistical flaws were identified. Two minor technical/biochemical limitations are noted below for completeness:

#### 3.1. Absence of Steady-State Enzyme Kinetics ($K_m, k_{\text{cat}}$) for the Newly Assigned Activities
All in vitro cascade and single-enzyme assays were performed as 16-hour endpoint incubations at $37\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ using fixed high enzyme concentrations (`0.5 mg/mL NaGT`, `0.5 mg/mL NaGR`, `50 µg/mL NicGS`, `5 µg/mL NicGH`; Methods line 157). While 16-hour endpoints are appropriate for multi-step cascade reconstruction, reporting basic steady-state Michaelis-Menten parameters ($K_m, k_{\text{cat}}, k_{\text{cat}}/K_m$) for the two stable single-substrate reactions—`NaGT` (`UGT1`) with nicotinic acid ($\mathbf{2}$) and `NicGH` (`$\beta$-GD1`) with $(S)$-nicotine $N$-glucoside ($(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$) versus $(R)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$—would establish their catalytic efficiency and clarify whether `NicGH` exhibits kinetic preference between the $(S)$- and $(R)$-diastereomers of $\mathbf{13}$.

#### 3.2. Elevated $R_{\text{cryst}} / R_{\text{free}}$ ($29.3\% / 32.4\%$) and Ligand Presence in the `apo-NicGS` (`9RUW`) Crystallization Condition
In the Methods ("Protein crystallization" and "X-ray structure elucidation", lines 16 and 21 of offset 46080), the authors report that *"Initial apo-crystals of NicGS (BBLa) were obtained... by co-crystallisation with 10 mM (S)-nicotine glucoside 13"* (whereas the ligand-bound `9RDR` structure used `50 mM (S)-nicotine glucoside`), and that `apo-NicGS` (`9RUW`, $2.5\text{ \AA}$) refined to relatively high $R_{\text{cryst}} / R_{\text{free}}$ values of $29.3\% / 32.4\%$. The text should briefly explain whether weak/partial ligand occupancy in the `10 mM` co-crystallization drop or lattice disorder contributed to the higher $R$-factors in `9RUW`.

### Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Ambiguous Isotopic Nomenclature (`nicotinic acid-d 6` / `2-d 6` Used for C6-Monodeuterated `[6-d 1]-2`):** On line 74 and in Fig. 5C/6B/6D, the authors write *"we used the singly labelled nicotinic acid-d 6 (2-d 6)."* In standard chemical nomenclature, the suffix `-d_6` denotes a molecule containing **six** deuterium atoms (just as `nicotinic acid-(ring-d_4)` / `2-d_4` in the immediately preceding paragraph denotes **four** deuterium atoms!). Using `2-d_6` to mean `[6-^2H]nicotinic acid` (which is a $d_1$ isotopologue yielding `1-d_0` or `1-d_1`) is confusing for readers and should be written as `[6-d_1]nicotinic acid` (`2-6-d_1` or `[6-^2H]-2`).
2. **Compound Numbering Typos in Results, Figure 9 Legend, and Methods:**
   * **Line 74:** *"consistent with a cascade where NaGR (A622) catalyses reduction of 2"* — `NaGR` catalyzes the reduction of **$\mathbf{10}$** (nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside), not $\mathbf{2}$ (nicotinic acid).
   * **Line 78:** *"Cascade formation of nornicotine-d 3 (15-d 3)"* — nornicotine is compound **$\mathbf{16}$** (`16-d_3`), whereas $\mathbf{15}$ is the precursor $\Delta^1$-pyrrolinium (see Fig. 4C).
   * **Line 108:** *"The NicGS (BBL) knockout accumulated pyridine glucoside (12) and nicotine glucoside (10)"* — compound $\mathbf{10}$ is **nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside**, whereas nicotine $N$-glucoside is **$\mathbf{13}$** (in Fig. 8A, $\Delta\text{NicGS}$ accumulates $\mathbf{10}$ and $\mathbf{12}$).
   * **Line 133 (Fig. 9 legend):** *"catalysis of the stereoselective Mannich reaction between 3 and 10 prior to oxidation, reversal of the non-desired Mannich reaction (opening (R)-5b into 3 and 10)"* — the Mannich reaction and retro-Mannich ring opening involve $\mathbf{3}$ and **$\mathbf{4b}$** (1,2-dihydropyridine $N$-glucoside), not $\mathbf{10}$.
   * **Methods (line 16 of offset 46080):** *"co-crystallisation with 5 mM nicotinic acid N-glucoside 1"* — nicotinic acid $N$-glucoside is compound **$\mathbf{10}$**, not $\mathbf{1}$.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Standardize Deuterated Isotopologue Notation and Fix Compound Number Cross-References
* **WHY:** Referring to C6-monodeuterated nicotinic acid as `2-d_6` conflicts with standard mass-spectrometry isotopologue notation (`2-d_4`, `1-d_3`, `1-d_1`, `1-d_0`) used in the same figure, and the five compound-number typos (`2` vs. `10`, `15` vs. `16`, `10` vs. `13`, `10` vs. `4b`, `1` vs. `10`) can confuse readers tracking intermediates across figures.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * Rename `nicotinic acid-d 6 (2-d 6)` to `[6-^2H]nicotinic acid ([6-d_1]-2)` in line 74, Fig. 5C, and Fig. 6B/D.
  * Correct `reduction of 2` to `reduction of 10` (line 74); `nornicotine-d 3 (15-d 3)` to `nornicotine-d 3 (16-d 3)` (line 78); `nicotine glucoside (10)` to `nicotinic acid N-glucoside (10)` (line 108); `between 3 and 10` / `into 3 and 10` to `between 3 and 4b` / `into 3 and 4b` (Fig. 9 legend, line 133); and `nicotinic acid N-glucoside 1` to `nicotinic acid N-glucoside 10` (Methods, line 16 of offset 46080).

#### 2. Report Initial-Rate Kinetic Parameters ($K_m, k_{\text{cat}}$) for `NaGT` and `NicGH` (or Discuss Endpoint Conditions)
* **WHY:** Quantitative kinetic parameters for the newly discovered glucosyltransferase (`NaGT`) and glucoside hydrolase (`NicGH`) against their physiological substrates ($\mathbf{2}$ and $(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$ vs. $(R)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$) would strengthen the enzymological characterization.
* **WHAT & HOW:** If initial-rate time-course data were collected during assay optimization, report $K_m$ and $k_{\text{cat}}$ for `NaGT` (with $\mathbf{2}$) and `NicGH` (with $(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$ and $(R)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$); otherwise, explicitly note in the Results (line 59) that diastereomeric acceptance of $(R,S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$ by `NicGH` was evaluated under 16-hour endpoint conditions. Also add a brief note in the Crystallography Methods explaining the `10 mM` $(S)\text{-}\mathbf{13}$ condition and $R_{\text{free}} = 32.4\%$ for `apo-NicGS` (`9RUW`).
