## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript investigates how stimulus- and dose-dependent dynamics of the tumor suppressor p53 translate into stochastic transcriptional bursting across four canonical target genes (*MDM2*, *CDKN1A*/p21, *PPM1D*/Wip1, and *BAX*) in A549 lung carcinoma cells exposed to ionizing radiation (10 Gy), UV-C radiation (3 and $6\text{ J/m}^2$), etoposide (5 and $50\ \mu\text{M}$), 5-fluorouracil ($5\ \mu\text{g/mL}$), and Nutlin-3a ($10\ \mu\text{M}$). Combining live-cell p53-mVenus time-lapse microscopy, dual-color exon/intron single-molecule FISH (smFISH), ChIP-qPCR of promoter-bound p53, and Western blotting of C-terminal p53 K382 acetylation with a custom Bayesian Markov-Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) inference framework for a polyploid random telegraph model, the authors report that (1) mature mRNA trajectories and promoter bursting are frequently uncoupled from both total nuclear p53 levels and DNA binding at response elements; (2) promoter regulation is dominated by the active promoter fraction $f = \lambda / (\lambda + \gamma)$ (burst frequency); and (3) p53 K382 acetylation and global transcriptional competence (e.g., UV-induced elongation arrest) better explain target gene activation than total p53 abundance.

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision Required (Mixed / Salvageable).** The experimental dataset—spanning multi-timepoint dual-color smFISH across four genes and seven treatments alongside live-cell p53 trajectories, ChIP-qPCR, and K382ac profiling—is rich and biologically informative. However, the mathematical modeling and technical reporting suffer from serious defects that must be resolved: (1) the Bayesian likelihood $L_1$ fits a **steady-state** analytical mRNA distribution independently at acute post-damage timepoints (2.5–9 h) during rapid 3–5 h p53 pulses and UV transcriptional shutdown, violating stationarity and confounding mRNA accumulation/decay lags with kinetic parameter changes; (2) the printed equation for the inactive-state probability mass $q_\theta(m)$ in Section 2.7 contains an algebraic error (`m - b`) that yields negative probabilities; (3) the 5-parameter MCMC model uses an improper flat prior on $[0, \infty)^5$ with severe scale collinearity among $(\lambda, \gamma, \delta)$ and relies on visual single-chain trace inspection without formal convergence diagnostics ($\hat{R}$, ESS); (4) Methods Section 2.12 describes **EdU** (DNA replication) labeling whereas Results line 114 reports **EU** (nascent RNA) labeling, and Section 2.10 lists identical sequences for the *CDKN1A* forward and reverse primers; and (5) neither the custom Bayesian MCMC code nor the repository accession number promised in the Data Availability Statement is provided in the manuscript.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1 (Stimulus- and dose-dependent nuclear p53 accumulation dynamics—pulsatile under 10 Gy IR, low UV, and $5\ \mu\text{M}$ etoposide vs. sustained/monotonic under high UV, $50\ \mu\text{M}$ etoposide, 5-FU, and Nutlin-3a—are frequently uncoupled from target gene mRNA expression):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Demonstrated across hundreds of single cells by comparing live-cell p53-mVenus trajectories and Western blots (Fig. 1C–G, Fig. S1C) against single-cell mature mRNA counts measured by smFISH across timepoints (Fig. 2C–J), such as identical target mRNA profiles under $5\ \mu\text{M}$ vs. $50\ \mu\text{M}$ etoposide despite a 2-fold difference in nuclear p53.
* **Claim 2 (Promoter occupancy of p53 measured by ChIP-qPCR is largely invariant across IR and chemotherapeutic stimuli, whereas C-terminal p53 K382 acetylation correlates with transcriptional induction):** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  Supported by ChIP-qPCR ($n=3\text{--}4$ biological replicates; Fig. 4A) showing comparable p53 binding across IR, etoposide, 5-FU, and Nutlin-3a, and by Western blots showing high K382ac specifically under IR and Nutlin-3a where early 3 h mRNA induction is strongest (Fig. 4C). Caveated by the qualitative nature of the Western blot comparison ($n=3$ repeats, unquantified densitometry) and assessment at a single peak timepoint per stimulus.
* **Claim 3 (The custom Bayesian random telegraph framework accurately resolves all five kinetic parameters $(\lambda, \gamma, \mu, \delta, \beta)$ at individual post-stimulus timepoints, showing that promoter switching $f = \lambda/(\lambda+\gamma)$ dominates over transcription rate $\mu$ and degradation rate $\delta$):** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  While the qualitative observation that active transcription site (TS) fraction $f$ drives induction is directly observable from raw intron-positive TS counts (Fig. S5A) and consistent with prior work (Friedrich et al., 2019), the Bayesian inference of $(\lambda, \gamma, \mu, \delta)$ at discrete timepoints during acute non-stationary responses is mathematically flawed. Likelihood factor $L_1$ assumes that total cellular mRNA $m$ has reached the **steady-state** Peccoud–Ycart distribution for the instantaneous parameters at time $t$. During a 3-hour p53 pulse or acute UV arrest, cytoplasmic mRNA (especially for stable transcripts like *BAX*) is far from steady state, forcing $\delta$ and $\mu$ to absorb non-equilibrium transient lags. Furthermore, $L_1$ depends only on ratios $(\lambda/\delta, \gamma/\delta, \mu/\delta)$ while $L_2$ (active TS nascent intensity) depends primarily on $\mu / v_{\text{elong}}$, leaving the absolute timescale $\delta$ poorly identifiable (as evidenced by the overlapping posterior traces of $\lambda, \gamma, \delta$ in Fig. 3F and strong $\lambda$–$\gamma$ / $\mu$–$\delta$ collinearity in Fig. 3E).
* **Claim 4 (UV radiation suppresses early p53 target gene induction via global nascent RNA transcriptional inhibition, as demonstrated by EU labeling):** `[Fragile]`  
  Biologically well established in the literature and supported by Supplementary Fig. S4, but undermined by a direct methods contradiction: Results line 114 states that cells were assayed with **EU** (5-ethynyl uridine, which labels nascent RNA), whereas Materials and Methods Section 2.12 (`Labelling of cells with EdU`, line 92) describes incubating cells with $1\text{ mM}$ **EdU** (5-ethynyl-2'-deoxyuridine, which labels S-phase DNA replication, not RNA transcription).

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Steady-State Assumption Violation and Algebraic Error in the Telegraph Likelihood $L_1$ (Pillar 3)
* **Applying a Stationary Distribution to Non-Stationary Pulsatile Dynamics:** In Section 2.7 (line 63), $L_{1,c}(\theta; k, m)$ models the probability of observing $k$ active TSs and $m$ total cellular mRNAs by convolving the **steady-state** Peccoud–Ycart (1995) solution across $K$ alleles, and Section 3.3 (line 126) states that *"Inference was done for each condition, gene, and time-point independently."* Because nuclear p53 oscillates with a ~3–5 h period under IR/low-UV/low-etoposide and target mRNAs have half-lives of $1\text{--}>6\text{ h}$, the cellular mRNA pool at $2.5\text{--}9\text{ h}$ is not in stationary equilibrium with the instantaneous promoter rates at that timepoint. Fitting a steady-state distribution to transient timepoints systematically biases $\delta$ (which the authors note in line 134 behaves erratically) and distorts the fold-change clustergram in Figure 3H.
* **Algebraic Error in the Printed Equation for $q_\theta(m)$:** In Section 2.7 (line 63), the partial probability mass function for an inactive promoter ($X_n = 0, Y_n = m$) is written as:
  $$q_\theta(m) = \frac{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(m+a)(m-b)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(m+b-1)} M\left(m+a, m+b-1, -\frac{\mu}{\delta}\right) \frac{\mu^m}{m!\delta^m}$$
  Because $b = (\lambda + \gamma)/\delta > 0$, whenever $m < b$ (including $m = 0$), the factor $(m - b)$ is **negative**, yielding negative probabilities! In the exact Peccoud–Ycart solution, the inactive marginal is strictly non-negative and involves the ratio $\frac{\gamma}{\lambda+\gamma} = \frac{b-a}{b}$ with Kummer parameter $m+b+1$ (or $m+b$), not $(m-b)/\Gamma(m+b-1)$.

### 3.2 Bayesian Prior Specification, Parameter Unidentifiability, and Single-Chain MCMC Diagnostics (Pillars 3, 4)
* **Improper Uniform Prior and Log-Space Jacobian Omission:** Line 126 states that *"An improper uniform prior over the non-negative real parameter space was chosen"*, while lines 72 and 132 state that MCMC sampling was performed in the logarithmic domain ($\log \theta$). Unless the log-transformation Jacobian ($\prod_{i=1}^5 \theta_i$) is explicitly multiplied into the target density, a random walk in $\log \theta$ with a flat acceptance ratio implicitly imposes a Jeffreys prior $p(\theta_i) \propto 1/\theta_i$ rather than a uniform prior on $[0, \infty)$. Moreover, because $L_1$ is invariant to common rescaling of $(\lambda, \gamma, \mu, \delta)$ and $L_2$ (active TS exon intensity during the ~2–4 min Pol II elongation window at $50\text{ bp/s}$) is insensitive to switching times longer than the elongation time, the absolute rate scale $\delta$ is only weakly identifiable—explaining why $\lambda, \gamma,$ and $\delta$ overlap in Figure 3F and exhibit strong collinearity in Figure 3E.
* **Lack of Formal MCMC Convergence Diagnostics:** Running a single chain for 30,000 post-burn-in iterations and assessing convergence *"visually from the sample traces (Figure 3F)"* (line 132) is insufficient for a 5-dimensional correlated posterior with rejection-sampled likelihoods ($L_2$ approximated via Monte Carlo average $m \approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N z_n$, which introduces pseudo-marginal simulation noise into the Metropolis ratio!). Multi-chain Gelman–Rubin $\hat{R}$ and Effective Sample Size (ESS) should be reported, along with the number of rejection-sampling trajectories $N$ per MCMC step.

### 3.3 Experimental Protocol Errors and Internal Text Contradictions (Pillars 6, 7)
* **Identical Forward and Reverse Primers for *CDKN1A* and RT Enzyme in qPCR:** In Section 2.10 (line 82), the RT-qPCR primers for *CDKN1A* are listed as:
  * `CDKN1A forward: TGGACCTGTCACTGTCTTGT`
  * `CDKN1A reverse: TGGACCTGTCACTGTCTTGT`
  The forward and reverse sequences are identical. Additionally, the preceding sentence states that cDNA was synthesized with `M-MuLV reverse transcriptase`, and then *"Quantitative real-time PCR was performed in triplicates using ProtoScript II Reverse Transcriptase (NEB) and SYBR Green reagent"*—listing a second reverse transcriptase instead of a thermostable Taq DNA polymerase master mix.
* **EdU (DNA Synthesis) vs. EU (Nascent RNA Synthesis) Discrepancy:** Results line 114 states that nascent RNA inhibition after UV irradiation was measured using **EU-labeled RNA** (Supplementary Fig. S4). However, Materials and Methods Section 2.12 (lines 92–94) is titled `Labelling of cells with EdU` and details incubating cells with `1 mM EdU` (5-ethynyl-2'-deoxyuridine), which incorporates into replicating DNA, not RNA.
* **Misclassification of A549 Cell Line:** Results Section 3.1 (line 102) describes A549 as a *"small cell lung carcinoma cell line A549"*, whereas Discussion line 22 (Part 2) correctly identifies A549 as a *"non-small cell lung carcinoma cell line"*.

### 3.4 Missing Repository Accessions and Unreleased Bayesian Inference Code (Pillar 7)
* **Incomplete Data Availability Statement and Missing Source Code:** Part 2 line 3 states that raw and processed measurements are available via an institutional repository `(see data availability section)`, yet the Data Availability Statement (Part 2 line 39) contains only the unedited publisher template placeholder: *"The datasets presented in this study can be found in online repositories. The names of the repository/repositories and accession number(s) can be found in the article/ Supplementary Material."* No accession number, institutional repository URL, or GitHub repository for the novel Bayesian MCMC inference code is provided in the manuscript.
