## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript presents the "LeishDog Atlas," a single-cell RNA-seq dataset of 68,323 peripheral blood immune cells isolated from 16 PBMC samples spanning uninfected control dogs (NLC, $n = 3$ samples) and dogs naturally infected with *Leishmania infantum* across four clinical stages defined by LeishVet guidelines: LV1 ($n = 2$), LV2 ($n = 6$), LV3 ($n = 3$), and LV4 ($n = 2$).

* **Claim 1:** Canine PBMC scRNA-seq resolves 15 major immune clusters (Level 1) and 47 distinct immune subpopulations across B cell, T/NK cell, and myeloid lineages (Levels 2–3). `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 2:** Disease progression across LeishVet stages (LV1 to LV4) is marked by a statistically significant shift ($p_{\text{adj}} < 0.0001$) from a lymphoid (T/NK)-dominated peripheral landscape in early disease to a myeloid (monocyte)-dominated landscape in advanced disease, alongside a declining $\text{CD4}^+/\text{CD8}^+$ T cell ratio. `[Fragile]`
* **Claim 3:** $\text{CD4}^+\text{ T}_{\text{H}}1$ cells exhibit progressive exhaustion signatures (`LAG3`, `CTLA4`) peaking at terminal stage LV4, whereas $\text{CD8}^+$ T cells differentiate from a progenitor exhausted ($\text{T}_{\text{PEX}}$-like, `TCF7`/`TOX`) state toward effector and proliferative trajectories peaking at LV3. `[Moderately Supported]`
* **Claim 4:** Peripheral monocytes expand into six transcriptionally distinct subsets that dynamically remodel their inflammatory state, shifting from pro-inflammatory in LV1 toward anti-inflammatory at LV3 before a terminal pro-inflammatory rebound at LV4. `[Fragile]`

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Singular Random-Effect Collapse and Massive Cell-Level Pseudoreplication in the GLMM (`glmer`)
To test for differences in major immune cell composition across clinical stages (Fig. 1H and Fig. 3E; Table S3), the authors fit a binomial generalized linear mixed model in `lme4` (p. 46080, lines 148–158):
$$\texttt{cbind(frequency, total\_freq - frequency) \sim 1 + LV.stage * cellgroup + (1 | orig.ident)}$$
In line 158, the authors explicitly report what happened during model fitting:
> *"In our datasets, the estimated variance of the random intercept approached zero (singular fit), indicating minimal between-sample variation after accounting for fixed effects. Consequently, the model behaves equivalently to a fixed-effects binomial GLM, and coefficients were interpreted as population-level effects."*

This statement reflects a critical statistical misspecification that invalidates all p-values and confidence intervals in Figs. 1H, 3E, and Table S3:
1. **Why `(1 | orig.ident)` is mathematically guaranteed to collapse to zero:** In a compositional count table where each sample (`orig.ident`) has $K$ mutually exclusive cell groups (`T/NK`, `B`, `Myeloid`) whose frequencies sum to `total_freq` ($\sum_{c=1}^K \text{frequency}_{ic} = \text{total\_freq}_i$), the average proportion across the $K$ rows for every single dog $i$ is **identically equal to $1/K$ ($33.3\%$)**! Fitting a scalar random intercept `(1 | orig.ident)` asks whether dog $i$ has a higher or lower total proportion across all cell types combined—which has **zero variance by mathematical identity**!
2. **Consequence (68,323-fold pseudoreplication):** Because dog-level heterogeneity actually resides in the **interaction between dog and cell group** (`(0 + cellgroup | orig.ident)` or an observation-level random effect `(1 | sample_cellgroup_id)` to capture binomial overdispersion), omitting this random slope forced the random effect variance to zero. As a result, the model degenerated into an unclustered fixed-effects binomial GLM that treated all **68,323 individual cells** as independent Bernoulli trials rather than $N = 16$ dogs!
3. **Impact on biological inference:** With $n = 2$ dogs in LV1 and $n = 2$ dogs in LV4, treating $\sim 10,882$ and $\sim 16,775$ cells as independent observations shrinks standard errors by $\sim \sqrt{5,000} \approx 70$-fold, producing tiny confidence intervals and $p_{\text{adj}} < 0.0001$ even when differences are driven by a single dog. Compositional models that properly model overdispersion at the biological sample level (e.g., Dirichlet-multinomial regression, `scCODA`, `propeller` logit/arcsin-transformed ANOVA on dog-level proportions, or `glmer` with an observation-level random intercept `(1 | obs_id)`) are mandatory.

### 2.2 Extreme Sample Size Imbalance ($n = 2$ in LV1 and LV4) and Heterogeneous Control Group (Pooled UK Beagles vs. US Dogs)
The cross-sectional cohort ($N = 16$ scRNA-seq libraries from 18 dogs, p. 46080 line 79) has severe structural limitations for stage-resolved disease progression claims:
* Stage sample sizes are **NLC: $n = 3$ libraries**, **LV1: $n = 2$ dogs**, **LV2: $n = 6$ dogs**, **LV3: $n = 3$ dogs**, and **LV4: $n = 2$ dogs**.
* Moreover, while the 13 infected dogs and 2 NLC dogs are privately owned US dogs (primarily hunting hounds with vertically transmitted *L. infantum* and potential tick-borne co-infections), the third NLC sample (`NLC1PBMC`) is a **pooled blood sample from 3 UK laboratory beagles (Envigo)** of unknown age and sex (p. 46080, lines 34 and 79). Pooling 3 genetically distinct laboratory beagles on a different continent into a single 10X library without demultiplexing (e.g., via `souporcell` or `demuxlet` on endogenous SNPs) underestimates control variance and confounds breed, environment, and batch with infection status.
* In all stage-progression visualizations (bar plots in Figs. 1G, 2D, 3D, 4D, 5D, 6D; dot plots and heatmaps in Figs. 2F–G, 4G, 5G, 6F,H; monocyte inflammatory trajectories in Fig. 6I–J), cells are pooled across dogs within each LV stage without displaying dog-level data points or inter-dog error bars. With only $n = 2$ dogs in LV1 and $n = 2$ in LV4, any dog with an idiosyncratic cell count or inflammatory burst dominates the stage average.

### 2.3 Flawed Bootstrap Null Model for Stage-Specific Signature Z-Scores (Figs. 4H and 5H)
To evaluate whether subcluster signature genes (top 50 DEGs per subcluster) change across clinical stages (Figs. 4H and 5H; p. 46080 line 167), the authors computed the average expression of each of the 50 signature genes across pooled cells in each LV stage, row-scaled each gene to a Z-score across the 5 stages, and compared the mean Z-score of the 50 correlated signature genes against a bootstrap ribbon of 50 randomly sampled non-signature genes.
* **Statistical flaw:** Randomly sampled non-signature genes across the transcriptome are uncorrelated with one another, so their Z-scores across the 5 stages cancel out to zero by the Central Limit Theorem (producing a narrow flat gray ribbon around 0). By contrast, the top 50 DEGs of a specific subcluster are **co-regulated genes selected for high covariance**. Any biological or technical batch fluctuation between the $n = 2\text{--}6$ dogs in each stage shifts all 50 co-regulated genes in the same direction simultaneously!
* Thus, exceeding the random-gene bootstrap ribbon only proves that the 50 signature genes are co-expressed with each other—it does **not** test whether the stage-to-stage trajectory is statistically significant across independent dogs ($N = 16$). Testing stage effects requires pseudobulk differential expression (`DESeq2` / `edgeR` aggregating counts per dog per cell type, $N = 16$).

### 2.4 Inverted Quality-Control Inequalities Reported in STAR Methods
In the STAR Methods under *Processing scRNA-seq data* (p. 46080, line 105), the text states:
> *"Cells with a Log10GenesPerUMI (log10(nFeature_RNA)/log10(nCount_RNA)) of >0.83, and mitochondrial/total RNA expression (percent.mt) < 5% were excluded."*

In standard scRNA-seq quality control, high `Log10GenesPerUMI` ($> 0.80$) indicates high library complexity and low `percent.mt` ($< 5\%$) indicates viable intact cells, whereas low complexity ($< 0.83$) and high mitochondrial read fraction ($> 5\%$) indicate debris or dying/lysed cells. As written, the text states that viable, high-complexity cells were excluded and only dying, low-complexity cells were retained. This must be verified against the GitHub codebase and corrected.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Manual doublet removal instead of algorithmic doublet detection:** Doublets were identified solely by manual inspection of Level 2 subclusters co-expressing canonical lineage markers (p. 46080, lines 129–138), without running computational doublet callers (`scDblFinder` or `DoubletFinder`) per 10X library prior to integration, and the `NLC1PBMC` pooled 3-dog library was not genotype-demultiplexed to remove inter-individual doublets.
2. **Mis-citation of K-means clustering:** Line 110 states *"This was imputed using nearest neighbor identification (FindNeighbors function) and K-means clustering (FindClusters function)."* Seurat's `FindClusters` implements Louvain/Leiden modularity optimization on the shared nearest neighbor (SNN) graph, not K-means clustering.
3. **Formatting typos throughout the text:** Multiple parenthetical formatting corruptions appear across the manuscript, likely from reference manager or typesetting conversion: e.g., line 90 `"Level (2"`, line 144 `"n = 1,(895)"`, `"T EM _(3)"`, `"Prolif_(2)"`, line 190 `"(LAG-3 and TIM-(3)"`, p. 46080 line 112 `"resolution 0.(3)"`, `"clustering (0.(8)"`.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Re-specify the compositional statistical model at the dog level:**
   * Replace the singular `glmer` model in Figs. 1H, 3E, and Table S3 with a model that properly accounts for dog-level overdispersion: either include an observation-level random effect `(1 | sample_cellgroup_id)` in `glmer`, or use `propeller` (`speckle` package in R) / beta-binomial regression (`glmmTMB`) on the dog-level cell proportions ($N = 16$).
   * Plot individual dog proportions as points overlaid on all bar/box plots (Figs. 1G, 2D, 3D, 4D, 5D, 6D, and 6J) so readers can directly see inter-dog variability within each clinical stage.
2. **Replace cell-pooled Z-score curves with dog-level pseudobulk analyses:** Aggregate raw UMI counts per dog per annotated cell type and use `DESeq2` / `edgeR` (or dog-level mean module scores with $N = 16$ as the unit of replication) to test whether $\text{CD4\_T}_{\text{H}}1$ exhaustion markers, $\text{CD8}$ effector signatures, and monocyte pro-/anti-inflammatory module scores differ significantly across LV stages.
3. **Demultiplex the pooled UK beagle control (`NLC1PBMC`):** Run `souporcell` or `freemuxlet` on the `NLC1PBMC` BAM file to assign cells to the 3 individual UK beagles, remove inter-individual multiplets, and assess batch/source differences between UK laboratory beagles and US privately owned control dogs (`NLC2`, `NLC3`).
4. **Correct the QC methods text and typographical errors:** Fix the inverted inequality signs for `Log10GenesPerUMI` and `percent.mt` in the STAR Methods, correct "K-means clustering" to "Louvain graph-based clustering," and fix all corrupted parenthetical numbers across the text.
