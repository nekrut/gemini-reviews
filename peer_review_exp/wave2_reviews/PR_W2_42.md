## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study investigates how adenosine-to-inosine (A-to-I) RNA editing by ADAR enzymes prevents endogenous double-stranded RNAs (dsRNAs) from activating the cytoplasmic antiviral sensor MDA5. Using an in vitro RNase A/T1 protection assay with recombinant murine $\text{MDA5}\Delta\text{CARD}$ on total liver RNA from 8-day-old *Adar$^{-/-}$; Mavs$^{-/-}$* and control *Mavs$^{-/-}$* mice ($n=3$ biological replicates), the authors report 1,190 peaks uniquely protected in ADAR1-deficient liver, predominantly mapping to repetitive elements (67–76% LINEs, 8–12% SINEs, ~5% LTRs). Selecting four candidate endogenous elements—two inverted SINE-containing 3' UTRs (*Rnf168*, *Knl1*), one LTR-containing 3' UTR (*Zkscan3*), and one monomeric LINE fragment—synthesized via a loopback-deficient mutant T7 RNA polymerase ($T7_{\text{FAFAG}}$), the authors show that the inverted SINE and LTR 3' UTRs (but not the single SINE or LINE RNA) stimulate MDA5 ATPase activity and EM filament assembly in vitro, and induce interferon-stimulated genes (*Stat1*, *Irf7*) and G3BP1 stress granules upon transfection into MEFs. They further show that in vitro A-to-I editing of these substrates by recombinant ADAR1p150, ADAR1p110, or ADAR2 abolishes MDA5 activation in vitro and in cells, whereas co-transcriptional substitution of GTP with ITP (at 6%, 10%, and 35% incorporation) does not.

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision Required (Mixed / Salvageable).** The manuscript provides valuable biochemical evidence that all three ADAR isoforms (ADAR1p150, ADAR1p110, and ADAR2) can edit overlapping sites in inverted SINE/LTR 3' UTRs in vitro to block MDA5 filament formation—supporting the model that ADAR1p150's unique in vivo requirement stems primarily from cytoplasmic localization rather than unique catalytic specificity. However, the study's central mechanistic control and genomic pipeline suffer from severe experimental and statistical flaws: (1) replacing GTP with ITP during in vitro transcription converts Watson-Crick **G:C** pairs into Watson-Crick **I:C** pairs, whereas ADAR editing converts Watson-Crick **A:U** pairs into wobble **I:U** pairs; thus, the IVT control conflates spatial distribution ("random vs. hotspot") with base-pair geometry (I:C vs. I:U); (2) the RNase-protection peak-calling pipeline relies on ad-hoc base-pair subtraction ($\ge 10\text{ bp}$ residual fragments) without statistical differential binding analysis, calls "peaks" on unenriched input RNA, and aligns 75 bp single-end reads to high-copy LINEs without multimapper controls; (3) cellular ADAR isoform comparisons conflate 24h transient electroporation of rat ADAR2 with 10–14 day stable lentiviral expression of human ADAR1; and (4) the manuscript contains unedited template placeholders (`Table X`, `Software XX, YY, ZZ`) and leaks a live GitHub Personal Access Token (`github_pat_...`) in the Data Availability section.

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1 (Inverted SINE and LTR elements in the 3' UTRs of *Rnf168*, *Knl1*, and *Zkscan3* form extended dsRNA structures that drive MDA5 ATPase activity, filament assembly, ISG induction, and stress granule formation, whereas monomeric LINE/SINE fragments do not):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Demonstrated across orthogonal biochemical (malachite green ATPase assay, negative-stain TEM filaments) and cellular assays (qRT-PCR of *Stat1*/*Irf7* and G3BP1 immunofluorescence in MEFs) using loopback-deficient $T7_{\text{FAFAG}}$ polymerase and 5'-dephosphorylated transcripts to exclude RIG-I and T7 foldback artifacts.
* **Claim 2 (In vitro editing of endogenous dsRNA substrates by any ADAR isoform—ADAR1p150, ADAR1p110, or ADAR2—is sufficient to prevent MDA5 polymerization and downstream IFN/stress-granule signaling):** `[Well Supported]`  
  Clearly supported by Figure 2 and Figure 3: recombinant ADAR1p150, ADAR1p110, and ADAR2 all edit overlapping hotspots in vitro and equally suppress MDA5 ATP hydrolysis, filament formation, and cellular ISG induction.
* **Claim 3 (Stochastic/random incorporation of inosines fails to suppress MDA5 activation, proving that non-stochastic ADAR hotspot positioning is required):** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
   Throughout the Abstract, Results (lines 39–53), and Discussion (line 77), the authors frame co-transcriptional ITP incorporation (replacing GTP) as a test of "random deposition of inosines" vs. "non-stochastic ADAR editing." Because ITP replaces GTP during T7 transcription, inosines are incorporated at **G** positions paired opposite **C** in the dsRNA stem, forming canonical Watson-Crick **I:C** basepairs (2 H-bonds, isosteric with A:U). In contrast, ADAR deaminates **A** opposite **U**, creating non-canonical **I:U** wobble basepairs that distort the dsRNA helix. Although the authors acknowledge the I:C vs. I:U difference at the end of the text (lines 64, 79), their experiment never tests random vs. hotspot **A-to-I** (I:U) substitutions and therefore cannot distinguish whether site-specific spacing or simply the presence of I:U wobble pairs suppresses MDA5.
* **Claim 4 (MDA5 uniquely protects 1,190 endogenous RNA peaks in *Adar$^{-/-}$; Mavs$^{-/-}$* liver, of which 67–76% are LINE elements, compared to 12% repeat content in input RNA):** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  The bioinformatic pipeline (lines 124–135) calls peaks independently in each genotype with `Genrich` and performs base-pair-wise subtraction of WT peaks from *Adar$^{-/-}$* peaks, classifying any leftover overhang $\ge 10\text{ bp}$ as a "unique ADAR-KO peak" without testing for statistically significant differential enrichment across replicates. Furthermore, baseline repeat content in input RNA ("12%, 717 peaks") was computed by running peak-calling on unenriched total RNA-seq (which selects highly expressed coding exons), and 75 bp single-end reads were mapped with `NextGenMap` without multimapper filtering on repetitive LINEs.
* **Claim 5 (ADAR2 exhibits significantly lower editing activity on endogenous 3' UTR substrates in cells than in vitro compared to ADAR1p110 and ADAR1p150):** `[Fragile]`  
  In the rescue experiment in *Adar1$^{-/-}$; Adarb1$^{-/-}$; Mavs$^{-/-}$* MEFs (Part 1 line 60; Part 2 line 1), ADAR2 was introduced as a **rat** cDNA via transient Neon electroporation and harvested 24 hours later (mixing untransfected cells and pre-existing unedited mRNA with transfected cells), whereas ADAR1p110 and ADAR1p150 were introduced via lentiviral transduction followed by 10–14 days of puromycin selection to generate pure stable lines.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Confounding of Spatial Distribution vs. Base-Pair Geometry in the IVT Inosine Assay (Pillars 1, 3, 5)
* **I:C Watson-Crick vs. I:U Wobble Base-Pairing:** In an inverted-repeat dsRNA stem, guanosines base-pair with cytosines (G:C) or uridines (G:U). Co-transcriptional replacement of GTP with ITP (10%, 20%, 50% ITP:GTP; lines 39, 157) replaces G:C pairs with **I:C** pairs (which preserve Watson-Crick backbone geometry) and actually *stabilizes* G:U wobble pairs into Watson-Crick-like I:U or alters G:A mismatches. It never converts A:U pairs into I:U wobble pairs. Consequently, comparing ITP-transcribed RNA against ADAR-edited RNA does not test whether "random vs. site-specific" inosines suppress MDA5; it tests I:C substitution against I:U wobble creation. To test whether ADAR's specific hotspot pattern is required over random I:U wobble pairs, one must compare synthetic/mutated dsRNAs carrying equal numbers of I:U (or G:U wobble mimic) pairs placed either at the conserved ADAR1p150 sites (Fig. 4) or at random A:U positions along the stem.

### 3.2 Bioinformatic and Biochemical Flaws in the MDA5 RNase-Protection Assay (Pillars 2, 3, 4)
* **Ad-Hoc Base-Pair Subtraction Instead of Differential Binding Analysis:** In the Peak Calling Pipeline (lines 129–131), WT MDA5 peaks were subtracted base-pair-wise from *Adar$^{-/-}$* MDA5 peaks, and any residual segment $\ge 10\text{ bp}$ was retained as an "exclusively ADAR-KO peak" (1,190 peaks). If the same dsRNA region is bound in both WT and *Adar$^{-/-}$* but the called peak is 10 bp wider in *Adar$^{-/-}$* due to sequencing depth or coverage variance, base-pair subtraction fabricates a "genotype-specific" peak at the boundary. Count-based differential enrichment across the 3 biological replicates (e.g., `DESeq2`/`DiffBind`/`csaw`) is required.
* **Calling "Peaks" on Unenriched Input Total RNA:** Line 28 states that *"input sequences had a 12% (717 peaks) repeat content in the Adar-/-; Mavs-/- sample (Figure 1b)"*, and line 124 confirms that `Genrich` was run on the `"no MDA5 & no RNase"` control libraries without a background control. Running a ChIP/RIP peak caller on unenriched total RNA-seq simply flags the top ~700 most abundant mRNA exons (which are depleted of repeats) as "peaks," creating an artificial baseline for repeat enrichment comparisons.
* **Use of Hydrolyzable ATP During MDA5 RNase Protection and 1,000-Fold Protein Concentration Error:** Line 114 states that $2\ \mu\text{g}$ of total RNA was incubated with `"100 μM-400 μM"` of recombinant $\text{MDA5}\Delta\text{2CARD}$ in the presence of `"1 mM ATP at 25°C for 10 minutes"` prior to RNase A/T1 digestion. First, $100\text{--}400\ \mu\text{M}$ of a ~90 kDa protein corresponds to $9\text{--}36\text{ mg/mL}$ in the reaction (exceeding the purified stock concentration and 50,000-fold higher than the $1.5\text{--}2\text{ nM}$ RNA / $1\ \mu\text{g}$ MDA5 in $50\ \mu\text{L} \approx 220\text{ nM}$ used in the ATPase and EM assays); this is almost certainly a unit error for $100\text{--}400\text{ nM}$. Second, in the presence of hydrolyzable $1\text{ mM}$ ATP, wild-type $\text{MDA5}\Delta\text{CARD}$ hydrolyzes ATP and disassembles from dsRNA stems (which is why the authors themselves used non-hydrolyzable $2\text{ mM}$ AMP-PNP for the EM filament assay in Part 2, line 16). Performing RNase footprinting in $1\text{ mM}$ ATP destabilizes bona fide dsRNA filaments while leaving non-productive monomer/dimer binding on structured single-stranded regions (such as the 67–76% LINE peaks that failed to stimulate ATPase or filament formation).

### 3.3 Species Mixing, Truncated Construct Design, and Asymmetric Transfection Protocols (Pillars 3, 6)
* **N-Terminally Truncated Human ADAR1p150 ($\Delta 2\text{--}133$) and Cross-Species Mixing:** The in vitro editing reactions on mouse RNAs used recombinant **human** ADAR1p110, **human** ADAR2, and an N-terminally truncated **human** ADAR1p150 lacking amino acids 2–133 (`Δ133 p150`, Part 2 line 36), whereas the cellular rescue in mouse triple-KO MEFs used **rat** ADAR2 (`pCDNA3-ratAdar2-FLAG`) transiently electroporated for 24h vs. **mouse/human** ADAR1p110/p150 stably transduced via lentivirus and puromycin-selected for 10–14 days (Part 2 line 1). Truncating residues 2–133 of human ADAR1p150 removes the nuclear export signal and the N-terminal portion of the $Z\alpha$ domain, which should be explicitly disclosed in the Results text when interpreting ADAR1p150 activity.
