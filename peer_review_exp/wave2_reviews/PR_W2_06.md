## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript combines high-density surface electromyography (HD-sEMG) decomposition of 1,669 motor units across six human muscles at two isometric contraction intensities ($10\%$ and $40\%$ MVC) with spiking neural network simulations and Sequential Neural Posterior Estimation (SNPE) to disentangle Renshaw cell–mediated recurrent inhibition from higher-frequency common synaptic input.

* **Claim 1:** Synchronization cross-histogram features (`trough area`, `trough timing`, `peak height`) track recurrent inhibition strength, loop delay, and higher-frequency common input amplitude in isolation, but exhibit strong mechanistic crossover because recurrent inhibition acts as a frequency-selective filter that amplifies $15\text{--}35\text{ Hz}$ coherence and short-timescale synchrony while suppressing $<15\text{ Hz}$ fluctuations. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 2:** Simulation-based inference (SNPE with Masked Autoregressive Flows) accurately and calibratedly recovers baseline excitation, recurrent inhibition strength, and higher-frequency common input amplitude ($r \ge 0.90$, $\text{RMSE} \le 0.124$, mean calibration error $\le 3.9\%$) on held-out simulations, though it cannot identify the exact frequency band center or half-width ($r \approx 0$). `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 3:** In human voluntary contractions, homonymous recurrent inhibition is lowest in the intrinsic hand muscle (FDI) and decreases from $10\%$ to $40\%$ MVC in the lower leg (GM, TA) and FDI, whereas both homonymous and heteronymous recurrent inhibition *increase* with contraction intensity in the knee extensors ($\text{VL}, \text{VM}, \text{VL}\leftrightarrow\text{VM}$). `[Moderately Supported]`
* **Claim 4:** Within motor neuron pools at $40\%$ MVC, higher-threshold (larger) motor neurons deliver stronger recurrent inhibition than lower-threshold (smaller) motor neurons, as indexed by negative-lag (`backward`) trough area ($R^2 = 0.38 \pm 0.19$, significant in 20/34 regressions). `[Moderately Supported]`

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Pooling Motor Units Across Participants Inflates Summary-Statistic Dispersion Relative to Single-Pool Training Simulations
In the simulation-based inference pipeline (Methods, p. 46080 line 117), the neural density estimator is trained on simulations of a **single motor neuron pool** (30 motor neurons receiving a single shared realization of $I_{\text{baseline}}$, $\mu_{\text{disynaptic}}$, and $\sigma(I_{\text{common,high}})$), summarized by a 16-element feature vector (`mean`, `median`, `SD`, and `IQR` of the 4 observables across the 30 neurons in that single pool). However, when applied to the experimental recordings (line 64 and p. 46080 line 117):
> *"Specifically, motor units were pooled across participants, and each observable feature was characterized by its mean, median, standard deviation, and interquartile range. These summaries yielded a 16-elements feature-summary vector (4 observables × 4 summary statistics) per experimental condition..."*

Pooling motor units across all 6 human participants before computing the `SD` and `IQR` conflates **within-pool neuron-to-neuron variance** with **between-participant physiological variance** (e.g., subject-to-subject differences in mean discharge rate, subcutaneous filtering, MVC estimation, and descending drive):
* By contrast, every training simulation has zero between-participant variance. Feeding an experimentally pooled `SD` and `IQR` (inflated by inter-subject heterogeneity) into a normalizing flow trained exclusively on single-pool `SD` and `IQR` represents a distributional domain shift (simulation-to-reality gap).
* Notably, in the posterior predictive checks (Fig. 4B, line 55), the authors only evaluated whether the posterior-predicted *means* matched the experimental feature *means*—they did not check whether the posterior-predicted `SD` and `IQR` matched the experimental `SD` and `IQR`, and `trough area` exhibited a substantial systematic posterior-predictive bias ($\text{RMSE} = 1.41$). Performing hierarchical SBI (or inferring subject-level posteriors for each participant and combining them via Bayesian hierarchical pooling / product-of-experts) would avoid distorting the dispersion summary statistics.

### 2.2 Unmodeled Spinal Circuits (Ib Non-Reciprocal Inhibition) and Intrinsic Conductances (PICs) at 40% MVC
As candidly noted in the Discussion (line 83), the leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) simulator omits persistent inward currents (PICs) and other spinal feedback loops (such as Golgi tendon organ Ib afferent inhibition and Ia reciprocal/presynaptic pathways). However, the specific impact of these omissions on the **contrast between $10\%$ and $40\%$ MVC in the vastii (VL/VM)** warrants closer scrutiny:
1. **Golgi tendon organ (Ib) di-/trisynaptic inhibition:** Ib afferents are sensitive to active muscle tension, which increases fourfold from $10\%$ to $40\%$ MVC, and project broadly across homonymous and heteronymous knee extensors ($\text{VL}\leftrightarrow\text{VM}$) with oligosynaptic delays ($\sim 15\text{--}30\text{ ms}$) that overlap the Renshaw cell trough window. Because the simulator contains only Renshaw cells as an inhibitory source, the neural density estimator is forced to attribute any tension-dependent oligosynaptic cross-histogram trough to $\mu_{\text{disynaptic}}$.
2. **Afterhyperpolarization (AHP) and PIC interactions:** At $40\%$ MVC, recruitment of higher-threshold motor units with shorter $\tau_{\text{AHP}}$ and steeper gain alters the baseline inter-spike interval curvature in the synchronization cross-histogram. In the simulator, soma diameters were fixed to a narrow range ($60 \pm 5\text{ \mu m}$, p. 46080 line 3) at both $10\%$ and $40\%$ MVC, rather than recruiting larger simulated soma diameters ($>70\text{ \mu m}$) as $I_{\text{baseline}}$ increased.

### 2.3 Confounding of the "Backward Trough Area" Size-Dependence Regression by Mean Firing Rate and Recruitment Duration
In Fig. 6C (line 74), the authors regress negative-lag (`backward`) trough area against motor unit recruitment threshold at $40\%$ MVC across 34 muscle $\times$ participant combinations, concluding that larger (higher-threshold) motor neurons deliver stronger recurrent inhibition.
* In a count-normalized synchronization cross-histogram constructed from the receiver perspective, the `backward` trough area (pre-reference spike interval) of comparison neuron $i$ reflects the reduction in firing probability of neuron $i$ *prior* to reference spikes across the pool—which by symmetry is equivalent to the post-spike suppression delivered *by* neuron $i$ to the pool, **plus** the auto-correlogram / refractory periodicity of neuron $i$ coupled through common drive.
* Because higher-threshold motor units recruited during a $40\%$ MVC trapezoid generally fire at lower mean discharge rates (onion-skin principle) and for slightly fewer total spikes than low-threshold units, and cross-histogram shape depends on the comparison neuron's discharge rate (as noted on p. 46080 line 33), a partial regression controlling for the comparison motor unit's mean firing rate and total spike count is needed to prove that the correlation with recruitment threshold is not a firing-rate artifact.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Symmetric Renshaw cell weight construction:** In p. 46080 line 12, the effective disynaptic weight matrix is defined as $\mathbf{W}_{\text{MN}\to\text{MN}} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{MN}\to\text{Renshaw}} \cdot \mathbf{W}_{\text{Renshaw}\to\text{MN}}$ with independent Gaussian entries ($\text{SD} = 0.4 \mu_{\text{monosynaptic}}$). Because normal distributions with $\text{SD} = 0.4\mu$ can take negative values (probability $\Phi(-2.5) \approx 0.6\%$), confirm whether monosynaptic weights were truncated at zero to prevent sign-violating excitatory Renshaw connections.
2. **Selection of a "representative training run" for SNPE:** In p. 46080 line 127, the authors note that SNPE training was repeated with different random seeds and that *"Posteriors reported in the Results section are derived from a representative training run."* In SBI (`sbi` toolbox), averaging the posterior across an ensemble of flows trained with different seeds (`NeuralPosteriorEnsemble`) is trivial and eliminates seed-selection variance, especially for broad posteriors like SOL at $40\%$ MVC.
3. **Male-only sample ($N = 6$):** While transparently explained in the Discussion (pilot decomposition yield in female VL/SOL), the sample size of $N = 6$ young physically active males should be noted in the Abstract so readers recognize the demographic scope.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Compare participant-level vs. pooled SBI inference:** Run the trained SNPE estimator on each individual participant's feature-summary vector (for muscle $\times$ intensity combinations passing the spike-count QC threshold) and compare the hierarchical distribution of subject-level posteriors against the pooled-unit posterior. Additionally, report posterior predictive checks for the dispersion statistics (`SD` and `IQR`) alongside the feature means in Fig. 4B.
2. **Control for mean firing rate in the size-dependence analysis (Fig. 6C):** Perform a multiple linear regression (or partial correlation) of `backward trough area` on `recruitment threshold` controlling for `mean firing rate` and `spike count` of the comparison motor unit, and simulate the `backward trough area` in the Brian 2 model with uniform $\mu_{\text{disynaptic}}$ across soma sizes ($50\text{--}80\text{ \mu m}$) to verify that uniform synaptic weights do not produce a spurious positive correlation with recruitment threshold.
3. **Test simulator sensitivity to recruited soma size distribution at 40% MVC:** Run a model-misspecification check where simulated soma diameters span a broader range ($50\text{--}85\text{ \mu m}$) at high baseline excitation to confirm that recruitment of larger motor neurons with shorter AHP time constants does not bias inferred $\mu_{\text{disynaptic}}$ upward at $40\%$ MVC.
4. **Ensemble the normalizing flow posteriors:** Replace the single "representative seed" posterior in Fig. 6 with a 5-seed ensemble posterior (`NeuralPosteriorEnsemble`) to rigorously incorporate neural network initialization uncertainty.
