## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript presents a chromosome-scale de novo nuclear and mitochondrial genome assembly and multi-tissue protein-coding gene annotation for a male Ansell's mole-rat (*Fukomys anselli*, $2n = 68$). Using Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT R10.4.1; 157.41 Gb, ~46× coverage after filtering), PCR-free Illumina short-read whole-genome sequencing (400.81 Gb, >175× coverage), and Arima Hi-C scaffolding (355.93 Gb, 13.3 million unique inter-contig reads), the authors assemble a 2.27 Gb nuclear genome across 412 scaffolds with a scaffold N50 of 72.4 Mb (L50 = 12), where the 34 largest scaffolds ($\ge 10\text{ Mb}$) capture 99% of the assembly length, matching the expected haploid number ($n = 34$). Gene completeness evaluated with `compleasm` against `glires_odb10` reaches 99.54% (99.09% single-copy, 0.45% duplicated), and BRAKER3 annotation incorporating stranded total RNA-seq from nine tissues alongside vertebrate/rodent protein evidence predicts 18,256 protein-coding genes (29,094 transcripts) with 92.35% Hystricomorpha HOG completeness via OMArk. The authors also assemble a 17,006 bp circular mitogenome validated across short and long reads and annotate transposable elements (41.26% of the genome) using Earl Grey. Raw sequencing reads, assemblies, annotations, and computational scripts are publicly deposited at NCBI (PRJNA1240209, JBPETH000000000, PV670003) and Zenodo.

The epistemic architecture is appropriate for a genome resource report: claims are largely descriptive, technical validation steps (e.g., omitting detrimental `medaka` polishing after empirical benchmarking, cross-validating the expanded mitochondrial D-loop with independent ONT assembly) are thoughtfully executed, and data/code availability is exemplary. The primary limitations are a risky uncharacterized contig filtering step during decontamination, absence of Y-chromosome identification in a male reference assembly, and minor numerical reporting omissions.

### Assessment of Specific Conclusions
* **Chromosome-scale nuclear assembly of *F. anselli* (2.27 Gb, N50 = 72.4 Mb, 99.54% glires BUSCO/compleasm completeness): [Well Supported].**  
  The hybrid Flye + NextPolish + `purge_dups` + YaHS/Juicebox pipeline is well-suited to ONT R10.4.1 + high-depth Illumina + Hi-C data. The 34 chromosome-scale scaffolds ($\ge 10\text{ Mb}$) match the known karyotype ($2n = 68$, $n = 34$) and show strong macrosynteny with *F. damarensis*.
* **Expanded 17,006 bp mitochondrial genome driven by D-loop expansion: [Well Supported].**  
  Cross-validation of the short-read `GetOrganelle` assembly against an independent long-read Flye + Pilon assembly (differing by only 1 bp) rules out short-read repeat-collapse or tandem-duplication artifacts in the control region.
* **Repetitive element landscape (41.26% genome coverage) reflects improved long-read repeat resolution rather than lineage-specific expansion relative to *H. glaber*: [Well Supported].**  
  The authors appropriately benchmark their Earl Grey repeat annotation against both legacy short-read bathyergid assemblies (~29–30% repeats) and the long-read `mHetGlaV3` naked mole-rat assembly (40.4% repeats), avoiding false evolutionary claims of TE expansion.
* **Decontamination via removal of all 6,715 BLAST-unassigned contigs without losing host dark matter: [Fragile].**  
  Discarding 6,715 contigs (~140 Mb between the 2.41 Gb contig stage and 2.27 Gb scaffolded stage) solely because they returned "no hits" in `blastn` risks removing rapidly evolving satellite DNA, centromeric/subtelomeric repeats, or Y-linked sequences rather than true non-rodent contaminants.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1. Aggressive Removal of 6,715 "No-Hit" Contigs During BlobToolKit Decontamination [Fragile]
In the Materials and Methods (Assembly, line 106) and Results (line 176), the authors state that prior to Hi-C scaffolding, contigs were removed as contamination if they "returned no hits (6,715 contigs) or were assigned to orders other than Rodentia and did not match any known mole-rat sequences (8 contigs)." Between the post-`purge_dups` assembly (7,581 contigs, 2.41 Gb) and the final scaffolded assembly (412 scaffolds, 2.27 Gb), approximately 136 Mb of sequence was removed almost entirely via the "no-hit" filter. In standard `BlobToolKit` best practices for non-model mammals, contigs with "no-hit" in nucleotide `blastn` against `nt` that exhibit read coverage and GC content consistent with the nuclear genome are retained, because divergent satellite arrays, pericentromeric heterochromatin, and Y-chromosome ampliconic sequences routinely fail to produce `blastn` alignments against reference databases. Removing all "no-hit" contigs indiscriminately can artificially deflate genome size and strip centromeric/telomeric or Y-chromosomal sequences.

### 2.2. Omission of Y-Chromosome Identification in a Male Reference Assembly [Moderately Supported]
The sequenced individual is a male (*2n = 68*, XY), and the authors identify Scaffold 1 as the X chromosome based on synteny with *F. damarensis* `NW_022900951.1` and half-depth sequencing coverage (line 188). However, the manuscript never identifies which scaffold(s) correspond to the Y chromosome, nor does it explains why there are 34 scaffolds $\ge 10\text{ Mb}$ when a male diploid genome with $2n = 68$ (33 autosome pairs + X + Y) should have 35 distinct chromosomes (or 34 if the Y chromosome is <10 Mb or fragmented/removed during the "no-hit" filtering step). Given that the authors specifically discuss sex-chromosome complexity in *F. anselli* (line 188), failing to screen the assembly for conserved mammalian Y-linked genes (e.g., *Sry*, *Zfy*, *Uty*, *Kdm5d*, *Ddx3y*, *RbmY*) leaves the status of the Y chromosome unresolved.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Merqury QV and $k$-mer Completeness Metrics Mentioned in Methods but Omitted in Results:** In Materials and Methods (line 115), the authors state they used "Mercury [sic: Merqury] with Meryl v1.4.1 (Rhie et al. 2020) to measure consensus quality (QV), error rate, and $k$-mer completeness... at all steps of the assembly pipeline" and to compare against other Bathyergidae genomes. However, neither Table 1 nor Table 2 nor the main text reports the final Merqury QV score, error rate, or $k$-mer completeness percentage.
2. **"Data not shown" for Mitogenome Validation:** Line 193 states that the ONT mitogenome assembly differed from the short-read `GetOrganelle` assembly by only one nucleotide ("data not shown") and that the 12S rRNA alignment had no gaps and few nucleotide differences ("data not shown"). Exact alignment coordinates, the position/nature of the single-nucleotide discrepancy, and pairwise percent identities should be reported directly in the text or a supplementary table.
3. **Inconsistent Anatomical Description of Skin RNA-seq Sample:** Line 99 (Methods) states total RNA was extracted from "abdomen skin tissue of a single *F. anselli* individual," whereas line 238 (Results) refers to "back skin." The anatomical site should be harmonized.
4. **Software Typo:** Line 115 spells `Merqury` as "Mercury".

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

### 1. Audit and Characterize the 6,715 Filtered "No-Hit" Contigs
* **WHY:** Discarding ~136 Mb of assembled sequence solely due to absence of `blastn` database hits risks deleting genuine *F. anselli* heterochromatin, satellite repeats, and sex-chromosome sequences.
* **WHAT:** Quantify the total length, coverage distribution, GC profile, and repeat/Y-gene content of the 6,715 removed "no-hit" contigs.
* **HOW:** Using the existing `BlobToolKit` dataset and `minimap2` BAM files, plot the GC vs. Illumina/ONT coverage distribution of the 6,715 "no-hit" contigs. Screen them with `Earl Grey` / `RepeatMasker` and `tblastn` using conserved rodent Y-linked proteins (*Sry*, *Uty*, *Kdm5d*, *Zfy*). If they represent host satellite DNA or Y-chromosomal fragments with normal (or half-depth) coverage, provide them as an unplaced pericentromeric/repeat FASTA file on Zenodo and document their composition in the text.

### 2. Identify Putative Y-Linked Scaffolds and Clarify Karyotype-to-Scaffold Correspondence
* **WHY:** A male reference assembly ($2n = 68$, XY) should account for both sex chromosomes, and readers need to know whether the 34 scaffolds $\ge 10\text{ Mb}$ represent 33 autosomes + X (with Y < 10 Mb) or another configuration.
* **WHAT:** Identify candidate Y-chromosome scaffolds/contigs in the final assembly (and purged/filtered sets).
* **HOW:** Map conserved rodent Y-chromosome protein sequences (*Sry*, *Zfy*, *Uty*, *Ddx3y*, *Kdm5d*, *Eif2s3y*) against the 412 scaffolds using `miniprot` or `tblastn`, cross-referenced with scaffolds showing ~0.5× autosomal Illumina/ONT read depth and low Hi-C trans-contact density with X outside the pseudoautosomal region. Report the identified Y scaffold(s) in the Results section.

### 3. Report Merqury Consensus QV, Error Rate, and $k$-mer Completeness in Table 1
* **WHY:** Merqury metrics were computed according to the Methods (line 115) and are essential standards for evaluating base-level accuracy in ONT + Illumina hybrid assemblies.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Add rows for Merqury QV, estimated base error rate, and $k$-mer completeness (%) to Table 1, and replace the two instances of "data not shown" on line 193 with exact numerical values (the coordinate of the 1 bp ONT vs. Illumina mitogenome difference and the exact 12S rRNA percent identity against `AY427022`–`AY427025`). Fix "Mercury" to "Merqury" (line 115) and reconcile "abdomen skin" (line 99) vs. "back skin" (line 238).
