## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript presents a blastospore-lawn confrontation bioassay combined with comparative genome mining to identify and mechanistically characterize *Pseudomonas* isolates that suppress the wheat foliar pathogen *Zymoseptoria tritici*. Screening 534 wheat-root-associated *Pseudomonas* isolates against *Z. tritici* reference strain `IPO323` identified 52 antagonistic strains (~9.7%). Quantitative radial inhibition assays of 5 antagonistic and 6 non-antagonistic *Pseudomonas* isolates across 12 genetically diverse European *Z. tritici* strains ($n = 4$ replicate plates per fungal genotype) revealed statistically significant variation in inhibition zone radii across *Z. tritici* genotypes ($p < 0.001$ for each of the 5 antagonists). Whole-genome sequencing (Illumina MiSeq, BUSCO $>99\%$), autoMLST phylogenetics, and antiSMASH 7.0 / BiG-SCAPE biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC) networking of the 11 *Pseudomonas* isolates showed that all three *P. corrugata*-subgroup antagonists (`Roth82`, `Roth107`, `Roth112`) carry a BGC matching the antifungal polyketide 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol (`2,4-DAPG`; `GCF_02551`), whereas two *P. mandelii*-subgroup antagonists (`BFR20`, `BFB20`) lack known antifungal MIBiG matches and harbor candidate orphan BGCs. Insertional mutagenesis of the core polyketide synthase gene `phlD` in `Roth82` (`Roth82 phlD::pKNOCK-Km`) abolished `2,4-DAPG` production in agar extracts as measured by UPLC-QTOF-MS (`m/z 209.0455`) and eliminated visible *Z. tritici* growth inhibition. Genomes (`PRJNA1164014`), raw phenotype data (`10.23637/wya4d1b7`), and analysis scripts (GitHub / Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.18865490`) are publicly deposited.

This is a well-executed, reproducible proof-of-concept study with exemplary data and code availability. However, three methodological and analytical limitations should be addressed: (1) **shared-plate diffusion interference and missing interaction modeling**—arraying all 11 *Pseudomonas* isolates on the same $120 \times 120\text{ mm}$ agar plate causes neighboring inhibition zones to coalesce (requiring directional censoring of overlapping radii) and introduces plate-level non-independence, while running five separate one-way ANOVAs omits a formal two-way mixed-effects test of the claimed `Pseudomonas` $\times$ `Z. tritici` genotype interaction; (2) **confounding of agar inhibition zone radii with fungal lawn growth kinetics**—differences in clearing zone radius after 12 days can arise from differences in *Z. tritici* blastospore division/melanization rates on PDA rather than intrinsic chemical insensitivity ($\text{MIC}$), and comparing a single 1984 lab strain (`IPO323`) against 11 modern strains cannot support claims of temporal selection for resistance; and (3) **technical reporting errors**, notably an impossible camera pixel scale (`2253 pixels = 1 mm` on a 10.2-megapixel Nikon D80 DSLR).

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **The blastospore confrontation assay reliably identifies *Pseudomonas* isolates that inhibit *Z. tritici* in vitro (52/534 isolates): [Well Supported].**  
  All 5 qualitatively antagonistic isolates suppressed all 12 diverse *Z. tritici* genotypes in the quantitative screen, whereas all 6 non-antagonistic isolates and medium controls produced zero clearing across all 12 fungal genotypes (Fig. 2, Fig. S7).
* **In vitro antagonism of *Z. tritici* by *P. corrugata*-subgroup isolate `Roth82` is mediated by 2,4-DAPG biosynthesis (`phlD`): [Well Supported].**  
  Supported by high BGC synteny with MIBiG references `BGC0000280`/`BGC0000281` (Fig. 3b), complete abolition of the inhibition zone in the `Roth82 phlD::pKNOCK-Km` insertional mutant, and loss of the `m/z 209.0455` peak matching authentic `2,4-DAPG` in UPLC-QTOF-MS (Fig. 5).
* **Antagonistic *P. mandelii*-subgroup isolates `BFR20` and `BFB20` lack known antifungal BGCs and harbor candidate orphan GCFs (`GCF_00030`, `GCF_00023`, `GCF_00028`, `GCF_02559`, `GCF_00016`): [Well Supported].**  
  The antiSMASH 7.0 and BiG-SCAPE network comparisons against 1,808 MIBiG 3.0 reference clusters clearly show that `BFR20` and `BFB20` lack `2,4-DAPG`, phenazine, pyrrolnitrin, or pyoluteorin clusters, making their unique GCFs logical candidates for follow-up mutagenesis/fractionation.
* **Natural *Z. tritici* populations exhibit genotype-by-antagonist quantitative variation in resistance to bacterial secondary metabolites, potentially reflecting historical selection since 1984 (`IPO323`): [Fragile].**  
  While the one-way ANOVAs ($F(11, 36)$, $p < 0.001$) confirm that clearing zone radii vary across the 12 *Z. tritici* strains for each *Pseudomonas* isolate, the authors did not fit a two-way `Pseudomonas` $\times$ `Z. tritici` interaction model, did not control for strain-specific fungal growth rates on PDA (which directly alter inhibition zone diameter under agar diffusion), did not determine broth $\text{MIC}_{50}$ values with purified `2,4-DAPG`, and have only $n = 1$ pre-2015 isolate (`IPO323`, maintained in lab culture since 1984).

## 3. Forensic Technical Audit & Constructive Remediation Plan

### Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

#### 3.1. Shared-Plate Diffusion Overlap, Directional Angle Censoring, and Omission of a Two-Way Mixed-Effects Model
In the Methods (line 126), the quantitative assay arrayed 11 *Pseudomonas* isolates and 1 negative control on the same $120 \times 120\text{ mm}$ PDA plate inoculated with a single *Z. tritici* strain ($n = 4$ replicate plates per *Z. tritici* strain).
1. **Inter-colony metabolite diffusion and censoring bias:** Because 5 antagonistic *Pseudomonas* colonies were grown simultaneously on the same plate for 12 days, secreted metabolites (`2,4-DAPG` and unknown diffusible compounds) diffused toward neighbouring colonies. The authors state: *"Where a randomly selected radial intersected a region in which zones of clearing from neighbouring colonies met or overlapped... that direction was treated as non-measurable and a new random angle was generated."* Censoring directions where zones coalesce systematically excludes directions facing other antagonists while retaining directions facing plate edges or non-antagonists, and does not eliminate sub-inhibitory additive diffusion of metabolites across the shared agar matrix.
2. **Missing two-way interaction test and plate random effect:** In the Results (line 45), the authors analyzed the data using five separate one-way ANOVAs (one per *Pseudomonas* strain, $F(11, 36)$). However, in the Discussion (line 91), they claim that *"no Z. tritici isolate tested was consistently the most sensitive or resistant towards all antagonistic Pseudomonas isolates screened"*, inferring distinct modes of action across *Pseudomonas* strains. Formally testing whether the rank order of *Z. tritici* sensitivity differs across *Pseudomonas* strains requires a two-way linear mixed-effects model ($\text{Radius} \sim \text{Pseudomonas} \times \text{Z\_tritici} + (1|\text{Plate\_ID})$) that tests the `Pseudomonas : Z_tritici` interaction term while accounting for the shared plate random effect.

#### 3.2. Confounding of Agar Clearing Zone Radius with Fungal Lawn Growth Rate and `IPO323` ($n = 1$) Historical Comparison
In classical agar diffusion assays (Cooper, 1963), the squared radius of the zone of inhibition ($R^2 \propto 4 D \cdot T_{\text{crit}} \ln(C_0 / \text{MIC})$) is proportional to the critical time $T_{\text{crit}}$ required for the target microorganism to reach a threshold cell density that commits to visible lawn formation. Genetically diverse *Z. tritici* field isolates vary substantially in blastospore division rate, lag phase, and melanization on PDA at $16\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$. A slower-growing *Z. tritici* isolate yields a larger clearing zone radius even if its intrinsic chemical sensitivity ($\text{MIC}$) to `2,4-DAPG` is identical to that of a faster-growing isolate. Furthermore, comparing `IPO323` (a single reference strain isolated in 1984 and passaged for four decades) against 11 isolates from 2015/16 ($n = 1$ vs. $n = 11$) has zero statistical degrees of freedom for historical epoch and cannot distinguish evolutionary selection for fungicide resistance from lab adaptation or individual strain variance.

### Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Impossible Pixel-to-Millimeter Calibration Factor (`2253 pixels = 1 mm`):** In the Methods (line 126), the authors state that FIJI/ImageJ was used *"to quantify the number of pixels (2253 pixels = 1 mm)."* A Nikon D80 DSLR camera has a $3872 \times 2592\text{ pixel}$ sensor (10.2 MP). Across a $120\text{ mm}$ square Petri dish, $2253\text{ pixels/mm}$ would require an image width of $270,360\text{ pixels}$! Evidently, the scale was **$22.53\text{ pixels} = 1\text{ mm}$** (i.e., $2253\text{ pixels} = 100\text{ mm}$, or $22.53\text{ pixels/mm}$).
2. **Repeated Typo `"blastopore"` Instead of `"blastospore"`:** In the Fig. 1a legend (lines 30, 33), Fig. 2a legend (lines 40, 43), Fig. 4 legend (lines 61, 64), and main text (line 66), fungal *blastospores* are misspelled as *"blastopores"* (an embryological term).
3. **Unclosed Parentheses and Inconsistent Strain Hyphenation:** Lines 16 and 52 contain unclosed citation parentheses `"(Levy et al., 13 ."`. Strain `BFR20` is written as `BF-R-20` on lines 66 and 68, and `AB20` as `A-B-20` on line 66.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Fit a Two-Way Linear Mixed-Effects Model to Formally Test the `Pseudomonas` $\times$ `Z. tritici` Interaction
* **WHY:** Running five separate one-way ANOVAs does not test whether *Z. tritici* sensitivity profiles differ significantly across *Pseudomonas* antagonists (the genotype-by-antagonist interaction claimed in line 91) and ignores the fact that all 5 antagonists were spotted on the same physical Petri dishes.
* **WHAT:** Fit a linear mixed-effects model on the existing quantitative dataset ($5\text{ Pseudomonas} \times 12\text{ Z. tritici} \times 4\text{ plates} = 240$ colony means) with `Plate_ID` as a random intercept.
* **HOW:** In the authors' R script (`lme4` / `lmerTest` + `emmeans`), run `lmer(log_Radius ~ Pseudomonas * Z_tritici + (1 | Plate_ID), data = df)` and report the $F$-statistic, degrees of freedom, and $p$-value for the main effects (`Pseudomonas`, `Z_tritici`) and the `Pseudomonas:Z_tritici` interaction term.

#### 2. Temper Claims of Evolved Resistance Since 1984 and Acknowledge Fungal Growth-Rate Effects on Diffusion Zones
* **WHY:** With only $n = 1$ isolate from 1984 (`IPO323`) and no measurement of fungal growth rate ($T_{\text{crit}}$) or purified `2,4-DAPG` broth $\text{MIC}_{50}$, differences in agar clearing zones cannot be uniquely attributed to evolved chemical resistance.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * In the Discussion (lines 89–93), explicitly note that variation in radial clearing zone size on solid agar can reflect differences in *Z. tritici* blastospore growth kinetics ($T_{\text{crit}}$) as well as metabolite sensitivity ($\text{MIC}$), and recommend broth microdilution $\text{MIC}_{50}$ assays with purified `2,4-DAPG` as the direct test to separate the two.
  * Temper the statement on line 89 attributing the difference between `IPO323` (1984) and the 2015/16 isolates to historical selection for broad-spectrum antifungal insensitivity, noting that $n = 1$ historical reference strain cannot separate temporal selection from individual strain variation or laboratory domestication.

#### 3. Correct the ImageJ Pixel Calibration Scale and Textual Typos
* **WHY:** Reporting `2253 pixels = 1 mm` is a 100-fold decimal error for a Nikon D80 image.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * In Methods line 126, correct `(2253 pixels = 1 mm)` to the true calibration (e.g., `22.53 pixels = 1 mm` or `2253 pixels = 100 mm`).
  * Replace all instances of `"blastopore"` / `"blastopores"` with `"blastospore"` / `"blastospores"` (lines 30, 33, 40, 43, 61, 64, 66), fix `"(Levy et al., 13 ."` (lines 16, 52), and standardize `BF-R-20` $\to$ `BFR20` and `A-B-20` $\to$ `AB20` (lines 66, 68).
