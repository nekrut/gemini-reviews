## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript investigates whether root-zone soil bacteria from two contrasting cropping systems—common bean (*Phaseolus vulgaris*) and switchgrass (*Panicum virgatum*)—reactivate from dormancy in response to exogenous plant stress hormones abscisic acid (ABA) and salicylic acid (SA). Field soils collected near roots from four replicate plots per crop were composited by crop, heat-dried at $55\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ for 3 days to induce dormancy, acclimated at 25% water-holding capacity (WHC) for 2 weeks, and then brought to 50% WHC with ABA (in 1 mL methanol), SA (in 1 mL methanol), a methanol carrier control (1 mL methanol), or sterile water across four replicate 100 g mesocosm jars per treatment. Sampling 3 technical subsamples per jar at Days 1, 7, and 14 ($N = 324$ passing paired DNA and cDNA libraries rarefied to 12,000 reads), the authors classify OTUs as "active" when $\text{16S rRNA (cDNA)} / \text{16S rRNA gene (DNA)} \ge 1$ (converting "phantom taxa" with $\text{DNA} = 0, \text{cDNA} > 0$ to $\text{DNA} = 1$) and analyze the filtered DNA counts using PERMANOVA, CAP, and DESeq2. They report that 3 OTUs (*Microbispora*, *Streptomyces*, and *Myxococcaceae*) were enriched at Day 1 in ABA-treated switchgrass soil, while 1–2 *Microbispora* OTUs were enriched at Day 1 in SA-treated bean and switchgrass soils and persisted to Day 14. Raw sequences are deposited in NCBI SRA (`PRJNA932434`) and R scripts are available on GitHub.

While using paired 16S rRNA/rDNA amplicon sequencing in a Before-After-Control-Impact (BACI) framework is a thoughtful ecological approach, the study suffers from four major experimental and statistical flaws: (1) **multi-level pseudoreplication**—compositing the 4 field plots per crop into a single homogenized bucket ($N = 1$ composite soil per crop/site) completely confounds crop identity with geographic site/edaphic properties, while sampling 3 technical subsamples from each of the 4 mesocosm jars ($4 \times 3 = 12$) without collapsing by jar ID pseudoreplicates all PERMANOVA, ANOVA, and DESeq2 tests; (2) **extreme methanol carrier and hormone dosing artifacts**—adding 1 mL of pure methanol ($792\text{ mg}$) per 100 g dry soil injects **$2,970\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}\text{ dry soil}$** of solvent carbon (6–12$\times$ more carbon than the $252\text{--}541\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$ of SA/ABA, which are themselves applied at $\sim 15\text{--}20\text{ mM}$, $>10^6$-fold above physiological nanomolar soil concentrations) after a $55\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ heat treatment that selectively kills non-sporulating bacteria; and (3) **invalid statistical application of DESeq2** to pre-rarefied, threshold-censored count tables without testing the direct `Hormone vs. Methanol Control` interaction. Computational re-analysis collapsing technical replicates and testing direct BACI interaction contrasts against the methanol carrier is essential, alongside substantial tempering of the "cry for help" signaling claims.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Root-zone microbiomes from different crops exhibit distinct crop-legacy activation dynamics in response to phytohormones (Hypothesis 1): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  Because field soils from the 4 plots at the East Lansing bean farm and the 4 plots at the Kellogg Biological Station switchgrass site were composited into a single bucket per site prior to the experiment (Methods line 44), there is zero biological replication at the crop/field level ($N = 1$ composite soil per site). As the authors acknowledge in the Discussion (line 166), crop identity is 100% confounded with site geography and baseline soil chemistry (e.g., 3.6-fold difference in Bray P and 2-fold difference in Ca and $\text{NO}_3\text{-N}$, Table 1), making it statistically impossible to attribute differences to crop legacy (H1).
* **Spore-forming Actinobacteria (*Microbispora*, *Streptomyces*) and *Myxococcaceae* become enriched within 24 hours of ABA/SA addition and persist for 14 days (Hypothesis 2): [Fragile].**  
  First, drying temperate field soil at $55\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ for 3 days pasteurizes vegetative Gram-negative bacteria and strongly selects for heat-resistant spores (*Microbispora*, *Streptomyces*, *Bacilli*, and myxospore-forming *Myxococcaceae*). Second, the DESeq2 enrichment analysis compared `Day 1 Hormone vs. Day 0 Water-Acclimated` rather than directly testing `Day 1 Hormone vs. Day 1 Methanol Control`; line 146 explicitly admits that both highlighted *Microbispora* OTUs *"were detected as active in methanol controls"*. Third, DESeq2 was run on $n = 12$ pseudoreplicated subsamples (4 jars $\times$ 3 technical subsamples) after rarefying and zero-censoring inactive counts.
* **Immediate (24 h) activation versus 14-day persistence distinguishes phytohormone "cry for help" signaling from carbon resource utilization: [Overstated].**  
  Adding $0.3\text{ mmol}$ of SA or ABA (~15–20 mM in soil pore water, supplying $252\text{--}541\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$ dry soil) dissolved in 1 mL of pure methanol (supplying $2,970\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$ dry soil) represents a massive organic carbon and solvent perturbation $>10^6$-fold above physiological nanomolar root exudation. Reactivation of thermotolerant actinomycete spores after $55\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ heat shock and methanol/millimolar carbon amendment cannot be equated to a physiological plant "cry for help" signal.

## 3. Forensic Technical Audit & Constructive Remediation Plan

### Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

#### 3.1. Two-Tiered Pseudoreplication: Field Compositing ($N = 1$) and Intra-Mesocosm Technical Subsampling ($4 \times 3 = 12$)
1. **Field plot compositing:** In the Methods (lines 42–44), soil was collected from 4 replicate field plots per crop, but *"the following day after sample collection, soils from replicate plots were composited into ethanol-sterilized buckets and hand-mixed to homogenize."* Compositing destroys field-plot replication ($N = 1$ composite soil per crop/location).
2. **Intra-jar technical pseudoreplication:** Each experimental condition consisted of **4 replicate mesocosm jars**, from which **3 technical 0.5 g subsamples** were extracted at each time point ($4 \times 3 = 12$ samples per condition per time point; Fig. 1 caption, line 63). In Table 2 (PERMANOVA), the alpha-diversity ANOVAs (line 117), Kruskal-Wallis tests (line 130), and DESeq2 differential abundance tests (lines 142–145), the 3 technical subsamples from the same glass jar (and repeated longitudinal samplings of the same jar across Days 1, 7, and 14) were treated as independent biological replicates ($n = 12$ per group) rather than being collapsed to the mesocosm jar level ($n = 4$ true replicates) or modeled with mesocosm jar ID as a random/blocking factor (`strata = Mesocosm_ID`).

#### 3.2. Massive Methanol Carrier Carbon Input ($2,970\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$), Equimolar vs. Equicarbon Contradiction, and $55\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ Heat Selection
In the Methods (line 72), the authors state:
> *"Phytohormones were dissolved in methanol to create a 0.3 M solution, and 1 mL of the phytohormone-methanol solution was filter sterilized (0.2 µm) before adding to the mesocosms... We normalized the hormone addition based on the amount of carbon added per g of dry soil, and a 3 M solution for ABA and SA resulted in approximately 250–500 μg C g-1 dry soil for SA and ABA."*

1. **Stoichiometric and unit contradictions (`0.3 M` vs. `3 M` and unequal carbon dosing):** Adding 1 mL of `0.3 M` (not `3 M`, which is a 10-fold typo) salicylic acid ($\text{C}_7\text{H}_6\text{O}_3$, 7 carbons) to 100 g dry soil delivers $0.3\text{ mmol} \times 84.08\text{ mg C/mmol} / 100\text{ g} = \mathbf{252\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}}$. Adding 1 mL of `0.3 M` abscisic acid ($\text{C}_{15}\text{H}_{20}\text{O}_4$, 15 carbons) to 100 g dry soil delivers $0.3\text{ mmol} \times 180.17\text{ mg C/mmol} / 100\text{ g} = \mathbf{541\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}}$. Thus, the hormone additions were **equimolar (`0.3 M`)**, NOT carbon-normalized, with ABA supplying 2.15$\times$ more carbon than SA.
2. **Methanol solvent dwarfs the phytohormone carbon input by 6- to 12-fold:** Delivering 1 mL of pure methanol ($\rho = 0.792\text{ g/mL}$, $24.7\text{ mmol CH}_3\text{OH}$) to 100 g dry soil adds **$297\text{ mg}$ of methanol carbon = $2,970\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}\text{ dry soil}$** (a ~10% v/v methanol concentration in the ~10 mL of added water!). This massive C1 substrate pulse and solvent stress explains why the microbial response was overwhelmingly driven by methanol (lines 146–148, Fig. S5E, Fig. S6).
3. **Pasteurization artifact from $55\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ drying:** Drying temperate soils at $55\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ for 72 hours (line 55) exceeds physiological field soil temperatures and thermally kills vegetative Gram-negative cells while preserving heat-resistant actinomycete spores (*Microbispora*, *Streptomyces*), *Bacilli* endospores, and *Myxococcaceae* myxospores.

#### 3.3. Invalid Use of DESeq2 on Rarefied, Ratio-Truncated Count Matrices and Missing Direct BACI Contrasts
1. **Violation of DESeq2 statistical model assumptions:** In lines 99 and 104, the authors rarefied all DNA and cDNA libraries to 12,000 reads, zeroed out all DNA counts where $\text{cDNA}/\text{DNA} < 1$, converted "phantom taxa" ($\text{DNA} = 0, \text{cDNA} \ge 1$ without any minimum read threshold) to $\text{DNA} = 1$, and then input this rarefied, zero-truncated DNA table into **DESeq2**. DESeq2's negative binomial GLM requires unrarefied raw counts to estimate library size factors and dispersion-mean shrinkage; artificial zero-censoring and $\text{DNA} = 1$ imputation distort both size factors and dispersion estimates.
2. **Difference-in-significance fallacy instead of a BACI interaction test:** Despite listing *"before-after-control-impact design (BACI)"* as a keyword (line 9), the authors identified hormone-responsive OTUs by testing `Day 1 Hormone vs. Day 0 Water-Acclimated` in DESeq2 and checking whether the OTU was also significant in `Day 1 Methanol Control vs. Day 0 Water-Acclimated`—while admitting in line 146 that the *Microbispora* OTUs *"were detected as active in methanol controls but not as consistently enriched as in the SA treatment."* A valid BACI analysis requires testing the interaction term $(\text{Day 1}_{\text{Hormone}} - \text{Day 0}_{\text{Hormone}}) - (\text{Day 1}_{\text{MeOH}} - \text{Day 0}_{\text{MeOH}})$ or directly contrasting `Day 1 Hormone vs. Day 1 Methanol Control`.

### Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Sample Count Arithmetic Discrepancy in Figure 1 Caption:** Line 63 states: *"Similarly, 12 samples were collected for each soil/treatment combination (bean ABA, bean SA, switchgrass ABA, switchgrass SA) at three time points (Day 1, Day 7, and Day 14) totaling 12\*4\*3 = 144 samples."* This omits the methanol control and water control treatments (which add another $12 \times 4 \times 3 = 144$ samples, bringing the time-series total to 288 samples and the grand total with baselines to 333 samples).
2. **Phantom Taxa Without Minimum cDNA Read Threshold:** Line 99 states that all $\text{DNA} = 0$ counts with $\text{RNA} > 0$ were changed to $\text{DNA} = 1$, and line 104 notes *"we did not set a threshold for detecting phantom taxa across samples."* Thus, a single singleton cDNA read ($\text{cDNA} = 1, \text{DNA} = 0 \to 1$) yields a ratio of $1/1 = 1.0$ and classifies the taxon as "active".
3. **Species Name Typo:** Line 34 spells *Phaseolus vulgaris* as `"Phasoelus vulgaris"`.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Collapse Technical Subsamples to Mesocosm Jar Units ($n = 4$) and Run Direct BACI Interaction Contrasts Against the Methanol Control
* **WHY:** Treating 3 technical subsamples from the same jar as independent replicates ($n = 12$) is pseudoreplication, and comparing `Day 1 Hormone vs. Day 0` without directly testing against `Day 1 Methanol Control` conflates methanol reactivation with phytohormone effects.
* **WHAT:** Aggregate technical subsamples within each mesocosm jar ($n = 4$ biological replicates per treatment) and test the direct contrast `Hormone (ABA or SA) vs. Methanol Carrier Control` at Days 1, 7, and 14.
* **HOW:** In the authors' R workflow (`phyloseq` / `vegan`), sum or average the 3 technical subsamples per jar ID so that $n = 4$ per treatment-timepoint. In `vegan::adonis2`, specify `permutations = how(nperm = 999, blocks = Mesocosm_ID)` for longitudinal models. For OTU-level testing, run `DESeq2` (or `ANCOM-BC2` / `MaAsLin2`) at the jar level ($n = 4$) using the explicit contrast `Treatment_ABA_vs_MeOH` and `Treatment_SA_vs_MeOH` at Day 1, and report which OTUs remain significant at $\text{FDR} < 0.05$ above the methanol carrier.

#### 2. Require a Minimum cDNA Read Threshold ($\ge 3\text{--}5$ Reads) for "Phantom Taxa" Classification
* **WHY:** Allowing a single cDNA read ($\text{cDNA} = 1, \text{DNA} = 0 \to 1$) to classify an OTU as "active" ($\text{ratio} = 1.0$) introduces noise from PCR chimeras, reverse-transcription errors, and index hopping.
* **WHAT & HOW:** Re-run the activity classification script in GitHub requiring $\text{cDNA} \ge 3$ reads (or detection in at least 2 technical subsamples of the same mesocosm jar) before converting $\text{DNA} = 0$ phantom taxa to $\text{DNA} = 1$, and verify that the *Microbispora*, *Streptomyces*, and *Myxococcaceae* responder trajectories in Fig. 4 are robust.

#### 3. Correct the Stoichiometric Reporting (`0.3 M` vs. `3 M`, Methanol Carbon Load) and Reframe Hypothesis 1 and "Cry for Help" Claims
* **WHY:** The 1 mL methanol carrier added $2,970\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$ dry soil (6–12$\times$ the hormone carbon), SA and ABA were added equimolarly ($252$ vs. $541\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$) rather than equicarbon, and compositing field plots ($N = 1$ per crop/site) prevents testing crop legacy (H1) separately from site edaphic differences.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * In Methods line 72, fix `3 M` to `0.3 M`, report the exact carbon added by SA ($252\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$), ABA ($541\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$), and the 1 mL methanol carrier ($2,970\text{ }\mu\text{g C g}^{-1}$), and fix the sample-size arithmetic in the Fig. 1 legend (line 63) and the `"Phasoelus"` typo (line 34).
  * In the Abstract, Results (line 155), and Discussion (line 166), replace claims of "crop-specific / crop legacy" effects (H1) with "soil-source-specific (bean-field vs. switchgrass-field)" effects, and explicitly note how $55\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ heat-drying and millimolar hormone + methanol carrier additions select for thermotolerant spore-forming Actinobacteria.
