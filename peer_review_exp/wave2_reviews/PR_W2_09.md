## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This manuscript evaluates the MIME-seq2.0 method—which uses an engineered chimeric methyltransferase (`HENMT1ΔC-T6B`, termed the MIME enzyme) that binds Argonaute (Ago) proteins and 2'-$O$-methylates the 3' terminal ribose of Ago-bound small RNAs to protect them from sodium periodate ($\text{NaIO}_4$) oxidation—to profile Ago-bound small non-coding RNAs (sncRNAs) in mouse MIN6B1 insulinoma cells and to track extracellular vesicle (EV)-mediated miRNA transfer from Jurkat T cells and C2C12 myotubes to MIN6B1 cells.

* **Claim 1:** Polyadenylation-based RT-qPCR (`miRCURY LNA`) and small RNA-seq following $\text{NaIO}_4$ oxidation accurately discriminate unmethylated from MIME-methylated miRNAs in MIN6B1 cells ($\sim 25\text{--}80\%$ recovery with MIME vs. $<0.1\%$ without MIME), while endogenous piRNAs remain unaffected by MIME expression. `[Well Supported]`
* **Claim 2:** In addition to miRNAs, specific Y-RNA fragments (Y1, Y3) and tRNA-derived fragments (`tRF Val-CAC`, `tRF Asp-GTC`) are protected from oxidation by the MIME enzyme and co-immunoprecipitate with Ago2, demonstrating their association with Argonaute proteins in living cells. `[Moderately Supported]`
* **Claim 3:** MIME-seq2.0 specifically tracks the intercellular transfer of EV-encapsulated miRNAs from human Jurkat T cells (`miR-142-3p`, `miR-142-5p`, `hsa-miR-155-5p`) and murine C2C12 myotubes (`miR-1-3p`, `miR-133b`) into recipient MIN6B1 cells, proving that elevated recipient miRNA levels reflect physical transfer rather than endogenous transcriptional induction. `[Fragile]`

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1 Invalid Statistical Testing ("One-Way Paired t-Test" on Exponentially Transformed Ratios, Uncorrected Multiple Comparisons, and Missing Statistics in Fig. 5)
The statistical analysis across all figures suffers from multiple foundational flaws:
1. **"One-way paired t-test" terminology and use of one-tailed tests:** In the Methods (line 101) and captions for Figs. 1D, 2D, 3B–C, and 4D, the authors state that significance was assessed using a *"one-way paired t-test"* ($p < 0.05$). In statistics, "one-way" refers to an analysis of variance (ANOVA), whereas $t$-tests are either **one-tailed (one-sided)** or **two-tailed (two-sided)**. Using a one-tailed paired $t$-test halves the p-value, which is particularly problematic when $n = 3$ biological replicates are used. Two-sided tests must be the default unless a strict pre-registered directional hypothesis is justified.
2. **Parametric t-tests on exponentially transformed percentage ratios ($100 / 2^{\Delta Ct}$):** In line 96, recovery is calculated as $\% \text{ Recovery} = 100 / 2^{\Delta Ct}$. Because Ct values are normally distributed on a logarithmic scale, $2^{-\Delta Ct}$ follows a heavily skewed log-normal distribution bounded below by 0. Running a paired Student's $t$-test on raw percentage recoveries comparing $\sim 0.05\%$ vs. $\sim 25\%$ across $n = 3$ replicates violates normality and homoscedasticity; statistical testing must be performed on the log-scale $\Delta Ct$ (or $\Delta\Delta Ct$) values, with family-wise error rate correction (e.g., Holm–Bonferroni) across the multiple sncRNAs tested in each panel.
3. **Complete omission of sample size and statistical tests in Figure 5:** In Fig. 5A–B (C2C12 myotube EV transfer of `miR-1-3p` and `miR-133b` to MIN6B1 cells, line 150), the figure caption and main text omit the number of biological replicates ($n$), the definition of error bars, and any statistical test or p-values.
4. **Underpowered small RNA-seq ($n = 2$):** The sequencing experiment in Fig. 2A–C and Supplementary Tables 2–3 was performed on only $n = 2$ independent experiments (line 122), precluding formal differential methylation / FDR estimation (e.g., via `DESeq2` interaction modeling of $\text{Genotype} \times \text{Oxidation}$).

### 2.2 Lack of Essential Controls to Exclude EV-Mediated Transfer of the MIME Enzyme/mRNA or Surface-Adhered EVs
In the intercellular EV transfer experiments (Figs. 4 and 5), donor Jurkat T cells or C2C12 myotubes were lentivirally transduced to stably overexpress the `HENMT1ΔC-T6B` (MIME) enzyme, and isolated EVs were incubated with recipient MIN6B1 cells for 24 h followed by two PBS washes and Qiazol lysis (line 76). Two major experimental confounders are not controlled for:
1. **Packaging of MIME enzyme protein or mRNA into donor EVs:** Overexpressed cytosolic/nuclear-periphery proteins and their mRNAs are frequently packaged into EVs. If the MIME enzyme (or its lentiviral mRNA) is transferred via EVs into recipient MIN6B1 cells, it could methylate endogenous Ago-bound miRNAs inside the recipient cells. While the lack of protection of endogenous `mmu-miR-155-5p` in the Jurkat-to-MIN6B1 experiment (Fig. 4D) argues against bulk methylation of pre-existing recipient miRNAs in that setting, no Western blot or RT-qPCR was performed on purified Jurkat or C2C12 EVs (or recipient MIN6B1 cells) to verify that the Myc-tagged MIME enzyme/mRNA is absent from the EV preparation. In the intraspecies C2C12-to-MIN6B1 experiment (Fig. 5), `mmu-miR-1-3p` and `mmu-miR-133b` have identical sequences in donor and recipient cells, making it impossible to distinguish transferred methylated miRNA from EV-delivered MIME enzyme methylating newly induced recipient miRNAs without a catalytically dead MIME mutant or donor-specific tag.
2. **Surface-adhered intact EVs vs. functional cytoplasmic uptake and RISC loading:** Washing cells twice with PBS after 24 h incubation with a high dose of EVs ($0.5\text{ to }5 \times 10^{10}\text{ particles/mL}$—a 10-fold variation in dose across replicates, line 76!) does not remove intact EVs adsorbed to the extracellular plasma membrane or trapped in endolysosomes. Thus, extracting total cellular RNA with Qiazol simply co-extracts the donor EV cargo stuck to the outside of the cells. To prove genuine intercellular transfer into the recipient cytoplasm, cells should be trypsinized and proteinase K/RNase-treated prior to lysis, or recipient Ago2 should be immunoprecipitated to show that donor-methylated miRNAs are loaded into recipient RISC.

### 2.3 Overexpression Artifacts in Flag-Ago2 Co-Immunoprecipitation and Low tRF Recovery
To validate that MIME-protected Y-RNA fragments (Y1, Y3) and tRNA fragments (`tRF Val-CAC`) genuinely bind Argonaute proteins (Fig. 3, lines 124–132), the authors transiently overexpressed `Flag-Ago2` in MIN6B1 cells alongside MIME overexpression.
* For `tRF Val-CAC`, $\text{NaIO}_4$ recovery increased only modestly from $1.9\%$ in control pcDNA cells to $6.0\%$ in MIME-expressing cells (line 124, vs. $44\text{--}80\%$ for canonical dominant miRNAs), and its recovery in the `Flag-Ago2` IP was very low (Fig. 3C).
* Transient overexpression of both `HENMT1ΔC-T6B` and `Flag-Ago2` increases free Ago2 concentration in the cytoplasm, which is known to promote low-affinity, non-canonical scavenging of abundant tRNA and Y-RNA degradation fragments. Including a T6B-mutant HENMT1 control (lacking the Ago-binding peptide) and performing endogenous Ago2 IP (rather than `Flag-Ago2` overexpression) would rigorously separate genuine endogenous RISC association from overexpression-driven proximity methylation.

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Dynamic Lattice Light Scattering (ZetaSizer) used for EV particle concentration:** In line 71, the authors state *"Particle concentration was assessed using a ZetaSizer."* Standard dynamic light scattering (DLS) on a Malvern ZetaSizer measures hydrodynamic size distribution and polydispersity index (PDI), but is notoriously inaccurate for absolute particle concentration compared to Nanoparticle Tracking Analysis (NTA / NanoSight) or tunable resistive pulse sensing (TRPS), which may explain the 10-fold variation in reported EV concentrations ($0.5\text{--}5 \times 10^{10}\text{ particles/mL}$, line 76).
2. **Missing EV characterization (MISEV guidelines):** In accordance with MISEV guidelines, EV preparations isolated by ultracentrifugation should be characterized by Western blotting for positive EV markers (e.g., CD63, CD81, syntenin-1/TSG101) and negative contamination markers (e.g., calnexin, Ago2 outside EVs).
3. **Grammatical issue in title:** *"The MIME-seq technique allows to monitor..."* should be *"The MIME-seq2.0 technique enables monitoring of..."* (`allows to [verb]` is non-standard English syntax).

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

1. **Correct the statistical analysis and complete Figure 5 reporting:**
   * Re-analyze all RT-qPCR datasets (Figs. 1D, 2D, 3B–C, 4B, 4D, 5B) using **two-sided** paired $t$-tests on the log-scale $\Delta Ct$ values (prior to $2^{-\Delta Ct}$ transformation), and apply Holm–Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons across tested sncRNAs.
   * Report the exact sample size ($n$), error bar definition (mean $\pm$ SD), and two-sided statistical test results in the caption and plot of Figure 5A–B.
2. **Add controls for EV-mediated MIME transfer and surface EV adsorption:**
   * Perform a Western blot (anti-Myc) and RT-qPCR on purified Jurkat and C2C12 EVs to test whether the MIME enzyme or its mRNA is packaged into EVs.
   * Harvest EV-treated MIN6B1 cells using trypsinization followed by PBS washes (rather than in-well Qiazol lysis after PBS rinse) to strip surface-adhered EVs, and include a $4^\circ\text{C}$ incubation or endocytosis-inhibitor control to verify active EV uptake.
3. **Validate endogenous Ago2 binding and T6B dependence for Y-RNA and tRF fragments:** Perform endogenous Ago2 immunoprecipitation (using a pan-Ago or anti-mouse Ago2 antibody in untransfected MIN6B1 cells) and test a `HENMT1ΔC` construct lacking the T6B Ago-binding domain to confirm that Y1, Y3, and `tRF Val-CAC` methylation strictly requires endogenous Ago interaction.
