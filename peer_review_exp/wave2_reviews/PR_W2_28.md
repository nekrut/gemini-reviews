## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This study analyzes task-fMRI data from $N = 415$ healthy participants performing a rapid 8-minute visual target-detection task organized into sixteen 15-second "foraging patches" (12–13 trials per block, mean ISI = $1.23\text{ s}$) cued for either high (20 cents/hit) or low (1 cent/hit) monetary reward. Modeling Time on Task (`ToT`) as the centered linear trial number within each 15-second block, the authors report: (1) widespread cortical activation increases (peaking in ACC and right anterior insula) and sensorimotor/visual association decreases across the 15-second block, both amplified under high reward; (2) main effects of reward in the ventral tegmental area/substantia nigra (`VTA/SN`) and nucleus accumbens (`NAcc`), but no decrease in `VTA/SN` or `NAcc` over `ToT`; (3) significant `ToT` and `ToT` $\times$ `Reward` increases in the basal forebrain mapped to the cholinergic `Ch4` nucleus basalis of Meynert (`NBM/Ch4`) and `VTA/SN` (but not `NAcc` or locus coeruleus `LC`). The authors interpret the `NBM/Ch4` interaction as a human neuroimaging phenotype of cholinergic compensatory cognitive effort.

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision (Mixed / Salvageable).**  
The sample size ($N = 415$) provides high statistical power, and the behavioral mixed-effects modeling and whole-brain permutation testing (8,000 resamples with voxel-level FWE control) are well executed. However, three major methodological and neurobiological issues undermine the specificity of the primary claims:
1. **Unmodeled First-Trial Transient and Canonical HRF Ramp Confounding in the First-Level fMRI GLM:** In the behavioral analysis (line 36), the authors found a massive first-trial slowing effect (+84.6 ms on Trial 1 relative to Trials 2–13, $t = 117.5, p < 0.001$; accuracy $z = -7.9$) and explicitly included a `first trial indicator` in their behavioral `lmer`/`glmer` models. Crucially, however, they omitted a first-trial indicator from the **first-level fMRI GLM** (line 161), where all 12–13 rapid trials ($\text{ISI} = 1.23\text{ s}$) within a 15-second block were modeled only by an unmodulated impulse train plus a linear `ToT` slope. Because 12 impulses spaced by $1.23\text{ s}$ convolve into a single 15-second boxcar, any neural/motor transient on Trial 1—or any regional deviation from the canonical HRF rise/plateau kinetics over 15 seconds—loads directly onto the linear `ToT` parametric modulator.
2. **Spatial Resolution Overreach in Distinguishing `NBM/Ch4` from the Centromedial Amygdala at $3\text{ mm}$ EPI Resolution:** Data were acquired with $3 \times 3 \times 2.5\text{ mm}$ voxels ($0.5\text{ mm}$ gap; $3\text{ mm}$ isotropic effective resolution) and smoothed at $4\text{ mm}$ FWHM after upsampling to $1.5\text{ mm}$ MNI space. In both the `ToT` main effect and the `ToT` $\times$ `Reward` interaction (lines 55, 66), the reported peak coordinates for `NBM/Ch4` (`21, -4, -11` and `-19, -1, -13`) and the `centromedial amygdaloid nucleus` (`21, -4, -13` and `-19, -2, -14`) are separated by **only 1.4 to 2.0 mm**—less than a single native $3\text{ mm}$ EPI voxel! Claiming specific isolation of cholinergic `Ch4` versus adjacent basal forebrain/amygdalar/pallidal structures from identical native voxels overstates the spatial precision of $3\text{ mm}$ 3T EPI.
3. **Behavioral Ceiling Effect (>99% Accuracy, 0.3 ms/trial Slowing) vs. "Increasing Cognitive Effort" Framing:** Equating a 15-second trial sequence of left/right dot detections (>99% accuracy; 0.3 ms/trial RT change after Trial 1) with "increasing levels of cognitive effort" and "compensatory cholinergic effort" stretches the psychological construct of sustained-attention effort, especially when task rules forced maximal effort on both low- and high-reward blocks to reach the 20-euro payout threshold.

### Assessment of Specific Claims
* **Claim 1 (Short `ToT` within 15 s blocks recruits an extensive cortical gradient peaking in ACC and anterior insula while deactivating sensorimotor cortex, modulated by reward level; Figs. 2–4): [Fragile].**  
  Statistically robust at $N = 415$, but mechanistically confounded because the fMRI GLM omits the Trial 1 indicator (which had an 84.6 ms RT cost, $t = 117.5$) and cannot separate true within-block effort changes over 15 seconds from Trial 1 onset transients and regional HRF shape differences across a $1.23\text{ s}$ ISI train.
* **Claim 2 (`VTA/SN` and `NAcc` track block reward level, and `VTA/SN` increases rather than decreases with `ToT` and `ToT` $\times$ `Reward`; Figs. 2, 5): [Moderately Supported].**  
  Replicates main reward effects in `VTA/SN` and `NAcc` and falsifies the authors' a priori opportunity-cost discounting hypothesis, though the `ToT` $\times$ `Reward` interaction in `VTA/SN` is modest ($t = 5.15, p = 0.025$ at $N = 415$).
* **Claim 3 (Basal forebrain `Ch4` (`NBM`) activity specifically increases with `ToT` and `ToT` $\times$ `Reward`, identifying a cholinergic neuroimaging phenotype of compensatory cognitive effort; Abstract, Figs. 2, 3, 5): [Overstated].**  
  The reported `NBM/Ch4` peak (`21, -4, -11`, $t = 9.19$) and centromedial amygdala peak (`21, -4, -13`, $t = 9.24$) lie within the exact same $3\text{ mm}$ native EPI voxel ($2.0\text{ mm}$ apart in $1.5\text{ mm}$ resampled space), and BOLD fMRI cannot distinguish cholinergic from non-cholinergic or amygdalar/striatopallidal activity at this resolution.
* **Claim 4 (The Locus Coeruleus (`LC`) is not involved in short `ToT` or its modulation by reward; Results line 66, Discussion line 109): [Fragile].**  
  Standard whole-brain $3\text{ mm}$ EPI with $4\text{ mm}$ smoothing, standard MNI registration (without neuromelanin-sensitive T1 localization), and no cardiac/respiratory physiological pulsatility regression (RETROICOR) lacks the spatial and physiological specificity required to rule out LC involvement.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Omission of the Trial-1 Indicator in the First-Level fMRI GLM and Hemodynamic Collinearity Across a 15-Second Block (Pillars 3, 4)
* **Location:** Results ("Behavioral data", line 36) and Materials and methods ("Data analysis", lines 161, 171).
* **Defect:**
  1. In the behavioral analysis (line 36), Trial 1 of each foraging block was 84.6 ms slower than subsequent trials ($t = 117.5, p < 0.001$) and significantly less accurate ($z = -7.9, p < 0.001$), whereas the linear `ToT` slope across Trials 2–13 was only 0.3 ms/trial ($t = 4.5$). Accordingly, the authors included a `first trial indicator` in their behavioral `lmer` and `glmer` models (line 171).
  2. In the first-level fMRI GLM (line 161), however, no `first trial indicator` was included: all 12–13 target presentations ($\text{mean ISI} = 1.23\text{ s}$) were modeled only by an unmodulated onset series and a centered linear `ToT` parametric modulator. Because Trial 1 has the largest negative weight in the centered linear `ToT` vector, a strong motor/visual/orienting response on Trial 1 induces a negative `ToT` slope in sensorimotor and visual cortices (explaining the prominent sensorimotor "deactivations" in Fig. 2 and Table S2!).
  3. Furthermore, over a 15-second block with $1.23\text{ s}$ ISI and $\text{TR} = 2.46\text{ s}$ (~6 TRs per block), a centered linear parametric modulator convolved with SPM's canonical HRF is mathematically equivalent to the temporal derivative / dispersion basis function of a 15-second block, meaning any brain region with a slower hemodynamic rise or prolonged plateau relative to the canonical HRF will appear as a positive `ToT` effect.
* **Remediation:** Re-estimate the first-level fMRI GLM including a separate `first trial indicator` regressor (matching the behavioral `lmer` model in line 171) and/or HRF temporal and dispersion derivatives on the main foraging block regressor, and verify whether the cortical sensorimotor decreases and subcortical `ToT` increases survive after controlling for the Trial 1 transition effect.

### 3.2 Sub-Voxel Spatial Separation Between Reported `NBM/Ch4` and Centromedial Amygdala Peaks (Pillars 3, 6)
* **Location:** Results (lines 55, 66) and Materials and methods (lines 149, 154, 159, 163).
* **Defect:** Native EPI voxels were $3 \times 3 \times 2.5\text{ mm}$ ($0.5\text{ mm}$ gap), resampled to $1.5\text{ mm}$ isotropic MNI space and smoothed with a $4\text{ mm}$ FWHM Gaussian kernel (effective smoothness $\ge 5\text{ mm}$ FWHM). In line 55 (`ToT` main effect), the reported peak for the centromedial amygdala is `(25, -1, -11)` ($t = 14.09$) and for `Ch4 NBM` is `(24, -1, -10)` ($t = 13.20$)—a Euclidean distance of $1.41\text{ mm}$. In line 66 (`ToT` $\times$ `Reward` interaction), the reported peak for `NBM/Ch4` is `(21, -4, -11)` ($t = 9.19$) and for the centromedial amygdala is `(21, -4, -13)` ($t = 9.24$) on the right, and `(-19, -1, -13)` ($t = 6.40$) vs. `(-19, -2, -14)` ($t = 5.29$) on the left—Euclidean distances of $2.00\text{ mm}$ and $1.41\text{ mm}$, respectively. Reporting adjacent $1.5\text{ mm}$ interpolated grid points within the exact same native $3\text{ mm}$ voxel as distinct anatomical activations of `NBM/Ch4` and centromedial amygdala creates a false impression of sub-millimeter anatomical specificity.
* **Remediation:** Explicitly acknowledge in the Results and Discussion that the `NBM/Ch4` and centromedial amygdaloid peaks fall within a single native $3\text{ mm}$ EPI voxel at the ventral pallidum / basal forebrain / dorsal amygdala border, and refer to the cluster conservatively as "basal forebrain / centromedial amygdala region (overlapping the probabilistic Ch4 mask)" rather than claiming isolated cholinergic Ch4 specificity.

### 3.3 Sign Error in Pallidum $t$-Statistic and Unspecified Multi-Site Covariate in Second-Level fMRI Permutation (Pillar 7)
* **Location:** Results ("Effect of ToT", line 55) and Materials and methods ("Participants", line 137; "fMRI data acquisition", line 149; "Data analysis", lines 163, 171).
* **Defect:**
  1. **Sign Typo (line 55):** *"In subcortical regions, ToT was associated with activity decreases in the pallidum (-15, -4, -8, t = -12.67, and 16, -2, -7, t = 9.58)"*—the right pallidum $t$-value for an activity decrease is printed as positive `t = 9.58` instead of `t = -9.58`.
  2. **Multi-Site Scanner Covariate:** Data were acquired on two different 3T scanner models at two sites ($n = 58$ on a Siemens Skyra in Bonn; $n = 357$ on a Siemens Prisma in Ulm). While `acquisition site` was included as a covariate in the behavioral `lmer` model (line 171), line 163 does not state whether `acquisition site` was included as a nuisance covariate in the second-level fMRI permutation tests.
* **Remediation:** Correct `t = 9.58` to `t = -9.58` on line 55, and state in the Methods ("Data analysis", line 163) whether scanner site (Bonn Skyra vs. Ulm Prisma), age, and sex were included as covariates in the second-level SPM/permutation models.
