## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript maps single-trial cortico-limbic effective connectivity and responsiveness across multi-day sleep-wake cycles in 15 hospitalized patients with pharmacoresistant epilepsy implanted with stereotactic depth electrodes (SEEG; $n = 13$ with scalp-EEG-scored sleep). Using Louvain community clustering on the 583-patient F-tract atlas to define 10 cortical macro-regions, the authors repeatedly probed 37,850 effective connections with 3 mA, 1 ms biphasic pulses (median 288 trials per connection over ~46 hours) and 14-step intensity curves (0.2–12 mA in 2,365 connections). To move beyond trial-averaged cortico-cortical evoked potentials (CCEPs), they introduce a single-trial detection metric ($\pm \rho^2 \cdot \text{LL}$, combining 250 ms line length with signed squared cross-correlation against connection-specific $K = 2$ waveform centroids) to estimate **signaling probability ($P_{AB}$)**, **Directionality Index ($\text{DI}_{AB}$)**, and **Excitability Index ($\text{ExI}$)**. They report that local connections ($<25\text{ mm}$) transmit reliably ($P = 99\%$) and bidirectionally ($|\text{DI}| = 0.01$), whereas long-range connections ($\ge 25\text{ mm}$) exhibit variable probability and marked asymmetry. Across both wakefulness and sleep, limbic structures (amygdala and hippocampus) evoke significant responses in neocortex roughly twice as often as neocortical stimulations evoke responses in limbic structures ($\text{DI} = +0.61$ and $+0.35$). During NREM and REM sleep, hippocampal-to-neocortical efferences show a 5–6% reduction in $\text{ExI}$ and up to a 25% reduction in response magnitude to cingulate cortex without reversing directionality. Preprocessed data, analysis scripts, and an interactive visualization GUI are publicly shared on GitHub.

This is an ambitious, technically impressive human intracranial dataset that advances CCEP mapping from static trial averages to time-resolved single-trial probabilities across vigilance states. However, two primary limitations affect the interpretation of the main conclusions: (1) **epistemic conflation of macroscopic electrical stimulation with endogenous information flow**—measuring whether a 3 mA current pulse in the hippocampus evokes a macroscopic LFP deflection in neocortex tests orthodromic/antidromic circuit excitability and anatomical fan-out, which does not falsify endogenous sharp-wave-ripple-mediated memory replay ("information flow reversal") during sleep; and (2) **statistical thresholding and modeling artifacts**, specifically zeroing out reverse connections that fail the centroid pre-filter (which artificially forces median $|\text{DI}| = 1.00$ in long-latency pathways), calling single trials at an uncorrected $\alpha = 0.05$ floor, and fitting a fixed-effects logistic regression (Eq. 3) across 37,850 non-independent connections without participant random effects. These issues can be addressed through computational sensitivity analyses and conceptual reframing.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **Single-trial CCEP transmission is invariant and bidirectional in local ($<25\text{ mm}$) connections ($P = 99\%$, $|\text{DI}| = 0.01$) but probabilistic and strongly correlated with connection excitability ($\text{ExI}$, $r = 0.60$) in long-range pathways: [Well Supported].**  
  The contrast between local short-latency responses and long-range responses across $>3\text{ million}$ trials is robust, and the correlation between multi-hour $P_{AB}$ at 3 mA and short-term dose-response $\text{ExI}$ (0.2–12 mA) in 2,365 connections provides strong internal validation of the single-trial metric.
* **Long-range long-latency ($\ge 65\text{ ms}$) connections are predominantly unidirectional with median $|\text{DI}| = 1.00$: [Fragile].**  
  Because $\text{DI}_{AB} = (P_{AB} - P_{BA}) / \max(P_{AB}, P_{BA})$ is computed after pre-filtering for "effective connections" (where at least one $K=2$ centroid exceeds the 95th percentile of baseline surrogates), any pair $(A,B)$ where $A \to B$ passes the centroid filter ($P_{AB} \approx 21\%$) but $B \to A$ falls just below the centroid filter has $P_{BA}$ treated as $0$, mechanically forcing $|\text{DI}_{AB}| = 1.00$. Conversely, when both directions pass the pre-filter, uncorrected one-sided single-trial testing at $\alpha = 0.05$ imposes a ~5% false-positive floor on both $P_{AB}$ and $P_{BA}$.
* **Electrical stimulation of limbic structures (amygdala and hippocampus) evokes neocortical responses ~2-fold more frequently than neocortical stimulation evokes limbic responses ($\text{DI} = +0.61$ and $+0.35$): [Well Supported].**  
  This asymmetry is confirmed at the participant level using FDR-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across the 15 individuals (Fig. 3c, e) and persists when excluding epileptogenic-zone channels (Supplementary Fig. 10). However, its biophysical interpretation should account for stimulation geometry (stimulating compact nuclear structures or dense output bundles like the alveus/fornix/amygdalofugal pathways versus stimulating a single column of laminated neocortex).
* **Cortico-limbic information flow does not reverse during NREM or REM sleep, challenging the classical two-stage rodent model of memory consolidation: [Overstated].**  
  The rodent two-stage memory model (Buzsáki, 1989) concerns *spontaneous, endogenous neural firing and replay* (waking sensory-driven entorhinal-to-hippocampal activity vs. NREM sharp-wave-ripple-driven hippocampal-to-neocortical replay). Probing the brain with an exogenous 3 mA, 1 ms electrical shock measures macroscopic orthodromic/antidromic responsiveness (evoked effective connectivity), not spontaneous information transfer. A 5–6% decrease in evoked hippocampal-to-neocortical $\text{ExI}$ during NREM sleep reflects sleep-state modulation of synaptic gain/refractoriness, not the absence of endogenous hippocampal-to-cortical replay.

## 3. Forensic Technical Audit & Constructive Remediation Plan

### Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

#### 3.1. Epistemic Conflation of Evoked Macroscopic Excitability (CCEPs) with Endogenous Neural Information Flow
Throughout the Abstract (line 5), Introduction (lines 12–16), Results (line 70), and Discussion (line 83), the authors frame single-pulse electrical stimulation (SPES/CCEP) directionality as a direct test of the two-stage cortico-limbic memory consolidation model. However:
1. **Exogenous 3 mA current injection vs. endogenous physiological signaling:** A 3 mA, 1 ms biphasic pulse across macro-contacts (0.8 mm diameter, 2 mm length) injects $3\text{ }\mu\text{C}$ per phase, directly depolarizing local axons of passage (including antidromic activation of afferent fibers) and synchronous populations of projection neurons.
2. **Anatomical convergence/divergence asymmetry:** Stimulating a compact, densely projecting structure (such as the basolateral amygdala or hippocampal CA1/subiculum output tracts) excites a high fraction of efferent fibers projecting broadly to orbitofrontal, cingulate, and insular cortices. Conversely, stimulating a single $3.5\text{ mm}$ bipolar site within a vast neocortical sheet excites only a tiny fraction of the distributed cortical neurons projecting back to the hippocampus/amygdala, which is further gated by strong entorhinal/perirhinal feedforward inhibition. Thus, $\text{DI} > 0$ reflects macroscopic fan-out and gating asymmetry under focal current injection, and the slight NREM decrease in hippocampal efferent $\text{ExI}$ ($-6.3\%$) does not contradict endogenous sharp-wave-ripple replay during sleep.

#### 3.2. Selection-Threshold Bias at $|\text{DI}| = 1.00$ and In-Sample Centroid Circularity in $\pm \rho^2 \cdot \text{LL}$
1. **Boundary spike at $|\text{DI}| = 1.00$:** In line 50 and Fig. 2b, long-range long-latency ($\ge 65\text{ ms}$) connections are reported to have a median $|\text{DI}|$ of exactly `1.00 [0.63, 1.00]`. From Eq. 2, $|\text{DI}_{AB}| = 1.00$ can only occur when $\min(P_{AB}, P_{BA}) = 0$. Because single trials are tested at an uncorrected one-sided 95th percentile ($\alpha = 0.05$, Methods line 31 of offset 46080), any connection where single trials are actually evaluated has an expected noise floor of $P \approx 5\%$ even under the null. Therefore, $P_{BA} = 0$ occurs because reverse connections ($B \to A$) that did not pass the initial average-centroid "effective connection" threshold were assigned $P_{BA} = 0$ rather than having their raw single-trial rate $P_{BA}$ quantified symmetrically! Conditioning on $A \to B$ passing a threshold while setting sub-threshold $B \to A$ to zero creates a severe truncation artifact at $|\text{DI}| = 1.00$.
2. **In-sample waveform centroid overfitting:** For each effective connection, the $K=2$ K-means waveform centroids are computed across the post-stimulation trials of that connection, and each trial's line length is multiplied by $\pm \rho^2$ (its maximum squared correlation with those same centroids within $\pm 10\text{ ms}$ lag) and compared to 400 pre-stimulation baseline segments (Methods, lines 1–6 of offset 46080). Because the centroids are fit in-sample to the post-stimulation data (and not cross-validated out-of-fold), post-stimulation trials have an upward bias in $\rho^2$ compared to pre-stimulation baseline segments.

#### 3.3. Unmodeled Hierarchical Clustering in Fixed-Effects Logistic Regression (Eq. 3) and Connection-Level Tests
In the Methods ("Participant level", Eq. 3, line 36 of offset 46080), the logistic regression model for signaling probability is specified as:
$$\text{logit}(P) = \beta_0 + \beta_1(\text{Region}_{\text{stim}} : \text{Region}_{\text{resp}}) + \beta_2 d$$
This model treats all connections (or region pairs) across the 15 participants as independent observations with fixed effects only, omitting random intercepts for participant ($\text{u}_{\text{participant}}$), stimulating electrode ($\text{u}_{\text{stim\_elec}}$), and recording electrode ($\text{u}_{\text{resp\_elec}}$). Similarly, the Kruskal-Wallis, Mann-Whitney $U$, and Pearson correlation tests in Fig. 2b ($n = 37,850$), Fig. 2d–e ($n = 2,365$), and Fig. 3f (connection-level ExI curves tested via Wilcoxon rank-sum without FDR correction) pool thousands of connections from 15 patients as if they were independent, inflating test statistics and yielding uncalibrated $p$-values (e.g., line 50: `"p = 0 for P and DI"`).

### Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Reporting Exact Zero $p$-Values:** Line 50 reports `"p = 0 for P and DI, one-way Kruskal-Wallis"`. Exact $p$-values in continuous/rank tests are never zero and should be reported as $p < 10^{-15}$ (or with the exact $H$ statistic and degrees of freedom).
2. **Inconsistent Minimum Stimulation Intensity in Methods:** Line 108 states `"Single-pulse electrical stimulations (1 ms biphasic square-wave, 0.5 to 12 mA)"`, whereas lines 21, 55, 108 (earlier sentence), and 16 (offset 46080) state `"0.2 to 12 mA"`.
3. **High Proportion of Mesiotemporal Lobe Epilepsy (9/15 Patients):** Nine of the 15 participants had mesiotemporal epilepsy (Methods line 93). Although Supplementary Fig. 10 compares connections inside vs. outside the clinically defined epileptic network, structural remodeling or gliosis in mesiotemporal structures should be explicitly discussed as a potential contributor to limbic stimulation responsiveness.

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Eliminate Truncation Bias in $\text{DI}_{AB}$ by Computing Unconditional Symmetric $P_{AB}$ and $P_{BA}$
* **WHY:** Setting $P_{BA} = 0$ when the reverse connection ($B \to A$) fails the centroid pre-filter while $A \to B$ passes creates an artificial spike at $|\text{DI}_{AB}| = 1.00$ (especially in long-latency connections where median $|\text{DI}| = 1.00$).
* **WHAT:** Recompute $\text{DI}_{AB}$ unconditionally for every probed bipolar pair $(A,B)$ where at least one direction is effective by evaluating single-trial $P_{BA}$ in the reverse direction using the same baseline-surrogate procedure (without zeroing out sub-threshold $B \to A$), and subtract the expected 5% false-positive floor ($\tilde{P} = \max(0, P - 0.05) / 0.95$).
* **HOW:** Using the preprocessed single-trial matrices in the authors' GitHub repository, compute raw $P_{BA}$ for all pairs where $P_{AB}$ is an effective connection, plot the resulting untruncated $|\text{DI}|$ distribution alongside Fig. 2b, and verify that participant-level cortico-limbic directionality (Fig. 3c–e) remains significant. Also implement 5-fold out-of-sample cross-validation when estimating the $K=2$ waveform centroids so that trial $i$ is never included in the centroid to which it is correlated.

#### 2. Add Participant and Electrode Random Effects to Equation 3 and Fig. 3f
* **WHY:** Pooling 37,850 connections from 15 patients into a fixed-effects logistic regression (Eq. 3) or connection-level Wilcoxon rank-sum test (Fig. 3f) violates independence due to within-patient and within-electrode clustering.
* **WHAT:** Replace the fixed-effects logistic regression in Eq. 3 with a generalized linear mixed-effects model (GLMM) including random intercepts for participant and stimulating/recording electrodes, and aggregate Fig. 3f stimulation-response curves to the participant level before testing.
* **HOW:** Fit a binomial GLMM (`statsmodels` `BinomialBayesMixedGLM` or `lme4::glmer` in R): $\text{logit}(P_{ijk}) = \beta_0 + \beta_1(\text{Region}_{\text{stim}} : \text{Region}_{\text{resp}}) + \beta_2 d_{ij} + u_{\text{patient}(k)} + v_{\text{stim}(i)} + w_{\text{resp}(j)}$, and report the cluster-adjusted $p$-values and marginal/conditional $R^2$. For Fig. 3f, test within-participant paired differences between limbic efferent and afferent ExI using a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test across the $N = 15$ participants with FDR correction.

#### 3. Reframe the Interpretation of Evoked CCEP Directionality vs. Endogenous Sleep Replay
* **WHY:** Macroscopic 3 mA electrical stimulation measures orthodromic/antidromic circuit excitability and anatomical convergence/divergence, not spontaneous endogenous information transfer during sleep replay.
* **WHAT:** Temper the claims in the Abstract, Introduction, Results (line 70), and Discussion (line 83) that CCEP measurements falsify the two-stage cortico-limbic information flow reversal model during sleep.
* **HOW:** Explicitly distinguish *evoked effective responsiveness* (probed via 3 mA SPES) from *spontaneous physiological information transfer* (e.g., sharp-wave-ripple-coupled replay). Discuss how nuclear vs. laminar stimulation geometry (stimulating compact hippocampal/amygdalar output tracts vs. focal neocortical sites) and entorhinal gating contribute to the observed $\text{DI} > 0$, and fix the minor text issues (`p = 0` on line 50; `0.5 to 12 mA` vs. `0.2 to 12 mA` on line 108).
