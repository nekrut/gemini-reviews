## 1. Summary of Claims & Epistemic Architecture

This methods and microbial ecology manuscript introduces MATRIX (Microbial Activity and Total cell quantification via Rapid Imaging and eXtraction), a workflow combining two-step Nycodenz density-gradient extraction of cells from soil, parallel fluorescence staining with SYBR Green I (total cells) and RedoxSensor Green (RSG, reductase-active cells), automated fluorescence imaging on a commercial slide counter (QUANTOM Tx), custom background-flattening and watershed segmentation image analysis (`process_quantom`), and hierarchical Bayesian generalized linear modeling (`brms` / `RStan`). The authors validate MATRIX across five experimental settings: (1) pure batch growth curves of *Escherichia coli* and *Pseudomonas protegens* (showing nonlinear, strain-specific relationships between $\text{OD}_{600}$ and total/active cell counts and a positive slope between cell growth rate and log cell volume), (2) 18 switchgrass rhizosphere isolates standardized to $\text{OD}_{600} = 0.1$ (demonstrating wide inter-strain variation in cell concentration, active fraction, and morphology), (3) spike-in recovery of GFP-tagged *Azospirillum baldaniorum* from alpine soil ($75\% \pm 18\%$ recovery) and lysozyme-resistance fractionation across three French soils, (4) comparison of summed single-cell RSG fluorescence with GC-measured bulk soil $\text{CO}_2$ respiration rates across 48 soil mesocosms (3 sites $\times$ 2 seasons $\times$ 3 timepoints), and (5) a 72-hour diurnal heatwave simulation ($35^\circ\text{C}/45^\circ\text{C}$) on temperate and alpine soils. Raw `.tif` images, processed `.csv` datasets, image-processing scripts, and Bayesian `brms`/`RStan` notebooks are all openly archived on FigShare and GitHub.

MATRIX addresses a genuine bottleneck in quantitative microbiome profiling, and the open availability of raw images, segmentation code, and Bayesian models is commendable. However, several mathematical, physical, and biological issues require correction: (1) an algebraic sign error in the delayed first-order respiration kinetic equation, (2) over-parameterization and failure to propagate extraction uncertainty or constrain $p_{\text{active}} \le 1$ when SYBR and RSG counts are measured on separate unpaired slides, (3) an artifactual protocol step in the lysozyme-resistance assay (`10 mM EDTA + 1% Triton X-100` for 100 min at $37^\circ\text{C}$), which lyses outer membranes and dissipates proton motive force/reductase cofactors in Gram-negative bacteria independently of peptidoglycan hydrolysis, and (4) unmodeled Nycodenz density-gradient bias ($1.0\text{ g/mL}$ vs. standard $1.3\text{ g/mL}$).

### Assessment of Specific Conclusions
* **Optical density ($\text{OD}_{600}$) is nonlinearly related to total cell concentration and fails to reflect the active cell fraction across growth phases and diverse rhizosphere isolates: [Well Supported].**  
  Demonstrated clearly across *E. coli* and *P. protegens* growth curves (Figs. 1–2) and 18 switchgrass rhizosphere isolates diluted to $\text{OD}_{600} = 0.1$ (Fig. 3), where cell size, shape, and redox state decouple light scattering from viable cell number.
* **Summed single-cell RSG fluorescence correlates positively with bulk soil respiration rate ($k$) and labile substrate pool ($C_0$) across soils and seasons: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The empirical association across the 48 soil samples (Fig. 5) is positive, though the first-order kinetic equation stated in the text contains a sign error in the delay term and the two-stage "regress estimates on estimates" procedure does not propagate full posterior covariance unless modeled jointly.
* **Lysozyme-resistance fractionation proves that $0\%$ of lysozyme-sensitive soil bacteria are inactive ($\text{SI} = 0\%$) and that most active soil bacteria are lysozyme-resistant (Fig. 4B): [Methodologically Flawed].**  
  First, the "lysozyme buffer" contains **1% Triton X-100 and 10 mM EDTA** incubated at $37^\circ\text{C}$ for 100 minutes (line 62) with **no buffer-only (Triton + EDTA without lysozyme) control**; 1% Triton X-100 + 10 mM EDTA solubilizes lipid membranes and permeabilizes vegetative cells even without lysozyme. Second, because total decreases by only 21–24% while active cells drop from ~74% to ~50% (a decrease of ~24% of initial total cells!), the algebraic deduction that $\text{SI} = 0\%$ rests on the false assumption that the 100-minute $37^\circ\text{C}$ incubation in 1% Triton X-100 + 10 mM EDTA + lysozyme only lyses sensitive cells without inactivating RSG redox activity in surviving unlysed cells!
* **The hierarchical Negative Binomial model jointly estimates total counts and active proportion ($0 \le p \le 1$) from unpaired SYBR and RSG slides: [Moderately Supported].**  
  Modeling $\mu_{\text{SYBR}} = \mu_{\text{total}}$ and $\mu_{\text{RSG}} = \mu_{\text{total}} \cdot p_{\text{active}}$ with a logit link on $p_{\text{active}}$ constrains the parameter $p_{\text{active}} \in (0, 1)$, which is good practice, though in samples where noisy RSG counts approaches or exceeds SYBR counts (e.g., *E. coli* in Fig. 1A where RSG $\approx$ SYBR), truncating the prior at 1.0 forces the posterior against the boundary ($100\%$, line 96) and can upward-bias $\mu_{\text{total}}$.

## 2. Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

### 2.1. Confounded Detergents (`1% Triton X-100 + 10 mM EDTA`) and Flawed Algebraic Subtraction in the Lysozyme Assay (Lines 62, 110) [Methodologically Flawed]
In lines 62 and 110 (Figure 4), the authors compare untreated Nycodenz-extracted soil cells (stained immediately with SYBR or RSG) against cells pelleted and incubated for **100 min at $37^\circ\text{C}$ in $1\text{ mg/mL}$ lysozyme, $50\text{ mM}$ Tris-HCl, $10\text{ mM}$ EDTA, and $1\%$ Triton X-100**. They observe that after treatment, total SYBR counts drop by ~23% (e.g., Miribel 7: from 100% to 76% remaining), while active RSG counts drop from 77% of initial total to $53\% \times 76\% = 40.3\%$ of initial total (or 53% of remaining cells). They then solve a 4-compartment conservation system ($\text{RI}, \text{RA}, \text{SA}, \text{SI}$) assuming that:
1. Every cell that remained intact after 100 min in 1% Triton X-100 + 10 mM EDTA + lysozyme at $37^\circ\text{C}$ retained its exact pre-treatment RSG redox status (i.e., zero active resistant cells lost reductase activity during 100 min of detergent/chelator exposure!).
2. Under that assumption, since total cells dropped by ~24% and active cells dropped by ~24–37%, the entire lysed ("sensitive") fraction must have been active ($\text{SA} \approx 24\%$), forcing the sensitive-inactive fraction to hit the boundary constraint $\text{SI} = 0\%$ in all three soils!
In reality, incubating live bacteria in **1% Triton X-100 (a non-ionic detergent above its critical micelle concentration) and 10 mM EDTA at $37^\circ\text{C}$ for 100 minutes** strips outer membranes, permeabilizes cytoplasmic membranes, leaks NADH/NADPH cofactors required for RSG reduction, and kills or inactivates cells that do not physically disintegrate (leaving their DNA intact for SYBR staining while abolishing RSG reduction). Thus, $\text{RA} \to \text{RI}$ conversion during the 100-minute detergent incubation is misattributed to $\text{SA}$ lysis, artificially driving $\text{SI}$ to $0\%$.

### 2.2. Mathematical Sign Error and Non-Identifiability in the Soil Respiration Kinetic Model (Line 71) [Methodologically Flawed]
In Materials and Methods (line 71), the authors write:
$$\text{CO}_2(t) \sim C_0 \cdot \left(1 - e^{-k \cdot (t + d)}\right)$$
where $C_0$ is the unknown concentration of limiting substrate, $k$ is the constant rate, and $d$ is the "delay ($d$) when recording started" (after the 30-minute acclimation).
1. **Physical/Boundary Condition Error:** At $t = 0$ when the flask is hermetically sealed and $\text{CO}_2$ accumulation begins, a delay or acclimation offset $d$ before recording starts means $\text{CO}_2$ accumulated during the acclimation window is subtracted from initial baseline or shifted by $t - d$. However, in the written equation $C_0(1 - e^{-k(t+d)})$ without baseline subtraction, $C_0$ and $d$ are **partially non-identifiable** because over a short 7-hour window on slow soil organic matter decomposition ($k \cdot t \ll 1$), $C_0(1 - e^{-k(t+d)}) \approx C_0 k d + C_0 k t$, where only the product $C_0 k$ (the initial linear respiration rate!) is well-identified by a 7-hour linear/near-linear $\text{CO}_2$ trace, while $C_0$ and $k$ trade off hyperbolically ($C_0 \propto 1/k$).
2. This hyperbolic trade-off between $C_0$ and $k$ explains why in Figure 5 (line 115), **both** $k$ and $C_0$ show nearly identical positive linear relationships with total RSG fluorescence! Over 7 hours at $25^\circ\text{C}$, soil heterotrophic respiration consumes less than 0.1% of total soil organic carbon; the curvature of $\text{CO}_2(t)$ over 7 hours reflects headspace equilibration or microbial lag rather than exhaustion of the total soil substrate pool $C_0$.

### 2.3. Nycodenz Concentration Discrepancy ($1.0\text{ g/mL}$ vs. $1.3\text{ g/mL}$) in Soil Cell Extraction (Line 55) [Fragile]
In line 55, the authors state: "Supernatant from 1 gram of soil was slowly layered on top of 5 mL of **1.0 g/ml Nycodenz solution**... and then centrifuged for 40 min at 12000 $\times$ g. We transferred 2 mL of the upper phase of Nycodenz containing microbial cells..."
Pure water / aqueous buffer has a density of $1.00\text{ g/mL}$, and bacterial cells have a buoyant density of $\sim 1.08\text{--}1.19\text{ g/mL}$ (vegetative cells) to $\sim 1.25\text{ g/mL}$ (spores). Standard Nycodenz density-gradient extraction protocols (including refs. [18, 22, 23] cited by the authors) use **$1.3\text{ g/mL}$ Nycodenz** (80% w/v) so that soil mineral particles ($\rho > 1.4\text{ g/mL}$) pellet through the cushion while microbial cells ($\rho < 1.3\text{ g/mL}$) float at the interface! If the Nycodenz cushion were truly $1.0\text{ g/mL}$, it would have the same density as water, the soil slurry would mix or sink through it, and bacterial cells ($\rho \approx 1.1\text{ g/mL}$) would pellet to the bottom during 40 min at $12,000 \times g$. This is almost certainly a crucial reporting error ($1.0\text{ g/mL}$ written instead of $1.3\text{ g/mL}$, or $1.0\text{ g}$ of Nycodenz per mL of water, which yields $\rho \approx 1.3\text{ g/mL}$).

## 3. Minor / Technical Issues

1. **Inconsistent QUANTOM Tx Volume Conversion Factors:** Line 45 states the cell concentration conversion factor is **14,342 cells/mL**, whereas line 55 states the QUANTOM Tx conversion factor is **14,319**. The exact slide chamber volume and formula should be consistent.
2. **Dimensional Inconsistency in Growth Rate vs. Cell Volume Slope (Line 98):** Line 98 states that the authors "estimated the culture growth rate from the total cell counts over time (Figure 1) by calculating the posterior probability distributions of the derivative of the change in cell numbers. We then fitted a linear model of the mean of the logarithm of the cell volume for both populations at each time point against the growth rate... (slopes for *E. coli* and *P. protegens* = 19.0 [16.6, 21.9] 95% CrI)." Note that the "derivative of the change in cell numbers" ($dN/dt$, cells mL$^{-1}$ h$^{-1}$) is the **absolute cell production rate**, whereas specific **growth rate** is $\mu = \frac{1}{N}\frac{dN}{dt} = \frac{d\ln N}{dt}$ (h$^{-1}$). Moreover, a slope of 19.0 for $\ln(\text{Volume})$ against $\mu$ (h$^{-1}$) would imply that an increase in $\mu$ of $0.5\text{ h}^{-1}$ increases cell volume by $e^{9.5} \approx 13,360$-fold, which is physically impossible; clarify the exact units of the $x$- and $y$-axes in Fig. 2D (e.g., whether $x$ is $\text{h}^{-1}$ or $\log_{10}$ units and whether $19.0$ is $\mu\text{m}^3$ per $\text{h}^{-1}$ or inverse).
3. **Two-Stage Regression of Point Estimates in Figure 5 (Line 71):** Fitting a linear regression on the posterior point estimates of $k$ and $C_0$ against the GLM estimates of RSG fluorescence ignores estimation uncertainty in both variables; errors-in-variables (`brms` `me()` measurement-error syntax) should be used.

## 4. Constructive Remediation Plan

### 1. Re-Evaluate the Lysozyme Compartment Model ($\text{RI}, \text{RA}, \text{SA}, \text{SI}$) Allowing for Detergent-Induced Inactivation ($\text{RA} \to \text{RI}$)
* **WHY:** Incubating cells in 1% Triton X-100 + 10 mM EDTA + lysozyme for 100 min at $37^\circ\text{C}$ can inactivate RSG reductase activity in unlysed cells ($\text{RA} \to \text{RI}$), which the current 4-compartment subtraction misinterprets as lysis of active cells, artificially forcing $\text{SI} = 0\%$.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly relax the assumption that unlysed cells suffer zero loss of RSG activity during the 100-minute detergent/lysozyme incubation, and bound $\text{SI}$ across plausible rates of treatment-induced inactivation $\delta \in [0, 0.5]$.
* **HOW:** In the `brms`/`RStan` model for Figure 4B, introduce a transition parameter $\delta$ representing the fraction of unlysed resistant active cells ($\text{RA}$) that lose RSG fluorescence during the 100-min Triton/EDTA/lysozyme incubation:
  $$\text{Total}_{\text{post}} = \text{RI} + \text{RA}, \qquad \text{Active}_{\text{post}} = (1 - \delta)\text{RA}.$$
  Plot the posterior bounds for $\text{SI}$, $\text{SA}$, $\text{RA}$, and $\text{RI}$ as a function of $\delta$, and discuss the effect of 1% Triton X-100 + 10 mM EDTA on membrane redox potential.

### 2. Replace the Non-Identifiable Asymptotic $C_0(1 - e^{-k(t+d)})$ Parameters with the Well-Identified Initial Respiration Flux ($R_0 = C_0 k$)
* **WHY:** Over a 7-hour incubation of bulk soil, $\text{CO}_2$ evolution does not deplete total soil substrate $C_0$, making $C_0$ and $k$ hyperbolically correlated in $C_0(1 - e^{-k(t+d)})$.
* **WHAT:** Report and regress the initial respiration flux $R_0 = \left.\frac{d\text{CO}_2}{dt}\right|_{t=0} = C_0 k$ ($\text{ppm CO}_2\text{ g}^{-1}\text{ dry soil h}^{-1}$) against total RSG fluorescence using a `brms` measurement-error (`me()`) model.
* **HOW:** From the existing MCMC posterior draws of $C_0$ and $k$ for each of the 48 samples, compute the posterior distribution of the initial respiration rate $R_0 = C_0 \cdot k$ (which is invariant to the $C_0 \leftrightarrow k$ non-identifiability), and fit `brms::brm(R0_mean | se(R0_sd, sigma = TRUE) ~ me(RSG_mean, RSG_sd))` to propagate uncertainty on both axes in Figure 5.

### 3. Clarify the Nycodenz Density ($1.3\text{ g/mL}$ vs. $1.0\text{ g/mL}$), QUANTOM Conversion Factor, and Figure 2D Units
* **WHY:** A $1.0\text{ g/mL}$ Nycodenz cushion cannot separate soil minerals from bacteria by density gradient centrifugation, preventing replication of the extraction step.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  1. Line 55: Clarify whether $1.0\text{ g}$ of solid Nycodenz powder was dissolved per mL (yielding $\sim 80\%\text{ w/v}$, $\rho \approx 1.3\text{ g/mL}$) and state the exact density in $\text{g/mL}$.
  2. Lines 45 and 55: Reconcile the conversion factors `14,342` vs. `14,319`.
  3. Line 98: State the exact mathematical definition and units of growth rate and cell volume used to obtain the slope of `19.0 [16.6, 21.9]` in Fig. 2D.
