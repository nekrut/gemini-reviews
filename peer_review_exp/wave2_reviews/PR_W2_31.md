## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

### 1. SUMMARY AND OVERALL IMPACT STATEMENT

This manuscript characterizes the metabolic reprogramming of murine B cells transitioning from quiescence to proliferation after ex vivo stimulation with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and interleukin-4 (IL-4). Using single-shot Data-Independent Acquisition (DIA) mass spectrometry and the histone-based "proteomic ruler" method, the authors quantify >7,100 proteins in FACS-sorted naive ($n = 3$ mice) and 24 hr LPS + IL-4 activated ($n = 4$ mice) lymph node B cells, alongside a second Astral DIA dataset examining splenic B cells treated with the HMG-CoA reductase (HMGCR) inhibitor Fluvastatin in normal versus lipoprotein-depleted (cholesterol-free, CF) media ($n = 4$ mice per group). The authors show that B cell blasting doubles total cellular protein mass and selectively upregulates cell-cycle machinery, ribosome biogenesis, plasma membrane amino acid transporters (notably the System L heterodimer SLC7A5/SLC3A2), and sterol biosynthesis enzymes (HMGCR, SQLE, LDLR). Using hematopoietic conditional knockout mice ($Slc7a5^{\text{fl/fl}}/\text{Tg}(Vav1\text{-iCre})^{+/-}$, $n = 4$), they demonstrate that SLC7A5 mediates the majority of System L kynurenine uptake and is required for LPS + IL-4 driven B cell survival, proliferation, and IgG1 class switching. Pharmacological inhibition of HMGCR (Fluvastatin, Rosuvastatin), squalene monooxygenase (NB-598), and protein prenylation (FGTI-2734, FTI-277, GGTI-298), combined with metabolite rescue (mevalonate-5-phosphate and geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate, GGPP), indicates that B cell expansion requires both cholesterol availability (via de novo synthesis or LDL uptake) and protein geranylgeranylation. Raw mass spectrometry datasets are publicly deposited in PRIDE (`PXD057739` and `PXD074568`), and raw flow cytometry files and uncropped western blots are provided as source data.

This is a data-rich, well-organized immunometabolism resource with exemplary raw data availability. However, the statistical analysis suffers from pervasive pseudoreplication across nearly half of the flow cytometry panels (treating $n = 3$ technical well replicates from a single mouse as independent biological observations in Student's $t$-tests and ANOVAs), an unaddressed ploidy/cell-cycle bias in the histone proteomic ruler normalization, use of poorly cell-permeable phosphorylated mevalonate-5-phosphate in rescue assays, and several reagent/unit reporting errors in the text and tables. Because the authors already performed 2–3 independent biological experiments for most of the pseudoreplicated panels, these flaws can be resolved through statistical re-analysis of biological replicate means and textual corrections without generating new wet-lab data.

### 2. SPECIFIC CONCLUSIONS AND ASSESSMENT OF SUPPORT

* **LPS + IL-4 stimulation doubles B-cell protein mass and selectively upregulates ribosome biogenesis, amino acid transporters, and sterol biosynthetic enzymes while scaling glycolytic/TCA enzymes proportionally to cell size: [Well Supported].**  
  The two independent DIA proteomics datasets (`PXD057739` on Exploris 480 and `PXD074568` on Astral) are performed with true biological replicates ($n = 3\text{--}4$ mice per condition) and show highly consistent upregulation of ribosomal subunits, SLC7A5/SLC3A2, and mevalonate/cholesterol enzymes (HMGCR, SQLE, LDLR), with explicit feedback upregulation when sterol synthesis or uptake is blocked (Figure 6—figure supplement 1).
* **SLC7A5 is the primary functional System L amino acid transporter in LPS-stimulated B cells and is essential for ex vivo B-cell survival, proliferation, and IgG1 class switching: [Well Supported].**  
  The genetic loss-of-function experiments in $Slc7a5^{\text{fl/fl}}/\text{Tg}(Vav1\text{-iCre})^{+/-}$ B cells ($n = 4$ biological replicates per genotype, Figure 4F–K) are properly powered and paired with competitive pharmacological inhibition (BCH) in kynurenine uptake assays.
* **B-cell survival and proliferation downstream of HMGCR require both cholesterol supply (biosynthesis or exogenous uptake) and protein geranylgeranylation: [Moderately Supported].**  
  The parallel use of HMGCR inhibitors (Fluvastatin, Rosuvastatin), a downstream squalene monooxygenase inhibitor (NB-598), and selective FTase/GGTase inhibitors (FTI-277, GGTI-298, FGTI-2734) across $n = 3$ biological replicates (Figures 6–8) convinces that both branches of the mevalonate pathway matter. However, the mechanistic interpretation of the GGPP vs. mevalonate rescue is complicated by two experimental confounders: (1) the authors used *mevalonic acid 5-phosphate* (`Sigma 79849`), a charged phosphorylated intermediate with poor plasma membrane permeability compared to mevalonolactone, explaining its weak/delayed rescue at 24 hr; and (2) the hypothesis that GGPP restores filipin staining in normal media by rescuing Rab-mediated LDLR endocytosis is plausible but not directly tested via LDL uptake assays.
* **LPS + IL-4 increases mitochondrial volume, p-Rb phosphorylation, OPP translation rate, CD98 surface expression, and MAPK/mTOR-dependent cholesterol accumulation: [Fragile].**  
  In Figure 1—figure supplement 2H, Figure 1—figure supplement 3C, Figure 2B, Figure 2H, Figure 3I, Figure 4E, Figure 5F–H, Figure 5—figure supplement 2J, Figure 7—figure supplement 1, and Figure 9B–F, the authors performed parametric Student's $t$-tests and one-/two-way ANOVAs on $n = 3$ technical replicates derived from a single mouse ($N = 1$). Treating technical wells from one animal as independent statistical units is invalid pseudoreplication that tests pipetting variance rather than biological variance across mice.
* **Metabolic requirement for HMGCR activity is conserved across TLR7, TLR9, BCR, and CD40 activation pathways: [Well Supported].**  
  Figure 3—figure supplement 1 and Figure 10A–E are performed across $n = 3$ biological replicates and demonstrate consistent induction of OPP incorporation and filipin staining, as well as sensitivity to Fluvastatin, across Resiquimod, CpG, anti-IgM, and CD40L stimulation.

## 3. Forensic Technical Audit & Constructive Remediation Plan

### Major Methodological & Statistical Flaws

#### 3.1. Pervasive Pseudoreplication in Flow Cytometry Hypothesis Testing
Across at least ten figure panels—Figure 1—figure supplement 2H (MitoTracker), Figure 1—figure supplement 3C (p-STAT1), Figure 2B (DAPI cell cycle), Figure 2H (p-Rb), Figure 3I (OPP uptake), Figure 4E (CD98), Figure 5F (filipin time course), Figure 5G–H (Fluvastatin and NB-598 titrations), Figure 5—figure supplement 2J (Rosuvastatin titration), Figure 7—figure supplement 1A–B (mevalonate toxicity), and Figure 9B–F (PD184352, VX745, and Rapamycin proliferation, survival, cell size, and filipin staining)—the figure legends explicitly state:
> *"The graph shows three technical replicates from cells isolated from one mouse and is representative of three [or two] independent experiments. Statistical power was determined using an unpaired Student's t-test / one-way ANOVA / two-way ANOVA..."*

Applying Student's $t$-tests or ANOVAs to $n = 3$ technical replicates from a single mouse ($N = 1$) violates the fundamental assumption of independent biological observations (Lazic et al., 2018). The resulting $p$-values ($p < 0.0001$) reflect intra-assay technical precision within one animal and cannot support population-level biological inference. Moreover, in Figure 3I and Figure 5F, only a single mouse is mentioned without any statement of independent repetition. Additionally, throughout every figure legend in the manuscript, the authors write *"Statistical power was determined using [test]"* when referring to null-hypothesis significance testing ($p$-value computation), not statistical power ($1 - \beta$).

#### 3.2. Cell-Cycle Ploidy Bias in Histone "Proteomic Ruler" Concentration Estimates and Arbitrary Thresholding
The authors use the histone proteomic ruler (Wiśniewski et al., 2014) to convert MS intensity into absolute protein copy numbers per cell and intracellular concentrations ($\mu\text{M}$). The proteomic ruler assumes a fixed diploid ($2N$) DNA and histone mass per cell (~6.5 pg DNA/cell in mouse). However, while naive B cells are quiescent in G0/G1 ($2N$), Figure 2A–B shows that after 24 hr of LPS + IL-4 stimulation, a substantial proportion of B cells have entered S and G2/M phases, where genomic DNA and core histones are actively replicated toward $4N$. Assuming constant histone mass per cell between G0 and cycling S/G2 cells systematically underestimates total protein copy numbers in the 24 hr stimulated condition and skews the estimated cellular concentration of proteins whose synthesis is phase-locked to G1 vs. S/G2. Furthermore, in Figure 6—figure supplement 1, the main text (line 24) states that differentially expressed proteins were defined using $q < 0.05$, whereas the figure legend (line 20) states that the horizontal dashed line indicates $q < 0.01$.

#### 3.3. Use of Phosphorylated Mevalonate-5-Phosphate in Metabolite Rescue and Unnormalized Filipin MFI
In the Methods (line 56), the reagent used for "mevalonate" supplementation (`2 mM`) is identified as **Mevalonic acid 5-phosphate lithium salt hydrate** (`79849`, Sigma Aldrich) dissolved in HEPES. Unlike unphosphorylated mevalonic acid / mevalonolactone, phosphorylated mevalonate-5-phosphate carries a dianionic phosphate group at physiological pH that severely restricts passive diffusion across the plasma membrane. This biochemical property explains why millimolar concentrations (`2 mM`) failed to rescue filipin staining at 24 hr (Figure 7A) and only partially rescued proliferation compared to `10 µM` GGPP (Figure 8A). Second, filipin staining is reported strictly as raw MFI per cell; because activated B cells increase markedly in size and membrane surface area between 0 and 48 hr (FSC in Figure 1C and 6D), raw filipin MFI conflates total cellular sterol mass with membrane sterol density.

However, in Figure 6 (line 17), Figure 6—figure supplement 1 (line 20), Figure 7 (line 31, C–F), Figure 8 (line 41, A–H), Figure 9 (line 53), Figure 9—figure supplement 2 (line 59), and Figure 10 (line 67), the IL-4 concentration is erroneously reported as `10 μg/ml`.

   * `PD184352` is misspelled as `PD18352` in Table 2 and Figure 9 legend, and its target in Table 2 is listed as `MSK1/2` instead of `MEK1/2` (MKK1/2).

### 3. CONSTRUCTIVE AND DOABLE SUGGESTIONS FOR IMPROVEMENT

#### 1. Replace Pseudoreplicated Technical-Well Statistics with Biological Replicate Inference
* **WHY:** Running $t$-tests and ANOVAs on $n = 3$ technical wells from a single mouse ($N = 1$) invalidates the reported $p$-values in Figures 1S2H, 1S3C, 2B, 2H, 3I, 4E, 5F–H, 5S2J, 7S1, and 9B–F.
* **WHAT:** Re-plot and re-analyze all flow cytometry summary bar graphs using the means of the $N = 3$ independent biological experiments (mice) as the unit of analysis ($N = 3$), or remove $p$-value asterisks from any experiment performed in fewer than $N = 3$ mice (e.g., if $N = 2$ for Figure 4E, 5G–H, 7S1).
* **HOW:** Since the raw FACS files for the independent experiments are already available to the authors, compute the mean MFI or percentage per mouse across the 3 technical wells, plot the biological replicate means ($N = 3$ mice) as individual symbols, and run the one-way/two-way ANOVA or paired/unpaired $t$-test across the biological replicates in Prism. Replace all instances of the phrase *"Statistical power was determined using..."* with *"Statistical significance was determined using..."*.

#### 2. Audit Histone Copy-Number Scaling Across Cell-Cycle Phases in the Proteomics Dataset
* **WHY:** The proteomic ruler assumes constant histone mass per cell ($2N$ DNA), whereas 24 hr LPS + IL-4 stimulated B cells have a substantial fraction of cells in S and G2/M phases (Figure 2A–B).
* **WHAT:** Quantify the expected DNA/histone ploidy correction factor from the DAPI cell-cycle distribution at 24 hr and verify that key upregulated metabolic pathways remain significant after ploidy adjustment.
* **HOW:** Using the DAPI cell-cycle percentages in Figure 2B ($f_{\text{G0/G1}} \times 1.0 + f_{\text{S}} \times 1.5 + f_{\text{G2/M}} \times 2.0$), calculate the mean genome equivalent per stimulated B cell (~1.2–1.3$\times$ diploid) and report in the Methods how this affects absolute copy number estimates versus intracellular concentration ($\mu\text{M}$, where histone-to-total-protein ratios cancel out ploidy only if histones scale isometrically with cell volume). Also harmonize the $q$-value cutoff between the main text ($q < 0.05$) and Figure 6—figure supplement 1 legend ($q < 0.01$).

#### 3. Clarify the Use of Mevalonate-5-Phosphate and Normalize Filipin Staining to Cell Size
* **WHY:** Using phosphorylated mevalonate-5-phosphate (`Sigma 79849`) rather than cell-permeable mevalonolactone explains why 2 mM mevalonate-5-P was less potent than 10 µM GGPP at 24 hr, and raw filipin MFI is partially confounded by cell size (FSC) expansion.
* **WHAT:** Explicitly state in the Results and Discussion that mevalonate-5-phosphate was used and discuss its lower membrane permeability relative to GGPP; additionally, provide FSC-normalized filipin ratios (Filipin MFI / FSC) alongside raw Filipin MFI.
* **HOW:** From the existing FlowJo workspaces in `Figure 5—source data 1/2` and `Figure 8—source data 2`, compute the ratio of Filipin MFI to FSC-A for each sample to demonstrate that sterol depletion by Fluvastatin/NB-598 and rescue by GGPP reflect genuine changes in sterol density per unit cell size.

#### 4. Correct Reagent, Unit, and Genotype Reporting Errors Across Text and Tables
* **WHY:** Unit errors of three orders of magnitude (`10 μg/ml` vs. `10 ng/ml` IL-4; `400 ml` vs. `400 µl` lysis buffer) and misidentified inhibitor targets (`MSK1/2` vs. `MEK1/2`) impede experimental reproducibility.
* **WHAT & HOW:**
  * Change `IL-4 (10 μg/ml)` to `IL-4 (10 ng/ml)` in the legends of Figure 6, Figure 6—figure supplement 1, Figure 7, Figure 8, Figure 9, Figure 9—figure supplement 2, and Figure 10.
  * In Table 2 and Figure 9 legend, correct `PD18352` to `PD184352`, change its target in Table 2 from `MSK1/2` to `MEK1/2 (MKK1/2)`, and correct `GGTI-289` to `GGTI-298`.
  * Reconcile `ODN 1826` (Figure 3—figure supplement 1 and Figure 10 legends) vs. `ODN 2006` (Methods, line 58).
  * Change `400 ml of lysis buffer` to `400 µl of lysis buffer` in Methods (line 137), and specify the exact genotype of the WT control mice used alongside $Slc7a5^{\text{fl/fl}}/\text{Tg}(Vav1\text{-iCre})^{+/-}$ mice in Methods (line 15).
