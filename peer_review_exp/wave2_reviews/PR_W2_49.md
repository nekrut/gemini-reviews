## 2. Executive Summary & Editorial Verdict

This manuscript re-evaluates whether adaptive niche differentiation is required for the establishment and persistence of autopolyploid lineages. Extending Felber's classical panmictic model via a gamete-pool iterated map that tracks haploid ($x_t$), diploid ($y_t$), and triploid ($z_t$) gametes parameterized by the unreduced gamete rate ($v$), triploid relative fitness ($\phi$), and triploid meiotic segregation proportions ($\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$), the authors show that the critical unreduced gamete threshold $v_{\max}$ required for tetraploid fixation equals $1/5 = 0.20$ when $\phi = 0$ and decays approximately exponentially as triploid fitness $\phi$ increases (e.g., $v_{\max} \approx 1.8\%$ at $\phi = 0.30$). Incorporating finite population size ($N$), restricted mating radius ($R$), and environmental gradients into spatially explicit individual-based simulations—including a simulation on a map of Australian annual precipitation (`Bio12`) inspired by the *Neobatrachus* frog complex—the authors argue that ecological drift at low-density range expansion fronts and dispersal limitation allow polyploids to establish, spatially cluster, and diverge in realized environmental distributions without any *a priori* ploidy-specific adaptive advantage. Java simulation code is publicly hosted on GitHub (`KauaiFe/PolyploidNiche`).

**Editorial Verdict: Major Revision Required (Mixed / Salvageable).** The gamete-pool formulation of the triploid-bridge model and the conceptual connection between minority cytotype exclusion, bistable spatial waves, and gene surfing at low-density expansion fronts are valuable theoretical contributions. However, a forensic audit of the mathematical derivations and simulation rules reveals a critical modeling flaw that invalidates the "neutral / ploidy-independent fitness" claim in the evolutionary simulations, alongside multiple algebraic errors in the printed equations: (1) in the evolutionary simulations (Figures 3 and 4; Methods line 84), organismal fitness is defined as $\psi = e^{-d_{\min}}$, where $d_{\min} = \min_{i=1\dots p} d(B_i, E(x,y))$ is the **smallest** Hamming distance across the individual's $p$ chromosomes; because the minimum of $p=4$ chromosomes (tetraploid) is strictly smaller in expectation than the minimum of $p=2$ chromosomes (diploid) and provides twice the mutational target size per individual under complete chromosome-level dominance, the model **hardcodes a substantial intrinsic adaptive advantage for tetraploids**; (2) Methods line 84 refers the reader to `(see results for an account of additive effects involving dominance at the allele level)`, which does not exist anywhere in the manuscript; and (3) the text contains numerous mathematical errors, including an incorrect bifurcation condition in Equation (5) ($x = \frac{1-v}{2-v}$ instead of $x = \frac{3(1-v)}{2(2-v)}$), inconsistent Moore neighborhood formulas ($(2R-1)^2$ vs. $(2R+1)^2$), a factor-of-2 error in disk area ($2\rho\pi R^2$), a corrupted limit ($R \longrightarrow 8$ for $R \to \infty$), and an inverted mating compatibility inequality (`"Hamming distance... at least 95%"` instead of similarity $\ge 95\%$).

### Assessment of Specific Claims

* **Claim 1 (In a panmictic population where both diploids and tetraploids produce unreduced gametes at rate $v$ and triploids are inviable [$\phi = 0$], the critical threshold for tetraploid takeover is $v_{\max} = 0.20$, which decreases approximately exponentially with increasing triploid fitness $\phi$ and smaller population size $N$):** `[Well Supported]`  
  The value $v_{\max} = 1/5 = 0.20$ for the fold bifurcation of the quadratic equilibrium Equation (4) ($-(2-v)x^2 + 3(1-v)x + v - 1 = 0$, discriminant $\Delta = (1-v)(1-5v) = 0$) is mathematically correct, as is the qualitative exponential reduction of $v_{\max}$ via the triploid bridge ($\phi > 0$) and stochastic drift in finite $N$ (Fig. 1A–B). However, the intermediate derivation printed in Equation (5) is algebraically incorrect (see Defect 3.2).
* **Claim 2 (Dispersal limitation [small mating radius $R$] and low local population density [$\rho$] accelerate polyploid establishment by reducing local effective mate numbers and promoting spatial clustering):** `[Moderately Supported]`  
  Consistent with bistable wave dynamics under positive frequency-dependent selection (minority cytotype exclusion). However, in Results line 39 (Fig. 2A), the preferential nucleation of polyploids at the corners and edges of the $100 \times 100$ lattice is an artificial boundary effect caused by truncating the mating neighborhood at the square grid walls (reducing partner count by $1/2$ along edges and $1/4$ at corners).
* **Claim 3 (In spatially explicit evolutionary simulations along environmental gradients, polyploids monopolize novel niche space ahead of diploids purely via low-density drift at the expansion front, *without any a priori adaptive advantage* or ploidy-dependent fitness difference):** `[Methodologically Flawed]`  
  Contrary to the statement in Results line 46 that individual fitness is *"independent of ploidy level"*, Methods line 84 defines reproductive probability as $\psi = e^{-d_{\min}}$, where $d_{\min}$ is the *minimum* Hamming distance between any one of the organism's $p$ chromosomes ($\Lambda_p$) and the local environmental optimum $E(x,y)$. Under this "best-of-$p$-chromosomes" dominance rule, a tetraploid ($p=4$) samples 4 chromosomes per generation (and accumulates mutations across 4 templates while shielding 3 copies from purifying selection), whereas a diploid ($p=2$) samples only 2. Because $\mathbb{E}[\min(d_1, d_2, d_3, d_4)] < \mathbb{E}[\min(d_1, d_2)]$, tetraploids possess a built-in fitness and evolvability advantage at every step of the range expansion!
* **Claim 4 (Simulated phylogeographic radiation on the Australian precipitation map demonstrates that the bimodal environmental distribution of *Neobatrachus* autotetraploids reflects neutral geographic displacement rather than adaptive niche divergence):** `[Fragile]`  
  Besides being confounded by the $\min_{i=1\dots p} d_i$ tetraploid fitness advantage above, the comparison to empirical *Neobatrachus* data (Fig. 4C) is purely qualitative, lacks any statistical goodness-of-fit test or parameter sensitivity analysis, and does not compare the neutral-drift model against an explicit stress-adaptation alternative model.

## 3. Forensic Defect Inventory

### 3.1 Hidden Ploidy Fitness Advantage in the "Best-of-$p$" Chromosome Dominance Rule (Pillars 1, 3, 5)
* **Order-Statistic Bias ($\min_{i=1\dots 4} d_i$ vs. $\min_{i=1\dots 2} d_i$) and Missing Additive Control:** In Methods (`Empirically motivated eco-evolutionary simulation of mixed-ploidy populations`, line 84), each individual carries $p$ binary chromosomes $B_1, \dots, B_p$ of length 100 ($p=2$ for diploids, $p=3$ for triploids, $p=4$ for tetraploids). Reproductive success is given by:
  $$\psi(\Lambda_p) = e^{-d_{\min}}, \quad d_{\min} = \min_{i \in \{1, \dots, p\}} d_{\text{Hamming}}(B_i, E(x,y))$$
  When individuals disperse into a novel grid cell along the environmental gradient or accumulate random mutations (rate $\mu$) and recombination ($m$), the minimum of $p=4$ draws from the chromosome distance distribution is systematically smaller than the minimum of $p=2$ draws. Furthermore, a tetraploid can maintain one locally adapted chromosome ($d_1 \approx 0 \implies \psi \approx 1$) while its other three chromosomes freely accumulate mutations without any fitness penalty, whereas a diploid can only shelter one chromosome.
* **Non-Existent Reference to Additive Effects in Results:** Methods line 84 attempts to justify this rule by stating: *"That is, we implement chromosome-level dominance for simplicity (see results for an account of additive effects involving dominance at the allele level)."* A full-text audit confirms that **no account of additive effects or allele-level dominance exists anywhere in the Results or Discussion**. To establish that range-margin drift and priority effects alone drive polyploid niche expansion without an *a priori* genetic advantage, the simulations in Figures 3 and 4 must be run under a ploidy-neutral fitness function (e.g., mean chromosome distance $\bar{d} = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^p d(B_i, E(x,y))$ with mutation rate scaled by $1/p$ so total genomic mutation supply is equal, or identical phenotypic trait inheritance).

### 3.2 Algebraic Errors in Analytical Equations and Spatial Formulas (Pillars 3, 7)
* **Incorrect Bifurcation Condition in Equation (5) (Results lines 30–31):**
  * To find the saddle-node bifurcation $v_{\max}$ of the equilibrium quadratic Equation (4), $f(x) = -x^2(2-v) + 3x(1-v) + v - 1 = 0$, the authors write: *"To find $v_{\max}$ we first find the intersection of (4) with its first derivative: (5) $x = \frac{1-v}{2-v}$."*
  * First, a fold bifurcation occurs where $f(x) = 0$ and **$f'(x) = 0$** (the vertex of the parabola touches the axis), *not* where $f(x) = f'(x)$.
  * Second, setting the first derivative $f'(x) = -2(2-v)x + 3(1-v) = 0$ yields:
    $$x = \frac{3(1-v)}{2(2-v)}$$
    Wait: the printed Equation (5), $x = \frac{1-v}{2-v}$, omits the factor of $\frac{3}{2}$! Substituting the printed Equation (5) into Equation (4) yields $\frac{(1-v)(-v)}{2-v} = 0$, which has no root at $v = 1/5$! Substituting the correct vertex $x = \frac{3(1-v)}{2(2-v)}$ into Equation (4) yields $\frac{(1-v)(1-5v)}{4(2-v)} = 0 \implies v_{\max} = 1/5$ (at $x^* = 2/3$).
* **Normalization of Equations (1–3) When $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 \neq 1$:** In Equations (1–3) (line 26), the common denominator is written as $x_t^2 + 2\phi x_t y_t + (y_t^2 + 2x_t z_t)(1-v)$, which equals the sum of the three numerators *only* if $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 1$. If triploids produce inviable aneuploid gametes such that $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 < 1$, the term $2\phi x_t y_t$ in the denominator must be multiplied by $(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3)$ for $x_{t+1} + y_{t+1} + z_{t+1} = 1$ to hold. Also note that in the inline text of line 26, tetraploid frequency is written as `(y_t^2 + x_t z_t)` missing the factor of `2` on `x_t z_t`.
* **Contradictory Moore Neighborhood Size Formulas:** Results line 37 states that for an extended Moore neighborhood of radius $R$, *"the number of grid cells around the seeker, is simply $(2R - 1)^2$."* In contrast, Methods line 82 states *"one of the $(2R + 1)^2$ cells around – and including that of – the seeker."* For Chebyshev distance $R$, the neighborhood contains $(2R + 1)^2$ cells (or $(2R+1)^2 - 1$ excluding the seeker, never $(2R-1)^2$).
* **Factor-of-2 Error in Disk Mate Count and Corrupted Infinity Symbol (Results line 39):**
  * Line 39 states: *"a seeker at the center of the landscape will have $2\rho\pi R^2$ mating partners, where $\rho$ is the population density. In contrast, there are only $0.5\rho\pi R^2$ mating partners available for a seeker that is located right at the corner... since the disc sector is exactly $1/4$ of the disc in the center."* The area of a full circle of radius $R$ is $\pi R^2$ (giving $\rho\pi R^2$ partners in the center and $0.25\rho\pi R^2$ at a corner, not $2\rho\pi R^2$ and $0.5\rho\pi R^2$).
  * Four sentences later in line 39, $R \to \infty$ is misprinted as *"when $R \longrightarrow 8$"*.
* **Inverted Mating Compatibility Inequality (Methods line 84):** Line 84 states: *"Finally, a successful mating event happens when the **Hamming distance** between two randomly selected chromosomes is **at least 95%**."* Because Hamming distance measures the number of *mismatched* bits, a Hamming distance of $\ge 95\%$ means the chromosomes are $\ge 95\%$ *different* (at most 5% identical). The authors meant **sequence similarity** $\ge 95\%$ (i.e., Hamming distance $\le 5\%$, or $\le 5$ bit differences out of 100).

### Actionable Remediation Plan
1. **Remove the Intrinsic Tetraploid Advantage in the Evolutionary Simulations (Figures 3 and 4):** Re-run the gradient and *Neobatrachus* simulations using a genuinely ploidy-neutral fitness function—specifically, replacing $d_{\min} = \min_{i=1\dots p} d(B_i, E(x,y))$ with the **mean chromosome Hamming distance** $\bar{d} = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^p d(B_i, E(x,y))$ (the additive model promised in Methods line 84) while adjusting per-chromosome mutation rate so that diploids and tetraploids have equal phenotypic variance—and compare the resulting NND distributions against the chromosome-dominance model ($\min d_i$).
2. **Correct All Mathematical Equations and Methodological Descriptions:**
   * Replace Equation (5) ($x = \frac{1-v}{2-v}$) in Results line 30 with the correct vertex condition $f'(x) = 0 \implies x = \frac{3(1-v)}{2(2-v)}$ and revise the preceding sentence (`"where the first derivative of (4) equals zero"`).
   * In Equations (1–3), explicitly state $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 1$ (or include $(\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3)$ in the denominator if aneuploid loss occurs), and fix `(y_t^2 + x_t z_t)` to `(y_t^2 + 2 x_t z_t)` in the inline text of line 26.
   * Fix $(2R - 1)^2$ to $(2R + 1)^2$ in Results line 37; fix $2\rho\pi R^2$ and $0.5\rho\pi R^2$ to $\rho\pi R^2$ and $0.25\rho\pi R^2$ in line 39; fix `R -> 8` to $R \to \infty$ in line 39; and fix `"Hamming distance... is at least 95%"` to `"sequence similarity is at least 95% (Hamming distance <= 5%)"` in Methods line 84.
3. **Eliminate Square-Lattice Boundary Artifacts in Spatial Simulations:** Repeat the spatial establishment simulations (Fig. 2) on a torus (periodic boundary conditions) or within a circular carrying-capacity gradient so that polyploid nucleation is driven by genuine demographic stochasticity rather than geometric clipping at the four corners of the square grid.
