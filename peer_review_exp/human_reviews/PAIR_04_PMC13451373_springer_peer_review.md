Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Buyanbadrakh et al. investigates pairwise interactions among 36 representative gut bacterial strains,
reporting that approximately 25% of interactions result in growth inhibition and 4% lead to growth promotion. The authors
also provide additional insights into specific interactions, particularly those between M. gnavus and C. perfringens, and
between Veillonella and P. merdae. Overall, the manuscript is clearly written, the data is well presented, and the figures are
of high quality. The methods are mostly sound, and the selection of representative strains is reasonably justified. The
incorporation of proteomic analyses of selected bacteria grown in spent versus fresh media is a strength. 
However, I remain uncertain about the ecological significance of the findings, and the extent to which they advance our
understanding of these interactions is somewhat limited, as I outline in my comments below. Despite these limitations, the
study provides a systematic dataset of interactions between representative gut bacteria. This offers a useful foundation for
future efforts to analyse microbe-microbe interactions. 
1. Many of the reported interactions appear to be driven largely by acidification of spent media. It is unclear how relevant
these pH shifts are for bacterial interactions in vivo, given that distal gut pH is generally stable and any local drops are
expected to be transient? Therefore, I would have expected that the interactions/growth in spent media were tested after
adjusting the pH of spent media. Indirectly adjusting pH by diluting with rich media, as the authors did, does not allow to
disentangle the effect of cross-feeding/nutrient depletion/inhibitory metabolites vs. pH, as it also dilutes spent media. Unless
the authors can convincingly discuss the ecological relevance of such changes in pH, further experiments on how pH really
affects results would be important, i.e. testing interactions with spent media with pH adjusted only (no/minimal dilution). 
1a. Lines 68-70: would be good to see the heatmap equivalent to Fig.1b when the spent medium was replenished with
nutrients. For instance, do interactions between M. gnavus and perfringens still hold? 
2. The OD measurements were performed using a Cerillo Stratus plate reader, which does not incorporate shaking. This
raises concerns about the accuracy of OD readings, especially for strains that tend to aggregate or form biofilms. Without
agitation, cells can rapidly settle, leading to inconsistent or misleading OD values. 
The uthors present in Fig.1c the replicate variability (I commend the authors for reporting this), but does one know whether
the device reliably captured cells in suspension rather than reading mostly settled cultures? 
3. The authors nicely show that vesicles produced by C. perfringens boost M. gnavus growth, and literature supports the
presence of nucleic acids in these vesicles - but addition of nucleic acids does not boost M. gnavus growth - why? How do
authors envision that vesicles containing DNA would? 
4. For the proteomics analysis, bacterial cells were harvested after 24
 
h of growth (methods). Is 24 hours an appropriate time
point to capture biologically relevant differences in the proteome? Table S1 suggests cultures would be already in the
stationary phase at 24h, for both M. gnavus and V. parvula. 
5. The mechanism via which Veilonella is increasing pH should be further explored: ammonia production would be an
obvious one, as levels of the enzyme guanidine deaminase are increased (Fig.4c), and this is known to catalyse the
hydrolytic deamination of guanine to xanthine and ammonia. Has ammonia production been measured?

6. The discussion lacks depth in addressing how the results from these in vitro pairwise assays can be generalized to
community level dynamics under the physiological conditions of the gut. A broader consideration of how these findings
translate to the complex, spatially structured, mostly buffered environment of the intestine is missing. 
In addition, some statements are insufficiently explained. For instance: “M. gnavus produces an anti-C. perfringens
bacteriocin emphasizing strong growth dynamics between these species.” What do the authors implicate by this, I do not see
any inhibition of perfringens growth in M. gnavus spent media. Is this because a different strain was used?

The way it is currently written, it gives the impression that all percentages reported are a % of inhibitory interactions, not of
the total interactions tested. This needs to be rephrased for clarity.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Buyanbadrakh et al profile the pairwise interactions between 36 gut bacterial strains using a spent medium assay. The
authors report that most interactions are inhibitory and due to resource depletion or medium acidification. The authors then
focus on a few positive interactions to investigate mechanisms. The dataset is valuable and the follow-up mechanistic
experiments on a few selected interactions are interesting. My main comments are about clarifying how interactions are
quantified, tone down a few general claims so they more closely match the experiments and data, and add quantitative
values/ statistics when missing. See my comments below for details. 
Main comments: 
1. The authors state that “Most interactions were inhibitory and largely explained by resource depletion or medium
acidification.” (L19) and “Widespread growth inhibiting and growth promoting ecological interactions among human gut
microbes.” (Figure 1 legend; L94). However, Figure 1d does suggest that most interactions are actually neutral under the 2-
fold change threshold. I think that the statement that “Most interactions were inhibitory” should be clarified and rephrased. 
It would also be helpful to show the following heatmaps: a heatmap like Fig 1b for the replenished medium data and
heatmaps with the 2-fold change threshold for both the spent medium and the replenished medium. This would make it
clearer which pairwise interactions are inhibitory and more likely driven by resource depletion vs other mechanisms of
inhibition, and which interactions are neutral or positive. 
2. L60. “Growth dynamics were quantified by calculating the area under the growth curve (AUC).” Because this metric (how
the interactions are measured) is key to the interpretation of most results in this paper, please explain why you chose AUC
instead of other metrics, and why “AUC up to early stationary phase”. Has this approach been used in previous work? Would
the conclusions hold with other metrics (e.g. yield), and if not why? Please clarify. 
Also , Fig 1a schematic says “exponential phase” AUC while Fig 1 legend says “The area under the curve of the early
stationary phase (AUC) was used as the final growth measurement.” (L499), and in the Methods Line 256 it says “Growth
curves were first trimmed at strain-specific time points when they reached stationary phase “. Please use consistent
terminology and clarify how reaching stationary phase was defined. 
3. I think the authors should tone down some of the claims about the broader ecological relevance of their findings. 
For instance: 
L26-L28. “This demonstrates the ecological relevance of V. parvula, a species that, despite being commonly present in
human gut microbiomes, is generally found at low levels. Overall, the comprehensive dataset and mechanistic insights
presented here provide a foundation for rational strategies to manipulate microbiome composition and function.” 
and 
L50-51. “We used these data to prioritize the study of molecular mechanisms behind specific interactions, which enables
establishing rational strategies to manipulate microbial community composition and function.” 
These statements should be rephrased to more explicitly and realistically acknowledge the limitations of the study (indirect,
pairwise, spent media assay) and to frame the broader implications of the mechanisms observed here as a starting point for
future testing. 
4. Some of the results are idiosyncratic or largely qualitative, and there are a few places where a more quantitative analysis
is warranted to support the claims made. See below for specifics.
